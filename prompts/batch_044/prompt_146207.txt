You will advise a speaker preparing for a joint philosophy-of-language and comparative-literature colloquium. The audience is precisely this mix: four translation theorists in the functionalist-descriptive tradition who regard formal preservation as culturally anachronistic and elitist; two formal linguists who accept meter as phonologically real but deny it carries pragmatic or semantic load; and two historians of Renaissance French poetry who know Marot's salon contexts intimately and will charge anachronism in applying modern pragmatic theory to sixteenth-century verse practice.
