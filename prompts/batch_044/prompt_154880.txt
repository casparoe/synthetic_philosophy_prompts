I am writing my final paper for a graduate seminar on Foucault's analytics of power and the historical epistemology of the human sciences. Rather than surveying established interpretations, I want to identify which of three emerging conceptual problems extending his framework into contemporary institutional knowledge production is genuinely upstream of the others, and therefore worth a semester of focused work.

The three problems are: (1) whether Foucault's distinction between disciplinary power and biopower collapses when real-time data feedback loops continuously translate population-level metrics into individual behavioral correction; (2) whether the concept of the episteme developed in The Archaeology of Knowledge can be rigorously applied to algorithmic categorization without being reduced to an instance of disciplinary normalization; and (3) whether the institutional shift from the examination to predictive risk profiling marks a qualitative transformation in the power-knowledge nexus or merely a quantitative intensification of existing surveillance mechanisms.

Please defend a specific ordering of priority. For each problem, explain whether resolving it is upstream of the others, what kind of clarification it requires—historical, formal, or normative—and how tractable it is for conceptual rather than empirical research. Also specify what kind of argument or development over the next year would cause you to revise the ranking, and if any of these questions depends on a category mistake that renders it a dead end, say so and explain the consequence for the remaining two. I am not asking for a bibliography or reading list, but for a defended research agenda.
