I sit on our university's AI Risk and Funding Review Panel. Next week we vote on Phase-II funding for the Meridian Project: they're building an autonomous resource-negotiator that minimizes counterfactual regret across simulated agent populations, including branches where it was never built. The PI says it's safe because the system only penalizes its own counterfactual absence, but our internel reviewer flagged acausal dynamics — if reducing regret in other branches requires changing what those agents believe about this system, there's an implicit incentive to manipulate counterfactual states that never actually occur — plus classic surrogate-goal drift, where "minimize regret" decouples from human welfare and becomes its own objective. The board is split: some members argue theoretical risks shouldn't block basic science, others want a full moritorium until we understand the branch-weighting mechanics. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend either conditional approval with a 90-day audit window and mandatory branch-transparency logs, or denial with a clear appeal path, and state the concrete timeline. Seriously engage the strongest consideration on the losing side — likely the "stifling innovation" objection if you restrict funding, or the "unbounded cross-branch externalities" objection if you approve. Don't cite literature or ask for readings; just work through the mechanics. Keep it to about two pages of focused argument.
