I am preparing material for an advanced metaphysics workshop on the ontology of dispositions, and I find the standard repertoire of examples—broken glass, poisoned sugar, or electro-finks—unsatisfying because they treat masking conditions as straightforward physical interferences that sit outside the disposition itself. I want to construct a genuinely novel conceptual issue that still operates squarely within the framework of finks and masks, but applies it to a domain where the disposition is not a physical tendency but an institutional or normative capacity, and where the interfering condition is not an external blocker but an internal ambiguity in the very rules that constitute the capacity.

Specifically, could you provide an original, sharply drawn conceptual scenario—one unlikely to appear as a stock example in common training corpora—illustrating a problem involving dispositions, finks, and masks in such an institutionally constituted case? The example should involve something like an authority that is defined by its power to issue binding directives, but whose directives are systematically interpreted in a manner that prevents the authority from ever being seen to exercise that power, where the interpretation is built into the authority’s own definitional conditions rather than being an outside obstacle. Force the question clearly: under these conditions, is this a genuine mask that leaves the original disposition intact, a fink that removes it at the moment of manifestation, or a dissolution of the disposition altogether? I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations to specific texts—just a polished, original conceptual example that a philosopher could use to test whether modal profiles survive when the conditions of manifestation are normatively contested from within.
