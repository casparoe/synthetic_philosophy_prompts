Write a sestina about the combination problem in panpsychism, using the form's mandatory rotation of six end-words to do the philosophical work. Select six end-words that name elements of the debate—such as subject, quality, bound, part, whole, and derive—so that their permutation across the stanzas mirrors the rearrangement of micro-subjects or micro-qualities into macro-configurations without achieving genuine integration. In stanzas one through three, present the subject-summing problem: many micro-subjects, each bearing its own qualitative character, arranged in successive permutations that still fail to constitute a single macro-subject. At the structural turn (around stanza four), shift to cosmopsychism and its inverted derivation problem: one universal subject from which parts derive consciousness, with the same six terms now dispersed across the rotation rather than gathered into unity. In the envoi, include all six end-words in its three lines, arranged so that their forced co-presence either parodies successful combination or exposes its absence—specifically, treat a postulated "phenomenal bonding" relation not as a resolution but as an unclosed loop within the rotation itself. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with the micropsychist combination problem and with cosmopsychist derivation recognizes both positions and their structural symmetry, and it should leave the tension suspended rather than resolved: the final tercet ought to make it unclear whether the macro-subject has emerged or whether the rotation has merely renamed the gap. Do not cite texts or offer a bibliography; render the concepts precisely enough that the form and the philosophy are inseparable.
