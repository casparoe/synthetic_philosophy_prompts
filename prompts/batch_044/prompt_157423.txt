I am a PhD candidate in political theory studying institutional autonomy in post-colonial legal systems. During a recent departmental seminar on historical materialism, a discussant deployed the slogan that the economic base determines the ideological superstructure to reject my claim that constitutional form can exhibit genuine independence from immediate relations of production. Rather than treat the phrase as settled doctrine, I want to understand precisely what is being asserted. Please disambiguate the slogan into a menu of distinct theses—distinguishing causal determination in the last instance, functional adaptation of institutions, structural constraint without direct causation, and strong reductive determination. For each reading, indicate whether it is a platitude, a defensible substantive claim, or false, and specify which version my interlocutor was likely invoking to dismiss legal autonomy. In particular, address whether any coherent materialist position—perhaps along lines of overdetermination or relative autonomy—can accommodate meaningful institutional independence without collapsing into tautology or abandoning the materialist core altogether. I am not asking for a bibliography, reading list, or textual exegesis; I want conceptual clarification that I can use to diagnose the move made in my workshop.
