I've been trying to work out a consistent view about representation in what I'm calling a careenium — a system of simmballs arranged in state-space where symbolic tokens are the relational patterns among them rather than the individual substrate elements. Before I present this in a workshop, I want a consistency audit on the following commitments, which I think hang together but I'm not sure:

(1) The careenium's symbols are multiply realizable: the same symbol can be constituted by different simmball configurations across time or across distinct careenia, so symbols are not identical to any particular substrate arrangement.

(2) All causal work in the careenium is done at the substrate level; symbols have no downward causal efficacy and do not "push" the simmballs around.

(3) Despite (2), explanatory and predictive success in understanding careenium behavior requires citing symbolic properties — for example, predicting an avoidance maneuver requires citing that the system represents an obstacle, and no substrate-level description alone delivers the same predictive scope.

(4) Symbolic content in a careenium is fixed either by an external interpreter or by internal meta-symbolic clusters that assign roles; in neither case is content an intrinsic physical property of the substrate.

(5) In some careenia, meta-symbolic clusters physically alter the dynamics of lower-level simmballs in ways that depend on the assigned content — so that two substrate-identical configurations behave differently depending on which meta-symbol is active.

My questions: Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look tense, and what is the least costly revision? In particular, does (5) contradict (2) in a way that forces me to abandon downward-causal inertness, or can (2) be preserved by reinterpreting what "causal work" means? Does (3) sit comfortably with (4), or does the need for symbolic explanation commit me to treating symbols as causally relevant in a sense that undermines (2)? And is there a hidden conflict between (1) and (5) — if symbols are multiply realizable, can meta-symbols really exert content-dependent causal influence without being reducible to substrate? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and at what cost. I want an audit of my package, not a lecture on representation in general.
