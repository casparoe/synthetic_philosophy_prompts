Write a one-semester research proposal (approximately 6,000 words, three months) on a precise variant of the Sleeping Beauty problem intended to test whether self-locating belief updates can be fully accommodated within Jeffrey conditionalization, or whether they require a separate formal apparatus. The variant: Beauty knows the coin is fair; if tails, she is awakened on Monday and again on Tuesday, but on Tuesday she is shown false evidence that it is Monday and that she is in the Monday room. Upon awakening—without knowing the day or whether she has been misinformed—she is asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. The core question: should her update treat her temporal location and the reliability of her evidence as part of the evidential parameter to be conditioned on, or as part of the hypothesis space over which she distributes credence? Frame the proposal to show why the standard halfer and thirder positions do not settle this case, since both assume that Beauty correctly identifies her temporal stage upon awakening. Specify the method: constructing a parameterized model of possibilities that distinguishes world-branch (heads vs. tails) from temporal stage (Monday vs. Tuesday) and evidential status (reliable vs. misled), then deriving the rational credence under each framing. State exactly what a result would look like—for instance, a demonstration that one framing yields a Dutch-book vulnerability while the other does not, or a proof that both converge to the same value and the dispute dissolves—and specify what would count as failure, such as the formalization collapsing into an existing answer without explaining why the false-evidence case behaves differently. Give a realistic scope assessment: can the formal model and philosophical defense be completed in one semester, and at what point should the project deliberately stop? Do not include requests for bibliographies, citation lists, or literature reviews.
