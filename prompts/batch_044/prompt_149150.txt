One question presented a hypothetical procedure called reverse-gradient neural replacement, in which a subject's neurons are replaced one by one beginning with the synaptic clusters of highest recent activation frequency and proceeding backward toward the oldest, least-active pathways. Third, the inversion of activation history creates a sharp discontinuity at the midpoint, where the newest patterns suddenly depend on old substrates, so abrupt uploading is actually preferable because it avoids hiding the cutoff inside a gradual process. Therefore, gradual replacement does not solve the identity problem but merely conceals it.
