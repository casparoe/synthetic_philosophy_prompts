I chair the research ethics board of our university's Institute for Synthetic Cognition. During our preliminary session last Tuesday—where we discussed a draft framework for machine-assisted discovery—two members raised a concern that I think should be central to our decision. We are now reviewing a formal proposal to grant co-investigator status, including authorship rights and privileged data-access, to a pipeline that couples evolutionary algorithms with a large language model to generate scientific hypotheses. The principal investigators, one a biologist and one a computer scientist, argue explicitly from Darwin's demonstration that design-level competence can emerge without a comprehending designer, together with Turing's demonstration that problem-solving competence can emerge without semantic comprehension. They claim this licenses treating the hybrid system as a competent but non-comprehending agent: human collaborators would verify empirical predictions but would not be required to interpret the generative reasoning process, since by hypothesis there is none to interpret.

The board is split along lines that mirror the philosophical inversion itself. One faction treats this as a rigorous institutional translation of the Darwin-Turing argument: if competence can be decoupled from comprehension in nature and in computation, then it can be decoupled in scientific practice, and the system should be recognized as a discovery mechanism entitled to credit but exempt from accountability for reasoning. The opposing faction insists that scientific authorship is not a reward for output competence but an ascription of epistemic responsibility; they argue that collapsing the mechanism-agent distinction in this context undermines informed consent protocols, corrupts data-provenance requirements, and creates unassignable liability when predictions fail. Please draft the board's formal recommendation memo—approve with binding conditions, reject, or approve with a modified oversight framework—to be circulated before Thursday's meeting. The memo should specify a concrete course of action with an implementation timeline (for example, particular authorship protocols, audit mechanisms, or sunset clauses), should treat the Darwin-Turing framing as a serious conceptual claim rather than a rhetorical flourish, and should specify exactly what oversight structures would preserve institutional responsibility without requiring the system to possess understanding. It should also engage honestly with the strongest consideration on the losing side: that describing a process as competence without comprehension does not, by itself, establish that the process can bear the normative weight of co-investigator status. Do not include requests for bibliographies or citations; I want the reasoning to stand on its conceptual architecture alone.
