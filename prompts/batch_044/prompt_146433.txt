I'm putting together a workshop for some friends who're doing a philosophy module on freedom, and I've realised that the usual 'Berlin's two concepts' framing is a bit tired — everyone can just recite that back. I want a sorting quiz, like a decision tree, that actually forces you to think about where you stand, rather than just picking the label you like. The domain is liberty: positive, negative, and republican or non-domination liberty, but I want to go deeper into the weeds where these actually come apart in concrete cases, not just in textbook summaries.

So here's what I'm after: build me a taxonomy or flowchart that starts with specific cases and commitments, not with the big names. For example, branch one might ask whether you think liberty is reduced when a benevolent AI nudges you away from harmful choices without you noticing — is that domination, interference, or neither? Another branch could ask about 'adaptive preferences': if someone genuinely doesn't want options they've never had, have they lost positive liberty, or is that not a loss at all? And what about structural conditions — if you can legally leave your job but have zero savings and no social safety net, are you negatively free but positively unfree, or is there a republican sense in which you're still dominated? The leaves should be named positions — include pure negative liberty, some form of strong positive liberty (maybe in the self-mastery or effective-freedom sense), and republican liberty as non-domination — but also flag any combinations that seem unoccupied. For instance, is there anyone who combines republican non-domination with a complete rejection of positive liberty? Or someone who thinks interference is only bad when it's arbitrary, but also denies that freedom requires any actual capacity? If a combination is missing, say briefly whether it's incoherent, just unfashionable, or genuinely unexplored. The questions should be phrased so someone can answer them without already knowing which position the answer points to. I'm aiming for about six to eight branch points, with the audience being people who've read Berlin's essay once and maybe heard of Pettit, but who'd struggle with the finer distinctions unless they're laid out clearly.

Oh, and please don't ask for bibliographies or reading lists — half the group is working offline and wouldn't be able to check citations anyway. Also try not to lean on super obscure theorists nobody's heard of; stick to distinctions that are genuinely part of the debate but just don't usually show up in the intro textbooks. I was discussing this with my flatmate last night and we realised we couldn't even agree on whether 'freedom from interference' and 'freedom from domination' split apart in the case of a kindly but absolute monarch — so maybe use that kind of case as a branch point. Make sure to note any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't seperate, rather than pretending the taxonomy is cleaner than it is.
