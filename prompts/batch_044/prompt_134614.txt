I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of science in earnest, and I have volunteered to conduct a single ninety-minute seminar for a reading circle of fellow retired scientists—mostly engineers and biologists with no formal philosophical training—on Otto Neurath's conception of a universal picture-language (ISOTYPE) and his associated claim that pictorial representations can serve as intersubjectively verifiable protocol statements without recourse to private mental content. I do not want you to deliver the lecture itself; I want a rigorous teaching plan. Specify the exact order in which the conceptual pieces must be introduced, with explicit attention to dependency: for instance, whether the group must first understand Neurath's rejection of introspective protocol sentences before they can see the motivation for a pictorial substitute, or whether the reverse ordering is pedagogically sounder for this audience. Identify the one carrying example that should anchor the entire session—not the familiar livestock-and-milk statistical chart, but a purpose-built illustration drawn from physical measurement, such as representing spatial temperature gradients through contour-like icons that deliberately carry no numerical axis—explain precisely what this example must contain and what it must deliberately omit if it is to illuminate both the power and the limits of picture-language neutrality. Map the misconceptions this audience will predictably import from their scientific-visualization habits—such as the assumption that every diagram is merely a compressed quantitative table, or that visual representation necessarily introduces subjective emotional coloring—and indicate the exact point in the sequence where each misconception should be anticipated and defused. Provide a single closing question that will reveal whether the participants have grasped the philosophical thesis about intersubjective verification rather than merely having learned to appreciate an elegant diagram. Finally, state explicitly what I should refuse to cover at this level—perhaps the later Vienna Circle disputes over physicalism and the unity of science—and justify the omission. The plan should be formal, self-contained, and should not request bibliographies, citations, or external reading lists, as I have no library access for this session.
