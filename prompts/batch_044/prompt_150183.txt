I run a philosophy workshop for about 12 grad students, 90 minutes, on whether self-deception is even conceptually possible. I want them to hit the paradox through doing something, not through me explaining it. Design a game or structured role-play that injects a kind of 'double-ledger' mechanic — each person keeps two running lists, one for reasons they genuinely accept and one for reasons they're only acting on — so they experience the split between deceiver and deceived inside one reasoning session. Give me roles, hidden cards or hidden information, rules, a timeline, and a small-stakes payoff scheme (nothing real, maybe they vote on the snack for the break). The rules should push the group toward a collective decision where individuals have to use arguments they privately reject, and then they have to guess whether they really believed them. Include a debrief that maps each move onto the philosophical problem — transparency of belief, the intentional structure, etc. — and be frank about which feature, probably the first-person feeling of not-noticing, the activity just can't reproduce. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time (someone will definately break the partition or the lists will collapse into one) and how to fix it. Keep it casual — a typo or two is fine — and don't ask for any citations or reading lists.
