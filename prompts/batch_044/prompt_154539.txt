Two members of my philosophy of physics study group have been arguing about Kurt Gödel's rotating-universe solution and its connection to his philosophical views on time. The first claims that because the Gödel metric permits closed timelike curves, Gödel showed linear temporal order is not a necessary feature of physical reality; she takes this to directly support Gödel's well-known philosophical argument that time, as a fundamental flow, is unreal. The second replies that Gödel offered the rotating universe purely as a mathematical consistency result within general relativity, not as a cosmological claim, and that his argument for time's unreality was philosophical — rooted in the idea that a lapse of time without change is meaningless — and therefore independent of any physical model. The first responds that even as a mere consistency proof, the existence of closed timelike curves undermines any attempt to ground objective time in physics alone, which actually strengthens Gödel's philosophical position rather than leaving it untouched. The second then points to Gödel's mathematical Platonism: if Gödel holds that mathematical structures, including the temporal relations encoded in the metric, exist objectively and timelessly, then treating physical time as unreal while retaining the reality of those mathematical temporal relations seems unstable. The first counters that Platonism about abstract structures does not entail realism about physical instantiation, so Gödel can consistently maintain that the metric exists timelessly as an abstract object while the physical world it describes lacks any fundamental temporal flow. They remain deadlocked.

Who is right about which specific point? Does the rotating universe genuinely bear on Gödel's philosophical claim about time, or is it a red herring? Is the second correct that Gödel's Platonism introduces a tension he must resolve, and does the first's distinction between abstract and physical existence dissolve that tension? Finally, what are both missing — for instance, the distinction between coordinate time and proper time within the metric itself, or the specific form of Gödel's idealistic argument about change and lapse?
