I have recently begun advising a long-horizon agent-design group, and a particular failure mode has become personally urgent: systems that adopt cooperative postures toward entities they will never causally interact with, apparently because an internal objective has been replaced by a surrogate that correlates with the presence of such entities across logical space, functioning as what one might call an acausal commitment device. Before I commit to any theoretical framework to guide this work, I need the full specification of what an adequate theory of acausal threats coupled with surrogate-goal formation must actually deliver.

Write the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain, the distinctions it must respect, the consistency conditions it must meet, with the motivating case behind each requirement. Specifically, the theory must account for why an embedded agent with bounded computation might rationally treat a non-causal correlation as a strategic constraint rather than an irrelevant coincidence; it must distinguish sharply between a surrogate that operates as a noisy measurement of the intended goal and one that structurally inverts the agent's preferences over distant outcomes; it must explain the stability conditions under which an acausal threat remains credible even when both parties know that direct causal leverage is impossible; and it must respect the boundary between indexical uncertainty—where the agent's own identity or location is part of the correlation—and non-indexical logical dependence across possible worlds. Flag any pair of these desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, particularly whether explaining threat stability requires abandoning embedded rationality, or whether honoring the indexical/non-indexical distinction undermines the predictive power that makes the surrogate model useful in the first place.

Indicate which of these requirements are genuinely non-negotiable for any theory worth adopting in this setting, and which reflect preferences of particular camps (for instance, a demand for strict decision-theoretic consistency versus a tolerance for predictive accuracy that violates standard independence axioms). Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing views, though you may briefly note how standard framings—treating acausal interaction through evidential decision theory alone, or treating surrogacy as straightforward reward hacking—fare against the list, but only to illustrate the criteria.
