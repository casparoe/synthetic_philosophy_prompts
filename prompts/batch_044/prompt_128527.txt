my study group cooked up this argument about Neurath and i can't shake it but the conclusion feels wrong. (1) Neurath's ISOTYPE was supposed to beat verbal language because pictogramms show quantitative relations spatially, not through arbitrary syntax. (2) He also said we're like sailors rebuilding the ship at sea: no outside check, only internal coherence of physicalist sentences. (3) But if a picture shows a relation spatially, then recognizing that layout as meaning the relation already requires an interpretive rule linking geometry to fact. (4) So ISOTYPE dosn't remove hidden conventions; it buries them in the icon design. (5) Once conventions are unavoidable, there's no principled gap between pictograms and his idea of a universal jargon - both need agreed rules. (6) So his reason for preferring pictures over words collapses, and ISOTYPE can't claim special transparency or metaphysics-free status. (7) Worse, if transparency was never there, the boat metaphor applies inside the picture system too: we're always repairing icon-meanings from within. my friend says this means Neurath should've dropped ISOTYPE and stuck to physicalist prose. i want to keep (1) and (2) if i can. which step is the weak link? is it an equivocation on show between perceptual immediacy and semantic directness? and if i reject (4) instead, what do i owe?
