Here's an argument I built for my moral revolutions seminar and now find suspicious because it makes progress look like fashion. (1) Appiah says dueling collapsed because it became embarasing, not because anyone won the argument — honor turned against it. (2) Williams adds that norms sometimes die by becoming unthinkable rather than being refuted. (3) If that's right, then a norm's survival is just a function of the social cost of violating it — call it the shame-price. (4) So moral change is just a shift in shame-prices, not a discovery of what's actually right or wrong. (5) Apply that consistently: slavery ended not because it was shown to violate human dignity, but because defending it got too expensive socially. (6) But then abolition is rationally on par with, say, the decline of powdered wigs — both are just shame-price shifts. (7) I don't want to say abolition was just fashion; I think it was genuinely progressive. So which step do I reject? If I keep (3), do I have to pay the price of saying moral progress is arbitrary?
