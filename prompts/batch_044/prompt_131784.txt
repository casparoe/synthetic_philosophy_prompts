I have been thinking on my own about the feeling that there is a little "me" inside my head sitting in front of a single screen where all experience comes together, and I read that philosophers call this idea the Cartesian theater. I also came across something called the multiple drafts model, which seems to reject both the single screen and the central watcher. Could you write a deadpan, pretentious theater review—about 600 words, in the voice of a high-end arts magazine critic—reviewing a fictional revival of the play The Cartesian Theater? The satire should come from taking the actual philosophical objections seriously: mock the homunculus fallacy, the assumption that consciousness needs a central observer seated at a specific point in the brain, and the notion that there is one unified stream of experience with a definite temporal location. Have the critic mention, in passing, that the rival repertory company next door is staging the Multiple Drafts production—no projection booth, no front-row seat for the self, and several unfinished versions of the same scene running in parallel without a master copy. Please invent one theatrical detail that accidentally exposes the Cartesian flaw, such as a program note stating that the Inner Eye is seated in Row Zero but Row Zero does not appear on the seating chart, or a curtain call in which every audience member must bow to themselves. The critic should stay deadpan and never wink. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or verbatim quotations from any philosopher; just write the review as if describing real performances.
