As a journalist covering science and ideas, I am preparing a feature on whether interpretability research is uncovering real cognitive structures inside neural networks or merely projecting human categories onto statistical patterns. Before I interview proponents of representational realism and their critics, I want the evaluation framework established on its own terms. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of representation in neural networks must satisfy to be taken seriously: the empirical phenomena it must explain—including adversarial vulnerability, linear concept encoding, and the superposition of unrelated features within single dimensions, where one neuron or direction participates in multiple concept representations in different contexts—the conceptual distinctions it must preserve (for instance, between a feature being encoded and being causally exploited by the network, or between localist and fully distributed storage), the consistency conditions with known training dynamics and architecture that it cannot violate, and the puzzles it must dissolve rather than dismiss. For each desideratum, provide the concrete case or experimental finding that motivates it. Then flag which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable for any realist account and which are camp-specific preferences, such as demanding human-legible representations versus allowing structurally coherent but opaque encodings. Crucially, examine whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable—for example, whether requiring both strict modular locality and complete causal efficacy in deep residual networks creates a tension analogous to a no-go result for certain realist programs. End with a brief scorecard for the three main positions—strong distributed realism, the mechanistic interpretability account centered on causal roles, and the instrumentalist critique that treats networks as stochastic pattern matchers—but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating the question.
