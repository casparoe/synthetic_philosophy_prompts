Design a final exam essay question for an upper-level philosophy of mind and metaphysics seminar built around the following hypothetical framework. The careenium is a continuous, non-symbolic substrate governed by local physical dynamics. Simmballs are discrete, structured symbolic tokens instantiated within the careenium; they encode relational properties but, on the standard interpretation, leave the substrate's fundamental dynamics unchanged. The push-back thesis contends that the relational structure of simmballs can causally influence the local topology of the careenium—not merely through the physical properties of their instantiation, but through their semantic content.

Write one essay question that asks students to evaluate whether simmballs genuinely push the careenium. The question must be unanswerable by reciting summaries of the symbol-grounding problem, mental causation, or downward-causation literature. Instead, it should compel students to distinguish precisely among: (a) causal interaction occurring only at the level of physical instantiation; (b) genuine semantic downward causation, in which the content of a symbol exerts force on the substrate; and (c) the appearance-generation hypothesis, according to which the careenium's own dynamics produce behaviors that merely look like symbolic push-back. The question should require students to assess whether the push-back thesis is coherent, whether it collapses into (a) or (c), and what would have to be true about the careenium for (b) to hold.

Provide a model answer of approximately 600 words that takes a clear, argued position and works through all three distinctions. Then provide a marking scheme totaling 20 marks that separates an A answer from a B answer by listing the specific argumentative moves required for full credit—such as correctly isolating instantiation-level interaction from semantic causation, identifying a specific tension in the push-back thesis, and addressing the appearance-generation objection—together with the marks assigned to each move and a one-line note on the typical error that costs marks in each case. The exam should be fully self-contained: do not ask for bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific texts.
