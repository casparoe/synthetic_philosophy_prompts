You serve on the Clinical Allocation Review Board of the Meridian Health System. Next month the Board must vote on whether to adopt the proposed Prioritarian Aggregate Protocol for distributing a limited quarterly supply of a one-time curative gene therapy for hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis. The protocol applies a social welfare function that weights quality-adjusted life years by each patient’s baseline life-expectancy deficit—giving greater priority to younger, worse-off patients—then selects the recipient combination that maximizes the weighted aggregate sum. A dissenting clinical ethicist has raised the separateness-of-persons objection: she argues that even prioritarian weighting treats welfare gains across distinct individuals as fungible inputs into a single calculus, so that denying treatment to Patient X (projected 55 QALYs) in order to treat Patients Y and Z (projected 30 and 28 weighted QALYs, respectively) violates the moral boundary between persons regardless of how the weights are distributed. She proposes instead a “person-level threshold” rule—treat every patient whose individual projected gain exceeds 40 QALYs, then allocate any remaining doses by clinical urgency, never trading one person’s substantial gain for the summed smaller gains of others.

Please draft the formal opinion (no more than 800 words) you will circulate before the vote. Recommend a concrete course of action—adopt the protocol with a specified amendment, reject it in favor of the threshold rule, or adopt a hybrid with a defined trigger condition—and include a clear implementation timeframe, such as a one-quarter pilot with mandatory review. Your reasoning should engage the separateness objection seriously: specifically address whether prioritarian aggregation genuinely resolves the concern about interpersonal fungibility or merely redistributes it, and present the strongest argument for the position you reject. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
