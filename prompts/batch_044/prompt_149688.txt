I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of science, currently writing on epistemic practices in computational research environments. I have noticed a recurring pattern in how researchers interact with generative reasoning tools, and I am unsure whether it corresponds to an established concept or whether it requires a new analytical category.

The pattern is as follows. A researcher employs an AI system not as an authority to be deferred to, but as a scaffold for intermediate reasoning. They request discrete steps—lemmas, distinctions, analogies—and evaluate each one independently for coherence and plausibility. They can defend every local component if challenged, and they genuinely understand each link in isolation. Nevertheless, they report an inability to reconstruct the overall argumentative trajectory without returning to the system. The difficulty is not that any individual step is obscure; rather, the specific path through the conceptual landscape—the selection of which connections are salient, which detours are necessary, and why this route was taken rather than an equally valid alternative—seems to have been shaped by an optimization process that the researcher can observe but not fully internalize. The result is a form of comprehension that is locally intact but globally navigational: the researcher knows the pieces and trusts the assembly, yet lacks the grasp of why this particular architecture was the right one.

I have observed analogues in mathematical proof verification aided by algorithmic suggestion, in legal reasoning supported by predictive modeling, and in clinical diagnosis systems that propose intermediate inferences. The phenomenon appears distinct from automation bias, since the researcher does not treat the system's outputs as authoritative; it also differs from standard divisions of cognitive labor, because the split is not between specialists or functional modules but between local intelligibility and global topographical understanding.

My question is whether this configuration already has a name in the literature on extended cognition, epistemic dependence, or scientific understanding. If not, should we classify it as a variant of distributed cognition involving artifacts, as a specific epistemic vulnerability introduced by generative systems, or as a loss of a distinct good—perhaps something like navigational grasp rather than propositional knowledge? I want to understand whether the erosion here is best analyzed as a deficit in understanding, in justification, or in an altogether separate epistemic capacity.
