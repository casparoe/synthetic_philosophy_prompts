I'm working on a handout for a philosophy of science reading grup and I'm tired of the standard 'do you think science is cumulative?' quiz. I want a decision tree that sorts people by what they actually think about incommensurability, using concrete cases rather than philosopher names.

Start with this scenario: two labs in 1895 study heat in sealed vessels. One describes the outcome as caloric fluid being displaced; the other says mean kinetic energy of particles rose. They agree on the temperature reading but disagree on what it means. First branch: are they talking past each other entirely (no shared observational language) or just giving different theoritical glosses on the same measurement? Then branch on whether a neutral observer could in principle decide between the frameworks using shared rules, whether the choice is ultimately pragmatic or conventional, and whether the older framework can be reconstructed as an approximaton inside the newer one. The leaves should be named positions — strong incommensurability, local incommensurability (Kuhn's later view), semantic holism without incommensurability, convergent realism, instrumental pluralism — plus any unnamed combinations. For unnamed ones, write one sentence on why the space might be empty (incoherent, unstable, unexplored).

Please phrase every node as a question someone could answer without knowing which philosopher it maps to. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't cleanly separate — for instance if Quine-style holism and moderate Kuhnianism collapse onto the same answers — and say why. This is for grad students, so don't ask for citations or reading lists; just build the tree from the logical space and then drop the names in at the ends.
