I am preparing a piece on the identity of indiscernibles for a science-and-ideas audience. Please construct a thought experiment that updates Black's spheres with the following injection: place two perfectly identical spheres inside an infinite, boundary-less crystalline lattice with no external reference frame and no other macroscopic objects. The spheres share every intrinsic property and every direct relational property to one another.

Your specifications: (a) a structural realist must be able to claim the spheres are discernible via their respective embedded lattice-positions; (b) a strict Leibnizian must deny that lattice-position counts as a genuine property and therefore treat the spheres as genuinely indiscernible; (c) the physics must remain ordinary—no quantum entanglement, no exotic topology, and the whole scenario must fit in one paragraph.

Please respond in these sections, in this order:

The Scenario (~120 words): present the lattice-embedded case in a single paragraph.

The Verdicts (~150 words): state what the structural realist and the Leibnizian each conclude, with the core reasoning for each.

The Engine (~100 words): identify the precise feature of the lattice setup that generates the philosophical disagreement.

The Counterattack (~100 words): show how the Leibnizian can resist the structural-realist reading without simply assuming the conclusion.

The Collapse Check (~80 words): explain what happens if the lattice is replaced by continuous empty space—does the argument revert to the original two-spheres case, and why?

Avoid bibliographies, citations, or appeals to texts I should consult; I need the analysis to stand entirely on its own reasoning.
