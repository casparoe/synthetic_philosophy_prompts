Rank the following five strategies for blocking Nozick's utility-monster objection—while preserving some form of aggregation—by philosophical coherence, assigning each a score from 1 (least defensible) to 5 (most defensible): (A) a steeply concave individual utility function with a hard per-agent ceiling; (B) average-utilitarian aggregation; (C) a lexical priority rule that protects basic interests regardless of aggregate surplus; (D) a contractualist restriction of moral standing to agents capable of reciprocal justification; and (E) an anti-hedonist revision that treats extreme, non-integrated pleasure as a normative "system error" lacking weight in the calculus. For each item, state in one sentence whether its central flaw is internal inconsistency, ad hoc stipulation, or a tacit abandonment of the utilitarian core.
