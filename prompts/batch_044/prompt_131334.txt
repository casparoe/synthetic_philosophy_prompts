I'm preparing material for a graduate seminar on Madhyamaka metaphysics, and I want to clarify a conceptual tension that standard expositions tend to smooth over. We have been examining Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi—the four-cornered negation of intrinsic nature (svabhāva)—and the familiar move of applying emptiness (śūnyatā) to emptiness itself, so that emptiness is not reified as a metaphysical absolute. Most commentarial treatments present this reflexive application as a straightforward therapeutic dissolution: once you see that emptiness is empty, you avoid falling into the fourth corner (the "neither both nor neither" position) as a hidden ontological claim. But I think there is a deeper logical instability here that is rarely addressed directly.

The specific conceptual issue is this: if the fourth corner is meant to block the reification of absence into presence, then applying that same blocking move to emptiness itself seems to require that emptiness lack intrinsic nature in a way that is itself not intrinsic. This generates a reflexive loop—emptiness is empty, but the statement that emptiness is empty must itself be emptied, and so on. Does this loop collapse the tetralemma into a mere rhetorical ladder, or does it indicate that Madhyamaka requires a non-classical logic of predication in which the law of excluded middle fails not only for conventional entities but for the ultimate truth as well? In particular, I am interested in whether the reflexive move necessarily opens a hidden "fifth corner"—a position that is neither within the fourfold scheme nor outside it—which would undermine Nāgārjuna's claim to have exhausted all metaphysical positions.

Please explain this conceptual knot. I want you to work through the logical structure of the reflexive application rather than rehearsing the standard commentary tradition, and specifically address whether the instability points toward a paraconsistent or relevance-logical reading of the tetralemma, or whether it can be resolved within classical logic by treating the four corners as pragmatically ordered stages rather than jointly exhaustive ontological categories. Your explanation should be intelligible to someone who knows the basic framework but has not worked through the reflexive problem in detail.
