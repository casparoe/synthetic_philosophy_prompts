There's a comparison that comes up constantly in discussions of Dennett's cranes versus skyhooks, especially when people try to explain why greedy reductionism fails. The analogy runs like this: explaining consciousness or culture is like explaining a running software application. The atoms and neurons are the silicon and transistors; the cognitive rules are the machine code; psychology and sociology are the source code and design documentation; and the actual behavior of the system is the running program. People deploy this in two opposite directions. Anti-reductionists say that just as inspecting every transistor won't tell you what Photoshop does — you need the intermediate layers of assembly, compiler IR, and source architecture — so physics alone won't explain mind, and skipping the middle cranes is a mistake. Defenders of greedy reductionism reply that the source code fully determines the behavior, there are no skyhooks in the compiler, and the intermediate representations are just compressed descriptions of the gate-level state, so nothing is lost by reducing all the way down.

I want to audit this analogy, but with a specific twist that I think exposes its hidden load-bearing parts. In real compiler stacks, there are genuine intermediate representations — LLVM IR, SSA form, register-allocation graphs — that have their own ontology, causal patterns, and explanatory autonomy. They are not merely convenient summaries of either the high-level Python or the bare silicon; optimization happens at these layers, bugs manifest there, and you can genuinely explain performance without referencing gates or source syntax. So the analogy might be smuggling in an engineered, layered architecture that biological evolution may not replicate.
