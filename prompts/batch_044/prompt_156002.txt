I've been reflecting on Parfit's hitchhiker thought experiment, specifically the claim that it can be rational to cultivate a disposition to do what is not rational in the particular case—paying the driver after being dropped off, even though at that moment the agent has no reason to pay. Doesn't this imply that rational irrationality is not a deep feature of practical reason but a contingent problem of imperfect commitment technology? Once we can bind future selves perfectly, shouldn't rational agents always prefer binding mechanisms over cultivating apparently irrational dispositions?
