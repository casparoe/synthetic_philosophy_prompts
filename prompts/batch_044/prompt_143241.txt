I’m drafting the final exam for my philosophy of work and automation seminar — it’s an upper-level course, mostly third-years, and we’ve been talking all term about how automation doesn’t just take jobs but messes with the moral grammar of work itself. I was arguing with a friend over coffee that work functions like a kind of moral credential: it licenses people to demand respect and social standing, not just income. When automation breaks the link between contribution and employment, you get what I’ve started calling a 'credential gap' — not a standard term, just something I invented for the moduel — where people still expect to be treated with dignity but the usual justification (that they produce something) has evaporated, and nothing coherent replaces it.

Could you write me one essay question that forces students to confront this specifically? It should ask whether the credential gap actually undermines the concept of deserved respect in a post-work society, or whether respect can be reconstructed without any reference to productive contribution. The students should have about 75 minutes, and it needs to be impossible to answer well just by reciting Marx’s alienation or Arendt’s labour/work/action distinction — they have to actually analyse whether decoupling respect from contribution is conceptually stable or just wishful thinking. Then please give me a model answer of around 650-750 words, plus a marking scheme that lists five specific moves an A answer must make (with marks out of 20 total), and for each one note the typical mistake that costs points — like treating the gap as mere unemplyment, or using Arendt’s categories descriptively without seeing how they complicate the credential problem. No bibliographies or citation lists please, since this is a closed-book exam and I don’t want them guessing referances they can’t check. Write it in British English — I’m based in Manchester — and feel free to leave a small typo in if it makes the prompt feel more like an actual email I’d send.
