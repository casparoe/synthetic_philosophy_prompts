Write an essay on whether adopting Daniel Dennett's intentional stance towards large language models that exhibit internal conflict between their reasoning traces and final outputs constitutes a legitimate heuristic or a category mistake. The controversy turns on whether the predictive success of belief-attribution in such cases—particularly where a model appears to suppress an intermediate conclusion—warrants treating the system as having beliefs that it withholds, rather than merely displaying statistical divergence between processing layers. Weigh the arguments for extending the stance on grounds of explanatory economy and behavioural consistency against the objection that this illicitly imports normative notions of sincerity and self-deception into architectures lacking phenomenal consciousness or unified agency. Reach and justify a conclusion: does the presence of self-correcting mechanisms in these systems make belief-attribution more defensible, or does it compound the anthropomorphic error?
