I just started dating someone who works in climate science and now I'm suddenly in way over my head arguing about model selection at dinner partys, lol. There's this pattern I keep seeing in how people defend big theoretical frameworks - not just climate, but everywhere - and I can't tell if it's a recognized problem or just my confusion. Here's the thing: a theory makes a surprising prediction, it comes true, and people say "see, best explanation!" Then later, when it makes a wrong prediction, they don't drop the theory; they invoke the same explanatory mechinsm that made the first success work, but now turned around to explain the failure. So the machinery that was evidence FOR the theory becomes its failure-absorber. It's not exactly confirmation bias, because they're not ignoring the failure - they're actively explaining it with the core tools of the theory. And it's not just tacking on an auxiliary hypothesis either, since the excuse comes from inside the framework, not bolted on from outside. I feel like there should be a term for when a theory's own explanatory successes turn into its shield against counterevidence, but I don't know it. Is this just inference to the best explanation running backwards, or does it have its own name? And am I being unfair when I say this makes the theory practically unfalsifiable even if it's technically falsifiable?
