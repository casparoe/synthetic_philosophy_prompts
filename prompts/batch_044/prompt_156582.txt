I serve as senior counsel for a constitutional-design office that reviews proposed self-amending provisions—clauses containing the rules for their own alteration—before they reach ratification. Write a reusable decision procedure my team can apply in under thirty minutes per proposal: an ordered checklist with explicit stop conditions, grounded in the conceptual distinction between procedural self-reference (rules governing how amendment occurs) and substantive self-reference (rules that directly alter their own normative content), and incorporating the specific concern—drawn from analyses of nomic legal self-reference—that a provision can generate a “delegated repeal paradox” when it grants an authority the power to destroy the very grant that empowers it. For each step, specify the exact question to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure issues a hard rejection, where it must hand the decision to a value judgment rather than a conceptual determination, and where it must remain silent. Apply the procedure to three proposals: (1) a constitutional clause that sets its own amendment threshold by referring directly back to itself; (2) a statutory rule that authorizes a regulatory agency to suspend the rule’s own enforcement provisions; and (3) a clause that delegates to a future legislature the authority to abolish the delegation mechanism itself—this third case should be the one where the procedure returns an answer the office will dislike. Then state explicitly what the procedure cannot determine, especially regarding democratic legitimacy versus logical coherence, and flag which parts of the analysis depend on genuinely contested interpretations of legal authority rather than on settled logical results. Separate what is conceptually settled from what remains genuinely contested among scholars of legal self-reference, and do not present any contested view as the consensus. Begin with the bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
