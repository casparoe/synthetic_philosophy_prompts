grading this for my phil/econ course, need point by point assesment with a grade. exam question was: Marx distinguishes formal and real subsumption in Capital. How does real subsumption relate to the four dimensions of alienation he lists in the 1844 Manuscripts? Are they describing the same transformation of labor, or does real subsumption add something the alienation framework misses?

student answer:
Real subsumption is when capital actually reshapes the labor process itself—machinery, pace, division of labor—not just takes the surplus. That lines up pretty closely with alienation from the act of production in the 1844 text: both are about the worker losing control over how he works. Formal subsumption is just owning the factory while leaving old craft methods alone, so you get surplus extraction but not real alienation yet, since the worker still directs his own labor. The four dimensions—product, process, species-being, others—map onto real subsumption cleanly: once capital redesigns production, all four kick in at once. Marx doesnt drop species-being in Capital, he just translates it into technical language about the worker being an appendage of the machine. Also, exploitation only becomes fully visible under real subsumption, because under formal subsumption the worker still produces value in a human way and keeps some dignity, so the extraction isnt truly alienating. Marx rejects Proudhons claim that property is theft, but he still thinks the worker has a moral right to the full value he creates, which is why real subsumption is the deeper wrong—its where that right gets systematically violated.
