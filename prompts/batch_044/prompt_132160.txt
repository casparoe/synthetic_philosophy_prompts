I'm stuck on a passage in Michelet's Le Peuple where he claims the people never appear in royal chronicals or state papers — they only show up in sudden collective ruptures, like footprints in mud after a storm, leaving no individual names but a collective trace. He says if you want history from below you study these breaks, not continuous archives.

But I'm not sure if this is really an ancestor of microhistory or just romantic crowd history. Ginzburg and the microhistorians seem to work the opposite way: they grab one weird fragment — a miller, a cheese trial — and reconstruct agency from archival silence. Michelet wants the mob in motion, not the single fragment. So is he actually closer to elite history, just replacing kings with masses?

There's also this concept called prosopographic silence — that archives erase non-elite lives structually, not randomly. Does Michelet's point anticipate that, or is he just complaining that common people don't write memiors?

Can anyone help me parse what he's actually claiming about evidence and collective agency? I'm not asking for a bibliography, just whether I'm misreading the leap from missing records to the presence of a collective will.
