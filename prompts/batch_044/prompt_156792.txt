Read Shakespeare's Twelfth Night through the lens of gender as a socially constructed kind, but with a specific conceptual distinction in view: gender understood as a collective status function that depends on institutional recognition (as in Searle's account of social ontology) versus gender understood as an individual performative repertoire that can be adopted or discarded (as in Butler's account). Viola's disguise as Cesario allows her access to spaces and relationships closed to women, yet the plot's comic tension depends on whether other characters accept her assigned status despite visible dissonance. Examine the scenes in which Olivia pursues Cesario and Orsino confides in him: do these moments suggest that gender is a collective institution that can be temporarily entered when recognition is granted, or that it is an individual performance that succeeds regardless of institutional context? Does the play's resolution—Viola revealed and paired with Orsino—restore an institutional order that was merely suspended, or does it expose gender categories as arbitrary costumes? I am preparing this for a philosophy of gender discussion and want a reading that does not reduce the play to an illustration of either view, that notes where the work resists the framework, and that says what the play adds to the philosophical debate about social kinds that pure argument does not capture.
