Strawson distinguishes reactive attitudes — like resentment, gratitude, and indignation — from the objective attitude. Reactive attitudes are participant stances where we care about another person's intentions and will. The objective attitude is when we view someone as an object of management or treatment, like we do with very young children or people with severe mental illness. The psychologist is in the objective attitude because she explains the violence causally through trauma rather than blaming the inmate's will. Strawson argues determinism doesn't undermine reactive attitudes since they don't depend on libertarian free will. But in institutional settings, professionals should permanently adopt the objective attitude because it's more humane and practical. So yes, she should stay in that stance, and Strawson's framework supports the institutional exemptoin for roles like hers.
