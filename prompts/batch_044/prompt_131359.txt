Write a dialogue between a relationalist about practical reasons and a dispositional realist about "latent rationales," set in the context of whether normative reasons can exist when no reasoners exist. The relationalist should defend the view that a reason is necessarily a relation between an agent's practical capacities and a possible outcome: without an agent, there are only causal regularities, not reasons. The dispositional realist should defend a niche but precisely drawn position—latent rationales are normative properties that inhere in structural configurations (for instance, optimal paths through state spaces) and that become reasons for an agent upon encounter, yet existed beforehand as rational potentials rather than as mere descriptions, analogous in form but not in kind to how a key's shape exists prior to meeting its lock. The dialogue must contain genuine back-and-forth: the relationalist should press the realist on whether "latent rationales" are distinguishable from physical dispositions except by stipulation, and should ask what work the normative vocabulary does if no agent is present to be guided; the realist should reply by distinguishing causal dispositions (what will occur) from rational dispositions (what ought to occur given a counterfactual agent) and should argue that the difference is tracked by modal structure rather than by semantic decoration. Both interlocutors should be equally competent—no strawman. Include a concrete test case: a universe containing only a single decaying proton with no observers and no future observers, and ask whether there is any reason—not merely a cause—for the decay to unfold one way rather than another. The dialogue should end with the disagreement intact but precisely located: the relationalist denies that "ought" can float free of any "is-an-agent" relation, while the realist insists that rational structure is part of the furniture of possibility spaces regardless of occupancy. Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or named texts; build the positions from the conceptual distinctions given above.
