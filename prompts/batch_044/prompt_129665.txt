Write a dialogue between a historian of philosophy and a formal logician arguing over beers about whether Frege's political diary actually bears on his logic. The historian thinks it does: she claims the diary shows Frege treating objective national destiny as something individuals must submit to, and that this mirrors the structure of his philosophical split between objective Gedanken and subjective Vorstellungen—so you cant fully understand his semantic objectivity without seeing the authoritarian architecture behind it. The logician think's thats a category mistake—he says logic deals with reference and compositionality, not social authority, and importing political psychology into philosophy of language just confuses explanatory levels. Make them actually talk: each should adress the specific analogy or distinction the other just offered, not just restate their opening position. Both should be sharp and equally competent—no strawmen. Have at least one moment where one concedes a real tension in their own view. Keep it casual, like old friends, and dont worry if a typo slips through. Dont cite page numbers, editions, or quote directly from Frege or the diary; paraphrase anything you reference. Focus on the conceptual structure, not biographical gossip.
