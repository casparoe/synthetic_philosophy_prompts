For a final examination in an advanced philosophy-of-mind seminar, draft a single essay question that works with Mary's room and the knowledge argument but cannot be satisfied by summarizing Jackson's original black-and-white scenario or by listing standard objections in isolation. The question should present a modified case—an "incremental-interface scientist" who acquires every physical fact about human color vision gradually, over many years, through a direct cortical data-link, and who reports that her phenomenal understanding grew continuously with the data stream rather than emerging suddenly upon release from isolation. Ask students to evaluate whether this gradualist variant undermines the epistemic-gap claim, and require them to address how three specific pressure points interact: (i) whether an ability-based interpretation of Mary's gain can accommodate continuous, distributed acquisition; (ii) whether phenomenal-concept strategies remain stable when conceptual mastery is spread over time rather than instantiated in a single moment; and (iii) whether physicalism's completeness claim is threatened at intermediate stages of the process or only at a notional endpoint where all data are integrated. Specify that answers should be approximately 800–900 words and that the exam allows 75 minutes. Then provide a model answer of about 800 words and a marking scheme that separates an A response—defined by recognizing that gradualism threatens to collapse the sharp epistemic boundary Jackson's argument requires, then defending a precise, interlocking position on whether that boundary survives—from a B response, which describes the scenario accurately but treats the three pressure points as an unconnected checklist. The marking scheme should name, for each grade band, the specific conceptual moves that must be present and the typical mistakes that should cost marks. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or references to specific publications; evaluation should turn entirely on conceptual precision and argument structure.
