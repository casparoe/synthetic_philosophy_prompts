A colleague in my philosophy of mind seminar summarized Dennett’s framework like this: “The physical, design, and intentional stances are just predictive strategies, but only the physical stance describes what literally exists. The design stance treats organisms as functionally organized, and the intentional stance treats them as rational agents with beliefs and desires, yet neither tracks real patterns in Dennett’s technical sense; they are merely useful heuristics that save computation. Dennett calls mental states ‘real patterns’ only loosely, meaning they compress behavior efficiently, but if one had the full physical dynamics, the intentional level would vanish without residue. So his view is essentially eliminativist: beliefs and desires do not exist, though attributing them is practically unavoidable. Moreover, because the intentional stance applies whenever a system’s behavior is best predicted by rationality assumptions, a sufficiently complex thermostat or language model could trigger it, which shows that the stance cannot distinguish genuine agents from artifacts that merely mimic rationality.” Is this a fair rendering?
