I sit on my university's Joint Committee on Curriculum and Historical Accountability, and we must vote Thursday on a proposed required history module, "Imperial Ledger," which asks students to apply a quantitative "balance-sheet" method—tallying railways, legal codes, and literacy gains against extracted revenue, famine mortality, and deindustrialization—to assess the British Raj, explicitly engaging Niall Ferguson's modernizing-defense thesis against Shashi Tharoor's structural-plunder indictment. Critics on the committee argue that the ledger format falsely universalizes imperial categories, masking that the metrics themselves were designed by the colonizer; defenders reply that without such a framework, evaluation collapses into incommensurable narrative relativism. Draft the committee's written opinion recommending approval, rejection, or approval with concrete conditions, including reasoning the dissenting side could actually respond to, a direct engagement with whether "balancing" can ever be neutral when the scales are inherited, and a clear time-bound implementation step should the recommendation be adopted.
