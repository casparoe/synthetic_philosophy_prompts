I hold what I definatly call the 'dispersed null' view: if phenomenal consciousness needs a specific kind of integrated information architecture that insects almost certainly lack, then multiplying a near-zero individual probability by ten quintillion bugs doesn't build moral weight — it just smears it thinner across uncorrellated nodes. So the scale argument fails, not because the numbers are wrong, but because aggregation assumes correlation where there is none.

What would actually count against this? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward: maybe empirical results showing convergent evolution of that architecture in arthropods, maybe a conceptual argument that moral weight aggregates non-linearly even with independence, maybe a hidden correlation in sentience probabilities across phylogeny I haven't considered. Also, has this kind of dismissal been qualified into unfalsifiability — like if I keep lowering the architecture bar whenever data comes in — and if so, is that a real problem for a view like mine? Be straight with me: what should move a retired ecologist who thinks he's being quantitative but might just be rationalizing indifference?
