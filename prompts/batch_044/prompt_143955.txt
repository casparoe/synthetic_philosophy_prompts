I’m putting together a Q&A for my podcast — nothing fancy, just me and a guest in a room with bad microphones — and next week I’m talking to this classics scholar who just wrote a book with a kind of wild take on Plato. Instead of the usual line that Plato hates drunkenness because it wrecks the rational part, she argues that in the Laws Plato actually treats regulated intoxication like a civic stress-test: the city lets people get drunk under supervision so it can see whose reason collapses and who stays in command. She calls it diagnostic symposia or something, and she thinks the Republic and the Symposium aren’t contradictory on this, they’re just testing different populations — philosophers versus citizens.

I want maybe eight or nine questions that don’t just ask her to summarize the thesis. I need at least one that presses her on how this reading deals with the Laws own warning that wine makes people behave like wild beasts — if it’s a diagnostic test, why all the animal imagery? Another should ask about the symposiarch: if the point is civic diagnosis, why does Plato care so much about the host controlling the drinking pace rather than just recording results? And I definatly want a follow-up for each of these keyed to her likely reply — like if she says the beast-language is just metaphor for pre-rational desire, then push her on whether that collapses her view back into the standard moralist reading she claims to reject.

Also can you include one question about the Symposium? She apparently thinks Socrates staying sober while Alcibiades and the others collapse is the hidden model for the ideal symposiarch — not just self-control, but a kind of observational authority. I want a question about whether that makes Socrates into a state survellor rather than a philosopher, with a follow-up for whatever she’d probably say about philosophical eros being different from civic oversight. No generic what-drew-you-to-Plato stuff — I want the sequence to actually corner her thesis, not let her deflect with research-program-style answers. Maybe end with one question asking what textual evidence could actually disprove her reading, since she seems pretty convinced.
