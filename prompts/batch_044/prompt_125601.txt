I would like to convene a 90-minute workshop in a seminar room with movable furniture, but I do not want to lecture them on the regress; I want them to inhabit it. Design a structured in-class game or deliberation with explicit rules, roles, and symbolic stakes in which the participants discover that licensing an inference seems to require licensing the licence itself, before anyone names Carroll's paradox.

Divide the group into Proponents, who must defend a chain of reasoning about a concrete normative claim (for example, whether a particular institutional obligation entails a specific action); Auditors, who may approve a step only if presented with an explicit warrant card; and a Registry, which records whether each warrant card has itself been warranted. Arrange the rules and time pressure so that the group feels a coordination failure arising not from disagreement over premises but from an accumulating deficit of unlicensed licences, pressing them either to halt or to invoke an unexamined premise precisely at the point where Carroll's Tortoise challenges Achilles. Tell me also what will predictably go wrong on the first run: which role tends to dominate, which misreading of the rules shortcuts the regress, and how I should correct the course without simply explaining the paradox away.
