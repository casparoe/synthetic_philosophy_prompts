I'm speaking next tuesay at a small philosophy club I joined — mostly metaphysics grad students and one physicist who thinks philosophy is just word games. My view is that if two theories predict every observable result identically, but one sneaks in extra "shadow nodes" in spacetime that can never causally interract with anything we measure, then they don't describe two different ontologies — they describe one, and the shadow nodes aren't real because we can't identify them. I call this the accessibility rule: no causal access, no ontological identification.

What objections will actually come up? The metaphysics crowd will probably mention structural realism, and the physicist will ask why it matters if there's no empirical difference. I need the objections in roughly the order they'll hit, which ones have real teeth, what I should concede upfront so I don't look stubborn, and the one reply that absolutely has to land. I'm hoping nobody asks about quantum entanglement as an example of inaccessible correlation, but if they do I'm not sure I've got an answer.
