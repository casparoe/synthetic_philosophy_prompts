Here's an argument I sketched for a tutoral and now it feels like it eats itself. (1) Republican liberty is non-domination: you're unfree when someone can interfere arbitrarily in your choices, even if they actually don't. (2) A power is arbitrary if it isn't tracked by the interests or voice of those affected — not just if it's actually abused. (3) Take zero-hours contracts with algorithimic shift-allocation: the firm can change forecasting weights, cancel shifts, or alter terms, and the worker has no say in the algorithm's design or outputs. (4) Even in a good week with full shifts, the worker lives under that discretionary capacity. (5) So by (1) they're dominated, hence unfree. (6) The same structure holds for landlords with discretionary eviction powers, banks with recall clauses, platforms that can change TOS — basically every private contract where one party holds unilateral adjustment rights. (7) So republican freedom condemns ordinary market relations as systematically unfree. (8) The usual republican defence is that power becomes non-arbitrary when constrained by law or democratic oversight. (9) But private markets lack democratic oversight; contract law only sets boundaries, it doesn't track workers' interests in the republican sense. (10) So either republican freedom is just negative liberty plus state democracy — not a distinct concept — or it demands replacing markets with fully co-determined institutions. (11) That second horn sounds like it's collapsed into positive liberty: the actual collective capacity to determine outcomes. (12) So republican freedom either proves too much about markets or it's not genuinely a third kind of liberty at all.

I want to hang on to (1) and the idea that private power can dominate, but I don't want to end up saying markets are all illegitimate or that republican freedom is fake. Which step should I give up, and what's the price? Also, is the slide between (6) and (7) doing more work than it looks like? Write the answer in British English please, and don't bother with citations — I just want the conceptual diagnosis.
