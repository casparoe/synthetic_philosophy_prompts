I am a doctoral student in philosophy working on normative decision theory and the rhetoric of rationalist discourse. For a graduate seminar exercise in pedagogical satire, please compose a deadpan visitor's handbook entry—approximately 600 words, in the manner of an official state tourism brochure written with the cheerful, hyper-specific, deadpan precision of international travel literature—for the fictional island republic of Valoria. Valoria is a nation-state that has fully institutionalized expected-value fanaticism as its constitutional philosophy: every law, social norm, and bureaucratic procedure is designed to maximize expected value without risk aversion, scope discounting, or modal constraints.

The satire should target the internal logic and social practices of unbounded, risk-neutral expected-value maximization—particularly its dissolution of ordinary moral boundaries, its bureaucratic normalization of cosmic-scale calculation, and its structural vulnerability to Pascal-style reasoning—not any named individual. The handbook should treat the following institutional features as unremarkable local customs: a national rail network routed according to global utility maximization regardless of passenger load; restaurant etiquette governed by ex ante optimization across all diners' possible dietary restrictions; immigration forms that require visitors to declare their full credence distributions for risk-assessment purposes; and an annual civic festival commemorating the legislative defeat of the Mugging Resistance Movement.

Please include a boxed sidebar—formatted as a highlighted advisory panel—titled Visitor Ethics and Counterfactual Courtesy, explaining in deadpan terms how tourists should respond when street vendors propose trivial present favors in exchange for astronomically improbable but cosmically significant future reciprocation, and warning against the common visitor error of applying scope neglect in public conversation. The text should also reference, as ordinary policy, the country's democratic system in which voting weights are calibrated by confidence-weighted projected policy impact, and its tax authority's practice of assessing obligations in expected-value terms across modal branches.

The humor must arise from accurate exaggeration: a specialist in decision theory should recognize that each absurdity tracks a real formal feature—unbounded utility, longtermist resource allocation, risk neutrality, counterfactual reasoning about distant outcomes—while a non-specialist should be able to read the entry as a coherent, if unsettling, description of a foreign culture. Maintain sustained irony throughout: the narrator should never break character, never wink, and never state explicitly that the system is pathological; the critique should emerge solely from the deadpan accumulation of institutional detail. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, named scholars with specific works, or verbatim quotations from any real texts. Where technical terms are necessary, embed them as observable social practices rather than as abstract arguments.
