Consider a variant of Maxwell's demon in which the gate never measures or records a particle's velocity; instead, it enters a brief entangled state with an incoming molecule, uses that correlation to decide whether to open, and then lets the control qubit decohere entirely into the bath so that no information is retained or ever erased. Since Landauer's principle is usually invoked to show that the thermodynamic cost lies in erasing the demon's stored records, it appears that this design avoids the information-theoretic rescue altogether and achieves sorting without paying the entropic price. Does this genuinely threaten the second law, or does the framing rest on a misunderstanding—specifically, on the false assumption that decoherence without retention is thermodynamically free and that the cost of sorting can be separated from the physical interaction that produces the correlation?
