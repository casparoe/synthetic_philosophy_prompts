ive been noticing this pattern in big orgs -- universities, government agencies, some corps -- and im not sure if it has a name. someone points out a real flaw, like that an evaluation system is biased or that a policy harms one group. the org doesnt deny it; they create a task force, staff it with critics, and then point to its existence as proof theyre self-correcting. but the task force has no power over the core system. instead it writes reports that refine operations and lend legitimacy. the original critic ends up supplying credibility to the very thing they attacked, and the flaw becomes a kind of upgrade. i know foucault talks about power being productive rather than just repressive, and this feels adjacent, but its more specific: dissent is metabolized into institutional authority and operational improvement. is there an established term for this exact cycle? how does it differ from plain co-optation or from foucaults idea of counter-conduct being absorbed? and if theres no precise name, can you coin one with a clear boundary -- for instance, excluding cases where the critic actually gains decision-making power and changes the system?
