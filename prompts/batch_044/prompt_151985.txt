I have been working through deflationary theories of truth—particularly the view that the truth-predicate functions solely as a logical device for disquotation and for expressing infinite conjunctions, without denoting any substantive property. If this redundancy claim is correct, then it seems there can be no metaphysically robust distinction between a belief being true and its being false; there is only a linguistic difference in which sentences we are disposed to assert. From this, I take it that deflationists must reject the traditional tripartite analysis of knowledge as justified true belief, since "true" in that formula cannot mark a genuinely distinct state of affairs that separates knowledge from justified false belief. Must a consistent deflationist therefore replace the tripartite analysis with a purely pragmatic or coherence-based account of epistemic achievement, or is there a way to preserve normative distinctions between knowledge and error without treating truth as a substantial property?
