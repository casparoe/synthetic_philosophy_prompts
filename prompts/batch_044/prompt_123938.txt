I'm delivering the retirement toast for my doctoral advisor at her university dinner. She is a philosopher of mind who spent her career developing the view that the "hard problem" of consciousness is not an unfinished scientific puzzle but a meta-problem: an inquiry into why our conceptual scheme makes physical explanations feel necessarily incomplete when they address subjective experience. About half the guests are philosophers, neuroscientists, and cognitive scientists familiar with her work; the other half are family, administrators, and students from unrelated fields who have never read a philosophy of mind paper. Write a five-minute toast that does three things: first, honor her contribution precisely, without claiming she "solved" consciousness, without suggesting she dismissed consciousness as an illusion, and without framing her legacy as a stepping-stone for future neuroscience to finish; second, explain the meta-problem to the non-experts by using one specific analogy she used in her teaching — the idea of a flat map, where every local measurement is accurate but the global projection makes distances seem distorted, so that the distortion is a feature of the representational tool rather than a feature of the territory — and use that analogy to show how she relocated the mystery from the world into our cognitive apparatus; third, let that relocation shape what the toast can and cannot say, so that praise for her takes the form of recognizing a conceptual achievement rather than a scientific discovery. The tone should be warm, intellectually serious, and accessible; no citations, no reading lists, no bibliographic references, and no mention of specific papers by title or date. Begin the response with the speech itself, with no introductory preface or commentary.
