I'm taking a seminar on comparative historical sociology, and we have been debating whether terms like "collapse" actually apply to well-documented cases such as the Bronze Age Eastern Mediterranean, the Classic Maya lowlands, and Rapa Nui (Easter Island). One view holds that centralized states, writing systems, and long-distance networks dissolved, but that this represents not societal failure but a reconfiguration into smaller, decentralized polities—what some call transformation rather than collapse. The opposing worry is that if every dissolution is relabeled as transformation, the concept of collapse becomes unfalsifiable and therefore useless as an analytical category. Rather than arguing the point abstractly, I want to see it dramatized.

Please write a short story set roughly three centuries after the abandonment of a fictional capital city—let's call it a river-basin metropolis whose monumental architecture, standardized weights, and interregional correspondence archives were once maintained by a centralized bureaucracy. The narrative should follow a protagonist who grew up in one of the dispersed valley settlements that replaced the capital, someone who understands the old system well enough to repair its machinery or decode its records, but who lives without any desire to restore it. Include at least one specific material trace—perhaps a deliberately dismantled monument, a measurement standard now used only for local barter, or trade tablets preserved as household talismans—that a distant future archaeologist could easily misinterpret as evidence of violent disruption or lost knowledge. Let the conceptual tension emerge organically through daily routine and interaction with these remnants: the character should experience the present not as a tragedy or a golden age, but as an ordinary, workable arrangement, so that the reader is left genuinely uncertain whether "collapse" or "transformation" is the more honest description. Keep the tone restrained and observational rather than didactic, and do not provide an explicit answer to the conceptual question within the story's conclusion.
