Construct a thought experiment to specifications. Design a realistic, one-paragraph institutional scenario in which members must signal cooperative intent before accessing a shared digital commons—specifically, an open-source verification network—under two contrasting conditions. In Condition H, entry requires burning a verifiable but genuinely useless amount of computing resources, with the waste proportional to the potential free-rider gain (a handicap-style signal). In Condition I, entry requires passing a biometric check that correlates with a stable, unobservable psychological trait—high intrinsic motivation—but imposes no resource expenditure on anyone (an index-style signal). Constraints: no science-fiction technology, no perfect predictors or demons, no references to real-world platforms, and all payoffs must be ones ordinary people could face. After presenting the scenario, do the following: (1) state the verdict the handicap principle gives for cooperation stability in H and in I; (2) identify the single structural feature that distinguishes H from I and makes cost unnecessary in the latter; (3) explain what happens to that distinction if the feature is removed; (4) show how a defender of the handicap principle could try to reinterpret Condition I as still involving a hidden cost, and why that defense fails given the scenario's constraints; (5) conclude by saying whether removing that feature collapses the case back into a standard economic signaling model where cost alone guarantees honesty.
