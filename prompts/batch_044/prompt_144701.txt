Two people in my study group got stuck arguing about Grice's distinction between saying and implicating, specifically in cases where the speaker violates the Maxim of Manner on purpose. (1) J offered an example: if someone asks 'did you finish the report?' and the speaker answers 'the document has reached a state of non-non-completion,' they're being obscure, but by assuming cooperation we recover the implicature that yes, it's done. So speaker meaning diverges from sentence meaning in a predictable, Gricean way. (2) K pushed back: deliberately flouting Manner suspends the Cooperative Principle locally, so there's no implicature mechanism running -- just the listener making a charitable guess. Grice can't cover this because the maxims presuppose that the speaker is trying to be cooperative, not playing word games. (3) J replied that cooperation can be layered: you can violate surface-level clarity while still cooperating at the propositional level, especially in technical or in-group contexts. So the implicature is still generated, just routed differently. (4) K said if you allow that kind of flexibility, Grice loses the ability to distinguish lying (violating Quality) from being obscure (violating Manner), since both involve saying one thing while meaning another, and you just invoke 'layered cooperation' to save whichever case you want.
