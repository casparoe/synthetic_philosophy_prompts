For a 60-minute advanced seminar of 12 students, design a structured role-play called "The Protocol Court" that makes participants feel, before it is named, the performative tension in denying shared rational norms. Divide them into Skeptics, who must reject any universal binding force of reasons, and Arbitrators, who apply the Mirror Rule: every objection must be translated into terms the opponent could recognize as rationally binding, with non-monetary stakes controlling speaking turns. Arrange the rules so that Skeptics must invoke intersubjective validity conditions precisely to register their denial, letting the contradiction emerge from play rather than lecture. Present the design as exactly five numbered parts — setup and roles; rules and stakes; timeline of play; debrief mapping game moments to the transcendental structure; and honest notes on predictable failures plus the feature of self-refutation the activity cannot reproduce — each part limited to one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
