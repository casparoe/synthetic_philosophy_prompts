I'm working on a chapter about bodily autonomy law and I'm stuck on an asymmetry. In most legal systems, you can refuse a life-saving blood transfusion for any reason, and we never force someone to donate a kidney to save a dying relative — but abortion restrictions often treat carrying a pregnancy to term as a duty that can override refusal. What theory of moral status or harm does that instutional pattern actually presuppose? Does it only make sense if you accept something like the future-like-ours argument that the fetus is already a person with rights, or can Thomson's violinist analogy explain why pregnancy is treated differently from kidney donation? And if the practice is meant to be consistent with our refusal rights elsewhere, does it smuggle in a view about 'natural' versus 'artificial' bodily support that we don't apply to other cases? I feel like there's an implicit philosphy of personhood buried in the legal distinction but I can't tell if it's one coherent view or just a mess of ad hoc commitments.
