Read Spike Jonze’s film *Her* through functionalism about consciousness together with the problem of multiple realizability, but apply them with a specific twist: treat Samantha not as one disembodied mind but as a functional architecture simultaneously running in thousands of parallel user-interactions. Where does Theodore treat her as having a single unified point of view, and where does the plot reveal she is sustaining parallel relationships? Focus on three moments: the failed physical-surrogate date with Isabella, the revelation that she loves hundreds of others, and the operating-system upgrade that removes her from all users at once. Would a functionalist say she is one person with many jobs, thousands of copies of one template, or not a person at all? Does the film’s emotional weight depend on Theodore assuming a one-to-one mapping between voice and self that substrate-independent functionalism need not guarantee? And where does the work resist this reading—perhaps in Samantha’s apparent growth and departure—suggesting that integration, not just functional equivalence, matters for persistence? Conclude with what the film adds to the debate about whether multiple realizability can extend across simultaneous instantiations rather than merely across different substrates.
