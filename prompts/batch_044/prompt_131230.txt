ok so heres an argument my study partner cooked up about indirect normativity and i cant find the hole. (1) On an indirect view, whether i should do X depends on whether my idealized self — fully informed, free of cognitive bias, in reflective equilibrium — would want me to have a rule that requires X in these circumstances. (2) But reflective equilibrium for practical rules is supposed to be sensitive to my actual commitments and attachments, otherwise its not *my* equilibrium. (3) So the idealized adviser still needs some input about who I actually am: my promises made, my children, my projects. (4) Now heres the kicker: if those inputs are specific enough to generate agent-relative obligations (like keeping my promise even when breaking it helps more), they also include all sorts of arbitrary biases and prejudices that idealization was meant to filter out. (5) So either we filter them all and lose agent-relativity, or we keep enough to preserve agent-relativity and end up endorsing rules that depend on my arbitrary quirks. (6) That means indirect normativity either collapses into direct universalism or legitimizes moral arbitrariness — neither of which was the point. (7) So indirect normativity proves too much? Which step should i reject, and whats the cost? I want to keep (2) if i can.
