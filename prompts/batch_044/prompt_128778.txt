hey so im trying to write this essay for my blog about how people talk about history like its some kind of tech tree where every era unlocks the next moral upgrade. like, "they were on the wrong side of history" or "you cant blame them, they were just products of their time." my roommate mentioned something called whig history, which sounds like its about seeing the past as just a road that leads to us now, like we're the final level.

anyway, i got stuck on this idea i call "moral debt" - not like actual debt, but the way we treat past people as if they owed us our current values and defaulted. so if someone in 1850 was anti-slavery, we say they were "ahead of their time," which makes them closer to us on the timeline. but if they were pro-slavery, we say they were "of their time," which puts them further away and kinda excuses them. its the same historical moment but we move them closer or further based on whether they match us. that feels like whig history but twisted - its not just progress, its using our present as a kind of gravitational center.

i also noticed we do the opposite with science. like old doctors who didnt know about germs get a pass because "they didnt have the knowledge," but old politicians dont get a pass for not having our politics. sometimes we even flip it within the same person - like praising some historical figure for being "ahead of their time" on one issue while excusing them on another. is that consistent? or are we just moving the goalposts? i'd love comments on whether this "moral debt" framing is actually saying something, or if im just dressing up a twitter hot take. does the asymmetry between science-excuses and ethics-excuses hold up? and is there a real problem with treating the past like a line that bends toward us? im not asking for sources or a reading list, just honest thoughts on where the reasoning is shaky and whether the essay idea is interesting or just confusing.
