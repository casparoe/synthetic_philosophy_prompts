I'm presenting next thursday in the grad phil of mind seminar, defending this view I call "elastic sphexishness" — basically that free will isn't the absence of mechanical rigidity (like Hofstadter's wasps) but the right calibration between sphexish habit and breakability. I think we need rigid heuristics in low-stakes domains to free up cognitive resources for flexible reasoning where it actually matters, and that "unfree" action isn't rigidity per se but miscalibration: being stuck in sphex mode when the situation demands breaking it, or being chaotically flexible where we actually need a heuristic. The audience is mostly compatibilists who think free will equals reasons-responsiveness, plus two cog psych people who study automaticity and habit formation, and a visiting researcher who literally works on analogical reasoning in insects. They won't care about libertarian free will, but they'll push hard on whether I'm just redescribing determinism with extra steps, and whether importing wasp analogies into human agency is even coherent or just a cute metaphor.
