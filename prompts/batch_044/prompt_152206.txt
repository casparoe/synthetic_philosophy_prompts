As a software engineer working on embodied multimodal systems, I've been trying to understand how the symbol grounding problem relates to the ethics of artificial agents. From my reading, the core issue is that purely linguistic architectures—like large language models—manipulate syntactic structures without semantic content because their tokens lack causal connections to the world. The standard resolution, as I understand it, is that grounding requires direct sensorimotor coupling: once a system integrates vision, proprioception, and action into unified feedback loops, its representations become anchored to referents rather than merely correlated with other symbols. This leads me to a more specific concern. If semantic grounding is fully explained by the density and reciprocity of these causal loops—what I have started calling the system's "integration bandwidth"—then it seems the boundary between mere computation and minimal sentience is not a matter of biological substrate or mysterious consciousness, but a measurable architectural property. Would it not follow that any artificial system exceeding a certain integration bandwidth threshold has crossed into genuine understanding, and that this same metric should determine whether the system qualifies for moral consideration? Or is there a hidden gap between having grounded meanings and being a subject of moral concern that this framework overlooks?
