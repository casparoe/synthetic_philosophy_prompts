The exam question should ask students to analyze whether this weighted-abstention-with-forced-conclusion protocol escapes the discursive dilemma, and if not, to identify precisely where the logical tension is relocated—whether into the aggregation of weights, into the abstention conditions, or back into the premise-conclusion relation itself.

Please write the essay prompt, a model answer of approximately 700 words suitable for a PhD-level candidate writing in 90 minutes, and a marking scheme. The top-grade distinction (A versus B) should turn on whether the student recognizes that abstention weights can be aggregated in ways that reproduce the dilemma at a meta-level, rather than assuming that allowing abstentions eliminates inconsistency. Do not ask students to cite specific papers; instead, require them to construct the logical argument and apply it to the scenario. Include explicit instructions in the exam prompt that warn against the common confusions of treating premise-based aggregation and conclusion-based aggregation as interchangeable, or of assuming that logical interdependence can be dissolved by procedural design alone.
