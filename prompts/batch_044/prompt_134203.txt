Write a deadpan parody of a declassified 1984 internal field manual—approximately 600 words, formatted in numbered procedural sections with subclauses—issued by the fictional Bureau of Radical Interpretation. The manual instructs field agents on how to interpret an unknown language and belief-system from scratch, with zero prior shared vocabulary, in strict accordance with Davidsonian radical interpretation. The satire should exaggerate real features of Davidson’s view: the principle of charity (interpreters must maximize agreement and rationality in the speaker’s beliefs), the holism of belief and desire (no belief can be attributed in isolation), the rejection of the scheme/content distinction and of alternative conceptual schemes, and the triangulation condition (that thought requires interaction between speaker, interpreter, and a shared world). Invent one bureaucratic mechanism that literalizes each commitment—e.g., a “Charity Adjustment Ledger” in which interpreters log revisions to their own belief systems to preserve speaker rationality; a “Holism Check” that prohibits attributing any single belief until the agent has mapped the speaker’s entire web; an “Indeterminacy Filing System” that treats translation underdetermination not as a problem but as a procedural feature, certifying incompatible translations as equally valid and billing the speaker for ambiguity; and a “Triangulation Equipment Requisition” form requiring three-party observation (interpreter, speaker, shared environment) before any mental attribution is authorized. The voice should be purely bureaucratic and deadpan: no winking, no explicit statement that this is absurd. The target is the practical overextension of Davidson’s philosophical conditions, not any living person. A reader familiar with Davidson should recognize that the manual understands the view being satirized and should find the exaggeration fair; a non-specialist should be able to read it as a strange government document and grasp that the procedure demands impossible self-effacement from the interpreter. Do not include bibliographies, citations, requests for further reading, or verbatim quotations from real philosophical texts.
