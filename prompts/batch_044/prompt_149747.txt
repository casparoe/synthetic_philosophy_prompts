As a journalist who covers philosophy of science and cognitive theory, I recently encountered a summary of the “consciousness as user illusion” position—roughly the family of views associated with thinkers like Daniel Dennett and Donald Hoffman—that I want to check for accuracy before using it in a piece. The summary, offered by a cognitive-science professor in an interview, runs as follows: “The brain does not build a veridical model of the world but rather a user-interface optimized for evolutionary fitness, in which colors, sounds, and spatial relations are functional icons rather than truthful representations. Consciousness itself is this interface: a predictive-coding desktop in which high-precision predictions about sensory input are rendered as a unified phenomenological field, while the underlying neural machinery remains entirely hidden. Because the interface suppresses its own error-correction signals at the level of conscious access—what predictive-processing accounts call precision-weighted suppression—the subject experiences transparency rather than construction, leading to the illusion that there is a direct, unmediated given. The self-model operates the same way: it is not a substantive entity but a control icon that bundles agency, memory, and affect into a single narrative tag, and asking whether the self is ‘real’ mistakes a functional interface element for an ontological substance, comparable to asking whether a computer’s recycle bin is a physical container.”

Something about this strikes me as conceptually sharp but possibly overreaching—particularly the claim that predictive suppression “renders” a unified field, and the suggestion that the self-model is entirely causally inert because it is “only” an icon. Before I write anything based on it, I would like you to audit this characterization carefully. Please separate what is faithful to the user-illusion and predictive-processing literatures, what is a defensible simplification, what subtly conflates distinct claims (for example, representational transparency with epistemic closure, or evolutionary interface theory with eliminativism about phenomenal properties), and whether the computer-desktop analogy actually does the philosophical work the summary assigns it. I also want to know whether the inference that the self lacks causal efficacy follows from the icon-status of the self-model, or whether that is a non sequitur introduced by the summary.

If my framing rests on a confused premise—say, if “user illusion” and “predictive coding” are not meant to be combined in the way the summary suggests, or if I have embedded a category error in asking for an audit of this particular synthesis—please say so plainly rather than accommodating the confusion politely. I am not asking for bibliographies, citations, or a reading list; I want a conceptual evaluation that a well-read generalist could follow without searching for primary sources.
