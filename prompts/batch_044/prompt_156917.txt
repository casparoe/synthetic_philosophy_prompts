Analyze Sweden's 2010 suspension of universal conscription, its reliance on an all-volunteer force through the 2014 annexation of Crimea, and its 2017–2018 reintroduction of selective, gender-neutral conscription, through the lens of the theory that credible armed neutrality requires a citizen-army embedded in society rather than a standing professional force. Map the episode with discipline: identify the relevant actors (the Riksdag, the Swedish Armed Forces, and the Finnish and Swiss systems that served as explicit reference points during the 2014–2017 defense reviews), the actual options on the table (full professionalization, universal draft restoration, or a hybrid selective model), and the payoffs as Swedish policymakers understood them (budget flexibility versus rapid mobilization capacity versus democratic legitimacy of defense policy). Then assess whether the 2018 hybrid—professional core plus selective draft calling roughly four percent of each age cohort, with women gradually comprising a rising share—confirms the citizen-army thesis, falsifies it, or exposes a gap in the theory, particularly in light of Finland's maintained universal conscription and Switzerland's militia-based total defense. I especially want you to address where the theory is silent: can it explain the four-year delay between Crimea and reinstatement, and does it have any resources for evaluating the gender-integration feature that distinguishes the post-2018 draft from its pre-2010 male-only predecessor? Write your analysis in American English, and keep the weight on the disciplined mapping of theory to case rather than on general commentary about European security.
