Write a villanelle—nineteen lines in five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains (A1 and A2) and only two rhymes—staging Saul Kripke’s Humphrey objection to David Lewis’s counterpart theory. Let refrain A1 voice the counterpart-theoretic claim: the figure who wins the 1968 nomination in another possible world is not Humphrey himself but only a counterpart, bearing modal properties derivatively through resemblance and shared origin. Let refrain A2 voice the objector’s reply: it is Humphrey who could have prevailed, for praising a mere likeness for an election victory is metaphysical confusion. In the tercets, build the branching scenario—the shared birth, the divergence of accidental intrinsic properties, the nomination split—so that the rhyme words track the stakes (for instance, rhymes on origin and separation). The final quatrain must place A1 and A2 side by side without resolving them, leaving the tension between literal transworld identity and modal continuity through counterpart relations fully open. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of modal metaphysics recognizes the Humphrey objection, Lewis’s reply about derivatively borne modal properties, and the pressure from origin essentialism, yet it should stand as poetry for a reader unfamiliar with the names. No footnotes, no citations, no reading lists, and no verbatim quotations from texts. Do not reduce either view to “copy” language; distinguish intrinsic divergence from essential origin, and keep the philosophical disagreement doing the structural work of the form.
