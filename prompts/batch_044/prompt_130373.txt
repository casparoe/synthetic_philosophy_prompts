Draft the joint statement that a negative-liberty theorist—who holds that freedom consists in the absence of actual interference—and a republican-liberty theorist—who holds that freedom requires the absence of arbitrary domination, whether or not it is exercised—could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The statement should include: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about the conceptual structure of liberty, the distinction between interference and domination, the master-slave relation as a paradigm, and the conditions that make power arbitrary rather than merely present; (2) the precise formulations of their residual disagreement, each phrased in wording that its own side would accept; (3) the cruxes—specifically, whether an unexercised but arbitrary capacity to interfere already constitutes unfreedom, and whether institutional safeguards that make domination practically impossible but leave its legal basis intact eliminate domination; and (4) for each crux, one concrete scenario both sides agree is a valid test—such as a legal regime in which citizens hold an unconditional formal right to practice a religion, yet an unaccountable state office retains the unreviewable legal authority to revoke that right without justification or appeal, with no history of having done so—together with each side's explicit prediction about whether the citizens are free in that scenario. Present the agreed claims as a numbered section and the disagreements, paired with their scenarios and predictions, in a table. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and do not frame the task as adjudicating which view is correct.
