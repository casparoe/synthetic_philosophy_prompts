I sit on our university's Committee on Institutional Neutrality and Public Partnerships — we vote next tuesday on renewing the heritage covenant with St. Mark's Episcopal parish. The chapel's been on campus since 1870 but the university technically owns the land. St. Mark's wants to shift from the old lease to something they call cooperative establishment: we'd collect an optional chapel stewardship fee from staff via payroll (a voluntary church-tax-style deduction, not mandatory), and in return they'd appoint a non-voting chaplain to the student wellness advisory board. They say this mirrors England's established colleges and the German Kirchensteuer system, and that ending it would be negative secularism that harms religious students.

Some members think any payroll-linked fee makes the university an agent of religious collection, even if voluntary, and violates our neutrality policy. Others say it's institutional pluralism — we already let the Catholic center use our auditorium free. There's also this framing about corporate religious freedom, the idea that the parish itself, not just individual students, has a positive right to institutional presence. I need a short memo for the committee reccomending approve with conditions, reject, or a modified version, with a concrete timeline. It should treat the corporate-freedom argument seriously rather than just citing the Establishment Clause and walking away. Also need to address whether this is closer to England's establishment model or France's laïcité, since both sides keep throwing those around. Can you draft this? Need it circulated by monday so people have time to read, and please don't just list books — I want reasoning we can actually use in the room.
