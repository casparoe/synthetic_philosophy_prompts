I am drafting a philosophical novel in which a pivotal chapter takes place during a closed-room graduate examination administered by a reclusive moral philosopher, and I want the exam to function as a genuinely difficult intellectual confrontation rather than a decorative set piece. I was discussing the scene with an editor who noted that if the question merely asks students to summarize lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, the drama collapses into recitation, so I have decided to center the problem on what I will call the Calibration Problem: the apparent tension that lexical ordering requires suffering and pleasure to be comparable on some shared scale—so that any amount of suffering can be said to outweigh any amount of pleasure—while the motivation for lexical negative utilitarianism often appeals to a qualitative asymmetry in valence that treats suffering and pleasure as not symmetrically measurable at all. The exam should ask students to examine whether this tension is fatal, whether a defender can resolve it by abandoning cardinal measurement in favor of an ordinal or categorical comparison, and what this implies for cross-personal and cross-modal judgments of affective states.

Please write this as a formal, upper-level seminar exam question suitable for a final in value theory, together with a model answer of approximately seven hundred words and a detailed marking scheme. The marking scheme should separate an A answer from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain—such as explaining why lexical priority does not strictly depend on cardinal commensurability, articulating how an ordinal framework might preserve qualitative asymmetry without quantitative comparison, and distinguishing the Calibration Problem from the more familiar problem of interpersonal utility comparison—while also identifying the specific mistakes that should cost marks, particularly the error of assuming that rejecting cardinal measurement automatically undermines lexical ordering, and the error of conflating qualitative valence asymmetry with full incommensurability. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, since the examination in my novel takes place without external resources, and please present the question, model answer, and marking scheme in polished American English.
