Draft the joint statement that a Somatic Integrationist and a Textualist Separatist in Nietzsche scholarship could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on whether Nietzsche’s writings about diet, digestion, and bodily regimen are philosophically central or merely biographical noise.

The Integrationist holds that Nietzsche’s prescriptions in Ecce Homo, his letters on climate and cuisine, and his notebook remarks on digestion form an integral part of his philosophy of self-cultivation, will to power, and the achievement of what he calls great health; the Separatist holds that these are contingent symptoms of his chronic illness, carrying no systematic weight for his metaphysics, epistemology, or ethics.

Produce a document with the following parts:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims about what Nietzsche wrote and experienced regarding food, climate, digestion, and health—phrased neutrally, without deciding whether these passages are philosophically central. Include agreement on his digestive ailments, his explicit dietary rules, his association of particular cuisines with particular temperaments, and his distinction between sickly and great health.

2. The precise formulations of disagreement, each phrased in wording its own camp would accept for its position, and each phrased in wording its own camp would accept for the opposing position.

3. The cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side. I expect at least two: (a) whether Nietzsche’s personal dietary discipline functions as a micro-asceticism that either exemplifies or undermines his critique of ascetic ideals in On the Genealogy of Morals; and (b) whether his concept of great health implies an embodied epistemology—where bodily states are conditions for the possibility of certain perspectives—that conflicts with his broader perspectivism. For each crux, specify a concrete case, passage pattern, or comparative argument both sides agree is relevant (for example, comparing his treatment of diet with his treatment of music and climate as conditions for thought), and record each camp’s explicit prediction about what that case shows.

4. A disagreements table summarizing the two positions on each crux, including the predictions from part 3.

5. A brief meta-note identifying where this framing is most fragile: specifically, which agreed claim or crux formulation either camp might reject if pressed, and what kind of textual discovery or conceptual argument would cause either side to revise its predictions about whether diet is philosophically central. Also include a note on where your own reasoning in drafting this statement is most likely to fail—for instance, in assuming that great health can be interpreted coherently without reference to his actual dietary rules, or in treating the asceticism comparison as more than analogical—and state what specific consideration or argument would change your assessment of which camp has correctly characterized the relationship between Nietzsche’s bodily practices and his philosophy.

The document should not request bibliographies, reading lists, or specific page citations; refer to works and notebook periods generally (e.g., Ecce Homo, the 1888 notebooks, On the Genealogy of Morals) without requiring exact editions or page numbers. Avoid inventing direct quotations; if you reference a specific formulation, paraphrase it without quotation marks or verify it from a source you can access. Inject at least one niche conceptual framing—such as the idea of an embodied counter-asceticism or a nutritional ontology underlying his perspectivism—that is not standard in introductory treatments but remains answerable from general knowledge of Nietzsche’s texts.
