Write an essay evaluating whether democratic backsliding retains analytical value for regimes that never breach formal constitutional text but instead degrade political competition through procedural instruments—omnibus bills that bundle unrelated provisions, emergency decrees kept within statutory limits, and judicial restructuring conducted through legally valid appointment rules. Consider who can legitimately designate such regimes as backsliding without circular reasoning, and assess whether applying the concept to Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the United States exposes a common structural pattern or collapses into partisan labeling. Reach and justify a concrete conclusion: either that backsliding should be reconceptualized around procedural degradation, or that it must be reserved for explicit rule-breaking and therefore excluded for these cases.
