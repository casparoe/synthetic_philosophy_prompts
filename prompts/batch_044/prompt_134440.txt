I'm working through a philosophical profile on consciousness for a reading group and want a consistency check before I present it. The view takes Schrödinger's Vedanta-leaning argument for the unity of consciousness seriously, but tries to combine it with commitments that are not always paired with it. Here is where I stand: (1) I accept consciousness monism: there is numerically one consciousness, and what we call individual minds are not ontologically separate subjects but differentiated perspectives or modalities within a single field, analogous to waves that are not separate from the ocean they inhabit. (2) Consequently, I treat personal identity—both diachronic and interpersonal—as a pragmatic organizational fiction rather than a metaphysically deep fact; the boundary between my experience and yours is conventional, not fundamental. (3) I nevertheless accept evolutionary neuroscience at face value: brains are causally integrated information-processing systems that evolved through natural selection, but I deny they generate consciousness; rather, they restrict, filter, or localize a pre-existing unified field, determining the particular content and boundaries of each perspective without producing the subjectivity itself. (4) On this basis, I claim that the hard problem of consciousness dissolves, since there is no need to explain how matter produces private first-person experience if all such experience is ultimately identical, and brains merely account for why it appears divided into localized streams. (5) Despite (1) and (2), I maintain that moral responsibility is genuinely individual: agents can be properly praised or blamed because the conventional self is the correct locus of practical reasoning and social coordination, even if it is not metaphysically ultimate. (6) I reject panpsychism, since I do not think consciousness is distributed as a fundamental property of every particle; rather, it is singular and undivided, with physical reality being either an aspect of it or its structural manifestation, not an independent realm that must be animated.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are in genuine tension—not merely surprising together, but pulling in incompatible directions once the motivations for each are made explicit—which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real tension, what is the least costly revision? I am especially unsure whether (5) can survive the deflation of the self in (2) without collapsing into a universalist ethics that makes individual blame incoherent, and whether the filter-model in (3) can accommodate severe brain damage, which seems to eliminate particular perspectives entirely rather than merely altering their content, without reintroducing the very generation problem I claim (4) dissolves. Rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each whether a commitment, a qualification, or an auxiliary premise must give, and tell me which single revision—if I were forced to make only one—would clean up the most problems at once. I have not decided which commitment I would be least reluctant to abandon, so please do not build in a presumed fallback.
