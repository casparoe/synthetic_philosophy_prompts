I'm working through a conceptual puzzle in a course on justice and social policy, and I keep returning to the same evaluative practice in wealthy, institutionally egalitarian societies—particularly the Nordic model—where gender-equality policy focuses on removing formal barriers, providing gender-neutral parental leave, and ensuring unrestricted career counseling. Despite these measures, occupational segregation by gender often persists or intensifies: women remain overrepresented in care and education sectors and underrepresented in engineering and executive roles. Commentators and policymakers routinely interpret this pattern as a fork between two explanations—either hidden cultural socialization still shapes preference, or the distribution reflects genuinely free, autonomous choice that equality should respect rather than correct. What philosophical commitments does that very framing—treating institutional neutrality as the baseline and interpreting resulting segregation as either culture's residue or freedom's expression—presuppose about the nature of autonomy, the metric of equality, and whether gendered preference is the kind of thing that can be isolated from social meaning?

Reconstruct the implicit theory: does the practice commit us to a procedural conception of justice that cares only about barrier-removal, or can it accommodate a relational or patterned-outcome view? Is it consistent with both biological-essentialist and strong social-constructivist accounts of preference formation, or does it quietly assume one? Distinguish what the evaluation framework strictly requires from what merely coheres with it, and identify points where the practice is philosophically underdetermined—where rival theories of value or of gender ontology could license the same policy posture. Then, specify what kind of finding—say, cross-national evidence that preference distributions shift dramatically with subtle framing changes, or a normative argument that statistical gender patterns in free societies are themselves unjust—would lead you to revise your reconstruction of the practice's commitments.
