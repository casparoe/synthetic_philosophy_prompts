To push past the standard debate, I'm considering a framing that doesn't come up often in the introductory literature: how Benatar's asymmetry should interact with radical uncertainty about the quality of future existence. For instance, if we take the asymmetry seriously, what does it imply about creating beings whose lives might fall into extreme suffering that is currently speculative—such as through severe climate collapse, engineered biological states, or even synthetic consciousness that lacks the evolutionary buffers that make human suffering somewhat predictable? Rather than simply asking whether it is wrong to have a biological child today, I'm wondering which research directions are genuinely upstream. Should philosophers first attempt to formalize the asymmetry in decision-theoretic terms so it can apply to outcomes with unknown probabilities? Or is the more urgent work meta-ethical—determining whether the asymmetry collapses if we abandon a strictly person-affecting framework? Alternatively, is there tractable empirical or sociological work about whether accepting the asymmetry actually shifts reproductive behavior in measurable ways?
