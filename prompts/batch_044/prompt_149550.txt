Please construct a rigorous argument for the position that membership in the demos should be defined by a co-authorship of binding law criterion rather than by citizenship, territorial residence, or the all-affected-interests principle taken alone. On this view, one belongs to the demos if one is subject to a jurisdiction's coercive legal framework in a way that makes reciprocal democratic revision of that framework conceptually possible—which includes long-term resident non-citizens integrated into the legal order, but excludes citizens who have permanently emigrated and are no longer subject to that authority, and also excludes future generations and non-human animals that are causally affected by present laws yet cannot enter into reciprocal legislative relationships. You should defend this boundary against its strongest objection: that it arbitrarily privileges present-tense presence by treating capacity for reciprocal revision as a necessary condition, thereby unjustly excluding future people whose lives will be profoundly shaped by current legislation, as well as cognitively disabled adults and young children who are clearly affected but lack full participatory capacity. In your answer, please indicate how confident you are in what you claim and explicitly single out the point about which you are least sure.
