Student answer:

“The interleaved-layer protocol clearly avoids the discontinuity objection because there is never a single moment when the whole layer becomes synthetic. Since replacement happens in two waves with biological neurons always present as intermediaries, the system never crosses a sharp boundary from biological to artificial. This means the subject’s phenomenal consciousness remains continuous, because functional continuity at the macro level guarantees phenomenal continuity at the subject level.

As for the combination problem, it does not apply here in the way it applies to standard panpsychism. In standard panpsychist combination, many micro-subjects combine to form one macro-subject, but in this protocol there are never many synthetic micro-subjects existing simultaneously without biological ones present. At every stage, the synthetic neurons are always embedded in a biological network, so there is no pure synthetic substrate that needs to combine. Once the layer is fully synthetic, it is just one unified system, not many. Therefore the protocol avoids both the discontinuity problem and the combination problem.”
