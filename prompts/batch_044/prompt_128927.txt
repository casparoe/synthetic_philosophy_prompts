I hold an institutional version of Wittgenstein's private-language argument: mental-state ascriptions are not reports of inner observation but socially licensed moves whose correctness conditions depend on intersubjective behavioral calibration, so that first-person authority is a revocable default within shared practice rather than direct epistemic access. What would count against this? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward—e.g., plausible models of private error-correction without circularity, empirical results about introspection independent of linguistic practice, or conceptual demonstrations that internalized norms can be self-correcting. For each, say whether defenders of the institutional reading would concede it or absorb it by broadening practice or public. Finally, assess whether the view risks unfalsifiability: if every counterexample can be redescribed as a hidden social framework, is that insulation a virtue or a vice?
