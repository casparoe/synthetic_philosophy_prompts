Write a sestina on the epistemic bootstrapping problem—what reliabilists call “easy knowledge”—in which a subject moves from a basic perceptual belief (say, that she has hands) through a conditional linking that belief-type to the reliability of the faculty that produced it, and thereby derives knowledge of the faculty’s own reliability without any independent verification. Choose six end-words that correspond to the nodes of this circular justification—words for the basic belief, the perceptual faculty, the conditional bridge, the derived reliability claim, the missing external check, and the loop itself—and rotate them through the sestina’s prescribed pattern (1-2-3-4-5-6, 6-1-5-2-4-3, and so on) so that the formal displacement enacts the philosophical displacement: the basic belief should not sit at the end of every stanza but should be carried through positions where it is first ground, then mediated, then returned only after it has been used to license the very faculty that licensed it. In the closing envoi, bring all six words together in three lines so that the distinction between unmediated and bootstrapped knowledge collapses into a single compressed tautology. The poem should be precise enough that an epistemologist recognizes the mechanics of the objection, and the form—not just the vocabulary—should do the work of showing why the circle feels both inevitable and hollow.
