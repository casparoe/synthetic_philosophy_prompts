I'm serving on a review committee for a research program that funds work on autonomous decision systems. One proposal argues that any system physically implemented in the world cannot maintain a clean separation between the part that makes choices and the part that is acted upon—both are just causal processes in the same substrate—and therefore that evaluation protocols should abandon the idea of an outside observer checking whether goals are satisfied. Instead, the authors say, evaluation must be rebuilt entirely from internal correlations, with no privileged external viewpoint. I object that if there is genuinely no outside position, then the distinction between getting something right and getting something wrong collapses into just another causal event, making normative evaluation impossible. But I also see that my objection assumes a framework where the decision-maker is logically prior to the world, which may be exactly the fiction this proposal rejects. How should I assess whether my resistance points to a real conceptual problem—whether evaluation can survive without an external vantage point—or whether I'm just defending a convenient dualism? And given that I, as a committee member, am myself part of the institutional machinery I'm evaluating, is there a coherent way for me to judge whether dissolving that boundary actually improves the design?
