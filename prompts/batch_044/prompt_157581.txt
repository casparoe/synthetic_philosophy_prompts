The standard interpretation treats the tortoise as committing a category mistake—demanding that an inference rule be treated as a premise, thereby generating an infinite regress—so that deduction needs no further justification beyond correct application. Against this, a less common view holds that the dialogue reveals a deeper problem about logical normativity: that the justification of rules like modus ponens must bottom out in procedural competence, in knowing how to apply an inference rather than knowing that a corresponding premise is true, which makes any purely propositional justification of deduction either circular or incomplete. Write an essay assessing whether Carroll's tortoise succeeds in showing that deduction cannot be justified. Weigh the category-mistake response, the primitive-norm view, and the procedural-competence interpretation. Reach a definite conclusion—does the argument expose a real limit on logical justification, or does it depend on conflating rule-following with premise-acceptance?—and defend your verdict with specific attention to whether a coherent, non-circular justification of modus ponens is possible.
