The debate over whether human life can be genuinely meaningful has been stalled for decades between absurdists—who deny cosmic justification yet defend passionate commitment to finite projects—and contemporary naturalists, who hold that meaning requires subjective fulfillment joined with objective value. Both sides reject transcendent purpose and pure hedonism alike, yet neither moves. Is this a substantive disagreement about value ontology, a verbal dispute over whether 'meaningful' functions as a binary threshold or a comparative scale, or are they answering different questions—one about cosmic justification, one about local integration—under one heading? Diagnose it by engaging what each camp actually says about engagement, value, and justification; determine whether a hidden empirical claim about the psychology of sustained commitment is doing the work, or whether the crux is an irreducible bedrock attitude toward human finitude. Given that diagnosis, could a distinction—say, between axiological density and narrative coherence, or between global and local frames of evaluation—resolve the impasse, or is permanent stalemate the only honest conclusion?
