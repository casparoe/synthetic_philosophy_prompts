I recently read an essay summarizing Frank Ramsey's position on the meaning of life, and I am trying to decide whether the account is trustworthy or subtly distorted. The author writes that Ramsey treated the question “Does life have meaning?” as structurally misplaced—analogous to asking whether a number is blue—because he held a redundancy theory of truth according to which asserting that something is true adds no content to asserting the thing itself; applied to existence, this allegedly makes “life is meaningful” either redundant or a category mistake rather than a false claim. The essay then notes that Ramsey understood belief as a habit of action calibrated by degrees of probability, and from this it derives a reconstructive thesis: what people call “finding meaning” is really just the stabilization of practical expectations across different contexts, with no single answer that unifies them. Finally, the author extends Ramsey’s view that mathematics is tautologous—true by convention rather than fact—to argue that any non-empirical answer to life’s purpose is similarly tautologous, informative only about coherence, not about the world. The essay concludes that Ramsey is therefore an unrecognized precursor to contemporary error-theory in existential philosophy: cosmic meaning is eliminated as a pseudo-question, while personal purpose is retained only as a distributed, probabilistic pattern of commitments. This sounds philosophically neat, but something about the leap from truth-redundancy to meaning-redundancy feels like a conflation, and I am unsure whether Ramsey ever applied his probabilism to existential questions in this way. Could you audit the summary? Please separate what is genuinely faithful to Ramsey’s philosophy—his pragmatism about belief, his skepticism about metaphysical pseudo-problems, his logical work on tautology—from what is a plausible simplification and from what is a subtle misrepresentation or overextension. In particular, is the analogy between truth-redundancy and meaning-redundancy fair, or does it confuse two distinct arguments? Does his probabilistic theory of belief actually support a reconstructive account of personal purpose, or is that an anachronistic projection? And does the conclusion that he anticipates modern existential error-theory follow from the accurate parts, or does it depend on the distortions? I am not looking for a bibliography; I want a conceptual accuracy check I can use to read Ramsey with the right expectations.
