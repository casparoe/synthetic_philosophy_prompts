You chair your university's Accessibility and Equity Funding Board. A proposal is before you: allocate the full annual discretionary budget of $180,000 to acquire a single cutting-edge neuro-adaptive laboratory interface for one graduate student with severe motor impairment, which would enable fully independent research for the first time, or divide it into twelve $15,000 accessibility grants for students with varied sensory and mobility needs, none reaching transformative independence but each improving daily access. The board's charter requires that allocations be defensible to every affected student as a free and equal person, reflecting a contractualist commitment, yet several members argue that refusing to aggregate welfare across students violates the board's equity mission. Draft the board's decision memo recommending either the singular allocation, the divided grants, or a structured compromise with conditions; include a concrete implementation timeline, engage the contractualist objection to pure aggregation seriously, and present the strongest reply from the aggregationist side without dismissing it.
