I'm a hard physicalist: phenomenal consciousness is literally identical to some neural/informational state, no spooky stuff. I also think phenomenal concepts are indexical-like, closer to "I" or "now" than to descriptive terms, which is why they feel isolated from physical descriptions; but I don't think that makes consciousness non-physical. On the meta-problem side, I buy the hyperactive agency detection story: our intuition that consciousness is special/non-physical comes from an evolved bias that over-attributes inner lives. I also think zombie worlds are conceivabely imaginable but metaphysically impossible, and I treat conceivability as fallible but not useless. Finally, I think that even if we fully solved the meta-problem—explained exactly why brains ask "why experience?"—we'd still be left with that first-order question genuinely unanswered; it wouldn't just dissolve as an illusion.
