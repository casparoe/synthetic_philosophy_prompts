For a philosophy discussion group, I am preparing a brief interpretive profile of Benjamin Franklin's moral perfection project and would like a consistency audit before I present. Below are the commitments I have adopted.

First, I interpret Franklin's chart of thirteen virtues as an early form of moral quantified-self: virtue is operationalized as the measurable frequency of failures in discrete domains, corrected through iterative self-monitoring, with no necessary appeal to an internal moral character or to transcendent norms.

Second, despite this behaviorist structure, the practice appears to depend on what I term reflexive moral accounting—the agent must maintain an accurate internal narrative of being a work-in-progress, for without that self-image the external tracking loses its motivational force.

Third, Franklin's deism implies that moral perfection carries no transcendent sanction or eschatological reward; therefore the entire project must be justified on purely this-worldly grounds of social utility, personal tranquility, and peer respect.

Fourth, Franklin's well-known admission that he never fully mastered Order or Humility, and that he eventually settled for the appearance of humility because pride came naturally to him, should be read not as a modest disclaimer but as a structural feature: perpetual failure in selected virtues is necessary to sustain the narrative of improvement.

Fifth, the Junto indicates that Franklin treated moral improvement as essentially collaborative and peer-validated rather than solitary, which implies that the internal narrative described in my second commitment is socially constructed rather than privately authored.

Are these five commitments jointly tenable? I ask for a precise audit: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to conflict; for each genuine tension, rank it by severity and state the least costly revision—whether abandoning the behaviorist framing, weakening the social-construction claim, or reconceiving the function of failure. I am not seeking a bibliography, a literature survey, or citations; I want to know whether my package is consistent and, if it is not, which single revision would resolve the greatest number of conflicts. Please trace exactly what each commitment commits me to, and do not treat all tensions as equally severe.
