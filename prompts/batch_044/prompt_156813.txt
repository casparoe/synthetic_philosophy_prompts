I'm defending my seminar paper—working title "The Hotel's Iteration Bound: Why Hilbert's Paradox Fails to License Actual Infinity Under Potentialist Formalization"—to our philosophy-of-mathematics working group. The thesis is that Hilbert's hotel demonstrates consistency only for potential-infinity extension rules: the "move every guest up one room" procedure is formally identical to the successor iteration that finitists already accept for arbitrary finite n, so it never requires treating the rooms or guests as a completed actual totality. Meanwhile, Cantor's diagonal argument shows that any attempt to complete such an infinite collection into an actual set immediately generates an element outside it. Taken together, I argue, they do not refute finitism but reveal that actual infinity is structurally unstable—approachable via rules, never completable. The audience is mostly second-year graduate students in metaphysics of mathematics who are set-theoretic Platonists, have taken the standard logic sequence, and regard finitism as a refuted historical curiosity; there is also a visiting logician who specializes in reverse mathematics and is deeply skeptical of metaphysical interpretations of formal results. I need a Q&A prep sheet: about eight objections I will actually face, ordered from most damaging to least damaging, each phrased exactly as someone in this room would put it, with a concise reply suitable for two minutes and an explicit note on where that reply is softest. Please include the objection I am hoping nobody asks—namely, whether my formalization of the hotel procedure implicitly smuggles in the very completed infinite totality I claim to avoid.
