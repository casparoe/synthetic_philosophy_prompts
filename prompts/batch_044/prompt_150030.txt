Having recently assumed stewardship of a small orchard now facing a destructive codling-moth infestation, I find that the usual framing of pest control—whether insects are sentient and whether direct killing is wrong—fails to capture the choice before me. A broad-spectrum treatment kills quickly but collapses local predator and pollinator networks; pheromone disruption and manual culling avoid that systemic damage yet prolong larval suffering and may simply displace harm onto future generations that replace the dead. Write an essay weighing whether ethical assessment should center on direct harm to individual insects, on the structural displacement of suffering across ecological networks, or on population-level replacement dynamics that complicate harm-based objections. Examine the strongest arguments on each side, and reach a justified conclusion about whether targeted chemical intervention is ethically permissible in a case like this, explaining which considerations ought to carry decisive weight.
