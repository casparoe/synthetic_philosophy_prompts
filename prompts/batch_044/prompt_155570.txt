Question: Douglas Hofstadter's well-known 1980s satirical essay inverts gendered language to expose masculine defaults. Analyze the argument in three parts. (a) Identify the specific linguistic feature—grammatical gender, semantic genericity, or pragmatic presupposition—that Hofstadter treats as the primary carrier of exclusion. (b) Explain why the satire reveals this feature rather than merely asserting it, with reference to the reader's own linguistic intuitions. (c) Apply this analysis to a parallel but non-obvious case: the phrase "the user" in accessibility-focused interface design, where documentation is grammatically gender-neutral and ability-neutral, yet the interaction model assumes a sighted, right-handed, neurotypical agent. Does Hofstadter's three-layer structure—grammatical form, pragmatic invisibility, rhetorical reversal—map cleanly onto this design case, or does one layer drop out? Be specific.

Student answer: "Hofstadter's primary target is grammatical gender: English pronouns 'he' and 'man' carry masculine grammatical gender that is semantically extended to cover all humans, which creates the exclusion. The satire works because readers have a pragmatic intuition that 'he' includes women, so when Hofstadter writes about 'the history of man' and then clarifies that this means only male history, he violates that intuition and exposes the contradiction. This is effective because it triggers a Gricean implicature failure—the reader expects cooperative communication but gets a deliberately uncooperative gender-specific reading. For the interface design parallel, the grammatical form is already neutral ('the user'), so the first layer is absent. The pragmatic invisibility remains: designers don't notice the able-bodied default. But the rhetorical reversal is impossible without a gendered or ability-marked grammatical form to invert. Therefore only the pragmatic layer applies, and Hofstadter's full structure collapses. The mistake in the design case is conscious omission rather than unconscious linguistic framing."
