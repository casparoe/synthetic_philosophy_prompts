Write a deadpan parody of an official bureaucratic manual — modelled on the dry, numbered clauses of a government operational guideline — issued by a fictional Ministry of Non-Aggregative Rescue. The manual should formalise the ethical view, associated with John Taurek, that in rescue scenarios the mere number of people at risk provides no moral reason to prefer saving the larger group, and that any allocation must instead rest on pairwise, non-quantitative considerations. Aim for approximately 600 to 700 words, formatted with formal headings (for instance, 'Scope and Definitions', 'The Pairwise Assessment Protocol', 'Prohibited Aggregative Methods', 'Tie-Breaking and Lottery Procedures', and 'Appeals').

The satire should remain philosophically precise: it should reflect Taurek's rejection of aggregation, his insistence that comparing one life to five does not yield a reason to save the five unless an independent pairwise consideration applies, and the problem that without aggregation there is no obvious way to break ties between groups of different sizes. Push this logic to an absurd bureaucratic extreme. Invent at least two niche procedural mechanisms that extend the view — such as a Singleton Preference Clause, a Non-Transitive Allocation Matrix, or a Group-Size Disclosure Waiver that must be signed before any headcount is revealed to rescue personnel — and include an appendix or sidebar, in the same deadpan voice, that addresses standard objections implicitly (for example, why not save fifty miners rather than one if the rescue cost is identical, what role social value or age should play, and whether the protocol collapses into arbitrariness).

The humour must arise from exaggerating real features of the position rather than inventing false ones, so that a reader familiar with the debate recognises the manual as a fair, if extreme, rendering of non-aggregative ethics. The document must never lapse into utilitarian, consequentialist, or compatibilist reasoning; it should stay rigorously consistent, even when this produces counter-intuitive rules (such as a mandatory coin flip between one trapped individual and fifty, absent any distinguishing pairwise feature).

Please write in British English. After the manual, provide a brief reflective note of no more than 150 words identifying where the parody takes liberties with Taurek's actual arguments or relies on a contested interpretation, and state clearly what argument or philosophical clarification would change your mind about the framing — that is, where the reasoning is most vulnerable and what would cause you to revise the satire's presentation of the view.
