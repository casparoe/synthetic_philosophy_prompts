Here are my intuitive verdicts on whether an agent acted intentionally across structurally varied cases, and I want to know whether they form a coherent pattern. Case one: a factory owner installs a cheaper filtration system knowing it will release a specific toxin into the river, caring only about saving money; the toxin is released. I judge that he intentionally poisoned the river. Case two: the same owner installs a different system that, as a foreseen byproduct, removes an unrelated pollutant and improves water quality; he cares only about regulatory compliance. I judge that he did not intentionally improve the water quality. Case three: a researcher deploys an autonomous drone network to map terrain, knowing the drones might accidentally deliver supplies to an isolated community but caring only about data collection; they do deliver supplies and local health improves. I judge that she intentionally improved the community's health. Case four: a hospital administrator terminates an ambulance contract in a remote region, knowing response times will lengthen and some patients will die, caring only about the budget; one patient dies. I judge that she did not intentionally cause that death. Case five: a teacher constructs an exam she knows will cause one particular student to fail and drop out, caring only about maintaining standards; the student fails and leaves. On this one I genuinely oscillate.

Is there one principle, or a tightly constrained family, that delivers the four settled judgments without ad hoc patching? Please state it precisely, map each clause to the case that motivates it, and say what it predicts for case five and for a further scenario—perhaps an AI safety system that autonomously blocks a harmful action that its human operator had foreseen but failed to prevent. If no single account can preserve all four, identify the outlier verdict and defend overturning it rather than multiplying conditions. I am especially interested in whether the harm-help asymmetry seen in cases one and two survives when the causal chain is mediated by autonomous systems or distributed across groups, and whether omission weakens intentionality for harm in the same way that directness seems to weaken it for help.
