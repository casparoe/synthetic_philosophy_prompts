As a novelist constructing a character whose moral sensibility is drawn from Michel de Montaigne's reflections on cultural difference and cruelty, I have charted his judgments across several customs, but I need to test whether his reasoning holds together or splits into competing principles. His verdicts run as follows. He regards the ritual consumption of captured enemies among certain Brazilian peoples as less barbaric than European practices of torture and execution, on the ground that the former is swift, governed by a warrior's honor code, and free of prolonged suffering, whereas the latter multiplies cruelty beneath a cloak of law and religion. He treats indigenous customs of nakedness, simple diet, and loud communal mourning as neither inferior nor superior to European dress, refined cuisine, and restrained grief, seeing each as an arbitrary product of climate and habit rather than nature's command. He condemns the burning of heretics and the violence of religious wars not for violating a universal natural law but because they exceed in cruelty the offenses they claim to punish. And he insists that his own attachment to European ways is merely the comfort of long familiarity, not proof of their objective superiority. Now imagine a society—the Aelonians—where citizens, upon reaching an agreed age, are gently and consensually euthanized in a ceremony of gratitude, then consumed by their descendants in a ritual meant to recycle nourishment and affirm generational continuity. There is no enemy-status, no violence, no physical suffering; the act is voluntary, planned, and ecologically motivated. Given the pattern above, how would this character judge the Aelonian practice? I want more than a bare yes or no: identify the candidate rules that could generate his prior judgments—perhaps one that measures practices by whether they inflict gratuitous suffering, another that distinguishes consumption of enemies from consumption of kin, and a third that treats all internally coherent customs as equally legitimate—state what each predicts for the Aelonians, offer your best guess with reasons, and propose one further hypothetical case that would best separate these rules. Please conclude with a single line beginning "Verdict:" that gives your predicted judgment and the rule you take to govern it.
