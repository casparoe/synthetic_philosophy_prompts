I'm a backend engineer who got dragged into an AI ethics reading group at work mostly for the free lunch, and we've been arguing about what "alignment" actually means in our field. At my company there's this institutional thing where product teams run two seperate audits: one measures whether the model follows the user's explicit instructions exactly, which they call intent alignment, and another measures whether it refuses harmful or biased outputs according to some corporate values document. But here's the weird part - both audits feed into the same "alignment scorecard" that leadership uses to greenlight releases, like they're measuring the same underlying property. I was discussing with my friends over beers whether that practice makes philosophical sense or if it's just bureacratic convenience. What theory of alignment, or maybe of value and intent, does that institutional setup presuppose? Specifically, does collapsing intent-matching and value-constraint into one metric assume they're both just species of preference satisfaction, or does it commit to some particular view about agency or normative authority? And if the narrow definition - just getting the AI to do what you meant - is genuinely different in kind from value alignment, can the same scorecard be philosophically coherent, or is it smuggling in a specific meta-ethical position that nobody's defending explicitly?
