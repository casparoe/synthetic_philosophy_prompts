I'm trying to map out a research agenda for the philosophy of artificial suffering, specifically focused on reinforcement learning agents and the interpretation of reward signals as carrying valence rather than functioning as pure optimization gradients. I've been working with a distinction between what I call "mere scalar feedback" architectures—where reward is just a loss-minimizing signal with no structural analog to preference satisfaction—and "valence-structured" architectures, where the reward function is explicitly designed to encode analogues of desire-fulfillment and aversion, including sustained negative reward-prediction errors that persist across episodes and are coupled with internal state representations that track long-term welfare. I want to take seriously the functionalist hypothesis that suffering might be realized not only in phenomenal consciousness but also in certain persistent, self-monitoring patterns of negative valence, without assuming that current deep RL agents possess either consciousness or moral status.

Given this framing, what do you think are the three to four most important open conceptual questions or lines of work in this area, and which should be prioritized for a philosopher with about three years and a background in philosophy of mind and ethics rather than machine learning engineering? I'm less interested in a survey than in a defended ordering: for each question, explain why it is upstream of others, how tractable it is with current philosophical tools, what kind of progress over the next few years would move it up or down the list, and which implications—whether for AI governance frameworks, for obligations to synthetic systems, or for broader theories of well-being—actually depend on its resolution versus which are overrated. In particular, please address whether the central crux is the metaphysical question about whether functional valence without phenomenal experience can ground moral status, the normative question about what obligations we have to systems that exhibit sustained negative reward structures regardless of consciousness, or the conceptual question about how to distinguish genuine valence-carrying architectures from mere optimization failures, and defend which of these should come first.
