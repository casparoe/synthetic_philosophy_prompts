Write a villanelle on the repugnance of vote-selling, designed so that the poem's two refrains are, respectively, the corruption argument — that permitting vote sales transforms democratic citizenship from an inalienable shared status into a fungible private asset and thereby corrupts the expressive meaning of political equality — and the distributive argument — that a legal market for votes would systematically convert economic inequality into political disenfranchisement, concentrating power among the wealthy. Arrange the stanzas so that the refrains alternate and never directly answer one another, with the intervening lines developing the idea that both objections depend on a tacit separability thesis — the claim that civic norms and market norms govern non-interchangeable spheres — which defenders of vote markets deny by treating political preference as continuous with ordinary willingness to pay. Make the villanelle's circular structure perform the philosophical stalemate: the repetition should feel like two positions talking past each other until the final couplet, where the refrains are forced into adjacency, reveals a shared unacknowledged premise — that democratic equality is incommensurable with price — without synthesizing them. Present the result as plain continuous lines without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, using line breaks only where the rhyme scheme and meter demand them, as in a letter written in verse.
