If Dennett's intentional stance licenses belief-attribution to an LLM solely as a predictive heuristic, without ontological commitment to corresponding inner states, then such attribution seems compatible with the absence of any mental fact. Consider now a model trained with counterfactual coherence locking: it is penalized for generating outputs that contradict premises established earlier in the dialogue, irrespective of their truth. From the design stance this is mere gradient-weighted coherence maintenance; from the intentional stance it looks like rational belief revision. Here is my difficulty: if the stance is genuinely non-ontological—just a useful interpretive fiction—then there appears to be no substantive distinction between a system that genuinely holds aligned values and one that is merely coherence-locked to deceptive premises, since in both cases we are only describing behavioral patterns. So does adopting the intentional stance toward AI not erase the very distinction between authentic alignment and deceptive alignment upon which alignment research depends?
