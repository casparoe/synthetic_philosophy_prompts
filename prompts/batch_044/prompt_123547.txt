Write a dialogue between an epiphenomenalist and an interactionist about the paradox of phenomenal judgment. Don't replay the standard "beliefs about consciousness aren't caused by consciousness" script. Instead, have them work through a specific framing: phenomenal judgments function not as descriptive reports about an inner object, but as self-referential indexicals calibrated by a subpersonal loop that tracks neural coherence rather than qualia themselves. The epiphenomenalist should argue this dissolves the paradox, since reliability depends only on the loop, not causal access to consciousness. The interactionist should respond by asking whether this model can preserve first-person authority—specifically the intuition that a subject is incorrigible about being in pain—if the referent never enters the causal chain producing the judgment. Keep it tight and adversarial: each speaker must respond to the other's actual move, not talk past them. Begin with the dialogue itself—no preface, no framing commentary outside the spoken lines, no restatement of the prompt.
