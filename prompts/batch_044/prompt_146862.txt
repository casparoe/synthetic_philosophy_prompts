I am a doctoral student working at the intersection of philosophy of mind and cognitive neuroscience, and I have become convinced that debates about global workspace theory tend to stall because participants disagree about what a successful integration of phenomenal consciousness and access consciousness would actually have to look like. Before assessing particular models, I want to establish the criteria themselves. Could you lay out the full set of desiderata that any theory attempting to reconcile phenomenal consciousness, access consciousness, and the architecture of global broadcasting must satisfy?

I need more than a checklist: for each requirement, specify the motivating phenomenon or conceptual distinction — for instance, the need to preserve a gap between there being something it is like to experience a state and that state being available for reasoning and report; the need to accommodate empirical findings about brief, masked stimuli that appear to carry structured phenomenal content, including figure-ground organization and face-level recognition, without entering sustained workspace availability (a constraint I will call binding-before-broadcast); and the need to avoid treating phenomenal consciousness as merely an unstable precursor to access. Please distinguish non-negotiable constraints from camp-specific preferences — such as whether determinism about workspace entry is required, or whether local recurrent processing can count as sufficient for phenomenal status — and identify whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable. In particular, I want to know whether accounting for rich phenomenal structure prior to broadcasting necessarily conflicts with the global integration condition central to global workspace architectures, and if one single requirement does the bulk of the work in ruling out the most common current approaches, please say which one and why. Present your answer in American English, in a structured format with brief justification for each item, and do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations; I am interested solely in the conceptual specification.
