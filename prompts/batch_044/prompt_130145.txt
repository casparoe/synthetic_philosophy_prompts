I'm on the aquesitions commitee for a small regional museum and we keep getting burned on contemporary works — we buy based on curator testimony, then visitors hate it, or we reject something because our gut said no and then it turns out to be a major piece. I want a decision procedure I can actually run in about 20 minutes before signing off on a purchase under $50k. Ground it in whatever philosophy of aesthetic testimony actually says — the autonomy thesis, whether you can learn beauty from someone else, the disagreement problem — but translate it into steps a non-philosopher can run.

The procedure should take three kinds of input in order: direct viewing (if anyone on the commitee can see/hear/experience the work), expert testimony (curator, artist statement, established critic), and secondary testimony (the expert is just relaying someone else's report, like a conservator describing affective impact from material decay). For each step say what counts as sufficient, where it stops with a hard no, and where it has to punt to a values call because theory doesn't settle it. Be honest about which steps are just dressing up a gut call.

Then run it on three proposals:
1. An abstract sound installation nobody on the commitee has heard live; the curator says it produces "a specific kind of temporal disorientation that rewards patience."
2. A figurative painting where half the commitee finds it emotionally moving and half finds it kitsch, with a well-known critic arguing its sentimentality is precisely its conceptual point.
3. A work where the curator's testimony is entirely second-hand — she's passing along a conservator's report that the work's unintended material fragility creates an aesthetic of decay.

One of these should come back with a verdict the commitee probably won't like. Also flag exactly what the procedure can't do.

After the procedure and cases, give me a brief structured summary in a table with columns: Case | Procedure Verdict | Judgment Call Flagged? | One-line reason. Then wrap the whole response in a JSON object with fields "procedure_steps" (array of strings), "case_results" (array of objects with keys "case_name", "verdict", "flag"), and "limits" (array of strings).
