I'm a referee for a competitive programming league, and we need a reusable audit procedure for proposed two-player deterministic perfect-information games. Specifically, I want to operationalize the strategy-stealing assumption into a checklist my team can run on a ruleset: the ordered preconditions that must hold for the argument to apply, what counts as sufficient evidence for each, the exact logical conclusion permitted at each branch—whether it rules out a guaranteed second-player win or genuinely establishes a first-player advantage—and the precise points where the procedure must halt and defer to human judgment because of continuous values, simultaneous resolution, or irreversible state changes that break the strategic isomorphism. Apply the procedure to three proposals: (A) a symmetric grid-capture game with an explicit pass move; (B) an asymmetric auction-style format where the opening action permanently depletes a shared resource pool; and (C) a symmetric format where the argument formally applies but experienced competitors consistently report a first-mover bias the proof misses. For case C, identify what the procedure fails to capture—computational complexity asymmetry, tempo effects, or hidden structural bias—and explain why the league will have to overrule the checklist with an undesirable but honest judgment call.
