I'm attempting to articulate a single normative principle that governs the boundary between formative coercion and rational autonomy in education, guided partly by Locke's writings on education but extending beyond them. Here are my verdicts on concrete pedagogical situations, which I want tested for consistency.

Case 1: A tutor beats a child for mispronouncing Latin, aiming to instill grammatical precision through fear. Verdict: clearly wrong; the method destroys the affectionate disposition toward learning that a sound education requires.

Case 2: A tutor withholds all correction from a child who asserts that the sun is a small lantern near the earth, reasoning that autonomy requires never imposing belief. Verdict: also wrong; the child is left with falsehood that will eventually undermine practical reasoning.

Case 3: A tutor gently redirects a child's false belief about astronomy through guided observation, but deliberately defers explanation of death, divine punishment, and political sovereignty until the child can reason about them calmly. Verdict: correct; the deferral protects the developing rational faculty without deception.

Case 4: A tutor teaches the child to scrutinize every adult claim about politics and property, yet demands unthinking obedience in prayer and moral ritual. Verdict: wrong, and structurally parallel to Case 3—both defer or compartmentalize complex material—yet I feel Case 3 is permissible and Case 4 is not.

Case 5: A tutor invents a fictional moral fable to prevent a child from lying, knowing the narrative is not literally true but that the child lacks the conceptual apparatus for abstract moral argument. Verdict: permissible, even if the child later discovers the fiction.

My central tension: Cases 3 and 4 both involve withholding or deferring content for developmental reasons, yet I judge them differently. Meanwhile Cases 1 and 5 both rely on non-rational influence (fear versus fiction), but I reject one and accept the other.

Is there a coherent principle—call it calibrated epistemic guardianship—that captures all five verdicts? If so, state it precisely, identify the clause that addresses each case, predict its verdict on a further case (for instance, a tutor who uses immersive virtual simulations to teach empathy without exposing the child to real suffering), and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon.
