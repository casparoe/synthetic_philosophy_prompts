In discussions of Otto Neurath's ISOTYPE project and his defence of a unified physicalist science, I keep encountering the maxim "words divide, pictures unite"—often deployed to argue that visual notation can overcome the ambiguity and cultural particularity of verbal language, and therefore that Neurath should have abandoned his insistence that protocol sentences be linguistically formulated. Before I assess whether that argument works, I want to understand exactly what the maxim amounts to. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses it might express—such as claims about semantic determinacy, cross-linguistic portability, emotional loading, logical syntax, pedagogical efficacy, or the possibility of replacing verbal reports in a physicalist protocol language—indicating for each whether it is trivially true, philosophically substantive and defensible within Neurath's own framework, or false? Please present the options as a menu rather than selecting a single interpretation, and say which readings, if any, would genuinely support displacing verbal protocol sentences with picture languages inside Neurath's "boat" of unified science. Where you draw on specific features of ISOTYPE or Neurath's philosophy of science, please separate what is well established from interpretations that remain genuinely contested among scholars of logical empiricism, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. I should be grateful if you could respond in British English.
