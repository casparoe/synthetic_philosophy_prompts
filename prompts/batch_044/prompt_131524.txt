I've ended up convinced that representational realism about neural nets is basically right, but only if you restrict it to features pulled out by sparse autoencoders rather than raw neuron activations or attention heads. The idea is that SAEs actually recover the underlying causal variables the model is using, while neurons are just noisy superpositions of them. So representations are real, but only in the reconstructed dictionary, not in the weights or raw activations themselves. What would actually count against this restricted view? I want defeaters sorted from fatal to awkward - things like evidence that SAEs just impose structure rather than discover it, or that the reconstructed features don't causally drive behaviour downstream, or that superposition is actually the true representational format and disentangling destroys meaningful information. Also be honest about whether this view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability, like saying representations are real wherever we can succesfully interpret them. Is that insulation a problem for a claim of this kind? Don't just recycle the standard anti-realist line; tell me what defenders of this specific SAE-restricted realism would themselves concede is genuinely damaging, and rank it.
