I sit on our city's autonomous vehicle pilot board and we vote thursday. One applicant, RecogniDrive, wants to deploy cars that run a 'slave-consciousness navigation layer' drawn straight from Hegel's master-slave dialectic. Thier design treats the car as the slave that only gains self-certainty through work and fear of death, and pedestrians as masters whose recognition it must seek. So the vehicle won't cross an intersection until it detects an explicit acknowledgment — a wave, nod, or sustained eye contact — because without that reciprocal recognition it remains just an object dominating space unilaterally. Safety numbers show lower collision rates but also deadlock at busy crosswalks where pedestrians don't signal. Half the board thinks this is a misreading of Hegel since recognition in the Phenomenology comes through labor and transformation, not waiting for a hand wave; the other half thinks the asymmetry is the philosophical point, and stripping the protocol treats people like obstacles. I need a memo for circulation recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions — include a concrete rollout timeline and conditions if that's your call. Treat the Hegelian framing seriously as a design principle, not marketing fluff, address the strongest objection to your position honestly, and write it so RecogniDrive's engineers could actually respond. Don't ask me to read Hegel; work with the idea as laid out here.
