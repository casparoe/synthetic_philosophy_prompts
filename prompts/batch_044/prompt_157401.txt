Please argue that Simone Weil's critique of political parties—her claim that parties generate collective passions which suppress individual judgment through a kind of social gravity (pesanteur)—applies with greater, not lesser, force to modern decentralized ideological clusters such as rationalist forums, prediction-market tribes, and effective-altruist networks. Build your case on the absence of formal membership, charters, or bureaucratic procedure in these clusters: precisely because they lack the institutional friction that Weil thought could occasionally brake collective delusion, their gravitational pull operates unchecked. Argue that this absence makes them more susceptible to the pathologies she diagnosed—manufactured consensus, preference falsification, and loyalty overriding perception—rather than immunizing them. Do not request citations or reading lists; develop the argument from her conceptual framework and from the dynamics of online epistemic communities as they are generally understood.
