I am preparing a short presentation for a philosophy of decision theory reading group, and I want to open with a poem that captures a specific conceptual point about imprecise probabilities that we kept circling back to: that ambiguity is sometimes not a temporary gap in information but a stable structural feature of belief, best represented by a credal set—an interval of probabilities that refuses to collapse to a single point. I would like you to write a villanelle of exactly nineteen lines, with two refrains that serve as the lower and upper bounds of such an interval. The first refrain should express a refusal to commit below some evidential threshold, and the second a refusal to commit above another; both should rhyme with each other and with the other A-lines according to the standard villanelle scheme.

The philosophical content should do structural work through the form itself. Through the first five tercets, the two refrains should circulate independently, never occupying the same stanza together, so that the reader experiences the bounds as separate, persistent voices that do not converge. Then, in the final quatrain, they must be forced into adjacency—occupying line eighteen and line nineteen—so that the poem’s turn is precisely the moment when both bounds are held simultaneously. That adjacency should not resolve the ambiguity into a precise value; rather, it should show that institutionalizing both bounds at once leaves the interval intact, perhaps even emphasizing that the space between them is where the belief genuinely lives. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with credal sets, the Ellsberg paradox, or maximin decision rules recognizes the concept being modeled, but please do not include bibliographies, citations, or references to specific papers, and avoid naming obscure formalisms that are not widely recognized. The tone should be contemplative and formal rather than didactic, and the ambiguity should remain genuinely open in the closing couplet, with the interval still standing unresolved.
