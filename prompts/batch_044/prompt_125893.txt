Two regulars in my philosophy discussion group got stuck arguing about a Sleeping Beauty variant, and I’d like a point-by-point adjudication. The setup is standard: Sunday fair-coin flip; heads means one Monday awakening with amnesia; tails means Monday and Tuesday awakenings with amnesia in between. Beauty is woken and asked her credence in heads. Mira takes the thirder line: there are three indistinguishable centered wake-up situations—Heads-Monday, Tails-Monday, Tails-Tuesday—so symmetry assigns each weight 1/3, hence heads gets 1/3. Jonas objects that this ignores world-identity: the prior over worlds is 1/2, being awake is guaranteed in both worlds (once in heads, twice in tails), so applying Bayes correctly leaves her credence at 1/2.

Mira then introduces what she calls the Epistemic Singularity Constraint: if two centered perspectives share a causal origin but are separated by an intervention that wipes all internal access to that origin, they should be treated as independent sample points for credence allocation, not as redundant copies of one sample. Thus the Tuesday awakening is not just a repetition inside the tails world; it is a genuinely separate epistemic position that must carry its own 1/3 share.

Jonas replies that the constraint is ad hoc and yields an absurdity: if the tails protocol ran for a thousand days with daily wipes, Beauty should approach near-certainty of tails just from being awake on day 999, even though the coin was flipped only once. The constraint, he says, mistakes epistemic inaccessibility for ontological independence.

Mira answers that the constraint is bounded: it applies only when the agent lacks any diachronic marker whatsoever. In a thousand-day run there would be bodily wear, calendar cues, and so on. Here Beauty genuinely cannot distinguish Monday from Tuesday, so the three centered worlds remain symmetric.

Jonas closes by noting that even within tails, Beauty knows she is either on Monday or Tuesday, so she can partition that world internally. By treating T-Mon and T-Tue as separate samples while keeping H-Mon singular, she applies an asymmetry that has no basis: if she can draw internal partitions inside tails, she can also ask whether she is in heads or tails, and the answer should depend on the single coin flip, not on how many subdivisions she creates inside one world.

Then state who is more right overall and point out anything important both miss. Keep the whole response under 350 words, using one concise paragraph per rated move and a final paragraph for the overall verdict.
