Write a dialogue between two sharp people—call them A and B—debating whether Montaigne's essay on cannibals is genuine cultural relativism or just normativity in disguise. A defends the relativist read: Montaigne only calls Europeans barbaric by their own Christian lights, and comparing Tupinambá ritual to European religious wars shows there's no neutral Archimedean point. B pushes back that if all judgment is embedded, Montaigne can't consistently rank European cruelty as worse without smuggling in a hidden universal about human dignity or natural law. A should then hit B with a weirder move: Montaigne's skepticism about reason applies reflexively, so his worse isn't a theoretical claim but a performative shock meant to destabilize the reader's confidence. B asks whether that saves Montaigne or just makes his view unfalsifiable—if every counterexample is just rhetoric, what's left? They should actually respond to each other, not talk past. Keep it casual, like a late-night argument over coffee, and don't turn either into a strawman. Maybe slip in a typo like cannibalsim.
