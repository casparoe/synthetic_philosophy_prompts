ok so i'm trying to map out where i actually stand on the value of information for a discussion group and want to check if it's one coherent package or i'm sneaking in contradictions. here's roughly my profile:

(1) i accept Good's theorem in the standard form — if information is literally free and you can update your beliefs and then act differently, then gathering it before deciding can't hurt your expected utility and usually helps.

(2) but i also think rational ignorance is a real thing — like, refusing a free genetic screening for an untreatable condition makes sense, because knowing you're going to die in five years with no cure available just makes the remaining years worse.

(3) my usual reconciliation is that "free" in the theorem hides cognitive and emotional processing costs, so the information isn't actually costless once you factor in the psychological damage of knowing.

(4) at the same time i think decision theory should model agents with stable preference orderings over world-states, not preferences that shift depending on what you happen to believe — so i don't think we should bake "feeling sad about knowing" into the utility function as a hidden information-cost.

(5) i also think a lot of popular intuition pumps about "knowledge is always power" are misleading precisely because they ignore these hidden downsides, but i don't think Good's result itself is just an intuition pump — it's a formal proof, not a rhetorical trick.

(6) and i hold that if there's genuinely no action available after learning — like the gene is untreatable and you can't change anything — then the information value collapses to zero, not negative, which saves (1) from being falsified by (2).

so my question: is this actually consistent, or am i helping myself to something in (3) that (4) rules out? also is there a tension between (5) and (1) that i'm glossing over, or does that dissolve? please rank the tensions by severity, say for each one what has to give and what the cheapest revision is, and flag any pair that only looks like a problem but isn't. don't just list them all as equally bad — i want an audit, not a lecture. also fwiw i don't have web access so don't ask me to look up a specific paper citation.
