There's this pattern I keep noticing in debates where both people actually share all the same data and even agree on basic logic, but they diverge because they apply totally different "filters" on what counts as good evidence -- one treats a vivid anecdote as demolishing a statistical study, the other treats the same anecdote as noise. They both update consistently inside their own filter, so they never converge, and here's the weird part: neither takes the persistent disagreement as a reason to doubt their filter; they just say the other person has bad taste in evidance. It feels near Aumann-style agreement failure, but the split isn't over the world-state prior -- it's over the prior about what kinds of updates are legitimate in the first place. Does this specific deadlock have an established name? I'm especially interested in whether there's a concept that separates "disagreeing about beliefs" from "disagreeing about the rules for belief-formation" when both sides are internally rational, or if I should just treat it as deep disagreement with extra steps.
