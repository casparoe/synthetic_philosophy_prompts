I'm working through where the physical, design, and intentional stances apply and whether they track genuinely real patterns, and I'd like to know whether my judgments cohere. A pendulum supports only the physical stance: there are law-governed regularities, but attributing beliefs or goals is pure fiction, and refusing the intentional stance misses nothing real. A chess-playing program robustly supports the design stance—its evaluation function was engineered for checkmate—but I also think some intermediate representations warrant a derivative intentional gloss, as when we say it sees a hidden threat, though genuine belief is absent. A bacterial colony navigating a chemical gradient supports the design stance collectively—the group is functionally organized—but I deny that the intentional stance applies even derivatively to individual cells or to the colony as a belief-holder. A hurricane supports only the physical stance; talk of its goal of dissipating energy is purely metaphorical, not a missed real pattern. Here is the tension: I think a human being in deep sleep, with no current behavioral or representational patterns expressing belief, still permits the intentional stance—treating them as agents with dormant beliefs—whereas the bacterial colony, also a functional system lacking current expressive patterns, does not. That seems to anchor the stance to the kind of system rather than to exhibited patterns, which conflicts with my refusal to extend it to the colony. Is there a coherent principle governing when each stance captures real patterns rather than useful fictions? If so, what does it predict for two unsettled cases: a market price mechanism that coordinates behavior without any designer or collective mind, and a large language model generating coherent philosophical prose—should it be treated like the sleeping brain, like the bacterial colony, or as a case that forces revision of one of my earlier verdicts?
