After a long and somewhat circular argument with a friend about whether deception alone can void consent or whether only coercion and incompetence carry that weight, I have been trying to clarify my own view by constructing a graded taxonomy of vitiation that treats these three conditions as interacting rather than isolated. Rather than asking for a general theory, I want to see how they blend at the margins. Please rank the following five hybrid scenarios—each combining two or three of these vices in a deliberately non-obvious way—according to how severely they undermine the validity of consent, using an ordinal scale of 1 (fully valid consent) to 5 (entirely nullified), and assign each a precise numerical score out of 10 with a brief justification for its placement relative to the others.

The cases are as follows. Case A: a fully competent patient consents to a risky surgical intervention after the surgeon describes outcomes using technically accurate but statistically misleading framing—specifically, defining success in a way that excludes meaningful functional recovery—without applying any explicit pressure and without withholding any fact that the patient directly asks about. Case B: an employee signs an arbitration agreement embedded deep within a forty-page standard-form contract, having been told the document is non-negotiable and routine; her supervisor never issues an explicit threat, but strongly implies that refusal will be noted in her personnel file, leaving her cognitively intact yet economically constrained and anxious. Case C: a psychologically competent but financially desperate research participant agrees to a study described only as involving mild stress, unaware that the protocol is specifically designed to trigger her documented social anxiety through structured exclusion tasks, in exchange for compensation she urgently needs for rent. Case D: a cognitively intact adult consents to an intimate act within a romantic relationship in which the other party has, over many months, systematically cultivated emotional dependency such that refusal now feels equivalent to catastrophic abandonment, despite the absence of any explicit threat and despite no false statements having been made. Case E: a voter casts a ballot after exposure to a deliberately ambiguous campaign message engineered to exploit a well-documented cognitive bias that she does not recognize in herself, with no material coercion present and no factual falsehoods asserted. For each case, please indicate whether deception, coercion, or incompetence is doing the primary invalidating work, whether the vices operate additively or multiplicatively, and whether you think consent invalidation here involves a sharp threshold or is always a matter of continuous degree.
