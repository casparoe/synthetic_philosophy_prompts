Write an essay on whether panpsychism carries any actionable ethical implications regarding large-scale physical processes. The controversy turns on how one resolves the combination problem: if micro-experiences remain unbound "phenomenal noise" without macro-integration, they may be ethically analogous to background radiation—present, but not harm-capable—whereas an aggregationist view holds that every negative-valence instance increments a cosmic moral cost regardless of binding. Weigh the argument that stellar fusion and entropy generation are therefore ethically neutral against the claim that reducing such processes minimizes unbound suffering. Reach a conclusion: does the absence of macro-subjects at the fundamental level render panpsychist ethics null, or does aggregationism still obligate us to treat physical constants as parameters of a universal suffering budget? Defend your verdict with explicit reasoning about which feature of the combination problem does the moral work.
