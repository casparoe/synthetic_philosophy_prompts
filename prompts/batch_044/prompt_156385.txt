I'm trying to work out what distinguishes a legitimate multi-level explanation from an illegitimate skyhook or an overly greedy reduction, and my intuitions about particular cases are not obviously consistent. Here are my verdicts. When evolutionary biologists explain the origin of the eye through successive small adaptive steps, I regard that as a paradigmatic crane and fully legitimate. When someone explains consciousness by invoking integrated information theory, defining consciousness as integrated information without deriving that integration from lower-level physics, I still think this is a legitimate crane rather than a skyhook, because the theory offers a formal structure that can be empirically tested even if it is not yet fully grounded in physics. But when someone says consciousness simply emerges once neural complexity crosses a threshold and that no further explanation is either possible or necessary, I treat that as a skyhook and reject it. When a historian explains the fall of a particular empire by citing both institutional decay and a specific drought, I think that is appropriate multi-causal explanation operating at the right level. When a physicist insists that the only real explanation of that empire's fall is the quantum-state evolution of every atom involved, I regard that as greedy reductionism that misses the relevant causal structure. When evolutionary psychologists explain altruistic behavior through reciprocal cooperation models, I accept that as a valid crane. Yet when they explain the specific normative content of Kantian moral philosophy as nothing but an evolutionary strategy for signaling cooperation, I think that is greedy reductionism, collapsing a normative domain into a descriptive one. When computer scientists explain chess-playing algorithms by referring to search trees and evaluation functions without mentioning transistor physics, I think that is exactly the right kind of non-reductive but non-skyhook explanation. But when philosophers explain mathematical knowledge by positing a Platonic realm that causally interacts with human minds, I treat that as a classic skyhook. I also think that explaining a thermostat's temperature regulation by attributing to it a goal or desire is not a skyhook, even though it does not reduce the thermostat to its physical parts, whereas explaining human artistic creativity solely through sexual selection mechanisms strikes me as greedy reduction that eliminates the phenomenon it purports to explain.

Is there one principle that captures all of these distinctions—perhaps involving the availability of intermediate mechanisms, the preservation of explanatory autonomy at intermediate levels, or some criterion about empirical tractability—and if so, what does it predict for a case I have not mentioned, such as explaining psychiatric depression primarily through inflammatory cytokine activity while largely bracketing psychological and social factors? Or do my verdicts contain an irreconcilable tension, particularly between my willingness to accept integrated information theory for consciousness without full physical grounding and my rejection of evolutionary explanations for moral norms? If a simpler principle forces me to overturn one verdict, which should it be, and why?
