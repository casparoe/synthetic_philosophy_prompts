The next branches ask whether geometric objects are treated as mental constructions, as descriptions of actual physical motion, or as linguistic-logical definitions; and whether the failure to square the circle is seen as a principled impossibility, a contingent limitation of tools, or as actually resolved in Hobbes's own constructions. Every leaf must state its most uncomfortable commitment in one sentence. Flag any pair of leaves the branching questions fail to separate. Present the tree immediately, with branches labeled by the diagnostic questions and leaves named or marked 'unnamed', then follow with concise reasoning on why these distinctions matter and which logical combinations remain unoccupied. Lead with the tree itself and then the reasoning, with no preamble, no commentary before the tree, and no restatement of the question.
