I'm preparing a ninety-minute seminar for advanced undergraduates who have taken an introductory course in philosophy of mind but have no background in psychoanalysis or clinical psychology. The session must address the conceptual coherence of self-deception: specifically, whether an agent can simultaneously hold a belief and cause herself to withhold that belief from her own conscious assessment, given that ordinary deception requires the deceiver to know what the deceived does not. I do not want a lecture; I want a teaching plan. Lay out the dependency-ordered sequence in which the conceptual pieces must be introduced—starting either from the coherence puzzle or from an example, and defend that ordering—so that each step prepares the ground for the next without circularity. Identify the single carrying case or scenario that should anchor the discussion: it must involve a belief that is sustained against available counter-evidence by a motivation the agent partly recognizes but does not fully acknowledge, and it must lack the features of simple ignorance, wishful thinking, or outright lying to another person; explain what properties make this case pedagogically suitable. Then map the predictable misconceptions in the order they will arise—probably that self-deception is merely weak will, then that it requires a literally divided self with two independent minds, then that it collapses into straightforward irrationality and is therefore conceptually unremarkable—and specify the precise point in your sequence where each should be defused, together with the distinction or counter-example that does the work. Design one closing check, in the form of a brief hypothetical scenario or direct question, that would reveal whether the students have grasped the coherence problem rather than merely conceding that people are sometimes irrational. Finally, state explicitly what parts of the broader debate—such as Freudian repression mechanisms or neurocognitive compartmentalization models—must be left out at this level, and indicate how you will flag that omission to the students without undermining the conceptual point they have just learned.
