(2) i think care is a form of skilled practical knowledge, closer to craft or phronisis than to emotional labor, and its normative structure is irreducible — no greedy reduction to evolutionary kin-selection or neurochemistry explains it away. (3) partiality is defensible on its own terms: i owe more to my child or my neighbor than to distant strangers, and that doesnt reduce to bias. (4) but i also want care ethics to inform institutional design — universal access to care networks, elder support, healthcare — which seems to require abstract rules that apply across cases. (5) i reject gender essentialism; care isnt a "feminine" virtue but a distributed human capacity. (6) i think the Smithian impartial spectator is useless here, since caring requires leaning into particular attachments rather than stepping back for critical distance.
