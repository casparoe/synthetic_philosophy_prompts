I am preparing a seminar comparing civic obligation in permanently neutral republics—Switzerland’s militia tradition, Sweden’s revived conscription, Finland’s total defense model—with the logic of professionalized forces elsewhere. The specific conceptual knot I want students to inhabit is this: in states that maintain universal conscription despite having no plausible prospect of deployment, the draft appears to function not as military preparation but as a ritualized sharing of vulnerability that sustains democratic equality, so that shifting to an all-volunteer corps risks converting citizens into clients of security rather than co-authors of neutrality. Would you write a short story that dramatizes this tension through a single character facing the choice between mandatory reserve service and paying the exemption tax that simultaneously funds the professional corps and the welfare state? Render the narrative in continuous prose only—no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables—written as a unified, letter-like account in which the philosophical stakes emerge from the character’s lived situation rather than from explicit argument.
