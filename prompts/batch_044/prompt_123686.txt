Hey, I'm prepping a taxonomy for my grad seminar on the procreation asymmetry—y'know, the idea that we have strong reason not to make suffering people but no corresponding reason to make happy ones. I want it built as a decision tree with concrete case-nodes, not just a list of named theories, so students can place themself without knowing the labels.

Start from this: you accept there's an asymmetry between the moral status of creating a sufferer vs creating a happy person. First split—wether you think the asymmetry is absolute, or does it depend on expected welfare falling below or above some threshold?

Then split on explanation. Is it harm-based (only harms need justification, benefits are optional), rights-based (potential people lack a claim to existence), or expectation-based (creating is an act we evaluate differently from omitting)?

Now inject the weird case: what about potential beings whose welfare is structurally netural—minds in a pure informational state without affect, neither flourishing nor suffering? Does the asymmetry extend, collapse, or reverse there?

For each leaf, name the position if it's actually defended (classic anti-natalist denial, total-view rejection, etc.), otherwise mark 'unnamed/unstable'. Note any pairs your tree can't seperate—I think harm-based and rights-based person-affecting views might blur in practice. Also flag unoccupied combinations, like accepting the asymmetry but rejecting all three explanations above: incoherent or just unexplored?

Aim for about 6-8 leaves, audience is grad students who know basic ethics but not population ethics in depth. Don't just lay out options neutrally—commit to which empty branches are genuinely incoherent versus merely unpopular, and defend that call. Keep it focused, no reading lists.
