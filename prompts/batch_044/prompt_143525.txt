I am designing a taxonomy for a graduate metaphysics seminar that sorts participants by their actual commitments on dispositions, specifically through the interaction of finking and masking with composite and artificial systems, rather than by the labels they prefer. The taxonomy should take the form of a decision tree with four to six branch points. Each node must be a concrete scenario or a yes/no commitment that a student can answer without knowing which philosophical position it corresponds to.

Start from the root commitment: 'There are genuine dispositions that can exist unmanifested.' From there, branch through the following distinctions: (a) whether a standard conditional analysis survives a fink case involving an internal interrupter; (b) whether a mask—an external or internal blocker that prevents manifestation without destroying the base property—removes the disposition or merely suppresses its display; (c) whether a composite entity, such as an institution with procedural veto rules or an artificial system with an override circuit, can possess a disposition that is systematically finked or masked by one of its own parts or sub-routines; (d) whether dispositional essentialism about properties like fragility or solubility remains tenable if masking is widespread across both natural and artificial domains.

The leaves must include at least the following named positions or coherent combinations: naive conditional analysis; bare dispositional realism, treating dispositions as irreducible properties; mask-tolerant dispositional realism, holding that dispositions survive masking but are defeated by finking; anti-realist eliminativism about dispositions in favour of categorical bases alone; collective or pluralist dispositionalism, according to which dispositions genuinely belong to composites and are not reducible to parts; and the hybrid view that dispositions exist only where neither finking nor masking is structurally guaranteed.

For each path through the tree, show the sequence of answers that leads to the leaf. Then, map the full logical space of answer combinations: identify which combinations are occupied by the leaves above, and for every unoccupied combination provide a brief diagnosis—whether it is incoherent (for example, accepting bare dispositionalism while denying that any property can survive masking), unattractive (for example, eliminativism restricted only to artificial systems), or merely unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate given the cases used—particularly where bare dispositionalism and mask-tolerant realism converge on standard physical examples.

The concrete cases should include at least one artificial-system scenario (for example, a robotic arm with a safety override that activates only on contact, preventing the manifestation of a grasping disposition) and one institutional or collective scenario (for example, a committee with a formal disposition to approve funding that is systematically masked by a mandatory quorum rule). Do not include bibliographies, citation requests, or reading lists. Do not ask for quotations from specific texts.

Please present your response in British English, using British spelling and punctuation (e.g., behaviour, analyse, programme).

Structure the response in the following named sections, in this order, with approximate lengths:
