I'm on the advisory board for this writer's residency we run in Lisbon—I'm a novelist, so mostly I'm there to read the fiction submissions and pretend I understand budgets. Anyway we've got a concrete mess. One of our fellows, a german writer, refused to tip cafe staff and kept jaywalking and skipping queues. She says these are just social choreography, not moral obligations, and that visitors shouldn't be forced to follow local conventions that have no universal claim; she even called herself a "normative tourist" sampling practices without subscribing to them. Now our local partners want her formally reprimanded or removed, while half the board thinks we're being provincial and should defend her right to opt out. I need a short memo for tuesday's meeting—recommend whether we should amend our guest charter to treat everyday conventions like tipping, queuing, and jaywalking as conditions of stay, or whether we should reject that and protect visitor autonomy. The reasoning should engage her position seriously, not just restate "when in Rome," and should honestly lay out the strongest consideration for whichever side loses. No citations or reading lists, please—half the board can't search and I don't want to fake references. Just a clear recommendation with a time frame for implementation if we go with it. Also, try to seperate the idea of being a respectful guest from being subject to local norms, since that distinction seems to be where the argument actually lives.
