I'm working through a passage from Descartes's First Meditation (1641), which I can only paraphrase from memory: he observes that the very thoughts and images he now takes himself to have in waking life could, for all he can tell from within the experience, be occurring in a dream, because there is no internal mark or sensory feature that is present in wakefulness and necessarily absent in sleep. From this he infers that if no such intrinsic indicator exists, then his present belief that he is awake lacks the kind of certainty he demands for knowledge.

I want to understand this in relation to the Sleeping Beauty problem, but not in the usual textbook way. In the standard setup, Beauty is put to sleep Sunday, a fair coin is flipped, and she is awakened Monday if heads and on Monday and Tuesday (with intervening amnesia) if tails. The puzzle concerns her self-locating credence upon awakening: should she assign 1/3 to heads, 1/2, or something else?

Here is the specific comparison that confuses me. Descartes seems to be running an argument from the absence of an internal distinguishing feature: because he cannot, from the first-person perspective, mark his current state as "waking" rather than "dreaming," he should withhold full assent. Sleeping Beauty, analogously, cannot from her first-person perspective upon awakening mark her current state as "Monday-only" rather than "Monday-or-Tuesday-in-the-tails-world." One might think this parallel supports a "halfer" intuition: just as Descartes should not boost his confidence in being awake beyond what the absence of a marker permits, Beauty should not boost her confidence in heads beyond 1/2, since her centered evidence is identical across all awakenings.

But I suspect the analogy may collapse once we notice a structural difference in the background conditions. Descartes is entertaining a skeptical hypothesis—an undetectable, globally deceptive mechanism (the dream, or an evil demon)—for which he has no independent causal story. Beauty, by contrast, is embedded in a precisely specified causal protocol: she knows the coin is fair, knows the awakening rules, and knows that duplication is externally orchestrated rather than epistemically deceptive. So her ignorance is not "might this entire framework be false?" but "where am I within a framework I accept?" 

My question is whether there is nevertheless a deeper principle about indexical ignorance that survives this disanalogy. Specifically, does the absence of an intrinsic temporal index in Beauty's centered world function the same way as the absence of an intrinsic "waking" index in Descartes's centered world, such that both cases demand a uniform prior over indistinguishable centered possibilities? Or does the fact that Beauty's duplication is known and mechanistic mean that her credence should be calculated over possible worlds weighted by the objective chance of the coin, rendering the first-person "unmarked" feature epistemically irrelevant—whereas Descartes, lacking any such weighting mechanism, is rationally compelled to suspend judgment?

Please help me understand exactly where the comparison holds and where it breaks, particularly regarding whether the absence of an internal distinguishing feature should affect credence differently when the duplication is externally orchestrated and probabilistically weighted versus when it is skeptically hypothesized without any background causal structure. I am especially interested in whether treating each awakening as a "centered world" with no intrinsic temporal marker introduces a form of self-locating belief that is genuinely analogous to Descartes's first-person epistemic position, or whether that framing illegitimately imports a skeptical structure into a case that is actually about location within a single, non-deceptive reality.
