I have recently become interested in why my own judgments feel like unified wholes rather than mere co-occurrences of representations—why thinking that Socrates is wise is one belief, not two simultaneous ones. This led me back to Bradley’s regress and the problem of propositional unity. For decades the dispute has seemed frozen: one camp dissolves the regress by treating relations as internal to their terms or by invoking mental acts of synthesis, while the other dissolves it by denying that relations require any further relating, insisting that “togetherness” needs no explanation. Each side accuses the other of begging the question about whether explanation must terminate, and the literature feels like it is replaying the same moves. Is this stall substantive—perhaps a hidden disagreement about whether a metaphysical structure of reality differs from the cognitive structure of judgment—or is it verbal, with one group asking about the ontology of facts and the other about how minds combine representations? Could a formal result in the logic of relations, or an empirical finding about conceptual combination, actually move it, or is it a bedrock clash about what counts as an explanation? Diagnose the disagreement using the actual commitments of each camp, and tell me what, if anything, should count as progress.
