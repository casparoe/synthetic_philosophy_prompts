In Hofstadter's careenium model—where low-level particles carom and high-level patterns he calls simmballs emerge—there is a persistent ambiguity about whether those symbolic patterns exert downward causal pressure on the substrate or merely summarize it from above. Imagine a philosophy-of-mind research group with two years of funding wants to settle, or at least sharply delimit, whether symbolic structures push the substrate around in this specific framework. Rank the following three research directions by how much they deserve that funding, treating them as competing priorities rather than complementary ones: (A) formalizing whether recursive self-modeling loops within a careenium alter the collision-statistics of the substrate in ways that cannot be predicted from substrate-level rules alone; (B) analyzing whether the explanatory indispensability of simmball-level descriptions entails that they are causally efficacious, or whether predictive sufficiency can be separated from causal power; and (C) constructing a hypothetical read-only observer layer—a structure that tracks simmball patterns without writing back to the substrate—to test whether downward causation disappears when the loop is broken. For each rank, explain in one or two sentences why it lands there: which questions are upstream of which, what kind of result (formal proof, analogical model, or conceptual distinction) would actually move the needle, and which line you would deprioritize on the grounds that resolving it would leave the core substrate-symbol relation unchanged.
