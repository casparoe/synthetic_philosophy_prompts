I am preparing material for an advanced epistemology seminar, and I have grown tired of the standard textbook rehearsals of Plato's Meno problem — why knowledge ought to be prized above mere true belief if both deliver the correct result. Rather than another comparison of familiar theories, I want you to write out the full specification of what an adequate answer to this value problem must actually achieve. Give me the complete list of desiderata: the phenomena it has to explain (including, but not limited to, the differential stability of knowledge under modal variation and its distinctive role in testimonial and collective epistemic practices), the distinctions it must not collapse (such as that between possessing knowledge and merely being justified, or between individual and distributed cognitive achievement), the results from cognitive science or social epistemology it cannot flatly contradict, and the counter-cases — including Gettier-style scenarios and lucky perceptual beliefs — it must accommodate without reducing knowledge to true belief plus an ornamental addition. For each requirement, state the motivating consideration and mark whether it is genuinely non-negotiable or merely a preference of a particular camp (for instance, whether the surplus value must be intrinsic rather than instrumental, or whether the explanation must avoid any appeal to practical success or reliability). I also want you to identify whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — particularly whether one can consistently hold both that knowledge is more valuable in every possible case and that there exist situations in which justified true belief carries precisely the same practical weight. Keep the weight firmly on the criteria and their logical relations rather than on scoring existing theories, though a brief note on which well-known answers fail which specific desiderata is welcome. Please write in British English, do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and treat this as a question that should be answerable without access to specialised archives or external sources.
