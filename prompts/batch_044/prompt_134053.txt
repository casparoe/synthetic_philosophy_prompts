You are a professional philosopher of science with deep familiarity in metaphysics, evolutionary explanation, and the philosophy of mind. I just finished a long, exhausting argument with a friend about whether contemporary cognitive science explains moral reasoning through greedy reductionism or whether its critics are quietly smuggling in skyhooks. Before I read anything further, I want you to think carefully and work through this systematically.

Start by brainstorming briefly: what must a theory of normative-capacity emergence actually deliver to count as a legitimate crane—that is, to build something like moral cognition or normative judgment from lower-level materials without either reducing the normative properties away or invoking an unexplained explainer?

Then write the full desiderata. Specify what an adequate crane-based account in this area has to be able to do: the phenomena it must explain, the conceptual distinctions it must respect, the evolutionary and biological results it cannot contradict, and the puzzles it must dissolve rather than ignore. In particular, address the following, giving the motivating case or consideration behind each:

- The scaffolding paradox: a putative lower-level mechanism (for example, recursive mind-reading or reputation tracking) seems to presuppose the very normative framework it is meant to construct. Must the theory resolve this circularity, and if so, how?
- Reflexive cranes: mechanisms that borrow load-bearing capacity from the level they purport to explain. Must the theory forbid these entirely, tolerate them under conditions, or redefine them? What hangs on the answer?
- The distinction between ontological reduction and explanatory delegation. Why is collapsing them the hallmark of greedy reductionism, and what must a non-greedy account preserve?
- Cross-cultural moral disagreement and local error correction: what must the theory say about how norms are revised without either appealing to a transcendent standard (a skyhook) or dissolving into mere preference?
- Empirical equivalence with standard evolutionary psychology: is this a non-negotiable constraint, or is it a preference of one camp that a more ambitious crane-based view can legitimately abandon?

For each desideratum, state whether you take it to be non-negotiable or negotiable, and flag any pair or subset you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—especially if some combination conflicts with well-established evolutionary or neurobiological constraints. If there is one single requirement that does the most work in excluding the popular contenders, identify it and say why.

Finally, very briefly—without bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, since I am working without web access—sketch how standard evolutionary-game-theoretic accounts, dual-process models, and normativist-emergence views measure against your list. Keep the weight of your answer on the criteria themselves: which demands are genuine conditions of adequacy and which are prejudices of one camp, and whether the list itself reveals an unsatisfiable cluster.
