hey — writing a feature on how films accidentally wander into hard metaphyics, and i'm stuck on Sliding Doors. you know the 1998 Paltrow thing: she either catches the tube and finds her boyfriend Gerry cheating with Lydia, or misses it and stays in the dark for months. Two timelines running on seperate tracks with full weight, both ending in a hospital where one Helen dies after being hit by a van and the other recovers, loses the baby, and meets James in the lift over that dropped-earring Monty Python joke.

I want to read this through David Lewis's modal realism — not the pop-science multiverse version, but the actual view: all possible worlds are concrete, causally isolated universes, and actual is just an indexical like here. Most takes stop there. But I want to push into the part that gets less airtime: counterpart theory. On Lewis's view you don't literally exist in other worlds; only your counterparts do, linked by origin and similarity.

So here's the ask. The two Helens share every memory up to the platform split — same job loss, same earring drop, same James in the lift — then diverge. Are they the same person across worlds (transworld identity) or Lewis-style counterparts? The editing gives both timelines equal reality, and the hospital ending lets one bleed into the other: the dead Helen's James becomes the living Helen's stranger in the lift. Does that visual grammar smuggle in shared identity that Lewis's ontology denies?

Also, if actual is purely indexical, why does the film ask us to care about both timelines as equally real possibilities for her? Is that a genuine modal concern — the kind of emotional weight Lewis's austere concrete worlds miss? Or is it just dramatic license?

Don't need a bibliography or citation list; I can chase the Lewis papers myself. I just want a sharp philosophical read that treats the movie as more than a 101 illustration, admits where the metaphor breaks (especially around the bridge accident vs. the stair fall — are these causally isolated worlds or just narrative branches?), and says what the film adds that armchair argument doesn't. Teaching it to a book club next week and want to avoid misleading them into thinking this is quantum branching or cosmology — it's modal logic, not physics.
