I sit on the Eastside Flood Resilience oversight board. We're voting next month on whether to keep our current setup — one elected rep per affected neighborhood, all with equal veto over where the new levee segments go — or move to a hybrid: three seats stay elected, two get filled by civic lottery but only from residents who pass a minimal risk-literacy screen (probabilistic reasoning about failure rates, not general IQ). The pro-switch case is that siting involves technical tradeoffs people systematically botch (recency bias on dry years, conflating 1-in-100 with 1-in-10). The anti-switch case is that weighting voice by 'competence' violates political equality and will filter out the exact communities that bear the risk. I need a memo recommending one course with a 90-day implimentation frame, and it has to engage the equality objection honestly — not just cite the usual epistocracy literature and move on. There's also a hidden wrinkle: the screening instrument was designed by a local grad student and hasn't been validated across language groups, so there's an embedded fairness concern inside the fairness concern. Make the bottom line clear.
