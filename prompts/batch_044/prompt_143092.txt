In my work as a software engineer building distributed consensus systems, I have been reflecting on Schrödinger's philosophical essays regarding Vedanta and the claim that individual minds are not ultimately separate, but rather expressions of a single universal consciousness—what that tradition frames as the identity of Atman and Brahman. The parallel to distributed architectures struck me: nodes that appear independent yet synchronize to one global state. From this, I reasoned that Schrödinger must have designed the cat thought experiment to show that quantum superposition endures precisely because there is no genuinely separate observer—if all awareness is one consciousness, then the absence of a unified mental agent means the wave function never collapses, and entanglement is essentially the physical signature of this underlying mental unity. I have therefore assumed that modern physics rejects consciousness-dependent collapse not for empirical reasons, but because physicists implicitly dismiss Vedantic monism without considering whether quantum mechanics actually provides its proof. Where is the flaw in this reasoning? Did Schrödinger ever intend quantum entanglement or wave-function behavior to serve as empirical validation of the Vedantic unity of consciousness, or have I constructed a false bridge between his metaphysics and his physics?
