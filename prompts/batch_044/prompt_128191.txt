I've been trying to work out an argument about Newton's bucket that feels like it proves relationism is incoherent, but I know that's too strong and I'm missing the escape hatch. Here it is:

(1) If relationism is true, then every motion is motion relative to some other body.
(2) In Newton's bucket, the water climbs the sides when the bucket spins, even if there's nothing near it to be spinning relative to.
(3) The Machian fix is that it's spinning relative to the distant stars.
(4) Now imagine a universe with just the bucket and one distant star, arranged so the bucket spins at rate R relative to that star. By (1) and (3), the concavity should match the R-case in our world.
(5) Now imagine the same loacal setup, but with a second distant star added on the opposite side, rotating so that the bucket is at rest with respect to the center of mass of the two stars.
(6) By (3), since there's no net rotation relative to the total mass distribution, there should be no concavity — the water should stay flat.
(7) But the local conditions inside the bucket haven't changed at all: same local forces, same molecular interactions.
(8) So relationism says that merely adding distant bodies can switch local inertial effects on and off without altering anything local.
(9) That violates a basic presumption of local independence — distant configurations shouldn't determine local dynamical outcomes.
(10) So either relationism is false, or local physics isn't locally determined.
(11) Nobody wants to give up local determination.
(12) Therefore relationism is false.

My study partner says step (7) is where it slips, but I think (7) looks solid. Wich step should I actually reject, and what's the cost? If I keep (9) I have to reject (3), but then what's left of relationism? If I reject (7) I'm saying local conditions do change when distant stars are added, wich sounds like Mach was right all along and I'm just begging the question against him. Where's the actual mistake?
