I sit on my university's Technology Governance and Procurement Committee, and after a rather protracted argument with a close friend over dinner last night — about whether inanimate tools can genuinely carry political imperatives or merely serve whatever purpose we assign them — I find I need to formalise our position for Thursday's meeting. We must decide whether to approve the procurement of a university-wide algorithmic workforce-management platform that automates shift allocation, monitors productivity metrics in real time, and integrates with our existing HR databases. The vendor insists the system is politically neutral: it optimises logistics, and any centralisation of authority is purely an implementation choice we make. My friend, however, pressed the view that this particular platform embodies what might be called a "soft technological determinism" — not that it physically forces hierarchy, but that its data architecture creates a "structural incentive lock-in" that makes decentralised, collegial governance practically unsustainable, since every decision leaves a digital trace that demands central auditing. I want our memo to take that distinction seriously — between hard technological prescription and this softer form of unavoidable political effect — without falling back on either pure neutrality or pure determinism.

Please draft the committee's short written opinion, addressed to the Vice-Chancellor for Operations, recommending a concrete course of action — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — together with a specific time frame for review or implementation. The reasoning should engage the vendor's neutrality claim honestly, treat the strongest argument for the opposing view with genuine respect, and set out criteria the committee could apply to future procurements. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; the memo should stand on its own reasoning.
