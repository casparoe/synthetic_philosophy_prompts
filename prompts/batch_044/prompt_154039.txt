I'm preparing a podcast interview with a philosopher of religion who advances a reconstructed, selectively uneven reading of Maimonides' accommodation thesis on animal sacrifice. On her account, the familiar claim in Maimonides' philosophical guide—that the sacrificial system was largely a divine concession to deeply rooted pagan practices—does not apply uniformly across the Levitical categories. Instead, she argues that while most offerings fall under accommodation, the olah (the whole burnt offering) functions within Maimonides' framework as a deliberately retained pedagogical instrument, one designed to direct the intellect toward the negation of materiality and corporeal attachment rather than to wean Israelites away from idolatry. She grounds this in Maimonides' differential treatment of voluntary and obligatory sacrifices, in his striking reticence about the ritual mechanics specific to the olah in the relevant chapters, and in the interpretive principle—common in Maimonides scholarship—that his legal writings serve an exoteric purpose distinct from his philosophical exposition. Consequently, she reads the uniform halakhic rigor of his legal code as consistent with, rather than contradictory to, a hidden pedagogical hierarchy in the sacrifices.

Write me eight to ten interview questions that move well beyond having her restate this view. I want at least one question that presses her on the standard objection that Maimonides groups all sacrifices together as a single accommodated institution; one that asks what her interpretation implies for Maimonides' broader theory of prophecy, symbolic religious action, and the limits of human cognition; and one that confronts her with the historical-critical point that his legal code treats every sacrifice with identical formal rigor, leaving no explicit textual trace of the pedagogical ranking she posits. For each of these three, include a specific follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—particularly where she appeals to the gap between exoteric legal presentation and esoteric philosophical meaning.
