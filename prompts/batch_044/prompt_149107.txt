I just finished a long and rather heated argument with a friend about whether biography can teach morality or whether it always collapses into either worship or gossip. Rather than picking a side, I want to settle the matter by building something reusable: I am a secondary-school ethics curriculum designer, and every semester I must decide which lives our students read and how we frame them, knowing that students will imitate what they see whether I intend it or not. I need an operational decision procedure—a checklist in strict order, with stopping conditions and explicit judgment-flag points—to determine whether a given biography is pedagogically usable for moral formation, not merely historically informative.

The principle I want translated draws on Plutarch’s practice of pairing lives to reveal character through comparable moral trials, but corrects it with an awareness of how hagiography and cynical debunking both destroy what makes a life instructive. I will call the core idea moral contingency: a biography teaches virtue only when it preserves moments where the subject’s character was genuinely open, where virtue and vice competed within the same person, and where the outcome was not predetermined by nature, narrative design, or retrospective justification. Hagiography destroys contingency by smoothing friction points into an inevitable arc of grace; cynical debunking destroys it by reducing every apparent virtue to hidden self-interest. The usable biography keeps the friction visible and allows the reader to inhabit the choice rather than admire or condemn from a distance.

Please act as an expert in moral philosophy, the philosophy of biography, and curriculum design. Before constructing the tool, spend a few minutes brainstorming the conceptual translations required to turn this principle into a practitioner-level instrument without losing its philosophical content—specifically, which distinctions must survive simplification and which can be dropped, and where the procedure will necessarily misfire.

Then deliver the procedure. It must be runnable by a non-specialist teacher in about thirty minutes per text. Structure it as ordered questions with: (a) what counts as a sufficient answer to continue, (b) what triggers an immediate “no,” and (c) explicit flags where the decision is a value judgment rather than a fact. Include small structural tests (sentence-level or narrative-arc checks) that reveal hagiographic smoothing and cynical reduction without requiring psychological expertise. State clearly what the procedure cannot do—what moral learning it excludes, where it will misfire, and which judgments remain with the practitioner.

Apply the procedure to three cases:

1. A widely assigned classroom biography of a celebrated twentieth-century scientist that frames her perseverance as innate, glosses over her documented collaboration with wartime military medicine, and treats a well-known extramarital relationship as purely private and therefore morally irrelevant to her exemplarity.
2. A recent polemical biography of a beloved humanitarian that explains every charitable act as strategic reputation management and every moral risk as calculated self-interest, leaving no structural space for genuine virtue.
3. A biography structured as an explicit parallel comparison—one political figure on the left and one on the right facing the same institutional crisis—in which both subjects make genuinely open choices, fail visibly, and are not redeemed by narrative closure.

For case 1, the procedure should deliver the answer that is philosophically correct but that I, the asker, will likely dislike, because I have taught with that biography for years and find it inspiring. Be explicit about that tension.

Finally, note what the procedure cannot settle—such as whether moral contingency is sufficient for pedagogical use, or whether some lives are too damaging to present regardless of narrative structure.

End with a single line beginning “Verdict:” summarizing whether the procedure succeeds in preserving the philosophical core without collapsing into mere taste.

Please think carefully step by step, and present the final procedure in a clear ordered format with the worked cases integrated.
