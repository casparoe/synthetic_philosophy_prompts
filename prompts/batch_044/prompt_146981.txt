Take the 1992 Newfoundland cod moratorium and the collapse of the Northern cod stock, and analyze it through the lens of Moloch, multpolar traps, and races to the bottom. Don't just give me tragedy-of-the-commons—map it properly: who the competing agents actually were (individual fishers, the big proccessing plants, Ottawa, the provincial goverment), what each one's local payoff function looked like, and where the race actually happened—between boats, between provinces, or between industry and the science? Then here's the part I care about: after the moratorium hit, a lot of the same capital and labor pivoted hard into crab and shrimp, and some ecologsts think that just moved the extraction pressure onto those stocks. Does that mean the moratorium solved the Moloch dynamics, or just displaced it into a 'shadow trap' where competitive pressure re-routed rather than disolving? And what does that do to the usual framing—does it show multpolar traps are brittle when you cut off one resource, or that they're more resilient because the agents just find new axes to race on? Be honest about where the framework is silent and where it actually explains the pivot.
