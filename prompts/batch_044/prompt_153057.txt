I've been trying to nail down my stance on Nyaya pramanas and I'm not sure it all fits together. I think pratyaksa is the only one that actually puts you in direct contact with the object, and anumana is basically just mental reconstruction that never touches the real thing. I also think sabda shouldn't count as its own pramana at all—it's just inference with extra steps, relying on the speaker's authority. But then I also want upamana to be genuinly irreducible, not collapsible into perception or inference, because recognizing similarity across different instances feels like its own thing. And I want anyathakhyati—the misplacement theory of error—to apply uniformly across all four, even to testimony, so that false testimony is just a case of attributing a wrong property through the verbal channel. Do these actually hang together? Which pairs are in real tension versus just looking weird, and what's the cheapest fix if I have to drop something?
