I am drafting a scene for a literary novel in which a physicist, grieving her partner, copies an original poem into her notebook to capture why loss feels irreversible even though the underlying mechanics are not. I need that poem: a Shakespearean sonnet in iambic pentameter with the rhyme scheme ABAB CDCD EFEF GG, written in American English, with the volta falling exactly at line nine. The concept to be rendered is what the character calls the “single-boundary condition”—the claim that the universe began in an extraordinarily low-entropy state, and that this one initial boundary, rather than any asymmetry in the fundamental laws themselves, is the sole source of the thermodynamic arrow. In the octave, present the reversible micro-world—collisions, quantum unitary evolution, equations that hold identically for t and for −t—using imagery that could be read forward or backward without contradiction. At the volta, collapse that symmetry: introduce decay, the one-way flow of memory, and the fact that every explanation we actually give runs only away from that singular origin. The closing couplet should state precisely what is sacrificed if one ignores that boundary condition—not determinism, but the very possibility of explanation running in one direction. The tone should be intimate and precise, accurate enough that a philosopher of physics would recognize the argument without wincing, yet never didactic or lecture-like. Do not include citations, footnotes, a bibliography, or references to specific texts; the poem must stand alone as art. Avoid generic refrains like “arrow of time” in the title or body; instead, embed the idea entirely in the imagery and structure.
