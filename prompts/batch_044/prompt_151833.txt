I am trying to understand a section of F. H. Bradley’s *Appearance and Reality* (1893), in which he argues that if a relation genuinely connects a quality to its term, then that relation itself requires a further relation to connect it to the terms it relates, generating an infinite regress. He concludes that relations and qualities are ultimately unreal and that true unity belongs only to the Absolute. I am particularly puzzled by how this regress is supposed to undermine the unity of the proposition — that is, the way in which a subject and predicate combine into a single meaningful thought — rather than merely undermining relationality in general.

Can you help me see exactly where in the argument the proposition’s unity is threatened? And further, if one responds to Bradley not by accepting his monistic Absolute but by adopting a modern non-relational account of predication — say, treating predication as primitive, or appealing to a trope ontology in which qualities are particularised instances — does the regress actually dissolve, or does it reappear at the level of how these tropes or primitive predications themselves cohere into a unified state of affairs? In other words, does Bradley’s challenge survive once we abandon his own metaphysical framework?

Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among specialists in Bradley scholarship and in the metaphysics of propositions. I want to know where the interpretive disagreement lies — for instance, whether Bradley’s regress is best read as a problem for relational logic, for ontology, or specifically for propositional unity — and please do not present any one contested reading as if it were the consensus.
