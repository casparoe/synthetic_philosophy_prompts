Two people in my study group got stuck arguing about Browning vs Goldhagen after we skimmed excerpts. Alex said Browning is basicly right: the guys in Battalion 101 were ordinary men, not monsters, and the drivers were peer pressure, authority, and step-by-step escalation. He noted some actually refused and werent punished severely, so it wasnt pure coercion — it was social dynamics. Jordan said thats too soft — Goldhagen showed these men volunteered for killing actions, went beyond orders, and came from a culture steeped in eliminationist antisemitism. She said refusal was rare and usually about disgust, not morality. Alex countered: if culture was the driver, why didnt regular Wehrmacht units in the same regions do the same? The variation between units fits situation and local leadership better than a blanket cultural story. Jordan replied the culture was widespread but expressed differently by role and opportunity — and she asked why they kept killing when transfer was technically open. Alex then said a lot of the eager accounts are post-hoc rationalization, people aligning beliefs with actions to reduce dissonance, so Goldhagen reads retroactive justifications as deep motives. Jordan called that a convenient way to dismiss testimony. They want me to adjudicate. Can you go through each move, rate them, and say who's more right overall?
