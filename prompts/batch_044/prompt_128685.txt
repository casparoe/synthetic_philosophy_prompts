I'm working through the First Essay of Nietzsche's On the Genealogy of Morals for an independent study project, and I'm stuck on a specific tension in his account of the slave revolt in morality. In the sections where he traces the inversion of noble values, Nietzsche describes how the priestly caste transforms an aristocratic evaluation—where strength and vitality are "good" and weakness is "bad"—into its opposite: the weak become "good" and the powerful become "evil." What I find conceptually murky is whether this inversion amounts to a genuinely creative act of value-positing, in which the slave actively legislates a new moral framework, or whether it remains purely reactive, gaining its entire content by negating the noble's self-affirmation.

To sharpen the question, I want to introduce a distinction I'm not sure the text fully supports: what I call a "double negation" structure. On this reading, the slave first negates the noble's evaluation of himself (the noble is not good but evil), and then performs a second, distinct move: negating that very negation in order to claim a positive identity for himself (we, the weak, are therefore good). If these are genuinely two steps, then slave morality contains an active, almost legislative moment that is not reducible to resentment. If, however, the second step collapses into the first—if calling oneself "good" is nothing more than the mirror-image of calling the noble "evil"—then the purely reactive interpretation holds without remainder.

Please help me understand whether Nietzsche's text supports this double-negation architecture or whether it undermines it, and take a definite position rather than surveying both sides. In particular, say whether the real conceptual work is being done by the distinction between reactive and active value-creation, or by the structural difference between the "good/bad" and "good/evil" pairings, and explain which reading makes better sense of the passage. I am not asking for a bibliography or citations; I need the conceptual architecture clarified precisely enough to build my own analysis on it.
