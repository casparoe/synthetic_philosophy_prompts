i've been stuck on this condition for moral worth i'm calling epistemic transparancy - the idea that you only get full credit for a right act if your motive actually tracks the real moral reason behind it, not just some nearby good feeling.

(1) if that's required, then a kid who shares because 'it feels nice' but can't grasp fairness gets no moral worth, even though she's being kind.
(2) same for a soldier who rescues civilians out of loyalty to his unit, not because saving them is right - right act, wrong reason, so no credit.
(3) but drop transparancy entirely and the guy who returns lost cash just to avoid guilt gets the same praise as someone who does it because it's just.
(4) weaken it to 'the reason just needs to be in the ballpark of morality' and we still can't separate loyalty-to-comrades from guilt-avoidance; both are in the ballpark, neither is the actual reason.
(5) so either we deny moral worth to good people who lack the right concepts, or we lose the ability to distinguish worthy motives from unworthy ones.

where does this collapes? i want to keep the kid and the soldier getting some credit, but i don't want the guilt-avoider sneaking in. is there a middle ground, or do i have to bite one of the bullets?
