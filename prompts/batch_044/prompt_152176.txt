My opening move: I want to press you with a discrete variant of the hole argument. Imagine a physical theory in which spacetime is represented not as a smooth manifold but as a finite directed graph: nodes carry local geometric data, and edges encode causal relations. Define a label-permutation symmetry that reassigns metric values across nodes while leaving every edge, every path, and every intersection of worldlines — the discrete analog of point-coincidence — exactly unchanged. If you are a substantivalist about these nodes, then this symmetry seems to generate genuinely distinct physical states with identical observable consequences, reviving the threat to determinism. You might reply that only the graph-relations are real and that nodes have no independent identity. But if you deny primitive node-identity, have you not simply adopted a form of relationalism? How do you, as a substantivalist, respond to this discrete hole argument without merely retreating to the continuous case?
