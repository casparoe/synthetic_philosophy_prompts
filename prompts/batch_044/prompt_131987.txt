Assume the role of a professional philosopher of rational choice theory with deep familiarity with diachronic consistency arguments, Dutch Book reasoning, and intertemporal preference dynamics. Before drafting, take a moment to brainstorm carefully: (1) the formal conditions that make a sequence of trades extractive against an agent; (2) the exact boundary between preference change driven by genuine evidential updating and change driven by framing, fatigue, or error; and (3) whether introducing continuity or smoothness in preference trajectories creates any genuinely new normative problem, or merely redistributes the discrete steps of a classic pump. Then produce a polished, formal adversarial-collaboration document.

The two parties are Party A, a committed diachronic Dutch-Book defender who holds that susceptibility to cyclic exploitation over time demonstrates irrationality and that rational agents must satisfy an intertemporal stability constraint absent genuine evidential updates; and Party B, a committed evidential-updater who holds that preference reversals are rational precisely when they track changes in belief or value-relevant information, and that money-pump arguments illegitimately presuppose that preferences should remain invariant across epistemic states.

Your deliverable should contain exactly two sections. First, a numbered list of agreed claims—no fewer than six—that both parties could sign without reservation. These should cover the formal structure of exploitation sequences, the conditions under which an adversary can guarantee extraction of value, the distinction between epistemic and non-epistemic drivers of reversal, and the shared normative commitment to avoiding guaranteed loss. Second, a disagreements table with columns for the crux (a precise sub-question), Party A’s formulation of its own view phrased so Party A accepts it, and Party B’s formulation of its own view phrased so Party B accepts it. Include at least three cruxes.

One crux must concern the “Gradient Pump”: a hypothetical continuous scenario in which an agent’s preference ordering over a fixed pair of goods shifts smoothly along a circular parameter path θ ∈ [0, 2π), returning to the original ordering after a full loop with no discrete reversal at any point, yet allowing an adversary to extract arbitrarily small increments of value at each point along the loop. Both parties should agree that this case is conceptually coherent and bears directly on whether continuity in preference change blocks or modifies the normative force of money-pump reasoning. For this crux, record in advance: Party A’s prediction about whether the Gradient Pump demonstrates diachronic irrationality, and Party B’s prediction about whether the agent in the Gradient Pump is rational, together with the brief reasoning each side would invoke.

For every crux—including the Gradient Pump—specify one concrete argument, comparison case, or structural feature that both sides accept as genuinely relevant, and record each side’s predicted evaluation of that item. Ensure that no formulation in the table is a strawman; each side’s wording should be one its own proponents would recognize and defend. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for further sources. The document should be self-contained and meaningful to a reader without web access. Write in a formal, academic register, and conclude with a brief note indicating which parts of the disagreement, if any, appear to be verbal rather than substantive.
