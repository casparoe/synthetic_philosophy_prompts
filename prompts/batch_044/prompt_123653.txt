Write a deadpan consumer-product review, in the style of Wirecutter or Consumer Reports, comparing three competing “well-being” lifestyle devices: (1) the Nozick Experience Machine, running on pure hedonism; (2) the Desire-Fulfillment Suite, which monitors your preferences in real time, updates them for consistency, and then satisfies the updated set; and (3) the Objective List Premium Package, which delivers goods such as knowledge, achievement, and friendship regardless of your subjective satisfaction. The review should be about 600 words, with ratings, pros and cons, and a “best for” recommendation. The satire must come from treating actual philosophical arguments—deception and the “experience requirement” for hedonism, adaptive preferences and manipulation for desire-fulfillment, and paternalism for objective-list theories—as literal engineering specifications, warranty exclusions, and customer-service complaints. Include at least one invented user testimonial complaining that switching to the Objective List improved “knowledge throughput” but caused “friendship firmware crashes.” Do not name philosophers other than Nozick; refer to other positions only by their functional descriptions. The humor should be legible to a specialist who recognizes that the objections are rendered accurately, and readable to a non-specialist who grasps the trade-offs. Keep the voice deadpan and avoid winking. End with a single line beginning “Verdict:” that states which device wins and the philosophical reason.
