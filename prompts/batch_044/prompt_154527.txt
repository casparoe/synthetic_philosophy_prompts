Compare the panpsychist ethical claim that suffering is distributed at the fundamental physical level with the standard "capacity for welfare" framework used in animal and AI ethics. Rather than asking for empirical plausibility, I want you to map a specific conceptual tension: if conscious experience is intrinsic to physical entities, then altering a field or particle's intrinsic state might constitute a welfare-relevant event in its own right, yet we have no principled way to individuate or aggregate these micro-level experiences without smuggling in macro-level functional criteria—integration, global availability, behavioral coherence—that panpsychism was introduced partly to avoid. To ground this, consider quantum vacuum fluctuations, specifically the continuous appearance and annihilation of virtual particle-antiparticle pairs. On a consistent panpsychist view, do these fluctuations instantiate brief, non-integrated experiences of harm or distress? What does the complete absence of any integration mechanism (no nervous system, no global workspace, no reportability) do to their moral weight? Does this push the view toward a trivial universal-suffering thesis in which every physical change is welfare-relevant, or can it be blocked without implicitly reintroducing the very macro-functional value specifications the framework claims to do without? And what does this tension reveal about whether panpsychist ethics can remain coherent without a full axiological specification of which configurations matter?
