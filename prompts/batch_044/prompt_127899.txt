I've been reflecting on Libet's classic result that the readiness potential precedes the conscious awareness of a decision to move by roughly 350 milliseconds, and on later neuroimaging studies that have detected predictive brain activity several seconds before a reported choice. If we take these findings seriously as evidence that the brain initiates action unconsciously, then it seems we should expect an analogous temporal gap for actions with much longer preparatory arcs—say, writing a philosophical treatise over months or orchestrating a political campaign over years. Specifically, shouldn't there be some neural signature that "decides" upon these complex, sustained projects months in advance, just as the readiness potential decides upon a finger movement hundreds of milliseconds in advance? Given that neuroscience has not identified any such long-range readiness potentials, does this not limit Libet-style determinism to spontaneous motor acts and leave deliberative, long-horizon agency untouched? Or does this line of reasoning rest on a mistaken assumption about how preparatory neural activity relates to the temporal structure of intentional action?
