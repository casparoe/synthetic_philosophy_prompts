Construct a taxonomy of philosophical positions on Jeffrey conditionalization applied to structurally ambiguous evidence — that is, evidence that fails to determine a unique, exhaustive partition of possibilities across which beliefs are revised. Present the result as a numbered list of exactly five items, with no introduction or conclusion, using British English and avoiding all citations, bibliographies, or references to obscure specialised texts. Each item must contain exactly two sentences: the first should specify the combination of answers (yes or no) to the four binary diagnostic questions that identifies this leaf — namely, (i) whether rational update requires a well-defined evidential partition; (ii) whether prior ratios must be preserved across updates that cross ambiguous boundaries; (iii) whether ambiguity should be resolved by expanding the frame or by assigning weights over frames; and (iv) whether Jeffrey's rule can legitimately apply to second-order uncertainty about the partition itself — and the second should name the position (or label it 'unnamed') together with its single most awkward commitment regarding belief revision. The five leaves must cover an orthodox Jeffrey conditionalizer, a strict Bayesian who rejects Jeffrey updating for ambiguous evidence, an imprecise-probability view that weights over frames, a frame-expanding contextualist, and a position that allows suspending revision altogether when structural ambiguity is present; in addition, within the list identify one logically possible combination of answers that your taxonomy leaves empty and diagnose in one sentence whether that gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Phrase every diagnostic question so that it can be answered without knowing the corresponding philosophical label in advance.
