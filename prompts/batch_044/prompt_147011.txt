Design a 90-minute workshop activity for 16 advanced philosophy students—graduate or upper-level undergraduate—on chaos, predictability, and determinism. The group should come away feeling, through their own manual calculations, that possessing the exact deterministic law together with an approximately correct initial condition is still insufficient for medium-term prediction, and that the failure is structural rather than careless.

Divide participants into four teams of four. The session runs in two rounds with symbolic token stakes, followed by a debrief.

Round One, “The Laplacean Bet” (25 minutes): Give every team the explicit deterministic rule x_{n+1} = 4x_n(1-x_n), a worksheet, and a displayed initial value x_0 = 0.40 rounded to two decimal places. Ask them to compute iterations one through eight by hand, rounding each intermediate result to two decimal places to model the resolution limit of any actual measuring agent. Before computing, each team wagers three symbolic tokens on whether they will match the hidden “World Engine” trajectory at iteration six. Once all predictions are sealed, reveal the true trajectory computed by the facilitator from an unrounded initial value x_0 = 0.40017, kept hidden in a sealed envelope. Award tokens by proximity: full payout within 0.05, partial within 0.2, none otherwise. Teams will be close at iteration two, visibly diverging by iteration five, and scattered across the interval by iteration eight despite having applied the correct law with perfect fidelity.

Round Two, “The Shadowing Round” (20 minutes): Retain the same rule and rounding protocol, but assign each team a slightly different displayed initial value—0.40, 0.4001, 0.3999, and 0.401—all presented with identical two-decimal display precision. Have them run the same eight iterations, then reveal both the hidden true trajectory and every other team’s trajectory. The divergence should now be inter-team as well as team-to-world: identical deterministic transformations executed with identical care produce outputs that have scattered across [0,1] by iteration seven.

Debrief (35 minutes): Map each phase onto the conceptual architecture. The bet represents the intuitive slide from ontological determinism to epistemic predictability. The mandatory rounding represents unavoidable measurement resolution, not human error. The inter-team scatter demonstrates that determinism governs every trajectory exactly, yet no agent-level approximation can shadow the true path beyond a short horizon. Introduce, only at this point, the framing idea of an “epistemic Lyapunov horizon”—the iteration boundary beyond which a deterministic system’s sensitive dependence renders any agent’s approximate knowledge predictively useless regardless of law-knowledge. Ask teams to annotate their worksheets at the exact step where their shadow broke.

Predictable failures and handling: Participants will initially blame calculation errors rather than structural divergence; the facilitator must withhold judgment until all four worksheets are displayed side-by-side, letting the pattern of divergence speak first. Some teams will try to “fix” the rule by adding correction terms; allow one minute of this, then show that any corrected approximation still diverges if rounded, reinforcing that the problem is not model misspecification but resolution limits.

Honest limitation: The activity reproduces discrete, finite-step divergence only; it cannot reproduce continuous-time flow, the full measure-theoretic structure of dynamical chaos, or the formal definition of Lyapunov exponents as real-valued rates. The facilitator must supply the point that in continuous systems the horizon is a temporal parameter, not an integer step count. The activity also does not address free will, moral responsibility, or quantum indeterminacy; its sole purpose is to separate determinism from predictability through embodied computation. No computers, citations, external readings, or named theoretical texts are required; the facilitator needs only a calculator with high-precision storage for the hidden true values.
