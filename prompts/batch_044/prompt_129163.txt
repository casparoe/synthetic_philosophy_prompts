I am a final-year undergraduate taking a seminar in moral philosophy, and I have been asked to deliver the opening remarks at a one-day interdisciplinary workshop on care ethics and professional obligation. The audience is divided evenly between philosophers of action who work on supererogation and clinical practitioners—palliative-care nurses, social workers, and clinical coordinators—who have no training in philosophy. I need a speech of roughly five minutes that makes the distinction between threshold supererogation, in which an act crosses a definite boundary into the heroic or saintly, and gradient supererogation, in which moral merit scales continuously without any non-arbitrary cut-off point, do genuine structural work rather than serving as decoration. The distinction must determine which forms of care labour can be praised without being demanded, and it must prevent me from saying anything that collapses ordinary professional duty into an unlimited expectation of self-sacrifice. The remarks should address the practitioners with respect rather than condescension, acknowledge to the philosophers present that the conceptual framework is being applied rigorously, and conclude with a specific observation about why the absence of a threshold in gradient cases renders gratitude structurally asymmetrical with obligation. Please write the speech in British English, do not include any citations, reading lists, or bibliographic references, and maintain a tone that is formal but not alienating to a non-specialist audience.
