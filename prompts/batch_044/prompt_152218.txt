Consider the following generalization of Benacerraf’s dilemma, which I have been trying to pressure-test in seminar. (1) Benacerraf showed that if natural numbers are objects—say, sets—then both the von Neumann and the Zermelo constructions fit the full structural role of arithmetic, yet they identify the number 2 with different sets; lacking any non-arbitrary criterion of choice, we either fail to know which set 2 is, or the singular term “2” fails to denote a unique object. (2) The difficulty is not peculiar to mathematics: whenever an entity is specified entirely by its relational place in a structure, and there exist ontologically distinct but structurally identical realizers, the same epistemic and referential gap opens. (3) Take theoretical entities in science—electrons, genes, rational agents—each fixed only by the functional and relational properties assigned in our best theories. (4) For each case there seem to be empirically adequate but ontologically divergent realizers: different quantum-field configurations, different genetic mechanisms across lineages, different computational substrates that satisfy the same decision-theoretic axioms. (5) By (2), either we are ignorant of which realizer our term denotes, or the term denotes nothing at all. (6) Yet we plainly possess theoretical knowledge about electrons and successfully refer to them. (7) The standard realist reply appeals to causal interaction or ostensive fixation, but those fixing relations are themselves specified structurally within our theories, so they appear to inherit the same ambiguity rather than dissolve it. (8) Hence either Benacerraf’s original argument was never valid, or it applies globally and forces us to choose between widespread theoretical ignorance and widespread reference failure. Where does the chain slip? If you reject (2), explain why mathematics is uniquely vulnerable; if you reject (4), show that the scientific “realizers” are not genuinely distinct in the relevant sense; if you reject (7), tell me precisely how causal access breaks the symmetry without smuggling in a non-structural property. I am inclined to preserve (1) and (6), but I want to know exactly what that commitment costs.
