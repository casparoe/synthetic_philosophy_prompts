I spent three decades in condensed-matter physics before retiring, and only in recent years have I begun studying philosophy of mind with any seriousness. I have arrived at a cluster of commitments about beliefs and desires, but before presenting them to a discussion group I would like a consistency audit. Here is where I stand:

(1) I am an eliminative materialist about propositional attitudes: beliefs and desires do not exist as discrete, causally efficacious internal states. Folk psychology is a false theory, on a par with phlogiston or vitalism.

(2) At the same time, I routinely adopt Dennett's intentional stance in everyday interaction; I regard it strictly as a pragmatic predictive strategy, carrying no ontological weight.

(3) I believe that hierarchical predictive-processing models in neuroscience already explain cognition and behavior without invoking propositional attitudes, and that they will eventually replace folk psychology entirely, leaving no explanatory remainder.

(4) Yet in clinical psychiatry—especially in diagnosing and treating schizophrenia—I take the attribution of specific false beliefs (delusions) to be indispensable. Therapeutic planning seems impossible without treating those attributions as tracking a genuine cognitive malfunction inside the patient.

(5) I also accept the principle of charity in interpretation: to understand an interlocutor at all, we must attribute to them mostly true beliefs.

(6) Finally, I maintain that moral responsibility requires an agent's actions to flow from appropriate beliefs and desires arranged in a rational, intelligible pattern.

Please audit this package. Identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to conflict; rank the real tensions by severity, stating for each the cheapest revision that restores consistency and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. In particular, clarify whether (1) and (2) actually conflict or whether that tension dissolves on inspection, and say whether I am implicitly smuggling a realist ontology into (4) and (5) that (1) and (3) reject. Can (6) survive if the causal-explanatory work I assign to beliefs and desires is performed entirely by neural prediction-error dynamics? Which single change to my commitments would resolve the greatest number of conflicts? Do not list readings or citations; I want an internal audit of the views themselves.
