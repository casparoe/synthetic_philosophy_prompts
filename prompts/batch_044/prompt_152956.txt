Provide a careful conceptual analysis of how finks and masks differentially interfere with compound, multi-track dispositions, using the following institutional scenario as your central example. Consider a regulatory body that is disposed to resolve disputes of type D through either adjudicative review or mediated settlement, with the choice of pathway determined by an internal procedural rule. Now suppose that, at the precise moment the adjudicative pathway is triggered, an internal veto mechanism temporarily disables that pathway without altering the body’s charter or underlying competence—while simultaneously, an external statutory reform systematically suppresses the mediated settlement pathway across all cases, though the body’s internal rules still nominally permit it. Explain whether the internal veto should be analyzed as a fink, the external statutory reform as a mask, or whether either analysis fails because compound dispositions require a different treatment of interference. Further, address whether the body retains the compound disposition to resolve D if both pathways are obstructed but its founding charter remains intact, and discuss what this implies for distinguishing base-property alterations from mere interference in institutional contexts.
