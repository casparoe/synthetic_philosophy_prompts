I serve on the Strategic Allocation Committee of the Meridian Research Foundation, and we face a concrete recommendation regarding a proposed thirty-year longitudinal study tracking neurodevelopmental outcomes in adolescent cohorts exposed to early cognitive-enhancement protocols. The scientific case is that only multi-decade tracking can determine whether subtle benefits compound or dissipate across social and biological change, but several members have pressed a consequentialist objection: because the study’s welfare effects will ramify unpredictably across generations, the committee is structurally clueless about whether net outcomes will be benign or catastrophic. A counter-faction argues that this cluelessness is symmetric—equally compatible with harm and with benefit—so it cannot privilege inaction, and that abandoning consequentialist evaluation entirely would leave us with no decision criterion whatsoever.

Please draft a formal policy memo for the committee recommending either approval, conditional rejection, or a structured approval-with-decision-nodes framework. The memo should treat cluelessness not merely as an epistemic limit but as a normative feature shaping institutional duty—specifically, whether a funding body may ethically deploy consequentialist reasoning when its predictive horizon exceeds its accountability horizon, and whether institutional cluelessness differs in kind from individual moral cluelessness in ways that affect our obligations. You must include an honest, rigorous treatment of the strongest opposing view (likely the symmetry objection coupled with a precautionary defense of action), reach a clear actionable bottom line rather than a non-committal balance, and state explicitly how confident you are in the recommendation. Finally, single out the specific point about which you are least certain—particularly whether the distinction between predictive and accountability horizons genuinely grounds a deontological constraint on institutional funding, or whether that distinction collapses under scrutiny.
