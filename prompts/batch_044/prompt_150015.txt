hey, grading some philosophy of mind essays and need a second set of eyes. question was: explain the paradox of phenominal judgment for epiphenomenalism, then assess whether an overdetermnation reply saves it.

student answer (rough):

epiphenomenalism says consciousness is like steam from a locomotive—caused by the brain but with no back-causal power. the paradox of phenominal judgment is that if my belief 'i am conscious right now' is produced entirely by neural firing, not by the conscious feeling itself, then it's just accidental that it's correct. so the view seems to cut off our own knowledge of mind from the inside.

they then consider the reply that both the brain state and the conscious state jointly cause the judgment—causal overdetermination. they reject it by appealing to the closure of the physical: since every physical event already has a complete physical cause, there is literally no causal gap for consciousness to fill. they conclude this doesn't save epiphenomenalism but just collapses it into interactionism, which abandons closure.

last, they distinguish this from the zombie argument: zombies lack consciousness entirely, but in the paradox consciousness is present yet causally inert.
