I'd like a consistency check on my position regarding Kripkenstein's rule-following paradox before presenting it in a seminar. My commitments are these: (a) I take the skeptical argument to depend on a Cartesian transparency premise—namely, that if a mental state fixes the meaning of "plus," the speaker must have direct introspective access to its full content—so rejecting that transparency dissolves the problem. (b) I also hold that meaning is irreducibly normative: grasping a rule is being subject to a standard of correctness, not merely possessing a descriptive mental representation. (c) I am a mentalist about rules in the sense that there are no Platonic norms; whatever grounds the normative commitment must ultimately be some mental or behavioral fact about the speaker. (d) I deny that communal agreement is necessary or sufficient for correct rule-following, since an entire community can be systematically mistaken. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear so? Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate the least costly revision for each, and say whether (a) fundamentally misunderstands the skeptical challenge in light of (b). Can (c) be sustained without collapsing the normative into the descriptive? And once (d) rules out community as the ground, does (c) leave me with any non-arbitrary resources for fixing correct application, or is the conflict between them only apparent?
