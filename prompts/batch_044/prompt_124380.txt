I'm framing the evaluation chapter of my dissertation on moral aggregation, and I want to avoid comparing specific theories before I have the full specification of adequacy. Write the desiderata that any theory of whether the number of people at risk should determine rescue priority must meet—taking Taurek's rejection of pure numerical aggregation in life-saving conflicts as a constraint to be respected, not an intuition to be dissolved. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or consideration behind it.

The list must cover: (1) the cases any adequate theory has to get right—standard pairwise rescue (one child versus five strangers in separate ponds), unequal-cost variants (saving the many requires abandoning the one who is closer or already being aided), and what I will call distributed moral proximity, where potential rescuers are embedded in social networks of varying density and causal connection to victims, so the binary framing of one-versus-many collapses into graded relational structures; (2) the distinctions it must preserve—between having moral status and being the object of a particular duty, between ex ante decision rules and ex post evaluation of outcomes, and between causal efficacy and the moral reason that justifies action; (3) consistency conditions with settled empirical and theoretical results—findings from moral psychology that people reject aggregation in concrete named-victim cases but accept it in abstract statistical framing, and constraints from decision theory that cannot be contradicted without serious cost; (4) tensions and possible joint unsatisfiability—especially whether respecting anti-aggregation in direct rescue can be maintained together with requiring consistency across network-structured obligations, and flag any subset of requirements you think is jointly unsatisfiable.

Mark which requirements are non-negotiable (those that preserve the core anti-aggregation intuition without collapsing into arbitrariness) and which reflect camp-specific preferences (e.g., determinism of duty, universalizability, or relational priority).
