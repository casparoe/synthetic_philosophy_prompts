I'm trying to stabilize my views on Berkeley's idealism for a discussion group and need a consistency audit. Here is my current package:

(1) I accept the master argument in its strongest form: any attempt to conceive an object as existing independently of all perception is self-defeating, because the act of conception itself makes the object present to a mind, so to be is to be perceived holds for everything we can think about.

(2) I treat clear conceivability as a fallible but substantive guide to metaphysical possibility. In particular, I think the zombie scenario—an exact physical duplicate of a conscious being that lacks phenomenal consciousness—is genuinely conceivable without hidden contradiction, and this gives real, if defeasible, evidence against physicalism.

(3) I hold that phenomenal concepts have an irreducible first-personal mode of presentation, which explains the explanatory gap, but I deny that concepts of absence, negation, or lack inherit this special status.

(4) I allow continuous divine perception, so that unperceived can only mean unperceived by finite minds, never outside every mind altogether.

(5) I think we can coherently intend non-existent objects—say, a round square—because the immediate object of thought is always an idea present to the mind, regardless of whether there is any corresponding external referent.

Do these hang together? Specifically: does the logic of (1) threaten (2), since conceiving a zombie involves conceiving an absence of consciousness, and if the master argument generalizes, shouldn't that absence be similarly unthinkable? Or can (1) be quarantined to cases where the absence conceived is the absence of the very mental relation that makes conception possible? Does (3) help resolve the tension between (1) and (2), or does it actually exacerbate it by giving phenomenal absence a special status I have just denied? Is there an apparent conflict between (1) and (5) that dissolves once we distinguish the idea in the mind from its referent? Rank the tensions by severity, specify the least costly revision for each, and tell me which single commitment I should drop if I want the cleanest package overall.
