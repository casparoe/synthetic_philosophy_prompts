Having spent thirty years in experimental particle physics before turning to philosophy in retirement, I am struck by the methodological gulf between empirical replication and G.E. Moore's proof of an external world. Please compose a satirical, deadpan scientific report—approximately 600 words, formatted as a formal peer-reviewed article with Abstract, Methods, Results, and Discussion—describing a multinational replication study of Moore's proof. The study should treat the presentation of hands as an observable phenomenon requiring statistical validation, complete with control conditions (phantom-limb presentations, virtual-reality gloves), calculated p-values for "existence," and reviewer objections that ontological conclusions exceed the data. The principal investigator should be an invented philosopher-scientist who insists that if Moore's argument is sound, it ought to be reproducible under double-blind conditions. The satire must remain internally consistent: the voice never admits that philosophical proof differs in kind from empirical verification, yet the content should make clear to anyone familiar with Moore that this category error is precisely what is being mocked. Write in the dry, formal register of a methods journal, avoid any request for bibliographies or citations, and do not depend on obscure texts a small model would not have encountered.
