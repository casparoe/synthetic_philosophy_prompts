Student answer:
APCC clearly sidesteps the demandingness objection that Scheffler raises against standard act-consequentialism. By shifting from individual optimization to collective fair-share baselines, the theory no longer requires agents to reduce themselves to the point of marginal utility. Instead, each person only has to cover their slice of the global shortfall—say, donating 10% of income—after which further giving is supererogatory. This is basically just applying Liam Murphy's division of moral labor to climate finance: institutions set the baseline and individuals just fill their slot. The only issue left is figuring out the baseline, but once that's fixed the problem is solved. Therefore demandingness doesnt apply.
