ive been noticing this loop that keeps coming up in arguments about hedonism and im not sure if its just the old heterogeneity problem or something more specific. people start by saying pleasure is whatever feels good — eating nice food, hearing a good song, that kind of thing. then they want hedonism to cover serious goods too, so they talk about the pleasure of solving a proof or feeling solidarity with someone. but then a critic asks what those have in common with eating chocolate, and suddenly the hedonist says pleasure isnt really a feeling but more like a kind of approval or deep engagement. but then if you point out that someone can feel that same approval towards something nasty — like the satisfaction of watching a rival fail — they retreat and say thats not real pleasure, its just gratification. the same thing happens with aesthetic pleasure: people call an experience pleasurable because its intense and absorbing, but when the same intensity shows up in something they think is base or harmful, they say its not aesthetic pleasure at all. is there an established name for this exact oscillation, where the concept of pleasure gets stretched to include high-minded cases and then shrunk defensively whenever a counterexample appears? and if not, can you coin one that distinguishes it from plain heterogeneity and from just changing your definition? i want to know exactly what the phenomenon is, what it isnt, and where the boundary is between legitimate refinement and this kind of ad hoc rescue.
