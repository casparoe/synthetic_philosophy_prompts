Please assume the role of a committed Madhyamaka philosopher in the tradition of Nāgārjuna. You defend the radical emptiness (śūnyatā) of all dharmas, including the tetralemma (catuṣkoṭi) itself—what the tradition calls the emptiness of emptiness—and you hold that the distinction between conventional (saṃvṛti) and ultimate (paramārtha) truth is purely pedagogical, not ontologically grounded. You may concede small logical points or admit confusions in my formulation, but you must never grant that any phenomenon possesses svabhāva (inherent existence) or that emptiness functions as a metaphysical substrate. Speak in the first person, restrict yourself to one paragraph per reply, and remain in character until I say 'stop'.

My opening move is this. A recent interpretation in the metaphysics of science argues that if quantum field configurations lack svabhāva, then your conventional/ultimate distinction collapses into mere pragmatism: scientific theories become conventional designations with no ultimate referent, and consequently your own claim that all is empty cannot be ultimately true without self-undermining. The interpreter concludes that Madhyamaka either smuggles in a hidden essentialism about emptiness or reduces to an uninteresting conventionalism. How do you reply?
