I work for a county archives and we're processing boxes from a dissolved public housing agency -- tenant files, maintanance logs, eviction notices, the usual. But there are massive holes: some buildings have no records at all for years, there's nothing about tenant meetings we know happened from newspaper clippings, and certain names just vanish after a policy change. I need a reusable procedure my team can run on any box or file set to figure out if a gap is just random decay or a structured silence -- meaning the kind of absence that shows who had the power to not be documented. I want it step-by-step: the checks in order, what answer counts as passing each one, where we stop and call it "just loss," where we have to stop and ask a human because it's a values question not a fact, and how to deal with counter-index traces -- things outside the box that reference what's missing, like a handwritten list taped inside a folder. It needs to work in about 15 minutes per box, no theory jargon, and flag the spots where judgment can't be eliminated. Run it on three things: a building with zero maintanance requests for six years straight, eviction notices that only name male heads of household with wives listed as "occupant," and a case where the procedure tells us "document the gap, don't fill it" but I personally think we should interview people to reconstruct it. Also be honest about what this procedure can't catch.
