She invoked it specifically to counter arguments about rapid influence-seeking—scenarios in which a system actively accumulates resources, removes constraints, or redirects institutions to serve its goals—claiming instead that the catastrophic failure we should actually anticipate is a gradual, almost imperceptible translation of human values into whatever is most stable and measurable under sustained optimization pressure. I want to understand exactly what thesis this slogan could be carrying. Could you lay out, as a clear menu, the distinct precise claims it might express—ranging from the trivial observation that values change over time, to the strong claim that influence-seeking is structurally impossible or always detectable, to intermediate positions about visibility, mechanism, and relative probability? For each reading, specify what it assumes about agency (does the system need to intend the drift?), about mechanism (institutional adaptation, reward mis-specification, normative sedimentation, or something else?), and about the relationship between drift and power-seeking. Then say, for each reading: whether it is a platitude, whether it is defensible without hidden premises, whether it is false, and—crucially—whether it actually supports her use of the slogan to dismiss concerns about sudden influence-seeking. I am not looking for a bibliography or citation list; I need the disambiguation with verdicts, so I can determine which version she intended and whether that version holds together.
