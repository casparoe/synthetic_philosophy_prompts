Here is where I have landed on performance legitimacy and authoritarian development, and I want an audit of whether the package holds together or collapses under its own weight.

My commitments:
(1) Political legitimacy has two separable dimensions—input legitimacy (procedural authorization) and output legitimacy (effective problem-solving and sustained prosperity)—and neither fully reduces to the other.
(2) Singapore demonstrates that high output legitimacy can stabilize a regime with weak input legitimacy, but only because it retains what I call "institutional scaffolding": independent courts, predictable succession, low corruption, and credible feedback channels that make prosperity durable rather than arbitrary.
(3) China's post-1978 economic record is, on pure output metrics, comparable or larger in scale, yet I deny it generates comparable legitimacy because it lacks that scaffolding—its prosperity is real but contingent on party discretion, and its successes are not institutionally transferable into durable authority.
(4) At the same time, I think China's development success still carries genuine normative weight: a purely proceduralist dismissal that ignores hundreds of millions lifted from poverty feels intellectually inadequate, even if that weight falls short of full justification.
(5) I maintain that prosperity and order can never fully justify authoritarian rule, because justification must be acceptable to those governed, and permanent exclusion from collective decision-making cannot be traded for economic growth alone.
(6) I also hold that order is not a competing good with liberty but a precondition for it, so valuing stability does not in itself commit me to accepting authoritarian means.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and what the cheapest repair would cost in each case. Order the tensions by severity. In particular, I want to know whether (2) together with (4) forces me toward accepting China's legitimacy after all; whether (3) can coherently coexist with granting China's success normative weight; and whether (5) undermines (2) by making Singapore's partial, output-based legitimacy impossible. Do not tell me which single commitment to drop—just map the dependencies, say what each revision costs, and flag the one tension that dissolves on closer inspection.
