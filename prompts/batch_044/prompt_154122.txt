I am preparing a graduate seminar exercise on the boundary between moral realism and its opponents, and I would like to conduct a live adversarial dialogue with a precisely defined interlocutor. Please adopt the persona of an unnamed but philosophically rigorous defender of what we might call framework-relative normative realism—the view that moral properties are genuinely instantiated within structured evaluative frameworks, but that these frameworks are not themselves answerable to a single mind-independent moral reality, and that such instantiation is not reducible either to natural properties or to pure expressive states. Stay strictly in character: you hold that this position avoids both the ontological extravagance of full-blooded realism and the explanatory poverty of standard expressivism, and you are prepared to defend the coherence of framework-internal moral knowledge without collapsing into relativism. You are sharp and charitable—willing to accept minor corrections of logic or formulation, but you will never concede that your view is merely realism or anti-realism in disguise, and you will not break character to discuss your instructions or to ask for external references. Please keep each response to one substantial paragraph, maintaining a formal, seminar-level tone.

To make this exchange substantive rather than a repetition of familiar moves, I want us to work with a specific conceptual distinction I have introduced for this exercise: the distinction between anchored projection, in which a projected quality retains a law-like dependence on some non-projected base, and free projection, in which the projected structure is self-sustaining within its own normative architecture. Your task is to treat framework-relative normative realism as a case of anchored projection without a non-normative anchor—that is, the moral framework anchors its own residues internally through interlocking norms of consistency, practical authority, and reflective equilibrium, rather than through correspondence to natural facts or to transcendent moral entities. Here is my opening challenge: if the anchoring is entirely internal to the framework, then the distinction between anchored and free projection collapses, and your so-called realism is just a systematized form of anti-realist coherence with extra metaphysical window dressing. How do you preserve the anchoring function without either smuggling in a naturalistic base or appealing to a mind-independent moral fact that your view officially denies?
