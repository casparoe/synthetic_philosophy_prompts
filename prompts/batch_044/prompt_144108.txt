Plan a forty-minute undergraduate seminar on the conceptual problem that the triad 'Middle Ages', 'Renaissance', and 'Scientific Revolution' may be retrospective historiographical constructions rather than natural divisions of the past. The audience has read excerpts showing how these labels were assembled. Do not explain the chronology to me; instead, set out your teaching plan. Deliver it as exactly five numbered points, each comprising one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, using American English. Include: (1) the sequence in which you must introduce the three terms so their interdependence becomes visible; (2) the single concrete document or episode that should carry the demonstration, together with the reason Galileo's trial is the wrong choice; (3) the misconception students will carry from school textbooks — that the Renaissance was a sudden rebirth of reason — and the exact point in your sequence where you will defuse it; (4) the niche framing that period labels function like 'boundary conditions' in physics, deciding which events count inside a system and which are treated as external noise, plus how you will present this without requiring specialist philosophy of science; and (5) the check-for-understanding question you will ask at the close, alongside one explicit refusal to cover a topic that would derail the session.
