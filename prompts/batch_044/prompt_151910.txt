I am preparing a single ninety-minute seminar for an MFA fiction workshop—writers fluent in narrative craft and metaphor, but with no formal logic beyond high school—where I want to treat Hofstadter’s MU-puzzle and the idea of JOOTSing, or jumping out of the system, not as mathematical curiosities but as precise analogues for how a fictional world generates internal rules that trap its characters while allowing the author to recognize the frame itself. I need the teaching plan itself, not the delivered lecture: the exact sequence in which the components must be introduced and what each depends upon; the one carrying scenario or constrained writing exercise that will make the distinction between in-system derivation and out-of-system recognition visceral rather than notational; the misconceptions these particular students are likely to form—such as conflating an author breaking the fourth wall with a character merely violating plot consistency, or interpreting the unsolvability of MU as a failure of imagination rather than as an intrinsic structural feature—and the precise moment in the sequence where each should be intercepted; a brief check for understanding that reveals whether they have grasped that JOOTSing is not a rule-violation but a recognition of the frame; and an explicit statement of what must be left out at this level, said plainly so I do not drift into it. Frame every step through narrative mechanics and scene logic rather than symbolic notation, but do not request bibliographies, further readings, or citations.
