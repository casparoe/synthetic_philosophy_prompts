My guest is a philosopher, let's call her Dr. Mara Okonkwo, who just published a book arguing that Buddhist anatta isn't just a mystical denial but actually solves the personal-identity problem more cleanly than psychological continuity, animalism, or the narrative view. She says the skandhas do all the functional work without smuggling in a hidden subject, and she thinks Western philosophy keeps missing this because it assumes persistence has to be ownership.

I need eight to ten sharp interview questions, definitely not the soft "how did you find Buddhism?" stuff. At least one has to press the karma and rebirth objection head-on: if there's literally no self persisting, who gets reborn and who recieves the karmic payoff? I want a follow-up keyed to the reply she'll almost certainly give, which is that conventional truth operates with a practical self — push her on whether that's just psychological continuity with Sanskrit branding. Another question should ask whether anatta is actually distinguishable from Hume's bundle theory, and the follow-up should hit the Buddhist claim that the aggregates are momentary in a way Hume's impressions never were. I also want something on autobiographical memory and first-person authority: if no self owns them, what makes "my" past mental state mine rather than just causally linked? For each, build in the likely answer and then a harder second punch. My editor said over lunch that Okonkwo sounds like she's collapsing into nihilism about moral agency, so please include a question that tackles that but doesn't let her escape with a vague Middle Way handwave. Keep the tone conversational — I'm genuinely curious — but my listeners want the weeds, not a press release.
