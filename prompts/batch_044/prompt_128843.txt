ok so i've been trying to figure out where i actually stand on hard choices when values don't seem to share a common currency. not just which is bigger but when comparing them feels like comparing shapes to sounds. here are my gut calls:

1. picking between a career in public defence and one in music, where both feel like expressions of something core. i think there's literally no fact of the matter which is better, but once i commit i shouldn't keep treating the other path as a loss that needs justifying.

2. tearing down an irreplaceable historical neighborhood to build flood walls that save thousands. do it, but there's a permanent moral residue - it's not that culture lost on a scale, it's that the wrongness doesn't disappear just because lives were saved.

3. breaking a promise to a close friend to help a stranger having a heart attack. help the stranger. and weirdly i don't feel any residue about the broken promise - the value of the promise just doesn't apply in the same space.

4. deciding whether to publish a flawed but inspiring scientific result that might mislead people but could also spark real progress. suppress it. here there IS a right answer, but the lost potential feels like a different kind of tragedy, not a weighed-against tragedy.

5. having kids knowing it will kill your capacity for a specific kind of long-form artistic or political work. i keep flip-flopping. some days it feels like case 1 - incommensurable, just pick. other days it feels like there's an answer and i'm being selfish if i pick art.

is there one principle behind all this? maybe something like value-blocking vs value-coexistence where in some domains values can silence each other but in others they just sit there in parallel? and what should i say about case 5, the one i can't stabilize? also please tell me what your principle predicts for a case i haven't mentioned - say, choosing between two equally good medical treatments where one preserves your memory of a dead loved one and the other doesn't but works slightly better. and be honest about which of my verdicts your principle has to overturn to stay clean.

please seperate what's actually settled in this area from what's still genuinely fought over - don't present a contested view like it's the obvious consensus.
