Write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines, followed by a three-line envoi—spoken in the first person by Maxwell’s demon, but treating the demon as a physical information-processing mechanism rather than a disembodied mind. Fix these six end-words and rotate them through the stanzas in the standard sestina order (stanza one ending gate / record / heat / observe / erase / pay; stanza two cost / gate / observe / record / erase / heat; and so on through the prescribed permutations, with all six gathered in the envoi): gate, record, heat, observe, erase, pay. The poem’s repeating lexical cycle should do structural work: each rotation should feel like another unavoidable loop in which opening the gate for fast particles demands a record, the record must eventually be erased, and every erasure pays its minimal thermodynamic price in heat, so that the form itself enacts the demon’s entrapment in entropy.

Let the fourth stanza be the volta or turn: here the demon must recognize that it is not outside the system it sorts. Its own memory is made of physical degrees of freedom—bits are not ethereal but embodied—so observing a particle is itself a thermodynamic interaction that leaves traces. To keep the idea from being a generic parable, inject the “demon’s demon” regress: the heat generated by the first demon’s erasures must be observed and managed by a second mechanism, which in turn records data and must erase them, producing more heat, so that intelligence and dissipation are inseparable. The poem should render this accurately enough that someone familiar with the physics of information recognizes the logic of Landauer’s principle (erasure costs heat) and Szilard’s insight that measurement carries an entropic price, yet it should stand as poetry for a reader who has never encountered the thought experiment.

Keep the tension unresolved: do not let the demon escape its loop, and do not turn the poem into either a sermon about intervention or a shrug about natural order. The envoi should hold all six end-words together in close proximity—gate, record, heat, observe, erase, pay—without dissolving the cycle they form. Do not include bibliographies, citations, named papers, or reading lists; if you allude to specific thinkers or principles, do so only as conceptual landmarks, not as scholarly references. Aim for about 36 lines of precise, image-driven blank verse within the sestina’s rigid architecture, so that the philosophical argument is carried by the rhyme-scheme of repetition rather than by exposition alone.
