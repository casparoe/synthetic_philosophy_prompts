I serve on our university's Committee on Institutional Equity and Resource Allocation. We must decide on a proposal to consolidate three specialized physical archives—containing irreplaceable artifacts used by a small cohort of historical philology graduate students—into a centralized digital repository. The administration argues that this serves thousands at lower cost; the affected students (fewer than ten) claim that direct material engagement is practice-constitutive for their research and that sacrificing them for aggregate gain violates a contractualist constraint: institutional policies must be justifiable to each person affected, not merely optimal in sum.

The committee is divided between those who see this as a routine aggregation problem—where benefits clearly outweigh localized harms—and those who think contractualism blocks treating serious, non-transferable burdens on specific individuals as fungible with widespread minor benefits. We need a formal memo recommending approval, rejection, or approval with binding conditions, including a concrete time frame such as a phased implementation or a permanent dual-access covenant. The memo should engage the contractualist position on its own terms: specifically, it should address whether aggregation of benefits can ever justify a burden that destroys the conditions for a particular form of scholarly practice, and it should present the strongest honest consideration for the losing side without dismissing it. Write this for circulation before Thursday's meeting. No bibliography or citations are needed—refer to arguments and positions in general terms only.
