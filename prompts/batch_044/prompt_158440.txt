So I've been trying to wrap my head round Benatar's asymmetry and I think I'm missing something obvious. He says absence of pain is good even when there's nobody around to enjoy it, but absence of pleasure is only not-bad because there's no one to be deprived. So it sounds like he's running two different moral metaphysics at once: an impersonal one for suffering and a person-affecting one for pleasure. But I read he rejects both pure impersonalism and pure person-affecting views elsewhere, so isn't there a hidden contradiction? Or is the asymmetry meant to be a kind of modal filter that applies differently depending on whether the subject is actual or merely potential? I can't see how that doesn't collapse into saying non-existent people have a kind of shadow-status where they can be 'benefited' by not suffering but not 'harmed' by not enjoying. What's the resolution — is it about the direction of comparison, or have I just misread the scope of the prinicple?
