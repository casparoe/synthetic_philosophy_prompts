He applied it specifically to argue that a conscript drafted into an aggressive war should not be judged differently from a volunteer defending her home, because once someone wears a uniform the justice of the cause "washes off" them and they become, in some sense, structurally interchangeable with their opponents. Could you lay out the precise theses that phrase could actually express? For each reading, please say whether it's trivial or substantive, whether it's defensible or false, and—most importantly—whether it actually supports my friend's specific conclusion about not judging conscripts differently from defenders. I want a clear menu I can take back to the conversation, showing exactly which version of the idea he's relying on and where it breaks down.
