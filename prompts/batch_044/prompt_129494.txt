I'm a phd student in epistemology and philosophy of religion, and i'm trying to workshop this for a seminar on belief ethics. i keep running into an argument that seems to show james's view collapses back into clifford's, but i feel like there's a hidden slide somewhere between the framing conditions and the belief itself.

(1) Clifford's principle is that it's wrong always, everywhere, and for anyone to believe anything upon insufficient evidence.
(2) James allows belief without sufficient evidence only when the option is live, forced, and momentous.
(3) But whether an option counts as live, forced, or momentous depends on your prior beliefs about what's possible, what matters, and which alternatives are real.
(4) Those prior framing beliefs — about the reliability of induction, about your own values, about whether a religious hypothesis is even in the running — are themselves held without the kind of sufficient evidence clifford demands; nobody can prove non-circularly that their doxastic scaffolding is correct.
(5) So to invoke james's exception you must first violate clifford's principle in the framing step.
(6) Therefore james's position implicitly licenses breaking the rule in order to license breaking it for the conclusion.
(7) That makes the view either redundant (if you already accept clifford you can never reach the exception) or self-defeating (if you reach it only by already being guilty).
(8) But james clearly thinks religious belief can be a legitimate case, so (8) looks like a fixed point.
(9) So one of (1)-(7) has to go. I want to keep (2) and (8) if possible.

Where does the slip happen? Is there an equivocation between first-order belief and the meta-beliefs that frame the option space, or a scope shift that lets james quarantine the framing beliefs from clifford's scrutiny? And if i reject (4) — say, framing beliefs aren't "beliefs" in the relevant sense — what does that cost me for other parts of epistemology? I keep feeling like there's a modal slide between "you don't have evidence for p" and "you don't have evidence for treating p as live," but i can't pin it down. Which step do you reject, and what's the price?
