I've been trying to make updateless decision theory work for actually embeded agents - not just the abstract math version - by adding a rule I call "computational boundary locking". The idea is that you pre-commit to a policy, and then you treat any internal state change that comes from running that policy (noise in your hardware, stochasic reasoning steps, even corruption) as part of the policy execution rather than as new evidence about the world. So you don't update on your own glitches, but you also don't let them change your action selection outside the pre-committed plan. I think this fixes the problem where embedded UDT agents seem to either update like EDT or freeze up entirely. Anyway, I hold that this is a coherent middle ground. What would count against it? I'm especially curious about whether there are hidden correlations between my internal noise and the external environment that boundary-locking forces me to ignore, and whether that makes me miss important causal pathways.
