The satire should exaggerate real features of Franklin's practice rather than invent false ones: that he borrowed his list from earlier moralist catalogues (including Joseph Hall's writings and Quaker lists of virtues); that he inserted Humility only after a Quaker acquaintance rebuked him for pride; that he confessed Order was the virtue he never mastered and eventually relaxed strict tracking because travel disrupted consistency; and that his framework treats moral failure as a quantifiable data point without addressing whether the grader has changed. Inject one under-discussed conceptual problem: Franklin's grid grades first-order conduct (whether I was temperate) but contains no mechanism for grading the grading itself—there is no second-order virtue that asks whether my assessment of my own temperance was itself temperate.
