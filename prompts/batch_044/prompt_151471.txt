Rank the following five normative grounds for opposing residential upzoning—(1) democratic self-determination by existing residents over local land-use rules; (2) protection of property values as investment-backed expectations; (3) preservation of neighborhood character as a collective cultural good; (4) ecological carrying-capacity or infrastructure constraints; and (5) an intergenerational trustee argument that present residents hold an obligation to preserve affordable housing stock for future in-migrants rather than to exclude them—according to how strongly each supports a moral right to block new construction. Assign each ground to a tier (strong, moderate, weak, or incoherent) with a single sentence of justification, then identify which two are most frequently conflated in public NIMBY discourse and briefly explain why that conflation distorts policy evaluation.
