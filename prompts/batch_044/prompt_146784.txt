Write a short research proposal of about eight hundred to one thousand words, suitable for a philosophy workshop or small interdisciplinary grant, on whether the "inviolate level" in tangled hierarchies—of the kind Douglas Hofstadter discusses through Escher's Drawing Hands—must be reconceived as a dynamically maintained equilibrium rather than a fixed substrate. State the question precisely: is the appearance of a base level in self-referential systems an ontological feature or an artifact of the descriptive frame? Explain why existing treatments in philosophy of logic and cognitive science leave this open, noting especially their tendency to treat hierarchy as static. Propose an approach that examines analogues outside formal logic—such as collaborative authorship loops or regulatory institutions that define their own jurisdiction—to test whether mutual bootstrapping can occur without hidden dependence. Justify why this analogical shift is necessary and not merely decorative. Say what a successful result would look like, perhaps a criterion for distinguishing genuine substrate-independence from concealed reliance on a base, and say what failure would entail, such as demonstrating that every loop collapses into an implicit foundation. Give an honest assessment of the risk that the project becomes either a trivial restatement of anti-foundationalism or an overly technical formal exercise, and specify constraints—scope, method, and deliverable—that would keep it tractable within a single semester or workshop cycle. Present the proposal entirely in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as in a formal letter. Do not include a bibliography, literature review by citation, or requests for specific sources; you may refer to positions and arguments by name and will supply citations yourself.
