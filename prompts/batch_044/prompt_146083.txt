I'm working through the boundary-problem casebook in my political philosophy seminar, where we have tracked a hypothetical Democratic Boundaries Tribunal's rulings on membership in the demos. Its verdicts so far: permanent non-citizen residents of ten or more years with full tax liability and criminal-law subjection are included; citizens living abroad continuously for over fifteen years with no domestic property, tax exposure, or family ties are excluded; great apes in local sanctuaries with documented individual preference-formation are excluded; employed sixteen- and seventeen-year-olds with full tax obligations are excluded; and autonomous administrative AI systems with adaptive learning but no conscious interests are excluded. The tribunal now faces a petition from a cooperative of forty climate researchers who have resided in the jurisdiction for exactly five months each year for the past eight years, maintain shared taxable property, are fully subject to local law, participate in public hearings via digital platforms, hold citizenship elsewhere with no path to permanent residence, and seek representation in long-term urban-planning decisions affecting that property. If the tribunal aims to remain consistent with its own precedents, what should it decide? Identify the closest analog among its earlier cases, articulate the two competing rules that both accommodate the prior verdicts but diverge on this one, predict the outcome under each, offer your best-guess recommendation with reasons, state what principle the tribunal adopts either way, and propose one further case that would best distinguish the rules.
