I manage a small, invitation-only online forum devoted to philosophical discussion, where participants write under pseudonyms and a recommendation algorithm I built promotes posts that gather rapid reactions, irrespective of their depth or the author's sustained commitment to their claims. Recently, I have observed a pattern: members post provocative opinions they do not genuinely hold, then, when challenged, disavow responsibility by appealing either to the anonymity of their pseudonym or to the algorithm's amplification of their words. The result is a collective atmosphere in which everyone seems to address an invisible, abstract audience rather than particular interlocutors, and in which taking a firm stand feels both obligatory and meaningless. I am considering abolishing both the pseudonyms and the algorithm in favor of real-name posting and chronological ordering, yet I fear I might be imposing an outdated model of discourse rather than remedying a genuine philosophical defect. Is there a deeper problem with the structure of this space that makes authentic individual commitment impossible, and if so, what form of restructuring—if any—would actually address it?
