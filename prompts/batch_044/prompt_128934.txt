I've been reading about the old Malcolm-Dennett thing on whether dreams are actually experiences, and honestly it feels like everyones been recycling the same arguments since the seventies. Malcolm says if youre asleep you cant be having experiences, Dennett says thats too quick and theres some mental occurence even if its not a Cartesian theatre moment. But in my reading group we recently got stuck on this weird edge case: people who wake with clear emotional residue but literally zero narrative content — they call it affective blankness. One camp says thats proof dreams dont need phenomenal content to count as experiences; the other says it just shows dreams are post-waking constructions with emotional hangovers. We keep talking past each other. Is this stalemate really just the same old disagreement about what counts as consciousness, or is there a hidden empirical question about REM micro-arousels doing the work? Or are Malcolm and Dennett actually answering different questions — one about the ontology of sleep states, one about the epistemology of dream reports — under one heading? Diagnose whats going on, using what each side actually says, and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress: a distinction, an experiment, or an admission that the question is ill-posed. And please, dont just survey interpretations — I want to know why were stuck.
