here's the gist of my essay notes:

- honor cultures treat respect like public currency. you lose face, you have to restore it, sometimes violently. its collective and performative.
- dignity cultures (mostly modern west) say worth is internal and inalienable. insults are like bad weather — they dont change your value. but this can make people seem cold or dismissive of real harm.
- victimhood culture takes dignity's internal worth but adds that society must constantly recognize and repair harm. minor slights become evidence of systemic failure.

i want to inject this idea i call moral weather — in dignity cultures people treat offenses like rain, you grab an umbrella and move on. in victimhood cultures offenses are climate change: every small event is part of a catastrophic pattern that demands collective action. honor cultures treat them like droughts: you have to fight for water or you die.

my question is: is any of these morally superior? i feel like victimhood culture has some good points about not ignoring real harm, but also seems exhausting and maybe paralyzing. dignity sounds healthier but maybe naive about power? honor sounds brutal but at least honest about human social needs.
