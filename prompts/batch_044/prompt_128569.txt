I am designing a workshop handout for a political-philosophy seminar that keeps stalling on whether "left" and "right" are anything more than tribal labels, and I want a branching taxonomy — a classification instrument built from logical space rather than from a list of named ideologies — that sorts positions by what they actually claim about coherence, dimensionality, and the source of normative authority. Start from the root question: does the left-right distinction survive once we separate moral-psychological content (equality versus hierarchy, liberty versus authority) from pure coalitional dynamics? From there, split on whether holding each side together requires a continuous underlying dimension or permits discontinuity; then on whether both sides must agree on which institutions (market, state, family, tradition) are the primary loci of normative authority, or whether they can be defined purely negatively against each other. Here I want you to inject an unconventional framing: include a branch that treats the axis not as a spatial line but as competing answers to the prior question of where normative authority should be distributed — institutional versus dispersed — and examine whether that reconstruction makes the old vocabulary either redundant or conceptually clarified. Each leaf should carry a standard name if one exists, otherwise the label "unnamed," together with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment — for instance, having to treat single-issue movements as conceptually derivative, or explaining away the persistence of left-right language in contexts where the reconstructed dimension seems absent. Wherever two branches fail to separate genuinely distinct positions, flag them explicitly rather than papering over the overlap. Note any combination of answers that yields an unoccupied leaf, and briefly diagnose whether that gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Every node must be phrased as a concrete claim or case that someone could answer without already identifying as left or right, so the tree sorts people by belief rather than by label. Finally, please conclude with a brief reflective note stating exactly what would change your mind about the tree's structure: which empirical observation about coalition behavior, or which conceptual distinction you might have missed, would lead you to merge two branches or abandon a leaf.
