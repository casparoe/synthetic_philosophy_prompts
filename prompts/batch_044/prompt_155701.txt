I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm assembling a dataset of conceptual prompts. Please draft the decision memo of the University Resilience Council — a body of trustees, faculty senators, and external risk officers — regarding Proposal 7-B, the "Adaptive Virtù Charter."

The charter would abandon predictive five-year strategic plans in favor of rotating contingency protocols that treat external disruptions not as quantifiable risks but as manifestations of institutional fortuna requiring distributed administrative virtù at every level. The council is divided. Proponents extend Machiavelli's river image — fortune as water that can be channeled but never eliminated — into a distinction between prima fortuna, the initial shock that selects which institutions survive, and secunda fortuna, the ongoing climate of variance within an organizational form that continuously reshapes its own terrain. They argue that bureaucratic bodies must therefore cultivate adaptive capacity rather than predictive control. Opponents reply that Machiavelli's virtù is inescapably singular: scaling princely action to committee deliberation does not master fortune but diffuses accountability, turning institutional flexibility into unowned risk.

Your memo should recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, with reasoning the council members on both sides could actually respond to. It must include an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Finally, append a brief self-assessment stating how confident you are in the memo's interpretation of Machiavelli, and identify the specific point — whether the river-to-climate extension, the scalability of virtù to collective bodies, or the accountability objection — about which you are least certain.
