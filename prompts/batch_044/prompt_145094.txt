I direct a small collective that designs public installations for local neighborhoods. During a review attended by a community partner, our senior member Tariq told our junior member Priya that her design was "a disgrace to our collective reputation and to the neighborhood we serve" and urged her to withdraw it. Priya has asked me to reprimand Tariq formally, issue a statement recognizing the serious harm done to her standing and emotional well-being, and record the incident in our organizational accountability log maintained for grant-makers. Tariq replies that reputation is collective and that he was exercising direct, face-to-face accountability; he warns that treating spoken criticism as an institutional offense turns artists into fragile dependents and destroys the mutual responsibility that sustains collective work. Meanwhile, our operations lead Jordan has proposed a binding protocol: all evaluation must pass through an anonymized written board, and any spoken judgment—positive or negative—will be treated as an automatic breach of equal standing, regardless of intent or context. I must respond to all three positions, yet each seems to presuppose a different account of where moral standing comes from. Can you help me adjudicate what is actually at stake and advise which response, if any, is justified?
