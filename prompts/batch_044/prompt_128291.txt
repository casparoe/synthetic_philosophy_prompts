Write a short story in American English, told in exactly five brief scenes with no introduction or conclusion and each scene rendered in one or two sentences, about a shopper named Eli who stands in a grocery aisle trying to choose between beef and chicken by using a homemade “harvest ledger” he invented: he counts not only the farmed animal killed but also the wild rodents and insects destroyed in the crop fields that grow feed, assigning every creature the same “suffering-year” unit regardless of species. In the first three scenes Eli calculates that chicken requires more acreage per calorie and therefore more wild deaths, so he reaches for beef; in the fourth scene he notices his ledger treats a year of chicken life and a year of field-mouse life as interchangeable numbers; in the fifth scene he puts both packages back under the fluorescent lights, unsure whether the comparison collapses once you stop pretending different kinds of lives can be weighed on one scale. Keep the details grounded and ordinary—price tags, the hum of freezers, the weight of plastic trays—rather than using philosophical jargon.
