As a retired physicist who has only recently begun working through philosophy systematically, I have been studying Hofstadter's account of tangled hierarchies and strange loops, particularly his use of Escher's Drawing Hands to illustrate a loop with no inviolate level. I constructed the following argument for a reading group, but it now seems to collapse into an unacceptable dilemma—either strange loops are epistemically inaccessible, or they secretly contain an external base after all. I would like to know precisely where the slip occurs.

(1) In any hierarchical explanatory system, rules flow downward: level N determines the behavior at level N-1, but not conversely. (2) A tangled hierarchy violates this by permitting upward determination, so that properties of lower levels constrain the form of higher ones. (3) In Drawing Hands, each hand draws the other; there is no foundational stratum, making this a pure strange loop lacking any inviolate level outside it. (4) To understand or represent a system, an observer must occupy a position that is not fully determined by that system's rules—otherwise the observer's own representational capacities are themselves phenomena within the loop that require explanation, and no explanatory asymmetry remains. (5) Consequently, in a pure tangled hierarchy with no inviolate level, no observer embedded within or constituted by the loop can fully understand or represent it. (6) Yet we clearly do understand Drawing Hands: we identify the circular dependency, classify it as a strange loop, and explain the artistic illusion. (7) Therefore there must be an inviolate level—our cognitive architecture, the physical paper, or the artist's intention—that stands outside the loop and supplies the explanatory base. (8) But Hofstadter's defining claim is that strange loops are precisely those in which no such external level exists; the loop is causally and representationally closed. (9) Hence either strange loops lie beyond all comprehension (contradicting our actual grasp of them), or they do possess an inviolate level after all (contradicting their definition). Thus the concept of a genuinely closed strange loop appears incoherent.

I am particularly reluctant to surrender step (4), since it seems to capture a genuine asymmetry between explaining and being explained, yet I also do not wish to deny Hofstadter's claim in (8) that loops like Drawing Hands genuinely lack an external base.
