Following a recent episode of transient global amnesia that left me unusually attentive to the etiology of my own mental judgments, I have found myself holding a cluster of commitments about consciousness and self-knowledge that I need to check for coherence before presenting them in a seminar. I take it that: (a) epiphenomenalism is true, so qualitative experience has no causal efficacy whatsoever, not even in the formation of beliefs or utterances about itself; (b) despite this, I possess authoritative first-person knowledge of my phenomenal states through direct acquaintance rather than inference; (c) the phenomenal concepts strategy explains the explanatory gap, but only on the condition that phenomenal concepts are acquired through that direct acquaintance and not through any functional or theoretical description; (d) my justification for attributing consciousness to other people is analogical, turning on my knowledge that my own behaviour is regularly accompanied by conscious experience; and (e) phenomenal judgments are "transparent" in a specific sense I wish to defend: they disclose their intentional content but are entirely opaque to their causal origins, so the fact that such a judgment is physically produced does not, by itself, undermine its epistemic authority provided the underlying physical process is stably correlated with the phenomenal fact.

Do these five commitments form a consistent package? I am particularly unsure whether (a) and (b) are genuinely in tension or whether (e) dissolves the appearance of conflict; whether (c) introduces a hidden dependency on causal efficacy in concept-formation that (a) rules out; and whether (d) can survive once (a) and (e) are both in play, given that analogical reasoning seems to require more than mere correlation. Please rank any tensions you find by severity, indicate which pairs only appear to conflict and why, and specify the least costly revision that would restore consistency—without assuming in advance which of (a) through (e) I would be willing to abandon. Please write your response in British English, using standard philosophical terminology but without bibliographic references or citation lists.
