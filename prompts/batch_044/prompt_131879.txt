Question:
In the Sleeping Beauty problem, Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday and a fair coin is flipped. If the result is heads, she is awakened once on Monday; if tails, she is awakened on Monday and Tuesday, with her memories erased between awakenings. Upon awakening and being informed that she is now in an awakening-phase of the experiment, what credence should she rationally assign to heads? Analyze the case by distinguishing updates on centered propositions (those containing an implicit indexical such as "now" or "I") from updates on uncentered world-propositions. Then assess the halfer claim that Beauty's prior of 1/2 should remain unchanged, and evaluate whether the thirder result (1/3) can be supported not merely by counting awakening-instances but by weighting worlds according to total "experience-time"—the summed duration of conscious awareness within each world—rather than treating each awakening as an independent sample point.

Student answer:
Beauty should assign credence 1/3 to heads. Before the experiment she assigns 1/2, but upon waking she gains centered evidence: the proposition that she is now experiencing an awakening is true in three centered worlds—Monday-heads, Monday-tails, and Tuesday-tails—whereas only one corresponds to heads. Because she lacks any reason to locate herself in one centered world rather than another, she applies the principle of indifference across these three, giving heads a weight of one-third.

The halfer objection claims her prior should stay 1/2 because she was already certain she would be awakened at least once regardless of the coin. This mischaracterizes the nature of centered evidence: halfers treat the awakening as an uncentered fact about the protocol, but it is actually an indexical occurrence whose distribution differs across worlds. In heads there is only one such occurrence; in tails there are two, spread across two days. Therefore ignoring the centered structure amounts to ignoring genuinely new information.

However, the standard thirder argument that simply counts awakenings is technically flawed. The Monday and Tuesday awakenings in tails are not independent observations; they are repetitions of the same mental state in a single world. One should not treat them as separate entries in a sample space. Instead, the 1/3 result should be derived by weighting worlds according to experience-time: the tails world contains twice as much conscious experience-duration as the heads world, so the rational credence is proportional to the inverse of that weight, yielding 1/3. Thus, while counting awakenings reaches the right number for the wrong reason, the experience-time weighting provides the correct justification.
