STUDENT RESPONSE:

In updateless decision theory, the agent selects a policy by computing the logical consequences of adopting that policy across all worlds, without updating on the particular action it takes. Here, because the predictor uses logical induction over a theory that contains the agent's decision procedure, the agent's choice is a logical fact relative to that theory. The agent should therefore treat its decision as causally independent of the predictor's past computation—the predictor has already fixed the box contents based on the logical structure—while still recognizing that the logical correlation between adopting the one-box policy and the box being full remains intact, just as in standard Newcomb reasoning.

As for the self-reference loop, logical induction resolves it by converging to a fixed point in which the induction process assigns probabilities that stabilize once the agent's procedure predicts its own output. This convergence guarantees internal consistency, so there is no remaining contradiction for the agent to navigate. Consequently, the loop is dissolved rather than relocated: the agent need not update on its own choice, because the convergence has already eliminated any inconsistency. The agent should therefore One-Box, since the expected utility of that policy dominates whenever the logical correlation is preserved.

Finally, this reasoning is essentially identical to evidential decision theory. Both frameworks depend on correlational structure rather than on causal intervention, and in this self-referential setting UDT reproduces EDT's recommendation with additional formal machinery. Thus UDT offers no genuinely distinct advantage here; the updateless framing is redundant.
