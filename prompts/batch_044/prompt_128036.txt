everyone in philosophy of mind keeps saying mental representation works just like a heart: evolutionary history fixes its proper function, misrepresenting is like a malfunctioning valve, and that lets us naturalize aboutness without spooky mental norms. they use it to argue teleosematics solves the normativity problem by reducing it to biology. can you audit how far this holds? lay out the correspondance table - heart / pumping / blood / selection history / malfunction mapped onto representation / content / distal object / selection / misrepresentation - then pinpoint exactly where it collapses. in particular, hearts dont seem to represent blood flow, they just cause it; and what about a swamp heart formed by lightning that pumps correctly but has zero evolutionary history? does that analog actually support the claim that history is essential, or does it show the analogy was smuggling in the conclusion? also check whether malfunction really needs a designer-like selection process, or if statistical deviation is enough. finally, tell me whether the relaxed conclusion - that mental norms are just biological norms - survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or if the heart picture was carrying the whole argument.
