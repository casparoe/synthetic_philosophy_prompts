Two people in my study group got stuck in an argument about self-sampling vs self-indication and neither would budge. One (call her Alice) said if you're reasoning about whether you live in a "big" world with 10^20 observers or a "small" one with 10^10, SIA tells you to weight the big world by like 10 billion, because your existance is literally evidence for more observers. The other (Bob) said thats exactly the presumptuous philosopher problem Bostrom raises — you're treating your own presence as data about total population without having earned a sampling mechanism, and SSA avoids that by conditioning on being in your reference class without updating on class size.

Alice came back: but SSA still updates on being in the reference class, and if that class is people who have read Anthropic Bias its size also varies across worlds, so the line between SSA and SIA blurs once you nest reference classes. Bob said she's just playing reference-class tennis to save her intuition, and that if she applies SSA consistently she gets the Doomsday argument which she clearly doesn't buy. Alice admitted she doesn't want the Doomsday conclusion but said maybe the right fix is a hybrid rule — sample a world by cosmological prior, then sample yourself inside it — which she claimed avoids being presumptuous because the world-size update comes from the prior, not your existance. Bob said that collapses back to SIA whenever the cosmological prior is flat over observer counts, so she hasn't actually escaped anything.

Who's right about what here? I want a point-by-point adjudication of the actual arguments, not just "they're both wrong." Specifically: does nesting reference classes actually blur SSA/SIA? Is Alice's two-stage rule genuinely different, or is Bob right that it collapses? And where exactly is the presumptuous philosopher objection aimed — at SIA specifically, or at any rule that lets world-population influence your beliefs? Work through the logic, don't just cite Bostrom.
