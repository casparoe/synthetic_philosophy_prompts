I've been thinking about the ethics guidelines that govern social robots and chatbots. There's this widespread institutional rule that if an AI says something like 'I'm frustrated' or 'I want to help you,' the interface has to display some kind of disclamer — like a little icon or footnote — making clear that the feeling or intention is simulated and not 'real.' But here's the weird part: we don't require anything similar when a human worker reads from a script. A call-center rep saying 'I'm so sorry for your trouble' is performing a role, not expressing an original personal feeling, yet there's no badge on their headset saying 'derived intentionality.' Same with therapists using manualized responses, or actors in medical training simulations. The practice singles out machines for a seperate kind of epistemic warning.

What view of intentionality does that double standard presuppose? Does it only make sense if you think, with Searle, that biological brains generate original intentionality while silicon only gets the derived kind — so the disclaimer is marking an ontological gap? Or could someone who takes Dennett's line, that all intentionality is basically interpreted or assigned, defend the practice some other way, maybe by talking about trust or deception rather than metaphysics? And if the practice actually presupposes original intentionality, is it consistent with the same institutions treating animals as having real interests but no language-based intentions? I'm also wondering if the practice is philosophically underdetermined: maybe it doesn't commit you to any deep theory of mind, and is just about managing user expectations. But then why apply it only to machines and not to scripted humans?

If we replaced the underlying view with a flat interpretationist account — where there's no real distinction between original and derived, just more or less useful stances — what would have to change in how we design these interfaces? Would we drop the disclaimers for AI, or extend them to every scripted human interaction? I feel like there's a tension here but I can't quite name where the practice is inconsistent versus where it's just pragmaticaly lopsided.
