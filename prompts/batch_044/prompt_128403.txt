I am in the final stages of revising a dissertation chapter on the metaphysics of synthetic biology, and I have encountered a conceptual difficulty that seems to turn on the interaction between philosophy of language and essentialism in natural kinds. In particular, I am trying to understand how Kripke's claim that natural kind terms function as rigid designators relates to his further thesis that certain identity statements involving such terms are necessary yet discoverable only a posteriori, especially when technological intervention produces entities that are structurally indistinguishable from members of a natural kind but lack the evolutionary or causal history typically associated with kind membership.

Consider the following hypothetical case, which is central to my argument: researchers synthesise a bacterial genome de novo, producing an organism that is genome-for-genome identical to Mycobacterium tuberculosis but assembled entirely in vitro without any evolutionary lineage linking it to wild-type strains. If the term 'Mycobacterium tuberculosis' rigidly designates a natural kind, and if belonging to that kind essentially requires not merely structural features but also a particular evolutionary origin (a property we might discover only through empirical investigation), then the statement 'the synthetic organism is not Mycobacterium tuberculosis' could be necessary yet known only a posteriori. Conversely, if kind membership depends solely on present structural properties, then the synthetic entity might necessarily fall within the kind, despite its artificial provenance.

Could you please explain the conceptual structure of this tension? I want to understand whether the necessary a posteriori applies primarily to the discovery of which properties are essential to the kind, or whether it also governs the kind-membership relation itself for entities that sit at the boundary between natural and artificial origins. Furthermore, I would like to know how rigid designation constrains modal reasoning in such boundary cases: does fixing the reference of a natural kind term across possible worlds require us to preserve the evolutionary history, or only the essential properties that happen to have been discovered empirically? I am not looking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature; rather, I need a clear analytical framework that I can apply consistently when assessing synthetic organisms that replicate the phenotypic and genotypic profile of established natural kinds.
