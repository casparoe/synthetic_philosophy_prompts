Read Pretty Woman through the lens of sex-work policy — the Nordic model, German/Dutch legalization, and NZ decriminalization. Vivian's arc is literally "buyer rescues worker," which sounds like a Nordic-model fantasy: demand is the problem, exit is the win. But look closer — she has no contract, no peer backup when Kit gets hurt, no way to complain except through Edward's social capital. Does that invisibilty of labor rights illustrate what NZ-style decriminalization tries to fix (brothel rules, peer networks, legal personhood)? Or does the romance just hide those institutions?

Map the key scenes — the polo match humiliation, the bathrobe/necklace negotiation, the hotel manager stepping in — onto what each model actually does: criminalize buyers, license brothels, treat it as labor with occupational health rules. Where does the fairy-tale rescue structure make collective/policy solutions invisible by design? And does Edward's personal transformation actually answer the institutional question, or is it a red herring? The film never shows a brothel, a peer organizer, or a labor inspector — is that absence just Hollywood, or structurally necessary?
