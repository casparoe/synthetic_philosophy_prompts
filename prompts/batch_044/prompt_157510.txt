I retired from experimental physics about five years ago and have been slowly working my way into philosophy since then, mostly metaphysics and philosophy of math. Lately I've become convinced of a hard finitist line about actual infinities: I think Cantor's completed infinite sets are formal fictions with no real ontological weight, and Hilbert's hotel isn't just a charming paradox but something closer to a reductio ad absurdum. The idea that a fully occupied hotel can still recieve more guests violates the part-whole principle - that the whole must be greater than any proper part - which strikes me as a basic condition on any coherent ontology. Potential infinity, like counting that never terminates or a physical process that keeps going, is all we ever actually encounter in nature or computation; treating that as a completed totality seems like a category mistake. Of course I know mathmaticians formalize this stuff all the time, but I take that as useful bookkeeping rather than a description of reality.

But I don't want to just sit in an echo chamber, so I'm asking honestly: what would count against this? Not just appeals to authority or lists of useful applications - I want the actual defeaters. Are there formal results showing finitism can't recover the analysis we actually need? Is there any physical phenomenon that genuinely requires a completed infinite collection rather than just an unbounded process? And what about the part-whole principle itself - is there a defensible version that applies to sets but not to infinite ones, or is that just special pleading? I'd like you to rank what's fatal versus what's merely awkward, and to say whether defenders of Cantorian infinity take any of these as genuinely damaging or whether they've mostly qualified the view into unfalsifiability. I also wonder if there's a hidden circularity in using potential infinity to ground mathematics - would exposing that damage my position or theirs? Basically I want to know how exposed my view actually is, including whether I've accidentally insulated it from evidence.
