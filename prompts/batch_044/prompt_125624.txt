I'm presenting at a moral philosophy workshop next Thursday on a view I call weighted non-aggregation in rescue cases. I start from Taurek's claim that in the classic one-versus-many rescue scenario the numbers shouldn't determine whom we save, but I restrict this: it holds only when the competing groups are composed of strangers lacking relational ties. Once we introduce what I'm calling structured vulnerability—situations where the larger group contains nested dependency relations such as parent-child pairs or care networks—the moral calculus shifts, not by reverting to utilitarian summation, but because the dependency structure generates a non-aggregative claim of a different kind, one that weighs the preservation of relational units rather than the counting of individual lives. The audience is precisely this mix: normative ethicists who teach Singer and Kamm and will smell a hidden aggregation; two decision theorists who think the whole framing is just confused expected-value reasoning with extra steps; a political philosopher working on relational equality who will care about whether my view privileges private dependency over public solidarity; and a graduate student in effective altruism who will push longtermist and astronomical-scale implications. I need a Q&A prep sheet ordered by how damaging each objection is, not by speaking order. For each, phrase it the way that particular interlocutor would put it, give me a two-minute reply, flag exactly where the reply is soft, tell me what I should concede up front and what I must hold at all costs, and include the question I am hoping nobody asks—specifically the one that forces me to say whether my view permits letting a small town drown if saving them requires diverting resources from a global pandemic response where the numbers are genuinely astronomical and the relational structures are diffuse. My own argument sketch is available if needed: the core move is that dependency relations change the modal status of harm from distributive to systemic, but I know that move is doing the heavy lifting and needs defending.
