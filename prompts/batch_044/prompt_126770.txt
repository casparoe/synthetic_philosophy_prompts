Please construct an argument that Adam Smith's opposition to colonial empire, when interpreted through his distinction between productive and unproductive labor and his account of capital circulation, supports a stronger institutional critique than the familiar claim that colonies are merely fiscally burdensome. Specifically, defend the view that imperial administration functions as a mechanism for converting circulating capital into fixed, non-productive administrative apparatus—thereby inverting what Smith calls the natural progress of opulence—not only in the colonies but also in the metropole. Your argument should explain why the true cost of maintaining mercantilist colonial privileges is best measured not in direct tax expenditure but in foregone accumulation of productive capital, and should show how this capital-diversion thesis accounts for Smith's judgment that such empires remain structurally self-defeating even during periods of apparent commercial prosperity.
