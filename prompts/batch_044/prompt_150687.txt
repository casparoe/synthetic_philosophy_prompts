I'm designing a final assignment for an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar, and my students have been arguing about whether taking a cognitive-scientific thesis "seriously but not literally" is actually possible. To push the exercise further, I want them to see what happens when a real, defensible view is treated as literal institutional doctrine. You are a philosopher of mind with deep familiarity with analogy, categorization, and conceptual structure, and also a writer of deadpan bureaucratic satire. Please begin with brief brainstorming: list three specific analogical mappings that a fictional research institute might treat as its foundational metaphors—mappings that exaggerate genuine commitments associated with Douglas Hofstadter and Emmanuel Sander's work on analogy and cognition, such as the claim that categorization is analogical, that high-level thought emerges from low-level analogical mapping, or that conceptual boundaries are fluid and context-dependent rather than fixed by essential properties. Then, using those mappings as internal scaffolding, write a parody job advertisement—approximately 600 words, in the flat, official voice of a university human-resources posting—for a tenure-track position in the "Department of Essential Analogies" at the fictional "Institute for Pure Cognition." The institute's founding charter holds the extreme reductionist thesis that analogy-making is not merely central to cognition but its sole substrate: perception, memory, logic, moral judgment, and emotional response are all taxonomized as analogical mappings operating at different scales of abstraction, with no remainder.

The satire should come from getting the actual conceptual content right rather than inventing straw features. Include a "Required Qualifications" section that lists competencies in a way that quietly eliminates anyone who believes in non-analogical cognition—treating terms like "direct perception," "symbolic computation," or "non-representational awareness" as disqualifying heresies. Add a bullet-point section on "Institutional Culture" describing peer review, tenure standards, and office rituals entirely in analogical terms; for instance, tenure should be granted only when a candidate's research program can be mapped onto the institute's founding analogy without structural loss. The tone must remain deadpan and legible: a specialist should recognize that the parody exaggerates real features of the analogy-centered view (the ubiquity of analogy in conceptual thought, the rejection of rigid essences, the idea that concepts are defined by family resemblances and contextual mappings), while a reader without specialized background should still understand that an extreme reductionist doctrine is being mocked. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote verbatim from any source; paraphrase all conceptual claims in your own institutional jargon. Keep the piece self-contained so that a student without web access could analyze the satire using only the text and general knowledge of cognitive science. Think carefully about how each bureaucratic detail—salary bands, committee structures, parking assignments—can be made to carry analogical meaning without breaking the deadpan voice.
