I am preparing a philosophical profile on pain, value, and neurological dissociation for a graduate seminar, and I would like a consistency audit before I present it. Here are the commitments I have arrived at:

1. The intrinsic badness of pain is constituted by its phenomenal affective quality—the specific hurting character—rather than by its sensory-informational content or by its causal-behavioral outputs.

2. Clinical cases of pain asymbolia demonstrate a genuine dissociation: patients can retain sensory discrimination of noxious stimuli and continue to apply pain-language correctly, yet the affective-motivational component is disrupted; in these cases, the residual experience lacks intrinsic badness.

3. Phenomenal consciousness is necessary but not sufficient for intrinsic axiological status. For a mental state to generate direct normative reasons—specifically, reasons for intervention—the subject must possess at least a minimal, possibly subpersonal, conative or evaluative orientation toward that state.

4. Nevertheless, I maintain that if a being experienced the full phenomenal profile of hurt, including its affective quality, but was constitutionally devoid of any conative or evaluative architecture whatsoever, that state would still be intrinsically bad and there would be a direct reason to relieve it.

5. I reject evolutionary debunking arguments against judgments of pain's badness, on the ground that these judgments are formed through direct phenomenological acquaintance rather than through evolved inferential mechanisms that selection might have skewed.

6. I take reflective equilibrium—not evolutionary genealogy, nor unmediated introspective report—to be the correct methodological framework for testing axiological claims about mental states.

Please audit this set. Identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each real tension, specify exactly which commitment must be qualified, restricted, or abandoned, and state what the revision costs me in terms of the overall view. Do not treat every pairing as equally problematic; distinguish the severe inconsistencies from the minor frictions. I am not asking for a general essay on pain asymbolia or on value theory—just a point-by-point assessment of whether these six commitments hang together.
