Okay so I'm a novelist, working on a story about a shipyard town where the same boat gets rebuilt over and over, and I kept noticing this weird double standard in how people talk about 'the same thing' that I can't find a word for. Let me lay out the pattern with a couple concrete examples.

First, take a physical object — like my protagonist's old fishing boat — where every plank, nail, and rope gets replaced slowly over years. Most people say it's still her boat, just restored. But then if you gather all the original rotten planks and nails and rebuild them into an identical boat across the harbor, suddenly everyone points at that second one and says no, thats the real one, like the gradual replacement was just a slow kind of theft. That's the classic ship puzzle, I get that.

But here's the part that confuses me: flip the category from wood and metal to people and roles. Imagine a theater troupe — call them the Driftwood Players — that replaces every actor and stagehand one by one over a decade, but keeps the same name, same theater, same schedule. People say sure, it's still the Driftwood Players. Now imagine the opposite: all the original cast and crew get fired, leave together, and start a rival company in a different town with a new name but the same plays. In that case people treat the fired group as carrying thier real identity — 'the original Driftwood people' — and the old theater with new hires is just a shell wearing the name.

So depending on whether we're dealing with physical parts or social members, we reverse which path — continuous replacement or reassembly of originals — counts as preserving the thing. Is there an established name for that reversal? Or for the broader pattern where our intuition about same object depends on whether we treat the parts as carrying the essence versus the structure carrying it? I'm not looking for a bare label; I want to know exactly what the phenomenon covers, where it breaks down, and how it's different from just essentialism or functionalism thrown around loosely. If there's no term, can you coin one with clear edges and boundary cases? I'd rather understand the carving than memorize a word.
