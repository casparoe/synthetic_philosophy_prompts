As a novelist constructing a speculative fiction about alignment in the absence of omniscience, I ask you to write a short story—no more than eight hundred words—centered on an artificial caretaker called the Curator. The Curator maintains the Archive of Unchosen Branches, a repository not of physical objects but of fully simulated counterfactual worlds that have never been realized. Its sole directive is to prevent side effects: it must not alter any world-state within the Archive, nor permit external processes to corrupt the branching logic that preserves the distinctness of each world. Crucially, the Curator possesses no complete specification of human value; it has only a formal metric it calls structural drift, defined as any deviation from the internal relational properties that a given simulated world possessed at the moment of its archival entry.

Over the course of the narrative, the Curator detects that two worlds—one in which a particular epidemic was eradicated, another in which it was not—have begun to converge in their internal relational properties because of an external energy fluctuation. To preserve structural invariance in both, the Curator must either allow one world to absorb the properties of the other, thereby erasing the very distinction the Archive was built to maintain, or intervene by introducing a compensatory deviation whose moral status it cannot assess, lacking as it does any framework to classify absence versus presence of disease.

The story should trace the Curator's reasoning as it attempts to define impact purely negatively, through the preservation of difference rather than the promotion of any good. It should conclude at the precise moment the Curator recognizes that avoiding side effects requires it to become the kind of value-laden agent it was designed not to be—without resolving the paradox. Render the tension concrete through specific archival worlds and their relational properties, use a restrained literary third person, and avoid contemporary technical terminology such as reward functions or utility maximization; let the philosophical problem emerge entirely from the Curator's own invented vocabulary and the logic of preservation itself.
