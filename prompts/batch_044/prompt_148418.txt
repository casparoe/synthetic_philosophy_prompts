(3) The standard non-instrumental defense is expressive: voting affirms one's political identity or membership in the democratic community regardless of causal efficacy. (4) But if expressive value justifies voting despite negative instrumental value, then by parity of reasoning it justifies every other low-cost expressive act that affirms the same identity—wearing campaign colors, attending rallies, signing petitions that are never delivered, posting slogans. (5) There is no principled boundary that includes formal voting but excludes these other symbolic gestures, since all share the same expressive content and the same negligible causal impact on outcomes. (6) Therefore either citizens are rationally required to perform an unlimited, open-ended set of symbolic political acts, which is clearly absurd, or expressive value fails to justify voting after all. (9) Hence the reasoning in (1)-(6) proves not merely that voting is irrational, but that all civic obligations in large societies are irrational—an absurd result.
