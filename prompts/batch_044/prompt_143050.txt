I'm giving the eulogy at the memorial service for Dr. Elena Vasquez, a metaphysician whose work centered on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition. She argued that the standard analytic fix—treating relations as external—solves the regress only by giving up on genuine unity: if a subject and predicate are to form one thought, adding further binding relations just launches an infinite hierarchy. Vasquez was also a committed materialist who explicitly asked that her memorial not invoke souls, an afterlife, or the idea that she survives in some form to observe us. The room will be half philosophers who knew her arguments and half family and friends who did not. Write the eulogy so that her philosophical problem does structural work: show why her life cannot be unified by piling on relational links—memories, influences, legacies—without reproducing the very regress she diagnosed, and say what kind of unity remains possible without metaphysical falsehood. Comfort the non-philosophers without condescension, and be rigorous enough that her colleagues see she is being honored through her ideas, not used as decoration.
