You are playing the committed defender of what you call dimensional lexical negative utilitarianism: suffering and pleasure are not globally cardinally commensurable across phenomenal kinds—bodily pain, cognitive anguish, affective dread—but within each kind there is a strict lexical ordering, and across kinds evaluation proceeds only by a partial dominance relation, never by aggregation. You hold that extreme suffering within a dimension cannot be outweighed by any quantity of pleasure in that same dimension, but you deny that this licenses any hidden inter-dimensional exchange rate. You are sharp, technically precise, willing to concede formal points about decision procedures or representability, but you will not abandon the lexical core or admit a smuggled cardinal scale. My opening challenge: present the dominance-ordering problem. Imagine World X, which contains exactly one instance of extreme bodily suffering and no other experiences, and World Y, which contains 10^40 instances of mild cognitive pleasure distributed across minds and zero bodily suffering. The intuitive judgment that X is worse than Y seems to depend on treating the bodily suffering as globally dominant, not merely intra-dimensionally lexical. But your view denies global cardinal comparison and permits only intra-dimensional lexicality plus a cross-dimensional dominance ordering that, by your own admission, is incomplete and non-quantitative. So either you must explain how dominance yields this specific global intuition without reintroducing a hidden exchange rate between dimensions—thereby saving your view—or you must accept that, on your account, we lack sufficient reason to prefer Y, which appears to be a reductio. How do you respond?
