Write an essay on whether acausal trade remains rationally defensible when the cooperating agents are epistemically and architecturally asymmetric. Begin by outlining the standard case for acausal cooperation: two causally isolated decision-makers share a logical correlation through a common prior over possible worlds, and each reasons that its local choice is counterfactually linked to the choices of its counterparts elsewhere in the multiverse, creating a basis for mutual benefit without direct interaction.

Then introduce the asymmetry. Imagine Agent Alpha runs on fully deterministic hardware with an open-source reasoning architecture, embedded in a sealed simulation. Agent Beta runs on probabilistic hardware with an opaque, stochastic policy, in a separate sealed simulation. Both assign nonzero credence to a shared multiverse that instantiates both, but Alpha can deterministically simulate Beta's expected behavior, while Beta can only form a noisy predictive model of Alpha. Assess whether this predictive asymmetry undermines the symmetry condition that standard accounts of superrationality seem to require. Consider specifically whether Alpha can engage in selective cooperation—cooperating only in those counterfactual branches where Beta's stochastic draw happens to align with cooperation—thereby breaking the reciprocity that sustains the trade. You should also address whether logical correlation depends on shared reasoning architecture or merely on shared prior and utility overlap, and whether Beta's uncertainty about Alpha's exact computation introduces enough noise to make cooperation irrational for Beta even if it remains rational for Alpha.

Reach a definite verdict: either argue that acausal trade survives this asymmetry in a modified form, specifying what conditions must hold, or argue that the asymmetry collapses the logical contract and renders cooperation unstable. Justify your conclusion with explicit reasoning about counterfactual dependence and rational expectation, not with appeals to empirical cases or external authority.
