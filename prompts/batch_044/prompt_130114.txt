I run a grad workshop on moral psych, 8 people, 90 min. I wanna run an in-class game that makes participants feel the actual structural tension in Benjamin Franklin's moral perfection scheme — not the generic habit-tracker version everyone knows, but the specific ordering he used (Temperance first because he thought it enabled the rest) and the hidden tradeoffs he kept hitting where pushing hard on Order or Silence made Humility collapse. Design it so each person gets a paper grid with black marks for failures across three rounds, but the rules secretly tie virtues together: every point gained in one automatically costs a point in another, so nobody can ever fully clear the board. They should run into the frustration themselves before any debrief. Include the round-by-round rules, materials (just paper and tokens), a payoff/scoring scheme with no real money, and a debrief that maps the grid onto Franklin's actual sequence and his admission that he never achieved perfection despite decades of trying. Also include an honest note on what feature of his view the game can't reproduce — probably the religious framing behind the virtues. Don't say Franklin's name or list all 13 virtues until the reveal at the end; let them think it's a generic self-improvement exercise until then.
