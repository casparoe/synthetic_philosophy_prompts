I've been reading about induction and Goodman's emerald example — green before some date, blue after, people call it a cheat predicate. But I see the same shape everywhere in science: a drug "works", then it fails in kids, so they say "except pediatrics"; a law of physics applies "except at quantum scales". Each exception is reasonable alone, but together they build a hidden boundary just like the grue case. Yet explicit made-up predicates get mocked while these implicit domain-carves are seen as good practice. Is there a specific name for that double standard — rejecting obvious grue-style predicates but accepting hidden ones with the same logical form? Or is this just the standard problem of induction, and if so what's the usual way to tell a legit auxiliary restriction from a sneaky grue-ification? I keep feeling there's a distinction here that nobody has named.
