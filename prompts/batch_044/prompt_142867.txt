I'm trying to nail down my stance on time and relativity for a grad seminar I'm running next term—mostly students in phil of science with some physics background—so I wrote out what I actually think and now I'm worried the package is incoherent. Here are my commitments, roughly ordered by how strongly I hold them:

(1) The Rietdijk-Putnam argument is basically sound: special relativity rules out any global hyperplane of simultaneity, so presentism is false unless you're willing to reject well-confirmed physics.

(2) Even so, I think causation is genuinely asymetric—not reducible to entropy or low-entropy boundary conditions—there's a real directedness that needs metaphysical explaining.

(3) I also want to save the phenomenology of temporal flow by treating 'now' as indexical, like 'here': it explains why experience feels directional without committing me to presentist ontology.

(4) But I'm also committed to saying that in a block universe all times exist equally, which makes me nervous about (2): if the effect-event is already 'there' in the four-dimensional manifold, in what sense does the cause bring it about?

(5) Finally, I'm skeptical that special relativity will survive intact at the quantum-gravity scale, though I don't want to hang my metaphysics on speculative physics.

So: does (3) actually undermine (1) in some hidden way, or are they genuinely consistent? Is (2) in deep tension with (4), and if so which one has to go? And is (5) just a cop-out that lets me avoid the hard conclusion of (1)? Please rank the tensions by severity, say what the cheapest revision is for each, and tell me which single change would clean up the most problems—though I should warn you I'm pretty attached to (2) and reluctant to drop (3). Also, please separate what's settled in the literature from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and don't present a minority view as the consensus.
