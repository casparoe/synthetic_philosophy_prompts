I am trying to work out a consistent interpretive package on Marx, automation, and the labour process for a philosophy of technology reading group, and I need an audit of whether the following commitments cohere.

(1) I interpret the Grundrisse discussion of machinery—often referred to as the Fragment on Machines—as Marx arguing that with full automation, direct labour time ceases to be the measure of wealth, which creates an immanent contradiction for capitalism since surplus value still requires living labour.

(2) I nonetheless maintain that in Capital Marx never abandons the labour theory of value; rather, he treats the falling rate of profit and the rising organic composition of capital as ways capitalism attempts to manage, not escape, this contradiction.

(3) I take Marx to be a technological determinist in a specific, non-reductive sense: the development of fixed capital and the general intellect eventually makes capitalist property relations historically obsolete, but this is a boundary-condition claim, not a claim that machinery causes revolution by itself.

(4) At the same time, I hold that Marx’s theory of transition is irreducibly voluntarist: conscious class struggle is necessary, and socialism is not an automatic output of advanced automation.

(5) I believe that the machinery developed under real subsumption—the direct domination of labour by capital—can be redeployed under socialism without carrying over its capitalist social form, because Marx distinguishes the technical basis from the social form of production.

Are these jointly tenable? Specifically, is there a hidden tension between (1) and (2): if automation genuinely makes labour time obsolete as a measure, how can Capital maintain that living labour remains the sole source of surplus value without contradiction? Does (3) genuinely avoid determinism, or does the idea that technology makes old relations obsolete implicitly reduce revolutionary agency to an epiphenomenon of machinery, thereby undermining (4)? And does (5) do real work, or is it a distinction without a difference—if the machinery was developed specifically to extract surplus value and control the labour process, can it be separated from that logic?

Please rank any genuine tensions by severity. Identify which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve on inspection—particularly whether the determinism in (3) and the voluntarism in (4) can be reconciled as setting conditions versus selecting mechanisms. For each real tension, say what I would have to give up and what that costs. Then tell me which single revision—dropping or modifying one commitment—would resolve the most conflicts with the least damage to the overall view. Answer in British English.
