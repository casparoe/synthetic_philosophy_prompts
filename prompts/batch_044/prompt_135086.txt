Write a deadpan parody of an institutional conservation manual, approximately 600 words, issued by a fictional "Bureau of Aesthetic Hygiene." Present formal protocols for "morally decontaminating" artworks so that the work may be displayed independently of its creator's biography. Treat the separation literally: describe technical procedures—quarantine periods, solvent baths for "biographical residue," spectral analysis of ethical stains, and certification of "artist-independent aesthetic value." The satire should exaggerate real positions in the debate over whether immoral art can be good art (aesthetic autonomy, the intentional fallacy, and whether moral knowledge alters aesthetic judgment) without naming specific living figures. Include at least one invented technical metric or unit—such as a coefficient measuring moral-formal dissociation—that sounds plausible but is your own coinage. Maintain a rigorously bureaucratic, deadpan tone with no winking; the irony should come from the literalization of "separating the work from the artist" and from pushing autonomy arguments to absurd procedural extremes, readable both to someone familiar with the philosophy and to someone who is not.
