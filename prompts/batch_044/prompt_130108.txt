As a retired molecular geneticist who has turned to philosophy of science in retirement, I will present a seminar paper next month to a philosophy of biology reading group. The audience is composed primarily of philosophers who defend informational or teleosemantic accounts of genetic function—several treat the genetic code as a literal semantic mapping rather than a heuristic metaphor—together with two practicing molecular biologists who regard the code language as instrumentally useful but ontologically neutral. My thesis is that DNA is not a semantic code in any philosophically substantive sense; it operates as a chemically reactive substrate whose transcriptional and translational dynamics are continuously mediated by cellular context, and the informational framing imposes a false discreteness on these biochemical processes that actively obstructs conceptual clarity. I require a Q&A preparation sheet: the objections this precise audience will actually raise, ordered from the most damaging to my position to the least; each objection phrased in the vocabulary its proponents would use—philosophers pressing issues of representational content, evolutionary function, and intentionality, scientists pressing predictive success and operational necessity; for each, a concise reply calibrated for a two-minute response, with an explicit note on where that reply is soft or vulnerable; and an indication of whether the objection originates chiefly with the philosophers or with the biologists. Include also the single objection I am hoping nobody asks—the concealed weakness in my own argument that, if pressed, would collapse the presentation. Do not provide bibliographies, citation lists, or further reading recommendations; I seek only the strategic map.
