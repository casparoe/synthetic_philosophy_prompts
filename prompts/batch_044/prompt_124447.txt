Consider the philosophical question of when an informational relation counts as a genuine code—characterized by arbitrary symbol-to-meaning mapping mediated through an interpretive system—rather than as causal correlation, structural complementarity, social convention, or metaphor. I have formed intuitive verdicts about a range of concrete and hypothetical systems, and I want to know whether they cohere under a precise principle, what that principle predicts for scenarios I have not listed, and which of my judgments is the weakest link that should be dropped for the sake of consistency.

My firm verdicts are as follows. DNA directing protein synthesis via triplet codons, tRNA adaptors, and ribosomes is a real code. So is a Python script executed by a compiler and CPU. So is human written language, and so is the honeybee waggle dance, in which movement patterns arbitrarily specify direction and distance. By contrast, a falling barometer preceding a storm is mere physical correlation. The immune system’s VDJ recombination generating antibodies that structurally match antigens is causal lock-and-key fit, not coding. The firing patterns of hippocampal place cells correlating with a rat’s spatial location seem representational, but I do not think they constitute a code in the philosophically relevant sense, since there is no discrete symbolic syntax being interpreted. Traffic lights—red for stop, green for go—feel like a minimal or “thin” code: the mapping is conventional and arbitrary, yet there is little combinatorial structure. A social “black-tie dress code,” however, strikes me as pure convention without syntax or an interpreter that transforms representations, so I judge it not to be a code at all. A smoke detector responding to particulate density is also a clear non-case: direct causal triggering.

The case I keep reversing on is a hypothetical synthetic “molecular printer.” This device reads artificial DNA containing non-natural base pairs and, using only a chemically embedded lookup table in its substrate—no ribosomes, no cells, no evolutionary history—synthesizes corresponding polymer chains. Sometimes I think it is still a code, because the sequence-to-product mapping is arbitrary and mediated by a physical interpreter (the printer’s substrate). At other times I think that, absent biological machinery or an intentional agent maintaining the mapping, the process collapses into complex chemistry no different in kind from enzyme-substrate specificity.

Given these verdicts, please propose one or more precise principles—perhaps appealing to arbitrariness of mapping, presence of an interpreter, combinatorial syntax, or some combination—stating clearly which clause is motivated by which case above. Then apply the principle to at least one scenario I have not listed, such as whether an AI trained to map patterns of human neural activation onto verbal self-reports is implementing, using, or merely correlating with a code. Finally, be explicit about which of my listed verdicts the principle cannot accommodate without ad hoc adjustments, and argue which one should be overturned.
