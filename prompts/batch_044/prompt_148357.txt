I am preparing an essay for my philosophy coursework on Scanlon's contractualist account and the aggregation problem. My central claim is that the framework struggles not only with aggregating simultaneous small harms across many people, but also with what I call temporal replacement aggregation: a principle might be reasonably rejectable by no single present individual because each bears only a minor burden, yet its cumulative effect across successive generations of replaced populations produces catastrophic harm to those future groups. I argue that this reveals an implicit reliance on synchronic personhood in the idea of reasonable rejection, and I propose that contractualism requires a diachronic extension to address it. I should be grateful for comments on whether I have misrepresented Scanlon's position, whether the intergenerational scenario introduces a genuinely distinct problem rather than a variant of the standard aggregation case, and whether my proposed diachronic extension is conceptually coherent. I am asking for conceptual feedback rather than a bibliography or citation check, as I shall compile references separately.
