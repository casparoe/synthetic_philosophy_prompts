Can you build a taxonomy / decision tree that sorts positions by how they answer four diagnostic splits? Fourth: when precaution conflicts with distributive justice (small farms collapse, malaria persists), is precaution a veto, an override weighted by severity, or does it dissolve?

For each leaf, name the position if it's a real named view (e.g., strong precautionary principle, risk-based critiques like Sunstein's, etc.) or mark it "unnamed" if it's an unoccupied logical combination. And please include the unoccupied combinations: e.g., maybe nobody holds "graded precaution that applies to natural risks with burden on opponents" — briefly diagnose whether that empty spot is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Make sure the tree is built from the logical space of answers, not from a list of names; I need about 8-10 leaves total, audience is philosophy grad students who know some decision theory but aren't policy experts. Take a definite stance: don't just map the space neutrally, but argue briefly for which splits are actually doing the philosophical work and which ones are red herrings. Oh, and no citations or reading lists — just the tree, the leaf descriptions, and your committed take on where the lines should be drawn.
