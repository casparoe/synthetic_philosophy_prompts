The workshop's working premise — which I want you to treat as structurally constraining, not decorative — is consciousness as a user illusion in the precise sense: subjective experience is not a transparent window onto mental states but an interface that conceals its own representational machinery, presenting a constructed self-model as immediate presence. Because the idea should shape what I can and cannot say, do not promise that we will bridge the explanatory gap, do not treat first-person authority as either absolute or dispensable, and frame clinical symptoms specifically as breakdowns in model-transparency rather than as revelations of hidden realities. Write the remarks: they should welcome the group, establish a shared vocabulary that respects clinical urgency and philosophical rigor, and set up the workshop's task — understanding where and why the interface fails — without claiming we have direct access to anyone's inner experience. Begin with the remarks themselves; include no preface, no commentary on your choices, and no restatement of this prompt.
