I'm preparing a handout for an advanced philosophy of mind workshop—about fourteen graduate students, ninety minutes—after a long argument with a colleague over whether Dennett's minimal two-bitser, a system that tracks exactly two binary distinctions (say, temperature above or below a threshold and presence or absence of a specific chemical marker), updates its internal state accordingly, and modulates behavior without any biological ancestry, conscious designer, or user who interprets its states as representational, could ever serve as a bridge between purely derived and genuinely original intentionality. Rather than asking for a comparison of named authors, I want a diagnostic taxonomy: a decision tree built from the logical space of commitments first, with actual positions mapped onto its leaves only afterward, so that someone can locate their view by answering concrete case-driven questions without knowing what philosophers call it.

The tree should begin with the two-bitser itself and split on four sequential questions, each phrased as a specific claim or counterfactual variation rather than an abstract principle. First: does representation in this system require an evolutionary or biological history of any kind? Second: does the complete absence of a conscious designer eliminate derived intentionality altogether, or merely reduce its richness? Third: is functional isomorphism between the two-bitser's state-transitions and a human belief-forming process sufficient by itself to cross into original intentionality? Fourth: do scale and integration matter—specifically, is an isolated two-bit distinction always derivative, while a densely interconnected network of such distinctions might reach a non-derivative threshold? Each leaf should carry a label—such as Strong Bio-Intentionalism, Pure Derivationism, Functionalist Crossover, Integrationist Threshold, or unnamed if the combination is unoccupied—plus a single sentence stating its most awkward commitment, for instance having to deny that a precisely engineered thermostat represents temperature at all, or having to accept that a sufficiently complex spreadsheet tracking market variables literally holds beliefs about future prices.

Please also flag combinations that are incoherent or unexplored: for example, a view that denies all derived intentionality yet accepts functional isomorphism as sufficient for original intentionality appears unstable—briefly diagnose why. And note which well-known positions, such as certain formulations of Searle's biological naturalism versus some teleosemantic accounts, the tree fails to separate given these four questions, explaining exactly where they collapse into the same leaf. The taxonomy should avoid bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific texts; it must be fully answerable by someone familiar with the general debate but without library access. Aim for six to eight leaves, include a brief scoring key that maps each sequence of four answers to its corresponding leaf, and end with an honest note on which feature of the original-versus-derived distinction the tree cannot capture and must be supplied by the facilitator.
