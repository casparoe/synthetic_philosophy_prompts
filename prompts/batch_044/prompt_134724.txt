Compose a villanelle—nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, with two refrains that alternate and finally converge in the closing couplet—centering on the borderline problem of defining life through the hypothetical case of a synthetic minimal cell. This cell possesses a stable, self-repairing lipid boundary and a replicating non-canonical nucleic-acid analog, yet it has been engineered to lack ribosomes entirely and cannot perform de novo metabolic synthesis. One refrain should articulate a boundary-maintaining, metabolic conception of life, suggesting that it constructs and preserves its own container from within; the other should articulate an evolutionary, replicator-based conception, suggesting that it varies, competes, and is subject to selection. Structure the poem so that the villanelle’s cyclical repetition enacts the entity’s oscillation: in some stanzas it clearly satisfies the metabolic refrain while failing the evolutionary one, in others the reverse, and never both together. The turn in the final quatrains should make the reader feel the classification slip away rather than settle. In the closing couplet, bring the two refrains side by side, but leave their compatibility genuinely unresolved—the poem should end in ontological ambiguity, not a verdict. The imagery and framing should be precise enough that a philosopher of biology or synthetic-biology researcher recognizes the specific tension between self-maintaining organization and Darwinian replication being staged, but do not name particular theorists, cite texts or experiments, or include any bibliographic request. Keep the language lyrical and imagistic rather than expository.
