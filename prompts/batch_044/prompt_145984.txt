I'm taking this philosphy of mind course and we read some Hofstadter about strange loops and whether a soul or self is really just a pattern that could in principle live in other heads. Now I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about whether an 'I' can be distributed across brains, going by my gut on cases.

If my brain gets split and each hemisphere is transplanted into a different body, both wake up thinking they're me with all my memories intact. I feel like for the first year or so both of them genuinely are me - it's one self spread across two bodies. Once they start having totally different experiences they diverge into separate people, sure, but initially it's not just copying.

But if you scan my brain at death and run a perfect simulation on a server that wakes up with my memories, I think that's not me at all - it's a convincing copy, like a twin who recieved all my data. No distribution there, just duplication.

Then there's my closest friend who, after I'm gone, takes over every unfinished project I had, remembers all my childhood stories, and basically continues my commitments. I wouldn't say she literally is me, but somehow she feels closer to being a real continuation than the computer scan does, even though she's a different brain with her own past.

Here's the pair that keeps breaking my intuition. There's a hypothetical bridge linking two brains so they share qualia and reasoning continuously for decades - no clear boundary, just one flow of thought across two skulls. In that case I think they might actually form one distributed 'I', not just two people cooperating. But then imagine my neurons get replaced one by one with synthetic equivalents, and each new neuron also connects into my partner's cortex. By the end there's one continuous network spanning both of us. Is that me distributed? Did I die in the middle? Or is it a brand new person?

So is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? I want something that explains why the split brain starts out distributed but the digital scan doesn't, why the friend feels like a faint echo but not real continuity, and why the bridge case might be genuine shared selfhood while the gradual synthetic replacement feels so ambiguous. Does it come down to causal continuity, pattern preservation, or the boundary of experience? And which of my verdicts do I have to throw out if I want the principle to stay clean? Also, what would your account predict for the case where only half my brain is linked into the bridge and the other half stays isolated?
