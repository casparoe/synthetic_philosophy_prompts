For a graduate seminar handout on modal metaphysics, construct a decision tree that sorts philosophical positions on whether a particular concrete object can exist in more than one possible world. Begin with a concrete diagnostic case: a ceramic vase that, in counterfactual discussion, might be (i) fired at a different temperature in another world, (ii) shattered and rebuilt with the same fragments, or (iii) matched by a qualitatively identical but causally disconnected vase elsewhere. The tree should contain four to six branch nodes, each expressed as a question a student could answer without already knowing the labels "counterpart theory", "transworld identity", "haecceitism", or "anti-realism". Essential splits must cover: whether qualitative similarity alone suffices to link an object across worlds; whether causal or spatiotemporal continuity is required; whether one world-object can have multiple counterparts in a single world; and whether there is any metaphysically privileged relation to "the actual world" that constrains which counterparts count. The leaves should include a Lewisian counterpart-theoretic position, a Kripkean transworld-identity position, a bare-identity haecceitist view, and an anti-realist or deflationary denial of cross-world individuals. Note any combination of answers that produces no named position, briefly diagnosing whether the gap is incoherent, merely unexplored, or logically possible but unattractive. Flag explicitly any pair of leaves that the branching questions cannot distinguish. Do not ask respondents to name philosophers; the instrument should classify them purely by their answers to the case-based questions.
