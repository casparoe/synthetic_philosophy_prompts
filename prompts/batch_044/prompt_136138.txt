Two colleagues were arguing about Douglas Hofstadter's distinction among frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages in Gödel, Escher, Bach. Maya said the inner message is where meaning ultimately resides: the frame is only an instrumental decoder, like a ribosome reading DNA, and if you swap the frame the interpreted result changes, but the meaning was always in that result rather than in the decoding mechanism. Jordan replied that this gets things backward — without the frame there is no interpretation at all, so the frame is constitutive of meaning, not merely causally prior to it. Maya then introduced a twist she called residual framing: even after successful decoding, structural constraints from the frame persist inside the inner message, so you can never fully isolate a pure inner content. She argued this means meaning is genuinely distributed across all three layers rather than being localized in any one. Jordan countered that residual framing only shows the frame's influence runs deeper than Maya admits; he said treating the outer syntax as carrying potential meanings across frames collapses the very distinction Hofstadter was trying to draw and leaves us with no way to separate message from medium. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: rate Maya's opening claim, Jordan's reply, Maya's residual-framing move, and Jordan's final counter, noting where each is strongest and weakest. Then say whether any of their positions successfully localizes meaning, or whether the disagreement itself reveals a problem with applying the frame/outer/inner taxonomy to self-referential or biologically analogized systems.
