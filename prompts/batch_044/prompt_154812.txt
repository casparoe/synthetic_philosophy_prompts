After a long and somewhat circular argument with a friend last night about whether conscious experience is best understood as a kind of constructed interface — a simplified display that helps an organism navigate without revealing the underlying neural machinery — I realized there is a specific argumentative loop I keep encountering but do not have a name for. The pattern runs like this: one person proposes that subjective awareness works like a user interface, a functional representation that is real in its effects but misleading about the processes that generate it. I then ask who or what is using this interface. If the answer is “the brain,” that seems to conflate the machinery producing the display with whatever receives it. If the answer is “the self,” we appear to have smuggled in precisely the entity the interface model was meant to explain away. If the answer is “no one — it is just a format for integrating information,” then I wonder why we should call it an interface at all, since the concept of an interface seems conceptually tied to a user. My friend kept shifting among these three responses, and each shift felt individually defensible, but together they formed a defensive loop: whenever the “user” was challenged as an unnecessary addition, it was redefined as a functional relation; whenever that functional relation seemed too thin to account for the feeling of being a subject, it was reified into something that genuinely receives the display. I have seen related moves in discussions of representational transparency and self-modeling, but I am not asking about those broad topics. I am asking whether there is an established concept specifically for this oscillation — between treating the conscious subject as the recipient of an interface and treating that recipient itself as part of the interface’s content. Is it a distinct variant of the homunculus problem, or does it belong to a different family of explanatory errors? If there is no standard term, how should one distinguish this loop from ordinary circular explanation, and what are its clearest boundary cases — for example, does it apply only to theories that preserve some form of first-person access, or also to views that deny representational content altogether? I will not be satisfied with a bare label; I want to know exactly what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
