Two members of a philosophy-of-mind reading group have been debating Daniel Dennett’s Orwellian and Stalinesque models of conscious revision, specifically with respect to the color phi phenomenon and the problem of experiential timing. I am reporting their exchange as a neutral observer and want an adjudication of the arguments themselves, not personal advice.

Person A opened by defending an Orwellian reading of color phi. He said that a subject genuinely experiences a green dot at location one, and only afterward—once the red dot at location two has arrived—does the brain retroactively edit the content of that prior experience, inserting an intermediate trajectory and a color shift. The result, he claimed, is that the subject’s memory of the first moment has been literally rewritten: there was a real, discrete experience that subsequently got altered.

Person B countered with a Stalinesque interpretation. She argued that consciousness never produces an isolated experience of the green dot in the first place. Both stimuli are registered, but conscious awareness delivers only a single, unified narrative—green turning to red and moving across space—after the second stimulus has been processed. The earlier experience is therefore not a rewritten record but a fabricated one, constructed at the time of report like the evidence in a show trial. There is no original truthful experiential file to corrupt.

Person A then raised an objection about temporal extension. If conscious experience is only manufactured after both stimuli have arrived, he said, then at no physical instant is there an actual present experience unfolding; there is only a belated reconstruction. Yet subjects report a continuous flow in which the green dot is present, then the transition occurs, then the red dot appears. Without a live stream, he argued, this subjective temporal thickness is left unexplained.

Person B replied that this confuses physical succession with phenomenal synthesis. She maintained that the brain constructs a phenomenal present that retains the recent past and anticipates the immediate future, giving consciousness its temporal thickness. The green-dot phase is retained within this synthesized present, not lived through as an independent present. So her view does not eliminate temporal extension; it only denies that such extension is an unedited recording of discrete physical moments.

Please evaluate their exchange move by move. Rate each of the four positions above—from clearly mistaken to clearly correct—and provide a brief justification for each rating. Then indicate which specific claims each interlocutor gets right, and identify anything important that both overlook, particularly whether a predictive-processing or postdictive framework might supply a hybrid explanation that neither the Orwellian nor the Stalinesque model fully captures. Treat this as a point-by-point adjudication of the reasoning presented, not as a request for my own view.
