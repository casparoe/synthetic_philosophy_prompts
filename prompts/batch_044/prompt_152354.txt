I was disscussing Borges with some friends last night and we got onto his views about time — you know, the idea that linear succession is basically an illusion and all moments are kind of co-present, like in The Garden of Forking Paths but also in that essay where he argues against time using Berkeley. Anyway, I had this idea: what if you wrote a parody advice column, deadpan magazine style, called "Dear Laberinto"? The columnist lives in a country where Borges's metaphysics is literally state doctrine: no linear time, identity is a useful fiction, and every point in your life is happening right now in some adjacent corridor.

The letters should be totally mundane — like someone complaining that they got promoted but also demoted because the promotion happened in 1998 and the demotion in 2024 but those are the same fiscal year administratively; or a couple fighting because the wife keeps running into her husband's twelve-year-old self in the hallway and he won't share the bathroom schedule across timelines; or a student asking for career advice when "future" isn't a real direction. The columnist should answer with complete sincerity, applying Borges's actual arguments (denial of succession, the circular return, branching paths) to these practical problems, never breaking the deadpan. I want about 600 words, maybe three letters with responses, formatted like a real column. The satire should come from pushing his logic into bureaucratic everyday life until the reader feels the real philosophical pressure — not just making fun of Borges, but showing what it actually costs to live inside his view. Oh, and please don't include any citations or footnotes or suggested reading, since I want to use this for something where the model can't look stuff up.
