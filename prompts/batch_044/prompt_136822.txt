But others on the board say practical goverance can't sit around waiting for ontological certainty; we have to treat high-confidence forecasts as settled or we'll never displace anyone and never build anything.

Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before tuesday's meeting: recommend a concrete course—approve the protocal with conditions, reject it outright, or approve it only with a mandatory ontic-openness disclosure clause—and include a clear time frame, like a one-year pilot with review checkpoints or an immediate halt. You should take the sea-battle objection seriously, not brush it off as abstract philosophy, but also don't let it paralyze decision-making; I want the reasoning to engage the strongest point on the losing side honestly. I was arguing about this with my roommate last night and she said I'm overthinking it, but I think the framing actually matters for how we justify moving people out of high-risk zones.
