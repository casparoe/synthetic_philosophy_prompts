Question: Explain Dennett's multiple drafts model of consciousness and how it avoids the Cartesian theater. Be specific about what, if anything, replaces the single 'center' where experience comes together.

Student answer:
Dennett rejects the Cartesian theater — the idea that there's one inner screen where consciousness happens. Instead, the brain produces lots of drafts of sensory information in parallel, like rough sketches being edited all over. The phrase 'Cartesian theater' is Dennett's name for the mistake of putting a single observer inside the head, and he traces it back to Descartes's idea of the pineal gland as the seat of the soul. Once you drop the theater, consciousness isn't a single stream but the draft that gets promoted by the brain's narrative center to become the final version. The other drafts disappear or never reach awareness. So there's no need for a homunculus — consciousness is just the winning draft.
