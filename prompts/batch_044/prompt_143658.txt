Specifically, I want your actual credence, expressed as a precise number between 0 and 1, that these narratives necessarily commit what I will call "scale-induced causal compression": the treatment of slow-moving structural variables (climate, agricultural surplus, information technology) as if they were discrete causal agents analogous to individual decisions, such that the narrative's explanatory power collapses once temporal resolution is refined.

Please decompose your reasoning into the following terms, assigning each a rough probability with one line of justification:

1. The probability that macro-historical causation is genuinely ontologically different from micro-level causation, rather than being a mere aggregation of individual actions.
2. The probability that the grand narratives in question actually commit this compression systematically, as opposed to using structural variables as legitimate heuristic abstractions.
3. The probability that, if such compression is present, it undermines the narrative's explanatory or predictive value rather than functioning as a necessary simplification.
4. The probability that critics—specifically the Graeber-Wengrow critique of state-formation narratives—have successfully exposed this error without substituting an inverse compression of their own.

Combine these into your overall credence. Then identify which term you are least confident in, and specify what kind of empirical or conceptual finding would shift that term by at least 0.2 and therefore move your final number most dramatically. I am not looking for a literature review; I want the reasoning with its weak points marked.
