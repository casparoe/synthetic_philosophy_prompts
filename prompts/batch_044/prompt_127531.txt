Write a dialogue between two philosophers, call them A and B, who disagree about whether inference to the best explanation is an autonomous rational principle or reducible to Bayesian updating with an implicit simplicity prior. Treat neither as a strawman; each must respond directly to the other’s actual points rather than talking past them.

Ground the exchange in a specific, contrived case study invented for the dialogue: researchers have observed that a formal model describing non-causal predictive alignment—defined here as the apparent coordination of mental-state ascriptions across physically isolated systems that share no causal pathway—predicts behavioral outcomes with 94% accuracy. Two competing explanations are on the table. Explanation X posits that the model tracks a real but non-causal structural relation that genuinely holds among the systems. Explanation Y treats the model as a useful fiction and accounts for the accuracy by appealing to a complex but fully observable network of micro-causal feedback loops that happen to converge on the same predictions.

A should defend IBE as irreducible: argue that X is genuinely better because it offers unified, non-ad-hoc coverage of the cross-system correlations, and that this explanatory virtue carries independent epistemic weight that cannot be collapsed into a prior on ontological parsimony. B should counter that the preference for X is merely a disguised prior, and that once the comparison is formalized, Y receives higher posterior probability because it avoids multiplying entities without independent empirical support. Crucially, both must address the specific objection that the 94% figure is theory-laden—since the data partitioning already assumes the validity of the alignment model—and that “unification” in this case may be an artifact of how correlations were selected.

Require real back-and-forth: A should reply to B’s charge of circularity by explaining how explanatory coherence can be assessed without presupposing the ontology it favors; B should reply to A’s claim about independent weight by showing how any metric of coherence can be reconstructed as a feature of the likelihood function combined with a sparsity prior. The dialogue should eventually converge on the precise locus of disagreement—whether explanatory virtues can be quantified independently of prior probabilities, or whether IBE is always parasitic on them. Keep the reasoning internal, formal, and philosophically precise. Do not include bibliographies, citations, references to specific texts, or requests for external sources; the characters should reason solely from the case as defined.
