I am trying to work out a consistent stance on partiality, integrity, and moral rescue, and I want a consistency audit before I present it. Here is where I stand:

1. I accept Williams's integrity objection in its standard form: a moral framework that can require an agent to treat their ground projects as permanently dispensable in the service of aggregate welfare is alienating, and that alienation is a serious moral defect in the framework.

2. I also take the point about an unnecessary mediating thought to apply beyond intimate relationships: an agent pursuing a ground project should not need to insert an explicit moral justification—this project is permissible for me to pursue—between their attachment and their action.

3. I hold that there is a direct, non-instrumental duty to rescue strangers in emergencies, and that this duty is not reducible to the agent's own projects or relationships.

4. I think that when an agent abandons or interrupts a ground project in order to rescue a stranger, the interruption is not necessarily alienating in Williams's sense, provided it arises from direct responsiveness to the other person's need rather than from an impersonal moral calculus.

5. I believe that ground projects are not fixed; they can be revised or relinquished through the agent's own reflective evaluation without producing alienation, so long as the revision comes from within the agent's outlook rather than being imposed externally.

6. On the specific case: if a musician is in the middle of a singular, unrepeatable live performance that is central to her identity, and she can stop to save a nearby stranger at moderate personal cost (ruining the performance), she should do so. I think this verdict is correct and that it does not show her integrity has been violated.

Please examine these commitments. Which pairs pull against each other, which conflicts are only apparent, and what is the least costly way to restore consistency? In particular, examine whether commitment 6 can be held alongside commitment 1 without weakening the integrity objection; whether commitment 3 introduces the kind of mediating moral thought that commitment 2 rejects; and whether there is a tension between 1 and 5 that dissolves once we distinguish external imposition from internal revision. Rank the genuine tensions by severity; for each, specify what must give and at what cost. Then identify the single revision that would resolve the most tension with the least damage to the overall view. I want an audit that traces what each commitment commits me to, not a general exposition of Williams.
