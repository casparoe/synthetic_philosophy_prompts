Read Ishiguro's Never Let Me Go through the experience-machine debate, but skip the usual "pleasant illusion" take everyone does. The clones at Hailsham recieve art, friendship, and love, yet theyre kept ignorant that they're just organ farms. I want you to ask whether Ishiguro is testing Nozick's condition about being in touch with reality against an objective-list view—where artistic achievement and deep relationships are goods regardless of belief-state. Focus on the gallery scene and Tommy's outburst: do those moments show the characters reaching for something the Hailsham setup cant fake? Or does the novel suggest shared illusions can ground real flourishing? Treat both the philosophy and the book seriously, and end with what Ishiguro adds to the well-being papers that pure argument misses. This is for a seminar, so avoid generic summaries.
