If René Descartes were alive today and encountered the Clark-Chalmers argument for the extended mind thesis—particularly the parity principle that external processes should be treated as cognitive when they mirror internal ones—how do you think he would respond to a technology unavailable in his time: a neural implant that stores autobiographical memories and executes reasoning steps that the wearer experiences as entirely seamless? Would Descartes classify this implant as part of the res cogitans, as part of the res extensa, or as some hybrid? Please ground your answer in his specific criteria for thought—indivisibility of the mind, clear and distinct perception, and the role of the pineal gland—rather than in general dualist labels. Then address a more unusual case: suppose the same implant is shared among several users, so that one physical memory-store and reasoning module simultaneously serves multiple minds. Would this shared architecture violate Descartes' requirement that a single thinking substance be individuated, and would it compel him to redraw the boundary between one mind and another?
