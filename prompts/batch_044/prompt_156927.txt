Heres the student answer, more or less verbatim except I fixed the spelling of causal:

Benacerraf's dilemma comes from Mathematical Truth, where he pairs a causal theory of knowledge with the idea that numbers are abstract and outside space-time, so platonists can't explain mathematical knowledge. Modal structuralism, developed jointly by Putnam and Hellman, avoids this by denying that mathematical objects exist at all; there are only modal structures spread across possible worlds. Since actual physical systems can instantiate these structures, empirical science gives us causal access to them, letting us learn the patterns indirectly. So Benacerraf's challenge is answered, since theres no abstract object left that needs direct causal contact. Of course, the full modal structure isn't in any one world, but Hellman says knowing the axioms that cover all possible instances is enough, and that logical grasp doesn't require causal transmission to every world.
