I serve on my university's Joint Discipline and Governance Panel, and by next Tuesday we must issue a ruling on an appeal concerning the "Distributed Epistemics Lab," an institutional research collective that operates through a decentralized contribution protocol. Individual members submit data modules to a shared repository without access to the aggregate, and release decisions are made by a weighted procedural vote that requires only a quorum of approval, with no condition that any voter understand the full assembled dataset. Last month, the aggregate dataset—whose harmful downstream use was intended by none of the individual contributors—was employed by an external party to produce systematically biased outputs in a high-stakes domain. Our sanction guidelines permit penalties against "collective entities" only when the entity acted with a shared intention, but they are silent on whether shared intention requires shared mental content across members or can be constituted purely by procedural joint commitment under conditions of distributed ignorance. A faction on the panel argues that, since no individual possessed the relevant mental content or intended harm, there is no collective agent to sanction and we should pursue only identifiable individuals linked to specific modules; the opposing faction contends that the Lab's procedural rules, mutual awareness of interdependence, and collective authorization of release constitute joint action regardless of distributed knowledge gaps. Please draft the Panel's formal recommendation memo—approving dismissal of collective sanctions, imposing collective sanctions with a restructuring mandate, or instituting a defined intermediate category—with a concrete recommendation, an explicit timeline for implementation or further proceedings, and reasoning that takes seriously the strongest consideration on the side you reject: specifically, whether procedural joint commitment can ground collective responsibility when mental content is radically distributed, and whether attributing agency to the Lab risks reifying a mechanism that lacks the unified perspective typically required for joint action.
