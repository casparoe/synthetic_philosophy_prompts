Consider the following reconstructed problem in the metaphysics of propositions, extending Bradley's regress argument into a domain that standard expositions rarely address. Bradley's familiar challenge holds that if a relation R genuinely connects relata a and b, then further relations must connect R to a and to b, generating an infinite regress that never achieves the original unity. The familiar escape routes—treating relations as internal, introducing a primitive binding operator, or denying that relations are entities—each carry well-known costs.

In my past seminars I have consistently delivered these five verdicts on whether a proposed structure achieves genuine propositional unity rather than remaining a mere aggregate or list:

(1) The bare list <Socrates, wisdom, instantiation> lacks unity entirely.
(2) The list augmented by a primitive binding-operator B achieves unity only if B is not itself a relatum requiring further binding.
(3) The structure that replaces the relation with an internal relation collapses the distinction between the relata and the relation.
(4) The adverbial structure in which the property modifies the subject—<wisely-Socrates>—achieves unity without an explicit binder, but only if we grant ontological priority to the modified whole over its parts.
(5) The structure that introduces a second-order relation relating the first-order relation to its relata fails, because it initiates the regress at the second order rather than solving it.

Now examine this new construction: a "triadic modal binder" M that takes exactly three items—an object a, a property F, and a modal context C (for instance, possibly)—and binds them into a single unified proposition. M is not one of the relata; yet M is ontologically constituted by the triad itself, so that without a, F, and C it does not exist, while with them present it is not an additional entity over and above the triad. Would this structure achieve unity? Would it fall prey to a variant of Bradley's regress? Or does it collapse into one of my five prior positions?
