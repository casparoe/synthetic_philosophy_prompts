Write the desiderata that a factored-cognition model of collective deliberation—modeled on the HCH (Humans Consulting HCH) idealization, in which human reasoners recursively delegate subproblems to copies of themselves—must satisfy to count as a normatively adequate representation of human reasoning at scale. The specification should cover four clusters: (1) the conditions under which a problem can be decomposed into independent subproblems without loss of epistemic value, including the role of shared conceptual vocabulary across nodes; (2) the mechanisms by which human consultants in the loop preserve analogical cross-contamination between subproblems—that is, reasoning about one factor analogically reshaping reasoning about another in ways that resist clean factorization, and whether such leakage is a bug to be engineered away or a feature necessary for creative insight; (3) the requirements for maintaining irreducible disagreement or epistemic friction across the hierarchy rather than converging on consensus, particularly in cases where disagreement stems from incommensurable framing rather than factual error; and (4) the empirical or conceptual constraints that would falsify the claim that such factorization captures real deliberation rather than imposing an artificial architecture upon it.

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or failure mode it addresses, indicate whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of particular camps (for example, those prioritizing scalable convergence over fidelity to cognitive messiness), and say whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—specifically, whether preserving analogical leakage and achieving full factorization stand in tension, and whether maintaining deep epistemic friction undermines the recursive stability that makes HCH attractive as an alignment target. Keep the weight on the criteria and their justifications rather than on scoring existing proposals; identify which single requirement does the most to exclude naive decompositional models, and clarify the purpose for which adequacy is being assessed (e.g., predictive accuracy of collective reasoning, normative legitimacy of the outcome, or scalability without cognitive distortion). Do not provide bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific papers.
