In debates over Landauer's principle and whether information carries intrinsic thermodynamic weight, Maxwell's demon is often analogized to a computer's central processing unit: just as a CPU routes signals through logic gates without an inherent thermodynamic cost per operation—paying only in the irreversible erasure of bits—the demon sorts fast and slow molecules by measuring velocities and opening a gate, with the apparent entropy reduction being compensated not by the sorting itself but by the subsequent wiping of its measurement record. Advocates of this framing use it to license the claim that information is not abstract mathematics but a physical resource governed by the same laws as matter and energy, and that the second law survives only because computation is ultimately irreversible.

Lay out the correspondence table explicitly: what in the demon plays the role of the CPU, the gate array, the memory register, the instruction set, the erasure step, and the heat bath? Then audit the disanalogies. Does the demon's measurement of a continuous velocity distribution map cleanly onto discrete binary logic? Can the measurement step be made arbitrarily reversible in the way a reversible logic gate can, or does the continuous thermal interaction with the reservoir force an unavoidable dissipation that the CPU analogy obscures? Does the analogy smuggle in an assumption of a detached observer-programmer that has no physical counterpart in the demon's embedded interaction with the gas?

Separate the disanalogies that threaten the argument from those that are merely cosmetic. In particular, ask whether the conclusion—that information is physical in precisely the sense needed to save the second law—survives once the continuous, embedded, and non-discretized features of the demon are restored, or whether the CPU framing was carrying the argumentative load. If the analogy collapses in a way that undermines the physicality claim, propose a more apt physical comparison (for instance, to a feedback controller or to a measurement apparatus coupled to a bath) and say what that replacement reveals about where the thermodynamic cost actually sits.
