Write an essay on whether the conceptual slippage characteristic of copycat analogies and fluid concepts undermines normative reasoning when a single term migrates across a tightly bounded microdomain. Take as your case the term "autonomy" as it operates in two adjacent regulatory contexts: adaptive dosing algorithms in medical devices (such as insulin pumps with closed-loop control) and decision architectures in autonomous vehicles. The controversy is whether the fluid migration of "autonomy" between these contexts introduces hidden normative commitments—about agency allocation, risk-bearing, and the scope of user consent—that remain invisible precisely because the word is treated as stable. Weigh the arguments on both sides: that cross-domain conceptual fluidity is productive and inevitable, allowing analogical reasoning to transfer insights; and that in high-stakes regulatory frameworks such slippage creates responsibility gaps by masking substantive differences in how agency, error, and accountability are structured. Reach a definite conclusion about whether regulators and designers should enforce terminological separation between these domains, or whether allowing the concept to remain fluid yields better practical and moral outcomes. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then provide the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
