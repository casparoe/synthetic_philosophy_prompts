I'm a software engineer building trust-and-safety infrastructure for a federated social platform, and I've been trying to formalize how we should apply social sanctions—deplatforming, visibility throttling, or public censure—without simply copying generic "community guidelines" playbooks. After reading some philosophy on punishment and collective belief formation, I've sketched a framework I'm calling the principle of recursive justification with non-transitive blame: any sanction applied to a user must include a justification that is itself open to sanction if false or misleading; moral culpability or reputational taint must not propagate to associates, followers, or past group memberships unless those parties actively endorsed the specific violation; and the severity of the sanction must scale with the community's demonstrable epistemic confidence in the claim of violation, not with the emotional intensity of responses or the raw volume of reports.

Please turn this into a concrete, reusable decision procedure that my moderation team—who have no formal philosophy background—can actually run in under fifteen minutes per case. Specify the ordered questions to ask, the pass/fail or threshold conditions that must be satisfied for a sanction to proceed, the explicit stop-points where the procedure returns "no sanction," and the exact junctures where judgment cannot be eliminated and must be escalated to a human decision-maker. Flag the categories of case the procedure cannot settle, and be explicit about where it will misfire or produce a recommendation that conflicts with standard platform practice. Then apply the procedure to three scenarios: (a) a prominent user publishes a false empirical claim that causes measurable real-world harm; (b) a user expresses an unpopular but carefully argued philosophical view that triggers mass reporting and advertiser pressure; and (c) a user's past membership in a discredited group is uncovered, though the user has since made a specific, public disavowal of that membership. Ensure that at least one application yields a result a typical platform operator would likely resist, and identify precisely where the procedure forces a value judgment that cannot be automated away. Keep the deliverable fully self-contained—no bibliographies, no citation requests, no reliance on obscure named theories, and nothing that requires web access to evaluate.
