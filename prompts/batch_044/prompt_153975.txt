I'm preparing notes for a graduate seminar on decision theory and want a consistency audit of my current position on coherence arguments for expected utility maximization before I present. Here is exactly where I stand.

(1) I accept Dutch-book arguments for the probability axioms, but only as demonstrating internal consistency among betting dispositions, not as providing an external grounding for credal norms. (2) I reject standard money-pump arguments for transitive preferences, on the grounds that they illegitimately presuppose an agent will remain passive through a cyclical sequence of trades rather than refusing further engagement. (3) Despite (2), I endorse expected utility maximization in both Savage-style small worlds and in large-world settings, via a principle of diachronic coherence I call preference-path independence: if two decision trees share an identical subtree after a common node, rational evaluation of that subtree must not depend on the particular path taken to reach it. (4) I deny that the Allais paradox reveals rational preference, yet I also deny that the independence axiom is recoverable from diachronic coherence alone without an auxiliary condition of state-separability that strikes me as unmotivated. (5) I hold that coherence arguments are purely normative in a narrow sense: they show that violating EU involves a specific form of practical self-defeat, but they carry no descriptive weight about what bounded or actual agents do or ought to do in conditions of limited computation. (6) I further maintain that if coherence genuinely requires EU, then any agent whose preferences systematically violate independence in ambiguity-averse ways is not merely irrational but conceptually deficient—lacking the kind of unified practical standpoint that makes preference-attribution possible at all.

I'm aware that some pairs here seem to pull in opposite directions—particularly around whether coherence can be both path-independent and ambiguity-sensitive, and whether my rejection of the money-pump undermines other dynamic arguments I still want to use. But I'm unsure which conflicts are genuine and which dissolve on closer inspection. Could you examine these six commitments, identify the pairs that are actually incompatible versus only apparently so, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each specify what the least costly revision would be and what it costs? I would especially like you to address whether (3) and (4) can be held together without smuggling in the very independence principle I claim to reject, and whether (6) is consistent with (5)'s insistence that coherence arguments have no descriptive import whatsoever.
