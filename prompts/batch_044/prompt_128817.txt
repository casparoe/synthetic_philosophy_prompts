Now draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps that have argued past each other for years.

Camp A: the Logical-Relational Formalists. They hold that Keynes’s probabilities are objective logical relations between propositions and evidential premises; that “weight of argument” is a measurable property of evidential completeness that can, in principle, be reconstructed within modern frameworks of ambiguity, robustness, or sets of priors; and that Keynesian “radical uncertainty” is best understood as extreme epistemic limitation rather than an ontological rupture in formalizability.

Camp B: the Ontological Uncertainty Advocates. They hold that Keynes’s distinction between risk and radical uncertainty marks an irreducible boundary: in genuinely open systems, not only are probabilities unknown, but the space of possibilities is structurally unspecified, so assigning even interval-valued beliefs is a category error; they hold that “weight” signals the limits of formalization—specifically, premise-incompleteness—rather than a parameter that can be internalized within any decision rule.

The camps have clashed over whether Keynes’s framework can handle “structural misspecification”: decision contexts in which the agent’s own model of the causal taxonomy is part of what is in doubt, and in which rationality may require suspending quantitative belief altogether rather than distributing it.
