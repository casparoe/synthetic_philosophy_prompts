Consider this passage from Schopenhauer's The World as Will and Representation: he writes that music is unique among the arts because it does not, like painting or poetry, reproduce particular Platonic Ideas—the graded objectifications of the Will—but rather copies the Will itself in its immediate objectivity. To illustrate this, he compares melody's continuous departure from and return to a fundamental tone with the pattern of human striving and satisfaction. At the same time, Schopenhauer insists that the Will lies entirely outside space, time, and causality. How should we understand this account? In particular, if the Will is non-temporal, how can a temporal art with essential rhythmic and harmonic structure express the Will directly rather than merely representing it through phenomenal form?
