me and a friend got into it over machievelli's fortune/virtù thing after skiming The Prince for a seminar. she said virtù is basically adaptive skill — if you roll with fortune's punches you have it, period, no moral content. i said that can't be right because he still warns princes not to be hated, which sounds like an internal check, not just "don't get killed." she said being hated is bad for long-term control, so it's still pure instrumentality. then i brought up the part where fortune is like a woman who should be beaten, and asked why gender it as domination if virtù is only technique? she said it's just rhetorical flair about aggression, not philosophy. i said okay but if fortune is pure randomness then virtù can't be a stable trait — it must be pure reactive flexibility. yet he praises founders like romulus for lasting qualities, not quick reflexes. she said it's both, but that dodges the tension. we circled for a while. can you adjudacate our moves one by one? who's right on whether there's any normative constraint inside virtù, on the gender metaphor, on whether virtù is stable or reactive? and who wins overall? also are we missing something — maybe fortune isn't randomness but the inertia of existing institutions that virtù has to reshape?
