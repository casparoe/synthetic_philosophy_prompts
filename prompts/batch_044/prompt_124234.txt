Here is the exchange as it actually unfolded. Even if observers don't know which branch is actual, there is a metaphysical fact — the "thin red line" — that privileges one branch as the real future prior to Wednesday. Calling truth "branch-relative" merely hides this pre-existing privilege. Once Wednesday passes, propositions about it become accidentally necessary in virtue of being past, but that retroactive necessity does not entail that Wednesday was causally predetermined on Tuesday. The openness was metaphysical, not merely epistemic. (4) Riley then pressed what he called the "selection problem," paired with a bivalence objection: in any growing tree, either the transition rules are deterministic — in which case the future is causally fixed — or they are not, in which case we need an unexplained mechanism for selecting the actual branch without a prior metaphysical fact. So Jordan has not dissolved the problem; he has merely distributed it between determinism and an unexplained selection mechanism.
