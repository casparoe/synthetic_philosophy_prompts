So there's this long-running standoff between people who treat social norms as Lewis-style conventions -- coordination problems solved by common knowledge -- and people who treat them as evolved stable strategies where no agent represents the rule. The conventionalists keep saying evolutionary accounts miss the normative 'ought', and the evoltionists keep saying conventionalism sneaks in unrealistic rationality. Noone moves. My hunch is that it's not really about mechanisms but about whether we're asking for a justification story or a causal origin story, and both sides are just talking past each other under the word 'norm'. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a hiddne empirical question about whether people actualy compute common knowledge when they follow norms. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is -- verbal, substantive, different questions, or bedrock intuitions about rationality -- supported by what each side actualy says. And given that diagnosis, what would count as progress? A model that separates 'commitment norms' from 'coordination norms'? A psychological result? Or should we just admit it's stuck because the concept does too much work? Don't survey every view; figure out the structure of the stall and be honest about whether anything could unstick it.
