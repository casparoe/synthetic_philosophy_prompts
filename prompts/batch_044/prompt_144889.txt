Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between Camp A and Camp B on philosophical zombies and the conceivability argument, restricted specifically to “affective partial zombies”—beings physically and functionally identical to conscious humans but lacking only affective phenomenal character (the felt quality of pain, pleasure, and emotional valence), while retaining full sensory-phenomenal experience and intact cognitive-functional organization.

Camp A maintains that conceiving such a being shows affective phenomenal properties are not metaphysically necessitated by physical-functional properties, and that this restricted case strengthens the broader anti-physicalist argument. Camp B maintains that conceivability here tracks only epistemic possibility or a feature of our conceptual apparatus, not metaphysical possibility, and that affective states’ tighter functional-causal integration makes the partial-zombie scenario less threatening to physicalism than the total-zombie scenario.

Structure the statement as follows: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims about the logical form of the conceivability argument, the precise definition of an affective partial zombie, and the distinction between metaphysical and epistemic possibility; (2) a disagreements table with columns for Camp A’s formulation, Camp B’s formulation, and the precise crux, each worded so the respective camp would accept its own statement; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp—such as whether our grasp of affective feeling reveals the intrinsic nature of those states or only their functional and causal roles, and whether functional-causal integration can in principle be separated from affective phenomenology; (4) for each crux, the specific argument, thought experiment, or empirical consideration both sides agree bears on it, with each camp’s explicit prediction recorded in advance; and (5) a brief concluding section identifying any disputes that are merely verbal rather than substantive, with justification. Do not present either camp as a strawman, do not request bibliographies or citations, and do not depend on named historical texts.
