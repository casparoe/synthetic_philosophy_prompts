hey so ive been thinking about this idea i call tranquilism — probably not a real word but whatever. the basic claim is that only two things actually matter: the absence of suffering, and the absence of craving. everything else, like happiness or achievement or love, is just decoration if youve got those two right.

i jotted down some rough notes for an essay im trying to write, and i wanted to see if they hold water or if im just talking in circles. here they are:

1. if tranquilism is true, the best life is one with zero suffering and zero craving. that sounds like death or being a rock, but i think its more like being really calm without wanting anything different.

2. but heres the problem i keep running into — call it the "last craving problem." even if you kill every normal want (tacos, money, attention), you still have to want the state of not-wanting. that desire for tranquility is itself a craving, right? so perfect tranquility is impossible because reaching it requires the very thing it forbids.

3. maybe the fix is that not all cravings are equal. theres surface craving (i want tacos) and then theres structural craving (i want to be someone who never wants tacos). if you eliminate the structural ones, the surface ones dont matter because they dont create suffering. but im not sure if im just inventing categories to save my idea.

4. also, what about other people? if i reach this calm state but my friend is suffering, does tranquilism say i should help them? or does it say suffering is just bad for them but not my problem? i think it says help, but only because seeing them suffer might trigger my own craving for things to be different. that feels kinda selfish and maybe wrong.

so yeah, those are my notes. can you read through them and tell me:
- is the "last craving problem" actually a solid objection, or just wordplay?
- do my two categories of craving make sense, or am i splitting hairs?
- is there a hidden mistake in how im applying this to other people?
- and generally, is this essay idea interesting, or am i just reinventing something everyone already knows?

i dont need citations or reading lists, just your honest take on the reasoning. also if theres a typo in my thinking (not just spelling) point it out.
