Write an essay on whether Germany's decision to phase out nuclear power and France's decision to maintain and expand its nuclear fleet represent competing answers to a single policy question—how to balance energy security, climate mitigation, and technological risk—or whether they are responses to conceptually distinct problems shaped by different institutional traditions and normative commitments. Weigh the arguments that treat the divergence as primarily a matter of risk tolerance, economic structure, and grid architecture against the arguments that treat it as a deeper philosophical disagreement about intergenerational obligation, the moral weight of low-probability high-consequence catastrophes, and the legitimacy of state-coordinated technological lock-in. In particular, examine whether comparing the two strategies requires smuggling in a universal metric of welfare or risk that both political cultures implicitly reject, and whether the German emphasis on precautionary reversibility and the French emphasis on infrastructure continuity can be reconciled within a single normative framework without collapsing one into the other. Reach and defend a specific conclusion: either that one framework genuinely subsumes the other and reveals a superior path, or that the comparison is a category error that obscures more than it reveals because the two countries are answering different questions about sovereignty, time, and responsibility.
