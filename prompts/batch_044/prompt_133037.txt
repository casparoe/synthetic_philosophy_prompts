(1) I think manipulation should be defined structurally rather than by deception or false belief: an influence is manipulative when it engineers an asymmetry in cognitive load, such that the influenced party bears the burden of detecting and correcting the influence while the influencer designs the interaction to make that detection or correction costly.

(5) I think emotional appeals in rhetoric—say, fear-based framing in a public-health campaign—are legitimate persuasion, not manipulation, because they supply premises rather than altering the structural conditions under which the audience reasons; they do not change the cost of deliberation itself.

(6) I think retrospective endorsement carries partial moral weight: if a user genuinely endorses a choice made through a dark pattern after learning exactly how the pattern worked—expressing that they are glad they kept the subscription—that endorsement does not erase the wrong but mitigates it, because autonomy includes not just procedural conditions but also the capacity for reflective ratification.
