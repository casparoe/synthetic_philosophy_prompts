Write a short story exploring whether a single "I" can be distributed across multiple brains. The scenario: three people, each carrying a partial cortical graft from the same deceased donor, begin experiencing fragmentary autobiographical traces—one as intrusive dreams, one as emotional residues without narrative, one as spatial intuitions she never learned—that only achieve coherence when all three are in close physical proximity. Drawing on Hofstadter's view that personal identity is a pattern capable of persisting across substrates rather than being locked to one brain, examine whether these fragments constitute one distributed self, three damaged selves, or something else entirely. Do not resolve the metaphysics neatly; instead, show how proximity alters each subject's first-person perspective, and ask what it would mean for agency and memory if the pattern genuinely requires inter-brain interaction to be complete.
