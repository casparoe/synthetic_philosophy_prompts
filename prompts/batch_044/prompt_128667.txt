I recently retired after several decades in evolutionary biology and behavioral ecology, and in the past year I have begun reading medieval philosophy of religion—mostly in conversation with a former colleague who completed a doctorate in philosophy of science and now teaches logic. We have been discussing Maimonides’ account of animal sacrifice, particularly the argument, developed in the Guide for the Perplexed, that sacrificial worship is not intrinsically desired by the divine but functions as a pedagogical concession: the law permits and channels a practice that neighboring cultures treated as central to religion because an abrupt prohibition would have exceeded the psychological and social capacities of the people, comparable in structure to a physician prescribing a diluted harmful substance in order to wean a patient from dependency. I want you to restate this position—including the claim that sacrifice retains a trace of the very idolatry the law seeks to eradicate, and that its strategic tolerance is part of a gradual, directed transition away from anthropomorphic worship—entirely in the vocabulary of my former field: evolutionary stable strategies, costly signaling, ritualized aggression, phylogenetic inertia, exaptation, evolutionary mismatch, and cultural niche construction. Translate as faithfully as you can, without smuggling in teleological or normative assumptions that belong to Maimonides rather than to evolutionary theory.

Then provide the residue report: identify which elements of the argument map cleanly onto evolutionary or ecological concepts, which ones can only be preserved through analogical distortion, and which ones have no counterpart whatsoever in that framework. In particular, address whether the missing residue points to a genuine limitation in evolutionary vocabulary—say, an inability to express normative directionality or intentional accommodation—or whether it indicates that Maimonides’ explanation itself depends on premises that become unclear once separated from their theological context. Please state explicitly how confident you are in the translation you have offered, and single out the one specific claim or mapping about which you are least certain.
