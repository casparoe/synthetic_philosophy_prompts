I'm preparing to interview a philosopher of neuroscience for a student-paper podcast series. The guest defends an uncommon position she calls the "integrated modularity" view of split-brain consciousness: rather than holding that callosotomy creates two persons, she argues that a single conscious subject was always composed of two semi-autonomous "access modules" — a left-hemispheric verbal-narrative system and a right-hemispheric spatial-motor system — that are normally bound by callosal synchronization rather than merged into one homogeneous field. On her account, post-surgical behavior — confabulation, independent manual action, disparate emotional responses — does not reveal a hidden twin, but exposes the underlying architecture of one mind that has lost its internal bridge. She concedes that this sounds like redefining "unity" to save the phenomenon, but maintains that the dual-subject interpretation illegitimately treats functional decoupling as ontological multiplication.

Write me a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that avoid generic biography and instead press the actual machinery of her view. Please include: (a) a question on how she distinguishes her position from standard dual-stream models without collapsing into them, followed by the reply she is most likely to offer and then a follow-up that makes the distinction costly; (b) a question about the left hemisphere's "interpreter" — specifically whether she can explain its confabulatory narratives as belonging to the same subject whose right module performed the action, without invoking a second agent — with a follow-up keyed to her likely appeal to narrative overlay or hierarchical self-modeling; (c) a question about congenital agenesis of the corpus callosum: if individuals born without a corpus callosum develop apparently unified consciousness despite never having interhemispheric binding, does that undermine her claim that callosal synchronization is what unifies the modules, or does it show that other mechanisms can substitute; and (d) a closing question asking what empirical result — perhaps in neurodevelopmental plasticity or in controlled hemispheric decoupling paradigms — would actually convince her that genuine fission has occurred rather than mere decoupling. The questions should be rigorous, anticipate her standard defenses, and include the follow-ups. Please provide the answer in American English.
