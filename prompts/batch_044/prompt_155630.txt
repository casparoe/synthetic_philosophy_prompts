As a journalist who covers the intersection of physics and philosophy, I keep hearing the same one-liner used to shut down debates about spacetime: “Newton’s bucket proves absolute space.” It came up most recently when a theorist invoked it to dismiss the claim that general-relativistic dynamical spacetime has finally made substantivalism obsolete. I need a precise conceptual disambiguation, not a historical lecture, and I do not want a bibliography—just the map.

Can you lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan could be expressing? Treat this as a menu rather than picking one reading. For each interpretation, specify: whether it is a logical claim, an empirical claim, or a metaphysical claim about ontology; whether it survives intact in general relativity or depends on a specifically Newtonian background with fixed spatial structure; whether it actually rebuts a contemporary relationist who appeals to dynamical geometry and field relations rather than to static inter-object distances; and whether it tacitly assumes that rotational motion is absolute in a sense that a homogeneous rotating cosmological model—of the kind Gödel proposed—would undermine.

Then, for each reading, deliver a verdict: is it trivial? Is it substantive and defensible? Is it false? And, crucially, which of these readings is the one the panelist probably meant when using the line to dismiss the dynamical-spacetime view?

Finally, say which readings, if any, actually do the work of supporting that dismissal, and which ones collapse once you separate them from the rhetoric of the bucket experiment. Keep the answer self-contained; I need to understand the logic without searching for sources.
