Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a mechanistic-interpretability realist and a functionalist anti-realist about whether neural networks genuinely have representations. The realist holds that interpretability can discover internal states carrying content, supporting misrepresentation, and playing irreducible explanatory roles in model behavior; the anti-realist holds that attributing representations is an observer-relative projection onto causal-functional organization that adds no predictive or explanatory power beyond mechanistic and statistical descriptions.

Produce a single document structured as follows: first, a numbered section of agreed claims—covering the formal structure of neural computation, the actual achievements of mechanistic interpretability (e.g., identifying feature directions, circuit motifs, and polysemantic neurons), the conditions under which representation-talk is at least heuristically useful, and what any adequate account must explain about generalization and error. Second, a disagreements table with columns for the crux, the realist formulation (worded so the realist accepts it), the anti-realist formulation (worded so the anti-realist accepts it), and the agreed empirical or conceptual case that bears on it. Identify at least three cruxes: (a) whether decoupling—activation of a state in the absence of its corresponding feature—occurs genuinely in network dynamics or is only imposed by the interpreter; (b) whether polysemanticity undermines realism (because one neuron participates in multiple representations) or is compatible with distributed representational schemes; and (c) whether representation-based explanations have predicted or explained behaviors that purely causal-functional descriptions failed to anticipate.

For each crux, specify a concrete case or finding both sides accept as relevant—such as a documented interpretability result or a documented predictive failure—and record in advance what each side predicts that case demonstrates. Conclude with a brief assessment of whether any portion of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, stating which portion and why.

Begin the response with the document itself, with no preface, no commentary, and no restatement of this prompt. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or verbatim quotations from specific texts.
