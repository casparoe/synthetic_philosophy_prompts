I hold a specific reading of Borges's argument in "A New Refutation of Time": that cyclical recurrence does not merely deny linear succession, but eliminates the conditions for diachronic personal identity altogether. If every mental configuration is destined to recur identically, then identifying a present self with a past or future instance of the same mental profile requires either an arbitrary selection or an illicit importation of linear continuity. The self, on this view, is not a substance traversing cycles but a recurrent pattern without numerical persistence; memory is not a tracking relation but the present occurrence of a mental state that has already occurred and will occur again. I want to know what would genuinely count against this specific conjunction—Borges's temporal metaphysics coupled with the dissolution of diachronic identity—not generic objections to eternal recurrence or to Nietzschean joy and terror.

Please rank the defeaters from decisive to merely awkward, distinguishing what defenders of this reading would themselves concede from what they would dismiss as smuggling linear assumptions back in. In particular:

(1) Could a minimal, "thin" subject or substratum persist across externally identical cycles without violating Borges's rejection of linear time, thereby rescuing numerical identity?

(2) Do phenomenological asymmetries—the directedness of memory toward the past versus the openness of anticipation—provide an internal, non-arbitrary principle for individuating cycles from within, even if they are externally indistinguishable?

(3) Are there coherent formal accounts of identity over circular temporal structures in modal logic or in the metaphysics of loops that either restore persistence or, conversely, show that its failure undermines Borges's position rather than confirming it?

(4) Has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability—say, by treating personal identity as already fictional or discontinuous independently of temporal metaphysics—so that cyclical time becomes redundant rather than destructive, and is that insulation a problem for a thesis of this kind?

Finally, identify the single observation or argument that would be most decisive: for instance, a demonstration that the inference from qualitative repetition to the absence of numerical identity commits a specific modal fallacy, or a phenomenological case in which first-person continuity seems intact precisely under conditions of perfect cyclical recurrence. What would it take for you to conclude that this Borgesian position is well insulated, and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice?
