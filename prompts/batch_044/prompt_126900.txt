I'm preparing a philosophical profile for a seminar and want an audit of whether these commitments are jointly tenable. I hold: (1) Tranquilism is correct—only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic value, while positive pleasures, achievements, or states of flourishing are instrumentally valuable at best; (2) the moral reasons generated by tranquilism are essentially first-personal, so my primary obligation is to diminish my own craving, with only derivative concern for others; (3) a culture can rationally choose to organize its institutions, education, and norms around tranquilist priorities rather than around achievement or positive flourishing, and this choice is rationally assessable rather than a mere preference; (4) there is a real distinction between pathological craving (tanha) and structural orientation (chanda)—the latter involves directed attention without suffering and therefore should not count against tranquilist evaluation; (5) if tranquilism is true, then bringing a being into existence is permissible only under conditions that guarantee a near-complete absence of both craving and suffering, which severely restricts reproduction or artificial creation. Do these hang together? Specifically, does (2) conflict with (3) in a way that makes collective tranquilist culture impossible? Does (4) smuggle in a positive valuation of certain mental orientations that (1) explicitly rejects? Is (5) genuinely entailed by (1), or does it depend on an unstated anti-natalist premise that tranquilism need not accept? Please rank the tensions by severity, identify which pairs only appear to conflict, specify the cheapest revision for each real tension, and tell me whether the profile can be saved without abandoning tranquilism itself.
