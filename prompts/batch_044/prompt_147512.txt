Here's an argument my study partener cooked up about Nietzsche's diet-and-health thing that feels like it collapses everything. (1) In Ecce Homo and the Twilight of the Idols Nietzsche keeps saying philosphers don't reason from pure logic but express their bodily conditions — bad digestion, climate, diet. (2) If your philosophy is just the symptom of your gut or your climate, then it has no rational authority over anyone with different guts. (3) Nietzsche applies this to Schopenhauer and the Christians, saying their pessimism and morality come from physiological decay. (4) But Nietzsche himself was sick all the time — migraines, vomiting, weak eyes — and he admits in Ecce Homo that his own ideas depend on his specific diet and regimin (he praises Italian food, dry air, etc.). (5) So by (2), Nietzsche's own philosphy is just a symptom of his sick body and particular diet, and therefore lacks rational authority. (6) But he clearly wants the revalution of values to be taken seriously as a reasoned argument, not as a migraine talking. (7) So either he must deny (2) — giving up the physiological reduction of philosophy to diet/health — or he must accept that his own philosophy has no authority. (8) Denying (2) seems to destroy half his critical method; accepting (6) seems to destroy the other half. Where does the argument slip? Is there an equivocation between "symptom" and "determined"? Or can we reject (4) by saying self-knowledge of one's conditions lets one transcend them? I really want to keep (1) and (3) if possible — I like the idea that philosophy is embodied — but I don't want Nietzsche to be self-defeating.
