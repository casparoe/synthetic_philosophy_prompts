I'm on our city's Historical Memory Commission and we need to vote next tuesday on whether to back the council's proposed Declaration of Intergenerational Responsibility. The declaration concerns a forced-labor camp from the 1880s that literally built our water system. It doesn't assign legal blame to anyone alive, but it states that the municipal corporation, as a continuing institution, bears what the drafters call "successor guilt" — not personal culpability, but an unbroken institutional obligation to repair harm done in its name. There's also this clause letting any current resident file a "moral dissociation" notice saying they personally reject the collective framing, and the commission has to decide if that saves the thing from being coercive.

The philosophy folks keep throwing Jaspers around — criminal guilt for the dead perpetrators, political guilt for citizens then, moral guilt for individuals now who identify with the legacy, metaphysical guilt for being human — but nobody agrees which box this falls into. Meanwhile legal warns that using the word "guilt" could expose us to lawsuits even tho we disclaim liability.

Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before the meeting. Recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a concrete course of action and a timeline the council can actually use. Take seriously the strongest argument that guilt is strictly personal and dies with the agent — don't just dismiss it — but also address whether the dissociation clause actually solves the coercion problem or just makes it worse. Don't just summarize Jaspers; use the distinction but push past it. The council needs to know exactly what we're asking them to do and by when, and the memo should be something a skeptical commissioner could read without rolling their eyes. Oh, and please include a note on what we should do if residents start filing those dissociation notices in bulk — does that undermine the declaration or is it just part of the design?
