I'm a phd student in philosophy of mind and i keep going in circles on Wittgenstein's private language argument with my cohort. Last week in our reading group, we hit the same wall we've hit all semester: one camp says the argument demolishes the idea of private mental reference because rule-following requires public correction — you can't "follow" a private rule since there's no difference between seeming right and being right — and the other camp says all Wittgenstein shows is that a private language wouldn't be communicable, not that it's impossible in principle. We've read alot of the standard literature, from the Robinson Crusoe objections to the normativity interpretations, and honestly nobody's moved an inch since october. What i'm wondering is whether the stall is actually hiding a deeper disagreement about first-person authority that neither side wants to name. Specifically, it seems like one group treats "knowing i'm in pain" as a linguistic achievement that only makes sense inside shared grammar, while the other treats it as a pre-linguistic phenomenological grip — something you have before language comes in to label it. So are we actually fighting about the architecture of self-knowledge, or is this just a verbal dispute where both sides use "private language" but mean different things? And if it's substantive, is there any conceptual distinction or thought experiment that could actually break the deadlock, or is the question itself ill-posed because it smuggles together semantic and phenomenological issues? I'd really like to figure out what kind of disagreement this is before i commit to writing my chapter on it.
