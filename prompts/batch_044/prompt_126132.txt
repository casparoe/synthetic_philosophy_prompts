If the canonical tripartite scheme—Antiquity, Middle Ages, and Modernity—depends on categories that achieved their canonical form largely through retrospective scholarly labor, and if designations like "Renaissance" and "Scientific Revolution" function partly as normative foils rather than neutral descriptions, does it not follow that these epochal divisions are analytically empty? More precisely, once one grants both the critique of the "Middle Ages" as a negative category defined primarily by absence rather than presence, and the growing evidence that practices central to the so-called Renaissance—such as linear perspective and humanist textual methods—have substantial antecedents in medieval Islamic and European contexts that render sharp boundaries unsustainable, is there any intellectually coherent defense for retaining tripartite periodization in serious historical analysis? Or should such schemes be relegated entirely to pedagogical convention, replaced by continuous narratives or thematically structured accounts that do not presuppose discrete epochs?
