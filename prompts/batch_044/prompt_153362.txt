In moral and political philosophy, speakers often invoke the familiar image—commonly linked to Martin Luther King Jr.—that the arc of the moral universe is long but bends toward justice. The phrase is rarely defended as an empirical prediction; rather, it functions as a metaphor that licenses a kind of cautious optimism about historical development, suggesting that injustice is systematically unstable and that progressive change has a directional tendency even across setbacks. I want to audit this claim specifically as an analogy to physical dynamics rather than as a historical thesis. If we take the metaphor literally—treating history as a trajectory through state space—what plays the role of the projectile, the curvature, the bending force, the coordinate system in which "justice" is a target or attractor, and the time parameter over which convergence is measured?

Lay out an explicit correspondence table, then identify the disanalogies that matter for the normative work the image is made to do: for instance, whether history exhibits the determinism of ballistic motion, whether there is any identifiable mechanism analogous to a potential gradient pulling events toward justice, whether reversals and catastrophes are merely frictional noise or structural features that break path continuity, and whether "justice" can be defined with enough precision to serve as a fixed point in a dynamical system. In particular, consider whether the analogy smuggles in teleology by presupposing a potential well or strange attractor for which social ontology offers no independent account. Once these gaps are mapped, does the conclusion—that history tends toward justice—survive in any form stronger than a tautology, or was the physical picture carrying the entire argument? And if one tries to salvage the analogy by replacing the simple arc with a high-dimensional, path-dependent attractor in chaos-theoretic terms, does that revision preserve the original hopeful conclusion, or does it dissolve determinism so thoroughly that the normative license is lost?
