Is there a name for this? In dark-forest-style reasoning everyone frames the choice as binary: stay totally hidden, or broadcast your location loud and clear. But I've been stuck on this intermediate case -- you send out a narrow-beam probe that only tells anyone in the beam path that tech life exists somewhere in that corridor, without giving them your star's exact coordinates. What's weird is that people analyzing this still talk like it's either safe silence or reckless shout, as if the probe doesn't create its own specific risk: anyone who recieves the beam knows there's prey nearby but can't pinpoint it, so they might start sweeping the sector aggressively, which actually raises the danger for every other quiet civilization in that region who never signaled at all. It's not the classic dark forest (where revealing your precise spot gets you destroyed) and it's not the security dillema either, since the harm isn't mutual suspicion between two actors -- it's an asymetric information spillover that hurts bystanders. I've seen this collapse happen constantly in forum debates, where analysts treat a continuous signaling space as if it only had two settings. Is there an established concept in strategic studies or signaling-game literature that captures this exact failure mode -- collapsing gradated disclosure options into hide/reveal, plus the sector-wide externality? If it's genuinely unnamed, can you coin a sharp term with boundary conditions that separate it from information cascades and from standard security dillemas? And please be self-critical: what formal result or counterexample would change your mind and show this is really just a disguised version of something we already have?
