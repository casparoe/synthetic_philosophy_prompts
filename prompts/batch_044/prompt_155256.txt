Here is my philosophical profile on hinge epistemology and Wittgenstein’s On Certainty. I want to know whether these commitments are jointly tenable or whether I am holding incompatible motivations without noticing.

My commitments:
(1) Hinge propositions—e.g., that I have hands, or that the earth existed long before my birth—are not ordinary empirical beliefs justified by evidence; they are structural conditions that make evidential reasoning possible at all.
(2) The brain-in-a-vat scenario is not an intelligible object of live doubt within our practice; trying to doubt it undermines the very hinges required for that doubt to have determinate content.
(3) At the same time, the conceivability of being a brain-in-a-vat is epistemically significant: it reveals that our framework is contingent rather than necessary, even if that contingency can never be activated as practical doubt.
(4) I read On Certainty as advancing a kind of “local foundationalism”—certainty is framework-internal and not globally transferable across frameworks—rather than as a direct refutation of Cartesian skepticism.
(5) Hinges are collectively revisable, but only in clusters during radical conceptual shifts (scientific revolutions, deep religious conversions), never one by one; I call this view “scaffolded fallibilism.”
(6) Philosophical skepticism remains a legitimate methodological exercise, not because it generates actionable doubt, but because mapping the boundaries of our hinges clarifies the architecture of knowledge.

Please audit this set for joint tenability. Identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs me. Finally, tell me which single commitment I should revise if my aim is to eliminate the most tension with the least damage to the overall view, and explain what that revision commits me to going forward.
