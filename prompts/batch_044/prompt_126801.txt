My friend insisted that David Lewis’s account of convention treats norms as self-sustaining solutions to coordination problems, arbitrary except for salience and common knowledge, and therefore predicts gradual, bottom-up evolution rather than overnight abolition by decree. I think the Swedish case is more complicated, and I want an analysis that runs in both directions.

Please analyze Dagen H through the lens of Lewis’s theory of convention and the broader question of how norms evolve. Be precise in the mapping: identify the relevant agents (private drivers, the state, vehicle importers, perhaps pedestrians), the coordination problem they faced, the available alternatives (left versus right), and what made one or the other salient—including the well-known fact that Sweden had driven on the left despite a preponderance of left-hand-drive vehicles imported from continental Europe and North America, which made the status quo already unstable. Then explain what Lewis’s model predicts about how such a convention should emerge or dissolve, and apply it to the Swedish switch: was the state merely revealing a new focal point, or was it destroying common knowledge and substituting enforced expectation? Identify exactly where the model explains the episode and where it breaks down—for instance, does Lewis’s framework have resources to account for a convention being legislated out of existence overnight, or for material technology (car design) destabilizing an arbitrary equilibrium?

Finally, I want you to consider a feature we barely touched on: the possibility of asymmetric divergence after the switch, where driver norms flipped immediately under legal and infrastructural pressure while pedestrian norms—habits of looking left before crossing—may have lagged, creating a temporary state of mismatched expectations between different types of agents. Does Lewis’s peer-based coordination model have the conceptual tools to describe such inter-group norm divergence, or does this reveal a limitation in applying convention theory to heterogeneous populations with different stakes and information?

In return, tell me what Dagen H shows about Lewis’s account of the evolution of norms. Does it confirm that conventions require a stable common-knowledge foundation, or does it show that top-down institutional scaffolding can replace evolutionary emergence?
