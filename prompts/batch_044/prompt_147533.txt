I chair our university's Committee on Logic and Reasoning Instruction. One member has proposed restructuring the first-year graduate logic module around the well-known dialogue in which Achilles attempts to justify a deduction to a skeptical Tortoise—specifically, the exchange in which every attempt to move from premises to conclusion is blocked by the demand that the governing inference rule itself be added as an explicit premise. The proponent argues that standard textbooks present modus ponens as entirely unproblematic and that students should confront the regress of justification that arises when a rule is not accepted unless cited. The opposing member contends that this misreads the dialogue: it is a reductio of psychologism, showing what happens when one confuses the psychological acceptance of a logical rule with its logical force, and that building the course around this confusion will prevent students from grasping the distinction between applying a rule and citing it. Our assessment coordinator raises a further practical worry: the proposed evaluation is a recursive defense exercise in which students must defend a simple syllogism against an interlocutor who accepts every explicit premise and every explicitly stated inference rule but still denies the conclusion, and she doubts this can be graded consistently without either conceding an infinite regress of rules or tacitly invoking an unstated principle of recognition.

We must deliver a concrete recommendation—approve the restructuring, reject it, or approve it with specific conditions—to the faculty senate by its meeting next month, and it must be actionable rather than merely advisory. Please draft the committee's formal written opinion in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, tables, or other formatting, as if it were a single letter or memo. Lead with the bottom-line decision. Then lay out the reasoning, treating the psychologism objection and the regress objection as the two strongest competing interpretations of what the dialogue actually shows about deductive justification. Identify the philosophical issue genuinely at stake—whether it concerns an infinite hierarchy of meta-rules, the separation of logical validity from psychological acceptance, or the role of an implicit practical capacity in rule-following—and explain how your recommended course resolves or manages it. Include an honest account of the strongest consideration on the side you reject. If your recommendation is conditional, specify exactly what conditions must be met, such as modifications to the recursive defense exercise or boundaries on how the dialogue is presented in relation to standard proof theory. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading; the memo must stand entirely on its own reasoning.
