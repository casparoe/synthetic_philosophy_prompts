Hey — I run a seminar on economic anthro with about 14 undergrads, one 75 minute session. I want them to actually feel why the agricultural revolution might've been a mistake, but not through the usual they got shorter and sicker story. I want them to feel the weirder thing about hunter-gatherer affluence: that it is not just having enough calories, it is the absence of stored surplus that creates hidden debt-claims between people. So design a classroom simulation where we run two groups in parallel — a foraging band and an early farming village — with roles, a simple resource flow, and conflict rules. The farming group needs a hidden mechanic: stored surplus automatically generates informal claim-obligations (if I store grain and you do not, I can demand labor or loyalty), but students should only discover this through playing, not through me explaining it first. Make sure the foraging group literally cannot store anything — resources spoil immediately. The debrief should map the exact moment the obligations appear onto the origins of hierarchy, and be honest about what the game misses — probably the health costs and the ecological lock-in that makes going back impossible. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong, like one student hoarding everything and breaking the simulation. Oh, and the two groups should run on seperate tracks so they do not just copy each other. Keep it casual — a typo or two is fine.
