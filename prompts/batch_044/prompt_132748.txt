Please describe the most important implications of what I will call “epistemic displacement”: the situation in which an external observer’s predictive model of an individual—constructed from behavioral traces rather than explicit disclosure—achieves greater accuracy and finer granularity about that individual’s preferences, habits, and patterns than the individual possesses about themselves. Focus on how this phenomenon bears on standard philosophical theories of the value of privacy, particularly those that ground privacy in autonomy, dignity, or contextual integrity. Address whether epistemic displacement can be accommodated as a variant of familiar informational harms, or whether it forces a revision in how we conceptualize control over personal information and self-knowledge. Separate what is relatively settled in the literature on privacy from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any disputed position as though it were the consensus view.
