I was discussing mind uploading with two friends from my philosophy of mind seminar, Maya and Jordan, and their disagreement got sharper than I expected. They were specifically debating a gradual-replacement scenario: over ten years, every biological neuron in someone's brain is replaced one by one with a synthetic analog that not only replicates neural firing patterns but also produces continuous meta-cognitive audit logs—internal records of processing that the original biological substrate never generated. Maya started by saying that if the replacement is genuinely gradual and each synthetic neuron preserves both causal and informational continuity with its predecessor, then personal identity survives intact; she compared it to the ship of Theseus, except here there is no moment of discontinuity because consciousness never lapses. Jordan pushed back that biological neurons possess a kind of first-person accessibility—an immediacy of subjective presence—that synthetic analogs lack by definition, regardless of behavioral equivalence; he argued that the audit logs do not merely record experience but actually restructure it, inserting a kind of internal spectator into the stream of consciousness so that the post-replacement subject is not the same person but a functionally similar successor with a different phenomenal architecture.

Maya accused Jordan of smuggling in vitalism: if the synthetic system reports identical qualitative states and integrates them into the same autobiographical narrative, then the audit logs are merely transparent access to what was already occurring, not an intrusive observer. Jordan replied that this misses the structural point—a cochlear implant extends sensory capacity without altering the architecture of observation itself, whereas embedding audit mechanisms inside the cognitive process transforms the subject from an unmediated experiencer into something more like a monitored system, and he insisted that narrative continuity is not enough if the mode of being-in-the-world has changed. They asked me to referee, but I genuinely could not tell where the argument turned.

Can you go through their exchange move by move—Maya's ship-of-Theseus analogy, Jordan's accessibility and restructuring claims, Maya's vitalism charge, Jordan's cochlear-implant distinction—and say who is right at each step? Also, please say whether there is a hidden premise they are both missing about what gradual replacement actually requires for identity, and who is more right overall.
