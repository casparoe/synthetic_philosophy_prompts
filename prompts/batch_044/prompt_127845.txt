I'm designing a taxonomy handout for a graduate seminar on Moritz Schlick's philosophy of life, centered on the framework from his essay on the meaning of life in which he distinguishes work—activity pursued for an external end—from play—activity pursued for its own sake in present absorption—and argues that only the latter can ground a meaningful life. The audience is students who have read Schlick's text but not extensive secondary commentary, and I want the instrument to sort them by what they actually believe rather than by which label they prefer.

Please construct a decision tree, rendered in text form with branch nodes as answerable questions and leaves as named or unnamed positions. The tree should be built from the logical space first, with named positions located afterward.

Start with this root question: Is the source of life's meaning primarily found in present experience or in future-oriented achievement?

From there, include the following branch points, each phrased as a concrete case or claim a student can answer without knowing which interpretation it maps onto:

1. Present-absorption: Must it be "pure play" with no instrumental structure—like a child playing with mud—or can rule-governed, competitive, or socially useful activities also be meaningful if the agent is fully absorbed in them for their own sake? Use this diagnostic case: a surgeon performs complex life-saving operations, cares little about the outcomes, and reports being entirely absorbed in the technique itself; is her life meaningful in Schlick's sense?

2. Pessimism about the human condition: If suffering dominates and play is rare or impossible for most people—imagine a world in which 99% of human existence is labor for survival and only 1% is play—does this undermine meaning entirely, or can Schlick's view accommodate it by locating meaning in rare moments?

3. Scope: Is meaning essentially an individual matter of private absorption, or does it require collective social conditions that make play widely available?

The tree should yield about six to eight leaves. Required named leaves: Schlick's own view (present-absorption, allows structured play, pessimism-compatible but rare, and specify whether he treats it as individual or collective); a strict achievement/work view that denies present absorption is sufficient; a nihilist/pessimist view holding that suffering or the impossibility of pure play makes life meaningless; and a transcendent or religious view that locates meaning in relation to eternal values beyond present experience. You must also include at least one unoccupied combination in the logical space—such as "future-oriented achievement is necessary for meaning" combined with "pessimism about suffering is fully compatible with meaningful lives"—and briefly diagnose why it is unoccupied: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Every leaf should carry the position's name (or "unnamed") and one sentence describing its most awkward commitment. Where your tree genuinely cannot separate two positions—perhaps Schlick's view from a mature "rule-governed play" view defended in philosophy of sport—flag that pair explicitly rather than papering over the overlap.

Keep the branch labels as questions or cases, not as philosopher-names. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or references to obscure secondary theories that a small model would not have memorized. The deliverable should be the taxonomy itself, not an essay about it.
