Given this, it appears that applying the falsifiability criterion in practice requires an inductive judgment about which auxiliary assumptions are legitimate to preserve and which should be abandoned—precisely the kind of reasoning Popper claimed to have eliminated. So my question is: doesn't this mean that falsificationism secretly collapses back into a form of inductivism, and that the demarcation criterion is therefore not a logical boundary but merely a conventional rule we adopt by induction?
