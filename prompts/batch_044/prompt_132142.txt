The challenge runs as follows: in contexts of deep epistemic possibility, where a speaker genuinely does not know which world is actual, evaluation of the name seems to require anchoring it to whatever descriptive condition fixes the referent in every world compatible with the speaker's evidence. If that is correct, then within those modal spaces the name behaves as though its primary intension is not constant but rather satisfied by different individuals in different epistemically accessible worlds—exactly the descriptivist behavior that two-dimensionalism was introduced to block for metaphysical modality.

Reach and defend a clear conclusion—either that the framework survives with a principled distinction, or that the epistemic-anchoring challenge exposes a structural limitation in extending rigid designation to epistemic contexts.
