Specifically, students should be required to address: (i) what conditions must obtain for a system to exhibit genuine sensitive dependence; (ii) how counterfactual reasoning in historiography differs from perturbation analysis in physics; and (iii) whether the epistemic asymmetry between retrospective causal tracing and predictive modeling undermines the claim that history is chaotic in any technically meaningful sense. Please provide the exam question itself—suitable for a 90-minute essay—followed by a model answer of approximately 700 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A response from a B response by identifying the specific conceptual moves each must contain. In particular, the scheme should award marks for clearly distinguishing dynamical chaos from narrative counterfactual fragility, for explaining why intentional explanation resists formalization in state-space terms, and for addressing the epistemic asymmetry without collapsing it into a generic skepticism about prediction. The scheme should also note the common error of treating the butterfly effect as straightforwardly transferable to human events without examining the representational prerequisites, and should allocate points across these dimensions with a brief note on how weaker answers typically fail on each.
