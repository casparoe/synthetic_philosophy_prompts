Draft a Q&A prep sheet for a seminar presentation. I am defending the view that criminal liability should be analysed through stage theory rather than endurantism: the accused at trial is not identical to the past perpetrator but is a later temporal stage linked by psychological and causal continuity, so responsibility must be reconstructed as an inter-stage relation rather than a property of an enduring subject. The audience is a philosophy-of-law reading group comprising analytic metaphysicians familiar with endurantism, perdurantism, and stage theory, and criminal-law scholars who regard personal identity as a practical presupposition and distrust ontological meddling with legal doctrine. Order the objections they will actually raise — from both camps — by how likely they are and how damaging, phrased in each camp’s vocabulary. For each objection provide a definite reply that takes a stand rather than surveying options, a brief explanation of the reply’s weakest point, and a clear note on whether I should concede anything up front. Identify the single question I most hope nobody asks. Take a committed position throughout: do not merely list options but defend which objections have real force and which are distractions. Write in British English, and include a brief, self-critical note on where this application of stage theory to legal responsibility takes liberties with standard metaphysical accounts or depends on contested interpretations of legal personhood.
