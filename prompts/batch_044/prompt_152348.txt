I have recently retired from theoretical physics and have begun working through the philosophy of science with some care. Before presenting my views to a discussion group, I want a consistency audit of the package I have arrived at.

First, I hold that scientific realism is warranted only for entities within what I call the causal footprint of observation—those that can, in principle, exchange information with our instruments. By this standard, I reject realist commitment to other string vacua, to regions beyond our cosmic horizon, and to any multiverse domains that are causally disconnected from us.

Second, despite that restriction, I regard string theory as our best available framework for quantum gravity, and I think rational belief in it is supported primarily by non-empirical virtues: mathematical consistency, uniqueness in the sense of having no free parameters, and broad explanatory coherence.

Third, I maintain that when direct empirical testing is structurally impossible rather than merely practically difficult—as it is for the full string landscape—those non-empirical virtues can legitimately carry epistemic weight; they are not merely aesthetic preferences.

Fourth, I accept that string theory structurally implies a vast landscape of vacua, but I deny that this multiverse is a scientific prediction in the same category as gravitational waves. I treat it as an ontological byproduct of the formalism, not as an empirical claim about reality.

Fifth, I hold that explaining the apparent fine-tuning of fundamental constants is a genuine theoretical virtue, even when the explanation appeals to entities—such as other vacua with different constants—that lie outside our causal footprint.

Sixth, though I am unmoved by evolutionary debunking arguments directed at moral intuitions, I do worry that our strong preference for parameter-free, mathematically elegant theories may reflect a cognitive bias shaped by evolutionary pressures for efficient problem-solving, rather than a reliable guide to physical ontology.

Do these six commitments hang together? Specifically, I want to know which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve on inspection, and what the least costly revision would be for each real tension. Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each what would have to be given up or modified, and tell me whether any single revision would resolve the most serious conflicts without collapsing the rest. I am asking only for an audit of coherence, not for a defense of any particular position.
