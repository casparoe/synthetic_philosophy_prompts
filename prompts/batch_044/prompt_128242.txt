I am currently preparing a seminar paper on the view that consciousness is best understood not as direct access to mental reality but as a user-illusion—an adaptive interface constructed by the brain for the organism's benefit, analogous to desktop icons on a computer screen rather than to the underlying code. In discussion with colleagues we kept returning to the same cluster of conceptual problems without being able to agree on which ones deserve sustained philosophical attention. Given that both cognitive-scientific and philosophical resources are limited, which open questions, implications, or lines of work within this specific framing ought to be prioritised, and in what order?

I am less interested in a survey than in a defended agenda: for each priority, explain why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for conceptual rather than empirical work, and what kind of argument or finding over the next few years would move it up or down your list. Among the issues I have in mind are whether the interface metaphor genuinely dissolves the hard problem or merely relocates it; whether a user-illusion account can accommodate the apparent transparency and immediacy of phenomenal experience without collapsing into eliminativism; and what ethical or legal consequences follow if consciousness is treated as a representational shortcut rather than an ontologically basic feature—though I welcome alternatives you consider more central. Please defend your ordering with explicit criteria, such as conceptual tractability, dependency relations between questions, and potential to reshape adjacent debates in philosophy of mind or ethics. Finally, please indicate clearly what consideration or argument would most likely change your mind about this prioritisation, and identify the point in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to objection. Please respond in British English.
