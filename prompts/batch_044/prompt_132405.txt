Writing as a novelist: craft a short, literary story—no more than three brief scenes—about a society in which legal personhood depends on maintaining a continuous autobiographical narrative, so that one’s “self” functions literally as a center of narrative gravity. The protagonist discovers an irreconcilable gap in her own record and must choose between inventing continuity to preserve her social weight or leaving the rupture open, thereby dispersing her identity into unintegrated fragments. Without moralizing or using philosophical jargon, show both options as intelligible. Include one precise detail revealing that the institutional archives hold no master narrative—only scattered, contradictory annotations—so that the “center” is exposed as a collective organizational fiction rather than an ontological core. Keep the tone restrained and focused on the texture of the bureaucratic language.
