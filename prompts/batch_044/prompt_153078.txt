In well-being debates people often analagize the experience machine thought-experiment to a patient choosing terminal sedation — both swap real-world engagement for guarenteed pleasant experience, and the comparison gets used to argue that if we reject the machine but accept sedation we're inconsistently treating reality-contact as valuable. Audit this: what maps onto what (agency, reversability, social presence, the fact sedation usually ends life rather than sustaining it), where it breaks, what it smuggles in about whether there's still a 'subject' experiencing in each case, and whether the conclusion — that reality-contact is either a constituent of well-being or just an instrumental good — survives once the disanologies are on the table. If the comparison collapses, suggest a better analogy for testing whether we value reality for its own sake.
