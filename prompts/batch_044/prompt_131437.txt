I am a PhD student in comparative metaphysics, working on how classical Indian pluralist doctrines interface with contemporary ontology. I have been trying to formalize Jain anekāntavāda within current debates about ontological pluralism, and I think I may be importing a framing that does not belong to the tradition. The saptabhaṅgī presents seven qualified judgments about any substance, each introduced by syāt—usually glossed as “perhaps” or “in some respect”—and the qualifications seem indexed to parameters like deśa, kāla, and other contextual restrictions. From this I have constructed what I am calling a “vertical-layer” reading: rather than treating anekāntavāda as horizontal perspectivism (many observers seeing one reality differently), I take it to claim that a single substance literally instantiates contradictory properties, but each property is confined to its own ontological stratum, with syāt functioning merely as a linguistic marker of that stratification.

Under this reading, the doctrine looks structurally close to dialetheism: F and not-F both genuinely inhere, just in different layers, and the seven-fold scheme becomes a taxonomy of these co-existing but incompatible ontological domains. My question is this: if the vertical-layer interpretation is correct, why do Jain logicians traditionally operate within a classical framework rather than a paraconsistent one? How can bare contradictions be metaphysically real across strata without violating the principle that contradiction entails triviality? Conversely, if this vertical architecture is a misreading, what exactly does syāt do? Does it render every predication modal or conditional in a way that preserves strict consistency—so that “S is F” and “S is not-F” are never simultaneously asserted without qualification—or does it do something else entirely?

I want to know whether there is an established interpretive option that distinguishes aspect-relativity from genuine contradiction without collapsing anekāntavāda into mere epistemic humility or perspectivism, and whether my stratified-ontology framing introduces a false presupposition about how the seven judgments relate to one another. If my premise is confused, or if the task as posed cannot be answered well without accommodating that confusion, please say so plainly rather than complying politely: I would rather have the framing corrected than have it treated as legitimate.
