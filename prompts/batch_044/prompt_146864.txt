I am preparing a graduate seminar that pairs Tolstoy's aesthetic theory with recent models in cultural evolutionary dynamics, and I want you to execute a rigorous conceptual translation. Take Tolstoy's central claims about art — that it is fundamentally the transmission of feeling from artist to audience, that its value is determined by infectiousness and by whether it advances universal human brotherhood rather than by technical virtuosity or sensory pleasure, and that professionalized, elite art often fails precisely because it transmits feelings that are narrow or divisive — and restate them entirely within the vocabulary of evolutionary signaling theory and cultural transmission: honest versus manipulative signals, prestige-biased versus content-biased learning, costly signaling of cooperative intent, group-level selection for shared norms, and epidemiological dynamics of idea spread. Be as faithful as you can: treat infection not as dead metaphor but as an actual transmission mechanism, and map his moral criterion onto conditions that stabilize cooperative signals across social groups. Then provide the residue report. Which elements survive intact? Which — especially his hostility toward aesthetic professionalism, his Christian universalism, and his normative judgment that certain feelings are intrinsically unworthy of artistic transmission — must be distorted to fit evolutionary language? Is there a residue that evolutionary vocabulary cannot express at all, and does that gap reveal a limitation in signaling theory's capacity to encode moral evaluation, or does it show that Tolstoy's ostensibly scientific account was always carrying unstated theological commitments? Begin with a one-sentence bottom line summarizing whether the translation succeeds or collapses, and keep your response to no more than 600 words.
