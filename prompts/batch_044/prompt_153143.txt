Following a prolonged bereavement that has left me without any craving for future goods—what I take to be an approximate realization of tranquilism, the position that well-being consists solely in the absence of suffering and craving, with positive states contributing nothing—I find myself confronting an unexpected implication. If the presence of pleasure, awareness, or accomplishment adds no value, and only the absence of negative states matters, then is there any axiological reason to prefer my present conscious but desireless condition over a state of permanent unconsciousness? Or does tranquilism implicitly treat subjective awareness itself as an unnecessary exposure to risk, so that the theory's true ideal is not a mode of living but the cessation of experience altogether? This has become personally urgent: I wish to understand whether my current lack of craving represents the fulfillment of the view, or merely an intermediate condition that should, if consistently pursued, lead toward the elimination of consciousness itself.
