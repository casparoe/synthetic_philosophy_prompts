As a novelist constructing a nested metafiction, I am attempting to map a precise conceptual difficulty that arises when narrative meaning is distributed across three encoding levels—the frame message, the outer message, and the inner message—of the sort discussed in the GEB treatment of self-referential encoding. Give an example of a conceptual issue in which these three layers are arranged not to produce a conventional unreliable narrator, but to generate a structural paradox about where the text’s meaning actually resides. Specifically, construct a case in which a frame asserts authoritative control over an outer message that prescribes how to read an inner message, while the inner message simultaneously encodes a self-referential instruction that nullifies both higher levels. Then explain why resolving the contradiction requires relocating meaning outside all three layers altogether, rather than privileging any single level as correct, and clarify how this differs from ordinary narrative ambiguity.
