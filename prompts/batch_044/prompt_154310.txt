Essay notes for a short piece titled "Recursive Luring in Platonia: A Superrational Cascade."

Standard Platonia: N isolated agents choose simultaneously whether to claim a prize. If exactly one claims, all N receive it; if zero or more than one claim, none receive it. Symmetric reasoning seems to push each agent to randomize, yet superrationality suggests they might coordinate on a single claimant without communication.

The Luring Lottery modifies Newcomb-style reasoning: entry conditions are tied to the agent's own decision procedure, creating an evidential loop that lures the agent toward a choice that is causally dominated.

My proposed hybrid—call it Recursive Luring Platonia (RLP)—nests the Platonia payoff inside a meta-lottery. Before the isolation round, each agent's reasoning architecture is assigned a "lure weight" based on how strongly it expects the others to apply superrational symmetry-breaking. If an agent predicts the others will converge on her as the unique claimant, she is evidentially lured into claiming, collapsing the Platonia payoff for everyone. The weight updates recursively: each agent's expectation about others' expectations feeds back into the assignment.

This raises three questions:

1. Does RLP collapse into standard Platonia once the meta-layer is resolved, or does the evidential loop create a genuinely new equilibrium?
2. Can a superrational agent use the recursive structure to justify not claiming, even when she has private evidence she is the designated claimant?
3. If N scales and reasoning architectures are partially correlated, does the cascade stabilize or diverge?

The essay would argue that RLP reveals a hidden instability: the symmetry-breaking intuition that solves Platonia becomes the very mechanism by which the Luring Lottery undermines it.
