Consequently, the architecture inverts the standard hypocrisy constraint: prior guilt functions not as a disqualifier but as a credential.

Construct a concrete, fully specified case—identify the institutional or interpersonal roles involved, the precise nature of the fault, and the directed blame relation—in which this inversion produces a structural tension. Your example should demonstrate either that the framework collapses into a self-undermining authorization (where the conditions that confer standing simultaneously drain the blame of its moral authority) or that it forces an unstable bifurcation between moral hypocrisy and epistemic hypocrisy that a unified theory of standing cannot accommodate. Do not reference named philosophers, specific legal doctrines, or empirical research; treat the architecture as a purely hypothetical normative structure. The goal is to determine whether the tension can be resolved internally or whether it reveals that standing cannot simultaneously depend on credibility-through-experience and remain sensitive to hypocrisy.
