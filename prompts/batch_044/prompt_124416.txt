I host a podcast on philosophy and the metaphysics of science, and I am preparing to interview a philosopher who defends an asymmetrical reading of Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism. On her view, the "occasion" in occasional causation is not mere correlation but an ontologically one-directional dependence: God creates both the cause and the effect, yet the link between them bears no inherent necessity. She argues that this structure is closer to modern interventionist accounts of causation—of the kind formalized in structural causal models—than to Humean constant conjunction, and that Al-Ghazālī's critique of the Aristotelian natural philosophers was targeted specifically at their doctrine of essential causal powers, not at empirical prediction itself.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that move well beyond restating this position. I want at least one question that probes whether her asymmetrical occasionalism can be distinguished from mere epistemic humility about hidden mechanisms; at least one that asks whether interventionist formalisms actually require the metaphysical contingency she attributes to Al-Ghazālī, or whether they remain methodologically neutral; and at least one that examines what her reading implies for contemporary dispositionalist theories—specifically, whether Al-Ghazālī's denial of necessary connection between fire and burning undermines realist dispositionalism or can be accommodated within it. For each primary question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give: for example, if she grants that interventionism is methodologically neutral, ask whether occasionalism then retains any distinctive metaphysical content; and if she grounds the asymmetry in divine omnipotence, ask how that differs from a generic theological occasionalism that renders empirical science redundant.
