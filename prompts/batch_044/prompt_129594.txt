In reporting on AI and philosophy of mind, I've been working through what philosophers call the Kripkenstein problem: the skeptical argument that there is no fact of the matter about which rule I mean when I apply a concept to a new case, paired with a skeptical solution that replaces such facts with conditions of communal acceptance. If that solution is correct, it seems to me that genuine rule-following is essentially a social phenomenon embedded in practices of correction and agreement among language users. Does this imply that an artificial system — lacking membership in any such linguistic community — cannot genuinely follow rules, only simulate them behaviorally? And if so, is the alignment community's talk of giving AI explicit rules to govern its behavior conceptually confused from the start, requiring us to abandon rule-based frameworks for pure behavioral conditioning? Or does the skeptical solution apply uniformly to all agents, human or artificial, so that the social conditions are conditions on normative attribution rather than a metaphysical gate that excludes machines?
