Because the statue is essentially an artwork with a specific form and history, its conditions for survival are stricter than the marble's: if the marble is melted and recast, the statue is destroyed, yet the marble might survive in a different shape. The speaker then added what he called a 'modal-substitution' thesis: during coincidence, the marble temporarily inherits the statue's fragility, so that for as long as it constitutes David, the marble literally could not survive being crushed into powder—its own, more permissive persistence conditions are suspended and replaced by the statue's. From this he drew the conclusion that constitution is best understood as a temporary, asymmetric identity: two natures share one modal career while they coincide, and that is why ordinary intuition pulls in both directions.
