Write a dialogue between two philosophers of equal competence—one defending the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) and one defending the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA)—who are applying anthropic reasoning not to cosmological models but to competing ontologies of consciousness. Theory Sparse holds that conscious experience requires a rare, structurally complex form of biological integration, producing relatively few observer-moments across the multiverse; Theory Abundant holds that consciousness is a graded, generic feature of sufficiently integrated information processing, yielding vastly more observers across artificial substrates, simple organisms, and distributed systems. The SSA advocate should argue that finding oneself conscious licenses reasoning only within the reference class appropriate to one's own evidence, not counting every potential observer that an alternative ontology might license; therefore one's existence should not strongly favor Theory Abundant merely because it multiplies minds. The SIA advocate should argue that the prior probability of being a particular observer scales with the total number of observers a theory predicts, giving Theory Abundant a direct anthropic boost—unless this reproduces Bostrom's "Presumptuous Philosopher" problem, where theories that postulate enormous populations in remote domains or substrates arbitrarily dominate our credences. Each must respond to the other's actual point: the SIA proponent should explain why self-indication does not collapse into counting every simple thermostat or distant simulated mind, and should address the charge that it privileges ontology-multiplying theories without independent empirical support; the SSA proponent should explain why conditioning on membership in a reference class does not render anthropic reasoning impotent when the competing theories themselves disagree about what belongs in the class, and should address the charge that SSA begs the question by defining the reference class using the very ontology being tested. The dialogue should contain real back-and-forth, not parallel monologues, and should end with both identifying the precise fracture point—whether the reference class must be fixed independently of the theories being compared, or whether the theories themselves determine the weighting—without either being reduced to a strawman. Keep the tone formal and analytical, as in a rationalist forum discussion, and do not include citations, bibliographies, or requests for further reading.
