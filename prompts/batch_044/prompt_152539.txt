I sit on my university's International Partnerships and Ethics Panel. We must recommend whether to sign a revised memorandum of understanding with a partner university in a country whose government has recently issued a 'Charter for the Protection of Western Values in Higher Education,' defining them as secular governance, gender-integrated research ethics, and market-oriented academic freedom. The partner rector claims this protects institutional autonomy from religious interference; our students' union contends that 'Western values' is an essentially contested concept that operates here as a civilisational label incompatible with our equality duties; and our legal office warns that contracting around an undefined normative category could expose us to liability should the partner state invoke the clause to justify exclusionary admissions policies. The draft contract asks both parties to 'actively uphold Western values in joint programmes.' Draft the panel's written opinion recommending approval with amended language, approval with conditions, or rejection. Your recommendation should address whether 'Western values' can be given coherent contractual content, what the phrase specifically includes and excludes in this institutional context, and how it functions argumentatively for each stakeholder. Lead with the bottom-line recommendation in the first sentence, then provide reasoning with no preamble and no restating of the question, and treat the strongest argument for the opposing position honestly.
