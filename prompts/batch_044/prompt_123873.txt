Consider the following pattern of verdicts on secession claims, reconstructed from a single consistent constitutional framework: Scotland’s 2014 independence referendum was legitimate because it occurred within an agreed constitutional process with democratic consent; Catalonia’s 2017 unilateral declaration was illegitimate because it ruptured constitutional order without prior negotiation; Quebec’s 1995 referendum was permissible only under a “clear question” and with federal partnership, not as a unilateral right; and Kosovo’s 2008 separation was justifiable solely as remedial secession after sustained rights violations, not as a primary right to exit. Now apply this framework to a new case: the autonomous region of Aethelmere, which lacks any distinct ethnicity or language but operates its energy grid through a distributed ledger protocol that conflicts with the central state’s carbon-tax regime, holds a non-binding referendum in which 62 percent vote to form a “protocol state” where citizenship is defined by node participation rather than birth or residence. Predict whether the framework permits this secession, identify which of the four precedents is the closest analog and why, state the competing rule each of two plausible interpretations adopts, and name a further hypothetical case that would distinguish them. Give your analysis in exactly five numbered items, each no more than two sentences and with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the predicted verdict for Aethelmere with the core reason; (2) the closest of the four precedents and the feature that makes it so; (3) the first plausible rule accommodating all prior verdicts and what it implies here; (4) the second plausible rule accommodating all prior verdicts and where it diverges; and (5) the further distinguishing case, stating what each rule predicts.
