I am writing in my capacity as the student delegate to my high school’s Institutional Equity and Policy Board, and I have been asked to draft the reasoning section of our formal recommendation memo to the administration regarding Case 2024-18. The case concerns our school’s Neutral Civic Space protocol, adopted last year and modeled partly on the French republican tradition of laïcité: all shared classrooms, hallways, and common areas are to remain free of visible religious, ethnic, or political symbols, on the theory that students should assimilate into a single, undifferentiated civic identity rather than organizing into publicly recognized group enclaves. The Baha’i Student Circle has petitioned for an exemption to display their tradition’s nine-pointed star symbol inside the library’s permanent multicultural exhibit case. They argue that this is recognition rather than proselytization, and that the protocol creates an asymmetric visibility burden—majority cultural norms already function as invisible institutional infrastructure, while minority practices must either vanish or seek special dispensation.

The student-government president opposes any exemption, insisting that even display-case recognition fractures common space and that the Anglo-American multiculturalist framework of parity of esteem is fundamentally incompatible with our school’s assimilationist mission. I need the board’s memo to recommend a concrete, actionable decision—approve the exemption, deny it, or approve it with conditions—that the administration can implement directly. Please take a committed position with clear justification rather than presenting a balanced menu of options, and please treat the strongest consideration on the losing side with genuine honesty rather than dismissing it. Write in American English. In your analysis, I specifically want you to engage the distinction between expressive assimilation, in which minority practices are permitted only when translated into majority-neutral terms, and structural assimilation, in which institutional architecture itself is redesigned to accommodate plural visibility without demanding such translation; determine which of these our current protocol actually enforces, and evaluate whether that enforcement is consistent with the board’s stated goal of equitable integration.
