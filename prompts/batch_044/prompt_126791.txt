I'm a software engineer with basically no formal philosphy background, but I got dragged into a long chat with two mates from my running club about this idea from L.A. Paul — the vampire one where becoming a vampire is meant to be rationally undecidable because it transforms what you value. Anyway, they had this back-and-forth and I couldn't tell who was talking rubbish. Can you go through it move by move?

Here's what happened. My friend Alex said deciding to have kids is exactly like Paul's vampire case: you've never done it, so you can't know what values you'll have afterwards, which means you can't rationally weigh the options now. Sam said that's definately overblown — you can read accounts, look at stats, talk to parents, so you're not flying blind. Alex came back that it's not just missing data; it's epistemically transformative, meaning you literally can't imagine the new values from thier current headspace. Sam then said well by that logic changing career or moving to Tokyo is also irrational, since you can't fully know those lives either. Alex said no, parenthood is personally transformative too — it changes who you are, not just your preferences — and that's what makes the seperate kind of problem. Sam reckoned Alex was just defining the problem away: anything big gets called 'transformative' and therefore off-limits to rational choice, which is a reductio. Alex answered that there's a real distinction between changes where you can still evaluate the outcome with your old values and ones where the old self is basically gone, and parenthood is the second kind. Sam said that's true of grief or falling in love as well — are they all irrational? He said without a principled line it's just 'scary big changes are bad'.

Who has the better point at each step? And is either missing something obvious — maybe about whether testimony can bridge the gap, or whether rationality even requires knowing the outcome in advance?
