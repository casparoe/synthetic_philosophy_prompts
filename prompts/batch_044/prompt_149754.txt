For an independent-study presentation in my philosophy course, I want to classify positions on the problem of universals around a specific diagnostic that standard surveys rarely highlight: whether a view can accommodate what I call uninstantiated structural roles—repeatable functional patterns, such as the algorithmic structure that generates prime numbers, that appear genuinely real yet need not be embodied in any single concrete object. Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy. The first node should ask whether the view admits repeatable properties at all; the second, for those that do, whether such properties must be instantiated in concrete particulars; and the third, whether they are mind-independent. Each leaf should identify a named position—extreme nominalism, resemblance nominalism, conceptualism, trope theory, immanent realism, and Platonic realism—plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment regarding uninstantiated structural roles. Any logical combination that yields no named view should be labeled unnamed, with a brief diagnosis of whether the gap stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Phrase every node as a concrete yes-or-no question that a student could answer without knowing in advance which philosophical label it leads to.
