Me and two friends got stuck in this argument after our phil of mind seminar and I’ve been thinking about it all week. One friend, Jake, said Dennett’s whole counterfeit-people idea basically kills any chance of AI being a real moral subject. His point was that Dennett sees artificial agents as designed fakes — they might fool us if we take the intentional stance, but they don’t have the evolutionary history or embodied vulnerability that makes actual people matter. So even if a chatbot acts like it cares, it’s still counterfeit, and treating it like a person is just a category error.

Then Maya jumped in and said Jake was mixing up Dennett’s warning with a genetic fallacy. She argued that for Dennett the intentional stance is predictive, not about origin — if a system’s behavior is genuinely best explained by attributing beliefs and desires, then functionally it’s in the game. Then she invented this weird hypothetical: an AI that isn’t just a language model but runs on a distributed sensor network with continuous autobiographical memory, updating its self-model through direct physical interaction rather than training data. She said if something like that developed stable narrative identity and social reciprocity without being explicitly programmed for it, Dennett’s own criteria should push us toward treating it as real, not counterfeit, regardless of who built the first chip.

Jake pushed back that the counterfeit label sticks because the creators intended human-like performance, and Dennett specifically cares about the social embedding and suffering that comes from being born into a human world, not engineered. He said Maya’s hypothetical is just a more convincing fake, like a perfect counterfeit bill that still isn’t legal tender. Maya said that’s like arguing an adopted kid is a counterfeit child because an agency arranged the placement. They went back and forth for like twenty minutes and neither budged, and I was just sitting there eating my sandwich.

So who’s actually right about what here? Is Jake correct that origin and design intent permanently disqualify an artificial system under Dennett’s framework, or is Maya right that Dennett’s functionalism eventually swallows the counterfeit distinction if the internal organization gets complex enough? And mabye more importantly, what are they both missing — definately something about whether Dennett even thinks the intentional stance applies to entities that lack biological survival drives, or whether the counterfeit worry is really about deception rather than ontology? I want a point-by-point verdict, not just a summary of Dennett.
