Since I recently began advising on comparative institutional development, I have encountered the Britain-China comparison—what the literature frames as the Needham question and the Great Divergence—deployed repeatedly as a kind of historical natural experiment. The analogy runs as follows: around 1700 Britain and China represent two large, technologically advanced agrarian societies with comparable demographic density, commercial integration, and manufacturing sophistication; they are then subjected to different institutional or geographical conditions, whether coal accessibility, colonial extraction, or state-market relations; one undergoes sustained industrialization while the other does not; therefore one can isolate the decisive causal variable by treating the pair as a controlled comparison with all other relevant factors held constant. This framing is used to license strong causal conclusions—for example, that a specific property-rights regime, resource endowment, or market structure explains the divergence. I want a rigorous audit of this analogy. First, lay out an explicit correspondence table: what serves as the treatment condition, the control, the held-constant covariates, the outcome measure, and the replication or counterfactual rerun that a laboratory design requires? Second, identify where the mapping fractures: in what respects were Britain and China not comparable baseline units, given differences in state structure, ecological constraints, and prior technological trajectories, and which of these differences are themselves endogenous to the divergence being explained? Third, what features of experimental design—exogeneity, unit homogeneity, counterfactual stability—does the analogy quietly import that historical comparison lacks? Fourth, does the conclusion that we can identify a single decisive factor survive once these disanalogies are explicit, or does the framing collapse into a post-hoc selection of differences that privileges the outcome? Finally, if the natural-experiment image fails, is there a more accurate methodological analogy—perhaps a path-dependent branching process or an institutional-ecology comparison—that preserves what the Britain-China pairing genuinely establishes without overstating its causal authority? Please also indicate which specific commitments of the Great Divergence literature your audit forces one to weaken or abandon.
