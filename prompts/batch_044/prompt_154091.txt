Her core claim is that in labor-market matching you can't decompose outcomes into individual preference orderings because the local network topology acts as a real causal constraint: it determines who even gets seen for an interview, and that constraint isn't reducible to summed-up individual beliefs about connections. Methodological individualists, she says, always redescribe this post-hoc by giving each agent a belief-state about the network, but that misses the emergent property. I want eight to ten interview questions that go way past "why not rational choice?" I also want one on whether bounded rationality alone can do all the work she's giving to the network, since if agents just have limited information that sounds like individual-level psychology again. For each question, include the follow-up. And please, no requests for a bibliography or specific citations — I'm trying to map the conceptual architecture, not build a syllabus.
