I'm preparing a position paper for a seminar on naturalizing intentionality, and I'd like a consistency check before I present. Here is the package of commitments I've arrived at in teleosemantics: (1) I endorse an etiological theory of mental content, holding that what a representation represents is fixed primarily by the selection history of the mechanisms that produce and consume it, rather than by present causal covariation alone. (2) I analyze misrepresentation as a malfunction in the consumer mechanism—specifically, as the token's failure to perform the proper function for which that particular consumer device was historically selected. (3) At the same time, I think representational content is genuinely context-sensitive and can be reshaped by present environmental scaffolding and social-linguistic practices in ways that outstrip any fixed ancestral assignment. (4) I deny that evolutionary success confers present normative authority: the fact that a predator-detection mechanism was selected for tracking threats gives me no reason, by itself, to believe there is currently a threat. (5) Nevertheless, I maintain that teleosemantics explains the normative dimension of mental content—why beliefs can be correct or incorrect—without importing non-natural properties. (6) Finally, I hold that genuinely novel conceptual content, such as theoretical scientific terms with no evolutionary precedent, can be accommodated within this framework because consumer mechanisms can be exapted for new representational roles.

Do these six commitments form a jointly tenable package, or do some pairs pull against each other in ways I haven't noticed? I am especially unsure about the relation between (1)-(2) and (3)-(6): if content is anchored in selection history, how can present scaffolding or exaptation alter it without breaking the etiological constraint? I also wonder whether (4) undermines (5)—if evolutionary history carries no normative authority for present belief, how can it ground the very correctness conditions that make misrepresentation possible? Please audit the set by identifying which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and ordering the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, indicate the least costly revision—whether weakening the etiological core, constraining the context-sensitivity claim, reconceiving the normative story, or allowing exaptation to operate outside the consumer-function framework—and note what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package. I have no predetermined preference for which pillar to sacrifice, so I want an even-handed assessment that traces exactly what each commitment commits me to, rather than a defense of any single view.
