Design a 75-minute simulation for a graduate seminar of sixteen students in political economy that makes participants feel a specific weakness in Lange’s trial-and-error method: not merely dispersed information, but production knowledge that is tacit, embodied, and impossible to declare to a planner without defeating local adaptation. Assign three students to a central board that adjusts point-prices across rounds to clear inventories, and thirteen to producer nodes each holding a hidden physical constraint—such as a left-handed tool limitation or an undisclosed material sensitivity—that alters their true production function but which they are forbidden to state aloud; the board sees only aggregate surplus and shortage, never these local misfits. Structure payoffs in points so the board earns for equilibrium and each producer earns for output plus a secret bonus only if their hidden constraint was actually satisfied, allowing the board to reach apparent price equilibrium in round two while several nodes suffer silent, invisible misallocation. After two rounds, hold a debrief that maps the hidden-constraint failures onto Hayek’s argument about the particular circumstances of time and place and the board’s adjustments onto Lange’s simulated-market reply, then state frankly which feature of tacit knowledge—its non-codifiable, bodily character—the game cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply verbally. Present the complete activity—setup, rules with payoff scheme, running instructions, debrief, and honest limitation—as exactly five numbered items, each one to two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
