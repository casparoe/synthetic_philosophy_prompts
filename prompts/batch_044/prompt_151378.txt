After a long and somewhat frustrating argument with a friend about Williams and moral progress, I want a consistency check before I present this in a seminar. Here is where I have landed:

(1) I accept Williams’s integrity objection in its strong form: a moral theory that can systematically require me to abandon my ground projects is unacceptable, not merely costly, because those projects partly constitute my identity.
(2) I accept his point about having one thought too many: in intimate relationships, saving my spouse for the direct reason that she is my spouse is appropriate, whereas saving her because a principle licenses it corrupts the reason.
(3) I think moral progress is largely the expansion of the circle of moral concern—extending direct consideration to distant strangers, future people, and non-human animals—which seems to depend on principled, impartial reasoning of exactly the kind that (1) and (2) treat with suspicion.
(4) I hold that agent-relative reasons (ground projects, special obligations) and agent-neutral reasons are incommensurable: they share no common metric, and neither can be fully translated into the other.
(5) I think there is a defensible emergency exception: if saving a very large number of lives requires temporarily suspending a ground project, I may do so, but only if I do not justify the suspension through a cost-benefit calculus—I act and accept the loss.
(6) I also hold a less common claim: ground projects have narrative non-substitutability. They are not interchangeable with other projects of equal value; abandoning one damages the coherence of one’s life story in a way that substitution cannot repair.

Do these hang together? Specifically, does (3)’s demand for principled expansion genuinely conflict with (4)’s incommensurability, or is that tension only apparent? Does (5)’s emergency exception secretly presuppose that agent-neutral and agent-relative reasons are commensurable after all, undermining (4)? Is the clash between (1) and (5)—integrity forbids abandoning projects, the exception permits it—dissolved once we notice that (1) targets systematic alienation rather than one-off suspension? Rank the real tensions by severity, identify the single least costly revision that cleans up the most damage, and state what that revision costs me. Keep your audit under 350 words, ordered by severity, with no bibliography.
