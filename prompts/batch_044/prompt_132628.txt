Goodman's grue paradox is frequently interpreted as demonstrating that inductive reasoning lacks rational foundation unless we can ground the projectibility of predicates in an underlying metaphysics of natural kinds—where 'green' is privileged over 'grue' by corresponding to a real universal rather than an arbitrary linguistic construction. Accepting this reading, how should we assess the claim that meta-learning architectures designed for non-stationary environments overcome Goodman's challenge? In particular, does the dynamic revision of internal representations in response to distributional shift constitute an algorithmic analogue to metaphysical grounding, or does it simply displace the original arbitrariness onto the selection of meta-learning rates and adaptation thresholds?
