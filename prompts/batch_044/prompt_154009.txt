I am taking a graduate seminar on moral psychology, and after working through P.F. Strawson’s well-known framework of reactive attitudes, I have been thinking about what would happen if his conceptual distinctions were administered literally by a regulatory body. Strawson distinguishes reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, forgiveness, and love—which presuppose a stance of interpersonal involvement or “participation,” from the objective attitude, which regards a person as an object of social policy, causal explanation, or management. He also argues that recognizing the causal determination of behavior does not furnish a genuine reason to suspend these reactive attitudes, since they are embedded in ordinary human practices rather than being optional beliefs we can discard on theoretical grounds.
