hey, I'm drafting an essay on the bootstrapping problem and want to push it into a weird corner. The standard setup is using a faculty to validate itself — eyes checking eyes, etc. The easy knowledge version says you can't bootstrap your way into knowing your perception is reliable. My twist: there's an analog in aestetics and taste. You train your ear for free jazz, then use that trained ear to declare free jazz objectively better than elevator music. If taste is knowledge, that's circular — you're using the very standard you're trying to justify. But what if taste is a skill, not a belief? Then you're not bootstrapping beliefs, you're just exercising a competance to assess its own products. So maybe the easy knowledge problem only bites propositional attitudes, not skills. Does this distinction actually hold water, or am I just redefining taste to dodge the circularity? Also, is this interesting or am I stretching the concept too thin? Would appreciate comments on accuracy and whether the skill/belief split does the work I want.
