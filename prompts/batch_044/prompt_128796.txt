I am delivering the opening remarks at a one-day memorial colloquium for Dr. Elena Voss, a philosopher of mind who argued for three decades that phenomenal consciousness is real but that the theoretical posit we call qualia is structurally incoherent — a view she associated with the strategy of “quining” a concept, in the spirit of arguments developed by Chase and Sanborn. The audience is deliberately mixed: her former doctoral students, several neuroscientists with whom she collaborated, and family members who are not philosophers. I want the address to do genuine philosophical work rather than serve as ornament: it should explain why she believed that asking “what is it like to be you” presupposes a category error, and why this matters for both scientific practice and ordinary remembrance. Specifically, she held that the incoherence of qualia lies in treating phenomenal properties as at once private and incomparable, yet also intersubjectively comparable, and as causally inert in physical explanation yet indispensable for explaining behavior. The remarks must navigate a delicate constraint: I should avoid the language of inner lights or private theaters that she rejected, but also avoid framing her view as claiming that experience is an illusion, which she explicitly denied. The speech should shape my account of her legacy — I must be able to say that her work cleared space for an interdisciplinary study of consciousness that does not require reifying phenomenal properties, without reducing consciousness to mere neural correlation. Please write a ten-minute address that introduces this core move, addresses both the specialists and the non-specialists in the room with equal respect, and closes with a reflection on what it means to honor a thinker whose central claim was that one of our most familiar concepts does not survive careful scrutiny. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation of specific papers by page or year; speak from direct knowledge of her position, but do not invent publication titles or dates.
