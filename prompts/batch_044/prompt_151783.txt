Consider the claim that, under a continuous-gradation model of phenomenal consciousness with no sharp phylogenetic cutoff, the aggregate expected moral weight of all currently living insects exceeds that of all currently living vertebrates. Give me your credence in this claim, decomposed as follows: first, the probability that phenomenal consciousness in animals is genuinely graded rather than all-or-nothing; second, conditional on gradation, the probability that the average sentience-weight of an individual insect is at least 1/10,000 that of a human; third, the probability that the global insect-to-vertebrate population ratio is high enough for the product of the second term and the population differential to dominate the vertebrate total; fourth, any correction factor you think belongs (for instance, differences in lifespan or in the intensity-duration profile of experience), with its own number and one-line justification. Multiply or combine the terms as you see fit, report the resulting credence, and identify which single term you are least sure about and what empirical result—such as a specific finding about centralized nociceptive processing in a given order—would most move it.
