Write an essay on the following controversy: when automation shifts from replacing manual execution to replacing the exercise of judgment itself—what can be called cognitive automation—does it undermine the meaning of work in a way that industrial automation did not? One side holds that industrial automation largely preserved work’s meaning by moving people from physical execution to oversight and repair, leaving intact both the social recognition of contribution and the phenomenological structure of problem-solving. The other side holds that cognitive automation is categorically different because it severs the link between effort and outcome at the level of reasoning rather than muscle, producing what might be termed “hollow roles”: social positions that retain titles, salaries, and organizational charts but lack the internal architecture of judgment that made them meaningful activities rather than mere occupancy.

Weigh these positions, but also consider a third framing: that work’s meaning may never have depended primarily on either social recognition or the exercise of judgment alone, but rather on what could be called “resistant engagement”—the experience of one’s intentions meeting friction in a material or social environment that genuinely pushes back. On this view, automation threatens meaning precisely when it eliminates resistance, whether physical or cognitive, while automation that preserves or redirects resistance (for example, managing unpredictable, failure-prone automated systems) might sustain meaningful work even as traditional tasks disappear.

Reach and defend a specific conclusion about whether cognitive automation poses a distinctive threat to the meaning of work compared to earlier waves of mechanization, and specify what conditions—if any—would allow work to remain meaningful in a highly automated economy. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Lead with your bottom-line conclusion in the first sentence, then develop your reasoning with no introductory preamble and no restatement of the question.
