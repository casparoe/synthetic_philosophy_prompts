I sit on our uni's mental-health research oversight board. We're deadlocked over a pilot study that wants to roll out a screening protocol for 'algorithmic dissociation syndrome' — attention-fragmentation and identity-blurring symptoms the PI says are being amplified by feedback loops with reccomendation algos. They cite Hacking-style looping: once you classify people, they start enacting the category, so prevalence data becomes self-fulfilling and predictive validity collapses. Half the board thinks we need the study to catch the loop before it hardens into a DSM-style entry. The other half thinks deploying the screen *is* the loop — we'd be manufacturing the condition we're claiming to study. Draft our decision memo with a real bottom line — approve with safeguards, reject, or whatever — and include the best argument for the side you overrule. Don't just list tradeoffs; take a stand we could vote on. Also be honest about how confident you are that looping applies here, since the literature on this specific syndrome is basically nonexistent and I don't want false precision.
