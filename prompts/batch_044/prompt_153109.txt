I serve as the senior interpretation officer at a regional museum that regularly receives proposals to display, label, or contextualise contested objects: items with colonial provenance, sacred artefacts claimed by origin communities, and scientific specimens acquired through now-discredited methods. We need a decision procedure grounded in anekāntavāda rather than in generic pluralism or liberal tolerance, because my team must apply it repeatedly — often under time pressure — and must be able to defend each step to trustees, community representatives, and educators.

Please construct the procedure as an ordered sequence of questions that a curator can run in under an hour. Apply the full procedure to three proposals: (a) a ceremonial mask requested for repatriation by its source community but sought for material-science study; (b) a wall text for a weapon used both in colonial suppression and in anti-colonial resistance; and (c) the botanical-specimen case mentioned above.

Write in British English, do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, do not quote any primary Jain texts verbatim, and keep the deliverable self-contained so that a model without web access could still execute it. State at each stage what the procedure can settle, what remains unsettled, and where an editor or trustee must make an unavoidable value judgement.
