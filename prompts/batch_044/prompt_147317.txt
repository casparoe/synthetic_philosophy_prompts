I am a doctoral student in philosophy of science, and I have been observing the evaluation criteria used by several interdisciplinary grant panels that fund evolutionary-cognitive research. There is a consistent institutional pattern: reviewers approve proposals that explain high-level cognitive traits—such as recursive syntax or normative reasoning—only when the applicant specifies a continuous, stepwise adaptive pathway assembled from lower-level mechanisms that are already independently established. Reviewers routinely reject accounts that invoke sudden, functionally irreducible jumps or top-down selection pressures as illegitimate skyhooks, even when the two kinds of proposal generate identical empirical predictions. At the same time, these panels reject purely molecular-level derivations that attempt to move straight from gene-expression dynamics to the cognitive trait without passing through intermediate organizational levels—what one program officer recently described, in a rejection letter, as an unacceptable form of greedy reductionism. The practice therefore enforces a narrow middle space: explanations must be built bottom-up, but not all the way down in one greedy leap, and they must avoid any mysterious top-down injection.

What philosophical commitments about scientific explanation, ontological levels, and evolutionary novelty does this screening practice actually presuppose? Does it strictly require a mechanistic or multi-level view of explanation, or does it merely fit well with one? Can a pluralist about explanatory autonomy—someone who thinks that functional-level descriptions have their own legitimate standards—accept the practice, or does the institutional rejection of both extremes quietly rule out pluralism in favor of a specific architectural ontology? Furthermore, if we replaced this criterion with one that permitted either pure molecular derivation or irreducible functional emergence, what exactly would have to change in the field's underlying conception of how cognitive traits are constituted?
