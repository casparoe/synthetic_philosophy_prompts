Quick essay sketch I'm calling "tranquilism with teeth." The basic claim is that only the absence of craving and suffering matters—pleasure is just decorative noise. But I want to push a weirder version: craving isn't a privation, it's an active ontological clutter, like dust that actually takes up space. So when it's fully removed you don't feel "happy"; you experience a kind of structural emptiness that isn't emotional flatness but spatial lightness. I think this might dodge the usual "this is just depression with branding" objection, but I'm worried I'm smuggling in stoicism without noticing. One concrete problem: if absence is the only good, is creating a new person wrong only if they actually end up craving, or wrong in principle because you're introducing potential clutter into the world? Would love comments on whether the "clutter" metaphor holds up, whether spatial emptiness is distinguishable from low affect, and if this collapses back into regular negative utilitarianism.
