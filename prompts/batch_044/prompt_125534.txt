I'm writing up my philosophical profile for our seminar on meaning and cognition, and I want a consistency check before I present it. Here is where I stand: (1) I accept the GEB-inspired tripartite division of meaning into frame messages (the contextual conditions that make interpretation possible), outer messages (the explicit syntactic and semantic content), and inner messages (the implicit intent or self-referential grounding of the communicator); (2) I hold that artificial systems currently lack inner messages because they do not realize the kind of self-referential "strange loop" that Hofstadter associates with genuine subjectivity, even when they parse frames and decode outer messages with high fidelity; (3) I think that in human ritual speech and irony, the outer message can carry the full semantic load even when the inner message is absent or directly contradicted; (4) I am inclined toward a weak intentionalism about content, according to which a system's functional and causal organization—not just its internal mental states—partially fixes what its representations are about; and (5) I believe the syntax-to-semantics gap can be closed in principle by treating frame-activation as a physical process governed by normative constraints rather than by private mental states. Do these hang together? Specifically, is there a tension between (2) and (4), since weak intentionalism might seem to allow artificial systems to have content via functional organization alone? Does (3) undermine (1) by showing that inner messages are dispensable for meaning? And is (5) compatible with (2)'s claim that self-reference is necessary for the normative grounding of frames? Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate which pairs are only apparently in conflict, and suggest the least costly revision if my package is not fully consistent. Answer in American English.
