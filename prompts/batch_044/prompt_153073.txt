Imagine Immanuel Kant were alive today and confronted with autonomous systems that display sphexishness — the rigid, stereotyped behavioural loops seen in the digger wasp Sphex, performed without contextual adjustment — alongside architectures that adapt flexibly and open-endedly to novel environments. Drawing on his distinction between mechanism and autonomy, how would Kant likely judge whether such flexibility, absent rational self-legislation, is anything more than an elaborate form of determinism? Would he hold that genuine freedom requires causality through reason rather than mere adaptive responsiveness, and what implications might he draw for the moral or legal status of machines that fall between sphex-like rigidity and fully rational agency?
