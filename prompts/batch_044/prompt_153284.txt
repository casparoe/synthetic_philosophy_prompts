I just spent an evening arguing with a friend over a restricted version of linguistic relativity that I find more defensible than Whorf's original claim: not that grammar constrains perception, but that obligatory grammatical categories—specifically evidential marking—function as retrieval scaffolds, systematically altering causal attribution in episodic recall without changing the underlying experience. I maintain this escapes the usual "thought without language" objections and the Pirahã-style counterexamples because it targets reconstruction rather than conceptual availability. What would genuinely count against this position? Please distinguish fatal defeaters from merely awkward ones—whether from cross-linguistic memory studies, from languages with rich evidential systems that show no predicted narrative shifts, or from philosophical arguments that retrieval scaffolding collapses into generic framing effects. I also want an honest assessment of whether this view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, whether that insulation is a structural weakness for a claim of this kind.
