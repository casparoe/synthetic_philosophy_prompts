I'd like a consistency check on my interpretive commitments regarding Machiavelli's account of fortune and virtù, specifically as developed in The Prince and the Discourses. Here is where I stand:

(1) I read Machiavelli's virtù as a form of practical intelligence that includes theatrical self-fashioning, not merely strategic calculation;

(2) I take the river metaphor in Chapter XXV of The Prince to imply that fortune is not probabilistic risk but a kind of structural indeterminacy that resists quantification;

(3) I think Machiavelli's claim that fortune is the arbiter of half our actions is compatible with strong voluntarism about the remainder only if we understand that claim as setting boundary conditions rather than allocating causal shares;

(4) I believe the gendered imagery of fortuna as a woman to be subdued is not ornamental but carries conceptual weight—namely, that fortune is figured as an external, resistant materiality that virtù must shape through forceful engagement;

(5) I hold that Machiavelli's republican writings in the Discourses modify the princely account by distributing virtù across institutions rather than concentrating it in one agent.

Do these hang together? Specifically, does (4)'s emphasis on gendered materiality conflict with (2)'s claim about structural indeterminacy? Does (5)'s institutional distribution of virtù undermine (1)'s focus on theatrical self-fashioning by individual agents? And is (3)'s boundary-condition reading actually available in the text, or does it smuggle in a distinction between determinism and freedom that Machiavelli would not recognize?

Rank the tensions by severity, identify any apparent tensions that dissolve on closer inspection, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems without abandoning the core reading.
