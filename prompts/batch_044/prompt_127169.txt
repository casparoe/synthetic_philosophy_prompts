Write a parody of an end-user license agreement, approximately 700 words, formatted as numbered clauses with lettered sub-clauses, for a fictional civic-governance platform called "OpenSource Social Contract" that replaces voting with program-equilibrium mechanisms: each citizen submits a transparent, executable source-code module that encodes their decision rules, and these modules are executed against one another within a transparent mechanism-design environment to produce binding social contracts. The satire should take literally the core philosophical and game-theoretic commitments of program-equilibrium models—specifically, that full source-code transparency eliminates strategic ambiguity, that commitment is unproblematic once code is published, that recursive self-modification can be handled by standard termination conditions, and that the distinction between a program and its execution trace resolves questions of personal identity over time. The humor must come from exaggerating these real features in deadpan legal prose. Include the following elements: (a) a "No-Fork Identity Clause" that defines whether a citizen remains the same contracting party when their module is copied with minor syntactic alterations, and whether the copy counts as an independent voter or as collusion; (b) an arbitration provision for "functionally equivalent but syntactically distinct" submissions, specifying whether the platform treats near-duplicate programs as redundant expressions of preference or as strategic duplication; (c) a deadpan sidebar or appendix clause titled "Concerns from the Frequentist Oversight Board," complaining—without breaking the legal register—that the platform's Bayesian update protocol assigns non-zero prior weight to worlds in which the mechanism itself malfunctions, and objecting to the use of old evidence (e.g., previously observed module behavior) to revise priors over other citizens' current code; (d) a "Termination, Refund, and Continuity" policy for failed uploads, specifying precisely what counts as "you" if the emulation crashes before convergence, whether a partial execution trace qualifies for a continuity claim, and under what conditions a refund is issued for identity disputes. The voice should be that of a meticulous but oblivious terms-of-service document. A reader familiar with mechanism design and Bayesian epistemology should recognize that every absurd clause maps onto a genuine theoretical problem—commitment, transparency, self-reference, old evidence—while a reader without that background should still grasp the satire. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or references to specific authors or texts; the prompt itself should provide all conceptual context needed.
