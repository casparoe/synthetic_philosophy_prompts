Please evaluate the following examination response submitted for my graduate seminar in metaphysics. The exam question asks the student to analyze a modal replacement scenario: an enduring biological organism O persists from t1 through t3 via gradual cellular replacement that preserves functional organization; in a counterfactual world w, the replacement is interrupted at t2, so that at t3 O retains all its original parts yet exhibits the same functional organization as in the actual world. The student must determine whether this case undermines endurantism or perdurantism, correctly deploy the distinction between four-dimensional temporal parts and complete presence at each time, and address the temporary-intrinsics problem without conflating distinct solutions.

Student response:

Perdurantists analyze O as a four-dimensional worm made of instantaneous temporal parts: O-t1, O-t2, O-t3. Because O-t3 in the actual world contains replaced cells, while O-t3 in world w contains original cells, the two slices differ in their intrinsic properties and therefore are not identical. Hence the counterfactual does not establish cross-world identity for the slice, though the four-dimensional worm in w might still be a counterpart rather than the same object.

Endurantists deny temporal parts entirely. For them O is wholly present at t3 in both worlds. Since O in w shares the same origin and the same functional organization as O in the actual world, numerical identity holds across worlds. The fact that O has different material parts in the two worlds is irrelevant, because endurantists do not treat parts as essential.

The problem of temporary intrinsics arises because an enduring object seems to have incompatible properties at different times, for instance being warm at t1 and cool at t2. Endurantists resolve this by indexing properties to times: O is warm-at-t1 and cool-at-t2. This strategy is essentially equivalent to the perdurantist claim that O-t1 is warm and O-t2 is cool, since both approaches distribute properties across temporal locations.

David Lewis argued that perdurantism is false because it conflicts with our ordinary belief that we persist as single entities through change. To defend endurance, Lewis proposed that enduring objects possess their properties only in relation to the times at which they exist, thereby avoiding contradiction without invoking temporal parts.

Consequently, the modal case actually strengthens the endurance view. Because O can survive with entirely different parts in world w without becoming a different object, we see that persistence is not tied to part-constancy. This flexibility is exactly what the four-dimensionalist denies, since for the perdurantist any change in parts produces a different slice and thus a different worm.
