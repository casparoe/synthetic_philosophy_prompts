i've been stuck in this weird stalemate with my advisor about borges and time. philosophers of time read A New Refutation of Time and The Garden of Forking Paths as actually advancing a serious metaphysical claim — that temporal succession is an illusion, that past and future are just present consciousness in disguise, maybe even a schopenhaurian idealism wearing literary clothes. they point to the explicit arguments about hume's bundle theory and the denial of identity over time. on the other side lit-crit people say that's the whole mistake: treating a fiction writer's paradox as a philosophy paper. they say the stories are designed to produce cognitive dissonence, and the refutation is self-undermining on purpose — the narrative voice in The Secret Miracle literally freezes time for the protagonist, which shows borges is playing with narrative possibility, not endorsing a view.

the thing is, both camps agree on the textual details. they both know about the cyclical time in The Doctrine of Cycles, they both see the references to hume and schopenhauer, they both admit borges never resolves the paradox. but they've been talking past each other for years. my working theory is that it's not really about borges at all — it's about whether literary paradox can ever count as philosophical argument, which is a methodological disagreement disguised as an interpretive one. but maybe i'm wrong and there's a hidden factual question about borges's actual philosophical training? or maybe they're just answering different questions — one asking what metaphysics is implied, the other asking what the artistic function is?

anyway, can you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is? use what each side actually says about the texts — the humean arguments, the narrative framing in the stories, the cyclical vs linear distinction — and tell me if it's verbal, substantive, or just bedrock intuitions about fiction's limits. and if it's stalled because the question is ill-posed, say so. also, what would actually move it? a distinction between philosophical content and philosophical method? finding some letter where borges admits he's kidding? or is there nothing that could settle it?
