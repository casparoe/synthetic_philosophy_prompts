I've been trying to understand the Löbian obstacle—the idea that a rational agent can't fully trust its own reasoning because of something called Löb's theorem. I built what I call an Oracle-Reflection argument, treating my future self like a black-box oracle whose outputs I should mirror at every timestep in a "credence ladder." Now the conclusion looks absurd, so I need to know where it breaks. Here is the chain:

(1) If I'm rational, then whenever I can reliably predict that careful future reasoning will lead me to accept P, I should now assign P high credence; refusing to do so seems like willful self-deception.

(2) Model my internal reasoning as a formal system with a provability predicate Prov(P): "this system eventually proves P."

(3) The Oracle-Reflection Principle—my version of self-trust—says I should accept: if Prov(P) then P. Otherwise I'm treating my own mind as unreliable by default.

(4) Löb's theorem states that in any system strong enough for basic arithmetic, if the system can prove [Prov(P) → P], then it can prove P outright, for any P.

(5) Applying (3) inside my own system yields Prov([Prov(P) → P]). By Löb I get Prov(P) for arbitrary P, and then by (3) I derive P itself.

(6) So a self-trusting agent could justify belief in contradictions, or in 2+2=5, purely by trusting its own ladder—without any external check.

(7) That can't be right; rationality doesn't license omniscience or contradiction, so (1) must be false and self-trust is impossible.

But I want to keep (1). I'd rather pay the cost elsewhere. Is the mistake in (3)—that I'm treating a normative attitude as an internal logical axiom? Or in (2)—that real belief updates probabilities, not formal proofs, so Löb's conditions don't apply? Or is there a hidden scope shift between "I trust my reasoning" and "my system contains a reflection axiom about itself"? Tell me which step to reject, why it looked innocent, and what giving it up costs—especially if I want to preserve both rational self-trust and the idea that I don't have to believe contradictions.
