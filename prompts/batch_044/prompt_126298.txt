im trying to figure out if my gut on exploitation and alienation actually hangs together, using marxist ideas but not just repeating the textbook stuff. heres my verdicts on cases.

1. 19th century factory worker: exploited yes, alienated yes. obvious.

2. senior software engineer at big tech, big salary and stock, but she never sees the end user, her code is proprietary, she feels like a replaceable part. exploited? i feel like no, she's getting more value back than she produces in surplus, or at least its hidden. alienated? yes, clearly.

3. freelance illustrator, owns her gear, picks clients, but every brief forces her to perform a 'quirky artistic persona' she dont recognize as hers, and clients mine her taste without paying for the emotional labor of maintaining that mask. exploited? maybe yes, but its not wage labor. alienated? yes, but its from her own style, not from a product.

4. worker-owned bakery coop, everyone votes, shares profits, but they still have to cut corners and waste food to compete with corporate chains. exploited? no capitalist extracting surplus, so no. alienated? weirdly yes, but its from the market and from each other, not from an owner.

5. rentier who lives off inherited property, does no labor, spends days on hobbies. exploited? he extracts surplus value from tenants, so in a broad sense yes. alienated? definitely not, he has no labor to be estranged from.

6. twitch streamer, owns the camera, builds community, but the algorithm and chat extract constant emotional performance; revenue split is unpredictable. exploited? yes, by the platform. alienated? she's hyper-visible but says her 'real self' is somewhere else.

the case i keep flipping on: a sessional university lecturer on rolling contracts. she technically designs the syllabus, but admin loads her with metrics and student evaluations that shape what she can say. exploited? salary is okay, but the university extracts her intellectual personality. alienated? yes, but is it marxist alienation or just burnout?

is there one principle that captures all six verdicts, linking exploitation and alienation? or are they independent dimensions that just overlap sometimes? if there is a principle, state it precisely and say which of my verdicts it forces me to drop. also predict for an unlisted case: an ai data annotator working from home, paid per task, owns her laptop, but the task design makes her feel her judgment is mined and then discarded by engineers she'll never meet. exploitation? alienation? and be honest about whether your principle saves my lecturer verdict or kills it.
