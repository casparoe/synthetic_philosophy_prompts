I've been following the analytic literature on meaning in life—Wolf's fitting-fulfillment condition, the hybrid subjective-objective accounts, Metz's debates over whether meaning comes in degrees, and the recent attempts to treat meaning as a measurable component of well-being—and it strikes me that we've reached a point where the philosophy is precise enough to be dangerous. In discussions with friends who read the same rationalist and effective-altruism-adjacent forums, we've joked about what happens when these frameworks escape the seminar room. I'm writing this because I want to see whether the logic holds up under institutional pressure, particularly when it has to accommodate both the demand for objective value and the absurdist claim that all such value is ultimately a fiction we maintain against cosmic indifference.

Write a deadpan, ironic op-ed of roughly 600 words, in the voice of a senior domestic-policy advisor addressing a serious public-policy quarterly. Propose, without ever breaking character, the creation of a federal "Meaning Audit and Absurdist Compliance Office" (MAACO) tasked with standardizing citizens' access to objectively valuable projects and certifying subjective fulfillment. The satire must come from extending actual philosophical distinctions: distinguish sharply between subjective satisfaction and objective worth; treat Wolfian "active engagement" as a quantifiable labor input; note the problem of "meaning inflation" if citizens claim Sisyphus-style revolt as a low-cost exemption; and establish a "Camus exemption" pathway for those who claim to generate meaning precisely through recognition of cosmic indifference. Include bureaucratic specifics—tiered citizenship ratings, an appeals board for citizens who score high on fulfillment but low on objectivity, and a national holiday marking the publication of the first standardized Meaning Ledger—while keeping every philosophical reference accurate enough that a specialist recognizes the view being exaggerated. The piece should stay deadpan throughout, never winking or signaling irony explicitly; the reader should feel by the end that if meaning is simultaneously a public good to be administered and, on the absurdist account, a necessary fiction maintained against an indifferent universe, then state administration is either absurd or tyrannical, and the ambiguity should remain unresolved. Please begin with the op-ed itself, with no preface or commentary, opening in its first sentence with the bottom-line proposal, then unfolding the reasoning with no restatement of this prompt.
