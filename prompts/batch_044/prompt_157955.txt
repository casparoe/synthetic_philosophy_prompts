"Both microhistory and history from below try to recover people who usually vanish from state-centered narratives. Microhistory—think of Ginzburg's study of the miller Menocchio—zooms in on one outlier and reads inquisitorial records against the grain to reconstruct a peasant cosmology. History from below—think Thompson's work on English working-class formation—tracks collective agency through strikes, rituals, and shared experience.

The key difference is how they treat elite archives. Microhistory depends on them: without the inquisitor's transcript Menocchio is silent, so the method accepts that state documents can be twisted to speak for subaltern voices if you read them closely enough. History from below, by contrast, actively avoids state papers; Thompson draws mainly on oral tradition, folk songs, and workshop artifacts because he believes bureaucratic records always reproduce elite categories and can never be read against the grain.

There's also the representativeness problem. Ginzburg chooses Menocchio because he is statistically typical of Friulian millers, which lets one case stand for a whole social group. Thompson rejects that kind of sampling altogether and just narrates collective struggle as it unfolded, without claiming any individual stands for the class."
