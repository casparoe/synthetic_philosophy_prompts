Give an example of a conceptual issue arising at the intersection of moral particularism and moral generalism, specifically in a case where the particularist denies not only the existence of universal moral principles but also the existence of stable, morally relevant properties that could ground analogical reasoning across contexts. Construct a scenario involving a novel institutional practice—say, the delegation of interpersonal conflict resolution to an artificial mediator designed to optimize for long-term relational stability—where a generalist appeals to a principle of procedural fairness and transparency, while the particularist rejects both the principle and any analogical appeal to face-to-face mediation on the grounds that the technological context eliminates the very properties that made the original cases morally significant. The issue should turn on whether the particularist can maintain that the mediated case is genuinely different without implicitly relying on a general claim about the moral importance of direct emotional reciprocity or physical co-presence. The example should be intelligible to someone familiar with the particularism-generalism debate but should not depend on citation of specific authors or texts.
