I sit on the funding and ethics review panel for our university's Center for Applied Philosophy. We've received a proposal that extends the Knobe effect—the asymmetry in ascribing intentionality to side effects based on moral valence—into institutional cognition. The PI wants to test whether collective decision bodies exhibit the same bias: specifically, whether board members are more likely to describe a harmful policy side-effect as "intentional" when they condemn the underlying decision than when they endorse an identical outcome. The design embeds modified vignettes in real deliberative contexts and defers debriefing until after recommendations are recorded. One panelist says this is routine methodological deception with collective subjects; another argues that applying individual-level intentionality ascriptions to boards is conceptually incoherent and that the individual-level literature is already well-replicated, making the extension redundant. The director needs our recommendation—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—by Friday, with reasoning that treats the conceptual objection about collective intentionality seriously rather than dismissing it, addresses whether replication concerns should affect funding, and proposes a concrete time frame for any approved pilot. Draft the short memo I'll circulate.
