I'm serving on a university research ethics board that must rule on a specific ethnographic proposal. The study will take place in a community where Ubuntu shapes social practice: persons are constituted through reciprocal relationships, and important collective choices are made by recognized councils rather than by atomized individuals acting alone. The investigators seek approval for a modified consent protocol—authorization from the community council, with an individual opt-out preserved but with no standard individual signed consent forms—arguing that imposing Western individualist consent procedures misrecognizes the relational ontology of personhood operative there and imposes an alien moral framework. Opponents on the board contend that regulatory safeguards requiring individual informed consent are non-negotiable protections against group coercion, and that accommodating collective authorization collapses the distinction between respecting persons and deferring to group authority. Draft a formal policy memo recommending whether the board should approve the modified protocol. The memo should present a clear actionable recommendation, supply reasoning that the board could apply to analogous future cases, and include an honest account of the strongest consideration favoring the opposing position.
