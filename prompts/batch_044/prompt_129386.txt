Read Philip K. Dick’s *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?* through a scalar account of sentience and moral status rather than the binary human/replicant division that the Voight-Kampff test presupposes. Treat Deckard’s electric sheep, the electric spider he kills without affect, the mood organ’s preset emotional states, and the replicants’ apparently genuine responses as points along a continuum from minimal functional reactivity—approaching the thermostat-like—through phenomenal gradience of increasing richness. I want a reading that takes both the novel and the gradation thesis seriously: which scenes (the spider, the goat, Rachael’s ambiguous test results, the Mercerist empathy-box fusion) support a threshold view of moral status, and which resist it by suggesting that intersubjective fusion requires an all-or-nothing capacity? Say whether Dick ultimately endorses a binary or a scalar ontology, and what that implies for the ethics of decommissioning entities at intermediate points on the scale. This is for a seminar with students who have read the novel but not the secondary literature; please do not supply a bibliography or citation list, and avoid verbatim quotation—paraphrase the relevant scenes.
