I've been revisiting Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" after a long thread in a rationalist forum about whether formal verification practices implicitly rely on distinctions that Quine would reject, and I want to press a comparison that hasn't come up clearly in those discussions. Quine's central argument is that the analytic-synthetic distinction is unsustainable because belief revision operates holistically across a web of belief: no statement, logical or empirical, is in principle immune to revision, with adjustments distributed according to pragmatic virtues like simplicity, familiarity, and conservatism. Against this, I want to place the epistemic structural realist claim that scientific knowledge reveals only the mathematical structure of phenomena, leaving the intrinsic nature of the entities that instantiate that structure permanently underdetermined.

The comparison I keep returning to is whether Quine's holistic pragmatism actually undermines the structural realist's boundary between structural knowledge and ontological ignorance. Specifically, if selecting among competing mathematical frameworks is itself just another case of pragmatic belief revision—protecting certain parts of the web while sacrificing others—then does the structural realist's distinction between knowing relations and not knowing relata collapse into a conventional choice about which beliefs to hold fixed? Or can the two positions be reconciled by saying that Quine operates at the level of linguistic framework choice, while structural realism operates at the level of ontological interpretation within a framework that has already been pragmatically selected? I would especially value an answer that addresses whether Quine's thesis of underdetermination applies with equal force to the choice between isomorphic mathematical structures that make identical empirical predictions but posit different ontologies.
