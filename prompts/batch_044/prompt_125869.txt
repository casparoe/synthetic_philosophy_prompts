I've come around to the idea that the Knobe effect isnt just a blip in moral psych but reveals that folk intentionality is built from the ground up via outcome valence—people dont track mental states first and then moralize; the moral evaluation comes first and carves out what counts as intentional. I think this probably extends even to weird edge cases, like judging whether a glitch in an image-generation model was deliberate based on whether the result looks ugly or offensive. So: what would actually count against this view? I want the serious defeaters—if it disappears in experts, if cross-cultural studies break it, if mental-state tracking is just masked rather than absent—ranked from fatal to merely awkward. And I want you to say what defenders of the effect would concede versus what they'd dismiss. Separate whats settled from whats genuinely contested among people who know the literature, and dont pretend there's a consensus if there isnt.
