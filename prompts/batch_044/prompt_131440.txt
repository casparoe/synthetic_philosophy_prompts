Please argue that Gödel's rotating universe model undermines the A-theoretic conception of an objective present and temporal flow, but not in the familiar way—by treating closed timelike curves as mere paradoxes to be avoided. Instead, defend the view that the Gödel metric demonstrates a deeper structural point: global temporal orientability, meaning the existence of a continuous everywhere-defined distinction between past and future directions, can coexist with causal curves that intersect their own past, thereby severing the link between temporal orientation and causal accessibility. From this, argue for a specific variant of B-theoretic eternalism in which temporal relations are fully real and four-dimensional, yet the arrow of time is not an intrinsic feature of the manifold but an emergent property of local thermodynamic asymmetries embedded in a globally time-symmetric geometry. Your argument should explain why this interpretation avoids Gödel's own Kantian inference that the possibility of temporal loops renders time unreal, and should address how treating world-lines in this model as closed one-dimensional submanifolds—rather than as histories traversing an independently flowing time—resolves the self-intersection problem without appealing to either presentism or modal realism about branching worlds.
