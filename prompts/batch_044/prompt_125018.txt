Argue that music does not transcend representation but instead isolates the pure form of time—the very framework of the principle of sufficient reason—so that its emotional force derives from exposing the listener to bare phenomenal succession rather than from revealing a metaphysical Will. Address Schopenhauer's distinction between music and the other arts, and explain why treating music as the purest art actually undermines his pessimistic metaphysics rather than supporting it.
