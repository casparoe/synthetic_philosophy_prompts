For each ticket n, the probability that it loses is 0.999, so rational belief requires us to believe it will lose. Applying logical closure, we then believe all 1,000 tickets lose, which contradicts our rational belief that one must win. In the preface paradox, an author rationally believes each specific claim in her book is true, but also rationally believes that at least one claim is false. Closure then forces her to believe both that every claim holds and that not every claim holds.

By allowing rational agents to maintain collectively inconsistent belief sets—relaxing closure only at the aggregate level—we can preserve her belief in each Ci alongside her general belief that some Ci fails.
