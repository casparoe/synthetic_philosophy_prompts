Construct a thought experiment that isolates origin essentialism about personal identity from psychological-continuity theories in a biologically realistic but conceptually sharp setting. Your scenario must satisfy the following specifications. (1) A being—call it Synth—comes into existence through laboratory synthetic embryogenesis using a digitized genome identical in sequence to yours, with no sperm, egg, or reproductive causal chain involving your biological parents. Synth possesses full psychological continuity with you: your memories, character, and first-person perspective are preserved intact. (2) The technology must stay within the bounds of present-day synthetic-biology capability (no cloning fantasies, no brain uploads, no exotic physics), so that evaluation does not depend on suspending disbelief about physics. (3) Demonstrate that a Kripke-style origin essentialist must deny that Synth is you, and identify precisely which feature of the causal history does the work—whether the absence of the specific gametes or the absence of any reproductive causal chain altogether. (4) Introduce the twist that the digitized genome was not copied from you but reconstructed statistically from population-genetic data about your parents, yielding by coincidence the exact same sequence, so that not even informational continuity with you remains. State whether this twist strengthens or weakens the essentialist denial, and whether a Parfit-style psychological-continuity theorist's verdict changes. (5) Pinpoint the structural feature that makes the two theories diverge, explain what happens if we restore gametic causation but substitute different parents who happen to produce an identical genome, and say how a defender of whichever theory comes out badly would attack the case. Do not invoke perfect predictors, demons, or exotic physics; do not request citations, bibliographies, or specific texts; and reason directly from the theoretical commitments.
