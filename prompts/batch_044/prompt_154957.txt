I'm a mediator for a small co-housing collective, and every week we get these squabbles about shared space - someone builds a shed that blocks light, plays drums near a sleeping baby, paints a shared wall a color that feels "hostile" to a neigbor, stuff like that. The board is tired of me falling back on generic rules like "be respectful" and wants me to actually use moral particularism - you know, the idea that moral reasons are totally context-dependent and you can't just apply one universal principle everywhere. I need a practical decision proceedure I can run in about 20 minutes with both people sitting right there, no lawyers, and then sign off on for the board. It should list the steps in order, what counts as a good enough answer at each one, where I stop and say no, and where I have to hand it over to a value call because it's not a fact question anymore. The key thing is it has to deal with cases where the exact same feature - like noise or smell - counts as a reason in one layout but not in another. Run it on three examples: (1) the drummer near the baby's window, (2) the wall color that "feels hostile", and (3) allowing a smelly compost bin that actually helps the garden - and make case 3 be one where the proceedure gives an answer I'd hate. Also tell me clearly what this can't do, like predict emotional blowback or cover disputes that involve money. Make it something I can literally read off a card during mediation.
