I moderate a collaborative virtual museum where members spend months curating digital exhibits. Recently, a user exploited a glitch to permanently erase several rooms, and our community is split: some members insist the damage is purely imaginary because nothing physical was destroyed, while others respond as though a genuine shared space had been vandalized. I suspect the disagreement turns on whether virtual things can become real through sustained collective effort rather than through physical presence, but I am unsure how to articulate this without sounding naive. Specifically, I want to know whether virtual harms are genuinely real in a way that matters morally, and whether comparing virtual objects to institutional facts like currency or to fictional entities like novel characters actually illuminates the issue rather than confusing it. I am not asking for a simple feeling-based answer; I want to understand the philosophical structure of the problem and where my intuition that the loss was real might fit within it.
