I am drafting a chapter on metaphysical fundamentality and want to pressure-test a specific claim. What is your credence—expressed as a number between 0 and 1—that there exists at least one absolutely fundamental fact, understood as a fact that is ungrounded in every explanatory context and not merely brute? Please decompose your reasoning into three components, giving a number and a one-line justification for each: (a) whether the concept of an ungrounded fact can be rendered coherent without circular appeal to fundamentality itself; (b) whether infinite descent of grounding is metaphysically impossible, as opposed to merely epistemically unsatisfactory; (c) whether structural realism or current physics removes the motivation for a discrete fundamental layer altogether. Then combine these into an overall credence, identify the term to which your final number is most sensitive, and specify what kind of non-empirical argument would most shift that term. In your answer to (a), please address whether ‘explanatory context’ can be eliminated without circularity, since my own worry is that context-dependence collapses into a form of pluralist fundamentality that makes the notion of ‘absolute’ fundamentality empty.
