I am especially interested in the distinction—often labeled Orwellian versus Stalinesque—between theories that allow the past experiential content itself to be retroactively altered and theories that allow only our present records or reports of that content to be falsified, leaving the original experience intact.

Write the desiderata: specify what any adequate account of the temporal architecture and revisability of perceptual experience must be able to deliver. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it—such as the standard color phi display (red dot, green dot, apparent motion with intermediate color change), the possibility of post-experiential confabulation without phenomenal alteration, the need to respect the subjective immediacy of the present, and the constraint that any revision mechanism must not collapse the distinction between experiencing an event and merely judging that one experienced it. Flag which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable (for example, consistency with the psychophysical data on reaction times and the absence of detectable intermediate neural representations) and which reflect camp-specific preferences (such as a commitment to continuous consciousness, to no hidden processing stages, or to the transparency of temporal properties).
