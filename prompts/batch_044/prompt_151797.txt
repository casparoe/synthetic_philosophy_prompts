After a long and rather exhausting argument with a friend about Leibniz's ethics, I keep returning to what seems like an unresolved tension in his system, but I suspect I'm smuggling in a confusion. Here is the puzzle. Leibniz insists that moral truths are eternal and necessary—part of the rational order that even God contemplates in choosing the best world—yet he also maintains that God permits moral evil only because it is woven into the overall harmony of that world, not because it is good in itself. My friend pushed this further: if the principle of sufficient reason demands that every event, including every sinful choice, has a reason that makes it part of the optimal whole, then in the 'nearest neighboring world' where I perform a minor deception but the global calculus of perfection is infinitesimally improved, that deception wouldn't merely be permitted—it would be rationally required, since the moral law itself would have to yield to the higher-order principle of maximal harmony. From this she concluded that for Leibniz, moral prohibitions can't be genuinely unconditional; they are world-indexed directives that invert whenever they conflict with the global optimization of perfection. I think she's conflating the metaphysical ordering of worlds with the internal structure of moral law, but I can't quite pinpoint where the slip occurs. So: does Leibniz's commitment to the principle of the best and his doctrine of moral necessity actually entail that there are counterfactual scenarios in which standard moral obligations are reversed for the sake of cosmic harmony, or is there a feature of his account—perhaps in the way he distinguishes juridical justice from distributive justice, or in his claim that charity of the wise is guided by eternal rational truths rather than by consequential calculation—that blocks this inference? And if the inference is blocked, why does Leibniz still speak of God 'permitting' evil in a way that sounds like strategic optimization rather than like the toleration of a genuinely wrong but free choice?
