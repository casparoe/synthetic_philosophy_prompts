hey so i've been mulling over this HCH idea - Humans Consulting HCH, where you set up a tree of copies of the same person, each breaking a big problem into smaller ones and passing answers back up. and the related notion of factored cognition, where reasoning is split into isolated chunks that dont talk to each other.

there's a real-world analog that already does something structurally similar: intelligence agencies and audit firms use compartmentalized analysis, where different teams work on fragments without seeing the whole, and a director integrates it. but HCH is weirder because the nodes are literally copies of one agent.

what philosophical commitments does that organizational form presuppose? does it assume practical reasoning is decomposable without loss - that my copy solving subproblem X is still exercising my rationality in a way that counts as one deliberation? or does it presuppose a functionalist view of mind, where personal identity is just pattern-continuity across nodes?

there's also an epistemic angle. if knowledge is built bottom-up from isolated judgments, does that presuppose foundationalism? could a coherentist or someone with a hinge-epistemology view accept the same tree but reject the root node's special authority?

and responsibility is puzzling. in compartmentalized institutions we blame the system or director, not the fragment-analysts. does HCH presuppose that moral responsibility distributes across the tree - that no single copy is fully accountable? and is that consistent with our usual practice of holding one person responsible for their whole reasoning?

can you reconstruct the implicit theory here, distinguish what's strictly required from what's just a convenient fit, and say whether the design is philosophically underdetermined? like, could a virtue ethicist or a Wittgensteinian about certainty still endorse HCH for totally different metaphysical reasons?
