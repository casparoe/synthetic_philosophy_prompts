You are a professional philosopher of science with expertise in metaphysics and philosophy of mind. Before writing, spend a moment brainstorming: identify the precise gap in current literature, the methodological risk of collapsing into familiar reductionist or anti-reductionist camps, and what a negative result would genuinely look like.

Write a short research proposal (approximately 1000 words) for a one-semester graduate workshop paper. The guiding question is this: Daniel Dennett’s framework of the physical, design, and intentional stances is usually read as an epistemic taxonomy—three predictive strategies we adopt toward a system. But there is an underexplored ontological reading: do the patterns detected in the design stance, such as functional organization, teleological structure, and robustness across implementation, constitute a genuinely intermediate kind of real pattern—neither reducible to physical-stance descriptions nor dependent on the mentalistic attributions of the intentional stance? Specifically, can we defend a functional realism about design-stance patterns without smuggling in either intentional psychology or full-blown biological essentialism?

Your proposal must contain the following sections, in this order, with approximate lengths:

1. The precise question (150 words): state it so that it admits a determinate answer, distinguish it clearly from the standard epistemic reading, and explain why the ontological version has been neglected.
2. Why existing answers leave it open (200 words): sketch the reductionist collapse (the design stance is merely the physical stance plus practical ignorance) and the intentionalist absorption (functions exist only for interpreters). Show that neither settles whether functional patterns carry autonomous ontological weight.
3. The proposed approach (250 words): explain the method—conceptual analysis of robustness and implementation independence, combined with examination of non-biological functional systems such as engineered artifacts, ecological niches, or artificial networks—and justify why this approach is preferable to pure metaphysics of science, empirical case studies alone, or history-of-philosophy exegesis.
4. What a result would look like and failure conditions (200 words): describe a successful outcome (a defensible criterion for design-stance reality) and give an honest assessment of what would make the project fail, for example if functional patterns always dissolve into either physical descriptions or intentional projections.
5. Scope and feasibility for one semester (150 words): provide a realistic timeline, explicit limits, and an argument for why the project is not too large.
6. Brief reflective note (50 words): one concise sentence on what makes this framing non-obvious.

Do not provide a bibliography, literature review by citation, or reading list. You may refer to Dennett’s idea of real patterns and to the three stances in general terms, but do not invent specific page numbers or use verbatim quotations from texts. Think carefully about whether your framing avoids treating function as either purely mental or purely physical.
