I've been asked to articulate my stance on anthropic reasoning and observer-selection effects for a seminar on cosmological epistemology. Here is my profile:

1. I accept Bostrom's strong self-sampling assumption (SSSA) for cosmological reasoning: given that I exist as an observer-moment, I should reason as if I were randomly selected from all observer-moments in my reference class.

2. I define that reference class not by physical type or spatiotemporal region, but by what I call informational closure—the set of observers for whom my current total mental state is epistemically indistinguishable.

3. I reject the self-indication assumption (SIA) because it succumbs to the presumptuous philosopher problem. If SIA were correct, the sheer possibility of a multiverse containing vastly more observers than ours should make my finding myself in a medium-scale universe wildly improbable, regardless of our cosmological measurements.

4. I think the standard presentation of the presumptuous philosopher misdiagnoses the error: SIA does not display philosophical arrogance but rather violates prior calibration by letting the bare fact of my existence swamp the non-anthropic evidence about cosmic scale.

5. I apply SSSA to the Doomsday argument, treating my birth rank as genuinely probabilistically informative about humanity's total future population, since those future observers fall within my informational-closure class.

6. I deny that SSSA applies across Everettian branches. Even though decoherent branches contain observers physically similar to me, they lack counterfactual accessibility: I could not, even in principle, have been in a branch where a different measurement outcome occurred, so they fall outside my reference class.

7. Despite my evidential rejection of SIA, I endorse a structurally analogous principle in population ethics: when evaluating policies that might create new conscious beings, I should weight the potential value of those lives positively in proportion to their number, without requiring that I sample from them.
