One might object by noting that aesthetic properties are response-dependent—constituted by appropriate human reactions—so that learning about others' responses through testimony should calibrate one's own judgments. But this objection fails, because response-dependence entails that there is no stable, mind-independent fact being reported; testimony can at best aggregate subjective preferences, making the transmission of aesthetic content conceptually impossible rather than merely unnecessary.

Finally, observers frequently revise their aesthetic judgments upon direct encounter with the work. This pattern of revision demonstrates that all testimonial beliefs in this domain are defeasible, and hence that testimony never produces knowledge—only tentative opinion that awaits correction by experience."
