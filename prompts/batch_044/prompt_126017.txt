ok so im writing up my views for our moral resposibility seminar and want a consistency check before i present. heres my package:

(1) im basically strawsonian: being responsible just means being a fitting target of reactive attitudes like resentment or gratitude, not having some metaphysical power to do otherwise.
(2) determinism is probably true, but that doesnt threaten responsibility on my view since reactive attitudes are interpersonal, not metaphysical.
(3) if someone has severe mental illness or is in a psychotic break, we should take the objective attitude and drop reactive attitudes entirely.
(4) but i think people stay fully responsible for wrongs done under extreme poverty or coercive social conditions, since those dont cancel the relevant kind of agency.
(5) reactive attitudes depend partly on shared social expectations, yet i think you can still appropriately resent someone even when the expectation you held was unfair or unrealistic.
(6) theres no deeper desert behind reactive practices — its all practice-internal — but we can still rationally criticize a culture for having wrong reactive norms, like blaming people for things outside their control.
(7) i think reactive attitudes can be properly directed at collectives or artificial systems in a distributed way, extending strawson beyond individual human agents.

does this hang together? i feel like there might be tensions im missing, especially around (3) and (4) or maybe (5) with the rest. please audit: which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like it, order the real ones by severity, and for each say the cheapest revision and what it costs. dont just flag everything — i want discrimination.
