As a retired experimental physicist now studying philosophy of mathematics, I ask you to restate the main philosophical arguments that extend Gödel's first incompleteness theorem beyond formal mathematics—claims that human cognition, physical law, or normative systems are thereby shown incomplete or non-formalizable—using only the conceptual vocabulary of my former field: effective theories, domains of applicability, decoupling of energy scales, emergent descriptions, and the conditions for consistently embedding a lower-level formalism within a higher-level framework. For each philosophical move, identify what plays the role of the formal system, the axioms, the undecidable sentence, and the meta-system; then deliver the residue report, specifying which elements translate cleanly, which require distortion (for instance, treating consistency as an empirically tunable parameter or self-reference as a cross-scale interaction), and which have no physical counterpart whatsoever. Conclude by judging whether the untranslatable residue reveals a genuine limitation in the physicist's framework or instead exposes an illegitimate projection of formal properties onto physical or mental phenomena. Please respond in American English and do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
