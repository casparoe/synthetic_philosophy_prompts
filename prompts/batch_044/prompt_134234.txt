Consider an institutional review board that permits behavioral researchers to use “staged disclosure”: participants are initially told they are completing a study on memory, but the task actually measures conformity to authority; the true purpose is revealed only in a mandatory debriefing, and participants may sign a retroactive consent form within twenty-four hours, with no effect on their course credit whether they sign or refuse. The board treats this as ethically valid consent, arguing that no explicit falsehood was told—only a framing omission—and that the post-hoc signature restores autonomy without coercion.
