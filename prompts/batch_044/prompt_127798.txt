I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have been turning over in my mind a question about whether I am essentially tied to the particular sperm and egg that produced me, or whether I could have come from different ones and still been me. I have tried to test my own intuitions against concrete scenarios, and while some judgments feel steady, others pull in opposite directions and I cannot find the hidden rule. Here is where I land: if my parents had used IVF with their own gametes and I had been carried by a surrogate, that still feels like me; if my father had provided a different sperm sample from the same genetic source, I lean that it could still be me, though I am less certain; if both the egg and sperm had come from entirely different people, yet I was raised by my actual parents, that does not feel like me at all. Yet there is tension with the case of an embryo created from my parents’ gametes and donated to another couple—if that child had been raised by strangers it feels like a different person, but if my parents had raised that very embryo, it seems as though it could have been me. I also think about a vanishing twin, where one embryo is absorbed in utero: I feel the absorbed twin was not me, but I cannot decide whether it might have been me had the absorption not occurred. Meanwhile, with mitochondrial donation involving a third person’s genetic material, I still feel that child is me, which makes me wonder why one kind of foreign genetic contribution is compatible with my identity and another is not. Is there a single principle—perhaps about genetic origin, or about the intentions and continuity of the parents, or about something like psychological continuity—that can organize all of these verdicts? Or are my intuitions genuinely in conflict, and if so which one should I be willing to overturn? I would especially like to know what the best principle you can offer predicts for a situation I have not listed: suppose my parents had used an artificial gamete grown from my father’s stem cells rather than his actual sperm—would that still be me, and what does that answer reveal about whether my identity depends on the specific biological cells or on something more abstract?
