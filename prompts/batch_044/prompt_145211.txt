We had a long and somewhat heated argument last night about whether a society can ever justifiably criminalize a practice that causes no direct harm to anyone outside it, solely because that practice weakens shared moral sensibilities or social cohesion. I want you to build me a precise thought experiment to specifications that isolates exactly where that disagreement lives.

Construct a realistic, one-paragraph scenario—no science fiction, no exotic physics, no demons or perfect predictors—in which a society is considering a criminal prohibition (not a mere regulation) of a voluntary, fully informed, and revocable service. The service should involve a technology or social arrangement that did not exist when the classic debates were formulated: specifically, a brief, non-invasive neurochemical protocol that consenting adults can undergo to selectively suppress their own emotional capacity for moral outrage, allowing them to work in artistic or professional contexts where internalized taboos are treated as obstacles. The practice must be entirely private, cause no physical, economic, or psychological harm to non-participants, and leave no lasting effects once discontinued.

After presenting the scenario, do the following, writing in American English and without including any bibliographies, reading lists, or citations:

First, state the verdict a Hart-style harm-principle position gives, with reasoning. Second, state the verdict a Devlin-style legal moralist position gives, with reasoning. Third, identify the precise structural feature of your case that forces these two verdicts apart—the feature that must be present for the divergence to occur. Fourth, explain what happens if that feature is removed: does the case collapse back into agreement, and if so, in which direction? Fifth, show how a defender of the position that comes out worse in your scenario would attack the case—specifically, whether they deny the harmlessness premise, deny that shared morality is genuinely undermined, or invoke some other reply, and why.

Keep the scenario grounded and make sure the verdicts are generated by the theories themselves rather than stipulated. Do not quote from any specific text; paraphrase positions in your own words.
