I've been thinking about this comparison people make between how different countries deals with dark pasts — like Germany's Vergangenheitsbewältigung, Japan's kinda ambiguous stance on comfort women and wartime aggression, Turkey's active denial of the Armenian genocide, and the American South's Lost Cause mythology. There's this framework some scholars use where they talk about "mnemonic citzenship" and whether a nation does "working through" vs "melancholic repetition" vs "active foreclosure." But I want to step back and imagine: if Sigmund Freud had lived to see the post-1945 world and these comparative debates, how would he map his own ideas — especially Nachträglichkeit (that retroactive, deferred-action thing) and the mourning/melancholia distinction — onto these four cases?

Specifically, I'm wondering if he'd see Germany's later Holocaust memory culture (the 1960s-80s turn, the Stolpersteine embedded in sidewalks, the state-funded memorials) as a genuinely successful collective "working-through," or if he'd be suspicious that it's actually a kind of institutionalized melancholia where the nation identifies with the victim-position to avoid confronting its own aggression. For Japan, would he interpret the 1990s emergence of comfort women testimony as a classic case of deferred action — trauma that only becomes traumatic retroactively — or would he say the state's refusal to integrate it into official curriculum is a textbook example of collective repression? And then there's Turkey, where the state doesn't just repress but actively criminalizes remembrance, which seems like something beyond standard Freudian denial — maybe closer to the disavowal he linked to fetishism? And the American South: the Lost Cause isn't exactly forgotten, it's constantly re-enacted through monuments and school textbooks that were updated well into the 20th century. Would Freud see that as repetition compulsion on a civilizational scale, or as a failed mourning where the lost object (the Confederacy/slavery) is never relinquished?

Do you think he'd find the comparison between these four cases illuminating, or would he warn that applying individual psychoanalytic categories to collective memory is a category error?
