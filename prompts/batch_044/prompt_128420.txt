As a doctoral student in the philosophy of logic, I am drafting an essay that treats Frege's 1924 diary entries not as biographical curiosities but as structurally relevant to his philosophy of language. My central claim is that Frege's distinction between Sinn and Bedeutung implicitly relies on a normative ideal of shared rational accessibility—an ideal that the diary undermines by revealing that Frege conceived of objective thought (Gedanke) as compatible with, and perhaps even dependent on, ethnically bounded cultural conditions. I develop this through three moves: first, diary remarks (paraphrased, not quoted) that associate democratic institutions with the corruption of scientific objectivity; second, Frege's logicist assertion that arithmetic truths are accessible to any rational being regardless of empirical psychology; and third, the tension that arises if the clause "any rational being" is tacitly restricted by the very cultural prejudices the diary expresses. I argue that this tension does not technically invalidate the derivations in Grundgesetze, but it does compromise the universalist framing of his anti-psychologism, particularly in his exchanges with Hilbert over axiomatic foundations. I also consider the alternative reading—that the diary records a transient political despair rather than a deep philosophical commitment—which would preserve the autonomy of his logical system. I would value comments on whether my inference from private prejudice to philosophical architecture overreaches, whether I mischaracterize the universalist ambitions of his logicism, and how best to navigate the evidentiary gap between a personal journal and a published system. Please lead with your bottom-line verdict in the first sentence, then give your reasoning with no preamble and no restating of the question.
