I am revising a graduate syllabus on the metaphysics of time and recently encountered a summary—circulated in lecture notes for an interdisciplinary seminar on temporal ontology and observational cosmology—that attempts to integrate the classical Rietdijk-Putnam reasoning with a more recent cosmological framing. The author, summarizing Rietdijk (1966) and Putnam (1967), writes in effect that special relativity establishes that simultaneity is frame-relative, so that for any two observers in relative motion—even two pedestrians passing on a street—there is no unique hyperplane of simultaneous events. Applying this to Penrose’s Andromeda scenario, the summary claims that one pedestrian’s present may include an alien fleet’s departure that, for the other pedestrian, remains an undetermined future decision. From this, the author concludes not merely that eternalism is correct, but that presentism is incoherent unless one rejects special relativity entirely, and further that because the Lorentz transformations hold locally regardless of cosmological model, the argument applies with identical force within the cosmic microwave background rest frame, undermining any attempt to rescue a globally privileged present by appealing to cosmological structure.

Which parts of this summary are accurate representations of the original argument, which are common simplifications or conflations, and which are substantive misrepresentations? In particular, is it fair to treat Penrose’s Andromeda paradox as an integral part of the Rietdijk-Putnam reasoning rather than as a later popularization? Does the conclusion that presentism must reject special relativity altogether follow, or does it depend on an unstated premise about the ontological significance of simultaneity hyperplanes? And does the extension of the argument to the cosmic rest frame strengthen the case for the block universe, or does it confuse local Lorentz invariance with global cosmological structure? Please separate what a careful defender of four-dimensionalism would accept, what they would qualify, and whether the summary’s final claim survives correction of its errors.
