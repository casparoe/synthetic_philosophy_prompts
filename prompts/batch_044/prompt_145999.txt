I co-run the ethics club at my high school, and I am planning a sixty-minute session for about fourteen students aged sixteen to eighteen. We will examine virtue ethics alongside the situationist critique, focusing on whether global character traits can survive evidence of sharp cross-situational variability. Could you design a structured in-class simulation—complete with rules, assigned roles, and a concealed payoff structure—that lets participants construct global trait attributions (for instance, labeling an agent as honest or compassionate) after observing behavior in one scenario, and then observe the same agent behave inconsistently in a second scenario that is structurally identical but differs only in subtle ambient emotional framing? The debrief should map this reversal onto the debate between global traits and local, situation-specific dispositions, and should address the virtue-ethical reply that practical wisdom activates virtues only once certain emotional or contextual thresholds are crossed. Please also be explicit about which aspect of that threshold-based reply the simulation cannot reproduce without direct explanation, and warn me about the predictable failure mode on the first run. The exercise should be self-contained, so that no student needs prior familiarity with the technical literature.
