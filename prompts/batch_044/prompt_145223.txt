my study partner extended Carroll's tortoise argument into something that sounds like it kills all deduction. here's how he lays it out:

(1) In the dialogue, Achilles applies modus ponens only after accepting it as an explicit premise.
(2) If that's required for modus ponens, it's required for every inference rule.
(3) Applying a rule R therefore always needs a meta-rule R' that licenses R.
(4) That meta-rule needs its own license, so we get an infinate regress.
(5) Every deductive step thus preseupposes an infinite stack of rules.
(6) No finite mind — human or machine — can actually hold or process an infinite stack.
(7) So no finite agent is ever rationally justified in drawing a deductive conclusion.
(8) But people clearly are justified sometimes, like when they check a proof or derive that 7 is prime.
(9) Therefore one of the steps 1-6 is wrong, but he can't see which.

he really wants to keep (1) and (6). he'd rather not say Achilles was just being irrational. is there a step he can reject without giving those up? and what does rejecting it cost — does it mean rules can be used implicitly, or that justification stops somewhere?
