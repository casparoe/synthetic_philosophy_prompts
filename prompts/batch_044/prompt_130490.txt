Here's an argument my study group cooked up and now I think it proves too much, but I can't name the slip. (1) Goodhart's law: once a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure—think of RLHF models optimizing for aproval ratings instead of truth, or schools teaching to standarized tests. (2) Every institutional design is just a bundle of measures turned into targets. (3) So every institutional design eventually gets gamed and collapses. (4) But "build anti-gaming safeguards" is itself an institutional design with its own measure—say, audit frequency or transparency scores. (5) So safeguards collapse too, just one level up. (6) There's no stable meta-level because you can always apply the law to the mechanism that applies it. (7) Therefore rational institutional design is impossible in principle. (8) Yet central banks and peer review exist and mostly work, so (7) is false. Which step do you reject, and what's the cost? I'd really like to hang on to (1) and (8) if I can.
