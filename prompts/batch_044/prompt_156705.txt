I am a novelist preparing remarks for a symposium on philosophy and narrative form, and I need a rigorous prep sheet for the question period rather than an abstract defense of my thesis. The audience will be divided into three groups I can already anticipate: literary theorists who regard fiction as essentially representational and who will hear my claim as an abdication of mimetic responsibility; analytic philosophers of language skeptical of pragmatism who will treat my argument as either self-undermining or merely therapeutic rhetoric; and scholars of Rorty’s neopragmatism who will defend the autonomy of the ironist’s redescription against my charge that it collapses in the practice of writing. My thesis is that Rorty’s antirepresentationalism, when applied seriously to long-form fiction, does not liberate the novelist but instead reveals hidden normative constraints: the “final vocabulary” available to a fiction writer is not freely redescribable but is disciplined by what I term “narrative entailment structures”—internal coherence conditions governing extended character arcs and temporal sequencing that exercise a regulative force functionally identical to representational truth-conditions, thereby dissolving Rorty’s distinction between finding and making within narrative art.

Please prepare me for the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased precisely in the vocabulary each group uses. First, address the literary theorists’ charge of mimetic loss and their worry that my position reduces the novel to a mere language game with no accountability to human experience. Second, address the analytic philosophers’ incoherence objection—specifically their claim that I have smuggled representational commitments back in through the very notion of “entailment,” and their demand that I either drop the normative language or admit I am a closet representationalist. Third, address the Rorty scholars’ defense of ironist autonomy: they will argue that redescription is never “free” in the naive sense I seem to require, that contingency is precisely the point, and that I misunderstand the ironist’s project by treating narrative coherence as a hidden metaphysics rather than as a locally adopted tool. For each objection, indicate its real force versus its rhetorical function, tell me what I should concede upfront in the opening minutes to avoid seeming defensive, and provide the brief reply I must deliver in under two minutes—along with an honest note on where that reply is weakest. I also need to know the one question I am hoping nobody asks, and I need you to identify the softest point in my own position—the place where “narrative entailment structures” are most vulnerable to being dismissed as either obscure or question-begging—so that I can prepare to defend it actively rather than hoping the audience overlooks it. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I am preparing for live debate, not writing a monograph, and I will supply any references myself.
