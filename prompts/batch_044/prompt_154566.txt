I am composing a novel in which the protagonist, after surviving a catastrophic event, begins to experience temporal moments as co-present rather than sequential, and I have turned to Kurt Vonnegut's Slaughterhouse-Five both as a formal model and as a philosophical interlocutor. I would like you to read the novel through the specific lens of the Rietdijk-Putnam argument for the block universe: that is, the inference from the relativity of simultaneity in special relativity to the conclusion that there is no globally privileged present, and therefore that past and future events possess the same ontological status as present ones. Where, precisely, does Billy Pilgrim's condition—his being unstuck in time and his exposure to the Tralfamadorians' view of all moments as equally present like a range of mountains—function as a genuine dramatization of eternalism rather than as a mere representation of trauma-induced dissociation? Conversely, where does the novel risk conflating an epistemic limitation, namely a damaged consciousness's inability to integrate temporal experience, with the metaphysical claim that temporal becoming is illusory? I am particularly interested in whether the narrative's detached refrain accompanying death operates as a phenomenological analog to the block universe's denial of privileged temporal moments, or whether it inadvertently reintroduces a presentist moral urgency that the physical argument cannot ground. Since I am leading a philosophy of physics reading group in connection with my research, please maintain fidelity in both directions: do not reduce the novel to an illustration of eternalism, and do not distort the Rietdijk-Putnam argument to accommodate the text. If possible, conclude by indicating what the novel contributes to the debate that formal argumentation alone does not—perhaps regarding what it feels like to inhabit a world without temporal flow. Finally, please distinguish clearly between what is well-established in the physics and philosophy of time and what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any disputed interpretation—especially the direct inference from physical simultaneity to ontological eternalism—as though it were the consensus position.
