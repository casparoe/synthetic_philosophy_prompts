Analyze the Iranian Revolution and its consolidation (1978–1983)—the overthrow of the Pahlavi monarchy, the rapid establishment of clerical rule, and the subsequent purges of leftist, liberal-nationalist, and secular allies—through the interpretive frameworks developed for the French Revolution: the class-struggle explanation, the ideology-driven or political-culture explanation, and the contingency or accident-driven explanation. For each framework, say how it accounts for the coalition that made the revolution, the speed with which it turned against its non-clerical partners, and the violence of the consolidation period; identify the feature of the Iranian case it best explains and the feature it most clearly misdescribes or leaves silent. Then apply the thesis that revolutions "devour their children"—the pattern in which revolutionary coalitions turn on and destroy the very groups that helped bring them to power—to the Iranian purges of the Tudeh Party, the Mojahedin-e Khalq, and figures such as Mehdi Bazargan. Does the pattern fit, and if so, does it confirm a structural tendency shared across revolutions or does the specifically religious and anti-imperial character of the Iranian consolidation break the analogy? Finally, say what this cross-case comparison reveals about the limits of using French Revolution historiography to interpret modern revolutions: is the framework portable, does the Iranian case expose a blind spot in one or more of the three explanations, and does the episode argue for contingency over structure, or vice versa?
