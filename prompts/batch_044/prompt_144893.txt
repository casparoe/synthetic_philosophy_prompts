Write an essay on the following controversy: Do grand historical narratives—exemplified by Yuval Noah Harari’s Sapiens, Jared Diamond’s Guns, Germs, and Steel, and David Graeber and David Wengrow’s The Dawn of Everything—inevitably distort the past by substituting retrospective coherence for the contingency of lived experience? Focus your analysis on the methodological clash between Diamond’s geographic and environmental determinism and Graeber and Wengrow’s emphasis on political choice, seasonal social flexibility, and the deliberate construction of hierarchy. Weigh the claim that big history must simplify to remain explanatory against the objection that such simplification commits a narrative fallacy—treating the survival of particular institutional forms as proof of adaptive superiority rather than as evidence of path-dependent lock-in. Reach a definite conclusion: argue either that one framework offers a genuinely more accurate account of long-term social development, or that both suffer from a shared, correctable methodological flaw. Justify your verdict with reasoning that addresses the strongest point on the opposing side.
