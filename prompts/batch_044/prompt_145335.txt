I have been thinking on my own about those neuroscience studies where a machine watches the brain and predicts a finger movement before the person reports deciding to move. From what I understand, the researcher Libet found some kind of electrical readiness that begins several hundred milliseconds before conscious awareness, which led people to say free will is an illusion. Yet I have also heard that Libet himself argued for a kind of last-moment conscious check—an ability to stop the action even after the brain has started preparing it. I find both ideas unsettling, and I am not convinced that either one really explains what it feels like to make a choice.
