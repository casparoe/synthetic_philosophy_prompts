I've been trying to wrap my head around the psi-ontic vs psi-epistemic debate in quantum foundations, mostly from skimming old papers and arguing with my roomate who's in physics. There's this one arguement pattern I keep hitting that I can't find a label for, and I'm wondering if it's just a well-known fallacy wearing a new hat or if it actually needs its own box.

Here's the pattern in concrete form. Someone says the wavefunction can't be a real physical thing because it's defined over a 3N-dimensional configuration space, not over actual 3-space. Fair enough. But then when you point out that other physical objects live in weird spaces too — like the electromagnetic field is a section of a fiber bundle over spacetime — they don't say "oh right, dimensionality isn't the issue." Instead they double down: the wavefunction is uniquely suspicious because its dimensions scale with particle number, and that scaling makes it "not a field in physical space." But if you ask what the principled cutoff is — at what point does a mathematical space become too weird to host an ontology — they just point back at quantum mechanics and say "well, we know it works, so we don't need to reify that particular object." Meanwhile they often bring up Bohmian mechanics as the example of what you'd have to do if you did want an ontic 3-space picture, but then they dismiss Bohm for being nonlocal or for having a quantum potential that's ugly. So they use the existence of Bohm to prove that 3-space ontology requires ugly machinery, which reinforces that we should reject the wavefunction's reality, but they never specifiy what the actual ontology is for their epistemic view — they just say it's "something underlying" that we don't need to name. The dimensionality objection ends up protected from counterexample by being tied exclusively to the wavefunction, and the absence of an alternative ontology is treated as a feature rather than a gap.

Is there an established term for this specific circular bundle — where a structural feature (config-space dimensionality) is treated as fatal only for the target entity, counterexamples are deflected by ad hoc uniqueness claims, and the absence of a specified alternative ontology is used to defend the negative claim? I know it's adjacent to special pleading and maybe to underdetermination turned inside out, but those don't quite capture the self-sealing part where the argument feeds on its own lack of a positive replacement. If there's no name, what should we call it — and more importantly, what exactly distinguishes it from ordinary special pleading or from a plain absence-of-evidence move?
