I have been sketching out a proposal for a small interdisciplinary workshop—perhaps a twelve-week reading group that produces a single working paper—and I would like you to help me turn my half-formed idea into a polished pitch. The background is that I have spent too many evenings arguing on a rationalist forum about mind uploading, and I keep noticing that participants treat digital minds as if they were biological organisms that merely happen to run on silicon. My proposed angle is that this is a category mistake: the genuinely open question about moral status is not primarily whether a digital system can suffer, but whether the structural properties of digital existence—perfect copyability, non-rivalrous instantiation, suspension without decay, and the absence of a unique spatiotemporal trajectory—render the standard individualist framework of harm and rights incoherent without revision.

More precisely, I want to split the moral-status debate into two layers that existing literature tends to conflate. The first is the experiential layer, which asks whether the entity has the functional or phenomenal properties that ground moral patienthood; functionalists and biological naturalists have said plenty about this. The second is what I am calling the topological layer, which asks whether the entity possesses the kind of bounded, non-branching, diachronically continuous identity that makes harming it a conceptually stable notion at all. If a mind can be paused, duplicated with perfect fidelity, and resumed in parallel, in what sense do we wrong it by deleting one copy while another runs? Is the harm distributed across instances, nullified by continuity elsewhere, or transformed into something closer to a violation of property rights over a template? Existing positions leave this second layer almost entirely untouched, because both assume that a moral patient must be a singular, continuous being.

Please also say what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing altogether. I am not asking for a bibliography or citation list; I will add references myself, but do name the relevant positions and arguments where they help frame the gap.
