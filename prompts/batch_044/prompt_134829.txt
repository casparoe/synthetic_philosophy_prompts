Write a dialogue between a Russellian about definate descriptions and someone who thinks empty terms should still carry semantic weight (call them the free-logic side, but don't make it about the technical system). They're stuck in an aiport lounge arguing over whether Russell's uniqueness clause can survive ties — like if two buildings are exactly the same height, does "the tallest building" fail to denote, and does that make the sentence false rather than meaningless? Both should be sharp, neither a strawman. Have them actually respond: the Russellian should defend that failure of uniqueness just means falsehood, not reference failure, and that adding a "salience" condition saves the analysis only by smuggling in pragmatics; the other side should push back that without letting empty terms have weight, you can't explain why "the tallest building is in Dubai" feels like it says something even when there's a tie. Make it feel like they're killing time before a delayed flight, not like a textbook.
