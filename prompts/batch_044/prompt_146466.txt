I was covering a small panel on panpsychism for my science-ideas newsletter and overheard teh argument in teh hallway afterwards. Mara was saying the combination problem is definately a category mistake: if you take Russellian monism seriously, consciousness isn't assembled like parts into a machine, it's just an intrinsic feature of matter that expresses itself at different scales — like charge, which you don't sum particle-by-particle to get a field. Leo pushed back hard on subject-summing. Even if experience is intrinsic everywhere, he said, you've still got countless micro-subjects and somehow only one me experiencing this conversation. Mara replied that Leo is treating subjects like discrete little people, but on her view subjectivity is field-like: you don't ask how points combine into a field, you just describe the field's structure. Leo then asked why splitting my brain with a corpus callosum cut doesn't produce two half-subjects with half my experience; instead I get either one unified stream or a split one, never a scaled-down copy. Mara said that's not special to panpsychism — any physicalist story about brain damage has teh same asymmetry, damage disrupts patterns, it doesn't halve physics. Leo said that proves her point: she's dissolving the problem by denying subjects need combining, but then she owes us an explanation for why I have exactly one point of view right now instead of a cosmic blur. They went back and forth like that and neither budged. Who's right about what? Is Mara correct that the combination problem sneaks in a wrong ontology? Is Leo right that dissolving it avoids explaining phenomenal unity? And what's the blind spot they're both missing — maybe about transparency, or about the difference between structural integration and phenomenal binding?
