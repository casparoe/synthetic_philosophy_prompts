I'm retired from marine ecology and lately I've been digging into Quine's indetermincy of translation — the whole gavagai puzzle with the native speaker and the rabbit. Anyway, I sit on the advisory board for the Meridian Ethnobotany Archive, and we have to vote by March 15 on whether to adopt the new digitization protocol our curator proposed. She wants us to stop mapping indigenous plant terms onto Linnaean species in our database, citing Quine: she says there's never a fact of the matter whether a word like 'koro' refers to the tree, its flowering stage, or the bark used for dye — all of it is underdetermined by observable behavior, so assigning a Latin binomial is just smuggling in an analytical hypothesis that isn't in the native ontology. Her plan is to record only phonetics, local use-context, and voucher specimens without taxonomic linkage, leaving the ontology permanently open.

The board is split. The biologists say this destroys cross-study comparability and makes the archive useless for conservation science; the anthropology members say forcing Linnaean frames is epistemic violence and Quine proves we can't do it honestly anyway. I need a short memo — recomend approve, reject, or approve with conditions and a concrete timeline — that I can circulate before our March meeting. It should take the Quinean argument seriously rather than just calling it philosophy overreach, but also engage the strongest counter-argument (probably that radical translation is a limit case about total linguistic isolation, not a practical reason to abandon collaborative taxonomy, and that refusing translation doesn't eliminate the analytical hypothesis — it just hides it in the choice of what contexts to record). Please don't ask me for bibliographies or citations; I don't have web access and neither do half the board members. Just lay out the reasoning and the bottom line, with an honest nod to the side that loses. And keep it casual — I might have a typo or two in my own draft, so don't over-polish the voice. Write it as my opinion memo.
