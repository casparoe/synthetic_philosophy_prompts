For my senior philosophy seminar, I need help adjudicating a disagreement I watched between two classmates—Jordan and Maya—about whether a legal system can genuinely contain a self-amending rule without falling into paradox. They were analyzing a hypothetical constitutional clause they named the Recursive Entrenchment Clause: Article 7 states that Article 7 alone defines the exclusive procedure for amending Article 7, and it includes a provision that any alteration to that procedure requires unanimous consent ratified through the very procedure being altered. They made four moves in sequence, and I need a point-by-point verdict rather than a general lecture.

(1) Jordan argued that the paradox is avoided by separating the authority layer—say, popular sovereignty as an external social fact—from the procedural layer inside the text. The authority is not part of the amendable rules; it merely delegates revision power to the procedure layer. So this is delegation, not logical self-reference, and no contradiction arises.

(2) Maya replied that in this specific clause the authority layer is embedded inside the text. Because Article 7 regulates its own alteration—including a double-lock that requires unanimous consent via the same procedure being changed—there is genuine object-language self-reference, not mere delegation from an outside source.

(3) Jordan countered with a diachronic resolution: at time t, Procedure P is in force and valid. At t+1, the people invoke P to enact P*. Because P and P* are not simultaneous, the system updates sequentially, like a running program rewriting its own source code. The self-reference is diachronic rather than synchronic, so the liar-paradox structure never actually activates in a single moment.

(4) Maya responded that diachronic rewriting only pushes the circularity backward. At the system’s origin—t0—either an external constituent power validates the initial P, which means legal self-amendment is not truly self-grounding, or there is an infinite regress of prior meta-procedures. Moving the problem into the past does not eliminate it; it either reveals a hidden non-legal foundation or an unending chain.

Please adjudicate move by move: for each of (1) through (4), rate it from clearly mistaken to clearly correct and briefly justify the rating. Then explain who is right about which specific point, identify what both parties are missing—such as whether a hierarchical meta-rule or a distinction between validity and efficacy could break the stalemate—and say who is closer to being correct overall. Please conclude with a line beginning exactly Verdict: followed by a single phrase indicating whether Jordan, Maya, split, or neither is closer to being right.
