I am designing a single fifty-minute session for first-year law students on genocide as a legal and moral category, centered on Raphael Lemkin's formulation and the politics of naming atrocities. Rather than a lecture script, I need a teaching plan that specifies: the precise order in which the conceptual pieces must be introduced and the dependency relations among them; the one carrying example or comparative case that should anchor the discussion and why an undifferentiated "mass killing" example would fail; the predictable misconceptions—particularly the collapse of genocide into large-scale homicide and the neglect of the coordinated plan to destroy a group as such—and the exact point in the sequence where each should be interrupted and corrected; a single closing question that reveals whether students understand the performative stakes of applying the legal label rather than merely reciting its elements; and an explicit statement of what portion of Lemkin's original framework (specifically the cultural-destruction dimension that was excluded from the international legal definition) should be deliberately omitted at this level and why.
