Quick question - is there a name for this pattern, or am I inventing it? I've been talking to people who lost a spouse after 40+ years together, and a few describe something weird that's not just grief. They say that for certain kinds of decisions - like whether to move, or how to read some social slight - they literally can't complete the thought process alone. Not that they miss having someone to talk it over with, but that the reasoning chain used to have a step that happened in the other person's head, and now the chain just stops. One guy said he used to know what he thought about politics only after his wife reacted to his half-formed opinion; now he has half-formed opinions that never resolve. I know Hofstadter writes about the self being distributed across other brains, but that feels more metaphysical - like your soul lives on in others. This is more like a live cognitive dependency: part of there 'I' that was doing the thinking was physicaly in another brain, and now it's gone, leaving a funcitonal gap. Does this have an established concept in philosophy of mind or psychology? If not, what's the right way to distinguish it from extended mind, from codependency, and from normal berevement? And what should we call the gap itself?
