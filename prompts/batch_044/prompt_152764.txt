I'm facilitating a philosophy discussion group for lawyers and doctors who have no formal training in metaphysics, and next week I have to lead a single ninety-minute session on origin essentialism — the claim that a person necessarily originates from the particular sperm and egg they actually came from, and could not have arisen from a different pairing. I don't want you to explain the view to me; I want a teaching plan. First, lay out the dependency order: what distinction — perhaps between an intrinsic property and an origin property, or between "this very zygote" and "a zygote with an identical genome" — must be secured before the essentialist thesis can even be formulated, and what defensive step should follow once it is stated? Second, for the carrying example, I am weighing two scenarios: siblings conceived naturally from the same parents but different gametes, versus a single embryo split in vitro with one twin implanted in a different country years later. Tell me which one actually carries the modal intuition without importing confusions about genetics, environment, or personal identity, and specify exactly what features the example must have and what it must lack. I also want to press a niche distinction I'm concerned will either illuminate or derail the session — the difference between essentialism about the gametes themselves and essentialism about the specific causal pathway (the particular laboratory conditions, the uterine environment, the temporal sequence). Where in the order should this appear, if at all?

Third, map the misconceptions I should expect: I anticipate they will conflate origin essentialism with genetic determinism, with psychological-continuity accounts of personal identity, and with the idea that one's parents or siblings are essential in the same way. For each, identify the precise point in the sequence where it will surface and the minimal intervention to head it off without derailing the flow. Fourth, give me one check question for the end that reveals whether they have grasped the modal claim itself rather than merely memorized a slogan. Finally, tell me explicitly what to leave out at this level — for instance, formal modal logic, debates about artifact essentialism, or the essentialism of biological kinds — and confirm that I should state this omission openly to the group.
