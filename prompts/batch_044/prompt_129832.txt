Someone brings up Bostrom-style simulation reasoning and the usual reply is that it should dampen fanaticism about the far future, since a lot of those future people might be simulated and therefore less real or less morally weighty. But then people immediately pivot to saying well actually the base reality is what matters so we should care even more about avoiding being in a simulation, and then they start treating the far future inside a potential simulation as having some kind of second-order value because the simulator cares about it, but they never quite cash out how that interacts with the original dampening claim. Its like there's a specific conceptual knot where the simulation argument is used both to deflate and to inflate longtermist urgency depending on which layer of reality you're talking about, and people slide between them without noticing. Is there an established term for this exact oscillation?
