He mentioned in passing that American federalism works because states are "laboratories of democracy," but he couldn't say whether that was an empirical prediction, a normative ideal, or just a metaphor for trial-and-error. He also seemed to treat subsidiarity as a scalar principle — that authority slides smoothly downward from supranational to national to regional to local — which made me wonder whether he was ignoring asymmetric federalism, where different policy domains have different optimal tiers regardless of geography.

Can you help me map out the precise conceptual terrain? For each, specify what kind of "can" is operative — technical capacity? absence of cross-border spillovers? democratic authorization? — and whether the claim is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false. Then tell me which of these readings my friend was actually invoking in each of his two examples (climate vs. data privacy), whether the same reading can consistently support both positions, and whether any version of the principle survives once we drop the scalar assumption and allow for asymmetric assignment across domains.
