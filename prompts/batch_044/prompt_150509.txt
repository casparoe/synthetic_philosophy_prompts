In standard physics pedagogy, Maxwell's demon is resolved not by denying the demon's ability to observe and sort molecules, but by insisting that its memory must eventually be erased, with each erased bit carrying an unavoidable entropic cost—Landauer's principle—thereby preserving the second law. At the same time, computer-science and information-theory curricula in the same institutions treat bits as substrate-independent abstractions whose logical manipulation carries no intrinsic thermodynamic price. What ontology of information does this institutional split presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view—distinguishing what the practice strictly requires from what merely accommodates it well—say whether it is coherent to treat algorithms as purely mathematical objects while treating computation in the demon's brain as necessarily physical, and tell me what would have to change in the standard presentation if one adopted a consistently non-physical conception of information. In particular, does the resolution depend on assuming that all computation involves irreversible erasure, and how does that assumption relate to the theoretical possibility of fully reversible computing architectures?
