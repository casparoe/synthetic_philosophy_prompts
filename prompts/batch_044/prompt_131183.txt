I'm taking this course on phil of mind and we got into Libet's wrist-flick experiments, and I built this argument that now feels like it's eating it's own tail. (1) Libet showed that the readiness potential in the supplementary motor area starts about 350ms before people report consciously deciding to move. (2) So the brain starts the action before conscious awareness shows up. (3) Libet tried to save free will with the conscious veto - you can't initiate, but you can stop the movement at the last second. (4) But deciding to veto is itself a mental act, and if Libet's logic holds, that veto-decision should also have its own readiness potential that preceeds awareness of vetoing. (5) Someone in my study group said maybe there's a higher-order monitor - a kind of meta-consciousness - that approves or blocks the veto consciously. (6) But that approval is just another event, so by the same reasoning it too gets preceeded by an unconscious neural signature. (7) So we get this reccursive cascade: every level of conscious control has a pre-conscious RP triggering it. (8) That means conscious will is never actually in the causal chain, not even at the meta-level. (9) Here's where it gets weird: if this reasoning is right, then my belief in it is also just the result of prior brain states, not rational evaluation. (10) And if my acceptance of the argument was causally determined rather than rationally arrived at, I don't actually have epistemic grounds to trust it - the conclusion occured to me because of neurons, not because it's true. (11) So the argument seems to prove that free will is an illusion and simultaneously destroys it's own justification. (12) Therefore either Libet-style reasoning is wrong somewhere, or we're stuck in a self-defeating loop where determinism undermines the belief in determinism.

I really want to keep the veto idea in step 3 and the cascade in steps 6-8 because they feel like the heart of the problem, but I also don't want to end up saying consciousness is totally epiphenomenal. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost? Is there a clean way to stop the regress without smuggling in a Cartesian ego?
