I'm working on a novel where one character is this old Jain monk who refuses to say anything is just one thing. I need a poem he might actually write — 14 lines, split into seven couplets — that uses the syādvāda structure: each couplet takes the same river stone and asserts one of the seven modes (it is smooth, it is not smooth, both, neither, inexpressible, and so on). Rhyme them so the stone keeps returning but the contradictions dont collapse. No final couplet should resolve everything into a neat lesson about relativity; let the form itself carry that weight. Keep the language plain, not academic, and dont cite any sources or drop extra Sanskrit — I want it to read like a real person wrote it, not a footnote.
