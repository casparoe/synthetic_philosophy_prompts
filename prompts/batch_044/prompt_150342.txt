Epigenetic methylation consists of chemical modifications to DNA that are not part of the nucleotide sequence itself, yet they regulate gene expression and are heritable across cell divisions. These marks can be said to carry information about past environmental conditions, but they possess no original semantic content; they are purely derived regulatory signals. Now consider a case in which such a pattern is erroneously interpreted by the cellular transcription machinery, triggering uncontrolled proliferation — a cancer.
