Construct a thought experiment that transposes the rational-irrationality structure of Parfit's hitchhiker into a realistic institutional setting—specifically, a multi-site clinical research consortium—while holding fixed that there is no omniscient predictor, no legal enforcement mechanism, and no life-or-death stakes. Your scenario should meet the following specifications: (1) A principal investigator must choose whether to adopt an internal governance rule that binds her future self to share preliminary negative results with partner sites, even though once the data are in her possession she will have a clear, moderate financial and reputational incentive to withhold them; (2) the partner consortium predicts her behavior not by reading her mind but by auditing her observable institutional design choices—e.g., whether she has embedded an irreversible data-release protocol in her lab's operating charter—using a predictor that is accurate but imperfect (roughly eighty percent reliable, with known false-positive and false-negative rates); (3) the investigator can, at a modest present cost, restructure her own decision architecture—for example, by delegating release authority to an independent biostatistician with a contractual duty to publish—in a way that is observable but not externally enforced by any court or regulator; (4) if she is predicted to withhold, she is excluded from the consortium and loses access to pooled patient cohorts worth a defined but non-catastrophic grant value; if she is included but then withholds, she retains the data and the grant but suffers no legal penalty. Present the scenario in one paragraph. Then walk through the verdicts: what a purely causal, ex-post rational agent should do at the commitment stage versus the execution stage; what a structural or evidential account of rational commitment recommends; and why the gap between them persists here rather than collapsing into a standard Newcomb-style problem. Identify precisely which feature—the noisy observability of self-binding architecture—does the work, explain what happens to the case if that predictor becomes perfect or if legal enforcement is added back in, and state how a defender of pure causal rationality would attack the premise that restructuring one's own decision architecture is genuinely available as a rational option rather than as a disguised causal intervention.
