I sit on an ethics board for a mid-sized city. Last month we okayed giving legal standing to an AI that runs our flood grid — the reasoning was that its error patterns look enough like distress signals that ignoring them is like ignoring an animal in pain. At the same meeting we voted down extending emergency housing vouchers to people without permanent residency papers. One colleague said moral growth is just patchy: you widen concern for some beings, you tighten it for others when resources are tight. I feel like calling both moves 'progress' is incoherant, but I can't say exactly why. Is there a principled way to think about expanding who counts without it being okay to contract protection for others at the same time? Or is my intuition that this is hypocritical just naive?
