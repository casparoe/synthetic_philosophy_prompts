I am a doctoral student in philosophy of action preparing a module for an advanced graduate seminar on rational agency. I need a structured, 90-minute in-class activity for approximately twelve students that forces the group to experience the precise logical pressure of Buridan's ass—not as generic indecision, but as a situation in which an agent faces two options strictly symmetrical across every recognized dimension of value, holds no hidden preference, and must still issue a single unified choice without that choice being rationally undermined by its own arbitrariness. The design should avoid any opening lecture; the rules and payoff structure must make participants discover the symmetry themselves, attempt resolution through standard mechanisms—randomization, temporal priority, or meta-preference—and then determine whether those mechanisms genuinely preserve agency or merely relocate the arbitrariness one level up. Please provide explicit rules, assigned roles (e.g., deliberator, symmetry-checker, record-keeper), a point-based payoff scheme requiring no real money, and a minute-by-minute timeline. The debrief should map each stage onto the conceptual anatomy of the problem: symmetry of first-order reasons, absence of preference, introduction of a selection mechanism, and the question of whether that mechanism belongs internally to the agent's rational architecture. To keep the exercise from being a generic repetition of the textbook case, I want a nested symmetry: participants must first choose between two collective actions that are mirror-images in moral and practical value, and then, having broken that tie by an agreed rule, discover that the rule applies symmetrically to two equally valid interpretations, generating a second-order Buridan case that tests whether diachronic consistency can serve as a non-arbitrary tie-breaker. Please also include an honest note on which feature of Buridan's original formulation this activity cannot reproduce and must therefore be supplied in discussion, and identify the failure mode most likely in the first run.
