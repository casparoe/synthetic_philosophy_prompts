As a journalist covering the philosophy of ethics and social policy, I am interested in arguments that treat Confucian role ethics as a structural challenge to liberal moral theory rather than a cultural supplement. Please construct a rigorous argument for the position that Confucian role ethics requires replacing — not merely expanding — the liberal model of autonomous, rights-bearing moral agents with a framework of role-constituted reciprocal vulnerabilities. Your argument should show how this framework resolves the tension in contemporary care ethics between human dependency and personal dignity without collapsing into relational determinism. In particular, develop the claim that, within this view, the self is constituted through its roles rather than prior to them, and explain why this relocation of moral agency within intergenerational attunement does not eliminate individual responsibility but transforms its conditions. Address the objection that making moral failure structurally co-constituted effectively absolves the individual agent. The piece should be accessible to an intellectually curious general audience, formally argued, and should not rely on specialist bibliographies or citations; I want the reasoning to stand on its own.
