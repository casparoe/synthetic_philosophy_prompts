I've come around to the view that so-called skyhooks in evolutionary and cognitive science—things like downward causation or group-level selection—are really just cranes we haven't traced far enough back. In other words, greedy reductionism gets unfairly maligned: if you can show exactly how a high-level pattern is assembled from lower-level interactions without smuggling in unexplained design, you've done the explaining, period. There's no extra emergent layer that does real work. What would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked—fatal first, then merely awkward. Include things like formal results showing macro-states genuinely underdetermined by micro-dynamics, or cases where reductionist research programms stalled not from lazyness but because the bottom-level story just couldn't recover the target phenomenon (maybe the origin of the genetic code?). Also, be straight about whether defenders can always retreat to we just haven't traced the crane far enough yet—if so, is the view unfalsafiable in a bad way, or is that okay for a metaphysical stance?
