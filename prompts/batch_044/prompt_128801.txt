I have been considering Newton's bucket argument and what I take to be its resolution within modern gravitational theory. Newton argued that water in a rotating pail climbs the sides even when the pail is stationary relative to all surrounding bodies, which he interpreted as evidence for absolute motion through space itself. Many contemporary relationists counter that general relativity's background independence, combined with Machian frame-dragging effects inside rotating massive shells, demonstrates that local inertia is fixed by the global distribution of matter rather than by an independent spatial substrate. Consider, then, a hollow spherical shell rotating in an otherwise empty universe, with Newton's bucket suspended at rest at its center. If the water remains flat when the bucket spins inside a stationary shell, but climbs the walls when the shell itself rotates around the motionless bucket, does this not provide an empirical demonstration that rotation is purely relational? Given that this Machian prediction is now embedded in the formalism of gravitational physics, why do philosophers of physics still present substantivalism and relationism as competing live options, rather than treating Newton's intuition as having been experimentally superseded? Is there a conceptual distinction I am missing that allows an absolute spatial structure to remain consistent with these gravitational phenomena?
