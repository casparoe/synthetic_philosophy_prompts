I serve on a committee deciding whether to fund a repository designed to preserve records across millennia. Having recently encountered the doomsday argument, I reasoned that my relatively low birth rank among all humans implies our species likely ends within centuries, making long-term preservation irrational. But the argument's force seems to depend on how I draw the reference class: counting only people who know the argument, or only those on planning boards, yields wildly different probabilities. At the same time, the self-indication assumption appears to favor larger future populations and longer survival. How should I adjudicate between these competing anthropic frameworks to reach a rational funding decision? Is the reference-class ambiguity a fatal defect, or can it be resolved without merely assuming the conclusion I want?
