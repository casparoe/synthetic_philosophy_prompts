I run a phil of mind workshop, about 9 people (grad students plus a couple undergrads who shouldn't be there but are), 75 min, we talked about Dennetts multiple drafts idea vs the Cartesian theater last week but I can tell half the room still secretly imagines theres a single inner screen being watched by some little observer. I want an in-class activity, not a lecture, that makes them feel why that theater can't exist.

Design a paired simulation: split into 'subjects' and 'editors'. Subjects do a rapid visual task -- like spotting color changes in a flashed sequence -- and immediately write private 'experience reports'. Editors get those reports plus some planted contradictions or missing entries and must construct one 'official record' of what was seen. Then switch roles. The rules should be arranged so subjects defend reports that contradict the official record, and editors realize they are manufacturing coherence rather than discovering it.

Specify materials (index cards, timer, etc.), timeline across the 75 min, roles, and a small-stakes payoff scheme (maybe public 'accuracy' points that actually depend on editorial choices, not on true experience). The debrief should map each phase onto the theater vs drafts distinction, but don't let them hear the phrase 'Cartesian theater' until after they've already argued about whose report was 'really' right. Predict what will go wrong the first run -- probably people insisting on first-person authority even after seeing contradictions. And be honest: which feature of Dennetts view -- maybe the denial that there is any intrinsic phenomenal property beyond what's available for report -- this activity can't reproduce and I just have to state plainly.
