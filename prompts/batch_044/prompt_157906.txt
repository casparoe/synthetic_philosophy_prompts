Write a research proposal—roughly 900 to 1200 words, appropriate for a philosophy-of-science graduate workshop or a small interdisciplinary grant—addressing the following conceptual problem. In expert institutions (peer-review systems, judicial panels, charitable governance boards), participants routinely endure procedures that are visibly slow, opaque, or administratively burdensome. One interpretation, derived from handicap-principle reasoning, treats these burdens as credible signals: the institution willingly bears a real cost, thereby distinguishing its commitments from cheap talk. The competing interpretation holds that evolutionary or competitive selection should have eliminated genuinely wasteful practices; therefore any burden that survives must covertly serve a functional role—error-correction, filtering, or coordination—making the handicap account redundant. Your task is to formulate a precise, non-reductive version of this dispute: specifically, ask whether there can exist a stable institutional regime in which a practice is genuinely costly (wasteful from the standpoint of individual rational optimization) yet persists not because of hidden functionality but because the cost itself is the mechanism that maintains collective credibility. Explain why standard evolutionary-game-theoretic models of costly signaling tend to collapse this intermediate possibility into either pure waste (unstable) or pure function (not a handicap). Propose an analytical strategy—such as a two-level selection model that separates individual-level inefficiency from institution-level equilibrium stability, or a structured comparative analysis of non-profit governance rituals—that could discriminate between the handicap interpretation and the hidden-function interpretation. State exactly what empirical or conceptual result would count as supporting the intermediate regime, what result would force a retreat to one of the two standard positions, and what scope limitations make the full argument a multi-semester project rather than a workshop paper. Do not provide a bibliography or cite specific works by author and date; refer to theoretical positions in general terms and indicate that formal citations will be supplied separately. Ensure that the framing does not depend on access to proprietary datasets or on verification of obscure historical claims.
