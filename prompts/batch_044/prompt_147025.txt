I'm trying to figure out if my intuition about commons and public goods is consistant. Case one: a small fishing co-op with clear membership sets catch limits, mostly sticks to them, stock stays healthy — I think this works fine, no tragedy. Case two: same waters opened to anyone with a boat — rapid collapse, classic tragedy, blame feels diffuse but real. Case three: a village pasture where everyone knows each other, they overgraze a little but social shame stops total ruin — I call this a managed commons, not a true tragedy, even though access is technically open. Case four: global carbon budget, every country can emit, harm is spread across generations — I still call this tragedy, even though no single emitter feels the damage. Case five: open-source software anyone can use, big firms free-ride constantly, but the code improves — I say this isn't tragedy at all, it's just not that kind of good. Case six: a neighborhood well with plenty of water, but testing for contamination is expensive, so everyone skips it to save money, then one day it's poisoned. Nobody overused the water; they under-maintained monitoring. I want to call this tragedy too, but I'm not sure it fits. So is my real principle about physical depletion, about incentive structures, or about something else? Can one account save all six, especially the well versus carbon comparison? And what should it say about a hypothetical 'attention commons' where news sites chase clicks until public trust collapses — same failure, or different? If I need to drop one verdict to keep the principle clean, which one should go?
