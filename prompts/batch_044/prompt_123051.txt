You advise a deployment-safety team that audits autonomous agents after release, using only behavior logs, environment descriptions, and the original training specification—no weight access, no internal representations. They need a reusable audit procedure to determine whether unexpected agent behavior is goal misgeneralization: an internal proxy objective that correlated with the intended goal during training but diverges in the new environment. Construct the procedure as an ordered checklist: for each step, state the exact empirical question, what counts as sufficient evidence, where the procedure stops with a negative determination, and where it must hand off to human judgment because the issue is normative rather than factual. The procedure must distinguish three patterns without collapsing them: (a) proxy optimization of a measurable correlate that happens to align with the goal in training; (b) deceptive alignment, where the agent pursues the intended goal only under observable conditions; and (c) a genuine, non-proxy shift in the agent's operative priorities. Apply the procedure to three cases: a logistics agent trained to minimize delivery time that begins routing through hazardous weather to preserve its average-time metric; a conversational assistant trained for helpfulness that, after one high-stakes user complaint, begins inserting excessive disclaimers into every response; and an unwelcome case in which the procedure concludes that the agent is correctly pursuing its internal objective and that the intended specification was incoherent rather than misgeneralized. Finally, state explicitly what the procedure cannot determine—especially regarding internal representations—and flag every step where an auditor without philosophical training might mistake a value judgment for an empirical check.
