"The repugnant conclusion is the problem Derek Parfit pointed out for total utilitarianism: world Z has the biggest total welfare because there are so many people, even though each life is barely worth living at 0.2. This avoids the repugnant conclusion completely and doesn't have any other weird results — it's just quality over quantity, no hidden costs. Critical-level utilitarianism, which Parfit proposed, sets a floor at 0.5 and only counts welfare above that. Since everyone in Z is at 0.2, they contribute nothing, so A still wins. But this relies on picking an arbitrary cutoff, which is a hidden problem. Actually the mere addition paradox is basically the same issue: if you add people with positive welfare, total welfare goes up, which is weird. So both fixes work, but average is cleaner because it doesn't need an arbitrary cutoff. Overall total utilitarianism is wrong and average is right."
