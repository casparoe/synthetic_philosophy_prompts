I am preparing a presentation for a philosophy-of-life seminar on Moritz Schlick's essay on the meaning of life, and I want to test whether I am applying his framework consistently. Schlick's verdicts, as I read them, are as follows: he holds that a child engaged in unstructured play is living meaningfully; that an adult laboring solely to earn a wage, with no enjoyment in the task itself, is not; that a scientist pursuing theoretical questions purely out of curiosity lives meaningfully; that an artist who creates for the sake of fame or external reward does not; and that a monk engaged in contemplative prayer, provided it is experienced as joyful absorption rather than fearful duty, lives meaningfully. From these I have extracted two possible governing principles. One is that meaning attaches to any activity experienced as intrinsically absorbing, regardless of whether it serves an external purpose. The other is that meaning requires not merely intrinsic absorption but also a kind of spontaneous, self-directed freedom—what Schlick associates with youthful play—that is incompatible with being structurally subordinated to an external goal, even if one enjoys the subordination.

Here is the new case I am unsure about. There is a phenomenon that labor scholars now call autotelic labor under algorithmic management: a worker spends eight hours a day in a state of flow optimizing delivery routes, reports that she would continue even without pay, and describes the task as like playing Tetris with the world, yet every parameter of her task is set by an optimization algorithm designed to maximize corporate profit, she has no authority over the rules, and the outputs serve no purpose she endorses. Would Schlick say her life, in that segment, is meaningful? Which of my two extracted principles predicts which verdict, and which of his earlier rulings is the closest analog? Finally, if the two principles genuinely underdetermine his view here, what further case—perhaps involving a virtual-reality play environment or a gamified therapeutic regimen—would best distinguish whether Schlick cares about the subjective quality of absorption or about the objective structure of self-direction?
