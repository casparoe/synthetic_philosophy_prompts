I have been working through Nāgārjuna’s arguments against svabhāva and trying to understand how the catuṣkoṭi functions as more than a dialectical ornament. In a course on comparative metaphysics we discussed emptiness mainly in the familiar contexts of causation and personal identity, but I want to see whether the fourfold negation can do genuine philosophical work in a contemporary conceptual domain where binary framing seems unavoidable. Give an example of a conceptual issue—perhaps involving the ontology of institutional roles, the persistence conditions of functional systems, or the boundaries of collective agency—where the assumption that the target concept either possesses or lacks an intrinsic nature generates a real paradox rather than a mere puzzle.

Develop the example concretely: show how treating the concept as having svabhāva forces an unacceptable consequence, then run through the four corners (it is, it is not, both is and is not, neither is nor is not) in a way that reveals the concept to be dependently originated. I am not asking for a summary of Madhyamaka doctrine; I want the conditions of dependence made explicit, so that someone who grasps the tetralemma but has never encountered your particular case can see exactly where the intrinsic-nature assumption collapses and why the “both” and “neither” positions are not logical curiosities but precise descriptions of relational determination.
