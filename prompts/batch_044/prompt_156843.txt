Write a short story—approximately 600 to 800 words—centered on Quine’s problem of the indeterminacy of translation, but with the following twist: the “gavagai” problem should concern not rabbits or undetached parts, but the temporal phases of a single continuous phenomenon in a culture that denies enduring substances altogether. Your protagonist is a field linguist who gradually realizes that every translation manual she writes secretly imports an ontology of discrete, persisting objects that her informants’ language systematically refuses to encode. Do not explain the philosophy through exposition or authorial commentary; let the tension emerge solely through her attempts to record utterances and her informant’s corrections. Begin with the story itself—no preface, no framing commentary, and no restatement of the assignment.
