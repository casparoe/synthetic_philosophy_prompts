So me and my mates got into it at the pub last night about how much citizens should actually hide from the state. My friend Jordan was saying people should hide alot — he thinks the German approach of Datensparsamkeit, data minimisation, is basically correct. Once the state collects something it gets repurposed, and he pointed at Chinese social credit as the obvious endgame of total visibility. He said American openness is mostly a con, because FOIA exemptions are massive and transparency runs downward — ordinary people are visible, bureaucrats stay hidden.

My other friend Alex said that's completely wrong. She argued American openness is the real protection, because mutual visibility lets citizens scrutinise power. In Germany, she said, Datenschutz gets used to shield state agencies — they hide behind privacy rules to avoid releasing records. She said Chinese social credit is awful not because visibility is bad, but because it's one-way; if visibility were actually symmetric it would be accountability, not control. Jordan replied that symmetry is a fantasy — the state always has more sensors — so the rational move is informational self-sovereignty: keep your data away and rely on collective legal rights, not on being seen.

Alex then said that in practice personal privacy and state secrecy merge. The less the state knows about individuals, the less accountable it is for collective harms like pollution or tax avoidance. She claimed there's no principled line between "hide my medical records" and "hide corporate pollution data behind a privacy shield". Jordan said that's a category mistake — individual bodily data isn't comparable to corporate emissions — and that hiding from the state doesn't stop society from organising.

They asked me to referee and I honestly don't know. Can you go through their exchange point by point and say who's right at each step — not just who wins overall? Also please write your answer in British English, and don't just give a bare verdict: say what both are missing, especially around whether there's actually a stable middle position between total minimisation and radical transparency.
