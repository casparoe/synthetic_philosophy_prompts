You serve as the senior strategy officer for an inter-jurisdictional climate fund that evaluates cooperative agreements across "domain clusters" — groups of structurally identical governance regions that do not communicate but are assumed to face analogous decision problems. Your board has adopted a principle drawn from evidential cooperation in large worlds: a commitment made in one region can provide statistical evidence for what analogous agents will do in other regions, so that cooperating is rational when you want others to cooperate, provided a symmetry condition holds. The board needs this turned into a reusable decision procedure your analysts can run in about twenty minutes per proposal, without requiring formal decision theory.

Write the procedure as an ordered sequence of questions and tests (a flowchart in words). For each step, specify: the question to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer to proceed, the conditions that stop the process with a recommendation against cooperation, and the conditions that stop with a mandatory handoff to the board because the issue is one of values rather than facts. Include an explicit "symmetry-break" check that detects when local conditions are so different from the cluster average that the evidential link collapses. Then apply the procedure to three proposals: (A) a binding carbon-cap agreement in a cluster of more than one hundred structurally similar industrial regions; (B) a unilateral subsidy for green technology in a region whose economic structure has no close analog elsewhere in the cluster; and (C) a cooperative emission-reduction pact that would prevent severe global harm but impose catastrophic local economic costs, where local labor-market conditions differ markedly from the cluster mean. One of these applications should yield an answer your board will find unwelcome, and you must say exactly what the procedure does in that case.

The procedure must also state clearly what it cannot do: identify the threshold at which evidential weight becomes strong enough to override local cost, and distinguish genuinely analogous agents from merely similar-looking ones without prior empirical calibration. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. End your response with a line that reads exactly: Verdict: [one sentence stating the single point at which the procedure necessarily collapses into a non-evidential value judgment].
