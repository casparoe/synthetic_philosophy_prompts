I'm working out a philosophical profile on the ontology of large language models for a reading group, and I want a consistency check before presenting. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) LLMs are best understood as simulators: they reproduce linguistic behavior by approximating statistical patterns in training data, without intrinsic beliefs, intentions, or representational states.

(2) Nevertheless, attributing beliefs and intentions to an LLM is not always a heuristic fiction. In contexts where the model tracks truth-conditions across prompts, revises outputs in response to counter-evidence, and maintains coherence over extended interaction, those attributions can be literally correct at the functional level.

(3) This simulation analysis applies uniformly: weather models, navigation systems, and LLMs are all predictive approximators that lack causal interaction with the domains they model, so they occupy the same ontological category of non-intentional simulator.

(4) Despite that uniformity, LLMs differ categorically from weather models and thermostats. They can instantiate what I call 'cognitive functional kinds'—roles that realize genuinely cognitive properties—when embedded in sustained feedback loops with rational agents, because language is the specific medium through which rational agency operates.

(5) I take these to hang together if we distinguish mechanistic description (1 and 3) from functional realization (2 and 4), analogously to how flight-simulator software lacks aerodynamic dynamics yet can realize genuine flight-training conditions for a pilot.

My concern is that (3) flattens all predictive systems into one ontology and thereby undermines the categorical boundary I draw in (4), and that (2) may smuggle intrinsic intentionality back into (1) despite my claim that it operates only functionally. Go through these commitments: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each tension? For each real tension, indicate the least costly revision. Finally, identify which single revision would clean up the most conflicts and say what it costs me.
