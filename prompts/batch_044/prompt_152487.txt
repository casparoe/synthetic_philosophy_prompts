I am a doctoral student in philosophy of science writing a dissertation chapter on belief revision when both the evidential outcome and the framing of the sample space are ambiguous. I would like you to adopt the role of an expert philosopher of science with deep training in formal epistemology and decision theory. Before you present your evaluation, please think carefully and take a moment to brainstorm the normative criteria that ought to govern rational belief revision in such settings—consider coherence, evidential responsiveness, avoidance of dogmatism, and tractability.

Consider the following scenario. A climate-modeling team receives satellite infrared readings that support one of three emission trajectories—low, medium, or high. However, the calibration of the sensors depends on atmospheric conditions that are themselves only partially observed. Consequently, the agent faces not merely an uncertain outcome within a fixed partition, but an uncertain partition: the very framing of the outcome space depends on calibration parameters whose values remain unknown.

Against this background, please evaluate and rank the following four revision policies from most defensible to least defensible for a rational agent in this situation. Group them into tiers if you judge some to be roughly equivalent.

A. Standard Jeffrey conditionalization applied solely to the emission trajectories, holding the sensor-calibration prior fixed and conditioning only on the observed signal within that predetermined framing.

B. A nested, dual-layer Jeffrey update that treats both the emission trajectories and the calibration-parameter values as jointly uncertain, constructing a single partition over the product space and revising credences in one simultaneous step.

C. A conservative Bayesian policy that suspends all updates to the emission-trajectory credences until the calibration uncertainty has been resolved to near-certainty.

D. A robust imprecise-probability strategy that computes standard Jeffrey posteriors separately for each prior within a small neighborhood around the original calibration prior, then aggregates the resulting family of posteriors by taking their convex hull.

For each policy, provide a one-sentence justification for its placement, explaining how it fares with respect to coherence, responsiveness to ambiguous evidence, and the risk of either dogmatism or excessive permissiveness. Then present your full reasoning. At the very end, state your final ordered ranking in a fixed, machine-readable format inside \answer{...}, for example \answer{B, D, A, C}.
