I have been trying to decide whether clearing an old forest for a solar farm reduces wild-animal suffering, and I keep encountering five different ways to account for welfare across ecosystems. Please rank these five approaches from the one you would trust most to the one you would trust least for an actual land-use decision, and in one sentence each explain why it lands there: (1) counting only the direct suffering of vertebrates killed or displaced by the project; (2) adding indirect changes to predator-prey ratios in the remaining habitat; (3) applying a replacement-rate adjustment for organisms that would have been born and lived net-negative lives in the destroyed habitat; (4) weighting every affected organism by a sentience coefficient; and (5) comparing the ecosystem's welfare stock before and after, treating suffering as a flow that ends when the habitat is removed. If this comparison rests on a confused premise, or if one of these cannot be evaluated without data a decision-maker will not have, say so plainly instead of forcing a rank. Respond with exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
