Some colleagues argue that accepting this tax is simply responsible engineering, comparable to adding input validation or error handling. Others argue that in a marketplace where response speed is a primary selection criterion, even a modest tax guarantees that users will migrate to a competitor who skips the loop, which in turn forces every player in the field into a race to the bottom.

Please describe the most important implications of the competitiveness constraint and the alignment tax for a team facing exactly this trade-off. I am not asking for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific works—only a conceptual analysis of how these two forces interact, whether a voluntary slowdown can ever remain stable, and under what conditions (for example, open-source release versus proprietary deployment) the tax might be smaller or larger than it initially appears. If either the pro-tax or anti-tax position relies on hidden assumptions that I should be aware of, please surface them.
