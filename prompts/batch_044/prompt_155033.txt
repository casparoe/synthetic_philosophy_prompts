Last week I was eavesdropping on my sister and her collegue arguing over coffee about funcitonalism and multiple realisability. The collegue said that if consciousness is just a pattern of causal relations, then in principle you could realise a human mind in a swarm of tiny drones, or even in a vat of slime mould arranged right, because the substrate never matters. My sister said that sounds okay in theory, but she brought up this thing she calls functional overlayering — the idea that in real brains the same mental job gets done by several overlapping mechanisms at once, so if you copy the brain's functional map onto drones you might actually be copying the redundancy too, and then it's not clear whether you've got one mind or several competing ones running in paralell. The collegue said that's not a problem for functionalism, that's just a design flaw in the copy, and that multiple realisability actually predicts you'd find redundant realisers in nature. My sister said but if functionalism can't distinguish between a single realised state and ten redundant copies of it, then it doesn't really tell us what mental states are made of, it just gives us a blurry photo. The collegue replied that philosphy of mind isn't engineering, and asking functionalism to resolve physical redundancy is like asking Darwinism to tell you which gene is the real one for eye colour. They kept circling back — she mentioned photonic chips, he mentioned Chinese rooms — but neither seemed convinced. Can you go through each round and say who's making the stronger point, and flag anything they're both missing? Don't need a bibliography, just your take.
