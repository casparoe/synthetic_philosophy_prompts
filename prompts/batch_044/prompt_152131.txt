You chair the accreditation board for a graduate program in philosophy of science. Every semester, faculty submit module proposals that must be tagged either Conceptually Fixed (presented as analytically structured and not open to empirical revision) or Holistically Revisable (presented as part of an interconnected network of belief that can be adjusted in light of experience), because the university's assessment rules and funding allocations depend on this classification. You are required to apply Quine's critique of the analytic-synthetic distinction—specifically, that there is no principled boundary between claims held true by virtue of meaning and claims held true by virtue of fact, and that confirmation applies to whole systems rather than to isolated statements. You have thirty minutes per proposal, must sign off with one other board member, and have no access to outside philosophical consultants.

Write a reusable decision procedure your board can run on any proposal. It must include: (1) the ordered questions to ask of the syllabus and module description, (2) what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, (3) the exact points where the procedure forces a tag versus where it stops and requires a human judgment call because Quine's holism makes the distinction itself collapse, and (4) explicit warnings about cases where the classification will be arbitrary. Then apply the procedure to three proposals: (a) a module treating the concept of bachelor as unmarried male as a definitional truth not revisable by demographic change; (b) a module on quantum interpretation that treats logical consistency as fixed but measurement outcomes as empirically open; and (c) a module you personally believe should receive the opposite tag from what the procedure yields—explain the result, identify the override point, and state exactly what the board must do in that case. Conclude by stating precisely what this procedure cannot accomplish, particularly regarding cross-disciplinary comparison and the preservation of Quine's central insight that the analytic-synthetic boundary is not sharp.

Please write your answer in American English.
