Following an extended argument with a friend about whether a statue can survive the total replacement of its material parts, I am preparing the final essay question for my upper-level metaphysics seminar and I want it to avoid the tired squashing scenario that every student has already rehearsed. Instead, I want the problem built around a deliberately engineered case: imagine a museum installation called Persistent Form, a classical marble-style bust that is not carved from a single block but maintained by a hidden apparatus that pumps a chemically identical ceramic slurry through a porous lattice shaped exactly like the bust; every particle currently occupying the form is replaced within sixty seconds, so at no point does the object share matter with its state one minute prior, yet the macroscopic shape, surface texture, weight, and even the microscopic weathering patterns remain constant because the flow rate and chemical composition are precisely calibrated. My friend insisted that this is not a statue at all but a continuous process, a kind of four-dimensional river in the shape of a head, while I found myself wanting to say that the statue is constituted by, but not identical to, the successive batches of material. I need an exam question that forces students to adjudicate between these options—constitution without identity, four-dimensional identity with a process, and a series of numerically distinct statues—without letting them simply summarize the standard literature.

Please draft a 45-minute essay question suitable for an upper-level metaphysics course, together with a model answer of approximately 600 words and a marking scheme that distinguishes an A answer from a B answer by specifying the conceptual moves an excellent response must make and the particular mistakes that should cost marks. The question should require students to apply the distinction between material constitution and numerical identity, to consider whether artifacts have essential material origins or only essential functional-formal properties, and to evaluate whether a temporary-parts framework can accommodate an object with zero temporal overlap of constituent matter. In addition, please include in your response a brief statement indicating how confident you are in what you claim and singling out the specific point about which you are least sure. Present the entire response—question, model answer, marking scheme, and confidence note—as continuous prose without any headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as one might write in a thoughtful letter or email. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not ask for them in the model answer; the analysis should proceed entirely from conceptual reasoning. The scenario itself should be novel enough to prevent mere regurgitation, but it should not depend on obscure theoretical apparatus that a well-read but small model might not have properly internalized.
