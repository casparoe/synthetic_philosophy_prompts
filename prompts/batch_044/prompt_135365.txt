I've been arguing with my roomate about whether happiness and suffering can even be weighed on the same scale, and I noticed this weird pattern that I don't think has a standard name. People seem to think that if the suffering is really mild — like a paper cut — then no amount of pleasure can justify it being present, almost like it's in a different category entirely. But then they turn around and say the same thing about extreme agony: no feast, no friendship, nothing makes it okay. Meanwhile, in the middle range — a bad back versus a fun vacation — everyone is happy to trade them off and say one is worth more than the other. So it's not a straight line where suffering always dominates, and it's not a flat "they're just different kinds of things." It looks like there are two separate dead zones at the ends where comparison breaks down, and a middle band where weighing works fine. I mentioned this in a philosphy study group and someone said it sounds like lexical negative utilitarianism, but that usually just means suffering takes absolute priority, not that both extremes are incommensurable with pleasure. Is there an actual concept for this specific double-threshold shape? And if there isn't, could you name it and say exactly how its borders differ from ordinary lexical views and from full incommensurability?
