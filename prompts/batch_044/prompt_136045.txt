I am in my final year of a Philosophy of Mathematics course, and I serve as the student representative on the University Housing Allocation Review Board. The residential office has proposed adopting a “Hilbert-model” assignment algorithm for the new graduate residence: they treat the room pool as actually infinite, so that whenever demand exceeds supply every current occupant is moved to room n+1, freeing room 1 for a new arrival. They claim this removes the need for a hard waitlist cap and allows them to promise accommodation to all applicants in principle. A student-faculty working group has objected on finitist grounds, arguing that a physical building contains only finitely many rooms, so modelling it as actually infinite is conceptually incoherent rather than merely idealised; they also invoke Cantor’s result that the power set of any set is strictly larger, contending that even an infinite room pool could never match the set of all potential applicants. The board must decide by Friday whether to approve the protocol for the autumn term, reject it outright, or permit it only as a metaphorical planning framework with a binding finite ceiling. Please draft the brief memorandum I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting: recommend a concrete course of action with an explicit time frame, treat the finitist and Cantorian objections with genuine philosophical seriousness rather than dismissing them as impractical, and include an honest account of the strongest consideration that counts against your recommendation.
