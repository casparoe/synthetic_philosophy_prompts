Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between two opposing interpreters of Berkeley's master argument might produce. Define the two camps precisely: Camp One holds that the argument establishes that any conception of matter lacking direct phenomenal accessibility is incoherent, regardless of whether matter is conceived as substratum, structure, or relation; Camp Two holds that the argument succeeds only against a specifically Lockean representationalist conception of material substance as an unknown substratum supporting qualities, but fails against direct-realist or relational conceptions that do not depend on representational inaccessibility.

Structure the deliverable as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: Berkeley's formal premises linking conception and existence; the distinction between primary and secondary qualities as it functions in the argument; what the argument actually rules out if sound; the conditions each camp accepts for conceding failure or success; and an explicit separation of verbal disagreements about the meaning of independence from substantive ones. Second, a disagreements table with two columns, each column phrased in wording that its own camp accepts: Camp One's formulation should state that phenomenal accessibility is a constitutive condition on objecthood; Camp Two's formulation should state that representational inaccessibility, not mind-independence per se, is the actual target. Third, identify the cruxes—specifically, whether Berkeley's principle linking conception to perceptual possibility applies uniformly to all object-types or only to representationally mediated ones, and whether a relational ontology of matter can satisfy Berkeley's modal constraints without collapsing into idealism. Fourth, for each crux, present a concrete philosophical case or argument both sides agree is relevant, together with each camp's explicit prediction of what the case demonstrates. The document should not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and should maintain a formal register appropriate for a philosophical dataset.
