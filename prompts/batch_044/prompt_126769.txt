Write a short story set in a public secondary school in a fictional suburb of Lyon, where a new administrative protocol—modeled on republican laïcité—requires teachers to document any visible religious expression as part of a quarterly neutrality audit. Meanwhile, a parents' collective, drawing on Anglo-American multicultural frameworks, insists that public recognition of identity is a harm-reduction measure rather than a state imposition. The protagonist is a history teacher tasked with enforcing the audit who discovers that its categories depend on a prior theory of what counts as private versus public identity—a theory that collapses when applied to a student whose religious practice is inseparable from her linguistic dialect. The story should dramatize how the institutional procedure generates a false binary between assimilation and recognition, and should include a classroom scene in which the teacher attempts to apply both frameworks simultaneously and fails not through personal prejudice, but because the two conceptual architectures share no common unit of measurement. Conclude with the teacher drafting a report that must translate an irreducible moral conflict into a checkbox format, leaving the resolution structurally unresolved.
