I write novels concerned with the architecture of memory and consciousness, and I serve as the literary representative on the Ethics and Programming Board of the Meridian Institute for Sleep, Narrative, and Consciousness. The Institute is preparing to launch a pilot collaboration—part clinical protocol, part immersive installation—in which high-fidelity neural signatures captured during REM sleep are translated into replayable audiovisual environments that participants can subsequently navigate. All subjects have consented to recording; the board must now decide, with implementation before the quarter ends, whether the consent framework must also classify replay as the reproduction of an actual experience (triggering enhanced psychological safeguards, privacy claims, and an opt-out clause) or as the mere reconstruction of a post-waking narrative (falling under standard artistic-use protocols). Please draft the board’s formal written opinion for circulation before Thursday’s meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action with a clear timeline, treat with intellectual seriousness the Malcolm-inspired denial that dreams are experiences at all rather than interpretive reports generated upon waking, and address the strongest Dennett-inspired counter-argument—particularly regarding whether the phenomenology of replay involves genuine experiential continuity or only narrative construction—without reducing either view to caricature. The memo must include an honest accounting of the strongest consideration on the losing side, since the board is divided between those who see ontological risk in treating replay as experience and those who see it as harmless retrospective narration.
