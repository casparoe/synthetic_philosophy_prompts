hey i run a philosophy seminar on enlightenment political thought, about 16 grad students, one 80 minute session. i want them to feel in their bones why rousseau thinks a public theater corrupts a republic, not just memorize that he was anti-theatre. specifically i want them to experience the structural shift he describes: citizens stop being co-present agents and become an audience that confuses watching virtue with possessing it, and this happens even when the play is morally uplifting.

design an in-class simulation. split the group into three rotating roles: performers (3-4 people) who stage a brief skit about a civic dilemma—say whether the town should tax the rich to fund a wall—spectators (the rest) who sit in fixed rows, recieve a hidden payoff for feeling moved, and then vote on the actual wall-tax decision; and festival citizens (a small group) who must reach the same decision through a brief participatory ritual with no audience and no script. the rules should be rigged so the spectators naturally defer to the performers framing, feel satisfied by the moral message, and then discover they never actually deliberated. include a payoff scheme with no real money—maybe esteem tokens that performers collect and spectators hoard—so people feel the incentive to stay passive.
