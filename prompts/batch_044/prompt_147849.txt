For a seminar on moral affect and cultural design I have tried to articulate my settled view on guilt, shame, and whether a society can choose its dominant moral emotion. Before I present, I need an audit of consistency. Here are my commitments:

(1) Guilt is structurally superior as a moral emotion because it localizes blame to discrete acts, leaving the agent’s core identity intact and thereby preserving autonomy.

(2) Shame is structurally superior because serious moral failure is often a failure of character or social standing rather than a single act, so guilt misrepresents the ontology of wrongdoing by treating it as detachable.

(3) A culture can genuinely choose its dominant moral emotion—through education, law, ritual, and what I will call its normative infrastructure—so that a society can become a guilt-culture or a shame-culture by design.

(4) The affective core of shame—withdrawal, gaze aversion, the urge to hide—is biologically invariant; culture can only assign different meanings to this core, never replace or eliminate it.

(5) Because that biological core is invariant, a culture that “chooses” guilt is not replacing shame but suppressing its expression, which creates a psychologically costly and unstable affective ecology in which the suppressed emotion mutates into other pathologies.

(6) Moral emotions are subject to rational evaluation only if they are responsive to reasons about actions; because shame targets the self globally, it is rationally opaque and therefore not a proper moral emotion at all.

(7) Whether a culture ought to prioritize guilt or shame depends on whether it values individual autonomy over communal belonging, but there is no culture-neutral reason to prefer one value over the other.

Please examine this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe is each real tension? For each tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs me. Rank them by severity rather than treating them equally. Finally, identify the single revision that would clean up the most tension with the least damage to the overall view, and explain what I would have to give up to make it. Do not treat this as a lecture on guilt and shame in general; treat my specific package as the object of audit.
