I'm writing the final assessment for a graduate epistemology seminar that has spent the term on external-world skepticism, and I'm tired of answers that merely rehearse the closure principle or the familiar semantic response to the brain-in-a-vat scenario. I want an exam question that works in the weeds of the problem rather than on its surface. Specifically, I want students to confront a variant I'll call the gradual vat: a subject whose experiential inputs are replaced, imperceptibly and over many years, with simulated ones, so that there is no discrete moment of entering the vat and no intact autobiographical memory of a prior unsimulated life. The question should ask whether this gradualist formulation changes the skeptical challenge in ways the abrupt version does not—particularly regarding the epistemic status of long-term inductive reasoning, the reliability of memory across time, and whether an appeal to epistemic conservatism can resist the skeptic without begging the question. Students should be required to distinguish the gradualist case from both the standard abrupt BIV and from mere empirical underdetermination, and to evaluate whether the absence of a boundary event makes the skeptical hypothesis more or less threatening to our knowledge of the past.

Please write the exam prompt suitable for a 48-hour take-home response of about 1500 words. Then provide a model answer of approximately 700 words, and finally a marking scheme that separates an excellent answer from a competent one. The marking scheme should list five specific conceptual moves the answer must make—for example, whether it correctly identifies why the gradualist case primarily attacks diachronic rather than synchronic justification; whether it distinguishes epistemic conservatism from mere dogmatism; whether it addresses the asymmetry between present experiential checks and past-oriented beliefs; whether it explains why the lack of a boundary event complicates appeals to abduction or simplicity; and whether it evaluates whether the gradualist hypothesis is even empirically distinguishable from a world in which technology slowly improves. For each criterion, state the marks available (out of a total of 25), and include a one-sentence note on the characteristic failure mode. Do not ask for bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific scholars; the answer should stand on its conceptual structure alone. Keep your entire response within about 1000 words, with the model answer not exceeding 700 words and the marking scheme presented as concise bullet points.
