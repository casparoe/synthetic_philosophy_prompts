Examination question: In The Querist, Berkeley develops a philosophical analysis of money and proposes an institutional remedy for Irish economic stagnation. Explain his distinction between money and real wealth, his critique of the prevailing emphasis on gold and silver, and the banking mechanism he advocates. Then evaluate whether his account treats money as a purely conventional token, a natural measure of labor, or an instrument that necessarily refers back to real goods and productive land.

Student answer: Berkeley argues that money is not wealth itself but only a token or counter representing real goods and labor. He criticizes the belief that gold and silver constitute national prosperity, noting that they are merely durable metals incapable of producing food or shelter, whereas true wealth lies in land, labor, and commodities. To remedy Ireland's stagnation, he proposes a national bank issuing paper currency backed by the nation's land and productive capacity rather than by bullion reserves. Here money functions like a ticket: it gains value through social agreement and state authorization, serving only to facilitate exchange. Consequently, Berkeley's position is radically conventionalist—money belongs to no natural kind, and even these bank notes depend ultimately on legislative trust rather than on any material foundation. His scheme therefore severs currency from commodity altogether, treating it as a self-sufficient instrument of exchange.
