Write a Shakespearean sonnet (ABAB CDCD EFEF GG) that takes Sen’s liberal paradox as its subject and lets the form do the philosophical work. In the octave, present the two conditions that seem compatible: Condition L, which grants each of two individuals a minimal, personal sphere of choice over whether he reads a particular controversial book; and Condition P, the weak Pareto principle that requires any outcome unanimously preferred by both to be socially selected. Use the classic four-state framing—who reads the book and who does not—to build the naive expectation that liberty and efficiency can coexist. At the volta in line 9, shift to the contradiction: map the four social assignments onto a cycle (state a dominates b, b dominates c, c dominates d, d dominates a) so that no linear social ordering survives. In the sestet, show that this cycle is not a quirk of the example but unavoidable whenever minimal rights overlap with unanimous preferences, even with only two agents. Close with a couplet that names exactly what must be abandoned—either the minimal liberty or the Pareto principle—without deciding between them, leaving the tension structurally unresolved, as the paradox demands. The rhyme and meter should be precise enough that a reader familiar with social-choice theory recognizes the mapping, while a reader unfamiliar should still hear a coherent argument in verse.
