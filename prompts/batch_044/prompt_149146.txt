I run a small community mental-health program and every Tuesday my team has to greenlight or kill activity proposals for our "meaning-first" track, but we dont have a shared framework for deciding what actually belongs there. I was arguing about this with a friend over coffee — she's been reading old Vienna Circle stuff — and she mentioned Schlick's view that meaning comes from play (doing something for its own sake) rather than from working toward some external end. That sounds right to me, but I need something I can actually run in fifteen minutes with case workers who havent read any philosophy. Can you act as an expert in philosophical counseling and operational ethics? Please think carefully, and start by brainstorming which parts of Schlick's distinction have to survive translation and which can be dropped without breaking the idea.

What I want is a reusable decision procedure — a checklist or flowchart-in-words — that asks the questions in order, says what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, stops with a hard "no" where appropriate, and clearly marks the spots where I (the director) have to make a values call rather than a fact call. Be honest about where the procedure will misfire, especially with what I've started calling "telic contamination": an activity looks like pure play on the ground but its funding framing or mandatory structure suppresses the intrinsic value, or vice versa. Then apply it to two proposals: (1) a mandatory journaling workshop for housing-assistance applicants, explicitly designed to "process trauma to improve job-readiness metrics" with attendance required and outcomes tracked; and (2) a senior chess league where the grant language ties every session to cognitive-decline benchmarks, but the participants genuinely just love playing and ignore the scores. One of these should come back with an answer I'm going to hate. Flag exactly where judgement cant be eliminated and a person has to sign off. No bibliographies, no reading lists — my staff doesnt have library access and I just need the procedure they can use.
