Here's an argument my study partner cooked up about Sapir-Whorf and I think it bites off more than it can chew. (1) If language shapes thought, then people with different grammars literally can't share mental states about the same event. (2) So if Whorf is right, a Japanese speaker using agentless grammer for an accident and an English speaker assigning blame aren't disagreeing about facts - they're in incommensurable worlds. (3) That'd make cross-linguistic psychology experiments impossible in principle, since there's no shared metric to compare results. (4) But people run these experiments and publish them all the time. (5) So Whorf must be false, period, including weak versions. (6) Apply the same logic to grammatical gender: if Spanish speakers literally perceive chairs as feminine, then they and German speakers can't both be right about the chair. (7) But they clearly can. (8) So even the weak color and gender effects everyone cites are dead. I feel like step 2 is sneaking in a strong incommensurability claim that weak relativists reject, but I can't see why the reasoning doesn't just scale down. Which step do I reject, and what's the cost? Also please answer in American English.
