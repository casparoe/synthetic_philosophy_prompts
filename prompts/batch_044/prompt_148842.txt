Consider four characterizations of the principle of charity in Donald Davidson's account of radical interpretation, each formulated as it might appear in a synthetic graduate text or a specialist's critical summary. Your task is to evaluate them.

Formulation A: Charity functions as an ethical norm of interpretive practice—interpreters should maximize agreement with their interlocutors out of respect for rational agency, and an interpreter who systematically attributes false beliefs or irrational inferences violates a moral obligation built into the act of understanding.

Formulation B: Charity is an inescapable epistemic and semantic constraint—because belief and meaning are holistically interdependent, an interpretation that renders a speaker massively irrational or systematically mistaken about her immediate environment fails to assign determinate contents to either beliefs or utterances, leaving the interpreter with no stable language to interpret.

Formulation C: Charity expresses a metaphysical thesis about mental content—possessing beliefs just is being interpretable as largely rational and largely correct about the world, so radical interpretation does not uncover pre-existing mental states but rather constitutes them through the application of the charitable constraint.

Formulation D: Charity is a defeasible pragmatic heuristic—interpreters initially assume rationality and widespread true belief to bootstrap interpretation, but if predictive success remains poor they may suspend charity and instead attribute pervasive conceptual divergence, irrationality, or error without thereby forfeiting the ability to interpret.

For each formulation, assign a fidelity score on a scale from 0 (seriously misrepresents Davidson) to 10 (a defensible, if compressed, statement of his view). Then rank the four formulations from most adequate to least adequate.

In your reasoning, distinguish clearly between what is textually and argumentatively settled in Davidson's writings—particularly the constitutive link between interpretation, belief ascription, and rationality—from what remains genuinely contested among Davidson scholars, such as whether charity carries metaphysical implications for the ontology of mental states, whether it applies uniformly to logical and empirical beliefs, or whether it should be understood as purely methodological rather than normative. Do not present a disputed interpretive claim as the consensus position. Provide concise justification for each score and for your overall ranking.
