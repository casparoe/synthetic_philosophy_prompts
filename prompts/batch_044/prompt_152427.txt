I was arguing with my friend about whether my old pickup truck is still literally the same object after I replaced the engine, transmission, both axles, the cab, and every door panel over the years — basically everything except the original frame with the VIN plate stamped into it. The DMV still registers it under the same title and number, but I learned that if I'd swapped out that frame instead, even while keeping every other part, they'd have made me apply for a reconstructed title or issued a brand new VIN. So there's this institutional practice where legal identity tracks exactly one physical part, and replacing that particular part breaks continuity, regardless of how much of the rest survives.

What theory of special composition or object persistence does that practice presuppose? Does it commit to some kind of essential-part metaphysics, or could a four-dimentionalist or relational view explain why the frame gets special status without treating it as metaphysically necessary? And is the rule even internally consistent — like, if I kept the frame but replaced every bolt and panel, it's the same truck, but if I kept every bolt and panel and only replaced the frame it's suddenly a different vehicle, even though the second case might preserve more of the original functional organization and mass? Reconstruct the implicit metaphyscis here, say whether it's philosophically coherent or just bureaucratic convenience, and tell me what a registration system built on a genuinely compositional or anti-essentalist view of material objects would actually have to look like.
