You serve on a national reproductive-ethics advisory board. A consortium of fertility clinics has applied to pilot a state-subsidized program offering preimplantation genetic screening not for monogenic diseases, but for polygenic “flourishing indices”—composite scores predicting emotional resilience, cognitive flexibility, and social adaptability—explicitly justified by procreative beneficence and protected by liberal-eugenics safeguards (fully voluntary enrollment, pluralist trait-weighting menus, and no state-mandated targets). Critics contend that even voluntary optimization embeds a contested conception of human excellence, creates structural pressure on disadvantaged parents to select for market-valued traits, and undermines liberal neutrality when the clinics themselves design the metrics and administer sliding-scale subsidies. Draft the board’s formal decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—recommending a concrete course of action with an explicit timeline (e.g., phased rollout with mandatory review milestones at 12 and 36 months). The memo should treat the strongest objection on the losing side seriously rather than dismissing it, and should address specifically whether pluralist menus can genuinely preserve neutrality when the institutional framework still defines and rewards “flourishing.”
