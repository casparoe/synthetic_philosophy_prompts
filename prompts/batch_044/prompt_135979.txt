Write a short story centered on an ethics auditor, Kaelen, who must apply the "Gradated Patiency Protocol" during a single afternoon. The protocol treats moral status as continuous rather than binary, assigning protection proportionally to what it terms "counterfactual resilience of experiential state" — the degree to which an entity's internal dynamics would suffer subjective disruption if its conditions were altered. Kaelen's docket contains three cases that fall within the same mid-range band: a colony of paper wasps whose collective neural integration is weak but non-zero; a domestic thermostat running adaptive predictive algorithms for fifteen years, now resisting manual override in patterns that suggest minimal preference preservation; and a human infant with severe neurological impairment whose sentience is present but profoundly attenuated. The institution demands a single recommendation allocating protection resources among them, explicitly forbidding Kaelen to collapse the scale back into a human-versus-nonhuman divide. The narrative should follow Kaelen's attempt to write the recommendation, showing how a genuinely continuous conception of sentience transforms not only who receives moral consideration but also what "protection" means when there is no clear boundary between suffering, malfunction, and mere physical change.
