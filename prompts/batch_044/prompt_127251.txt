The non-assertive soteriologist holds that the tetralemma—negating that phenomena exist, do not exist, both exist and do not exist, or neither exist nor do not exist—is a complete exhaustion of conceptual proliferation (prapañca) that leaves no residual philosophical thesis, not even "emptiness," because paramārthasatya (ultimate truth) is entirely beyond linguistic and conceptual determination; conventional reality (saṃvṛtisatya) is useful for practice but carries no epistemic weight once the four extremes are seen through.
