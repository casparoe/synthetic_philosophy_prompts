I'm putting together a problem set on killing vs letting die, and I've been noticing a pattern in student papers that I don't have a label for. Someone argues that witholding life support and withdrawing it are morally the same, which is fine, but then they jump straight to "so there's no real difference between polluting and failing to clean up pollution" or between shutting down a harmful factory and never having built it. In the medical case there's an underlying pathology that's going to kill the patient regardless; the agent is just choosing whether to block it. In the factory or climate case there's no analogous "natural" harm running on its own -- the baseline is already human-made. So they're taking a local equivalence that depends on a specific causal background and using it as a universal solvent for the doing/allowing distinction. Does this specific failure mode have a name in the literature? If not, what's the sharpest way to distinguish it from just misapplying an analogy? And please be explicit about how sure you are in what you claim, and flag the one point you're least confident about.
