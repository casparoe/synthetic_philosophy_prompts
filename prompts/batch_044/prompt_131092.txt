Here is an argument I have been turning over that extends Sen's minimal liberalism well beyond the familiar two-person case, and I worry it collapses into a general indictment of any liberal constraint on collective choice. (1) Minimal liberalism holds that for each agent there must exist at least one pair of social states, identical except within that agent's personal sphere, over which that agent's preference is decisive. (2) Any social state in a collective choice problem can be decomposed into a vector of individual-state coordinates, one per agent per relevant dimension. (3) So minimal liberalism applies coordinate-by-coordinate: for each agent-dimension pair assigned to that agent's sphere, the social choice function fixes that coordinate according to the agent's preference. (4) Given any profile with interdependent preferences—agents caring about others' states through altruism, expressive concerns, or externalities—this coordinate-wise fixing can yield an outcome that every agent ranks below some other feasible vector, specifically one that violates at least one coordinate-level liberal assignment. (5) Because interdependent preferences are typical rather than exceptional in social choice, minimal liberalism is therefore systematically liable to produce Pareto-inferior outcomes. (6) A decision rule structurally guaranteed to generate results that all affected parties agree are worse is self-defeating and should be rejected. (7) Hence minimal liberalism should be abandoned as a general constraint, not merely qualified in isolated paradoxes. The move from (3) to (4) feels like it sneaks in an equivocation between a substantively protected personal sphere and an arbitrarily decomposed coordinate of a collective outcome, but I am unsure how to block it without also weakening liberalism. Meanwhile I want to keep both (1) and the idea that Pareto efficiency is a minimal rationality condition, yet (6) seems to force me to drop one. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost—particularly for the status of liberal rights in multi-dimensional collective decisions?
