I'm working with a research collective that is trying to adapt coherence-based norms—specifically Dutch book arguments—to institutional forecasting environments, such as climate-risk assessment boards or epidemiological model-ensemble committees. In these settings, individual experts operate with heterogeneous priors, bounded computational resources, and shared payoff structures, yet the classic Dutch book literature was developed for solitary, ideally rational agents with precise credences. Before we invest effort in formalizing our framework, we need to know which conceptual gaps in the Dutch book tradition are genuinely upstream and which are distracting detours. Among the live issues—whether coherence constraints can be sustained for agents with genuinely imprecise credences without forcing artificial precision; whether diachronic Dutch books survive when agents revise beliefs in response to structurally uncertain model outputs rather than straightforward propositional evidence; whether practical Dutch books can be constructed against bounded-rationality forecasters by adversarial counterparties without smuggling in logical omniscience; and whether there is a defensible distinction between pragmatic and epistemic Dutch books when institutional decisions carry irreversible resource commitments—which should we treat as foundational?

Please provide a defended prioritization rather than a survey: for each issue you rank highly, explain how its resolution conditions the others, what makes it tractable for philosophers and formal epistemologists rather than empirical scientists, and what kind of argument or result over the coming years would cause you to revise its rank downward. If one of these questions strikes you as a framing artifact that collapses upon inspection, identify it and explain why the apparent openness is illusory. I am less interested in a literature map than in an agenda: given limited theoretical effort, where should it go first, and what is the specific dependency that makes that ordering rational?
