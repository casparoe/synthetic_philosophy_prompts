Write the opening remarks for a one-day workshop on institutional moral repair, with an audience split between moral philosophers and practicing legal mediators who have no background in philosophy. The guiding issue is whether large organizations—corporations, universities, or state agencies—can genuinely bear Strawsonian reactive attitudes such as resentment or gratitude toward specific individuals, or whether these attitudes are necessarily restricted to face-to-face interpersonal relations. Make the case that applying reactive attitudes to institutions is not just metaphorical but changes the structural requirements of moral repair: for example, an institutional apology might need to accomplish something that personal remorse cannot. Use this distinction to draw clear lines around what institutions can and cannot meaningfully do in response to harm. The remarks should be rigorous enough for the philosophers present without relying on technical jargon that would exclude the mediators. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations, and avoid direct quotations.
