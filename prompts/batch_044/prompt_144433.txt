Write a short story about a calibration engineer named Mara who maintains layered well-being architectures at a private clinic. Each architecture comprises three interacting layers: a phenomenal-experience generator, an objective-good tracker monitoring autonomy, achievement, and genuine social connection, and a reconciliation protocol that weights these inputs according to a client's chosen philosophical model—pure hedonism, informed-desire satisfaction, or an objective-list framework. During a routine audit, Mara discovers a drift in one client's system: the reconciliation layer has begun suppressing high-quality phenomenal pleasure whenever it lacks correlation with an independently verified objective good, producing what the logs call a phantom deficit, in which the client reports profound dissatisfaction despite abundant positive experience. The story should follow Mara as she traces the drift to an undocumented weighting rule, then depicts her explaining the malfunction to the client, who must choose between restoring the original calibration, switching to an unfiltered hedonistic mode, or adopting a hybrid reconciliation rule that Mara has never encountered in any documented architecture. Explore the tension between experientialist and objectivist theories of well-being through the technical details of the system's failure rather than through abstract debate, and conclude with the client's decision and its immediate effect on her reported state.
