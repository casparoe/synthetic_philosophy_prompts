I have been asked to contribute to an interdisciplinary symposium on science and religion, and in revisiting Galileo's defense of scriptural accommodation I have become preoccupied with a problem he could never have encountered. Galileo maintained that biblical language adapts itself to the capacities of ordinary people, and that Scripture's proper authority concerns salvation rather than cosmological mechanics—famously distinguished as teaching humanity how to reach heaven rather than describing the physical structure of the heavens. This principle of accommodation, paired with his conviction that nature and revelation are two books authored by the same divine source and therefore cannot genuinely contradict one another, formed the backbone of his argument for the autonomy of scientific inquiry within its own sphere.

My question concerns a distinctly modern development: the emergence of algorithmic hermeneutics, in which large language models and statistical pattern-recognition systems generate interpretations of biblical texts without reference to ecclesiastical tradition, human spiritual formation, or historical-critical scholarship. If Galileo were presented with these automated systems—treating sacred texts as data objects to be optimized for coherence, predictive accuracy, or semantic consistency—how would he classify their activity? Would he view them as a dangerous literalism that mistakes mechanical processing for understanding, thereby violating the spirit of accommodation? Or would he regard them as legitimate extensions of the rational instruments God granted humanity, analogous to the telescope's extension of vision, provided they remain confined to the sphere of textual mechanics and do not claim authority over matters of salvation? I am seeking a substantive verdict grounded in the logic of his distinction between the two books, not an equivocal summary of his biography, because my own symposium remarks depend on whether his philosophy of accommodation can survive the automation of interpretation.
