Write a short story that translates the logic of the dollar auction—where rational actors escalate commitment because the accumulated cost of withdrawal exceeds the cost of continued bidding—into an epistemic rather than monetary domain. Set the story within a small, isolated research collective that maintains a shared taxonomy of natural phenomena observed only through indirect traces. When two senior members discover that a foundational category in their taxonomy contains an internal contradiction, they enter an implicit auction over who will bear the burden of systemic revision. Rather than bidding currency, each proposed revision requires the bidder to permanently renounce previously held observational commitments, and the first to withdraw must accept irreversible exclusion from the collective's authority to interpret future traces. Show both characters fully aware of the mechanism, yet unable to stop, because every prior renunciation binds them more tightly to the coherence of the position they have already staked out. Focus the narrative tightly on their interaction and the growing absurdity of what they are willing to surrender, letting the structure of the escalation itself carry the conceptual weight without explicit philosophical commentary. Write the response in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as in an email or a letter.
