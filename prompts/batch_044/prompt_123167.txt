I have noticed a recurring argumentative configuration in discussions of planning mechanisms—especially those derived from Lange's iterative procedure or its contemporary algorithmic analogues—and I want to know whether it carries an established name.

Here is the pattern in concrete form. A defender of central planning first meets Hayek's knowledge objection by demonstrating that a planning board can, in principle, converge on equilibrium prices using only locally available data, without requiring omniscience. Once this static-defence succeeds, the same defender advances to claim that the mechanism also accommodates dynamic change—technological innovation, shifting preferences, entrepreneurial discovery—without offering a separate argument for how the convergence proof, which presupposes stable production functions and given preference orderings, extends to domains where those very functions and orderings are in flux. When a critic presses precisely this point, the defender retreats sincerely to the narrower claim: "the mechanism need only approximate current allocations; dynamics can be handled separately." Yet once that retreat is granted, the defender resumes speaking as if the mechanism itself delivers dynamic efficiency, sliding back to the broader claim as though it had never been qualified.

This is not exactly motte-and-bailey, because neither version is an indefensible extension of a defensible core; both are contested substantive claims that happen to share a formal apparatus. Nor is it plain equivocation, because the speaker genuinely means both claims in different contexts. Still, the oscillation creates a distinctive rhetorical immunity: the theory borrows the credibility of its static proof when facing dynamic objections, and borrows the modesty of its static scope when facing knowledge objections.

Does this configuration have an established label in philosophy of science, economic methodology, or argumentation theory? If not, can you name and define it precisely—specifying whether it requires shared mathematical formalism, shared normative vocabulary, or merely shared institutional context—and map its boundary cases? In particular, distinguish it from ordinary scope ambiguity, from defensive qualification, and from the kind of theoretical retreat Popper discussed. I am not asking for a bibliography; I want the conceptual carving.
