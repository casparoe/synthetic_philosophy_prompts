I have recently been asked to help organize a civic arts program in a small municipality that governs itself through direct town meetings, and this has made Rousseau's suspicion of theatrical spectacle unexpectedly personal for me. Would you write a dialogue—formal in tone, with both interlocutors equally versed in Rousseau and in contemporary performance theory—between a defender of a strict Rousseauian view and an advocate of participatory, non-mimetic civic performance? The Rousseauian should argue that theatrical representation is structurally incompatible with direct democratic presence: by placing citizens in the position of spectators rather than agents, theater substitutes aestheticized pity for political solidarity, and this corruption is rooted not in the content of plays but in the representational form itself—the inevitable gap between the actor who pretends and the audience who watches. The defender of participatory performance should counter that Rousseau's critique applies only to the institutional spectacle of the proscenium stage, and that when performance is reorganized as a fête—where audience and performer share space, roles circulate, and narrative emerges from collective ritual rather than scripted representation—the alienation of both actor and spectator can be dissolved together. Crucially, I want the exchange to turn on a distinction that is often collapsed in discussions of Rousseau: the difference between the alienation of the performer (forced to be what he is not) and the alienation of the citizen-spectator (reduced to passive judgment rather than active participation), and whether eliminating the first necessarily remedies the second. Each speaker should engage directly with the other's actual claims—if one argues that dissolved roles still leave a residual spectatorial consciousness, the other should address that specific mechanism rather than change the subject. Neither should be a strawman. Please avoid bibliographies, citations, page references, or lists of further reading. Refer to Rousseau's arguments only by paraphrase, and do not place any words in quotation marks drawn from an actual text.
