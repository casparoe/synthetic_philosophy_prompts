I'm a graduate student in philosophy of science trying to articulate a recurring pattern I observe in reductionist debates across fields, and I want to know whether it already carries an established technical name. The pattern unfolds like this: a research program announces that phenomenon P has been fully accounted for by reducing it to a base-level mechanism M. When critics then point to a feature F of P that does not obviously derive from M, the researchers do not treat F as a falsifier; instead, they reclassify F as "emergent" and place it outside the scope of the reduction. Crucially, they continue to advertise the P-to-M reduction as a complete explanatory success, deploying the emergence clause only defensively—never allowing it to constrain the positive claims they make about P. For example, a neuroreductionist might treat first-person reports as an emergent "overlay" on neural dynamics while still insisting that consciousness "just is" those dynamics; similarly, an economist might invoke emergent institutional properties when individual-level models fail to predict collective behavior, yet continue to maintain that macroeconomics is fully reducible in principle.

This strikes me as structurally different from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis, because the exception is not a specific boundary condition but a wholesale ontological category that is invoked selectively—accepted when it protects the reduction from counterexample, ignored when the reduction is being marketed. It also does not seem to be mere equivocation on "reduction," since the same researchers often genuinely hold both that emergence is ontologically real and that reduction is total, without recognizing that the two commitments undermine each other's explanatory force. Is there a recognized concept in the literature on emergence, scientific explanation, or theory immunization that captures exactly this oscillation? If there is no precise term, how should I characterize its logical structure so as to distinguish it clearly from standard ad hoc adjustments, and what would be the most accurate label for this particular failure mode?
