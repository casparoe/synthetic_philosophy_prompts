I am preparing a chapter on determinacy failures in infinite decision procedures, and I need a precisely engineered thought experiment that modifies Thomson's lamp so that the usual parity-based indeterminacy is resolved by a coupled continuous parameter, yet remains vulnerable to removal of that parameter.

Please design a scenario meeting the following specifications. Imagine a macroscopic mechanical lamp—no quantum or exotic physical apparatus—situated in a clinical workshop. An agent can toggle the lamp at intervals that halve in duration, converging to a limit time T. Crucially, the toggle mechanism is coupled to a hydraulic reservoir: each toggle releases a fixed volume of fluid into a tube feeding a stabilising system for a patient, and the reservoir's total capacity is exactly twice the volume released per toggle. The flow rate to the patient is continuous and proportional to the remaining pressure in the reservoir. The patient's stability requires that the pressure at T be strictly positive. The agent follows a deterministic rule—toggling on every even-numbered step—so that in the infinite sequence there is no final toggle.

Present the scenario in a single paragraph, using realistic rather than science-fictional details. Then demonstrate: (1) the physical state of the reservoir and the flow condition at T, derived from the continuity of the hydraulic parameter; (2) why Thomson's original argument—that the lamp cannot be determinately on or off at T because it has undergone infinitely many toggles—fails to settle the state of this coupled system; (3) the exact structural feature that does the work in resolving the indeterminacy, together with an explanation of why removing that feature (leaving a purely discrete lamp) collapses the case back into Thomson's original problem; (4) a brief defence against the objection that this merely changes the subject from a lamp to a hydraulic device, arguing instead that it is a legitimate variant of the supertask that isolates the role of continuous coupling.

Constraints: do not invoke perfect predictors, demons, or exotic physics; avoid bibliographic requests or citation lists; keep the reasoning self-contained so that it does not depend on access to specialised literature; and maintain a formal register appropriate for doctoral work in philosophy of action or philosophy of mathematics. Write in British English.
