I am interested in whether embedded agency poses a principled, rather than merely practical, obstacle to diachronic rationality. Consider the specific claim that, because an agent is physically embedded in the environment it models, it cannot represent its own future decision procedure as an independent variable without either breaking the causal consistency of its world-model or generating an infinite regress of self-prediction; therefore, the independence conditions required for standard money-pump arguments fail necessarily, not just contingently, for embedded agents. Give me your credence that this claim succeeds — that embeddedness genuinely undermines the coherence conditions for diachronic rationality, rather than only complicating their implementation. Decompose your reasoning into three components: first, assign a rough probability to whether embeddedness creates a distinctive epistemic barrier to self-modeling that does not apply to an embedded agent's models of other agents; second, assign a probability to whether standard conditions of diachronic coherence (such as immunity to money pumps) depend on an independence assumption between present and future decision nodes that embeddedness necessarily violates; third, assign a probability to whether a sufficiently reflective embedded agent could recover coherence through fully anticipated self-modification or internal commitment mechanisms, without relying on the external commitment devices that standard accounts presuppose. Aggregate these into an overall credence, identify which component is the dominant driver of your number, and specify what kind of theoretical result or conceptual clarification would most move that component.
