Please argue that saying Malthus was 'right but early' actually messes up the logic of long-run falsifcation. The standard rescue is he missed the demographic transition and tech growth, so the mechanism was sound but countervailing forces showed up late. Flip that: if a causal mechanism is real, then watching its predicted outcome get avoided for two centuries shouldn't count as delayed confirmation -- it should count as evidence the mechanism wasn't dominant. Use the idea of 'structural dormancy' from geology -- fault lines can sit quiet for millenia without being 'wrong' about earthquakes -- and apply it to Malthusian pressure. The mechanism can be present but dormant, which means 'right but early' confuses a sleeping cause with a premature forecast. Does this bury Malthus or save him? And does the dormancy framing generalize to other long-range predictions, like climate tipping points?
