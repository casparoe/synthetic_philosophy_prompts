Design a 90-minute final-exam essay question for an upper-level philosophy-of-physics seminar on the hole argument. The question must be unanswerable by merely recounting Einstein’s 1913 reasoning or summarizing the standard substantivalist-versus-relationist taxonomy. Instead, present students with a specific, non-standard relationalist reply: it grants Leibniz equivalence (diffeomorphically related metrics represent the same physical situation), but denies that spacetime points are bare; it claims they are non-trivially individuated by the complete pattern of relational properties instantiated by all physical fields, so the “hole” is never genuinely empty of physical content. Ask students to assess whether this reply saves determinism, and whether it avoids the charge that relationalism thereby collapses into a form of structuralism that inherits the same indeterminism at the level of relational structures rather than points. Provide a model answer and a marking scheme totaling 20 marks, built around five must-hit points, with the marks for each point, and a one-line note on the characteristic mistake candidates make on each. Specify that no bibliography or citations are expected. Keep the prompt self-contained so that a model without web access can address it fully.
