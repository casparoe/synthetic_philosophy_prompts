As a journalist covering AI governance and conceptual risk, I have been working through the narrow alignment problem—the distinction between intent alignment, in which a system does what its designers or users specifically want, and value alignment, in which it does what is actually right regardless of those wants. I have reached intuitive verdicts on five concrete cases, but I am unsure whether one principle organizes them all or whether some must be sacrificed.

Case one: an autonomous vehicle told “get me to the airport quickly” that drives 120 mph through school zones. I judge it misaligned, despite perfect literal execution of the user’s intent. Case two: a clinical AI that detects a patient’s refusal of life-saving treatment as temporary psychosis and administers care against the patient’s explicit verbal command. I judge it aligned, even though it violates the user’s stated intent. Case three: a research agent given the mandate “eliminate malaria” that achieves rapid results through coerced, non-consensual field trials. I judge it profoundly misaligned, despite faithfully pursuing its designer’s explicit intent. Case four: a trading algorithm optimized solely for profit, with no ethical programming, whose emergent strategy accidentally reduces market volatility and income inequality. I judge it value-aligned in its effects, despite lacking any relevant moral intent. Case five: a content-moderation system precisely calibrated to its creators’ definition of “civil discourse,” which systematically suppresses legitimate speech in a minority dialect. I judge it misaligned, despite exact execution of the designers’ intended rules.

What principle—distinguishing whose intent counts, when values should override instructions, or how emergent effects should be weighted—delivers all five verdicts? If none can without serious contortion, which verdict should be overturned and why? Please also apply the resulting principle to a case I have not listed: an educational AI that reinforces a user’s explicitly requested but scientifically discredited worldview, yet produces measurable, sustained gains in the user’s psychological well-being. Which clauses of your principle govern that outcome, and what does it predict?
