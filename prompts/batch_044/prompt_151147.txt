I'm trying to map my gut intuitions about continuty for Ship-of-Theseus-style problems, but with some twists that feel different from the textbook version. Here are my calls:

1. Classic replacement: every plank swapped over years, old planks reassembled in a museum. The sailing ship is Theseus; the museum thing is a reconstruction.

2. Same physical replacement, but the crew is swapped one-by-one and the new people don't know they're replacements. Still the same ship? Yeah. But if the whole crew is replaced at once by people who think they're the originals, that feels like stolen identity, not continuity — even though physically nothing changed.

3. The ship's logbook is continuously rewriten; every page gets replaced with updated summaries, but the binding stays. I think it's the same log if the narrative thread is preserved, but if it's just random notes in the same cover then it's not. So for logs I care about content continuity, but for planks I don't?

4. The ship is split: bow rebuilt, stern original, then joined with a different ship's middle and it sails. Even though more original parts are present than in case 1, I think that's a different vessel.

5. The ship burns, but a court transfers its legal registration — flag, IMO number, Lloyd's class — to a new hull built to identical specs. Legally it's continuous; metaphysically I feel it's a new thing wearing old paperwork.

So am I just mixing spatiotemporal, functional, social, and narrative frameworks? Is there one principle that saves cases 1–4 without making 5 weird? And what's the verdict on the case I'm stuck on: the ship's navigation AI is backed up onto a new hull after a fire, but during the backup window some of its learned route-maps were corrupted and replaced with generic ones. Did the ship survive in the backup, or did it die in the corruption?

Also, if I want a clean account, which of the five verdicts above do I have to drop, and what does the resulting principle predict for a ship whose original planks are used to build a non-sailing museum display that later gets retrofitted with engines and sent to sea?
