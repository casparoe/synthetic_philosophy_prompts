As a novelist constructing the philosophical spine of a dialogue between two critics of fiction, draft the joint statement that a narrative realist and a narrative constructivist could both sign after an adversarial collaboration on whether thematic universals—such as tragic irony or narrative melancholy—possess real ontological status or exist only as relations between textual structures and interpretive practices. The realist holds that a novel can correctly or incorrectly instantiate an abstract thematic universal independent of any single reader; the constructivist holds that such qualities are generated entirely within the interaction of text and audience, with no corresponding abstract object. Produce: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims, including what any adequate account of thematic quality must explain, the permissible role of authorial intent, and the conditions under which two culturally distant novels genuinely share the same quality; (2) a disagreements table stating each camp’s precise position in wording they themselves would accept; (3) the cruxes—specifically whether intersubjective agreement about a thematic quality requires a shared abstract object, and whether a novel widely misread thematically is defective in itself or only in its reception; and (4) for each crux, one concrete literary case both sides accept as bearing on it (for instance, whether two independent traditions recognizing an identical melancholy in structurally different narratives supports realism), with each side’s predicted interpretation recorded in advance. Keep the document formal, avoid all bibliographic references or citations, and build the positions from first principles without invoking obscure named doctrines.
