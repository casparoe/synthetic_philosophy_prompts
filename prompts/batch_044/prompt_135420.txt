I've been mulling over pure time preference since a thread on LessWrong about longtermist grantmaking, and I've come to hold the pretty strong view that there's literally no rational justification for discounting future welfare just because it's in the future—like, if you wouldn't care less about a suffering person just because they're a thousand miles away, why care less about yourself in ten years? I know the standard move is to say it's all risk and epistimic uncertainty in disguies, but I think that's a cop-out; I want the view that zero pure discounting is correct even for a perfectly rational agent with perfect foresight and no death risk. To make this less generic, I've been playing with a weird framing: what if the argument from diachronic consistency actually fails under self-locating uncertainty, where your future self is kind of like a probabilistic copy rather than a guaranteed continuation? Even then, I can't see why temporal location should matter.

So here's my ask: what would actually count against this? I'm not looking for the usual "people feel closer to now" psychology—I'm asking for the defeaters that should move someone who thinks pure time preference is conceptually on par with spatial discrimination. Are there formal results in decision theory (maybe involving infinite utility streams or St. Petersburg-style paradoxes) that definately require a non-zero pure rate to avoid paradox? Could a metaphysical view like presentism or the open future ground a principled difference without collapsing into egoism? And has the anti-preference position been qualified so much—"all discounting is really about risk"—that it's become unfalsifiable, and is that a problem? I'd like them ranked from fatal to merely awkward, plus an honest take on whether defenders of pure preference have any arguments that don't just reduce to "but I feel it." Also, if there's a hidden commitment in my view—say, that it forces an implausible level of present sacrifice for distant future goods—that counts too. I want the map of where this position is actually exposed, not a survey of what economists usually say.
