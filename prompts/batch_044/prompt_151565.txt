my roommate summed up the corruption literature like this (he read it somewhere, idk): corruption isnt just breaking rules, its a mismatch between two economies of obligation. There's "gift corruption," where you exchange goods inside a legit social bond and create reciprocal debt, and "extraction corruption," where you take resources and destroy the bond's legitimacy. Clientelism is basically gift corruption scaled up—the patron gives protection, the client gives loyalty, and neither thinks they broke anything because they're inside a moral economy of reciprocity. Western anti-bribery law frames all of this as extraction, which is why it fails in places where clientelism is just normal politics. So Western standards arent universal; they're one economy's rules forced onto another.
