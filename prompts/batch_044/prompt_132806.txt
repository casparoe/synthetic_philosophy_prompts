I've come around to this sphexishness-adjacent view about free will: that what we call flexible agency in humans is really just mechanical rigidity with extra recusive monitoring loops stacked on top, not a qualitative break. So a sphex wasp drilling into a burrow in a loop and a person deciding to change careers are both running stereotyped programs — the human version just has more nested checkpoints that can halt or reroute, but the core is still sphex-like rigidity. The view implies that if you damage or fatigue those monitoring loops, behavior shouldn't become randomly open-ended; it should collapse back into simpler, more predictable loops. What would count against it? I want the defeaters ranked — fatal first, then awkward — including empirical results on executive dysfunction, arguments that true openness exists (not just hidden loops), and whether cognitive science's hierarchical control models actually support nesting or undermine it. I also want to know if the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability: can any apparently free choice be retrofitted as a loop? Is that a problem for a view like this? And what would defenders of the standard anti-sphex view — that flexibility is genuinely non-mechanical — concede as counting against them?
