Quick question from someone who reads too much history of science. In arguments about Newton/Leibniz or Darwin/Wallace I keep noticing the same slide: people say two independant discoveries prove the idea was in the air, but then they quietly shift to saying the exact same formulation was inevitable and therefore who gets credit is basically fungible. It's not just discovery was ripe — it's using independence to smuggle in that the discoverer doesn't matter at all, even when the two versions actually differ in notation or framing. Is there an established term for this specific conflation? If there is, how does it differ from ordinary arguments about inevitability? If not, can you coin one with clear boundaries — like, what would have to be true for it NOT to apply? Keep your answer to one paragraph, max about 180 words.
