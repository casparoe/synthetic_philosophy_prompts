For a graduate seminar on philosophy of mind and artificial cognition, draft a single take-home essay question on which computations matter morally and where the boundaries of sentience lie. Frame the question around this constructed contrast: System R is a recursive self-modifying network that updates its internal weights via nested error-correction signals but has no sensory modalities and no global broadcast mechanism; System S is a feedforward architecture that detects thermal gradients and initiates avoidance behavior through representations that carry no indexical or self-referential content. Ask students to evaluate whether either system merits moral consideration if sentience requires (i) integrated information processing, (ii) a global workspace, or (iii) only a specific causal-functional profile, and to defend a principled boundary that avoids both substrate chauvinism and unrestricted pancomputationalism. Provide a model answer of approximately 650 words that takes a definite stance and addresses all three conditions. Include a marking scheme built around four must-hit analytical moves, each worth 5 marks (total 20), with a one-line note for each on the typical way candidates miss it. The question should be unanswerable by summarizing standard literature and should force explicit reasoning about how computation is individuated for moral attribution.
