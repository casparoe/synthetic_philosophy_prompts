Okay so my study partner cooked up this argument about Hacking-style looping in psych classification and now I'm stuck. (1) Psychiatric categories are interactive kinds: labeling someone schizofrenic changes how they act and how others treat them. (2) There's also a second loop — clinicians revise DSM criterias over time to match the behaviors they're actually seeing in diagnosed patients, so the taxonomy chases its own tail. (3) If both loops run together with no outside anchor, then the disorder just is the stable equilibrium of label-behavior-criterion adjustments. (4) So schizophrenia exists only as long as that particular loop is running; if we stopped using the category the corresponding cluster of behaviors and social responses would dissolve or reconfigure. (5) That means the disorder is ontologically dependant on the classification system, not just causally affected by it. (6) But then asking whether someone really has schizophrenia outside the system is like asking whether a chess piece is really a bishop outside the rules of chess. (7) This applies to every psychiatric diagnosis, so none of them track mind-independant conditions. (8) Worse, psychiatrists can never be mistaken about a diagnosis, because any misclassification just starts a new loop that eventually gets incorporated into the criterias. (9) So looping effects don't just complicate psychiatry — they erase the distinction between discovery and invention entirely.

I think (1) and (2) are basically right and I don't want to give them up. But (8) feels like a reductio and I don't want to end up saying psychiatry is just fiction. Where does the slip happen? Is it between (3) and (4), or is (5) the bad step? And if I reject (5), am I stuck admitting that mental disorders are just like chemical elements — indifferent kinds that don't care what we call them? That seems wrong too but maybe less wrong.
