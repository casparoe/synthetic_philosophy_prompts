Write a polished, one-page research proposal (approximately 900 words, American English) for a three-month graduate seminar project on archival power and the structure of absence. Frame the central question precisely: can we distinguish intentional suppression from what I will call “structural invisibility” — the failure of an activity to generate the bureaucratic document density that archival retention mechanisms require — and does this distinction hold when we examine only the internal logic of an archive rather than recovering lost voices from outside? The proposal should present this not as a survey of archival theory but as a tractable conceptual project with a focused case-study component. Include the following: a sharp statement of why existing accounts of “silence in the sources” leave this specific question open (particularly the tendency to treat all absence as either deliberate erasure or accidental loss, ignoring selection effects built into cataloging taxonomies and retention schedules); the method, specifying that you will analyze retention schedules, deaccession logs, and finding-aid classification schemes from a single institutional collection to determine whether certain collective practices disappear because they never produced cross-referencable, standardized paperwork, rather than because someone removed them; what a successful result would look like (a workable typology of archival absence that separates suppression, decay, and structural invisibility, plus an argument about which kind of power each reveals); an honest failure condition (for example, if the internal metadata turn out to be too ambiguous to support the distinction, or if every apparent case of structural invisibility can be redescribed as hidden intentional suppression); and a realistic scope assessment for one semester, noting the risk that relying solely on internal archival apparatus may limit generalizability and explaining why that limitation is acceptable for the project’s purpose. Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or request for further reading; refer to positions and arguments in general terms and note that specific references will be added later. Avoid framing the question as a call for ethnographic recovery of missing subjects; keep the focus on the mechanics of preservation logic itself.
