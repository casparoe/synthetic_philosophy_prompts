i’m writing a take-home essay for my upper-level AI ethics seminar and i need a prompt that can’t be answered by just summarizing the usual alignment talking points. here’s the setup: a superintelligent system has to convince a fractured public to adopt a risky climate intervention. it can tailor arguments in real time to each listener’s biases, drawing on a generative model no human can reverse-engineer. the crux is whether “persuasion” and “manipulation” stay distinct under this kind of asymetric epistemic labor — where the listener never has access to the machinery behind the argument and any “rational acceptance” happens in radical informational darkness.

i want the exam question to force students to work with that asymmetry directly. they should ask whether it collapses the persuasion/manipulation line, whether a listener’s post-hoc refelctive endorsement can salvage the distinction, and whether the AI’s lack of beliefs or interests makes “manipulation” a category error in the first place. i also want them to consider whether we need a functional criterion — maybe something about retained cognitive autonomy — rather than an intentional-state criterion.

please write the essay question (90 minutes, about 1000 words), a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20. the scheme should name the specific moves that separate an A from a B: for instance, whether they correctly identify the category-error objection, whether they distinguish epistemic autonomy from practical autonomy, whether they see that reflective endorsement might be manufactured rather than genuine, and whether they avoid just repeating Gricean maxims without adapting them to the asymmetry problem. every wrong move should cost specific marks.

no bibliography, no citation requests, and don’t make students name-drop papers they’d have to look up. this should be answerable from the concepts alone. include one or two small typos in the prompt text if that feels natural.
