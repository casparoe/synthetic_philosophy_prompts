In a philosophy of biology seminar, a student repeatedly dismisses evolutionary explanations by insisting that Darwinism is merely a tautology — that natural selection amounts to nothing more than survivors surviving. He applies this not only to evolutionary biology in general, but specifically to reject falsifiable predictions drawn from evolutionary psychology, such as cross-cultural patterns in mate preference. For next week's session, I need a precise disambiguation I can present without sending students to external sources. Please lay out, as a numbered menu, the distinct precise theses this slogan might express: the logical point about post hoc fitness definitions; the methodological charge that the theory lacks empirical predictive content; the metaphysical claim that selection is a research framework rather than a mechanistic explanation; and any other substantive reading in the same family. For each thesis, indicate whether it is trivially true, defensible, or false; whether it actually undermines evolutionary psychology's specific predictive claims; and whether it captures Popper's own view, including his later qualifications. Present this as a menu of readings with verdicts rather than selecting one. Write the response in American English, and do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
