Read Orwell’s 1984 through the philosophical debate over whether history is written by the victors, paying special attention to the distinction between epistemic control of archives and a stronger ontological claim that present power literally constitutes the past. Focus on Winston’s work altering records at the Ministry of Truth, O’Brien’s interrogation about whether the past survives only in present documents and in human memories, and Winston’s final collapse into accepting the Party’s reality. Does the novel’s horror depend on a realist commitment that there is an objective past being distorted, or can it be read as endorsing a constructivism in which no independent past exists? Identify where Orwell takes a philosophical position, where he merely dramatizes a psychological mechanism of domination, and whether the text resists a purely relativist interpretation. Conclude by saying what the novel adds to the discussion of historical relativism and narrative power that abstract argument alone does not capture.
