I'd like a consistency audit of my philosophical profile on human extinction, meaning, and intergenerational value, particularly in light of Scheffler's claim that much of present meaning depends on an expected human future rather than personal survival. Here is my position:

(1) I accept the core idea that present lives derive significant meaning from the expectation of a continuing human future — a kind of collective afterlife — and that biological extinction would therefore harm present people in a way that goes beyond the loss of future lives.

(2) I think this condition is satisfied not only by biological descendants but also by functionally equivalent successors, such as advanced artificial minds or deliberately engineered beings that preserve our projects and values, so that the extinction of Homo sapiens need not destroy present meaning.

(3) I am a project-dependent realist about meaning: I believe long-term collective projects genuinely constitute reasons for action, but only because and insofar as conscious beings endorse them; there is no mind-independent value in the projects themselves.

(4) From (2) and (3), I conclude that if biological extinction is unavoidable, we are morally required to create such successors, even at considerable present cost, because doing so preserves the source of present meaning.

(5) I also endorse a strong precautionary stance toward existential risk: we ought to avoid actions that risk human extinction, even under uncertainty, because the loss of this collective afterlife represents an irreversible and poorly quantified harm.

Please examine whether these commitments form a coherent package. Specifically: does (2) conflict with (1) if the psychological mechanism of meaning-dependence requires belief in biological continuity? Does (3) undermine the normative urgency of (4) by reducing project-value to contingent endorsement? Does (5) sit uneasily with (4), since actively replacing humanity with successors might itself be treated as an existential risk? Rank the tensions by severity, identify the least costly revision, and say whether a single change could resolve the most conflicts. Please answer in American English.
