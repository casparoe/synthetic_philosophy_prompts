hey so im helping my teacher grade practice essays for our phil elective and im stuck on this one. the prompt was: Sartre says humans are radically free and that bad faith is self-deception about that freedom, but he also talks about facticity—the parts of our situation we didnt choose. explain how radical freedom and facticity fit together, then apply it to a student who says they have no choice but to take AP Calc because their counselor put them in it. be sure to address both denying freedom and denying facticity.

heres the students answer:

"Sartre thinks radical freedom means we can always choose our response to any situation, even if we cant change the situation itself. Facticity covers the given stuff—our body, past, and the roles we find ourselves in. Bad faith is when we lie to ourselves by denying our freedom, but it can also happen if we deny our facticity and pretend we have no limits at all. In the AP Calc case, the student is partly in bad faith because they claim they have zero choice, which denies their radical freedom to drop the class or fail on purpose. But theyre not denying facticity—instead theyre over-emphasizing the counselors power as a fixed fact, so its only half bad faith. Also Sartre talks about the Look, where other people turn you into an object. The counselor is probably looking at them as just a college-app number, and that objectification is what really makes them feel trapped. Still, Sartre would say they can always reject that role."
