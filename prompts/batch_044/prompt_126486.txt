So I've been dealing with some heavy stuff lately — my dad got a rough diagnosis a few months back and suddenly I'm watching these really bleak movies where everyone dies and nothing gets resolved, and I can't figure out why I keep doing it. My roommate says I'm just "processing," my sister says it's catharsis, but both feel like cop-outs that don't actually explain the weirdness. I tried to formalize the problem and now I've walked myself into an argument that seems to prove either tragedy is just fancy entertainment with extra steps, or we're all being irrational about art, and I can't find the exit.

Here it is: (1) Rational agents avoid experiences that are net harmful unless they provide a compensating benefit that can't be recieved elsewhere. (2) Tragedy is net harmful — the grief, dread, and moral horror it induces aren't canceled out by "aesthetic pleasure," they're just layered on top, so the overall affective load is negative. (3) The usual compensating benefits — catharsis, emotional education, moral insight — aren't unique to tragedy; you can get catharsis from exercise or therapy, and moral insight from philosophy books without sitting through two hours of suffering. (4) So tragedy offers net harm with fully replaceable benefits. (5) Therefore rational agents should avoid it. (6) But people actively seek tragedy, especially when they're already in pain — like me right now. (7) So either people are systematically irrational, or tragedy isn't actually net harmful. (8) If it's not net harmful, then the grief it produces must be "quasi-grief" — simulated affect rather than real grief. (9) But if the grief is only simulated, then tragedy can't genuinely educate us about real suffering or build real empathy, since we're never actually confronting pain. (10) So we end up with three awful options: tragedy is irrational to pursue, or it's no better than a rollercoaster (calibrated pain-plus-pleasure entertainment) and our high-cultural reverence is bogus, or it doesn't really move us at all. I feel like there's a hidden slide between (7) and (8) — maybe rejecting that tragedy hurts doesn't force me to say the grief is fake? — but I can't name it. Which step should I give up, and what does giving it up cost? I'd really like to keep (1) since it feels like basic decision theory, but I'm open to losing (3) if that's where the rot is.
