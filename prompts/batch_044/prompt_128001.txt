There's this stale debate about whether relying on LLMs for reasoning erodes human "understanding"—the calculator anology gets thrown around and then everyone just repeats themselves. I think part of the stall is that people smuggle in different theories of understanding (causal-process vs grasp-of-relations) without admitting it, but I also want to push a weirder framing: what if the issue isn't erosion at all but a shift toward "distributed understanding" where the human is just one node in the chain, not the owner of the belief? Is the disagreement actually about ownership of cognitive states rather than about understanding per se? Diagnose the stall—verbal, substantive, hidden empirical, bedrock intuition?—and say wether this distributed-node framing dissolves anything or just renames the problem. And be honest: what would count as progress, a conceptual distinction, an empirical study on cognitive offloading, or is it basically unresolvable?
