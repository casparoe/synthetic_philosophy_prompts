I just spent the better part of an evening arguing with a friend about whether Hofstadter’s strange-loop model of the self actually dissolves personal identity or merely relocates it into a symbol. We kept circling back to the same tension: if “I” is nothing but a self-referential pattern with no central owner, then any system that maintains that loop—biological, artificial, or hybrid—should qualify, yet something still feels structurally different about being the loop from the inside. Rather than continuing in circles, I want to see the logic pushed to an absurd, deadpan extreme. Write a parody of a corporate end-user license agreement—approximately 700 words, formatted as numbered legal clauses—for a fictional subscription service called “StrangeLoop™ Identity Maintenance,” which sells continuity of the self-referential symbol “I.” The agreement should quietly embed the core conceptual claims: that the self is a pattern rather than a substance; that the symbol “I” derives its force from recursive reference rather than from a soul or homunculus; and that personal continuity is a matter of causal-symbolic linkage rather than identity of atoms. To keep this from being a generic pop-science rehash, inject two specific conceptual twists that are not standard summaries of Hofstadter. First, include a “Symbolic Decoherence Clause” addressing what happens when the loop begins referencing external databases faster than it can integrate them, causing the “I” to fragment into competing sub-symbols that each claim primary continuity. Second, include a “Gödelian Exemption” allowing the symbol to assert self-descriptive propositions that the platform’s verification layer can neither prove nor disprove, effectively letting the user “escape” the system’s ontology from within. The satire should land the standard objections—who licenses the symbol, what happens when two loops claim the same “I,” whether subscription lapse counts as death—without ever stating them outright. Keep the tone relentlessly corporate and legalistic; a reader familiar with the strange-loop literature should recognize that every clause exaggerates a real feature of the view, and the piece should remain legible to someone who has never encountered the concept. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or references to specific page numbers or editions.
