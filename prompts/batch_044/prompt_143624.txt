ok so i'm reading darwin's descent of man for a philosophy of bio class and i keep noticing this pattern that feels like it should have a name but i dont know it. darwin explains religious belief by saying our brains evolved agent-detection and causal reasoning, so belief in god is basically a spandrel - the evolutionary origin is supposed to undercut the belief. but then with morality he says the moral sense also evolved out of social instincts, yet he doesnt treat that origin as undermining morality at all. in fact he uses moral standards - sympathy should expand, cruelty is wrong - to judge evolutionary outcomes, almost like morality stands outside the evolutionary story and grades it. so he's doing evolutionary debunking on religion but shielding morality, even though both have basically the same kind of evolutionary explanation.

is there a specific term for this asymmetrical move, where you cite evolutionary origins to dismiss one domain but protect another domain with identical origins? i know it's not just the genetic fallacy, and it's not exactly special pleading because there might be a principled reason origins affect belief differently in different domains. but i want to know if philosophers of science or metaethics have named this specific pattern, and if not, how you'd draw its edges - like is it always inconsistent, or can there be real distinctions between how origins bear on descriptive vs normative claims? i really just want the carving, not just a bare label: what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where it stops being the same thing.
