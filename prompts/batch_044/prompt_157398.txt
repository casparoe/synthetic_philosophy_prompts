I'm looking at populism's claim that the leader just expresses 'the will of the people.' The usual critique is that this ignores pluralism—there's no single will, just competing interests. But I think there's a deeper problem: what I'm calling the 'recursive mandate.' Populists treat the will as already there, waiting to be discovered, but the mechanisms they use to reveal it—mass rallies, social-media sentiment framing, binary polling—are also what construct the people as a unified subject in the first place. So the mandate is both cause and effect of the representation. The leader doesn't just reflect the will; the will only becomes visible through the leader's framing, yet the framing claims to be purely transparent.

This is different from normal representative democracy, where representatives have some autonomy, and from direct democracy, where aggregation is explicit and procedural. Populism denies both: it insists on immediate identity between leader and led, which hides the loop. The result is a self-fulfilling prophecy that denies its own prophecy-ness—like a mirror that claims it's just showing you your face, but it's actually the only mirror in the room.
