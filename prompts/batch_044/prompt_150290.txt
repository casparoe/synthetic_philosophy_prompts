Here is an argument I have been turning over since a seminar on epistemic rationality, and it now strikes me as suspicious because its conclusion seems to collapse into skepticism about any large belief-set. (1) Consider an agent whose rational belief-set B contains N independent propositions, each supported at credence 0.99. (2) By the reasoning behind the lottery paradox, the agent should not accept the conjunction of all members of B, since the joint probability 0.99^N quickly falls below any plausible threshold for rational belief. (3) By the reasoning behind the preface paradox, the same agent can rationally accept each individual member of B while also accepting that B contains approximately N(1-0.99) errors—say, ten false beliefs if N is one thousand. (4) Now introduce a principle of epistemic risk budgeting: rationality requires that the agent distribute her total tolerated error budget of 0.01N across specific members of B, explicitly identifying which ten propositions are the likely failures. (5) If she refuses to allocate—maintaining uniform 0.99 credence in every member—then she knows that ten specific beliefs she actively holds are false, yet she assigns each of them near-certainty. (6) If she does allocate—downgrading ten specific propositions—then her remaining 990 beliefs are held at 0.99, but she knows her budget only covered ten errors, so she must recognize that some of the 990 are also false; iterating the allocation forces either an infinite regress of downgrading or an admission that rational specificity is impossible at scale. (7) Therefore either the lottery-paradox threshold against conjunction is wrong, or the preface-paradox tolerance for known error is incompatible with rational belief in any large, specific set of claims. Where does this go wrong? I am reluctant to surrender (3), since the preface intuition seems robust, but I also want to preserve the core idea in (2) that rational belief should not survive arbitrary conjunction. Which step is the weakest, and what does giving it up cost?
