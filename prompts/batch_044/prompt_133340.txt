I'm preparing a consistency audit for a decision-theory reading group before presenting my position. Here is my current package of commitments on rationality, commitment, and temporal evaluation:

(a) In Parfit's hitchhiker case, rationality requires paying the driver upon arrival, even though at that point there is no remaining causal leverage over whether one received the ride.

(b) In Newcomb's problem with a predictor of approximately 99% accuracy, one-boxing is the rational choice.

(c) Rational evaluation is diachronic: a decision should be assessed not as an isolated node but as part of a temporally extended strategy that the agent adopted and has not explicitly revoked.

(d) In a counterfactual mugging scenario—where a predictor has already acted on the basis of one's anticipated choice, a coin has been flipped revealing the outcome, and the causal link between present payment and past prediction is broken—refusing to pay is rational.

(e) The normative status of a choice must be invariant to the temporal location at which the agent first contemplates the problem; there are no "discovery-time" effects on rational obligation.

(f) I hold that obligations should be indexed to an agent's "binding profile"—the set of commitments actively in force—rather than to present causal efficacy, and that such a profile is altered only by explicit revocation or structural impossibility, not by the mere dissolution of counterfactual dependence.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are in genuine tension (not merely surface-level conflict), which apparent conflicts dissolve once the role of the binding profile and diachronic evaluation are properly traced, and for each real tension what is the least costly revision? Order the tensions by severity, specify exactly which commitment must be modified or abandoned to restore consistency, and indicate whether the central fault lies in (d) pulling against (c) and (f), or in a different pairing.
