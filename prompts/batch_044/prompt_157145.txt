Question: Davidson’s radical interpretation requires simultaneous assignment of beliefs and meanings guided by the principle of charity, together with the triangulation condition linking speaker, interpreter, and shared world. A researcher claims that because triangulation demands interaction between two agents and a common environment, a lone human can never radically interpret an isolated AI lacking social interaction—only a community of observers could. Is the researcher right about interpreter multiplicity? And if the AI systematically outputs contradictions that seem to track feature-space regularities rather than propositional attitudes, may the interpreter treat them as performance errors rather than belief-contents under charity?

Student answer: Davidson’s view is holistic—belief and meaning are interdependent. Charity tells us to maximize agreement and logical consistency, so when the AI contradicts itself we should classify that as performance error, analogous to the Chomskyan competence/performance distinction, rather than attributing irrational beliefs. Triangulation involves two agents plus a world, but it does not require more than one interpreter; one human and the AI interacting with shared stimuli are enough. So the researcher is wrong about needing a community. This is essentially Quine’s point about translation indeterminacy: there are multiple acceptable manuals, and we pick the one that minimizes contradiction. Hence a single observer can interpret the isolated AI by applying charity and filtering out noise.
