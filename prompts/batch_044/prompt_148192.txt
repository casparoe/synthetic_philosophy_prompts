ok so I'm prepping for a philosophy of mind / value seminar and I want to sanity-check my take on pain before I open my mouth. I was chatting with my roommate about those weird clinical cases where people say "it hurts" but don't seem to care at all - pain asymbolia, I think it's called? - and I realized my commitments on this might not actually hang together.

Here's roughly where I've landed: (1) I'm a non-reductive physicalist - phenomenal properties are real but depend on brain stuff, not reducible to it. (2) Pain asymbolia is a real neurological dissociation, not just stoicism or misreporting; the sensory and affective pathways genuinely split. (3) The badness of pain is entirely in the negative affective valence; bare nociception is value-neutral. (4) So an asymbolia patient with intact reports but zero distress isn't being intrinsically harmed by the injury. (5) But I also think harm can be unconscious - like if you're anesthetized and someone violates your autonomy, that's real harm even though you don't feel it. (6) I think pain is special though: it's the paradigm of direct, non-derivative badness, precisely because it's conscious and felt. (7) I also think our access to others' pain is purely inferential and behavioral; we should be skeptical about declaring we've "proven" they lack hidden affect. (8) Despite (4), I still think we should treat them with full analgesic care, but that's epistemic humility, not hidden suffering. (9) And I think pain reports have a performative dimension - they're partly requests for help, not pure descriptions - which makes reading them in clinical settings even trickier.

Do these hang together? I'm especially worried about whether (3)-(4) conflict with (5)-(6) over what counts as direct harm, and whether (1) creates tension with treating phenomenal valence as the sole bearer of value. Is there also an apparent tension between (7) and (2) that actually dissolves once you seperate neurological evidence from first-person access? I want an audit: rank the real tensions by severity, say what'd have to give in each case, identify any fake tensions, and tell me which single revision cleans up the most mess. Don't need citations or reading lists - I just want to know if I'm smuggling in something I don't actually believe.
