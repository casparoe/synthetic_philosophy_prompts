Argue that illusionism about phenomenal consciousness is best understood not as an introspective error about real qualia but as a grammatical misplacement: the formal, indexical conditions that structure subjective representation are systematically treated as intrinsic qualitative properties of mental states. Deliver your argument in exactly five numbered points, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Defend the claim that this misplacement is structurally unavoidable rather than correctable by improved observation; explain why opponents confuse representational form with phenomenal content; show how the view accommodates the reality of subjective experience without conceding irreducible qualia; address what it says about reports of supposedly ineffable phenomenal properties; and identify one conceptual consequence for theories that ground first-person authority in direct access to intrinsic mental features.
