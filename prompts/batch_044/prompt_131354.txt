I am preparing interview questions for a podcast colloquium with a philosopher who defends a revised version of Dennett's four-way taxonomy of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. Her specific contribution is the claim that engineered cognitive systems, such as large-scale language models, constitute a distinct fifth kind she terms 'design-creatures': entities whose representational architectures are shaped by intentional engineering rather than evolutionary selection or individual trial-and-error learning, and which she argues perform Popperian-style internal selection without possessing the social-linguistic scaffolding that Dennett takes to define Gregorian minds. Draft eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her to restate the taxonomy. Include at least one question that presses whether design-creatures genuinely lack Gregorian social scaffolding; one that asks how she distinguishes engineered internal selection from Skinnerian conditioning by reward; one that probes whether her revision makes the original evolutionary ordering normative rather than merely descriptive; and, for each of these three, provide a follow-up keyed to her most likely reply—namely, that engineering can shortcut evolutionary history while preserving functional architecture, and that normativity properly enters only at the Gregorian level. Keep the questions conceptually focused; avoid biography, generic philosophy prompts, and any request for citations or reading lists.
