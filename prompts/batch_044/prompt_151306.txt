Proponents of evolutionary debunking arguments in metaethics often analogize moral cognition to a smoke detector calibrated for extreme risk-aversion: it evolved to minimize false negatives (missing a real threat) at the cost of frequent false positives (reacting to harmless stimuli), so its outputs track reproductive fitness rather than objective fire-risk. The analogy is then extended to moral belief—our moral “alarms” may track social coordination rather than mind-independent moral facts. Audit this analogy: provide an explicit correspondence table (calibration target, false-positive rate, evolutionary pressure, objective standard in each domain); identify where the mapping breaks, particularly whether moral cognition admits of an independent calibration standard analogous to actual combustion; determine whether the analogy silently imports a particular metaethical framework (e.g., robust moral realism) as the “objective danger” pole; and assess whether the debunking conclusion survives once these disanalogies are made explicit.
