As a novelist composing a philosophical chapter, write a short story that dramatizes the cognitive penetration of perception through an unacknowledged social lens. Center the narrative on a single evening: a woman prepares for a dinner party, studies her reflection in a hallway mirror, and then purchases figs from a street vendor. In each scene, her visual experience of other people—the sharpness of a guest's features, the warmth in her own reflected expression, the vendor's posture—should be subtly reshaped by her unexamined class assumptions, without her recognizing the distortion. Avoid clinical examples, psychological citations, or expository summary; let the philosophical claim emerge solely through precise image, dialogue, and a final moment of silent recognition that her sight was never neutral. Keep the tone formally literary and the concept present only in the architecture of the scenes.
