I am preparing a seminar handout on the normative architecture of labour and rest, and I want a decision-tree taxonomy — built from logical distinctions rather than from a list of named authors — that sorts a reader’s actual commitments about work, leisure, and entitlement into one of several positions. The root scenario is concrete: a jurisdiction is debating whether to legislate a thirty-five-hour working week, whether to classify death from overwork (karoshi) as employer liability, worker autonomy, or structural inevitability, and whether leisure should be treated as a distributive good, a recovery mechanism, or a negative liberty only. Each branching node should be phrased as a claim or case that a student could answer without knowing which view it leads to — for example, whether a worker who voluntarily exceeds safe hours forfeits a claim against the employer, or whether capping hours improves welfare even when it reduces total output. The leaves must include at least: a strict Protestant-work-ethic position that denies leisure any moral entitlement and treats overwork as virtuous; a social-democratic regulatory view anchored in the thirty-five-hour limit; a libertarian autonomy view that treats karoshi as a self-regarding risk; a distributive-justice view that counts leisure hours among primary goods; and at least one unnamed combination that the logical space permits but that lacks defenders — for which you should briefly diagnose whether the gap stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The distinctions that matter for my purpose are whether leisure is a positive entitlement or merely freedom from interference, whether the justification for limiting work appeals to welfare, equality, or autonomy, and whether the state’s proper role is protective, distributive, or absent. Please flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely cannot separate — for instance, positions that agree on every practical case but diverge only in rhetoric — rather than papering over them. The audience knows basic normative theory but should navigate without recalling specific texts or statutes, so do not ask for bibliographies, named theorists, or citations, and write the prompt in British English with enough framing to show why the taxonomy is being constructed.
