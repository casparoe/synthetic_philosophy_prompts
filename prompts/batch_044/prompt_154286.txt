I am trying to clarify my position on collective memory, historical injustice, and the ethics of remembrance, and I want a consistency audit before presenting it in a seminar. Here is roughly where I have landed.

(1) I accept a broadly Margalit-style view: societies that have perpetrated or tolerated grave systemic injustice have a genuine moral duty to remember the victims, grounded in solidarity with both the dead and the living rather than in mere prudence. This is a present-tense obligation, not only an instrumental safeguard against repetition.

(2) I am also convinced by Rieff-style worries: state-mandated remembrance often produces memory fatigue, can retraumatize survivors, and violates what I will call mnemonic autonomy—the right of individuals and affected communities to determine whether, how, and for how long they carry the past. There is a real right to be forgotten that competes with collective remembrance.

(3) I think forgiveness is a moral virtue, and that it requires not erasure but mnemonic bracketing: the wrong must remain acknowledged, yet it is structurally deprived of present moral authority over the relationship. The past is not denied; it is sidelined from active governance of current obligations.

(4) I hold that democratic legitimacy requires the state to remain neutral among competing interpretations of history. The state should maintain factual archives, protect against denialism, and secure access to evidence, but it should not prescribe emotional practices, commemorative curricula, or national monuments.

(5) I believe local, civil-society commemorative rituals are morally superior to centralized state monuments because they avoid the spectacle of state-managed grief and allow survivors and affected communities to control the terms, intensity, and duration of remembrance.

My question is not whether these commitments are true, but whether they hang together as a package. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled with minimal cost, and what is the least costly single revision that would restore coherence? Please rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging.
