I'm giving a talk thursday in our AI-philosophy working group and I need a prep sheet for the q&a. My thesis is that HCH — that idea where you factor cognition across copies that only communicate through answers, like in scalable oversight setups — gets misclassifed as an idealization of group deliberation. I think it's actually closer to an idealization of one person's reasoning stretched across time, with each temporal slice treated as an independent node that only gets the previous output. Real committees break the independance condition constantly: they talk back, revise shared premises, build social common ground. So HCH doesn't capture collective reasoning well at all, but it might capture diachronic solo reasoning if we stop treating the "copies" as spatial.

The audience is eight people: three alignment folks who've actually used HCH-style framing for oversight, two philosophers of mind who know extended cognition but not the tech details, and three decision theorists who will zero in on information flow and common-knowledge problems.
