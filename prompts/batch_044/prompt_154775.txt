hey i've been chewing on this argument about teleosemantics and i think it proves way too much but i can't see the exit. here's the chain:

(1) on millikan-style biosemantics, a representation's content is fixed by what its producing mechanism was selected for indicating.
(2) if a mechanism was selected for tracking predators, then whenever it fires — even if there's no predator — its content is still "predator present" because that's its proper function.
(3) now take a mutant or an engineered system with a physically identical mechanism but no evolutionary history. by (1) it has no content at all, or at least not the same content.
(4) so evolutionary history alone determines content; physical structure and current causal role don't.
(5) but then any mental state in me that uses a co-opted or derived mechanism — like using my temperature-tracking circuitry to think about stock prices — actually has content about temperature, not stocks, regardless of what i intend.
(6) that means first-person authority about what i'm thinking is systematically wrong, and cognitive scientists can't study mental content without doing evolutionary genealogy for every subject.
(7) since that collapses psychology into evolutionary biology and makes self-knowledge impossible, teleosemantics is self-undermining.
(8) therefore we should reject (1).

where does this slip? i feel like step (5) might be smuggling in something, but i also worry the argument is actually valid and the cost is just biting the bullet on first-person authority. please seperate what's actually settled in the literature from what's still genuinely contested among people who actually work on this — don't present one side as if it's the consensus. and if there's a typo in my reasoning above just tell me, i wrote this late and my brain is foggy.
