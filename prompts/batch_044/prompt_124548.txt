"The humean tradition, from Hume through Ayer to Lewis, treats laws as regularities rather than governing rules. Lewis's best-systems account improves on naive regularity theory by saying laws are the generalizations that belong to the axiomatic system describing the world with the best balance of simplicity and strength. This distinguishes laws from accidental generalizations, since accidents are left out of the best system. Anti-humeans like Armstrong reject this and say laws are external relations of necessitation, N, holding between universals. These N-relations genuinely govern instances and therefore ground counterfactuals. Dispositional essentialists such as Bird go further and locate governance in the essential natures of properties like charge or mass. So the humean can reject governing relations, but she pays a price: lawhood depends on the global mosaic of particular facts, which reverses the explanatory order — we want laws to explain the mosaic, not for the mosaic to determine what counts as law."
