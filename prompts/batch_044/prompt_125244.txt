I'm a university student in a philosophy of social science course, and I'm trying to build a study aid that sorts positions on social construction by their answers to concrete cases rather than by the labels people choose. Please construct a branching taxonomy — a decision tree with named or clearly described leaves — for how race, gender, and monetary kinds are constructed, or not. Start with this diagnostic case: imagine a physical banknote that was legal tender in a sovereign state which dissolved about thirty years ago; today it circulates only among collectors with no institutional backing or legal status. Is it still money? From that node, branch on three further questions: (1) does sustaining the kind require explicit collective agreement, or can distributed practices do the work alone? (2) does being classified in the kind create feedback loops that change members' properties or behavior? (3) do biological correlates play any causal role in the kind's social function, even if they are not definitional? Each leaf should state a position — for instance, institutional realism, interactive kinding, structural reproduction, eliminativism about race and gender, or pluralist naturalism — together with one sentence describing its most awkward commitment regarding either race, gender, or the defunct banknote. The tree should also flag any logical combinations that remain unoccupied — e.g., accepting feedback loops for gender but denying them for race — and briefly indicate whether such a gap looks incoherent or merely unexplored. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Write the answer in British English, though this prompt is written in American English.
