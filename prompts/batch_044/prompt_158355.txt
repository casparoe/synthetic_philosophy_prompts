Since I retired from biophysics I've been sitting in on philosophy seminars, and last week two grad students got into it over gradual mind uploading. (1) Lena said replace 1% of your neurons each year with silicon copies that match spike trains and synaptic weights exactly, and after 100 years you're still you -- what matters is causal-pattern continuity, not wetware. She noted our own molecules turnover constantly, so we're already gradual copies of our past selves. (2) Dave said that's the trap: you've slowly died and been replaced by a perfect fake that inherited your memories. There's no "travelling" subject, just adjacent copies. He compared it to a river that keeps its shape while all water is swapped for oil -- same shape, different substance entirely. (3) Lena replied Dave must be sneaking in a "further fact" like a Cartesian ego, because physical and psychological continuity are fully intact. Without that extra ingredient she doesn't see how you can claim it's a different person. (4) Dave said he doesn't need souls -- he just needs branching: if you copy the pattern at year 50 into two silicon bodies, both can't be the original you. So pattern-continuity alone can't ground uniqueness, which means biological process being uninterrupted must matter. (5) Lena said branching is a red herring since there's no split in linear replacement, and Dave is just doing vitalism with extra steps -- treating metabolism as magically essential when it's only one implementation of computation. They asked me to referee but I honestly couldn't. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each argument from weak to strong, say exactly where each speaker is right or wrong, point out what's missing on both sides (especially about whether there's even a fact of the matter here), and tell me whether gradual replacement preserves the original subject or just builds a very convincing successor. (P.S. I may have mispelled "synaptic" in my notes but you get the idea.)
