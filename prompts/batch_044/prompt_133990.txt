Write a deadpan parody of an internal corporate onboarding manual—approximately 650 words, formatted as numbered sections with subheadings—for a fictional management-consulting firm called Formalist Partners. The firm sells “Jump-Out-Of-The-System” (JOOTSing) workshops and claims that its organizational architecture is built directly on Douglas Hofstadter’s MIU formal system. The voice should be pure deadpan HR and management-consultant jargon: scalable, synergistic, vertically integrated, never winking.

The manual must treat the MU-puzzle as the company’s founding myth and operating model. Include an accurate presentation of the axiom (MI), the four transformation rules (if a string ends in I, append U; if a string begins with M, double the remainder after M; replace any III with U; drop any UU), and the proof of insolvability: the invariant that the number of I’s is never divisible by 3, which makes the target string MU (zero I’s) unreachable from within the system. The satire should target the belief that formal or bureaucratic systems can transcend themselves through “meta-level thinking” or “framework-breaking” leadership exercises. Show the firm attempting to market JOOTSing as a technique to overcome the invariant—perhaps through “scalable invariant audits” or “third-order reflection retreats”—while the manual accidentally reveals that every supposed “jump” merely reinstates the same invariant at a higher administrative level.

Requirements:
- Keep all logic correct; do not invent false rules or misstate the invariant.
- Do not use bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
- Do not quote Hofstadter or any other author verbatim; paraphrase without quotation marks if needed.
- Include at least one fictional employee testimonial (e.g., a senior consultant describing the attempt to apply Rule 3, III → U, to a meeting agenda, only to discover that the invariant governs PowerPoint slide counts as well).
- The irony must remain sustained and legible: a reader familiar with Gödel, Escher, Bach should recognize that the firm has misunderstood “jumping out” as an internal management hack rather than a logical-level shift requiring an external argument, and should find the misunderstanding fair rather than caricatured.
- The piece should be readable without specialist knowledge but should reward it: the jokes come from applying precise formal-system vocabulary to corporate processes, not from inventing absurd logic.
