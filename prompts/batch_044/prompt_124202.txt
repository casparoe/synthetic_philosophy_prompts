I am a software engineer building governance rules for a cooperative platform where members vote on policy changes, but an expert arbitration board may block outcomes that breach core norms. I need a reusable decision procedure—derived from the philosophical debate over judicial review and the counter-majoritarian difficulty—that lets the board determine, in structured sequence, whether vetoing a democratic vote is legitimate. Specify the tests in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to pass or fail each, the point where the procedure mandates "no veto," the point where it mandates "veto," and the points where the issue is one of values rather than rules and must be handed back to human judgment. Apply it to three proposals: (A) a vote to disable end-to-end encryption to ease content moderation; (B) a vote to redirect surplus maintenance budgets toward marketing incentives; (C) a vote to exclude a minority user group from revenue-sharing. For case A specifically, show how the procedure permits a veto that I—favoring minimal board intervention—would find objectionable. State clearly what categories of dispute the procedure is not designed to settle. Write no more than 400 words; do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or references to specific scholars by name.
