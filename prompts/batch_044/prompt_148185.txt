I am a high school senior enrolled in an advanced ethics seminar, and after studying the utility monster thought experiment in our unit on distributive justice, I have found both the standard utilitarian defense and the familiar rights-based rebuttal somewhat unsatisfying. For my capstone essay, I want to develop a more structurally specific objection that does not rely on generic appeals to fairness, but instead targets the internal coherence of aggregating the monster's welfare against that of everyone else.

Please argue for the following position: that if we stipulate a temporal asymmetry in the monster's phenomenology—specifically, that its supernormal satisfaction always occurs as brief, discrete flashes that do not overlap or integrate into sustained experiential streams—then accommodating the monster does not maximize aggregate welfare in any meaningful sense, because its utility is non-cumulative across time and therefore cannot be summed against the sustained, integrable welfare of ordinary agents. You should explain why a classical utilitarian might try to salvage the mandate by proposing a discounted temporal-integration function, then show why that salvage attempt fails when satisfaction lacks the minimal duration required for aggregation. Finally, address whether this reasoning implies that interpersonally comparable utility presupposes not only comparable intensity but comparable temporal architecture, and clarify what that meta-implication means for aggregate welfare maximization generally.
