hey, im on the clinical ethics and research review board at county general, and we have this proposal thats been stirring up debate. its a pilot called calibrated disclosure: attending physicians would have to include in every discharge summary a brief calibration statement whenever higher-order statistical evidence conflicts with their first-order clinical judgment. for example, if our internal data shows that docs in a certain experience bracket systematically overestimate recovery rates for patients from a specific income group, the physician has to note whether that data affects her call about this particular patient. the goal is to make bias visible.

a patient advocacy coalition filed an objection saying this forces epistemic akrasia on clinicians. they argue that if a doctor genuinely believes, based on direct exam, that patient X is stable, but the group-level stats say she probably overestimates stability for people like X, then requiring her to document and weight that higher-order evidence undermines her rational agency. they want us to reject the pilot outright.

our board meets next thursday. my co-chair asked me to draft the memo we'll circulate beforehand. please write it: recommend a concrete course of action with a clear time frame — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — and treat the coalitions philosophical concern seriously rather than just citing hospital policy. specifically, engage with whether higher-order evidence about ones own reasoning should be conciliationist or level-splitting in this clinical context, and whether institutionalizing that tension respects physician autonomy or erodes it. keep it formal but readable, about two pages, and please dont include any citations or reading lists — i just need solid reasoning. oh, and i was chatting with a colleague who mentioned level-splitting vs conciliationist takes on akrasia but im still fuzzy on the distinction, so if you can weave that in clearly that'd help.
