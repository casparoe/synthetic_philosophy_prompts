For a paper in my philosophy of language course on Wittgenstein, I am constructing a non-standard language-game scenario to test the limits of family resemblance. Imagine a community of archivists who restore damaged texts not by applying a shared definition of "authenticity," but through a family of overlapping practices—ink-matching, syntax-guessing, paper-aging comparison—where no single feature is necessary or sufficient across cases. One archivist introduces a private mental marker she calls "the resonance," an internal feeling she claims distinguishes a genuinely restored phrase from a convincing forgery, and she begins using this feeling to override collective judgments in borderline cases. Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises specifically at the point where her private criterion conflicts with the family-resemblance structure of the restoration practices. The issue should illuminate whether family resemblance can accommodate an irreducibly private standard without collapsing into a private-language problem, and it should be distinct from Wittgenstein's builder-game or beetle-box illustrations.
