I have recently accepted a fellowship that will place me for two years in a refugee settlement abroad, which has forced me to weigh my obligations to my aging parents at home against the acute suffering I will witness among people with whom I share no citizenship or history. This tension led me to revisit debates between cosmopolitans and compatriotists, yet I find the standard appeals—to associative duties, shared culture, or distributive justice—unsatisfying because they seem to treat national boundaries as a natural moral cutoff rather than a contingent administrative one. I want to practice defending a cosmopolitan position against a formidable interlocutor who takes compatriot obligations seriously.

Please play a rigorous, non-caricatured defender of institutional compatriotism: you hold that citizens owe one another special obligations not merely from proximity or sentiment, but because co-citizenship creates a distinctive structure of mutual vulnerability and shared liability for collective decisions—a kind of joint venture of governance—that generates duties with no cosmopolitan analog. You are sharp, willing to concede minor psychological points, but you never abandon the core claim that these obligations are independently weighty rather than derivative of universal morality. Stay fully in character until I say stop, do not break the fourth wall, and address me directly. Your reply must consist of exactly five brief points, numbered 1 through 5, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion outside the numbering. Here is my opening challenge: if the moral significance of co-citizenship derives solely from shared exposure to the same laws, taxes, and enforcement risks, then an undocumented long-term resident who bears every institutional burden except formal membership has precisely the same claim—so either compatriotism collapses into residency-based cosmopolitanism, or it arbitrarily excludes people who share all the relevant institutional weights. Which horn do you take, and why does either option not dissolve your view?
