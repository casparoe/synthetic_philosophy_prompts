Present the following scenario: A driver travels a highway with two consecutive exits. Exit A leads to a detour with payoff 1; Exit B leads home with payoff 4; missing both yields 0. Because he is absent-minded, upon reaching an intersection he cannot tell whether it is Exit A or Exit B, nor can he recall whether he has already passed one, so his strategy must be identical at both nodes—either exit or continue. Now inject this modification: before departing, the driver may purchase a hidden commitment device for a fixed cost of 0.5 that randomly enforces continue at exactly one of the two intersections; if he declines it, he applies a uniform rule at both nodes. Ask students to (i) compute the ex ante expected utility of each available strategy both with and without the device, (ii) derive the belief-dependent reasoning that applies ex post at an intersection, (iii) explain why the ex post optimal action conflicts with the ex ante optimal plan, and (iv) evaluate whether this conflict genuinely undermines the coherence argument for dynamic consistency or whether it can be dissolved by appealing to plan-dependent preferences or the commitment mechanism. The answer should take about 75 minutes and run to roughly 800 words.

Please supply a model answer of approximately 700 words and a marking scheme totaling 30 points built around five specific analytical moves—such as correct computation of the strategy space, accurate derivation of the ex post belief structure, identification of the dynamic-inconsistency tension, assessment of the commitment device's rationality, and a reasoned verdict on the coherence challenge. For each move, state the marks available and provide a one-line note on the typical student failure (for example, computing payoffs as if the driver could condition on location, or treating the paradox as a pure labeling exercise). The difficulty should come from requiring students to perform the reasoning and evaluation themselves, not from recognizing a named paradox. Finally, please state how confident you are in your own analysis—especially regarding the payoff arithmetic and the interpretation of the coherence argument—and single out the specific point about which you are least sure, such as whether the hidden-commitment variant genuinely resolves the tension or merely displaces it onto the pre-departure choice.
