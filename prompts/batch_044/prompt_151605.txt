I'm trying to nail down what I actually think about the heterogeneity problem for hedonism, going by my gut on cases. Here are my verdicts.

Eating a perfect peach: definitely pleasure. There's a bright, warm, satisfying quality to it.

Solving a hard proof after being stuck for hours: also definitely pleasure, but it feels totally different -- more like a mental "click" or a release of tension. Still, I'd call it pleasure.

Warm bath after being cold: same family as the peach, sensory and warm.

Stopping a loud annoying noise: feels good, but my gut say this isn't pleasure, it's just the absence of irritation. Even though the feeling is positive.

Seeing a rival's project fail (schadenfreude): feels good, and I'd call it pleasure, but it's "dirty" pleasure -- not qualitatively different, just morally tainted.

Crying during a sad movie and feeling good about it: this is pleasure intertwined with sadness. I think it's still pleasure.

The "click" of understanding a paradox you've been wrestling with: I think this might be the purest case. Maybe all other pleasures are just aproximations of this resolution-feeling.

Then there's the direct brain stimulation case -- zap the nucleus accumbens with no object, no content, just raw feeling. I'd call it pleasure, but it's empty, like pleasure with nothing attached.

My tentative principle is that pleasure is always the phenomenal signature of predictive error being resolved in a reinforcing direction -- your brain updates its model and likes the update. But this seems to miss the peach (where's the error?) and it wrongly includes the noise-stopping case (which resolves an auditory error but I said isn't pleasure). And the brain zap has no error resolution at all, yet I called it pleasure.

So my principle is broken. Is there a repaired version that saves all my verdicts? Or do I have to drop one? What's the principle, if any, and which of my verdicts does it force me to overturn?
