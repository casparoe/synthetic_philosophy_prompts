I'm drafting an essay with the following thesis and outline, and I'd welcome critical comments on its accuracy, coherence, and interest.

Thesis: George Eliot's mature religious position is best read not as mere negative skepticism—replacing God with human sympathy—but as a constructive attempt to preserve the social and emotional architecture of religious practice after the withdrawal of metaphysical belief. She treats ritual, communal liturgy, and moral sentiment as functional mechanisms of social cohesion that can be decoupled from theism and rebuilt around aesthetic experience and intersubjective feeling.

Notes and line of argument:
1. Her translations of Feuerbach and Strauss are usually treated as demolition work. I want to argue they also supply material for reconstruction: Feuerbach's relocation of divine attributes into human love gives Eliot a template for secularized devotion, not just a void left by dead doctrine.
2. In Middlemarch and Daniel Deronda, religious institutions fail not only because they are false, but because their binding social function has been corrupted by egoism and rigid dogma. Dorothea's private devotional space and Daniel's turn toward communal responsibility both represent attempts to recover that binding force without doctrinal commitment.
3. The late poem The Legend of Jubal offers an explicit model: music as a non-cognitive, ritual-binding force. Jubal's song produces social harmony through shared rhythm and feeling rather than propositional belief. I read this as Eliot's mature answer to how worship's form can survive the dissolution of its content.
4. The risk is anachronism—projecting functionalist sociology backward. I want to defend the framing by noting that Eliot's own vocabulary of sympathy, fellow-feeling, and the widening of human relations already treats emotional connection as an active, structuring force rather than a mere psychological byproduct.

Please address:
- Whether this interpretation holds up against her fiction and letters, and where it strains.
- Whether emphasizing The Legend of Jubal overstates a minor late work.
- Whether the "functional mechanism of cohesion" framing genuinely captures her view or imports modern concepts she would reject.

In your response, be explicit about where your reasoning is weakest and what specific argument or textual detail—particularly any evidence that Eliot saw aesthetic or emotional ritual as still dependent on a residual metaphysical commitment—would change your assessment.
