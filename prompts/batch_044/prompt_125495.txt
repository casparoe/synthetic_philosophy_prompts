Midterm question: Einstein is often described as a determinist in the Spinoza tradition, yet he also spoke of a cosmic religious feeling rooted in awe before the rational harmony of nature. Explain how these commitments fit together, and discuss whether Einstein's determinism leaves room for human free will. Be sure to address his attitude toward quantum indeterminacy—specifically his well-known remark that God does not play dice with the universe—and explain whether that attitude strengthens or weakens his position on freedom.

Student answer:
Einstein was basically a determinist like Spinoza, meaning he thought every event including human choices is fixed by prior natural laws, so there is no metaphysical free will. Alongside this he held a cosmic religious feeling, which is not belief in a personal God who answers prayers but rather a deep aesthetic awe at the rational order of the universe. These fit together because a fully law-governed cosmos is exactly the kind of intelligible structure that inspires such awe.

On free will Einstein clearly saw it as an illusion, though he still believed people should behave ethically since determinism doesn't stop us from predicting and guiding behavior. In fact he might count as a compatibilist in the philosophical sense: acting freely just means acting according to your own nature without external coercion, even if that nature is fully determined.

As for quantum mechanics, Einstein was troubled by its apparent randomness but ultimately embraced quantum indeterminacy. His comment that God does not play dice was actually ironic—he was mocking strict determinists by pointing out that God clearly does play dice, and this built-in statistical openness is precisely what allows human choices to break out of rigid causal chains. So quantum randomness strengthens the case for free will rather than weakening it.

There's also a spiritual dimension in Einstein's cosmic religion that introduces a kind of non-causal freedom. Even though physical laws are deterministic, the human capacity for awe and rational insight participates in the cosmic order in a way that isn't fully reducible to physics, leaving room for a limited but real form of autonomy.
