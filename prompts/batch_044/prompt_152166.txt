I keep noticing something weird in how benefit conditionality works. Say you miss a mandatory appointment. If you've got the right paperwork and you're in the "vulnerable" category, the system treats it like a health crisis and pauses sanctions. If you haven't got the label, same missed appointment, you get penalized. The reason for missing — anxiety, no bus fare, confusion about the time — can be identical. What's different is whether you've sucessfully navigated the bureacracy to prove vulnerability in advance. So the practice sorts people into deserving and undeserving not by what they did or why, but by administrative skill. What view of moral desert does that presuppose? Can it be squared with the usual idea that conditionality is about enforcing responsibility?
