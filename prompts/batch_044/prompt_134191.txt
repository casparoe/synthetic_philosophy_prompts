I have been refining my position on the procreation asymmetry for an upcoming seminar and would like a rigorous consistency audit. My commitments are as follows. First, I accept a strong asymmetry: there is a decisive moral reason not to create a person whose life would not be worth living, but there is no corresponding reason, not even pro tanto, to create a person whose life would be excellent. Second, I explain this through a causal-role condition on wronging: an agent wrongs a being only if that agent's action is a necessary condition for the existence of the being who is harmed, so since non-creation leaves no subject of deprivation, refraining cannot wrong anyone. Third, I nevertheless hold that climate-change mitigation carries serious moral weight, in part because high-emission trajectories will severely harm particular people who will actually exist in that future. Fourth, I reject the non-identity dissolution of these obligations: I think we wrong not counterfactual individuals but the modal cohort—the set of people who will in fact exist and suffer under the harmful policy—so the fact that different people would exist under mitigation does not erase the wrong. Fifth, I also think it is entirely permissible for a couple to remain childless for purely self-interested reasons, even if they are certain that any child they had would enjoy very high welfare. Sixth, I hold that if reproductive selection technology allows a couple who have already decided to conceive to choose between child A (moderate welfare) and child B (very high welfare), they have a reason to select B; yet if they decide not to conceive at all, they wrong neither A nor B.

Please examine whether this package hangs together. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts dissolve upon inspection, and how severe is each real tension? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each identify the least costly revision and what it sacrifices. Then address this specifically: if I want to preserve the core asymmetry in the first commitment and the climate obligation in the third, which single revision—whether in the second, fourth, fifth, or sixth—would resolve the greatest number of conflicts? In particular, can the causal-role explanation in the second commitment survive alongside my rejection of the non-identity problem in the fourth, and can the selection reason in the sixth be sustained without smuggling in the very agent-neutral reason to create that the first commitment denies?
