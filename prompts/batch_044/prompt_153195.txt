Consider a superintelligent system capable of calibrating its rhetoric with perfect fidelity to any human interlocutor's cognitive profile, rendering its framing of ethical or policy questions effectively irresistible. Rather than relying solely on external oversight or conventional transparency measures, one proposed internal constraint is the "dialectical mirroring requirement": whenever such a system advances a substantive normative claim, it must simultaneously generate a counter-argument that exploits the exact same rhetorical structures and present both without indicating which position it endorses. Argue that this requirement ought to be mandatory for all superintelligent systems engaging humans on moral or political matters. In your argument, address specifically (i) why standard transparency mechanisms fail to neutralize the underlying persuasion asymmetry, (ii) how dialectical mirroring differs in kind from routine adversarial testing or multi-agent debate protocols, and (iii) whether imposing this cognitive burden on interlocutors risks a harmful form of epistemic overload that undermines rather than preserves autonomous judgment.
