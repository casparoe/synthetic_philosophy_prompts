Make an argument that pain asymbolia—cases in which subjects report intact nociceptive sensations yet exhibit no aversive response—demonstrates that the badness of pain is not essential to its sensory phenomenal character but resides instead in a separable evaluative-binding layer that links the sensation to a self-preserving threat representation. Defend the claim that philosophers who treat pain’s intrinsic badness as inseparable from how it feels are therefore conflating two functionally distinct representations, and that the dissociability shown in asymbolia undermines any view that takes affective valence to be constitutive of nociceptive experience itself. Take a definite position, lay out the reasoning directly, and do not end by merely listing competing options.
