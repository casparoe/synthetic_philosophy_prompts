Explain Sartre's concept of radical freedom and its relation to bad faith (mauvaise foi). Discuss the waiter in the café example, and clarify the two directions in which self-deception can occur—denying transcendence and denying facticity.

here's the student anwser, pretty much verbatim:

Sartre argues that human consciousness is radically free because it never coincides with itself—there is no fixed human nature or essence that determines our choices. This freedom is total and inescapable; even refusing to choose is itself a choice. Bad faith is the attempt to escape this burden by lying to oneself. In the café, the waiter moves with exaggerated precision and treats his job as an essential identity, playing at being a waiter rather than recognizing that he is always free to quit or redefine himself. He denies his transcendence—the fact that he exceeds any social role.

Sartre pairs this with the concept of facticity, which is basically Heidegger's thrownness (Geworfenheit) renamed: the brute givens like our body, past, and social situation that we never chose. Bad faith can also run the other way, by denying facticity and pretending we are pure freedom with no constraints.

The cure for bad faith is sincerity. If the waiter stopped performing and sincerely said I truly am a waiter, this is my essence, he would be living authentically and escaping self-deception. Sincerity means fully embracing one's facticity without pretense, accepting the given conditions as one's true self.

Sartre also connects this structure to anguish—the vertigo that comes from realizing we are totally responsible for our choices and that no external authority can decide for us.
