I'm prepping an interviw for my little philosophy podcast — mostly grad students listen, which is probably for the best — and next week's guest is this philosopher who's been pushing a structural fix for the wrong-kind reasons problem. He calls it domain misregistration: the idea that a reason counts as wrong-kind not because it lacks motivational punch or doesn't track the object's value, but because it's registered in the wrong normative domain, like trying to weigh something with a thermometer. He says there's a formal feature, evaluative transparency, that tells us whether a reason is properly indexed to the attitude in question, and he thinks this handles both belief and action without collapsing into the usual motivational story everyone else tells.

I want eight to ten questons that really press on the machinery, not just asking him to restate the view. I need at least one that hits the boundary worry: if domains are discrete, don't we end up with sharp cutoffs about which reason belongs where, and isn't that just as puzzling as the original distinction? Another should anticipate his move of saying this is just a redescription of the evidential/normative divide, and then ask whether the redescription actually explains anything or just renames it. Then I want one about mixed cases — say a belief is both well-supported by evidence and deeply satisfying — and ask how he determines which domain is 'active' without secretly relying on the value judgments he's supposed to be avoiding. For each of these, please include a folow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give — for example, if he says attitude-types fix the domain, the follow-up should ask whether that makes the account circular. I'd also like one question that asks what a genuine counterexample to misregistration would even look like, since he has a habit of absorbing objections into the framework. Keep it conversational, precise, and please don't ask for bibliographies, citations, or a reading list — I need to stay in the weeds with him, not send listeners to a library.
