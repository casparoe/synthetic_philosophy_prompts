I am a high school senior preparing a forty-five minute session for my school’s philosophy club, whose members are mostly juniors with no prior coursework in logic or formal philosophy. I need to teach them Wittgenstein’s notion of language games and family resemblance, but I want to avoid the standard board-game and chess openings because several members already associate the phrase with wordplay or poetry slams rather than with meaning and use. Instead, I want the explanation carried by a single running case: the word rule as it operates in a courtroom, in a board game, and in an unspoken friendship. Plan the teaching sequence rather than delivering the explanation itself.

First, lay out the order in which the conceptual pieces must appear and why that dependency holds—specifically, why the failure of the picture-theory of meaning has to be established before family resemblance can do any philosophical work, and whether language-games should be introduced before or after that failure in the sequence.

Second, specify exactly what this carrying case must include and exclude: which of the three contexts should be introduced first, what contrast between them is doing the philosophical labor, and what feature of each context must be highlighted so that the absence of a common essence becomes visible without collapsing into the idea that the word is merely ambiguous.

Third, map the misconceptions the audience will form at each stage—particularly the belief that family resemblance means there are literally no shared features among uses, the belief that Wittgenstein is reducing meaning to arbitrary social agreement, and the belief that this is only a fancy way of saying language is vague—and indicate precisely where in the sequence each misconception should be headed off.

Fourth, design one closing question that will reveal whether they have actually grasped the dependency between use and meaning, rather than simply repeating that there are no necessary conditions.

Finally, state explicitly what portion of the private-language argument I should leave untouched and openly acknowledge as beyond this session’s scope, since I do not want to pretend we have covered it. Do not provide a bibliography or suggest further readings.
