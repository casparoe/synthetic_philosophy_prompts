I teach an upper-level metaphysics seminar, about 12 grad students, 90 minutes. Design an in-class activty that makes them actually feel Berkeley's master argument — not just understand it intellectually but hit the contradiction themselves. I want you to build in Berkeley's passive-idea / active-idea distinction (from the Principles) as hidden mechanics: students work in pairs trying to construct a shared sentence describing "the tree in the quad right now with absolutely no mind perceiving it." The rules should constrain them to only use agreed-upon language, no pointing, no instruments, and they must label each feature they add as either recieved (passive) or constructed (active). The setup should force them to realize that every attempt to specify the tree's existence outside minds collapses into describing their own mental operations or prior recieved ideas. Include specific roles, a timeline with checkpoints, and failure conditions — maybe they "win" only if they can produce a contradiction-free sentence, which should be impossible. Then a debrief mapping each failure mode onto Berkeley's steps in the Three Dialogues. Also tell me what will predictably break first time: I think one student will retreat to "well it's in my head but I represent it as outside," and I need the facilitator move to block that retreat without just lecturing. Be honest about what feature of Berkeley's full view this can't reproduce — probably the divine perpetual percetion part — and don't ask for citations or a reading list.
