Please argue that Skolem's paradox poses no serious threat to mathematical realism about uncountable sets. Specifically, defend the claim that the existence of a countable model of ZFC in which an internally "uncountable" set is externally enumerable only demonstrates that cardinality is model-relative with respect to the pairing functions available within the model, rather than showing that uncountability is an illusory feature of the theory. Address the counter-objection that if an external bijection exists, then internal uncountability is merely an artifact of the model's restricted ontology and therefore lacks metaphysical substance. Maintain that internal uncountability remains objectively meaningful, since the absence of a bijection within the model reflects a genuine structural property of the collection relative to that model's own domain and relations, and that privileging the external enumeration illegitimately assumes the meta-theoretic domain is metaphysically prior to the object-theoretic one. Keep your argument focused on the relation between model-internal structure and realist ontology, without appealing to bibliography or citation.
