I'm applying for a one-semester research grant to develop a philosophical paper on a tension within pragmatist theories of truth that standard overviews tend to gloss over. Rather than asking whether Peirce's long-run convergence view and James's claim that truth is made in the course of experience conflict at the level of propositional correspondence, I want to examine how each thinker treats commitments that seem to function as preconditions for inquiry itself—beliefs we cannot easily suspend despite lacking decisive evidence, such as confidence in inductive reasoning or in the minds of others. Peirce tends to treat these as regulated by the self-correcting methods of science, whereas James appears to justify them on practical and volitional grounds. The core question is whether James's position is genuinely incompatible with Peirce's, or whether they address different phases of belief-formation—Peirce the communal control of hypotheses, James the individual's adoption of working assumptions—so that the conflict dissolves once we stop applying a single uniform notion of truth across both contexts. Write a two-page proposal stating this question with precision, explaining why existing Peirce-James comparisons leave it open, outlining a reconstructive method that works from each thinker's texts on belief and inquiry without importing later neo-pragmatist frameworks, describing a deliverable useful to epistemologists and philosophers of science, and specifying what outcome would count as failure—specifically, if the two accounts prove irreconcilable on this point or if the distinction between phases cannot be sustained. The scope should be realistic for a single semester. Do not provide a bibliography or citation list; references will be added separately.
