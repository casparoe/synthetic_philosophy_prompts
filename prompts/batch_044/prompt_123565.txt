(2) at the same time i think Cartwright is basically correct that causal capacities are real and they only manifest inside stable nomological machines. so if a model assumes away all the institutional and physical background that makes supply curves actually behave like supply curves, it's not describing a real causal mechanism - it's just curve-fitting with fancy math.

(3) but i also think the point of good idealization is exactly to falsify background conditions deliberately - like treating molecules as non-interacting or markets as perfectly competitive - in order to isolate the mechanism you're actually interested in. and i think this explanatory isolation has value even when the predictive accuracy is mediocre, because it tells you what the mechanism would do in a shielded environment.

(4) i hold that predictive success and explanatory isolation are genuinely distinct desirata, neither reducible to the other, and that a mature science needs both.

(5) finally, i think Friedman got the justification backwards: unrealistic assumptions aren't ok just because predictions work; they're ok because a false model can be embedded in a broader framework of true claims about capacities, so the model functions as a calculational shortcut rather than a literal description.
