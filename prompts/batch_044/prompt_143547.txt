Im trying to figure out what I actually think about liberty—positive, negative, republican, whatever—by checking my gut verdicts on concrete cases. I want to know if there's one principle behind them or if I'm just being messy, and if I have to drop one.

1. A factory worker can legally quit anytime, but theres only one plant in town and leaving means her kids go hungry. I say shes not free.

2. A citizen lives in a democracy where the constitution technically lets the majority ban her religion tomorrow, but the culture makes it unthinkeable. I say she IS free, even though the power is sitting right there.

3. A guy was raised in an isolating cult; every desire he has was shaped by it, and he "chooses" to stay without ever conceiving of leaving. I say hes unfree.

4. Heres a weirder setup: predictive pre-emption, where an AI never blocks you but predicts your choice with 99% acuraccy and subtly tweaks prices and wait-times in real time so its preffered path is always smoothest. 5. A prisoner sits in a cell with an unlocked door. He genuinely believe its locked and never tries to leave. I think he IS technically free—theres no actual barrier—though he doesnt experience it. But that sounds like case 3, where he also doesnt realize theres an exit, and there I said unfree. So maybe Im contradicting myself?

6. Someone takes a drug that rewires her preferences so she now genuinely wants to work eighty hours a week. Before she valued leisure. I think shes less free now, even though she feels zero internal conflict.

So: is there a coherent account—maybe mixing Berlin and Pettit with something extra—that gets 1, 3, 4, and 6 unfree, case 2 free, and makes sense of case 5 without just saying Im wrong about 3? Or which verdict should I toss?
