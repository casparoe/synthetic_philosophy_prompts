I cover philosophy of normativity and cognitive science for a science-and-ideas publication, and I am interviewing a philosopher who defends a precise variant of free-floating rationales: she holds that normative reasons can obtain and exert directional force in systems that lack any capacity for rational agency, specifically by being instantiated through structural constraints on information flow rather than through mental states or propositional attitudes. I need eight to ten interview questions that move well beyond asking her to restate this view. Please include: one question that presses the distinction between a genuinely normative free-floating rationale and a merely functional optimization target; one on whether her account avoids collapsing into the kind of reductive moral realism she explicitly rejects; and one probing what her position implies for artificial decision architectures that appear to track normative patterns without any comprehension—each paired with a follow-up calibrated to the deflection or qualification she is most likely to offer. The questions should be rigorous enough for a philosophically literate general audience, should not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and should avoid leaning on obscure named doctrines. Frame them as a journalist would ask them: direct, precise, and designed to expose both where the position is strongest and where it is most conceptually exposed.
