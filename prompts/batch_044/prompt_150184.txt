I was talking with a friend who recently read Derek Parfit’s work on personal identity—she tried to summarize his teletransporter thought experiment for me, and her account seemed coherent but left me uneasy about the conclusions she drew. She explained that Parfit imagines a scanner that records your brain state at the molecular level, destroys your original body, and then reconstructs an exact replica on Mars with all the same memories, intentions, and personality traits. According to her, Parfit argues that because this replica is psychologically continuous with you in every respect that feels subjectively important, bodily continuity is not what matters for survival; what matters is just this psychological connection, which means the replica literally is you, carrying over not only your consciousness but also your legal obligations, marriages, and moral responsibilities.

She then added a twist I have not heard elsewhere: she claimed that if the scanner malfunctions and produces two perfect replicas simultaneously, Parfit’s view forces us to treat both as being you, but since personal identity cannot be literally duplicated, each replica only inherits a “fractional share” of your original identity—like dividing an estate—so neither has a full claim to your debts or relationships. She said this proves identity is not an all-or-nothing relation but comes in degrees, and she mentioned that modern neuroscience supports the idea because brain patterns are essentially information that can be copied like software. I am not sure whether the branching case really yields divided identity, whether the jump from psychological continuity to legal status is legitimate, or whether she has smuggled in ideas that Parfit would reject. Could you audit her summary? Please distinguish what accurately reflects Parfit’s arguments about psychological continuity and the teletransporter, what counts as a common simplification, and what is a subtle misrepresentation—especially regarding branching, identity as a matter of degree, and the inference to legal or moral status—and tell me whether the claim that two replicas would split your rights like property follows from anything true in his actual view.
