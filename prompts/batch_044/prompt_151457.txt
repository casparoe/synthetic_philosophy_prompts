I'm defending a thesis I'm calling instrumental infinitism at next month's philosophy of science colloquium: in empirical science, no observational report or measurement outcome functions as an epistemically basic foundation, because every datum depends on an open-ended regress of calibration assumptions, background theoretical models, instrument-design commitments, and auxiliary hypotheses about error distributions. Rather than collapsing into skepticism, scientific rationality survives because practitioners deploy context-sensitive closure rules—pragmatic conditions that provisionally arrest the regress for a specific inference task without granting the stopping point any special foundational status. These rules are revisable, domain-specific, and explicitly acknowledged in laboratory practice, but they do not terminate justification in self-warranting beliefs.

The audience is precisely mixed, and I need objections phrased in each group's vocabulary. There are two moderate foundationalists in epistemology who hold that perceptual beliefs about instrument readouts can be self-warranting when formed under proper conditions; two coherentists who think holistic mutual support among theoretical beliefs solves the regress without needing an infinite chain or arbitrary closure; three working scientists—an experimental physicist, a climate modeler, and a biologist—who will insist they "just look at what the detector shows" and will treat theory-ladenness as a philosophical abstraction that doesn't affect bench practice; plus one infinitist who accepts the endless chain but will press me on why my closure rules avoid arbitrariness. I want the prep sheet ordered by how likely and damaging each objection is, each phrased the way that subgroup would actually put it, with a concise reply, a note on where the reply is weakest, what I should concede up front, and the one question I'm praying nobody asks—probably something about whether my view makes scientific disagreement incommensurable once closure rules diverge. Include my own weakest point: I haven't fully explained how closure rules are selected without circular appeal to the very theoretical framework they are meant to support.
