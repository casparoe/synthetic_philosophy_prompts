Question: Explain tranquilism about well-being — the view that only the absence of craving and suffering matters, and that pleasure or achievement have no independent value. Then address the exclusivity objection: that a life with zero craving and zero suffering but also zero joy or engagement is still deficient. In your reply, be careful to distinguish craving that is constitutive of suffering from craving that is only instrumantal to it.

Student answer:
Tranquilism says well-being is entirely negative: it's just the absence of suffering and craving. Positive states like pleasure don't add anything; at best they temporarily reduce craving. The exclusivity objection claims a perfectly tranquil but flat life is missing something important. I think the tranquilist can answer this by saying craving is mainly bad instrumentally — it causes frustration when desires aren't met. So if you eliminate craving, you eliminate the risk of frustration, and there's no remaining problem. The objector is smuggling in the idea that well-being needs positive ingredients, but tranquilism denies that. Also, pleasure is just relief from craving, so in a state with no craving there's nothing to be relieved from. That means there's no pleasure either, but that's fine because well-being was never about having pleasure, only about lacking pain. So the flat life is actually ideal, not deficient. The distinction between constitutive and instrumantal craving doesn't really change the outcome, since either way removing craving removes the bad part.
