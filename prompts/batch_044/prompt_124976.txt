Read Stevenson's Jekyll and Hyde through the metaphysics of dispositions, finks, and masks. The usual story is that Jekyll has a hidden violent disposition that Hyde expresses, but I want you to consider a weirder reading: what if the potion doesn't create Hyde's violence but acts like a fink that removes the social mask blocking a disposition that was already there? Or is Jekyll actually the masked disposition and Hyde the unmasked base? Take both the novella's scenes—the broken door, the final transformation, Jekyll's statement about inheriting evil—seriosly alongside the philosophy. Do theories about finkish and masked dispositions illuminate what's happening, or does the text resist being reduced to a metaphysical example? In particular, does the idea of a reverse fink—where a chemical change strips away the environmental mask rather than triggering a new property—help explain Jekyll's belief that he was always carrying that evil? I want a reading that doesn't just illustrate the philosophy but also says what Stevenson adds that the abstract arguments miss, especially where personal identity and moral responsibility don't map neatly onto dispositional properties.
