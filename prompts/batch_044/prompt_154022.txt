Draft the joint statement of an adversarial colllaboration between two camps fighting over blame for the First World War. Side one, call them the Fischer intentionalists, holds that Berlin's leadership deliberately engineered the July crisis as part of a long-standing push for continental hegemoney, and that "Germany" can be held responsible in roughly the same way you blame a single conspirator. Side two, the structural diffusionists, holds that blaming a nation mistakes a distributed system of alliance obligations, mobilization timetables, and bureaucratic inertia for one unified will, so responsibility should attach to decision architectures rather than countries.

Have both sides sign onto agreed claims: that Fischer argued the crisis was seized as an opportunity rather than suffered as an accident; that war aims expanded rapidly once fighting started; that the archival record shows real ambivalence among some decision-makers and real enthusiasm among others; and that the hard conceptual issue is whether collective responsibility requires a shared intention, or whether structural outcomes can be attributed even when no single actor held the complete plan.

Then lay out the disagreements in a table: what is the right unit of blame -- the state, the ruling elite, the military-industrial complex, or the decision-making architecture itself? Does the absence of a formal cabinet resolution explicitly authorizing world war weaken the intentionalist case? Does the fact that individual actors could have pulled back weaken the structuralist excuse?

Name the cruxes. One should be whether saying Germany wanted war is a claim about executive intentions or about systemic incentives. Another should be how to weigh the blank check given to Austria-Hungary against the failure to halt mobilization after Russia's partial mobilization -- both sides should agree these moments are relevant, but each should predict what they actually show.

Finally ask whether part of the dispute is verbal: do both camps share one concept of national responsibility and just disagree about the historical facts, or are they running different theories of collective agency altogether? Keep it casual, like you're writing to a reading group that's been arguing for months. Drop an article or two, and maybe misspell hegemony as hegemoney somewhere.
