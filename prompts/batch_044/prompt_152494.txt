I have been working through an argument about Rawls's veil of ignorance and the difference principle on my own—mostly from reading general summaries and trying to follow the reasoning to its end—and I think I have built a chain that proves far more than Rawls intended, perhaps to the point of absurdity. Here are the steps as I understand them:

(1) Behind the veil of ignorance, rational agents lack knowledge of their social position, natural talents, and conception of the good, though they know the general facts of human psychology and social cooperation.

(2) Because they might turn out to be anyone, they apply maximin reasoning: they choose principles that make the worst possible social position as good as it can be.

(3) This yields the difference principle: social and economic inequalities are permitted only when they work to the greatest benefit of the least advantaged members of society.

(4) Now, the veil removes knowledge of one's position just as thoroughly in time as in space or class: you do not know whether you will be born in 1800, 2024, or 2300.

(5) If the maximin reasoning in (2) is genuinely driven by ignorance of position, consistency demands it apply across generations: rational choosers should maximize the position of the worst-off generation, not merely the worst-off class within one generation.

(6) But Rawls measures advantage by primary goods—basic liberties, opportunities, income, and the social bases of self-respect—which are partly indexed to the technological and economic context of a given era. What counts as deprivation for a person in 2300 may be incommensurable with deprivation in 2024.

(7) To preserve maximin across time, we must therefore either abandon primary goods for some subjective welfare metric that applies across all eras, or we must treat temporal position as falling outside the veil.

(8) The first option opens the door to adaptive preferences: a future society accustomed to scarcity might report high subjective welfare despite severe objective deprivation, which undermines the very point of protecting the worst-off.

(9) The second option—keeping primary goods and excluding temporal position—requires a principled reason why ignorance of one's social location matters but ignorance of one's historical location does not.

(10) Without such a reason, the veil becomes arbitrarily bounded: we apply maximin where it feels intuitive and suppress it where it would demand intolerable present sacrifice for distant, unknown futures.
