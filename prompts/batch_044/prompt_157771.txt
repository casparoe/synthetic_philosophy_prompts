In debates over whether philosophy is useful to science, people often invoke the comparison that 'philosophy of science is about as useful to scientists as ornithology is to birds,' sometimes pairing it with Hawking's claim that philosophy has fallen behind physics, or Weinberg's warning that philosophical fashion obstructs scientific progress. I am trying to determine exactly what thesis that ornithology line is meant to establish. Can you lay out the distinct precise readings it could carry -- for instance, that philosophical reflection plays no causal role in discovery; that it is redundant because science corrects itself internally; that it actively harms inquiry by imposing obsolete conceptual schemes; or that normative guidance of research is categorically different from descriptive study of it? For each interpretation, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive empirical claim, or false; indicate which version, if any, Hawking's or Weinberg's broader dismissal actually depends on; and point out which version philosophers usually attack without recognizing that it was never the claim being made.
