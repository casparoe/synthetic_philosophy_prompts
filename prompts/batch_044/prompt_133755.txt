I'm on the national reproductive-health advisory board - basically an ethics committee that actually writes rules, which sounds bureaucratic and it is. We have a meeting thursday to vote on the 'Wellbeing Embryo Ranking' protocol for state-subsidized IVF clinics. Under the proposal, parents wouldn't just screen for serious monogenic diseases; they'd get a polygenic 'life-flourishing score' for each viable embryo covering predicted mental health, cognitive capacity, and even subjective wellbeing metrics. The argument is pure procreative beneficence: if society is already funding the IVF cycle, and parents genuinely want the best life for their child, shouldn't we at least present the option to select the embryo with the highest predicted welfare? The liberal eugenics folks on the board - including my co-chair - say this is fine as long as it's opt-in, not mandatory, because the state remains neutral about which traits parents value; it's just giving them more information.

But I'm worried we're smuggling in a normative standard about what counts as a 'good life' under the guise of science. I need a memo for the committee that takes a clear position: either recommend we approve the protocol with strict conditions, or recommend we reject it entirely for publicly funded programs, with a concrete timeline (say, pilot program banned for 24 months, or implementation within 6 months with audit requirements). The memo needs to treat the beneficence/liberal-eugenics position honestly - don't strawman it as 'state eugenics' when it's actually about parental autonomy - but also press on where exactly the line is between 'giving parents tools' and 'creating social pressure to optimize children,' and wether the state can really stay neutral once it pays for the screening. I want the reasoning to include the strongest argument on the other side: that refusing to provide the ranking is itself a value-laden choice that prevents parents from fulfilling their moral duty to benefit their future child. Also, since my roommate in the bio dept mentioned that these polygenic scores have serious ancestry-bias issues, maybe flag that as a practical limitation without turning the memo into a genetics report, and make sure to seperate the ethical reasoning from the technical concerns. Write it as something I could actually circulate wednesday night before the vote. Don't cite a bunch of philosophers by name - I know the literature, I need arguments, not a reading list.
