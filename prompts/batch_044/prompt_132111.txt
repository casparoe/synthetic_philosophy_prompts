I'm prepping for a talk next thurs at the interdiscplinary philosophy/econ workshop. I'm defending the view that fiat money is a socially constructed kind, but not in the boring 'we all agree' way — instead its stability comes from institutional epistemic closure. Central banks and payment rails systematically suppress counterfactual information about failure modes, so 'value' gets tracked by structured ignorance rather than shared belief. That's why hyperinflation is always a surprise even to insiders. My sketch: the kind 'US dollar' is maintained not because people jointly intend it, but because the information environment is engineered so that agents can't acquire reliable knowledge of the worlds where it collapses, which creates a tracking loop that looks like belief but functions like closure.
