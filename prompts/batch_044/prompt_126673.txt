I've been working through the symbol grounding problem for an embodied AI system. Imagine a robot whose distributed vector representation for "apple" is causally coupled to real apples through its sensory pipeline; by standard accounts this gives it only derivative intentionality, since the coupling was engineered by designers. The usual reply is that human intentionality about apples is intrinsic because it originates in biological function rather than design. But human intentionality was itself shaped by evolutionary causal couplings between ancestors and apples — so if causal origin in evolutionary history makes human intentionality intrinsic, why doesn't a sufficiently long and autonomous causal chain make the robot's representation intrinsic too? This seems to yield a dilemma: either all intentionality is derivative (including ours), or causal coupling alone solves grounding for machines. Where exactly does this reasoning break down?
