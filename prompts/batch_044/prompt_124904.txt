As a retired biochemist who has recently begun studying philosophy of mind, I have noticed a recurring pattern in expert practical reasoning that I cannot place precisely within the literature I have encountered. An experienced practitioner—say, in chemical synthesis—makes a sequence of adjustments to distinct parameters, treating all other conditions as stable at each step. Because each change is small, the practitioner correctly preserves her implicit background framework of what is relevant and what is not. Yet when the accumulated adjustments eventually produce a state in which some previously stable background condition should have been revised—such as the solubility envelope of an intermediate compound—the practitioner is genuinely surprised, having never propagated the need for revision through the chain. The failure is not a lapse of memory or attention at any single stage, but rather a systematic omission of frame updates across locally valid transitions. Is there an established name for this specific phenomenon—the progressive decoupling of a relevance frame from the cumulative state it should govern? How does it relate to, yet differ from, the frame problem as discussed in artificial intelligence? If no precise term exists, how should its boundaries be defined: does it require a causal chain, or can analogous failures occur in logical inference or social deliberation?
