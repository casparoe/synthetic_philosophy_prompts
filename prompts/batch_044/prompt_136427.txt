You are an expert philosopher of mind and rational-choice theory with extensive experience designing assessments for advanced philosophy seminars. Before drafting, briefly brainstorm the precise conceptual crux at which embedded agency becomes reflexive—specifically, a case in which an agent's decision modifies the evaluative architecture by which it judges that very decision, so that standard expected-utility maximization confronts a self-reference loop rather than a mere computational constraint.

Then produce the following for a 60-minute, upper-level seminar final exam. The expected answer length is about 800 words; the total marks are 25.

1. The exam question. Present a precise, somewhat constructed scenario: an embedded agent must choose between retaining its current utility-weighting architecture, call it U, and adopting a revised architecture, call it U*, that systematically discounts future experiential states in exchange for greater coherence with its current meta-preferences. Crucially, the agent evaluates both options using both U and U*, so the choice of architecture is partly self-justifying. Ask students to do three things: (i) explain why this is not merely ordinary self-modification but a reflexive loop that undermines the independence assumption required by standard decision theory; (ii) assess whether causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, or both fail to resolve the loop, and why treating one's future reasoning as an external environment is unavailable to an embedded agent; (iii) propose or critically evaluate a minimal non-arbitrary stopping rule—such as a fixed-point or reflective-stability constraint—that could allow rational choice without collapsing into infinite regress or arbitrary bootstrapping. The question should be answerable without citing specific texts, but it should be impossible to answer well by reciting a generic summary of embedded agency. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.

2. A model answer of approximately 650–750 words. It should contain, in clear sequence: (a) a precise diagnosis of the reflexive loop, distinguishing it from simpler feedback; (b) an explanation of why externalization of the future self fails for embedded agents; (c) a disciplined analysis of the resources and limits of causal versus evidential decision theory in this setting; (d) a proposed or evaluated minimal constraint with its strengths and remaining problems; (e) an explicit acknowledgment of what remains unresolved. Write in formal philosophical prose, using paraphrase rather than verbatim quotation for any named concept.

3. A marking scheme totaling 25 marks that distinguishes an A-range answer (22–25) from a B-range answer (17–21). For each component, specify the marks, the specific move required to earn full marks, and the typical mistake that costs marks. Suggested breakdown:
- Diagnosis of reflexive loop (0–5): full marks require showing that the evaluation framework is not independent of the option being evaluated; typical error is describing ordinary feedback.
- Failure of externalization (0–6): full marks require explaining the embeddedness constraint, not merely asserting it; typical error is treating the agent as if it could step outside itself.
- Decision-theoretic analysis (0–6): full marks require distinguishing causal and evidential resources and stating where each breaks; typical error is naming the theories without applying them.
- Stopping-rule proposal or evaluation (0–5): full marks require offering a specific constraint and noting its cost; typical error is asserting an arbitrary halt.
- Clarity and acknowledgment of limits (0–3): full marks require explicit recognition of unresolved tension; typical error is claiming a complete solution.

Constraints and reminders: do not invoke obscure technical results or named theories that a well-prepared student at this level could not be expected to know from standard coursework; the reflexive architecture scenario above is the intended novel framing. Keep the difficulty in the conceptual mapping, not in hidden factual recall. Write the entire output formally, suitable for direct insertion into an exam-writing document.
