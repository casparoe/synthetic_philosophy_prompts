Trying to figure out my actual view on what counts as a disease, going by gut reactions rather than reading the standard lit. My verdicts on cases:

- Someone with total color blindness living in a society that never uses color for navigation or art: just a diffrent way of being, not sick.
- Same condition in our world where traffic lights and UI depend on color: feels like a real disability, maybe even a disease.
- Celiac triggered only by modern processed wheat: still a disease, since the immune system is misfiring regardless of the artificial trigger.
- BIID, where a person wants a healthy limb removed and reports relief after: I lean toward not-a-disease, more like an identity/body-mapping mismatch, but I keep flipping.
- Normal aging with cognitive decline: not a disease, just lifecycle.
- A genetic variant that makes you metabolize alcohol twice as fast, zero harm: just variation.
- Prosopagnosia where the person learns to recognize people by gait and voice: still a disease, because the face module is broken even if they've routed around it.

So my rough principle is internal biological dysfunction that lowers capacity or causes harm, independent of social framing. But then why does color blindness flip with environment? And why does BIID feel different from prosopagnosia when both seem like body-mind mismatches? There's tension here. What's the principel that captures most of these, which verdict has to go, and what does it predict for my stuck case -- someone born unable to feel physical pain, living in a dangerous environment, constantly injuring themselves accidentally? Disease, disability, or just a risky trait?
