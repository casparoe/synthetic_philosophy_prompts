Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for a graduate philosophy seminar that classifies interpretive responses to the butterfly-dream episode in Zhuangzi. The tree should have three successive binary branch points, yielding eight leaf-positions, with occupancy noted as present, unnamed, or empty.

Third branch: How is the notion of transformation (wuhua) best understood—(X) as an ontological thesis that all phenomenal states are equally real, (Y) as a pragmatic-therapeutic suspension of reality-claims, or (Z) as a normative practice of cultivating fluid identity-boundaries regardless of ontological verdict?

For each occupied leaf, assign a descriptive label (use unnamed if no canonical name exists) and state in one sentence its most awkward commitment—specifically what it must concede about personal continuity across dream and waking. For each empty leaf, diagnose the vacancy: logical incoherence, implausible consequence, or merely unexplored.

Additionally, embed one injected interpretive option—the volitional-enactment view—according to which the butterfly's flight is not a passive illusion suffered by Zhuang Zhou but an active, embodied exercise of consciousness analogous to lucid-dream agency, though without appealing to modern neuroscience. Specify exactly where this view sits in the tree, and flag whether it collapses into an existing leaf or remains structurally distinct.

The instrument should contain no bibliographies, no reading lists, and no requests for citations. Do not reproduce any passage from the classical text inside quotation marks; paraphrase if reference is necessary. Every node should be phrased as a concrete claim or case on which positions genuinely diverge, so that the tree sorts respondents by their commitments rather than by the labels they prefer. Fix the audience as graduate students in comparative philosophy and keep the tree to exactly three levels with eight potential leaves.
