Construct a thought experiment, in one paragraph and in a realistic institutional setting (a hospital ward or factory floor, not a trolley problem), that engineers the aggregation problem for Scanlonian contractualism in the direction opposite to the standard literature. Usually the worry is that contractualism cannot justify imposing a severe burden on one identifiable person to save many, because that one individual can always reasonably reject the principle permitting it. Design your case so that, applied rigorously, Scanlon’s reasonableness-of-rejection framework actually requires imposing a severe burden on a single individual in order to prevent a distributed harm from falling on a large group—despite the fact that each group member, taken in isolation, faces a harm so slight that they could not reasonably reject a principle allowing them to suffer it. The reversal must not come from consent, coercion, or an obviously malformed principle; it must fall out structurally from the geometry of the harms. Specifically, inject the following niche structural feature: the distributed harms are individually sub-threshold, but they are cumulatively linked so that they jointly cross a tipping point, generating a collective burden comparable in severity to the single individual’s burden. After presenting the scenario, walk through the contractualist analysis step by step: state the principle being evaluated; list the complaints available to the single victim and to representative members of the group; explain why the group members’ complaints gain decisive collective weight under the threshold-crossing structure, even though contractualism rejects simple additive aggregation; and show precisely why the victim’s complaint is not decisive here. Then identify the feature—the cumulative threshold with individually sub-threshold harms—that is doing the work, and explain what happens if it is removed (for instance, if the harms are independent rather than jointly crossing a tipping point). Finally, say how a defender of the standard anti-aggregationist reading of Scanlon would attack the case: would they deny that the threshold creates any genuinely collective weight, insist that contractualist comparison must remain strictly pairwise, or argue that the victim’s complaint has been undervalued? Keep the scenario grounded in human-scale stakes, avoid exotic technology or neuroscience, and do not appeal to bibliographies or named commentators beyond Scanlon’s framework itself.
