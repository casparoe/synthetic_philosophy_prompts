What might Erwin Schrödinger—who died in 1961 and was profoundly shaped by Advaita Vedanta’s claim that individual consciousness is merely an appearance of one universal Atman-Brahman—have made of Orchestrated Objective Reduction (Orch-OR), developed long after his death? Orch-OR proposes that consciousness is not an emergent property of classical neural networks but arises from quantum computations in neuronal microtubules, with discrete objective reduction events generating unified moments of experience. Given Schrödinger’s insistence that minds are not separate substances but “facets” of a single consciousness, together with his refusal to invoke vitalism or non-physical causation, would he have seen Orch-OR as a welcome physical mechanism that preserves Vedantic unity at the quantum level? Or would he have objected that tying consciousness to specific subcellular structures—however quantum-mechanical—reinstates precisely the localization and multiplicity that Vedanta denies, thereby missing the point of monism rather than explaining it?
