You are drafting questions for a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who defends a triangulated-recognition reading of Hegel's master-slave dialectic. On her view, the dialectic's central failure is not merely asymmetrical intersubjectivity but the intrusion of a mediating object—the slave's labour-product—which acts as an unacknowledged third term: the master comes to depend on the object for self-certainty, while the slave achieves a distorted form of self-consciousness only through the object rather than through the master's gaze. She applies this to contemporary platform labour, arguing that algorithmic management functions as precisely such a mediating object, obscuring the binary struggle and preventing mutual recognition from ever arising.

Write eight to ten focused interview questions. Include at least one that presses her on why standard dyadic readings of recognition miss the structural role of the mediating object; one that asks whether recognition remains genuinely intersubjective if it is always routed through an external artefact; one that probes how her reading changes the interpretation of the section on lordship and bondage in cases where the slave is not a person but an automated system performing labour; and, for each, a follow-up keyed to her most likely reply—particularly anticipating her tendency to deflect criticisms by saying that critics are still trapped in a pre-phenomenological, subject-centred ontology. End with a question about what empirical or historical evidence could, in principle, count against her claim that every stable master-slave relation necessarily requires such a mediating object. Avoid generic biographical questions and do not ask for bibliographies or citations.
