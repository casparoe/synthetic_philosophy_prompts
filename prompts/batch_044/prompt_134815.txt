This has recently become personally relevant to me because I have begun working in a consciousness research group where first-person reports are treated as primary data, and I have noticed that our methodological debates feel circular in a very specific way. The debate concerns heterophenomenology — the approach, associated with Dennett, according to which we study consciousness by treating subjects' first-person reports as descriptions of a heterophenomenological world, without committing to whether that world maps onto real brain states or onto genuinely private phenomenal properties. Proponents present this as rigorous epistemic humility: we build a predictive science of report without importing untestable metaphysics about qualia. Critics respond that this is not neutrality but eliminativism by another name: by treating auto-phenomenological reports as texts about a fiction rather than as evidence for real phenomenal consciousness, the method rules out in advance the very phenomenon it claims to investigate, reducing explanatory success to predictive accuracy about verbal behavior.

For years I have watched this exchange repeat without movement. Defenders say critics confuse method with ontology; critics say defenders confuse prediction with explanation. My own hypothesis is that part of the stall is verbal — a disagreement about whether methodological neutrality can be separated from ontological deflation — and part is structural: the two sides may be answering different questions, one about how to model report-generation, the other about what it is like to have experience, under the single heading the science of consciousness. But I also suspect a hidden disagreement about explanatory adequacy is doing the real work: whether accounting for the structure and content of reports is sufficient, or whether science also owes an account of why those reports carry the specific phenomenal character they claim to have.

Please diagnose this impasse. Is it verbal, substantive, or a case of different questions being pursued under one name?
