Consider the absent-minded driver problem: a motorist intends to take the second of two consecutive highway exits to reach home with the best available payoff, but he suffers from imperfect recall, so that upon reaching an exit he cannot tell whether it is the first or the second. In decision-theoretic discussions, this scenario is often analogized to a GPS navigation system that recomputes the optimal local maneuver at every junction from scratch, retaining no trip memory—no log of traversed nodes, no global route-state—so that the identical decision procedure is executed at Exit 1 and at Exit 2. The analogy is usually deployed to license the claim that rational consistency itself forces the driver to miss the superior exit, since any plan that conditions on being at Exit 2 is cognitively unexecutable for him.

Audit this analogy thoroughly. First, provide an explicit correspondence table: what in the driver scenario plays the role of the GPS routing algorithm, the road-network topology, the absent state register or transposition table, the local reward signal, and the global destination? Second, identify precisely where the mapping fractures: a real GPS typically possesses perfect geospatial observability (it knows exactly which node it occupies), whereas the driver’s deficit is epistemic—he lacks self-locating information, not necessarily a storage mechanism. Does the analogy therefore conflate imperfect recall with imperfect state-observation? Does it quietly import assumptions—deterministic computation, discrete state spaces, utility-maximizing architecture—that belong to engineered systems but are not automatically warranted for human agency? Separate the disanalogies that genuinely undermine the normative conclusion from those that are merely decorative. Third, ask whether the conclusion that rationality compels the driver to take Exit 1 survives once these breaks are visible, or whether the analogy was carrying the argumentative weight by smuggling in a particular computational model of rational choice. Finally, if the GPS picture collapses under scrutiny, suggest whether a different analogy—perhaps drawn from divided-attention dynamics or habit-loop formation—would capture the predicament without importing the misleading machinery.
