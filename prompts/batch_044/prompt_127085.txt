I hold the view that Fernand Braudel's longue durée should be interpreted not merely as a methodological preference for long timescales but as an ontological claim: geographical and climatic structures possess a form of causal efficacy that is independent of, and prior to, the causal contributions of individual agents and discrete political events. On this reading, events—whether military campaigns, democratic deliberations, or the formulation of philosophical arguments—are not just harder to explain at the long scale; they are causally redundant, doing no explanatory work that the underlying geographical and social rhythms do not already perform. This implies that historical explanation should be graded not by proximity to human intention but by temporal depth, with individual-level narratives serving at best as illustrative color.

What would count against this position? I want the objections sorted by how decisive they would be. Specifically: are there well-documented cases in which a discrete event altered a geographical or climatic constraint rather than merely operating within it? Would evidence of rapid institutional restructuring driven by singular decisions—rather than by slow-moving ecological or economic shifts—undermine the claim of causal redundancy, or can such cases always be absorbed as surface disturbances? Furthermore, does the position risk unfalsifiability if every apparent counterexample can be redescribed as an epiphenomenon of deeper structures? I am particularly interested in whether defenders of this ontological reading of Braudel would themselves concede any form of threshold event that genuinely feeds back into the longue durée, and whether the absence of such concessions renders the view determinist in a way that is philosophically problematic rather than merely methodologically austere.

Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, indicate which ones a committed defender of this view would likely dismiss as misinterpreting the framework, and assess whether the position has been insulated from refutation in ways that should worry us.
