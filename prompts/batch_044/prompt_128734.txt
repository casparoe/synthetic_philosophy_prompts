I have been thinking through Newcomb's problem without any formal training in philosophy or decision theory, and I am struck by how little the disagreement between one-boxers and two-boxers ever shifts. The setup is familiar enough: a highly accurate predictor, a transparent box containing one thousand dollars, and an opaque box that either holds a million or nothing. The one-boxers argue that taking only the opaque box is the choice that correlates with the large payoff being present; the two-boxers reply that, once I am standing in front of the boxes, the contents are already fixed, so I should take both regardless of correlation. I have tried to break the stalemate by constructing a variant in which the predictor does not model my psychology or examine my past behavior, but instead exploits a quantum entanglement between my decision process and a measurement device, producing a statistical correlation without any backward causal influence. Even in this revised case, the one-boxers still insist on taking one box, appealing to the correlation as a reason, while the two-boxers still insist on taking both, appealing to the absence of backward causation and the fixity of the outcome. This suggests to me that the deadlock may not be about the empirical reliability of prediction at all. My own hypothesis is that the two sides are answering different questions—one about which action maximizes expected value given all correlations, the other about which action is rational given the actual causal structure of the world at the moment of choice—but I am unsure whether this is right, or whether the disagreement is instead verbal (about what counts as a reason), or whether it rests on bedrock intuitions about rational agency that neither side can argue for. Could you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is? Please engage with the positions as I have described them, and tell me whether anything—a new conceptual distinction, a formal result, or a change in how the question is framed—could genuinely move the debate, or whether no progress should be expected and why.
