I work as a software engineer leading infrastructure for a logistics firm. Last quarter we deployed an anomaly-detection system that predicts supply-chain disruptions with significantly higher accuracy than our previous rule-based monitors. The architecture is not an opaque black box—we can inspect weights and feature maps—but for any specific alert, neither I nor the original researchers can reconstruct the exact interaction of variables that produced it. The competence is clear; the step-by-step comprehension is not.

Our CTO has proposed a strict policy: no automated system may trigger an operational halt unless an engineer can provide a complete causal explanation for that particular flag. When I argued that disabling our best-performing tool over this standard seems counterproductive, she countered that human experts also operate with tacit judgment, but an artificial system is an artifact we authored, so deploying it without full internal transparency violates our professional duty. She added that we do not accept evolutionary processes as engineering models precisely because nature owes us no explanation, whereas our own code must.

This struck me as possibly repeating a strange inversion I encountered recently: Darwin constructed an account of adaptation that was extraordinarily predictive despite his having no comprehension of the genetic mechanism that made heredity work, and Turing suggested that we might recognize intelligence in machines without understanding their internal mechanics—partly because we grant human minds the same benefit despite not understanding our own brains. In both cases, demanding that comprehension precede acceptance of competence would have blocked progress.

I am not asking for generic management advice. I want to know whether my CTO is applying a sound engineering principle—authorial responsibility requires full comprehension—or whether she is inconsistently demanding for artifacts a standard we do not apply to natural processes or human cognition. How should I think about the moral and epistemic weight of deploying a system that works well but resists complete explanation?
