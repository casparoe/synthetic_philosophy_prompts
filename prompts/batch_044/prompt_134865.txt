Write a dialogue between two interlocutors—call them M and N—who hold opposing views on whether Darwin’s evolutionary theory and Turing’s theory of computation represent the same philosophical inversion: the emergence of complex, goal-directed competence without any underlying comprehension, design, or intentionality.

M should defend a strong analogical thesis: natural selection and universal computation are structurally parallel mechanisms that both replace a “mind-first” explanation with a substrate-level process capable of generating functional sophistication. M should argue that this parallel undermines the intuition that biological adaptation requires teleological understanding and that artificial computation requires semantic understanding, suggesting a unified account of competence without comprehension across nature and machine.

N should resist the analogy. N should argue that evolutionary competence is historically contingent, population-level, and constituted by environmental selection over deep time, whereas computational competence is syntactic, formally specified, and substrate-independent in ways that make the comparison misleading. N should specifically challenge whether Turing’s inversion actually removes understanding or merely relocates it to the programmer and interpreter, and whether Darwin’s process can be called “competent” in the same sense as a computing system without smuggling in teleological language.

The exchange should be genuine back-and-forth: each speaker must directly respond to the other’s specific arguments rather than delivering parallel monologues. Include at least three rounds of direct reply. Avoid strawmanning either Darwinian biology or theoretical computer science.

Keep the dialogue philosophically rigorous, but do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, and do not invoke obscure technical formalisms a generalist model could not be expected to have encountered.

After the dialogue, include a brief meta-commentary in which you state how confident you are in the philosophical claims advanced in the exchange and explicitly single out the point about which you are least sure.
