I have recently assumed directorship of a small research initiative tasked with advancing conceptual frameworks for AI alignment under an explicit institutional constraint: we assume no fundamental theoretical breakthrough in machine learning, cognitive science, or philosophy of mind within the next decade. Our mandate is strictly prosaic—we work only with methods currently available or in late-stage development, including reinforcement learning from human and AI feedback, mechanistic interpretability, scalable oversight protocols, and compositional architectures in which multiple specialized models and tools are stitched together through APIs and iterative refinement loops.

Within this constraint, a particular problem has become personally urgent. Most existing alignment discourse implicitly treats the object of alignment as a unitary agent with a coherent utility structure. But the systems we are actually building are aggregates: a language model calls a code interpreter, which queries a database, whose outputs feed back into a planning loop, with no single component possessing a unified representation of the overall goal. The alignment of the parts does not obviously compose into the alignment of the whole, yet we possess no conceptual framework for diagnosing or preventing emergent misalignment in such stitched architectures.

Given this framing, which open conceptual questions or lines of theoretical work should occupy the majority of our limited resources—approximately eight researchers over three years, split between philosophers of science and practicing ML engineers? I am not seeking a survey, a bibliography, or a reading list. Rather, I want a defended prioritization: for each of your top three recommendations, explain why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for a team operating without access to a breakthrough in agency theory or consciousness studies, and what specific empirical or theoretical development over the next two years would cause you to reorder them. Please also identify one apparently important question that you would deprioritize, and explain whether its unimportance stems from being already solved, being intractable under prosaic assumptions, or being a red herring that dissolves once the compositional framing is taken seriously.
