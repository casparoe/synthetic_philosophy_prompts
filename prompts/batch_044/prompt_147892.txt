If socio-ecological resilience theory frames societies as cycling through growth, conservation, release, and reorganization—so that the Classic Maya shift to dispersed settlement and the Easter Island transition away from monumental construction are normal adaptive releases rather than failures—then treating the Late Bronze Age as a "collapse" seems inconsistent. Bronze Age populations and local production clearly persisted; only writing and long-distance exchange networks disappeared, which also looks like restructuring rather than extinction. Is the distinction between collapse and transformation therefore just a value-laden preference for centralized complexity, or is there an objective feature—beyond the mere presence of change—that makes the simultaneous loss of literacy and interregional trade a genuinely different kind of systemic breakdown?
