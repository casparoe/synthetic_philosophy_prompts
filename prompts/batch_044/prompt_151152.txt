I have recently begun work in atmospheric modeling for agricultural forecasting, and a recurring structural feature of our practice has begun to trouble me in ways that feel more philosophical than technical. We operate with fully deterministic fluid-dynamics models, yet our useful prediction horizon remains stubbornly fixed regardless of computational investment. The pattern is this: we increase grid resolution to capture smaller-scale dynamics, and for a brief period our forecasts improve, because we have resolved instabilities that the coarser grid had smoothed into noise. But almost immediately, those finer scales reveal still smaller instabilities—previously invisible and therefore harmless to the prediction—that now dominate the error growth. The horizon of reliable forecasting does not extend; it merely shifts downward to the newly resolved scale. My colleagues consistently interpret this as a temporary technological deficit, always assuming that the next increment of resolution will finally break through. I am struck by the possibility that this is not a contingent limitation but a structural feature of deterministic chaos itself: an infinite regress of refinement in which each solved layer exposes an equally unpredictable layer beneath, leaving the aggregate predictability unchanged.

I am trying to determine whether this specific configuration—deterministic dynamics coupled with a resolution-independent prediction ceiling that generates perpetual model refinement without epistemic progress—has an established name in philosophy of science or in the literature on chaos and determinism. I am aware of sensitive dependence on initial conditions and of the turbulence cascade, but neither seems to capture precisely the epistemic trap I am describing, in which practitioners systematically misread a structural feature of the system as a remediable ignorance. Is there a concept that distinguishes this kind of regress from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis, from a Popperian immunization strategy, or from mere computational limitation? If no precise term exists, I would welcome a careful delineation of what makes this pattern distinct, particularly regarding whether it undermines the practical significance of determinism even while leaving determinism itself intact.
