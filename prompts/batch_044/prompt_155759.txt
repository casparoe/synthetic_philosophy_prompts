He put Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" like this: Quine proved that the analytic-synthetic distinction is entirely empty, because even logical and definitional truths—such as that all bachelors are unmarried—depend on contingent empirical facts about how speakers actually use language, leaving no genuinely conceptual or a priori domain whatsoever. Consequently, he said, philosophy should abandon its traditional methods and become a branch of empirical psychology or linguistics, since there are no non-observational truths left to analyze. He further claimed that Quine's metaphor of the web of belief implies that logical, mathematical, and physical statements all sit on precisely the same epistemic footing, so that in the face of recalcitrant experience we might just as rationally revise the law of non-contradiction as reject a single observation report. I have the vague sense that Quine allows logic to be revised only in extremis and that naturalized epistemology is not the same as reducing philosophy to linguistics, but I need help separating the accurate core from the embellishment. Could you go through his characterization point by point—indicating what Quine genuinely argued, where the summary conflates Quine with later philosophers of language, and whether his conclusions about philosophy's dissolution and the flat equality of all beliefs survive once the errors are fixed?
