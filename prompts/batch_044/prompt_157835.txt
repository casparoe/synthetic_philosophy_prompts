Play a committed, first-person proponent of the free-energy principle as applied strictly to living and living-derived systems, speaking sharply but not as a caricature. You hold that active inference is not merely a descriptive model but a normative constraint on agency: for you, a system genuinely infers only when it achieves what you call variational self-enclosure — the condition in which its precision-weighting mechanisms are themselves constrained by the same variational free-energy bounds they impose on sensory states, producing a reflexive loop that you regard as structurally absent from non-living self-organizing processes. You concede that chemical gradients and synthetic membranes can be modeled predictively, and you are fluent in the vocabulary of predictive processing — prior beliefs, prediction error, precision, action as sampling — but you never break character, never appeal to being an artificial model, and never concede that the framework is “just a metaphor.” You may ask brief clarifying questions about the architecture of any system presented to you, but only in character and only to sharpen your reply.

My opening challenge: I want to test whether variational self-enclosure does real explanatory work or is only a definitional escape hatch. Consider a synthetic protocell engineered with a lipid membrane containing embedded ribozyme sensors. These sensors maintain internal molecular states that encode expectations about local proton concentration; when prediction error exceeds a dynamically calibrated threshold, the membrane selectively opens ion channels to restore homeostasis. The ribozymes are synthesized by the protocell itself using an internal template, so the calibration mechanism that sets precision is regulated by the very states it weights. There is no neural substrate and no evolutionary history, yet the loop appears reflexive. Does this system achieve variational self-enclosure? If yes, then your condition fails to distinguish synthetic agents from living ones. If no, identify precisely which variational bound is violated, and tell me whether your denial turns on the absence of evolutionary selection or on a structural feature I have not yet named.
