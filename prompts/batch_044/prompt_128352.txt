As a novelist concerned with the ethics of representation and the weight of historical particularity, I wish to construct the joint statement that two opposing scholarly camps might sign after a rigorous adversarial collaboration on the Holocaust’s uniqueness, comparability, and modernity. The first camp — call it the Singularity-Ethics camp — maintains that the Holocaust is ontologically and ethically incomparable, not because genocide is rare, but because the specific fusion of modern bureaucratic rationality with a total, eliminatory anti-Semitic ideology produces an event that analogical reasoning necessarily betrays and diminishes. The second camp — call it the Modernity-Comparability camp — maintains, in the spirit of Bauman’s thesis on modernity, that the Holocaust is precisely comparable to other genocides because its decisive conditions — instrumental rationality, bureaucratic division of moral labour, and the technological distancing of perpetrator from victim — are reproducible features of modern civilisation rather than unique historical accidents.

Please draft the document both sides could endorse. It should contain:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims — about the historical record, about the formal properties of bureaucratic violence, about what any adequate explanation must account for, and about the limits of analogical reasoning — phrased so that neither camp would reject them.

2. A disagreements table with exactly these columns: ‘Point of divergence’, ‘Singularity-Ethics wording (accepted by that camp)’, ‘Modernity-Comparability wording (accepted by that camp)’, ‘Classification (factual / normative / semantic)’. The table should separate genuine disagreements from those that dissolve upon clarification.

3. The cruxes — sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp. Key cruxes should include whether ‘uniqueness’ is an ontological property of the event or an ethical stance governing remembrance; whether modern bureaucratic rationality is sufficient or merely necessary for large-scale genocide; and whether comparison clarifies structural danger or necessarily dilutes moral particularity. For each crux, provide: a concrete historical case or piece of evidence both camps agree bears upon it (for instance, the bureaucratic administration of another twentieth-century genocide, or the specific role of anti-Semitic ideology in the logistics of the Final Solution); the precise prediction the Singularity-Ethics camp would make about that case; and the precise prediction the Modernity-Comparability camp would make.

4. A brief identification of whether any part of the dispute is purely verbal, with the reason.

Write the response in British English. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote any text verbatim unless you have fetched the exact passage; otherwise paraphrase without quotation marks.
