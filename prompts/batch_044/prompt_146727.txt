I serve on my university's disciplinary appeals panel. We must issue a recommendation to the faculty senate on sanctions for two graduate students who each, independently and identically, left a reactive compound uncapped overnight due to fatigue. By chance alone, one lab suffered no harm; the other experienced ignition and equipment destruction. Our current code assigns penalties solely by mental state and conduct, excluding outcomes. The dean urges adoption of an "outcome-weighted" rule that doubles penalties when damage occurs. One panelist proposes a third path: identical base sanctions for both, but for the harm-case only a non-punitive "restorative obligation"—structured engagement with affected staff—justified not as extra moral blame but as accountability to the actual harm, deliberately separated from questions of desert. Draft the panel's opinion recommending one of these three courses: equal base sanctions, outcome-weighted sanctions, or base sanctions plus differentiated restorative obligation. Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence; then provide reasoning that takes seriously the intuition that the harm-case feels different, includes an honest treatment of the strongest objection to your recommendation, and contains no preamble or restatement of the question.
