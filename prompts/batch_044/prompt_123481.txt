Dennett describes the self as a center of narrative gravity—an abstract theoretical point analogous to a center of mass, not a concrete entity in the brain. If we take the analogy seriously, it seems that contradictory autobiographical narratives should generate competing gravitational centers, producing not one self but several distributed attractors, much as an irregular physical body can have multiple centers of mass. Does this mean Dennett's view implicitly requires narrative uniqueness to avoid a fragmented, polycentric self? Or is the inference mistaken because it treats narrative coherence as governed by the dynamics of physical fields rather than by functional integration? In particular, can the model accommodate radically disjoint first-person reports without either dissolving into eliminativism about the self or smuggling in a hidden author who arbitrates between the competing narratives?
