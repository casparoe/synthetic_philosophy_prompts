Write an essay about sphexishness and free will, but don't just retell the wasp checking its burrow over and over — I'm interested in a distinction that usually gets glossed over. There's loop-rigidity, where the creature repeats the same behavioral sequence, and then there's architechtural rigidity, where it can't even form a representation of doing something else. Now imagine an agent that can repsent alternative actions but is mechanically locked into executing one anyway. Is that closer to free will, or is it sphexishness all the way down? Weigh both sides and defend a verdict: does free will depend more on representational flexibility or on executional flexibility, and can you have the first without the second? Don't sit on the fence — reach a conclusion and justify it.
