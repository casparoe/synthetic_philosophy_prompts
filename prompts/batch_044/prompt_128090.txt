I'm preparing a presentation for a philosophy of mathematics and metaphysics reading group, and I'd like a consistency audit before I defend these commitments. Here is my current profile: (1) I read Plato's Cave allegory as a diagnosis of institutional knowledge-production rather than a generic warning about sensory deception—the shadows are collectively maintained representations that become self-reinforcing, so escaping the cave requires dissolving social epistemic dependencies, not merely an individual's change of perspective. (2) I interpret the Forms through a structuralist lens: they are not perfect particular exemplars but stable relational structures, which means mathematical objects are Forms, while physical objects participate in them only derivatively. (3) I reject the Third Man argument on the grounds that it illicitly applies a comprehension principle across levels of analysis, conflating the relation of partaking with the Form it instantiates. (4) Despite that structuralism, I maintain that knowledge of Forms requires direct, non-discursive intellectual intuition (noesis) that cannot be reconstructed through conceptual analysis or empirical inference. (5) I also deny that Forms are causally inert: if they were, they could not account for the intelligibility of the physical world, so I hold that they exert a non-physical, normative pull on reasoning itself.

Please examine which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and what the least costly revision is in each case. I am especially concerned about three possible fractures: whether (1) and (4) conflict, since an institutional diagnosis of the cave suggests that liberation should be collective or structural rather than a private intellectual ascent; whether (2) and (5) can coexist, given that abstract relational structures do not obviously exert causal or normative force; and whether (1) and (2) are incompatible, because socially maintained shadows seem like concrete representations while Forms are purely abstract structures. Rank the tensions by severity, indicate which single commitment is doing the most damage if retained, and specify what I would have to give up—without signaling which one I am secretly attached to. I want an audit that traces what each view commits me to, not a lecture on Platonism.
