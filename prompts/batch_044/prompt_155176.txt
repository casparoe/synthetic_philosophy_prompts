Having spent three decades in experimental physics before retiring, I have recently turned to epistemology and find myself treating knowledge conditions with the same rigor I once applied to error propagation in calibrated measurement chains. I understand that Robert Nozick's tracking theory analyzes knowledge through sensitivity and adherence conditions, and that he rejects epistemic closure—the principle that knowing p and knowing that p entails q yields knowledge of q. Rather than a formal derivation, I want a conceptual explanation of the precise mechanism by which closure fails under tracking. Specifically, please use an analogy to nested instrumentation: suppose direct sensor A tracks property X, indirect sensor B tracks A's readout of X, and from X we derive property Y through a deterministic physical law. At which link in this inferential chain does the tracking condition fail to transmit knowledge of Y, and is that failure best understood as a modal discontinuity—worlds where q is false despite p being true being too remote for B to track—or as a structurally different kind of epistemic break? I want to grasp whether Nozick's rejection of closure maps onto an analog of instrumental error accumulation or onto a categorically distinct failure of modal proximity in the knowledge relation.
