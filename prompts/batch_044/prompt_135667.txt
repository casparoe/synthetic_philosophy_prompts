Write a deadpan parody of an internal corporate “Ethics & Operations” training memo (about 600 words) that satirizes Hannah Arendt’s account of the “banality of evil” and her portrait of Adolf Eichmann. Address it to middle managers and present “Strategic Thoughtlessness” as an organizational competency, drawing on Arendt’s claim that evil often stems not from monstrous intent but from routinized, unreflective bureaucratic functioning and from the suppression of internal dialogue (the “two-in-one” she describes in her later work). Include an invented case study of a logistics coordinator who, like Eichmann, insists he never personally hated anyone and was only following protocols, framed with bureaucratic approval rather than moral horror. The satire should remain fully in character: exaggerate real features of Arendt’s view—her emphasis on cliché, her argument that Eichmann was not a fanatic, the distinction between thinking and knowing—without attributing to her positions she never held. After the memo, append a brief analytical note (about 150 words) separating settled interpretations in Arendt scholarship (for example, that she located Eichmann’s wrongdoing in thoughtlessness rather than in sadistic intent) from genuinely contested ones (for example, whether she underestimated his ideological commitment, whether “thoughtlessness” is best read as a cognitive or moral failure, and whether her historical portrayal was fair). In that note, also flag exactly where the parody takes liberties or relies on a contested reading of her argument. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and do not quote verbatim from Arendt’s texts; paraphrase her ideas in your own words.
