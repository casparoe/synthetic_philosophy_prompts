(2) If such a physical explanation succeeds—say, by citing introspective opacity, dual-process reasoning, or evolved heuristics—then our judgments about the hard problem are fully accounted for by mechanisms that make no essential reference to consciousness actually being non-physical. (3) But when a judgment is fully explained by mechanisms that do not depend on the state of affairs it purports to track, that judgment is epistemically undermined, as with perceptual illusions; therefore we should dismiss the intuition that consciousness is irreducible. (4) Now generalize: for any concept C—normative authority, intentionality directed at abstract objects, aesthetic value—if physicalism is true, there should be a physical explanation of why people judge C to be irreducible. (5) If such an explanation succeeds, then by the reasoning in (3) those judgments about C are undermined; if it fails, physicalism is false for that domain. (6) Hence for every non-physical domain, either our judgments are systematically unreliable or physicalism has failed. (7) We do not treat mathematics, chemistry, or ethics as either systematically unreliable or as defeating physicalism; therefore the reasoning in (1)–(3) proves far too much.
