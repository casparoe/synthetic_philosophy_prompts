me and my philosophy of mind reading group have been stuck on the same loop for like six months. we started with Dennetts attack on the cartesian theater — the idea that there's a central stage in the brain where 'it all comes together' for a conscious self. everyone in the group agrees that's probably wrong. then we moved to his multple drafts model, where consciousness is more like parallel editors writing different versions of the story with no master copy. but then the debate just... froze.

the split is weird. half the group says the drafts model kills the theater for good because there's no single place or time where experience is 'presented' — just distributed, competing narratives. the other half says this just smuggles the theater back in through the back door: they say there still has to be a 'point of view' that gets the winning draft, otherwise you've got experience with no experiencer, and that's not an explanation, that's just avoiding the problem. they keep bringing up what they call the 'arbitration gap' — if there's no central screen, what actually resolves conflicts between drafts, and doesn't that resolving have to happen for someone? both sides cite the same brain data (no global workspace, no single binding site) and both agree there's something-it's-like to be conscious. but nobody moves.

my working theory is that this isn't about brain science at all. it's about whether 'unity of consciousness' is a datum that needs explaining or just a useful fiction that the drafts model can dissolve. but i could be wrong — maybe there's a hidden disagreement about what an explanation even has to do, or about whether first-person access is to the drafts themselves or only to the final narrative.

can you diagnose this stall? is it a verbal disagreement about 'unity' or 'presentation', a substantive disagreement about whether distributed coherence is enough, or are we actually asking two different questions (one about mechanism, one about phenomenology) under one label? use what each side actually says — the 'no master copy' people versus the 'arbitration gap' people — to support your answer. and tell me honestly: is there a distinction, experiment, or change of framing that could unstick us, or is this the kind of impasse where progress shouldn't be expected?
