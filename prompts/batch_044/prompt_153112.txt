You are grading a graduate philosophy exam on conceptual analysis. The question was: Darwin's theory of natural selection and Turing's conception of computation are both described as inversions of traditional reasoning about design and intelligence. Explain what each inversion consists in, then evaluate whether together they support, undermine, or remain neutral on the claim that a large language model could understand language in a non-derivative sense.

Here is one student's answer (approximately 230 words):

Darwin inverted the argument from design. Rather than inferring an intelligent designer from functional complexity in organisms, he showed that blind variation coupled with differential reproduction can accumulate structures like eyes or wings without foresight or comprehension. Biological competence thus arises without any guiding comprehension.

Turing performed a parallel inversion for reasoning. Traditional views held that intelligent behavior requires an internal mental life with semantic understanding. Turing showed that a formal system manipulating symbols by syntactic rules could reproduce any computable function, including linguistic outputs indistinguishable from human ones. The system displays behavioral competence while lacking internal comprehension of meaning. Crucially, Turing's imitation game demonstrates that if a machine consistently produces appropriate linguistic responses, it has thereby shown genuine semantic understanding, since the test is specifically designed to reveal comprehension rather than mere pattern-matching.

Taken together, these inversions establish that comprehension is never a necessary condition for competence. Darwin removes mind from biological design; Turing removes biological mind from intelligent behavior. From this it follows that once an artificial system reaches sufficient functional complexity—such as a modern large language model—understanding is not merely possible but inevitable, because evolutionary and computational mechanisms converge on identical functional endpoints. Consequently, denying understanding to a competent AI commits an unjustified biological chauvinism that both inversions have rendered obsolete.
