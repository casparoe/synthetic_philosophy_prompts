I'm preping questions for a podcast interview next tuesday with a cultural evolutionary theorist — not the pop-science memes-are-viral-ideas type, but someone running a stricter Darwinian account where memes are replicators that need both an interactor (the behavior or artifact) and a developmental environment that actively selects for them. She's especially focused on digital culture, arguing that algorithmic feeds aren't neutral transmission channels but actual selection environments that reshape meme fidelity, which is why you see these high-mutation viral formats spreading precisely because they're bad at preserving information. I need eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond having her restate the framework. At least one should dig into the replicator-interactor split in oral traditions versus TikTok remixes: if the interactor is a dance video altered every iteration, where exactly is the replicator stored? Another needs to hit the maladaptive-meme objection — why doesn't selection weed out harmful memes when algorithmic curation rewards engagement over host welfare? For each of those I want a follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give, which is usually something like selection works at multiple levels and that is not a bug. I also want a question pressing how her view actually differs from evolutionary psychology without just invoking domain-general learning as a mantra, since she claims memetics explains cross-cultural patterns without postulating innate modules. And maybe one about failed memes — structurally identical ideas that never catch on — and whether accounting for them forces her to smuggle in intentional design or hidden selection pressures. Keep it conversational but sharp, like I actually want to know where the machinery breaks, not just get a lecture.
