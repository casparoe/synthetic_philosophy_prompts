me and my roommate overheard her two guildmates arguing after someones ten-year-old virtual pet got deleted in this old legacy MMO. one guy said its obviously not real harm — its just database entries, no sentience, no pain receptors, and treating it like a real loss is anthropomorphism run wild. the other said thats missing half the story: the pet was a persistent object with its own state-history, shared quests, and a role in her social routine for a decade, so destroying it breaks an actual relational structure, not just feelings about pixels. the first guy pushed back: but that "relational structure" lives entirely in human minds. if she had the same attachment to a randomly generated screensaver, we wouldnt say she harmed the screensaver, so the harm is just her own sadness misplaced onto an object. she answered that promises work the same way — they bind us through practical architecture and mutual expectation, not because the promise itself has inner life, so virtual objects can be nodes in that architecture too. he replied promises require mutual recognition, which code cant provide, so the analogy fails. then she brought up this idea about "counterfactual welfare nodes" — that harm can happen to entities that function as stable parts of someones wellbeing-network even without consciousness, and deleting the pet altered not just her mood but the actual trajectory of her social play-routine in a lasting way, so theres a derivative harm to the virtual object as a functional part of that system. he said thats just double-counting her disappointment with fancy words.

they asked us to settle it but we got lost in the weeds. whos right about what here? is her relational-structure point actually strong, or does he win on the sentience gap? and is that "counterfactual welfare nodes" thing doing real philosophical work, or is it just relabeling her sadness? also what are they both missing that would change the picture?
