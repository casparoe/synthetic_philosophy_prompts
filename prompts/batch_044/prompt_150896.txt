Write an essay addressing the following controversy, which I have been turning over in my own mind without any formal training in philosophy. I recently came across the notion of sphexishness — the extreme mechanical rigidity seen in certain wasps that repeat an identical behavioral sequence regardless of changed circumstances. The usual contrast is that humans possess free will because we are flexible: we adapt our actions to new situations. But this explanation seems too quick. I have observed highly adaptive automated systems — supply-chain algorithms that reroute around disruptions, or learning models that revise their own parameters — that display remarkable flexibility without anyone attributing freedom to them.

This leads me to wonder whether free will might depend on a specific kind of anti-sphexish structure: not mere behavioral flexibility, but a recursive capacity to revise the very rules by which one revises behavior. One view holds that human freedom consists precisely in this layered self-modification, which escapes deterministic loops by constantly rewriting its own conditions. The opposing view holds that recursive self-modification is itself mechanizable; a system can update its weights or its heuristics according to fixed meta-rules, so pushing flexibility up one level merely relocates the sphexishness rather than dissolving it.

Please weigh these arguments with care. Is free will genuinely distinct from sophisticated mechanical flexibility, or is it ultimately nothing more than the absence of sphexishness at every level of analysis? Your essay should reach a clear conclusion and provide rigorous justification for it, treating the strongest objection to your position seriously rather than dismissing it. I am not looking for a survey of named philosophers or a bibliography; I want the conceptual structure examined on its own terms.
