I am assembling exercises for a postgraduate seminar on Berkeley's immaterialism and have been discussing with colleagues how to design prompts that are philosophically precise without inviting a model to reproduce standard textbook summaries from memory. I would like a sustained parodic piece — approximately 600 to 700 words — in the form of a deadpan corporate Frequently Asked Questions document for a fictional subscription service named Esse, which offers ontological hosting for clients who wish to maintain the existence of their possessions not as material substance but as persistent ideas within a shared infinite spirit. The document should be formatted as numbered clauses and should adopt the cheerful, bureaucratic voice of a customer-support portal. The satire must arise from the philosophy itself: the answers ought to deploy, in plain paraphrase without quotation marks, Berkeley's claim that existence consists in being perceived, his distinction between ideas and notions, and especially the master argument that sensory ideas cannot be caused by other ideas and therefore require an active, non-ideational agent. There should be a section addressing cancellation — specifically whether a client's shoes cease to exist when payment lapses or whether they persist in the divine substrate — and another addressing the objection that two subscribers report incompatible sensory qualities of the same hosted object. I also want an invented correspondent, Mr Hylas, to raise the worry that if all is idea, then the subscription fee itself lacks material reality, and the reply should navigate this without collapsing into materialism. The piece should be readable to someone unfamiliar with Berkeley but should reward a reader who recognises the actual tensions in his system — particularly the redundancy of matter alongside the mysteriousness of divine causation. Please write in polished British English, avoid any request for bibliographies, citations, or specific publication dates, and do not include verbatim quotations from Berkeley's actual texts; all philosophical positions should be rendered in your own paraphrase. Keep the irony sustained and deadpan: the author of the FAQ should never wink at the reader.
