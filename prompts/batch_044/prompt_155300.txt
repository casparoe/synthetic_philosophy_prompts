student answer: Schopenhauer distinguishes music from other arts by saying that while architecture, sculpture, painting, and poetry all present Platonic Ideas—the direct objectifications of the Will at various grades—music is not a copy of an Idea at all. Music, however, bypasses the Ideas entirely and expresses the inner essence of the world, the Will, in its pure form. This is why Schopenhauer ranks music highest: it is the most abstract representation of the Ideas, since it doesn't depend on any particular phenomenal form. Within music, harmony is what expresses the Will directly—Schopenhauer says the bass line represents the inorganic world and the higher voices represent the grades up to human consciousness—while melody is just the surface representation of Ideas like joy or sorrow. Because music doesn't represent Ideas, it also lacks any emotional content; Schopenhauer thinks pure instrumental music is essentially meaningless without a text or program to give it representational content. So music is unique because it shows us the Will directly through its harmonic structure, though this means it has no descriptive meaning on its own.
