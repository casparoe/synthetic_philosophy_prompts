I'm writing a paper for my social ontology course on whether generational categories—Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z, and so on—denote real kinds rather than convenient demographic bins. Rather than surveying existing literature, I want to establish the criteria first. Specify the desiderata that any adequate ontology of cohorts must satisfy: the cases it must handle correctly (for example, boundary individuals born within months of an arbitrary cutoff, "micro-cohorts" shaped by shared exposure to a discrete technological shift rather than by birth year, and cross-national divergence where the same birth years map to radically different formative conditions), the distinctions it must not collapse—particularly between statistical aggregation, marketing construct, and causally integrated kind—the empirical findings from demography and developmental psychology it must respect, and the explanatory work it must do if it is to count as more than a labeling convention. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or consideration and indicate whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. Then identify which single requirement does the most damage to popular "common-sense" treatments, and briefly indicate how a strict constructivist view, a birth-interval essentialist view, and an event-based cohort theory measure up against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their justifications; do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
