I am a retired condensed-matter physicist who has, since leaving active research, begun studying philosophy of mind with the same intolerance for unexamined theoretical commitments that I once brought to anomalous experimental data. For a small podcast series aimed at scientifically literate non-specialists, I will be interviewing a philosopher—let us call her the defender—who advances the Chase-Sanborn argument that qualia are not merely elusive or empirically hidden, but conceptually incoherent, on a par with abandoned scientific posits such as absolute velocity or caloric fluid rather than with false but well-formed empirical claims.

I want a sequence of six to eight interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the view. The sequence should include: at least one question that forces a precise distinction between an incoherent theoretical posit and an entity that is merely undetectable, drawing explicitly on an analogy from the history of physics; at least one question that anticipates the standard reply that reports of phenomenal similarity are reducible to functional or behavioral descriptions, together with the follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to offer; and at least one question probing whether the incoherence argument overgeneralizes, threatening intentional content or other mental states she presumably wishes to preserve. For each main question, please indicate the probable reply and the precise follow-up that should come next. I also want the concern that the argument may smuggle in a prior physicalist commitment woven into the sequence, not appended as an afterthought. Please avoid generic biographical questions, do not request bibliographies or reading lists, and keep the tone respectful but rigorous—suitable for an audience accustomed to asking why a theory fails rather than merely accepting that it feels strange.
