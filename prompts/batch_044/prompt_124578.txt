I have no formal background in philosophy, but I have been thinking about a framework I came across informally: that real patterns in the world can be approached through three different lenses. There is the physical stance, which looks at the underlying forces and materials that produce a pattern; the design stance, which asks what function or purpose the pattern serves; and the intentional stance, which asks what mind or agent meant for it to be that way. I would like to apply this to a specific situation I have been observing.

In my neighborhood there is a public garden laid out in a spiral that channels rainwater inward toward a central drought-resistant bed. The layout was originally drafted by a municipal contractor around 2018, but for the past five years an automated irrigation system with soil-moisture sensors has been running continuously. The sensors adjust water flow in response to real-time data, and over time the planting zones have gradually shifted so that the spiral now directs water more efficiently than the original blueprint specified. The gardeners who maintain the space say they simply follow the sensor alerts; none of them intended the revised geometry. At the same time, the local soil chemistry and microclimate physically allow this arrangement, yet they do not uniquely determine its precise shape.

Please rank the three stances—physical, design, and intentional—in order of how well each explains why the garden currently exhibits this specific water-channeling spiral. Give me a clear 1-to-3 ranking, with a brief sentence of reasoning for each placement.

Then, on a scale of 1 to 10, rate the explanatory importance of what I am calling the feedback-loop stance: treating the pattern as the accumulated result of an autonomous optimization process that no individual designed or intended. Finally, tell me whether you think this feedback-loop stance belongs inside one of the original three categories or requires its own, and if it belongs inside one, which one it most closely resembles and why.
