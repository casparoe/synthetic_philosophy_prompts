I am drafting the opening statement for a metaontology reading group and want a consistency check before presenting it. The view I have tentatively adopted combines several strands that seem individually plausible but whose joint tenability I now doubt. First, I hold that many traditional ontological disputes—those over universals, mereological composition, or the existence of mathematical sets—are best understood as verbal disagreements in the Carnapian sense: they reflect different choices of quantifier meaning or linguistic framework rather than competing descriptions of a shared reality. Second, I maintain that the dispute over whether phenomenal consciousness is reducible to physical brain processes is not verbal at all; both parties share the relevant concepts and disagree about an objective matter of fact. Third, I accept Quine’s ontological criterion—being among the values of bound variables in our best total theory—but I apply it only to entities indispensable to empirical science, leaving abstract objects discussed in pure metaphysics or mathematics outside its scope. Fourth, I take reflective equilibrium to be the correct method in metaontology: we should adjust either our theoretical ontological commitments or our pre-theoretic intuitive judgments until they achieve coherence, parallel to the method in normative ethics. Fifth, I deny that pragmatic considerations such as theoretical simplicity, explanatory fruitfulness, or predictive power should ever directly determine whether something exists; existence claims must be settled by indispensability arguments or conceptual clarification, never by utility alone. Sixth, I defend a specific diagnostic for verbal disputes: disagreement is genuinely semantic only if full clarification permits a conservative translation between the disputants’ frameworks that preserves every theoretical role played by the disputed terms; if translation requires abandoning a theoretical role that one party regards as indispensable, then despite surface semantic divergence the dispute remains substantive.

Please audit this package. Which commitments genuinely pull against one another, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Order the tensions by severity, stating explicitly what must be abandoned or weakened for consistency, and recommend which single change would resolve the most conflicts without collapsing the overall position. Do not treat every pairing as equally problematic; discriminate carefully.
