At a pub near the conference centre, two colleagues of mine—Sam and Jules—got into it over whether these big translation models actually understand anything. Sam keeps citing Hofstadter's old view that statistical translation is shallow, and he reckons LLMs haven't changed the picture: it's still just fancier pattern-matching across token pairs, not real grasp of meaning. Jules, who works on language models, said that's just biological chauvinism. If a system handles ambiguity, tone, and cultural references consistently, she argued, then denying it understanding just because it runs on weights rather than neurons is like saying a plane doesn't fly because it doesn't flap.

Sam came back with a scenario he'd cooked up: imagine the model translates he broke a leg correctly in a theatre review, but then you feed it he broke a finger in the same performative context, and it misses the irony entirely because it has no embodied sense of stage fright. So its success is just interpollation inside the training distribution, not actual comprehension of the concept. Jules said that was unfair—humans trip on novel compounds too, and modern LLMs do generalise to made-up metaphors if the semantic structure's clear. She reckoned Sam's brittleness argument proves too much.

Then Sam shifted: humans ask clarifying questions when confused; the model just recieves the highest-probability token. That lack of meta-cognitive awareness, he said, is part of what understanding requires. Jules called it moving the goalposts—from comprehension to consciousness. Sam replied it wasn't consciousness, just belief revision: a system that can't be genuinely surprised has no beliefs to update. Jules said that was circular—defining understanding as belief-revision, then declaring statistical systems out by fiat.

They turned to me and asked who's right at each turn. I honestly can't tell—Sam's pointing to something real about interpolation, but Jules is right that he's smuggling in human-only criteria. Can you walk through thier exchange move by move and rate each point? Say who's right about what, what both are missing, and whether functional success across genuinely novel contexts counts as understanding or just very good mimicry.
