I am a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun reading philosophy of time, and I want to organize my notes for a small interdisciplinary discussion group. Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy—not a prose comparison of named theories—that sorts positions on temporal ontology according to concrete, theory-independent commitments a respondent could endorse without knowing philosophical labels. The tree should branch at four successive nodes. First node: does the view treat past and future entities or events as existing in the same ontological sense as present ones, or not, or only in a derivative sense? Second node: if the view denies full existence to non-present items, is the present a globally extended hypersurface, a locally point-defined set, or undefined at physical scales; and does maintaining this commitment require a privileged inertial frame given the structure of special relativity? Third node: regardless of ontology, is tense treated as an irreducible, mind-independent feature of reality, or as reducible to tenseless relations, or as a purely linguistic device with no metaphysical import? Fourth node: for past- and future-tensed statements, does the view supply direct truthmakers in presently existing entities, indirect truthmakers in present traces or dispositions, branching possibilities, or deny bivalence altogether?

Map the following named positions onto the leaves: standard presentism with absolute simultaneity; relativistic presentism that denies any globally extended present; eternalism with a B-theoretic reduction of tense; the growing-block view; the moving-spotlight view; and a deflationary linguistic view that denies substantive temporal ontology. Where two positions occupy the same leaf, flag them rather than merging them. Identify any unoccupied combinations—such as global presentism combined with rejection of both privileged frames and relativistic constraints—and briefly diagnose whether the combination is incoherent, physically implausible, or merely unexplored.

Include two concrete diagnostic cases that can be answered without knowing theory names. Case one: two observers in uniform relative motion each correctly judge, by their own physical measurements, that a distant stellar event is occurring "now"; ask which branches each of the three plausible responses selects. Case two: every physical trace of a particular past event has been erased, yet a well-supported statement asserting that the event occurred is maintained; ask how each branch treats the truth conditions of that statement. Present the taxonomy as nested questions in words, with yes/no/partially answers at each node, and explicitly flag the points at which special-relativistic physics forces a philosophical choice rather than leaving it open. This intersection of physics and ontology is the angle I care about most.
