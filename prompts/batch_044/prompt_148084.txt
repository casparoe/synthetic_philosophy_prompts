I am a high school student taking an upper-level philosophy seminar, and our instructor has asked me to design an original examination item for our module on free will, moral responsibility, and the ethics of punishment. Rather than asking students to summarize standard compatibilist or hard-incompatibilist positions, I want a sixty-minute essay question built around a synthetic scenario: imagine a jurisdiction that completely abolishes retributive sentencing and replaces it with a predictive dangerousness algorithm that assigns preventive detention solely on statistical risk profiles, with no reference to past actions, mental states, or culpability.

Please draft the essay question itself, which should ask candidates to evaluate this system through Daniel Dennett's idea that free will functions as practical elbow room necessary for social coordination, and through Gregg Caruso's rejection of just deserts in favor of treating harmful conduct through a public-health quarantine analogy. The prompt should require students to assess whether the algorithmic framework satisfies Dennett's conditions for morally meaningful agency, whether it fulfills or undermines Caruso's quarantine ideal, and where the two accounts unexpectedly converge or diverge on the dignity of the detained person. Alongside the question, provide a model answer of roughly five hundred words and a marking scheme with five explicit criteria totaling twenty marks; for each criterion, state its point value and include a one-line description of the characteristic mistake that costs marks. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or verbatim quotations from texts; paraphrase all theoretical claims, and avoid referencing obscure secondary literature outside the scope of an advanced secondary-school course.
