I've been thinking about whether Frankfurt-style hierarchical compatibilism genuinely avoids van Inwagen's consequence argument or merely disguises an incompatibilist premise. If an agent is responsible only when she acts from second-order volitions with which she identifies, and those volitions are themselves fixed by prior causes she did not choose, then doesn't her identification depend on her being the ultimate source of her character—exactly the libertarian condition the compatibilist rejects? So is the deep-self view covertly incompatibilist, or is there a principled way to hold that I can genuinely identify with a causally determined character without requiring that I caused that character? Please push back plainly if my framing conflates identification with sourcehood, or if I'm misrepresenting what Frankfurt's hierarchy was meant to solve.
