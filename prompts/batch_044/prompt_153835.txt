In orthopedic evaluation, a total knee replacement is routinely graded not only on pain reduction and load-bearing capacity but also on “kinematic fidelity”—how closely the prosthesis reproduces the patient’s pre-arthritic gait mechanics. A device that improves stability and comfort can still be rated inferior if it yields motion patterns that deviate from the joint’s “natural” kinematic profile, and clinical guidelines typically treat restoration of physiological movement as an independent success criterion. By contrast, prosthetic limbs for amputees are assessed almost entirely through functional metrics—walking speed, energy expenditure, balance—with no comparable demand that they replicate the biomechanical signature of the missing limb.
