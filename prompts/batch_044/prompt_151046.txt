my sister's retiring from running a hospice next month and she's asked me to speak at her leaving do. about half the room will be palliative care docs who know nozick's experience machine inside out, the rest are family and nurses who wont. she's always joked that her job was running the machine in reverse -- not feeding false pleasures, but pulling the plug on machinery that kept people in prolonged suffering when there was no real welfare left to preserve. i want the speech to actually turn on the conceptual gap between experiential well-being and life-satisfaction accounts, using her 'reverse experience machine' framing as the spine. specifically, it needs to address whether stopping suffering counts as 'improving welfare' on an experiential view if the patient never reports feeling better, and whether a life-satisfaction theorist can even make sense of 'pulling the plug for their own good' without smuggling in experiential data. it needs to comfort people without pretending death is just another experience, and mustnt sound like a philosophy lecture. five minutes, casual, british tone. please dont quote nozick directly -- i dont want to misremember him in front of the experts.
