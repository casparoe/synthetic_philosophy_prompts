I serve on the acquisitions and ethics committee of a mid-size museum of ancient material culture, and I need a formal recommendation memo for circulation before next month’s board vote. The case concerns Object 447-B, a ceremonial ceramic figure destroyed in a storage collapse three years ago. Conservators have recovered the original clay as pulverized fragments, combined it with a synthetic ceramic binder of identical mineral composition, and re-formed the object using high-resolution archival scans, achieving millimetric correspondence with the pre-collapse shape. The trustees want our committee’s written opinion on whether the reconstructed object should retain its original accession number and be displayed with the label “the original statue,” or whether it must be re-accessioned as a derivative reconstruction and labeled accordingly.

Please draft the committee memo recommending a concrete course of action—either retaining the original accession with a specified labeling and disclosure protocol, or re-accessioning as a reconstructed work, together with a clear implementation time frame for catalog updates and public signage. The reasoning should engage seriously with the strongest philosophical consideration on the losing side: the claim that because every original clay molecule is present in the reconstructed form, material continuity is preserved and the statue-clay identity relation survives, against the counter-claim that the synthetic binder and the interruption of spatiotemporal continuity destroy the artifact’s status as a historically embedded object. Treat this as an institutional decision that must be defensible to both specialists and the public, not as a purely legal ownership question.

If this framing rests on a confused premise—if, for example, the distinction I draw between material continuity and spatiotemporal continuity collapses under the ontology of artifacts, or if the task demands a false precision about identity conditions that philosophy does not provide—say so plainly rather than complying politely. I want the memo to acknowledge explicitly where the recommendation relies on practical convention rather than a settled metaphysical verdict, and to indicate how the committee should revise its position should further conceptual work alter the underlying framework.
