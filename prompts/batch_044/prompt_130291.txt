Hey, I'm taking a grad course on conceptual structure and we've been arguing about Hofstadter and Sander's Surfaces and Essences — specifically their claim that analogy isn't just one mental tool among others but literally the core of all cognition. My studdy partner built this argument that seem to turn their own framework against them and I can't find the exit. She says (1) if analogy is the heart of cognition, then every concept we possess must have been generated by mapping relations from some prior concept. (2) They distinguish surface analogies, which just match superficial features, from deep analogies that map underlying relational structures, and they claim the deep ones are what drive real conceptual change. (3) But to recognize which surface features are irrelevant and which relational structures should be mapped, you already need the conceptual categories that the analogy is supposed to create. (4) So deep analogy can't bootstrap concepts from scratch; it can only reshuffle structures you already have. (5) Therefore analogy can't be the core mechanism of cognition — it has to depend on some prior non-analogical machinery, maybe innate constraints or direct structural perception. (6) But Hofstadter and Sander reject innate conceptual frameworks precisely because they think analogical slippage does all the bootstrapping work. (7) So either deep analogy is circular, or analogy isn't fundamental, and either way the view collapses. I feel like (3) might be confusing learning a concept with applying it, but if I drop (3) I need to explain how anyone ever makes a genuinely deep analogy without already owning the relevant concepts, which sounds like I'm sneaking in the innate stuff they explicitly deny.
