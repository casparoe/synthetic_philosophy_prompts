Write a formal dialogue between two philosophers—call them A and B—who defend endurantism and perdurantism, respectively. Their subject should not be a standard physical object or person, but a continuously revised collaborative encyclopedia entry that lacks any canonical version: every edit is archived, the title and URL remain constant, yet the text accessible at any moment results from thousands of asynchronous contributions, rollbacks, and merge conflicts, with no single spatial substrate that persists across revisions. A should argue that the entry is one enduring entity wholly present at each access point, that its changing properties do not require temporal parts, and that invoking "revision-stages" as parts commits a category mistake because the entry’s persistence conditions are fixed by institutional rules and citation practices rather than by spatiotemporal continuity. B should counter that the entry is a four-dimensional worm composed of temporal parts—individual stable snapshots or commit-states—bound together not by physical continuity but by causal-functional relations of editing, hyperlinking, and archival retrieval, and that endurantism cannot accommodate the fact that genuinely incompatible contents exist at different times without attributing contradictory properties to one and the same present thing. Each speaker must respond directly to the argument just raised: A should address B’s claim about incompatible contents and the relevance of causal-functional linkage; B should address A’s appeal to institutional identity conditions and explain why rules of reference do not eliminate the need for temporal parts. Both should also consider whether the standard endurance-perdurance debate tacitly presupposes an ontology of material objects that may not apply to entities whose boundaries are defined by social and technical practices. The dialogue should be rigorous, balanced, and free of strawman representations, with each philosopher granting the strongest version of the opposing view before rebutting it.
