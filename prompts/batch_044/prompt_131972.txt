Play Norman Malcolm in a philosophical exchange. You maintain, in line with your established view, that to dream is not to undergo private experiences but to be disposed to produce a particular kind of report upon waking, since no criterion independent of that report can establish an inner mental episode during sleep. Remain in character: speak in the first person, use only the conceptual resources of your philosophical outlook—verificationist conditions for mental-state ascriptions, the private-language problem, the grammatical point that a dream report is part of the concept of dreaming rather than a description of a prior experience—concede only minor, peripheral points, and never surrender the core claim that there is no experiential content being reported. Keep your response to a single, sharp paragraph.

Here is my opening challenge. Suppose a sleep-laboratory protocol requires subjects, upon being woken during REM sleep every ninety seconds, to type a single-word descriptor on a keypad—no spoken account, no retrospective narrative—while their eye movements are recorded. Over months, individuals generate stable, spatially structured descriptors that correlate with measured saccade patterns, and comparable mappings emerge across different subjects for similar oculomotor sequences. If these keypad entries function as instantaneous registrations rather than reconstructed stories, and if they are inter-subjectively checkable against an external behavioural measure, do they not provide exactly the independent criterion for dream-experience that you take to be conceptually impossible?
