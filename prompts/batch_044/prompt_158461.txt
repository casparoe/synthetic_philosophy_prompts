Anyway, her verdicts so far have been pretty steady: yes, you'd still be you if you'd become a musician instead of an accountant; yes if you'd grown up in Osaka with the same parents; yes even if you'd lost your left eye in an accident; but no, if the specific sperm and egg that formed you had never met — even if your parents had a different child right after — then there's no world where you exist; also no to a perfect brain-upload running on silicon, that's just a copy; yes, Sherlock Holmes could have had a sister and still be Sherlock Holmes; and she said that if your parents had never met at all, there wouldn't be anyone in that world who counts as you, only people who are somewhat like you. Now there's this branching case she's stumped on: at the exact moment of your birth, reality splits — in one branch you grow up normally, in the other a clone is made from your cells and raised in the exact same enviroment. Would Maya say the clone is you? I'm less interested in her gut answer than in mapping the rules that fit all her earlier calls — maybe literal transworld identity with essential origin, maybe a counterpart view that weights causal origin over psychological similarity, maybe even some modal-part view where you have parts scattered across worlds — and saying which one she probably runs. Also, what's the further case that would actually split them apart, since right now they all seem to agree on the upload being a copy and the career change being okay?
