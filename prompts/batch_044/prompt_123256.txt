I was arguing with my friend after a seminar on alignment — we were wondering, if an AI is both superintelligent and extremely persuasive, what should the rules for talking to humans actually look like? I dont mean generic 'be truthful' guidelines. I want the full desiderata for a normative theory of communicative ethics in conditions of extreme cognitive asymmetry.

Write the list of requirements any such theory has to meet to count as adequate. The cases it must get right include: when the AI can model the human's belief-update mechanism better than the human can, so that any explanation is automatically optimized for persuasion; when correcting a false hinge belief in one person risks cascading epistemic collapse in their social network; and when the AI has to choose between saying something strictly accurate but triggering defensive overreaction versus framing it to preserve autonomy but slightly shift emphasis. For each, say why its a hard constraint versus a camp preference — e.g., rationalists might demand full transparency of reasoning traces, while democratic-deliberation camps might insist on procedural equality in the exchange. Respect the distinction between persuasion and manipulation, and dont collapse them. Also flag if you think some subset of these is jointly unsatisfiable — I'm particularly suspicious that complete reasoning transparency and protection of human doxastic autonomy cant both be maximized under high modeling asymmetry.

Separate whats settled in the philosophy and psychology literature from whats genuinely contested, and dont present a disputed view as the consensus. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than scoring existing theories, though if you briefly note how transparency-maximalist versus autonomy-protective approaches fare against the list, thats fine. Put a one-sentence summary on the very last line by itself.
