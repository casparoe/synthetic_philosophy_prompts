I'm working through Wittgenstein's On Certainty for a small reading group, and I want to close our session on hinge epistemology with a poem that does structural work rather than just name-checking hinges. Write a Shakespearean sonnet—strict iambic pentameter, rhyme scheme ABAB CDCD EFEF GG—on the status of hinge propositions such as the claim that I have hands, or that the earth existed long before my birth. The octave should present the apparent paradox: these propositions stand fast without evidential support, yet they make all other knowledge and doubt possible. Use the ABAB rhyme to bind pairs that evoke a secure foundation—say, ground with found, stand with hand—so the rhyme scheme itself enacts the foundationalist picture that the content will undermine. At the volta in line 9, pivot sharply: the speaker should recognize that hinges are not especially well-supported empirical claims but something closer to rules of grammar or to the slowly shifting riverbed of thought—scaffolding that holds the language-game up from outside rather than foundations inside it. The sestet should then show that doubting a hinge is not defeated by stronger evidence but is revealed as a move that cannot get started without the hinge already in place. Close with a couplet that uses a deliberate slant rhyme—perhaps game against frame—to signal that the hinge has no independent descriptive name outside the practice it makes possible. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows the distinction between hinge commitments and empirical propositions, and the riverbed metaphor, recognizes the argument being made, and the turn at line 9 should feel like the conceptual shift from epistemic justification to grammatical framing.
