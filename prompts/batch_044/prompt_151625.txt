You mediate disputes in a worker-owned cooperative whose charter abolishes formal titles and hierarchical ranks. Members increasingly file grievances that they are not “recognized,” but the conflicts mix status, labor attribution, and distributive fairness. Drawing specifically on Hegel’s master-slave dialectic—distinguishing one-sided recognition, the struggle for pure prestige that risks life and death, and the achievement of mutual recognition through formative labor and the emergence of self-consciousness—construct a twenty-minute decision procedure that a mediator with no philosophical training can apply. The procedure should ask ordered questions, specify what answer stops the process with a clear finding, identify where the mediator must make an irreducible value judgment rather than apply a rule, and avoid reducing recognition to modern liberal notions of respect or dignity. Apply it to two cases: first, a veteran member demands that newcomers address her by an honorific title the charter explicitly forbids, claiming that without it her experience is erased; second, a designer claims her contribution is invisible because the cooperative’s internal attribution system only logs the final assembler of a product, not the conceptual originator. Include one case in which the procedure yields a result the mediator will find normatively unwelcome, and explicitly flag the point at which the dispute becomes a question of cooperative values that the conceptual framework cannot settle.
