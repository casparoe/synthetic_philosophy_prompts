In our philosophy of language seminar we discussed Douglas Hofstadter's argument in Le Ton beau de Marot that formal constraints—meter, rhyme scheme, syllable count—are not ornamental but partly constitutive of poetic meaning, so that a translation which abandons them alters content rather than merely form. A classmate then applied this thesis to machine-generated verse: if a large language model can reproduce the formal architecture of a highly constrained poem in another language without any semantic understanding of the source, does this demonstrate that form and meaning are separable after all? Or does it instead expose that Hofstadter's account implicitly requires an intentional author whose grasp of meaning binds form and content together? Please describe the most important implications of this challenge for translation theory, particularly regarding whether formal preservation can ever be sufficient for meaning preservation in the absence of authorial comprehension.
