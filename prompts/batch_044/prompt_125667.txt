I am drafting a 90-minute essay question for our political philosophy PhD comprehensive exam on dirty hands, and I want it to resist summary-level answers about Walzer or Sartre. To that end, I am building the prompt around a distinction I want candidates to manipulate rather than recall: call it delegated stain versus executive stain. Delegated stain occurs when a leader authorizes a wrongful act—say, coercive interrogation—through institutional layers so that she does not personally execute it, yet knows and approves; executive stain occurs when the leader personally orders or performs the wrong. The question should ask candidates whether this proximity-based distinction resolves, complicates, or collapses the classic dirty-hands problem, specifically by testing whether moral responsibility tracks authorization regardless of institutional distance and whether democratic citizens inherit the stain through representative consent. I need the exam question itself, a model answer of approximately 650 words, and a marking scheme totaling 20 points that separates A-level work from B-level work by specifying five required conceptual moves—each with its point value and a one-line note on the typical failure mode—so that graders can apply it consistently without relying on citation density. The prompt should be answerable through conceptual reasoning alone, with no expectation of obscure bibliography, but it should be impossible to answer well by merely rehearsing the standard debate.
