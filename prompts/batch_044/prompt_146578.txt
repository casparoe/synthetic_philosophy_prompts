I'm helping a small philosophy-of-mathematics research group design a three-year conceptual program for four researchers with no empirical laboratory or formal logic specialization. They want to focus on actual infinity—specifically the conceptual status of Hilbert's hotel, Cantor's distinction between transfinite and absolute infinity, and the various finitist positions that reject completed infinite totalities—but they need a defended agenda rather than a survey. They must decide which open questions deserve their limited effort, in what order, and with what success conditions.

To make the discussion concrete, I've been working with a distinction that doesn't map neatly onto the standard potentialist versus actualist divide. Call it the difference between uniform-pairing finitism and sequential-potentialist finitism. The first holds that comparing the sizes of two collections requires a uniform pairing procedure applicable to every element simultaneously, and that Hilbert's hotel fails precisely because accommodating an additional guest demands re-pairing the entire completed set of occupants, an operation that no finitist ontology permits. The second accepts that each individual reassignment—moving the occupant of room 1 to room 2, then room 2 to room 3, and so forth—is conceptually legitimate one step at a time, but denies that there exists a completed state in which every reassignment has been performed and the hotel is fully occupied with no vacant rooms. I want to know whether clarifying which of these, if either, captures the core finitist objection is genuinely upstream of other work, or whether it is largely a side branch that can be bracketed.

Given that framing, what are the three or four open conceptual lines of inquiry in this area that matter most for such a group? Please defend a specific ordering: for each question, explain why it is tractable for philosophers without specialized backgrounds in physics or advanced set theory, how resolving it would affect the others on your list, and what kind of conceptual clarification or result over the next two to three years would cause you to reorder them. I'm particularly interested in whether resolving the status of Cantor's absolute infinity—whether it functions as a coherent limit concept or harbors an internal contradiction—carries real weight for adjacent debates, such as conceptualizing unbounded spatial extension in cosmology or the coherence of infinite ethical obligations, or whether those connections are mostly rhetorical and should not drive the research agenda. I do not want bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want the reasoning that justifies where limited intellectual effort should go and why one upstream resolution changes what is rational to pursue downstream.
