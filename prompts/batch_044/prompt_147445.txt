I'm on our hospital ethics commitee and we have a case that's tying everyone in knots. A 67-year-old patient was declared dead by neurologic criteria after a stroke. The family are practicing Daoists and they don't dispute the brain-death finding — they accept she's not breathing on her own — but they insist that declaring her 'dead' now is metaphysically premature. They read Zhuangzi as saying death is not a point-event but a transformation (hua) in which the bodily and spiritual elements gradually disentangle over several days, like the butterfly not knowing whether it's the dreamer or the dreamed. They want us to delay organ procurement and burial arrangements for a full week so this process isn't interrupted. Our policy allows reasonable religious accomodation but caps it at 24 hours for infection control. Some commitee members say this is just biology denial; others say we're forcing a biomedical ontology onto a legitimate metaphysics and committing hermeneutical injustice. I need a short memo for Thursday's meeting recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a concrete timeframe. Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble and don't restate the question. Take their view seriously — don't just say 'science wins' — and include an honest, strong argument for the side you don't take. No bibliographies or citation lists, please — just the opinion I can circulate.
