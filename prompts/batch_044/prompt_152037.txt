A study-guide summary of Confucian role ethics presents the view as follows: all moral obligations are generated exclusively by one's position within five fixed relational pairs—parent and child, ruler and subject, husband and wife, elder and younger sibling, and friend and friend—so that there is no meaningful idea of a moral agent independent of these roles. It describes zhengming, or rectification of names, as functioning like an automatic behavioral script: once a role-label is correctly applied, the corresponding conduct follows mechanically, leaving no space for individual moral judgment or cultivation. The summary further claims that classical texts embed what it calls an asymmetric transparency norm, requiring subordinates to make their reasoning fully visible to superiors while allowing superiors strategic opacity, and that this structural feature makes role ethics fundamentally incompatible with democratic equality. Finally, it concludes that because these roles are assigned by birth, gender, and age, Confucian ethics amounts to a biological caste system with no resources for social mobility or adopted kinship. How fair is this characterization? Separate what is broadly accurate, what subtly misrepresents standard interpretations, and what is a serious misreading; then say whether the conclusions about automatic scripting, asymmetric transparency, and biological determinism actually follow from the accurate premises.
