Write a Petrarchan sonnet—octave with enclosed rhyme (ABBA ABBA), sestet with interlaced rhyme (CDE CDE)—that enacts Foucault’s distinction between sovereign and disciplinary power as developed in Discipline and Punish. Let the octave establish the panopticon not as a dungeon but as a luminous architecture of perpetual visibility: the guard unseen, the inmate always potentially observed. The rhyme’s enclosure should mirror the circular economy of surveillance—each line turning back on itself—while the imagery stays precise: the central tower, the cellular division, the light that renders bodies readable rather than hidden. At the volta (line 9), pivot sharply: abandon the model of power as mere repression or prohibition (the scaffold, the sword, the sovereign’s right to take life) and introduce the productive, capillary logic of normalization. In the sestet, render the examination—the record, the file, the clinical gaze—not as an intrusion upon a pre-existing subject but as the very mechanism that constitutes the subject as an object of knowledge. Show the norm operating simultaneously as measurement and production: deviation is not prior to the standard but generated by it. The closing couplet should crystallize the paradox that disciplinary power works best when it is internalized, so that the watched become the watchers and reproduce the norm without external command. The form should do philosophical work: the octave’s locked rhymes should feel like the closed circuit of visibility, and the sestet’s turn should enact the conceptual shift from “power over” to “power through.” Write it so that a reader familiar with Foucault’s claim that knowledge and power are co-constitutive—especially his analysis of the examination as a ritual of objectification—would recognize the argument in the structure, while a reader unfamiliar with it would still encounter a coherent, evocative poem about how being seen becomes a way of being made.
