I was arguing with my friend last night about that Dennett color-phi thing and the Orwell vs Stalin editing metaphors, you know, the ones about whether your brain rewrites the past or just assembles fragments? He walked me into this argument and I honestly cant find the exit.

So here's the chain he laid out, step by step:
(1) In color phi you see one dot slide from left to right and turn green in the middle, but there's never actually any stimulus in the middle position.
(2) The brain only registers the red dot on the left and the green dot on the right, then produces the report of smooth motion after both have arrived.
(3) Dennett frames this as either an Orwellian revision - you experienced red then green, but your memory gets retroactively rewritten so you think you saw motion - or a Stalinesque revision, where there's never a unified experience at all, just fragments spliced together like propaganda footage.
(4) Either version means the conscious report of "I saw motion" is constructed post-hoc, not a direct read-out of whats happening in real time.
(5) But if the brain can do that for motion and color, there's no principled reason it cant do it for literally everything - every moment of consciousness might just be retroactive editing or proactive stitching of sensory fragments.
(6) So either all conscious experience is some kind of fabricated narrative with no direct access to reality, or we have to claim color phi is a special case with a different mechanism.
(7) Claiming its special just protects our intuition that normal perception is direct, which feels circular.
(8) Therefore the revision model either collapses into universal skepticism about experience, or its ad hoc.

He says this proves Dennett's framework proves too much - it either makes us doubt everything we see or it saves itself by special pleading. I feel like something slipped between (4) and (5), maybe about whether "post-hoc report" equals "post-hoc experience," but I cant name the mistake. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost? I'd really like to keep (2) since the psychophysics seems solid, and I'd prefer not to end up saying all my experience is fake.
