For a philosophy of population ethics seminar, I want to move beyond the standard rehearsal of totalism versus average utilitarianism. Before I evaluate any specific axiology, I need the full specification of what an adequate theory of population value must actually deliver. Write the desiderata: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it cannot collapse, the puzzles it must dissolve or accommodate, and the constraints from rational choice under uncertainty that it must respect.

The theory must, of course, address the repugnant conclusion—where a vast population of lives barely worth living is rated better than a smaller population of flourishing lives—but I want the list to go further. Include a requirement that the theory handle a reverse variant, in which an enormous population of extraordinarily happy lives is judged worse because welfare is distributed across identity-indeterminate generations in a way that violates some structural constraint, such as continuity or the moral significance of replacement versus addition. It must navigate the non-identity problem without collapsing into pure actualism; it must preserve distinctions between adding people, replacing lineages, and altering the welfare trajectory of existing ones; and it must yield determinate comparisons under conditions of partial ignorance about which potential people will exist, since real policy never operates with omniscience about future populations.

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or theoretical tension behind it, flag whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of a particular camp, and identify any subsets that appear jointly unsatisfiable—especially whether avoiding both the standard and the reverse repugnant conclusions conflicts with transitivity, completeness, or continuity of the ordering. Then, very briefly, indicate how totalism, a critical-level view, and a person-affecting restriction fare against the list, but keep the emphasis on the criteria themselves: my aim is to see where the philosophical labor is actually being done, not just to crown a winner.
