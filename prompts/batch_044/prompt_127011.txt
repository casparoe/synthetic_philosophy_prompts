I sit on our city's Climate Legacy Board (we meet every other tuesday, usually in the basement). There's this proposal for a 200-year carbon storage covenant that binds future residents to maintain the site, with penalties if they walk away. The engineers say its just long-term infrastructure, but one board member — she's a philospher — argues you can't have joint obligations to people who don't exist yet, since collective intentionality needs co-present agents with shared mental states. She also brought up this niche idea she calls "asymetric dependency": we're not merely leaving them risks, we're locking them into a structure where stopping the project causes collapse, so they're coerced into continuing our "joint action" without ever having consented. The other side says future people will be net beneficiaries, and framing climate adaptation as an intergenerational joint venture is more honest than pretending each generation starts fresh. We vote next thursday. Can you draft the board's decision memmo? Recommend approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, with a concrete course of action and a time frame. Take the strongest objection seriously — especially the non-existence and coerced-continuation point — don't just dismiss it by saying they'll be grateful. Don't ask for citations or a reading list; I need something we can actually circulate and act on.
