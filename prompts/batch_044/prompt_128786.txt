I want a classroom activity/game that makes them actually feel the force of illusionism about phenominal consciousness, not just nod along. The specific angle I care about is what we might call "phenomenal ghosting": the idea that when you introspect, your brain generates a kind of empty placeholder — a ghost of presence — and then mistakes that placeholder for an actual qualitative property. So participants should run into the problem themselves: they try to pin down exactly what they're experiencing, and keep finding that the pin only touches representational content, never a raw feel.

Design the rules, materials, and debrief. I want roles: one student is the reporter who has to describe their current experience in real time, and the others are interogators who ask questions designed to show that every description collapses into functional or relational terms. Include a payoff scheme using points or tokens (not real money) that rewards the interogators for catching a ghost reference and rewards the reporter for avoiding one. Then map each round onto parts of the illusionist story — the transparency of introspection, the misattribution of presence, etc.

Also tell me what will predictably go wrong — probably the reporter insists they really do have direct access — and be honest about which feature of illusionism the activity can't reproduce, specifically the claim that there is literally no phenominal property at all, since the game still requires participants to have experiences they're describing. Keep it runnable without tech. Oh and no lecture up front; they shouldn't hear the term illusionism until after the game.
