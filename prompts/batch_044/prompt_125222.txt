I was discussing with a colleague whether Pascal’s well-known pairing of justice and force—whereby justice lacking power remains impotent, and power lacking justice becomes tyrannical, so that we ought to render force just—retains its coherence in institutional contexts where enforcement is not exercised by a single sovereign will but is instead distributed across opaque, automated, or multi-layered mechanisms. I am particularly interested in situations where the rules governing the application of coercive power are themselves produced and executed by systems that no individual agent fully authors or comprehends. Give an example of a conceptual issue, rather than a purely practical or technical difficulty, that arises when one attempts to apply the Pascalian framework to such a setting. Your example should make precise where the tension lies—perhaps between the visibility of justice and the invisibility of enforcement logic, or between the moral responsibility of those who design the system and those who are subjected to its force—and should explain why it poses a genuine philosophical puzzle about legitimacy rather than an engineering problem to be solved by better transparency. Please present your answer in British English, and do not include bibliographic references, citations, or requests for further reading.
