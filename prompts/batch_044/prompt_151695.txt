At the same time, in "Truth and Probability" he develops a subjectivist account of belief as partial credence governed by coherence and linked to action through expected utility, an account that permits rational degrees of belief in propositions that outstrip direct empirical verification. The precise question for the project is whether Ramsey's subjectivist framework implicitly undermines his dismissal: if belief and value can be rationally assigned even where full empirical verification is absent, then treating life as having a particular meaning might be rehabilitated as a meaningful practical commitment—an object of graded belief coupled to utility—rather than a pseudo-factual query. Alternatively, does his logical analysis in the Epilogue block this move by denying that meaning can function as a property assigned to life in the way utility can?
