I'm not an academic or anything — I just volunteer running repair approvals for our little housing co-op in Leeds, twelve flats max, but the by-laws are weirdly formal and date back to hte 70s. I've been thinking about that idea from Hofstadter's book, the MU-puzzle and jumping out of the system — JOOTSing I think it's called? Where you're stuck inside rules that literally can't reach the answer you want, and the only fix is to change the rules themselves rather than trying harder within them.

We get about three or four requests a week that don't fit the categories — like replacing a boiler with a heat pump when the rules only mention 'like-for-like gas repairs', or using shared funds for a tenant's private mould treatment, or this awkward one where a neighbour wants emergency cash for legal fees after a dispute I'd really rather not approve. I need a decision procedue I can run in 20 minutes flat, with my co-chair signing off, that tells me step-by-step: is this a real MIU-style dead end where the current by-laws can't produce a fair answer, meaning we have to propose a rule change (jump out), or are we just being lazy and there's actually a valid path inside the system if we read it right?

Write it as ordered questions, what counts as enough to move on, where it stops with 'no, stay inside the rules', where it hands over to the committee because it's about values not logic, and flag the bits that are still judgement calls dressed up as steps. Then apply it to three cases: solar panels on a roof never mentioned in the rules; that legal-fees request I mentioned — be honest even if it tells me to approve something I hate; and a genuinely ambiguous one where it's not clear if we're stuck or just confused. Also say clearly what the procedue can't do — for instance, it won't tell us whether a new rule is fair, only whether we need one — and keep it practical, I'm just a volunteer with a spreadsheet and no philosophy degree.
