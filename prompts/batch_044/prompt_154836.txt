Consider Bertrand's paradox: selecting a chord at random in a circle yields probabilities of 1/3, 1/2, or 1/4 that it exceeds the side of an inscribed equilateral triangle, depending on whether one randomizes endpoints, radial directions, or midpoints. The principle of indifference directs us to distribute belief equally over outcomes that are indistinguishable given available information. If we apply this principle at a meta-level—treating the three parameterization methods themselves as indistinguishable because we lack any prior reason to favor one—should we assign each method equal weight and combine their results? Or does this move misunderstand the paradox, which seems to show that indifference must be applied within a precisely specified sample space rather than across competing specifications?
