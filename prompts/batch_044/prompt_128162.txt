The other day I was arguing with a friend over coffee — she kept saying that training yourself to introspect is basically like calabrating a misalligned telescope: there's noise and bias, but with enough repeated observation you can Bayes your way to reliable first-person data. I said that sounds wrong because of the transparency thesis — when you turn your attention inward you don't see mental machinery, you just see the world — so there's nothing 'there' to calibrate, more like that refrigerator-light illusion where consciousness only seems constant because you're always checking. But now I'm wondering if I've smuggled in a false dichotomy. Is the mistake in treating introspection as a measurement instrument at all, or is it in assuming that unreliability in the perceptual sense (noise, misattribution, etc.) applies to phenominal states in the first place? Could you map out exactly where the analogy with instrument calibration breaks, and whether there's any coherent sense in which we could 'update' on our own experience without presupposing the very access we're trying to validate?
