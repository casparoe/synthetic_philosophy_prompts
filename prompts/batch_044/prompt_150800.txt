I am preparing a consistency audit for a discussion group on historical explanation. By background I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy or sociology, but I have tried to work out a coherent personal stance on Weber’s Protestant ethic thesis and on whether cultural values can be genuine causes rather than post-hoc rationalizations. Before I present, I want my commitments checked for hidden conflicts. Here is where I stand:

(i) Weber was essentially correct that the Calvinist work ethic contributed causally to the emergence of modern capitalism; it was not merely an ideological reflection of already-changing economic structures, but an independent factor that helped set the trajectory.

(ii) Explaining an outcome by citing values or norms belongs to a different logical form from mechanistic causal explanation — it is interpretive understanding of meaningful action, not subsumption under covering laws or physical mechanisms.

(iii) Despite that logical difference, a cultural explanation should still satisfy a counterfactual condition: if the relevant value or norm had been absent with other conditions held constant, the outcome should have differed; otherwise the cultural factor is redundant.

(iv) The subjectivity and normativity of values do not bar them from objective causal narratives, provided they are socially institutionalized and actually motivate agents. There is no contradiction in treating “anxiety about predestination” as a factor shaping investment behavior.

(v) In organizational settings — especially technology firms — appeals to “culture” are typically ex post facto rationalizations of structural incentives: hiring filters, equity structures, and market selection. Attributing success to “a culture of innovation” usually redescribes material conditions in normative language.

(vi) At the same time, culture can do explanatory work that pure incentives cannot: shared normative frameworks can render certain institutional arrangements literally unthinkable to actors, thereby constraining the design space of possible economies rather than merely selecting among pre-given options.

Please treat these as my settled views. I am not signaling which ones I would prefer to abandon.

Audit them as follows:

- Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection. In particular, examine the relationship between (ii) and (iii); between accepting macro-historical cultural causation (i) and dismissing organizational cultural explanations (v); and between allowing institutionalized values into objective accounts (iv) while remaining skeptical of cultural talk in tech (v).

- For each genuine tension, rank them by severity (most severe first). For each, state the cheapest revision — which single commitment should be modified or weakened, and what that costs the rest of the set.

- Finally, tell me which single revision — abandoning or substantially weakening one commitment — would clean up the most tensions with the least overall damage, and give the full audit ordered by severity rather than as a flat list of equally bad conflicts.
