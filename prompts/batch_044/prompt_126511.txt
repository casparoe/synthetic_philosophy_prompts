In my grad seminar on personal identity, students keep reaching for the server-migration analogy for Parfit's teletransporter: your body is the old hardware, your psychologcal profile is the OS and data, and the scanner/printer is just moving the files to a new machine. They use it to push that physical continuty is irrelevant and that psychological continuity is all that matters for survival. Can you map out exactly what corresponds to what — hardware, software, teh destructive scan, the new server — and then tell me where teh analogy breaks? I'm especially interested in whether it quietly imports a functionalist picture of the self that Parfit himself doesn't commit to, and whether fission cases (where the scan goes to two printers) have any analog in server migration. Once the disanalogies are on the table, does the conclusion — that you genuinely survive — still stand, or was teh analogy carrying all the weight? I don't need a bibliography, just the argument.
