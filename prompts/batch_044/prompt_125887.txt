For my epistemology seminar I am drafting a methodological profile on reasoning under disagreement and want a consistency audit before I present it. Here are the commitments I am trying to hold together:

(1) In empirical and forecasting domains I take the outside view—reference-class base rates—to be the rational default, permitting inside-view adjustments only when I can decompose the problem and identify specific causal differences that distinguish me from the class.

(2) I think epistemic modesty requires treating my philosophical judgments as falling within the reference class “trained philosophers evaluating conceptual claims in domains lacking predictive resolution criteria,” for which historical base rates show persistent, structurally unresolved disagreement.

(3) At the same time, I hold that philosophy genuinely lacks predictive resolution criteria—there is no scorecard—so constructing that reference class requires artificial stipulation of analogies, which undermines the reliability of the base rate itself.

(4) From (3) I infer that in philosophical domains I should weight my inside view more heavily than the outside view, on the grounds that my training gives me differential access to argumentative structure and that an ambiguous base rate should not dominate a well-structured inside view.

(5) I reject the claim that modesty entails deference to disciplinary consensus, because I take consensus formation in conceptual domains to be driven partly by sociological and rhetorical dynamics rather than by convergence on truth.

(6) I also maintain that overconfidence is a structurally predictable bias in philosophical reasoning, and that calibration requires systematically downward-adjusting confidence in direct proportion to the ambiguity of the relevant reference class.

The package feels coherent, but I suspect hidden pulls. Does (4) contradict (1) in a principled way, or can I defend domain-sensitive weighting without ad hocness? Is there a genuine tension between applying the high-disagreement base rate in (2) and then discounting its force in (4) via (3), or does (3) genuinely dissolve the conflict? Does (5) sit comfortably with (2) and (6), or does rejecting consensus deference undermine the calibration mechanism I invoke? And is (6) even coherent with (3): if there is no resolution criteria, what supplies the metric for “downward adjustment”?

Please audit these commitments pair by pair. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear so, rank the real tensions by severity, specify the least costly revision for each, and then say which single revision would clean up the most tension overall. Do not treat every pair as problematic; discriminate carefully. I am not signaling which commitment I most want to preserve, so treat the audit as neutral.
