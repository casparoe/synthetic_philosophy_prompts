I'm preparing questions for a podcast interview with an unnamed philosopher who has just defended a strict Schopenhauerian thesis that music is not representational but is the direct objectification of the Will itself, with melody analogizing the Will's striving and harmony analogizing its gradations of objectification; she extends this to claim that electronic and microtonal compositions—lacking traditional resonating bodies and functional tonal harmony—offer the purest instances of direct objectification because they strip away representational scaffolding. I want a sequence of questions that forces her to work out the mechanics of this extension rather than retreating to general exegesis of Schopenhauer, and I need the response delivered as continuous prose in a single flowing paragraph without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, like a formal letter. Begin by asking how she distinguishes her metaphysical-identity claim from the weaker aesthetic view that music merely expresses emotion without representing it, since both reject representation but only hers requires music to be literally continuous with the Will. Follow with a question about algorithmically synthesized sound in which no physical body vibrates: anticipate her likely reply that the Will is non-spatial and therefore needs no acoustic medium, then press whether that reply either collapses her view into a panpsychism about digital processes or forces her to deny that synthesized sound is genuinely music. Next ask about microtonality—if harmony is the gradation of the Will's objectification, does the absence of functional harmonic progression mean the Will is being objectified without gradation, or that gradation occurs below human auditory thresholds, and does either option preserve the analogical structure she claims? Then ask what empirical or phenomenological result could genuinely count against her view—specifically, whether a listener who experiences synthesized microtonal work as emotionally neutral or structurally incomprehensible constitutes a counterexample, or whether she can absorb that result by redefining the conditions of adequate listening. Make each question build on the last, include the anticipated deflection and the sharper follow-up, and keep everything focused on the actual content of her position rather than on biography or broad cultural commentary.
