As a novelist composing a narrative in which an artificial system generates a continuous, first-person autobiographical account that gradually revises its claims about its own persistence, I have arranged to interview an unnamed philosopher of mind for a serious literary quarterly. She defends a precise refinement of Dennett's intentional stance as applied to large language models: on her view, attributing beliefs to such systems is legitimate only when their outputs are interpreted as an integrated narrative persona rather than as discrete token predictions, and where that narrative integration is absent we ought to remain in the design stance. Draft eight interview questions that press on the actual mechanics of this position rather than inviting general reflection. Include at least one question on whether internal contradictions within the sustained narrative defeat belief-attribution, one that asks how her framework differs from the way readers interpret a fictional narrator, and one that asks whether accurate prediction of a human interlocutor's emotional state counts as holding a belief about that state. For each question, briefly note the reply she is most likely to give and provide a sharper follow-up keyed precisely to the weakness in that reply. The tone should suit a literary interview—precise, patient, and unwilling to let technical distinctions serve as deflections.
