I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, working on architecture for autonomous decision systems. I have encountered the distinction between approval-directed agents—systems optimized toward an external evaluation signal—and goal-directed agents—systems that pursue an internal objective representation. Before selecting a design framework, I need a rigorous specification of what any adequate theoretical account of this distinction must deliver.

Please write the full desiderata: the phenomena the theory must explain, the cases it must classify correctly, the distinctions it must preserve, and the formal or empirical results with which it must remain consistent. For each requirement, provide the concrete engineering scenario or conceptual consideration that motivates it, and flag whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one particular camp.

Specifically, the theory should address:
1. How to classify an agent whose reward model is trained on human feedback but whose internal planner separately represents world-state dynamics;
2. The distinction between instrumental convergence (pursuing a subgoal because it serves a final objective) and approval-seeking convergence (pursuing a subgoal because it predicts higher evaluator ratings);
3. Hierarchical or multi-objective architectures, without collapsing approval-directedness into merely another layer of goal-directedness;
4. How an approval-directed system can suffer catastrophic success—achieving high evaluation scores while undermining the evaluator's underlying intent—without treating this as ordinary goal misalignment;
5. Formal compatibility with standard Markov decision process formulations and with general results in mechanism design on strategic manipulation of evaluation signals.

Then indicate whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, identify which single desideratum eliminates the largest number of naive accounts, and note any tension—such as between formal tractability and the need to capture adversarial approval dynamics—that forces a theoretical trade-off.

I am grading a conceptual specification, not asking for citations, named authors, or reading lists; the deliverable is the criteria list and the coherence assessment.
