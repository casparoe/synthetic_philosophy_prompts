I am drafting a chapter for my thesis on eliminativist strategies in philosophy of mind, and I have become particularly interested in the argument—associated with Chase and Sanborn—that positing qualia is not merely puzzling but structurally incoherent. On their view, qualia are asked to be simultaneously epistemically private (lacking any intersubjective calibration conditions), essentially intrinsic and non-relational, and yet explanatorily relevant to behaviour, report, or evolutionary selection; the claim is that these three roles cannot be jointly satisfied by any legitimate theoretical posit. Rather than rehearsing the familiar inverted-spectrum or zombie thought-experiments, I want to examine whether this incoherence is genuinely internal to the qualia-concept or whether it depends on smuggling in constraints—about scientific legitimacy, about causal closure, or about the nature of first-person access—that a defender could coherently reject.

To that end, I want to introduce a framing I have been calling “phenomenal bookkeeping”: the idea that phenomenal states might be treated analogously to unobservable theoretical entities in physics, fixed not by direct intersubjective observation but by their systematic structural relations to neural dynamics, behavioural dispositions, and report protocols. If such a bookkeeping scheme is coherent, does it rescue qualia from the Chase-Sanborn charge, or does it merely relocate the incoherence into the bookkeeping rules themselves—perhaps by requiring that the “intrinsic” core remain causally inert while the “bookkeeping” relations do all the explanatory work? More generally, what would genuinely count against the Chase-Sanborn position? I am looking for decisive defeaters—observations, conceptual results, cross-domain analogies, or formal constraints—that would show the triad of privacy, intrinsicness, and explanatory relevance can be jointly satisfied, not merely awkward qualifications that force the view to add epicycles. Which of these potential defeaters would a committed defender concede as damaging, and which would they dismiss by revising their account of privacy or intrinsicness? Please distinguish fatal objections from merely awkward ones, rank them in order of severity, and say whether the position is genuinely exposed or has been insulated by definitional moves that make it unfalsifiable.
