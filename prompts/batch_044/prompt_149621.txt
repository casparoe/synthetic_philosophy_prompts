Here is an argument about Ubuntu that seems to prove the community cannot consistently exclude anyone without contradiction, and I cannot tell whether the error is logical or metaphysical.

(1) Ubuntu holds that personhood is constituted by relations of mutual recognition and care: a person is a person through other persons.

(2) Yet Ubuntu also draws a normative boundary: those who systematically violate communal obligations—say, the outcast (isitha) who refuses recognition—are not full persons, or are morally diminished.

(3) So negative relations—exclusion, the withdrawal of recognition, being shunned—are not merely causal effects but constitutive: they partly make the outcast into a non-person.

(4) If a relation is constitutive of both relata, then when community C excludes outsider O, the relation of exclusion belongs both to C's constitutive web and to O's constitutive status.

(5) But a constitutive web is closed under the relations it contains: if a relation inside the web involves O, then O is a node within that web's relational closure.

(6) Therefore O is simultaneously inside C's web (as the necessary relatum of an internal, constitutive relation) and outside it (as the one whose exclusion defines the boundary).

(7) That yields a contradiction: O is both fully constituted as a person within the web and constituted as a non-person by exclusion from it.

(8) So either negative relations are not genuinely constitutive—which would strip Ubuntu of its normative capacity to demote wrongdoers—or the inside/outside distinction collapses.

(9) I want to preserve (1) and (3), since Ubuntu's moral force seems to depend on exclusion being real rather than accidental, and I want to preserve the inside/outside boundary; but I do not see how.

Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Is there an equivocation in (4) between being a relatum and being a member? Does (5) wrongly apply mereological closure to social relations? Or is the problem in (3), that exclusion can only be regulative, not constitutive?

Please say how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the point you are least sure of.
