For a handout in an advanced political ethics seminar, construct a branching taxonomy that classifies positions on the moral obligations of voting, organised by logical commitments rather than by familiar theory-names. Begin at the root with the claim: "Voting can be morally required even when it is causally inefficacious." From there, build four successive binary splits that separate positions genuinely, each phrased as a concrete claim or case so that a respondent can answer it without knowing which label it leads to.

The splits must cover:
1. Whether voting possesses non-instrumental expressive value that persists when every available candidate is morally flawed.
2. Whether casting a ballot for a flawed but relatively better candidate is permissible as a "lesser evil," or whether it constitutes impermissible structural complicity in that candidate's harmful policies.
3. Whether voters bear an epistemic duty to research candidates adequately, such that uninformed voting is worse than abstaining.
4. A niche conceptual distinction you introduce: "delegability anxiety" — specifically, whether the moral culpability a voter incurs for government harms is amplified or diluted by the institutional design of representation, contrasting systems where post-election coalition bargaining obscures direct accountability (proportional representation) against those where accountability is concentrated (winner-take-all).

At each leaf, provide either the name of an established position that occupies that node (e.g., civic-republican duty, expressive democratic participation, strict abstentionism, epistemic abstentionism, complicity-sensitive abstentionism) or label it "unnamed" with a one-sentence diagnosis of why it is vacant (incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored). Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate. The taxonomy must address duty, expression, and lesser-evil reasoning, but must never ask the reader to self-identify by theory-name. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.

Conclude with a single line beginning "Summary:" that states the taxonomy's total number of leaves and identifies its most controversial split.
