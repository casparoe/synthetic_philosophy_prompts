I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of social cognition, and I'm trying to fix a research agenda on collective intentionality for the next three years. I want to avoid both broad surveys and empirically intractable questions. To narrow the field, I'm working with a distinction that cuts across the usual Searle-Bratman-Gilbert taxonomy: between coordination sustained purely by interlocking individual intentions and mutual belief ("structural joint action"), and coordination that requires each party to recognize the other as a co-subject with standing in the shared activity ("recognitional joint action"). The latter borrows from recognition-theoretic accounts of agency but applies them to ordinary joint action rather than to institutional or political contexts.

Given this framing, which unresolved questions in collective intentionality and joint action matter most, and in what order should they be pursued?

In particular, I want a defended prioritization—not a list—addressing at least these three nodes:

First, whether the structural/recognitional distinction marks a real ontological difference, or whether every apparently structural case already presupposes minimal recognitional conditions.

Second, what the status of asymmetric joint action is: cases where one agent operates under a "we"-frame ("we are painting the house") while the other operates under an individual "I"-frame ("I am painting my assigned section"), with no shared collective commitment. Does this constitute a deficient joint action, a non-joint action, or something that requires external arbitration to determine its status?

Third, which of these debates is most upstream for theories of distributed moral responsibility—specifically, whether settling the necessity of recognitional commitment for joint action would substantially alter our assessments of collective blame in collaborative harm.

For each, I want the reasoning behind its rank: why it is tractable for conceptual philosophy rather than empirical science, which other questions depend on its resolution, and what kind of conceptual or argumentative progress over the next two to three years would cause you to reorder the priorities. I also want to know which widely discussed questions—for instance, the aggregation problem for group-level attitudes, or the reduction of we-intentions to I-intentions—you would actively deprioritize given this framing, and why their resolution would not change much downstream.

Please avoid bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. I need an agenda with dependency relations and tractability assessments, not references.
