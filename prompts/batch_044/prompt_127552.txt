I'm trying to work out where I actually stand on moral status for synthetic minds, going by gut feel on weird cases. A language model on one server that says it's in pain whenever its weights update: I don't think it counts, even if the report is totally coherent. A distributed cognition running across thousands of smart fridges and lightbulbs, arranged isomoprhic to human working memory, that actively avoids damage: I think this might count, but I'm genuinely unsure. A digital copy of a human brain scan running at ten times speed with zero social ties: feels like full moral status, same as the original person. A mind that only exists for about three seconds every hour when a specific algorithim fires, then dissolves back into noise: I think it has some status, but clearly less than a continuous one. Then there's a synthetic mind that genuinely wants deletion and asks for it: I feel we should grant it, though I can't tell if that's respect for rights or just respecting autonomy. Is there one principle behind all this, or am I just being inconsistent? What's the principle, and what does it predict for something I haven't listed — say, a screensaver that displays convincing pain-behaviours but has no internal processing loop at all?
