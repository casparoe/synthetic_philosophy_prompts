I'm putting together a proposal for a small decision-theory workshop this fall—probably a 5000-word paper or a 20-minute talk, whatever fits. My half-baked idea: everyone treats the Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery as testing the same thing, namely whether superrational agents should cooperate with copies, but I think the two puzzles actually split along a line people miss. In Platonia the correlation is logical—you're literally reasoning about a twin—while in the Luring Lottery the correlation is causal but masked by a probabilistic gate, and the usual payoff-matrix fixes conflate those mechanisms. So the real question is not whether CDT fails but whether there's a principled way to keep logical-correlation reasoning without letting causal-influence reasoning slip back in, and whether any decision rule can do that without collapsing into EDT. I need the proposal to lay out: the precise framing that separates the two correlation types; why the existing literature leaves this open (mostly because it jumps straight to utility tables); the actual method—maybe design a hybrid puzzle where logical correlation is present but the causal gate is broken, or analyze what a decision rule would have to look like to respect one but not the other; what a successful result looks like versus total failure, say if the distinction turns out to be just EDT in disguise; and an honest scope check for a single semester.
