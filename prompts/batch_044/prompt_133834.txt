I'm writing a feature on the Stoic revival for a general-interest magazine — you know, the apps, the morning routines, the corporate wellness seminars that quote Epictetus like he's a productivity guru. What's struck me is that the dichotomy of control is everywhere now, but it's being used more like a mood ring than a serious framework. I was talking to a friend who's behavorial economist and she pointed out something I hadn't seen discussed in the popular stuff: in an environment where reccomendation algorithms are constantly shaping what you even think to care about, the old boundary between "within my power" and "outside my power" starts to blur in a weird second-order way. It's not just that I can't control the feed; it's that I might not be able to control what I consider controllable in the first place.

So I'm trying to figure out what the actually important open questions are here — not the ones that have been recylced since the nineties revival, but the ones that would change how someone uses the framework if they were answered. If you were advising, say, a small interdiscplinary group (philosophers plus people who study digital environments) with about three years and limited funding, which conceptual problems about the dichotomy should they tackle first? I want you to rank them and defend the ordering: which ones are upstream of the others, which are tractable without getting deep into neuroscience, and which ones sound interesting but wouldn't actually change practice if resolved. I'm less interested in a survey than in the reasoning — what makes one question worth years of work and another one overrated? And please, no bibliography or reading list; I need arguments my readers can follow without hunting down obscure journals.
