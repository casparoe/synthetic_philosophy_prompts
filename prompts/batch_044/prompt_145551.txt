I'm trying to work out my actual views on self-locating belief and indexical uncertainty by testing them against concrete cases, and I'd like help finding the principle that captures the pattern—or deciding which intuition has to go.

Here are my verdicts.

Case 1. The classic Sleeping Beauty setup: a fair coin is flipped Sunday night. If heads, she is woken once on Monday and interviewed. If tails, she is woken on Monday and again on Tuesday, with full amnesia of any prior waking. Upon being interviewed, I think her credence in heads should be 1/3.

Case 2. A decision-theoretic variant of Case 1. Before sleeping, she can take a bet that pays $90 if heads and costs $30 if tails, with settlement occurring once per waking (so twice on tails). I think she should reject the bet at these terms, which to me is consistent with treating heads as having one-third the "weight" of tails across centered worlds.

Case 3. The temporal-ignorance variant. The protocol is identical to Case 1, but this time she is told upon waking only that she is awake "now" and does not know whether it is Monday or Tuesday. I still think her credence in heads should be 1/3; the absence of a calendar does not seem to change the structure of her indexical uncertainty.

Case 4. The identity-break variant. The physical protocol is identical to Case 1—wake once on heads, wake twice on tails with amnesia—but on tails the Monday consciousness ends completely and Tuesday's is a numerically distinct person with copied memories, not the same continuing subject. Upon waking, I think the occupant should assign 1/2 to heads, not 1/3, because there is no single self that is located in both wakings.

Case 5. The branching-copy variant. A quantum measurement either produces one copy of her in world H or two copies in worlds T1 and T2, with subjectively identical experiences. Upon finding herself awake with no further information, I think she should assign 1/3 to being in H.

The cluster of Cases 1, 2, 3, and 5 pulls me strongly toward a thirder-style principle: center the possibility space on wake-up moments, weight them equally, and condition on being one of them. Case 4 pulls in the opposite direction, suggesting that the 1/3 result depends on there being a single continuing self that could be "located" in either waking.

So my questions:

First, is there a precise principle—stated with explicit conditions on personal identity, centered worlds, and conditioning rules—that delivers 1/3 for Cases 1–3 and 5 but permits 1/2 for Case 4? If so, what is it, and what clause handles the identity-break?

Second, if you think the simpler principle is to keep the 1/3 rule uniform and overturn one of my verdicts, which should go and why? In particular, does Case 4 misapply indexical reasoning, or does Case 1 illegitimately treat Monday and Tuesday as "the same person" in a way that inflates the denominator?

Third, apply your resulting principle to a case I haven't listed: the coin is biased, with only a 10% chance of heads, and on tails there are nine copies (T1 through T9) with identical experiences. What credence should the awakened subject assign to heads, and does your answer depend on whether the copies share a continuing consciousness?

Please state the principle precisely, identify the case that motivates each clause, list its verdicts on any further cases you consider, and be explicit about which of my five verdicts it cannot accommodate.
