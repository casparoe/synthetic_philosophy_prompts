I sit on the Civic Technology Oversight Committee of a regional public-library consortium, and at next month's plenary we must vote on whether to renew the contract for our participatory-budgeting platform. The platform presents fiscal trade-offs through short, first-person narratives that embed numerical data inside stories about community identity—showing, for instance, how a particular millage rate translates into a specific resident's experience of library access. One committee member contends this is manipulation in the philosophical sense: it exploits framing effects and affective heuristics to circumvent rational deliberation, thereby compromising user autonomy regardless of factual accuracy. Another member argues it is legitimate persuasion, because the narratives are factually accurate, voluntarily accessed, and render complex budgetary reasons intelligible to non-expert users who would otherwise disengage. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—renew with enforceable conditions, suspend for redesign, or terminate—with a clear time frame, and it should supply criteria the committee can apply in future vendor reviews. In particular, the memo should distinguish structural manipulation, which exploits cognitive architecture regardless of content, from substantive manipulation, which distorts the reason-giving structure itself; it should treat the autonomy-based objection with full seriousness rather than reducing it to a legal disclaimer; and it should examine whether reversibility of framing—the user's capacity to access the raw numerical presentation without narrative overlay—alters the moral assessment. The reasoning must include an honest and substantial treatment of the strongest consideration supporting the position you do not recommend.
