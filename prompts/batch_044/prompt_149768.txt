I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have been working through Quine’s claim that we are ontologically committed only to what our best scientific theories must literally quantify over in their canonical notation, and I tried to apply that standard consistently to moral values. I built an argument that seems to turn his own criterion against him, and now I cannot find the exit. (1) Quine accepts mathematical entities into his ontology because they are indispensable to the formal apparatus of physics—without binding variables for numbers and sets, our best physical theories collapse. (2) By the same token, formal models in cooperative game theory and social choice literally bind variables for preference orderings, obligation structures, and normative constraints, and stripping those variables out destroys the predictive and explanatory power of those sciences. (3) So if Quine applies his indispensability criterion consistently across all sciences, he must treat moral and normative properties as real entities quantified in our best total theory, just as he treats real numbers. (4) Yet Quine clearly denies that moral values are part of the world’s real furniture, treating them instead as culturally relative expressions of sentiment that lack objective quantification. (5) To maintain that denial he must argue that normative quantification in social science is merely heuristic and can be replaced by purely descriptive behavioral laws, but then he owes an identical reduction for mathematics in physics—replacing formal equations with pure observation sentences—which he steadfastly refuses to do. (6) Therefore his view seems to prove too much: either it commits him to moral realism, or it undermines his own defense of mathematical realism by showing that indispensability is selectively applied; I feel the slip lies somewhere between (2) and (5), but I cannot name the precise step. Which premise should I reject, and what does giving it up cost if I want to preserve both Quine’s naturalism and his rejection of moral objectivity?
