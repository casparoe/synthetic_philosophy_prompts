ok so ive been trying to read this late wittgenstein thing, On Certainty i think its called? and im stuck on the hinge idea - like theres all this stuff we treat as fixed, not because we have reasons for it, but because without it nothing else could even be doubted. he compares it to a riverbed holding still under the water. i was talking to my friend about it and she said just write a poem instead of explaining, so here i am.

can you write a villanelle, like the real 19-line kind with two refrains that keep coming back? i want one refrain to be about the bed not moving, and the other to be about the water asking why. the point is the form should do the work: the bed-line stays exactly the same, rhyme and all, while the other lines swirl around it. near the end they should meet in the final couplet but still not resolve - like the hinge isnt an answer, its just what makes the questions possible. also maybe slip in that the bed isnt a belief we hold in our heads but more like the ground we stand on without noticing. dont make it sound like a textbook, just something that feels strange and true whether youve read wittgenstein or not. and sorry if i spelled villanelle wrong, i always do.
