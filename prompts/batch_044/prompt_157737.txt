Imagine that G.E. Moore—who died in 1958 and therefore never encountered generative artificial intelligence—were asked to evaluate a contemporary language model engineered to emit first-person doxastic reports. Specifically, the model outputs the sentence: "The capital of France is Paris, but I do not believe that the capital of France is Paris," while its internal calibration layers simultaneously register near-maximal confidence in the propositional content and near-zero confidence in the self-attributed belief state. The model lacks any unified cognitive architecture, persistent mental states, or agential center; its utterance is produced by distributed statistical weights rather than by a subject of experience. How might Moore analyze whether this scenario instantiates the paradox he articulated—namely, the absurdity of asserting p while denying belief in p? In constructing your answer, introduce and deploy a distinction between performative doxastic reporting (the behavioral production of belief-state language without an underlying mental state) and genuine doxastic commitment (the presence of an actual mental attitude). Would Moore maintain that the logical form of the utterance alone is sufficient to generate the paradoxical absurdity he identified, regardless of whether there is a real believer behind the words? Or would he argue that without a single subject capable of both asserting and disbelieving, the contradiction collapses into a mere mechanical malfunction, thereby dissolving the philosophical tension?
