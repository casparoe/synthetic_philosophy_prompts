I've been chewing on Kripkenstein's skeptical argument — not the standard "no fact about meaning" line, but a stronger version I cooked up. The idea is that even the skeptical soluton, which appeals to community agreement, doesn't stop the regress. If I don't know what plus means, how does checking with my neighboors help? They'd need to already share my concept to correct me, but whether they share it is itself up for grabs. So I think the real upshot isn't just private language being impossible, but that there's no stable correct/incorrect distinction even for public rules — kind of normative nihalism about linguistic practice. What would count against this? Not just "people communicate fine," but specific arguments, cases, or formal results that show the community-level fix actually works, or that the regress stops somewhere. Also, has this view been qualified into unfalsifability — like, any apparent communicative success gets dismissed as mere conformity? I want an honest assessment of how exposed it is, including whether defenders of the skeptical soluton themselves would concede anything here, and which defeaters would be fatal versus just awkward.
