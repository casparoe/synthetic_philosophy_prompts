Hey, I've been retired from physics for about five years and lately I've been trying to read some philosophy of math. I got stuck on this argument I cooked up about Hilbert's hotel and whether it breaks statistical mechanics. I think there's a hidden slide somewhere but I can't pin it down.

Here's the chain:
(1) Take an infinate square well. The energy eigenstates are countably infinite, call them E1, E2, E3...
(2) By Hilbert's hotel reasoning, the subset {E2, E4, E6...} can be put in one-to-one correspondance with the full set {E1, E2, E3...} via n -> 2n and back.
(3) So the half subset has exactly the same cardinality as the whole.
(4) Now in stat mech we often assign probabilities over infinite ensembles. If every state is equally likely in some uniform sense, then being in the even subset should have probability 1/2, since it's half the states.
(5) But (3) says the even subset isn't smaller in cardinality, so there's no set-theoretic basis for treating it as half.
(6) So either uniform probabilities don't exist over countably infinite sets, or every nonempty subset gets probability 1.
(7) But we use non-extreme probabilities (like 1/3 for being in the left third of an infinite lattice) in physics all the time.
(8) Therefore, if Hilbert's pairing applies to physical state spaces, statistical mechanics is incoherent.
(9) The only escape is to deny that Cantor-style pairing applies to physical states—i.e., be a finitist about real-world state spaces.
(10) But then we'd have to reject Cantor's theorem altogether, which feels like throwing the baby out with the bathwater.

I'd rather keep (7) and (8) than become a finnitist, but I'm not sure which link is rotten—maybe (4) confuses cardinality with measure, maybe applying Hilbert's pairing to physical states is already wrong, or maybe I missed a quantifier shift somewhere. What's the actual weak point here, and what does fixing it cost?
