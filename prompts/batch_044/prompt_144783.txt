Rank the following four strategies for navigating hard choices between genuinely incommensurable values into tiers (S through D) according to how well each respects the incommensurability rather than disguising it with a hidden metric: (1) lexical priority rules that always override one value; (2) satisficing thresholds that accept the first option meeting minimal conditions across values; (3) contextualist weighing that lets situational features determine importance without a general algorithm; and (4) tragic acknowledgment that treats the loss of the unchosen value as an unavoidable moral remainder. Give a one-sentence justification for each tier placement. Then provide a fraction between 0 and 1 representing how much a culture could deliberately shift its dominant strategy through institutional design alone, without reshaping individual moral psychology, and defend that figure in two sentences.
