I am a journalist covering science and ideas, and I am preparing a feature on whether biography can still function as a technology of moral education. Please compose a formal, polished dialogue for the piece between two equally capable interlocutors: Dr. Lena Vasquez, a narrative ethicist who defends Plutarch's parallel lives as a method of moral triangulation—using the comparison of Greek and Roman exemplars not to invite imitation but to calibrate judgment about virtue—and Dr. Marcus Webb, a critical historian of hagiography who argues that exemplary biography, whether sacred or secular, disables moral learning by substituting admiration for critical analysis and by erasing the social and structural conditions that made the subject's choices available or unavoidable. Their exchange should center on a concrete, somewhat novel pedagogical proposal now being piloted: a secondary-school curriculum called counterfactual biography, in which students study a historical figure alongside an algorithmically generated alternate life showing the same person making the opposite moral decision under materially identical conditions. Vasquez should argue that this extends Plutarch's comparative logic; Webb should counter that it collapses biography into fiction and that Plutarch's own practice of paired comparison (synkrisis) already contained the danger of reducing moral complexity to rhetorical symmetry. The dialogue must exhibit genuine back-and-forth, with each speaker responding directly to the other's actual claims rather than talking past them. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the reasoning should stand entirely on its own.
