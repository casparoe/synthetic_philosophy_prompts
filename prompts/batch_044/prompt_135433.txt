In recent debates about whether large language models can genuinely refer to natural kinds, people keep invoking the familiar slogan that meanings just ain't in the head as if it ends discussion—most recently to claim that an AI system trained only on text cannot think about water, since it lacks the right causal-historical connection to H2O. I want to bring this to a philosophy-of-language discussion, so please lay out the different precise theses this slogan could be expressing, arranged as a menu rather than a single interpretation. Include at least: (a) a trivial reading about mental images or introspective access not being sufficient for reference; (b) the standard Twin-Earth reading about natural-kind terms being fixed by causal interaction with the environment; (c) a stronger social-externalist reading that shifts reference-fixing to expert communities rather than individual causal chains; and (d) a maximally broad reading that applies the claim to all conceptual content, not just natural kinds. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or false, and—crucially—whether that specific reading actually supports the conclusion that the language model lacks water-thoughts. I am especially interested in which readings depend on direct causal contact with the referent itself versus which only require membership in a linguistic community, since that distinction determines whether the AI case is genuinely parallel to Twin Earth or whether it smuggles in an extra premise.
