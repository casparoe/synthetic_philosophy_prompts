Write a dialogue between two people arguing about what emotions actually are—one holds that they're fundamentally evaluative judgments (like deciding something is dangerous or unfair) that carry a felt quality, the other holds that they're interoceptive predictions the brain makes about bodily states, tagged with value but not actually judgments. They should discuss this made-up case: someone experiences "grey dread"—a vague negative affect with no clear object, no named bodily change, and no conscious evaluation they can articulate, yet it consistently appears right before hidden shifts in their environment that they haven't noticed yet. Each should say whether thats genuinely an emotion on their view, and they need to actually respond to each others points instead of talking past one another. Don't make either side a strawman: the judgment advocate should concede that not every affect is a fully formed propositional belief, and the prediction advocate should concede that conscious appraisal can reshape the prediction. Keep it casual, like tired grad students over coffee, don't cite specific authors or texts, and it's okay if there are a couple typos since this is just a rough prompt draft.
