I’m putting together a workshop for my philosophy of econ reading group—about 10 grad students, one 90-minute session, and we’ve been talking about Pareto improvements but I think we’re all being too casual about what makes one actually safe. I want an in-class negotiation game where the rules themselves make people feel why "safe" is stricter than just "nobody objects." The setup: everyone gets a private envelope with a base point score and a hidden reservation clause. There are three rounds of voting on a redistribution proposal that, on the public sheet, leaves every visible score flat or up. But in each round, two envelopes contain a hidden injury—if the proposal hits a specific keyword or number, that person’s true score crashes, even though the public board says they’re fine. They can talk and bargain, but they can’t open envelopes or directly reveal the clause; they can only say they feel "uneasy" about part of the deal. The collective payoff is a shared snack fund scaled to the group total, so private hidden losses drag everyone down slightly, but nobody can point to the exact mechanism.

Can you design the full rules, the envelope distribution (how many hidden injuries per round, how they’re triggered), the voting protocol, and the materials? I want round one to feel like smooth coordination, round two to produce weird suspicious silence, and round three to either collapse into total refusal or spiral into an exhausting verification ritual where people demand to audit each other’s envelopes. Then a debrief that maps each round onto the formal parts of a safe Pareto improvement—public verifiability, common knowledge that no hidden injury exists, and robustness to preference revelation after the fact. Be frank about what this can’t reproduce: probably the normative question of whether we should demand safety at all, or the infinite regress of checking that everyone knows that everyone knows there’s no hidden clause. And tell me what will predictably go wrong—maybe someone figures out teh pattern of hidden injuries by round two and either games it or kills all trust, and I should have a fallback rule for that.
