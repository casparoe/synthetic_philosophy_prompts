I am writing a feature for a science-and-ideas magazine on the anatomy of moral revolutions—how practices such as dueling or slavery cease to be honorable rather than merely illegal. To ground the piece, I need a university-level examination instrument: one essay question suitable for an upper-level undergraduate philosophy exam, to be answered in 45 minutes at approximately 800 words, together with a model answer of about 700 words and a precise marking scheme. The question must turn on the competing explanations for the collapse of entrenched norms—rational argument, material interest, and the redirection of honor—specifically engaging Bernard Williams’s thesis that moral change often originates in internal contradictions within honor codes themselves, and Kwame Anthony Appiah’s analysis of how honor is redirected rather than eliminated when practices like dueling disappear. To prevent mere paraphrase, the prompt should introduce a hypothetical, contemporary analog: an unwritten rule in high-energy physics granting senior researchers automatic co-authorship on junior colleagues’ papers, sustained for decades by an honor culture of “founder’s privilege” and now rapidly abandoned. Students must apply both the Williams and Appiah frameworks to diagnose this decline, distinguish genuine explanation from post-hoc rationalization, and confront a specific meta-problem—if honor can be invoked to defend a practice and equally to justify its abandonment, the honor-based account risks unfalsifiability. The marking scheme should total 25 marks and separate an A answer from a B by requiring three specific, named moves: (1) an accurate conceptual distinction of the three mechanisms with explicit reference to Williams and Appiah; (2) a concrete application to the hypothetical physics case showing how honor operates differently under each framework; and (3) identification of the unfalsifiability problem together with a proposed methodological remedy. For each move, specify the point value and the typical failure mode that should cost marks. The deliverable should contain no bibliography, reading list, or citation requests, and should avoid asking for recitations of biographical or publication details.
