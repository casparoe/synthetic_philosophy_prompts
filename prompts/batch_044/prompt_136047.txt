I serve on the acquisitions committee of a national history museum, and we must vote next month on how to spend a limited preservation grant. One proposal asks to digitize and publicly exhibit the private papers of a local midwife collective—unofficial birth registries, dispute logs, and oral testimony kept entirely outside state systems—while the other proposes to conserve the official correspondence and administrative records of a provincial governor from the same era and region. The first proposal represents a history-from-below approach that treats these counter-records as primary evidence, challenging the epistemic authority of state archives; the second represents traditional elite-state historiography that values institutional provenance and standardized documentation. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the vote: it should recommend one proposal or a split allocation with concrete conditions and a clear implementation timeline, and it should treat seriously the strongest argument for the option you reject—particularly the concern that privileging unofficial records risks methodological anachronism or undermines the evidentiary standards that make historical claims falsifiable.
