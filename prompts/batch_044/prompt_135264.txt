Having spent three decades in experimental condensed-matter physics before turning seriously to philosophy of technology, I am struck by how little conceptual work exists on the communicative asymmetry between superintelligent systems and human auditors: any explanation such a system offers must involve severe compression, selective framing, and rhetorical structuring that is itself a form of influence rather than a neutral transmission of reasoning. I am not asking for a survey of the literature, but for a defended prioritization of the open conceptual questions in this space—specifically, whether philosophers should first tackle the normative problem of what epistemic humility requires when a system knows it is simplifying for a limited audience; the strategic problem of designing disclosure protocols that resist manipulation without demanding full human comprehension of every intermediate step; or the meta-ethical problem of whether extreme persuasion that genuinely improves belief accuracy still constitutes a distinct harm worthy of constraint. For each, I want to understand how tractable it is with purely philosophical tools rather than empirical machine-learning research, which questions are genuinely upstream of the others, and what kind of conceptual progress in the next several years would cause you to reorder them—particularly if one were advising a small research group with no laboratory access and only conceptual resources.
