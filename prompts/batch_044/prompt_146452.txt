Existing historical and policy accounts tend to assign causal weight either to specific planning errors or to external climate drivers, but neither asks whether the conceptual distinction between base and superstructure collapses when the base accelerates past the middle layer, or what that collapse implies for using longue durée thinking in contemporary climate-adaptation policy. I propose a six-week conceptual study comparing Braudel's temporal model with the hydrological and agricultural history of the Aral basin, not to conduct original empirical fieldwork but to test whether his categories can be reordered without contradiction and to identify precisely which category fails if they cannot.
