I suspect this misses a deeper, structural asymmetry: the epistemic conditions for establishing guilt with the absolute certainty that retributive justice demands are different in kind from the conditions for establishing innocence, because absence of exculpatory evidence is not evidence of absence, and post-conviction review mechanisms have asymmetric access to inculpatory versus exculpatory material. If this asymmetry is ineliminable rather than merely probable, then retributivist justifications—which require precisely calibrated, deserved punishment—face a distinctive defeat that deterrence-based justifications do not face in the same way. At the same time, abolitionists who lean on this argument may implicitly commit to an epistemic standard that, if applied consistently, would also undermine very long terms of imprisonment.
