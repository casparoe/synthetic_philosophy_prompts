My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about antinatalism, specifically the asymmetry Benatar advances between the absence of pleasure and the absence of pain: if someone never comes into existence, they miss no pleasure and this is not bad, yet they avoid suffering and this is genuinely good. We both know the standard repertoire by heart—the symmetry objection that imagines a corresponding possible person for whom missing pleasure is equally regrettable, the personal-affect reply that harm requires an actual subject, Benatar’s counter that the absence of pain is good impersonally, and the various attempts to ground the asymmetry in consent or in the logic of harm versus benefit. Despite this, neither of us moved an inch. My friend now suspects the stalemate is largely verbal: he thinks “bad for” is being used in two different ways—once as “worse for this particular subject relative to an alternative,” once as “making the world worse in an impersonal register”—and that once we disambiguate the phrase, the apparent contradiction dissolves. I am less convinced; it seems to me that beneath the semantic skirmish lies a substantive disagreement about whether potential beings have normative standing independently of actualization, but neither of us can defend that claim without simply restating our initial intuitions. There is also a structural feature we kept brushing aside: if reproductive choices determine which specific individuals come to exist, then comparing “this person exists with suffering” against “this person never exists” involves comparing populations across possible worlds, which raises the non-identity problem in a way that neither Benatar’s formulation nor the standard symmetry replies directly address. So I want a diagnosis rather than another round of adjudication. Is this debate stalled because the two sides are answering different questions—one about the semantics of welfare ascriptions, one about the metaphysics of potential persons—under a single heading? Or is there a hidden formal disagreement, perhaps about how counterfactual welfare should be evaluated when identity is contingent, that is doing the real work while everyone argues about harm and benefit? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a conceptual distinction that separates de dicto from de re evaluations of potential harm, an argument that potentiality is normatively inert and therefore cannot ground asymmetry, or an honest admission that the dispute bottoms out in irreconcilable axiological commitments about whether existence itself carries a default value?
