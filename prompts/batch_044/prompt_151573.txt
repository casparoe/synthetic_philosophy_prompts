I'm drafting the ontological framework for my dissertation in property metaphysics and want a consistency audit before I build further arguments on it in my prospectus. Here is my current package: (1) I reject Platonic universals and hold that all properties are tropes—particularized, non-repeatable qualities; (2) I nonetheless maintain that natural kind terms successfully refer, but I account for this via a primitive kind-membership relation that holds among particulars rather than via shared universals; (3) I claim that resemblance among tropes is fully reducible to internal structural similarity and requires no primitive resemblance relation; (4) I hold that particularity itself—the feature of being this specific trope—is not a trope but a formal, non-qualitative universal applying to every particular; (5) I take this combination to solve the one-over-many problem without collapsing into robust nominalism about kinds, since kind-membership does the grouping work that universals traditionally perform. Which commitments pull genuinely against one another, which conflicts dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision? In particular, does (4) reintroduce a universal at the ground floor in a way that undermines (1), and does the reduction in (3) tacitly presuppose shared qualitative features? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify what each revision costs, and indicate which single commitment should be abandoned or reformulated to restore coherence.
