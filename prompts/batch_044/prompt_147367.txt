At a graduate seminar on epistemic logic, two students—Maya and Noah—debated how Judy Benjamin should revise her beliefs after receiving the message that if she is in Blue Company then she is in Headquarters. Maya argued for the standard conditioning analysis: with four initially equiprobable cells (Red-HQ, Red-Field, Blue-HQ, Blue-Field), eliminating Blue-Field leaves three equally likely possibilities, so her credence in being in Blue should fall from one-half to one-third and her credence in Headquarters should be two-thirds. Noah replied that this mistakes logical form for evidential import: since company assignment and location are causally independent, she should apply Jeffrey conditioning, updating her conditional belief P(HQ | Blue) to near one while leaving her marginal P(Blue) at one-half, which yields a Headquarters credence of three-quarters rather than two-thirds.

Maya countered that Jeffrey conditioning suits noisy observations of statistical frequencies, not authoritative messages that directly constrain possibility space; if Blue-Field is genuinely excluded, normalization over the remaining worlds forces the marginal downward. Noah then introduced a further twist: Judy knows the message was transmitted on a radio band monitored exclusively by Blue Company, so receiving it might itself be selection evidence for Blue membership, potentially raising P(Blue) regardless of content. Maya dismissed this by noting that Judy is aware she monitors all regiment frequencies, so the delivery mechanism carries no evidential weight about her assignment.

Noah pressed one final point: even if delivery is neutral, Maya’s normalization treats the exclusion as global, but if Judy regards the speaker as a local informant about her own regiment rather than an omniscient source, the exclusion applies only inside Blue, leaving the Red-to-Blue weight ratio intact—hence P(Blue) should stay at one-half. Maya responded that local scope does not rescue Noah, because within Blue the exclusion removes one of two equiprobable locations, doubling Blue-HQ’s internal weight, and this restructuring must register globally unless one arbitrarily shields the marginal.

Please adjudicate their exchange move by move: assess Maya’s initial conditioning argument; Noah’s Jeffrey-conditioning reply; Maya’s objection about authoritative elimination; Noah’s broadcast-frequency selection point together with Maya’s rebuttal; and Noah’s local-informant normalization argument with Maya’s counter. For each, say whether it is clearly mistaken, partly right, or clearly right, with brief justification. Then state who is more right overall about Judy’s updated credences, and identify what both disputants are missing—particularly regarding how an indicative conditional differs from a material exclusion in belief revision, and whether the delivery mechanism genuinely introduces an independent selection effect.
