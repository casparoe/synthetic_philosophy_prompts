My central claim is that Abraham's silence in Fear and Trembling isn't just a failure to speak within the ethical universal—it's a kind of positive epistemic strategy. When he refuses to justify himself to Isaac or Sarah, he isn't hiding an ethical reason that happens to be secret; he's working with a reason-structure that literally can't be translated into ethical language without turning into nonsense. So the teleological suspension of the ethical isn't like a temporary state of emergency where ethics gets paused and then resumes—it's more like a permanent ontological gap between two ways of reasoning, faith-reasoning and ethical-reasoning.

To capture this I made up this idea of "incommensurable justification spaces"—probably just fancy dressing, but the point is that Abraham and the ethical community aren't disagreeing about weights on the same scale; they don't share a scale at all. That makes Abraham less of a moral monster and more like someone stuck between languages that don't map onto each other. But I'm genuinely unsure: is the knight of faith actually unable to understand himself in ethical terms, or is he just choosing not to?
