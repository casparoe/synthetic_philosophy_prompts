You are an expert philosopher of science with particular familiarity with cosmological reasoning, anthropic arguments, and the conceptual architecture of fine-tuning debates. Before giving your answer, please take a moment to brainstorm the different interpretive rules that might govern judgments about physical fine-tuning—especially distinctions between parameter sensitivity, boundary-condition dependence, dynamical inevitability, and chemical necessity. I have just spent several exhausting hours arguing with my friend Marcus, a philosopher of physics who is deeply skeptical of intelligent-design conclusions but also finds multiverse explanations unsatisfying, and I am trying to determine whether his positions form a coherent pattern or merely an ad hoc collection of intuitions. First, regarding the cosmological constant being within roughly one part in 10^120 of the life-permitting range, he insists this is a genuine case of parameter fine-tuning that demands explanation, treating the multiverse as live but unproven. Second, on the extremely low initial entropy of the early universe, he denies that this counts as fine-tuning at all, classifying it instead as a boundary-condition problem requiring an altogether different kind of explanation. Third, concerning the Higgs boson mass lying near the electroweak stability boundary, he argues this is not genuinely surprising because renormalization-group running makes the proximity inevitable given the other couplings; he calls this “dynamical convergence,” not tuning. Fourth, about the near-equality of proton and neutron masses—without which nuclear stability and carbon chemistry would fail—he says this is clearly tuned and points to something deep. Fifth, regarding the fact that large spatial dimensions number exactly three, he rejects the tuning framing entirely, claiming other dimensionalities are dynamically unstable or necessarily compactified, so three dimensions are selected by physical law rather than calibrated.

Now consider a new hypothetical case that neither of us has discussed. In a speculative “mirror-sector” cosmological model, three independent dark-sector couplings—call them g₁, g₂, and g₃—must align within a band of about 0.01 percent of their parameter space for stable galaxy formation to occur, but only in universes with a positive and relatively large cosmological constant. In universes with a negative cosmological constant, gravitational dynamics change sufficiently that the couplings need not align at all, since galaxy formation proceeds through a different collapse mechanism that is insensitive to their values. Based on Marcus’s track record, predict what he would say: is this a case of genuine fine-tuning? Would he invoke multiverse selection, deny the tuning by appealing to dynamical inevitability in the negative-Λ branch, or treat it differently? More importantly, identify the two or three competing rules that could each accommodate all five of his earlier verdicts—for instance, a rule tracking whether the sensitivity concerns dimensionless ratios essential to chemistry versus cosmological structure, or a rule tracking whether an alternative dynamical branch exists that removes the sensitivity. Say what each rule predicts about this triad-alignment case. Finally, propose one further hypothetical case that would best discriminate between those competing rules, explaining exactly how Marcus’s verdicts would diverge across them. I am less interested in whether he is cosmologically correct than in whether his judgments are governed by a stable principle.
