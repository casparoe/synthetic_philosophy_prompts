Here's an argument my study partner built around no-trade theorems that seems to prove market prices are basically useless for learning. (1) Milgrom-Stokey shows rational traders with a common prior won't trade, since any offer reveals private info and kills the deal. (2) But the same logic works backwards: if I stay silent and don't offer to buy at price P, that omission reveals I think it's overpriced. (3) So in a rational market, every actual price is wrapped in a "halo of silence" from all the agents who refused to participate, each withholding their signal. (4) That means the trades that do happen must involve people who failed to update on all that silent information — so real transactions are just pockets of irrationality. (5) Meanwhile the price itself is shaped more by who stayed out than by who traded. (6) So if you use a stock price or prediction market to update your beliefs, you're mostly tracking stategic ommisions, not shared judgments. (7) Therefore either markets can't aggregate knowledge at all, or we have to deny that market silence carries information. Which step is wrong? I'd like to keep (1) and (6) if possible but I feel like (3) is doing sneaky work.
