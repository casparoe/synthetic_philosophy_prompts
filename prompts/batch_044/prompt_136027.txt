Consider the Darwinian theory of culture—memetics—not as a historical curiosity but as a live conceptual framework that must now account for algorithmically mediated transmission. In traditional memetics, replication fidelity, variation, and selection operate through human cognition; but in platform environments, recommendation algorithms function as an intermediate replication mechanism that selects for engagement rather than cognitive retention, and they actively mutate content through compression, remixing, and decontextualization. This creates at least three serious conceptual tensions: whether the unit of selection is still the meme or has shifted to the algorithmic feed structure; whether evolutionary explanations of cultural change remain tractable when selection pressures are engineered rather than emergent; and whether the analogy to genetic evolution breaks down precisely where replication fidelity becomes too high (viral replication) or too low (context collapse).
