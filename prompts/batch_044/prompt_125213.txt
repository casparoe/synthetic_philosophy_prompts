Write a dialogue between two equally informed interlocutors—one defending the Hayek-Mises knowledge critique of central planning, the other defending Oskar Lange’s competitive socialist model—set around a concrete, engineered institutional problem: a regional planning bureau in a worker-managed economy must set prices for bespoke, non-standardized industrial machinery (e.g., custom forging equipment built to unique local specifications) where no prior market analog exists and where dispersed shop-floor engineers hold tacit knowledge about material tolerances that cannot be fully codified in reports to the center. The Hayekian should press the problem of particular circumstances of time and place, the epistemic role of entrepreneurial profit-and-loss feedback, and the risk that statistical aggregates from worker councils miss local constraints; the Lange defender should invoke trial-and-error adjustment of shadow prices, the informational equivalence of simulated markets, and the claim that iterative revision can converge on correct relative prices without private capital ownership. Each speaker must respond directly to the other’s prior argument rather than talking past them, and neither should be a strawman.

After the dialogue, provide a brief committed assessment: take a definite position on whether Lange’s mechanism genuinely resolves Hayek’s objection in this specific machinery-pricing case, identify the single structural feature of the case that does the work for your verdict, and explain precisely how a defender of the losing side would attack that feature. Do not present a neutral balance of pros and cons; argue for one side.

Constraints: keep the scenario realistic and institutional rather than science-fictional; do not invoke modern computing, artificial intelligence, or information technology unavailable to the historical debate; avoid bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific editions; do not quote any primary source verbatim—paraphrase all ideas in your own language. End with a one-paragraph note on where your assessment leans on a contested interpretation of Hayek’s notion of tacit knowledge or of Lange’s procedural assumptions.
