me and two people in my phil seminar were arguing about Nozick's tracking conditions and epistemic closure -- well they were arguing, i was mostly just listening. here's what went down.

mara said Nozick actually saves closure, not kills it. her point: if i track 'i have hands' (sensitive and adherant in nearby worlds), then i automatically track 'i'm not a brain in a vat' because the closest worlds where i'm BIV'd are also worlds where i don't have hands. so the counterfactual failure that kills my hand-belief also kills my BIV-belief, meaning both survive together and closure holds.

derek said that's smuggling in a bad proximity metric. BIV worlds aren't 'near' just because they share hand-loss; Nozick never said modal distance tracks physical similarity. in the BIV world i still believe i'm not BIV, so condition 3 fails for that specific belief. closure breaks because tracking is belief-by-belief, not entailment-preserving.

then mara came back with something weird i'd never heard. she said tracking should be 'domain-restricted': you only check counterfactuals inside the mechanism's reliable domain. for hands that's normal perception; for BIV there's no mechanism, so we don't expect tracking. but closure still applies within a shared domain -- if P and Q are both in my perceptual domain, knowing P lets me know Q. so closure isn't dead, it's just mechanistically bounded.

derek said that's not closure, that's a tautolgy. you only get closure for beliefs that happen to sit in the same lucky box. the classic closure principle was that deduction preserves knowledge regardless of content, and mara just replaced it with 'knowledge propogates when my gadget works for both.'

they asked me to call it but i honestly can't tell. can you walk through each move and say who's right, where each side is missing something, and whether they're both assuming one specific way modal spaces have to be ordered? don't just crown a winner -- map out what's actually going on.
