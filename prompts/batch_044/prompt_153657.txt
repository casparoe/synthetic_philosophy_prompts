Give an example of a conceptual tension in the philosophy of science concerning natural kinds, arising specifically from a synthetic-biology scenario: imagine a laboratory-constructed minimal cell that replicates with high fidelity and displays a stable, interdependent cluster of biochemical and morphological traits, yet was designed de novo and lacks any evolutionary lineage or history of natural selection. Describe the scenario concretely, then articulate the conceptual issue it raises for the view that scientific classification tracks objective structures in nature rather than human interests. In particular, explain whether this organism belongs to a natural kind in virtue of its present functional clustering alone, in virtue of its causal-historical origins, or only relative to the classificatory purposes of researchers, and identify which of these options undermines the idea that kind-membership is independent of our conceptual schemes. Do not appeal to named theoretical frameworks or provide bibliographic references; build the tension directly from the conceptual features of the case.
