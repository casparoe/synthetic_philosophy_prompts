In a discussion about reconstructing Bronze Age institutional structures from settlement assemblages, a colleague kept repeating "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence" to block an abductive inference: because no standardized weights-and-measures artifacts appear in the ceramic and lithic record, we should not conclude that a centralized redistributive authority was absent. Before I respond in writing, I need the slogan precisely disambiguated. Could you lay out the distinct theses it could be expressing -- for example, logical non-entailment of ontological absence from missing data; probabilistic underdetermination given sampling bias and preservation conditions; methodological conservatism that assigns the burden of proof to positive claims; or an ontological claim about the material invisibility of certain institutions -- and for each indicate whether it is trivial, defensible, false, or actually irrelevant to blocking that specific inference? I want a menu rather than a single reading, with explicit verdicts on which version supports protecting a decentralized-exchange reconstruction from the heterogeneous distribution of goods, and which versions collapse into platitudes once you specify what counts as "evidence" in stratigraphic context.
