I have no formal training in philosophy or political science, but over the past two years I have worked through the problem of dirty hands in politics on my own, and I have developed a view I have not seen in the popular essays and introductions available to me. I argue that the classic framing focuses too narrowly on the visible decision-maker who personally authorizes a moral wrong for the sake of a greater good, and it therefore misses what I call distributed dirty hands: in modern administrative governance, the elected official designs a policy that requires morally ambiguous execution, but the actual carrying-out is delegated to career bureaucrats, contractors, or automated systems. The result is not that the politician's hands are dirty, but that the dirt is dispersed across an organizational chart in which no single node can be held responsible, and the official retains moral cleanliness precisely because the citizenry authorized the structure rather than the specific act.
