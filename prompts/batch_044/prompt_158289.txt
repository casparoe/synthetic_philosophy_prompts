Below is the package of commitments I intend to defend in a graduate seminar on explanatory pluralism, and I need an audit of joint tenability before I present.

(1) Real patterns in Dennett's sense are ontologically weighty—genuine, not mere predictive conveniences—though non-fundamental.
(2) The physical, design, and intentional stances are irreducibly plural: for some domains, no other stance can in principle deliver the same explanatory goods.
(3) At the same time, every true prediction licensed by the design or intentional stance is derivable in principle from the physical stance, however intractable the computation.
(4) I adopt a compressibility filter: a pattern counts as real only if a stance-relative description achieves a substantial Kolmogorov-complexity reduction over the best physical-level description.
(5) The intentional stance is never appropriate for present large language models, since they lack the holistic rational integration that makes intentional ascription genuinely compressive.
(6) Moral responsibility attaches only to agents for whom the intentional stance is both appropriate and ineliminable; entities explained solely through the design stance bear none.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear so? Specifically, does (2) sit uneasily with (3) once we distinguish explanatory irreducibility from derivational sufficiency? Does (4) threaten (5) if intentional ascription of an LLM turned out more compressive than architectural design-level explanation? Is the tension between (1) and (4) merely apparent, dissolving once we separate ontological weight from fundamentality? Rank the tensions by severity, state the least costly revision for each, and tell me which single commitment, if sacrificed, cleans up the most. Please also indicate how confident you are in this audit and single out the point you are least sure about—especially whether (4) genuinely supports (5) or might accidentally include artificial systems.
