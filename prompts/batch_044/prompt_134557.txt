I am preparing a single seventy-five-minute session for advanced undergraduates in metaphysics—students familiar with Quine and basic possible-worlds semantics but who have never encountered Lewisian concrete modal realism. I do not want an explanation of the view itself; I want a teaching plan structured as an ordered sequence of conceptual dependencies, specifying what must be in place before concreteness, spatiotemporal isolation, and indexical actuality can be introduced without collapse.

For the carrying example, avoid both standard semantic cases and the twin-Earth physical-duplicate scenario. Instead, design one concrete scenario centered on a single agent reflecting on a counterfactual career path—one that can bear the full weight of the distinction between an ersatz representation and a genuinely existing concrete world, and that makes room for the indexicality of actual without prematurely importing counterpart theory or modal parts. Specify precisely what features this example must possess and what features it must deliberately lack in order to do this work.

Then map the predictable misconceptions: that modal realism is merely Everettian multiverse physics by another name; that being the actual world is an intrinsic property rather than an indexical; and that concrete worlds, if real, must be causally accessible from ours. For each, indicate the exact point in the sequence—before, during, or after the relevant component—where it should be defused, and briefly how.

Provide one diagnostic question for the close that distinguishes genuine comprehension from mere slogan repetition—specifically, one that tests whether a student understands why the indexicality of actual does not entail that every world is actual for itself in the same sense.

Finally, state explicitly which parts of the theory—counterpart theory, modal parts, the ontology of properties and relations across worlds, and the analysis of counterfactuals—must be left entirely unmentioned at this level, with a brief justification for each omission. Present the plan as a structured outline, not as prose exegesis of the view.
