I serve on the City Council’s Joint Welfare Reform Oversight Committee. The Department of Social Services has submitted a pilot proposal—Adaptive Conditionality with Structural-Choice Decomposition (AC-SCD)—for approval before the Q3 implementation window. The pilot replaces uniform workfare rules with tiered conditionality based on an internal Structural-Choice Decomposition (SCD) score. For each recipient, the model estimates the proportion of current unemployment attributable to exogenous structural barriers (local plant closures, disability onset, childcare gaps) versus voluntary labor-market withdrawal or non-compliance. Recipients scoring below 0.30—high estimated “choice attribution”—enter Tier A: mandatory weekly verification, immediate benefit suspension for missed appointments, and compulsory job-search workshops. Those scoring above 0.70—high structural attribution—enter Tier B: quarterly voluntary check-ins, no sanctions for the first two missed contacts, and case-management support rather than enforcement. The Department argues this aligns obligations with desert: we should not sanction people for conditions they did not choose. Opponents—including advocacy coalition representatives and several committee members—argue that assigning citizens different moral statuses via an opaque predictive model institutionalizes precisely the “undeserving poor” logic the welfare state was meant to reject, violates equal respect by treating poverty as a moral failure for some and a misfortune for others, and relies on counterfactual estimates that are epistemically unreliable and likely biased. Our committee must recommend one of three courses: approve the pilot outright; reject it; or approve it with binding conditions (for example, an independent ethics audit after six months, a prohibition on using SCD scores for any purpose outside this pilot, or a mandatory ceiling on the disparity between Tier A and Tier B sanctions). I need a formal written opinion—no more than two pages—to circulate before Thursday’s vote. It should state a clear, actionable recommendation with a concrete time frame specifying what must occur before Q3 if the pilot proceeds; it should present reasoning accessible to members without philosophical training; and it should engage honestly with the strongest philosophical objection to the recommended course rather than dismissing it. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or appeals to external authorities; the memo must stand on its own reasoning.
