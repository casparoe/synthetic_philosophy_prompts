I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher of biology who defends a position she calls "thin structuralism" about genetic information. On her view, DNA carries information only in the minimal, Shannon-theoretic sense—reducing receiver uncertainty about phenotypic outcomes—rather than in any rich semantic or blueprint-like sense. She argues that this thin reading is the only one compatible with a rigorous causal-mechanistic account of gene expression, and that biologists' talk of genes coding for traits or replication errors is either heuristic shorthand or can be fully translated into causal-conditional language. I want a sequence of six to eight questions that avoid asking her to restate the view, and instead press on three specific points. First, whether her translation scheme can genuinely preserve the normative force of biologists' judgments about mis-coding or malfunction without smuggling in semantic content. Second, whether the interpretation schemes required even for Shannon information—specifying which state-space counts as the message and which as noise—reintroduce the very intentional or functional context she wants to eliminate. Third, whether abandoning the blueprint metaphor also requires abandoning talk of developmental programs, or whether she can reconstruct that talk using only causal templates and feedback loops. For each point, include a primary question and a follow-up tailored to her likely reply: if she appeals to selection history to ground normativity, ask how that avoids teleosemantic commitments; if she distinguishes syntactic structure from semantic interpretation, ask where the boundary lies in the actual molecular machinery; and if she offers a causal reconstruction of programs, ask whether it retains any explanatory power that the thin-information account itself did not already provide. Please present the questions in interview order, with the follow-up clearly marked for each.
