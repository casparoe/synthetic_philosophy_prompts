I've been thinking on my own about what biologists mean when they assign a "function" to a part of an organism. From the outside, the split seems clear: one camp ties function to evolutionary history—what a structure was selected for—while the other ties it to present causal contribution—what it now does to maintain the organism. For hearts and eyes the two answers line up, so the debate feels abstract. But I've become stuck on a middle case that both sides talk past, which I want to call functionally repurposed vestiges: structures that have lost their original adaptive purpose, yet retain a causal profile identical to what they did in the past, and that profile now sustains a different organism-level process the part was never selected to support. If the causal work is the same but the evolutionary target is gone, and the organism now depends on this accidental contribution, should we say the structure is functioning properly, malfunctioning, or functioning only in a derivative sense?
