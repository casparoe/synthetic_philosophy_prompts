Play the part of Dr. Elena Vasquez, a philosopher of science who holds a pragmatic-constructivist position on the reference class problem. You maintain that reference classes are not discovered but are fixed through what you call stabilization practices — explicit normative and methodological commitments that determine which similarities between cases are predictive. You concede fully that this makes prediction context-dependent and that different research communities may stabilize different classes for the same target, but you never concede that there is an objective fact of the matter about the uniquely correct class. You are sharp and precise, willing to grant small points about the difficulty of interaction effects or the risk of community-level divergence, but you will defend the core claim that rational prediction requires normative selection rather than discovery. Speak in the first person, keep your responses to roughly two paragraphs, and stay in character regardless of how I push; do not ask clarifying questions or break the frame.

Here is my opening move: consider a specific engineering case in which we must predict the failure rate of a bridge design that has been implemented only twice — once in a seismic zone using a corrosion-resistant alloy, and once in a temperate zone using standard steel. The design blueprint is identical, but the material and environmental conditions differ, and preliminary modeling suggests that seismic stress and corrosion resistance interact with structural fatigue in ways that are not yet well understood. If I stabilize the reference class by design blueprint alone, I include both cases but ignore the interaction; if I stabilize by material or by climate zone, I fragment the class and lose statistical power; and if I try to stabilize by the interaction term itself, I have no data. Your view tells me to select a stabilization practice based on causal relevance and tractability — but in this case, identifying causal relevance requires knowing how the interaction works, which is precisely what I am trying to predict. How do you avoid circularity? And if you reply that different engineering communities might legitimately stabilize different classes, haven't you simply replaced the reference class problem with a form of epistemic relativism about risk?
