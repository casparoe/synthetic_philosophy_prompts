ok so i'm writing up my views on populism and democratic legitimacy and want a consistency check before i present them at a reading group. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) the "will of the people" isn't empirically readable; democratic institutions pursue surrogate goals (voter satisfaction, deliberative coherence) that approximate an unobservable true will, but these can diverge systematically.
(2) populists are right that liberal constitutionalism suppresses genuine majoritarian preferences through elite-captured proceedural filters.
(3) judicial review is legitimate when it blocks outcomes violating basic rights, but only if courts appeal to constitutional commitments with democratic pedigree, not just their own moral reasoning.
(4) direct democracy via referendum expresses the popular will more authentically than representative democracy, since reps are captured by party elites.
(5) but direct democratic outcomes are often unstable and emotionally driven, so the "will" revealed might just be a distorted snapshot, not the real thing.
(6) if a stable, informed majority consistently wants something deeply unjust—like stripping citizenship from a minority—that's part of the empirical will, yet courts should block it.

i feel like i'm pulling in incompatible directions: i endorse the populist critique in (2) but defend courts in (3); i champion direct democracy in (4) but dismiss its outputs in (5); and (1) makes the true will unobservable while (6) assumes i can identify an empirical will that needs blocking. which tensions are real and which dissolve on closer look? rank by severity, say which single commitment is the weakest link, and what's the least costly fix. no need for references, just a conceptual audit.
