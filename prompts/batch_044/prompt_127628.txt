I am working out my position on inheritance taxation and intergenerational justice and would like an audit of whether my commitments are jointly tenable. Here is the package:

(1) Estate taxation is justified primarily as a safeguard of fair equality of opportunity: its purpose is to block the intergenerational transmission of unearned economic advantage that would make life prospects depend on family birth rather than on talent and effort, not to reduce inequality for its own sake or to raise revenue.

(2) Parents have weighty special obligations to provide for their children, and bequest is a legitimate expression of those obligations; the family is a valuable institution and the state should not treat the desire to leave wealth to one's children as presumptively illegitimate.

(3) Individuals are entitled to the surplus value that flows from their own efforts and talents, and this entitlement extends to the disposition of legitimately accumulated wealth, even though the distribution of natural talents is arbitrary from a moral point of view.

(4) Fair equality of opportunity is satisfied when social institutions guarantee universal access to basic goods and formal openness of positions; it does not require restricting private family expenditure on education, cultural capital, or social networks, even when such expenditure reproduces class differences in life prospects.

(5) Tax policy should treat inherited resources and earned income neutrally—both are resources at the individual's disposal—and differential rates require a justification stronger than an appeal to the source of the resources, lest they collapse into paternalism.

(6) Not all advantages conferred by wealthy parents are unjust; only those that depend on state-protected monopolies or that directly corrupt democratic equality are genuinely problematic, whereas market-based advantages are permissible.

Do these hang together? I am particularly unsure whether (1) can survive alongside (4) and (5), and whether (2) genuinely conflicts with (1) or only appears to. For each pair that is in tension, say how severe it is, which commitment is doing the work, and what the least costly revision would be. Please also identify any tensions that dissolve on closer inspection. Rank all tensions by severity, and be specific about exactly which thesis would have to be modified or abandoned if consistency is to be restored.
