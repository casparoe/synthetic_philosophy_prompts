I was arguing with a friend about Wittgenstein's private language argument for a phil. of mind course, and we kept circling this case that doesn't fit the standard either/or framing. Usually it's presented as: either you've got a fully public language with shared correction rules, or you've got someone trying to privately baptize a sensation with literally zero criteria and it collapses. But what about someone who keeps a years-long private notebook in invented symbols tracking their own moods? At first the symbols are arbitrary, but over time they develop internal consistency habits -- they won't apply symbol X today if it clashes with how they used it six months ago, they build up little diachronic rules, but nobody else ever reads the notebook or corrects them. It's not the bare 'name a pain' scenario because there's temporal structure that functions kind of like a rule, yet it's still private since there's no social check. People call this the diary case, but in the texts I've seen they just dismiss it back into the private-language bucket without looking at whether the long-term internal consistency changes anything. So I'm wondering: is there an established term for this middle zone, where a practice is private in audience but internally rule-governed across time? And if not, does the consistency over months actually address Wittgenstein's no-criterion objection, or does the absence of external correction still make it collapse? I'm not looking for a reading list, just whether the carving I'm trying to make here is already named and where exactly its edges sit.
