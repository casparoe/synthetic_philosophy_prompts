After a long argument with a friend about whether a society can ethically design its own relationship to a traumatic past, I want to compare Avishai Margalit's ethics of shared memory with Philip Rieff's critique of therapeutic culture. Margalit argues that we have a duty to remember wrongs done within thick moral relations, though he allows that forgetting can be permissible where relations are thin; Rieff, meanwhile, treats modernity's turn away from guilt and historical moral failure as part of a therapeutic dissolution of interdictory culture. To make the comparison concrete, consider a society that builds what I will call a mnemonic architecture—deliberate institutional rituals that preserve some guilt-laden histories while ceremonially releasing others. Does this kind of selective, engineered remembrance realize Margalit's ethical distinction between thick and thin obligations, or does it necessarily collapse into the therapeutic erasure Rieff describes? In particular, can institutional design ever preserve the moral weight of thick relations, or does formalizing remembrance always convert guilt into a managed psychological resource?
