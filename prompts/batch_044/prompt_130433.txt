Hey, I've been lurking on LW and ACX for years and there's this pattern I keep seeing in rationalist-adjacent self-improvement writing that I don't have a word for. People coin a local failure mode — like "status-quo bias in career choice" or "identity-protective reasoning about your productivity system" — and then start treating it like it's in the same ontologial bucket as base-rate neglect or the conjunction fallacy. The slippage goes both ways: they defend an intervention by citing the debiasing literature that only applies to the classic Kahneman-style errors, and when you point out their local concept has no reproducible experimental backing, they retreat to "well, system 1 is messy and bias is bias." It's not exactly concept creep, since that usually tracks moral or psychiatric terms expanding, and it's not plain equivocation because there's a real mechanistic resemblance. Is there an established name for this kind of microdomain taxonomy bleed — where a blogger-coined concept gets copy-pasted into the formal cognitive-bias framework and starts to recieve its epistemic authority? If there's no term, can you coin one and draw the edges: how it's different from analogical reasoning, from family-resemblance defenses a la Wittgenstein, and from ordinary auxiliary-hypothesis tweaking? I don't need a bibliography, just the carving — what exactly it is and where it stops being this thing.
