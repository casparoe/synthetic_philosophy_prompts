Construct a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts interpretive positions on Zhuangzi's attitude toward death, designed so a reader can arrive at a named stance by answering concrete questions about the text rather than recognizing labels. Root node: is death primarily a harm, a cosmological transformation, or a conceptual error about personal persistence? First-level branches should split on the function of the drum-beating episode—whether it demonstrates performative subversion of ritual mourning, acceptance of seasonal change, or epistemic humility about post-mortem states—and on whether the butterfly dream undermines the life/death distinction itself or merely suspends certainty about it. Include a node that distinguishes "ontological cycling" (death as redistribution of qi without personal residue) from "phenomenological dissolution" (the dissolution of the experiential boundary between self and environment at death), treating this as a genuine split rather than a restatement. Each leaf should carry a position name—such as transformative naturalism, ritual deconstruction, agnostic quietism, or unnamed—plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment, for example whether it must deny that grief is ever rational. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, particularly those that differ only by weighting inner versus outer chapters. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the tree must be navigable from the texts alone.
