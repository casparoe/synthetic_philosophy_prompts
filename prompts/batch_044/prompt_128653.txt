You are delivering the opening address at a two-day workshop titled Cooperation Without Mirrors, held for an audience split between formal decision theorists and practitioners who design multi-party agreements—mediators, urban planners, and treaty negotiators—most of whom have never encountered superrationality arguments. The workshop is built around two framing devices: the Platonia dilemma, in which two agents with identical reasoning capacities cooperate in a prisoner's dilemma purely through mutual recognition of shared logic, and a related variant referred to in the program as the Luring Lottery, in which cooperation is engineered not through symmetric reasoning but through a public, irreversible commitment that makes defection costlier for the committing party than for the recipient, so that the other party's response becomes rational for reasons other than mirroring. Your address should run about twelve minutes, must work structurally rather than decoratively—meaning the Platonia model should explain why small, homogeneous groups can coordinate without contracts, while the Luring Lottery should explain why larger or heterogeneous groups require visible vulnerability rather than shared epistemology—and should use both to illuminate a single concrete case: a transboundary water agreement in which one upstream nation permanently decommissions a diversion structure before any downstream reciprocity is guaranteed. Do not treat either experiment as a simple metaphor; explain the actual logical structure, including why Platonia cooperation collapses when agents lack certainty about each other's reasoning architecture, and why the Luring Lottery introduces a permanent-trap risk if the other party never responds. The decision-theorists in the room should recognize every reference without being lectured; the practitioners should grasp the stakes without learning game-theoretic notation. Do not conclude with a generic call for trust or communication; instead, end on the specific tension that cooperative structures sometimes depend on the strategic removal of exit routes, and that this removal is ethically and practically different from mere optimism. Avoid bibliographies, citations, and verbatim quotations from any source; paraphrase all theoretical claims in your own words. Do not invent historical details about the origin of the Luring Lottery.
