ok so for my diss i'm digging into that Chase and Sanborn argument about qualia being incoherent — the quining move where you apply Quine-style parsimony to first-person phenomenology and end up saying phenominal concepts are just grammatically confused placeholders. I'm mostly convinced, but I want a real audit of what could knock me off this.

Specifically, the view says qualia aren't just hard to explain physically but actually violate logical constraints on scientific posits: they have no causal role, they resist intersubjective verification, and they generate an epistemic friction with physical descriptions that doesn't map onto any real feature of the world. If that's right, then zombies aren't just conceivable but conceptually malformed, and the explanatory gap is just a linguistic artifact.

But what would actually count against this? I don't want the standard zombie intuition pump — I think the quining strategy already dissolves that by undermining the coherence of the concept being pumped. Instead I want the specific defeaters: maybe an argument that Quine's translation indeterminancy doesn't analogize to phenomenology the way Chase and Sanborn claim; or empirical results showing phenominal concepts do real cognitive work in scientific reasoning; or a formal result that any elimination of phenominal vocabulary collapses into a rival physicalist view rather than standing alone.

Also, has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability? If I respond to every objection by saying that's just the grammatical confusion talking, am I insulating the position? And is insulation actually bad here, or is it appropriate for an eliminativist thesis?

Rank the defeaters: what's fatal, what's awkward, and which ones defenders of the view would honestly concede versus dismiss. Be specific about the mechanism of defeat, not just someone might disagree.
