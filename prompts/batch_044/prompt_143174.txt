I serve on my university’s Committee on Research Ethics and Institutional Risk. The physics department has proposed Project Meridian, a five-year experiment that will maintain macroscopic quantum superpositions in a cryogenic lattice and repeatedly induce irreversible decoherence events—on the order of 10^23 per measurement cycle—to map decoherence dynamics. The proposal cleared physical-safety and data-governance review, but a dissenting member (a philosopher of physics) has filed a minority brief arguing that if panpsychist or minimal-integrated-information accounts apply at quantum scales, then forced decoherence is not ethically neutral disturbance but the annihilation of brief, substrate-level phenomenal states. She introduces what she calls the decoherence harm asymmetry: reversible interaction with a quantum system may leave any minimal experience intact, whereas irreversible collapse eliminates it, making collapse structurally closer to killing than to touching. She asks the committee either to reject the proposal or to mandate a redesign using reversible coherence-cycling protocols that preserve superposition states rather than destroying them. The majority view holds that this applies moral categories to entities lacking nervous systems, behavior, or any independent claim to status, and that conditioning research funding on speculative metaphysics would paralyze physics. Draft the committee’s formal decision memo—approve, approve with conditions, or reject—giving reasoning that the researchers could actually respond to, including an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. The memo should treat the philosophical issue on its merits without requesting citations, bibliographies, or external reading lists.
