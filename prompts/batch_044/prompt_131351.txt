I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about moral learning from biograpy, going by my gut on cases. Reading Plutarch's parallel lives — Cato's austerity set against a more indulgent Roman — feels like I'm doing practical reasoning on a grid, not just absorbing a story; that counts as real learning. A medieval hagiography where the saint never doubts and miracles fix everything gives me a warm feeling for an hour but doesn't shift my behavior; not real learning. A modern warts-and-all biography of a civil-rights organizer: seeing her grudges and broken friendships makes her courage feel copyable, where the polished version felt like a monument I couldn't approach — transparency helps. But then a biography of a cruel dictator that explains every atrocity through childhood trauma and context makes me understand him too well; I come away excusing things I shouldn't. That's negative learning, not neutral. I also think growth narratives teach well — seeing someone become good over time shows the path — but I also think static-virtue narratives teach well — seeing someone good from youth proves it's not just repair. Those two verdicts fight each other. And I keep goign back and forth on a 19th-century reformer who sacrificed every personal bond for one cause: heroic focus or monomaniacal warning? I can't tell if the ambiguity is in the life or in me.
