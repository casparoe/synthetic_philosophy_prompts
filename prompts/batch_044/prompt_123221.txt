I've been working through some formal alignment literature on low-impact agents with colleagues, and we keep encountering a specific conceptual knot that feels like it should have an established name, though none of us can identify it. The pattern is this: someone attempts to define "side effects" or "impact" by comparing the actual world — with the agent present and acting — against a counterfactual baseline in which the agent never activates. To construct that baseline, one must specify precisely which features of the world are held constant and which are permitted to vary. If the baseline freezes the entire environment except for the agent's direct outputs, then enormous indirect consequences are excluded from measurement by definition; but if the baseline allows variation only in variables that are explicitly designated as "goal-related," then one has already performed the normative work of specifying what counts as valuable or irrelevant, which is exactly what the low-impact framework was intended to avoid specifying in full. I have noticed an analogous structure in ecological reasoning, where measuring an organism's "net impact" requires selecting whether to hold climate dynamics, competing species, and evolutionary trajectories constant, and each selection implicitly encodes a judgment about what the ecosystem ought to preserve. In both cases, the formalization of "do no harm" appears to collapse into either triviality — the agent is compared against a world in which nothing else can change — or hidden value-loading through the choice of baseline.

Does this circularity have an established name in decision theory, philosophy of science, or the alignment literature — perhaps as a variant of the frame problem, a self-reference difficulty for embedded agents, or a specific kind of counterfactual baseline failure? If there is an established concept, what precisely distinguishes it from ordinary counterfactual dependence and from the general difficulty of defining side effects without a complete utility function? If there is no recognized term, please propose a precise definition together with clear boundary conditions that separate it from related issues, such as the non-identity problem applied to causal baselines or the problem of specifying an agent's own presence within the environment it evaluates. Please provide your answer in American English.
