Write an essay addressing the following controversy: if the simulation argument carries any weight, ought it genuinely to dampen fanatical commitment to the far future, or does it instead generate a disguised variant of longtermist urgency—call it "simulation-indexical longtermism"? Examine the claim that accepting a high probability that we inhabit an ancestor simulation requires us to discount the moral importance of distant future generations, not on the grounds that they lack consciousness, but because our present actions might be evaluated by unknown base-reality observers whose preferences are inscrutable. Weigh this against the compartmentalisation thesis: that being simulated is metaphysically orthogonal to the suffering and flourishing of conscious beings within the simulation, so practical reasoning about climate stability, artificial intelligence alignment, or interstellar settlement ought to remain untouched. Reach and justify a definite verdict on whether the argument should temper our commitment to the far future, and address whether there exists a principled distinction between "being simulated" and "occupying a lower-level reality" that permits one to retain longtermist urgency without falling into metaphysical arrogance. Take a committed position rather than surveying options neutrally, and identify precisely where you think the reasoning succeeds or collapses.
