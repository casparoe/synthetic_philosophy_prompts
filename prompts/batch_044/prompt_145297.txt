I've been trying to organize my thoughts on embedded agency for a reading group and want a consistency check before I open my mouth. Here's what I actually think:

(a) Any agent that's physically embedded in the world it models can't have a fully transparent self-model—its reasoning is just another causal process out there, not a privileged internal view.

(b) Even with that opacity, the agent should still do expected-utility reasoning over actions, treating its own decision procedure like a black-box prediction rather than a direct choice.

(c) I reject the Cartesian boundary thing—there's no sharp line between agent and environment—but I still want to say there's a real functional difference between beliefs about the world and beliefs about yourself.

(d) I also think an embedded agent can be genuinely surprised by what it ends up doing, and that this surprise should update its self-model, not just be treated as noise.

(e) And I'm a compatibilist about free will: freedom is just absence of external coercion, and that applies fine even when your decision procedure is opaque.

Wether these hang together? I'm especially worried about (b) and (d)—if I'm predicting my own behavior like weather, can I really be surprised by it in a meaningful way? And does (c) collapse once you drop the Cartesian line? Rank the tensions by severity and tell me the least costly fix, not just that everything is broken.
