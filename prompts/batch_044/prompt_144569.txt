I've been following the debate about whether large language models and similar systems should be classified as tools or as agents, and I'm struck by how little movement there seems to be despite years of discussion. On one side, people emphasize that these systems lack continuous existence outside prompts, have no intrinsic goals, and are designed and deployed entirely within human-directed workflows; they argue that calling them 'agents' is a category error that risks anthropomorphism. On the other side, people point out that these systems can formulate sub-goals, adapt strategies to novel contexts, and initiate sequences of actions—such as running code, querying databases, or revising their own outputs—that are not fully specified by the user in advance; they argue that denying agency here is just definitional gatekeeping. I've started to wonder whether the impasse isn't about the technology at all but about which features of agency we treat as essential. In particular, I'm drawn to a distinction I haven't seen clearly drawn in the literature: between what we might call 'instrumental agency,' where a system's goal-directed behavior is fully traceable to a human directive that remains active and revisable at every step, and 'operational autonomy,' where the system generates intermediate objectives that are not individually authorized but are constrained only by high-level parameters. If this distinction is sound, it might split the difference: current AI might have operational autonomy without instrumental independence. But I'm unsure whether this resolves the disagreement or just relocates it.

Is the stalemate here really a verbal dispute about the word 'agent,' a substantive disagreement about which metaphysical or functional conditions matter, or are the two sides actually answering different questions—one about moral accountability and one about system architecture—under the same heading? I'd like a diagnosis that engages with what each side actually emphasizes, not a survey. And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a conceptual distinction like the one I sketched, an empirical finding about system behavior, or a change in the framing question? If my premise is confused—say, if the debate isn't actually stalled but just genuinely hard, or if my distinction collapses—please say so plainly rather than accommodating the framing politely.
