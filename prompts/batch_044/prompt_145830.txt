Write a short story about a sound archivist who thinks she's cracked the paradox of tragedy—why we actually enjoy sad art—by inventing preemptive nostalgia. Her theory: people only weep happily at tragic music when the grief is for a loss they haven't lived yet; the art lets them borrow sorrow from a future self so thier present feels less lonely. She builds an algorithm that predicts each listener's likely future grief and pairs it with an old opera aria. Write it from her pov as she tests it on a stranger in a quiet room, and have her slowly realize the paradox was never about catharsis or safe distance, but about this weird temporal borrowing. Don't cite philosophers, don't make it a lecture—just let the idea breathe. Keep it under 700 words or so.
