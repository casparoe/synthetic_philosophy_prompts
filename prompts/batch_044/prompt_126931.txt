There's this weird split in some enviromental agencies: they run strict cost-benefit analysis for policies with 10-year payoffs, but flat-out reject the same math for anything past 50 years, saying uncertanty makes comparison impossible. At the same time they still pour money into carbon sequestration that won't matter for centuries, citing vague intergenerational duty arguments. What view of moral reasoning under deep ignorance does that pattern presuppose? Is it consistent with taking outcomes seriously at all, or does it smuggle in a non-consequentialist commitment? And if we actually took the cluelessness idea seriously, wouldn't we have to change how we justify the long-term projects, not just reject the long-term math?
