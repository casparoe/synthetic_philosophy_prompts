Write a dialogue between two people debating what Descartes's cogito actually proves. One holds the thin view: all you get is that some thinking is happening right now, and you can't squeeze a persisting self out of it. The other holds the thick view: even if you grant that, the very act of doubting forces you to acknowledge a single subject that's doing the doubting, not just a scattered mental flash.

But don't just do the usual soul-vs-event replay. Instead, inject this twist: have them discuss a hypothetical case where someone's consciousness is technically continous but their autobiographical memory gets totally erased every few minutes, so they never experience themselves as lasting longer than that window. The thin-view person should use this to argue that the cogito applies fresh each window and never builds a lasting 'I'; the thick-view person should argue that continuity of reasoning within the window still requires a unified subject, and maybe that the erased memories don't erase the thinker.

Make them actually talk to each other—no strawmen, real back-and-forth, each answering the other's actual point. Write it casual, like they're arguing over coffee. A typo or two is fine.
