I've been trying to make sense of Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition in relation to grounding, and I think I've spotted a simpler fix then people admit. Bradley's worry is that if 'the cup is blue' is one unified fact, then the relation linking the cup to blueness needs its own relation to both terms, and so on forever. But if we treat grounding as primitive and unanalysable — not an extra thing but just the 'because' that holds — there's nothing left to relate. So isn't modern grounding theory basically Bradley's regress with a fancy name, except we've already solved it by declaring the connection primitive? Or am I wrong that grounding is meant to dodge exactly this kind of regress?
