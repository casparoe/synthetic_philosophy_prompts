I want a classroom game that makes students feel the discursive dilemma from the inside — specifically why premise-based aggregation feels more legitimate even when it spits out a conclusion that literally nobody in the room endorsed. Then roles are revealed: half are 'premise specialists' who only care about P and Q, half are 'conclusion specialists' who only care about R. They have to apply the rule they picked. The payoff: the group gets shared points only if, after seeing the result, at least two-thirds can write a one-sentence defence of it. Design the cards, the hidden-role draw, the voting protocall, and the debrief that maps their frustration onto the distinction between premise-based and conclusion-based procedures.
