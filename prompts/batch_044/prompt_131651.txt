Hey, I need a sanity check. Our town council just replaced the usual town-meeting vote with this thing they call "preference weaving" — everyone fills out a form ranking issues like parking, green space, taxes, noise, and then a committee stitches together a compromise plan that literally nobody proposed but that scores lowest on "collective regret." The mayor went on local radio and said this reveals the "authentic will of the people" far better than counting heads ever could, since it captures our deeper values instead of surface preferences. But the plan they adopted cuts funding for the skate park I actually care about, and a bunch of neighbors are furious because they never signed on to this exact package. The council keeps saying that if you object, you must think democracy is only legit when you win, and that real popular sovereignty means accepting the revealed collective will even when you dislike it. I feel like thier using fancy math to manufacture consent and then branding it the real voice of town. Is there actually a philosophical gap between "what most people want on a ballot" and "what the people truly want as a collective," or am I just salty because I lost?
