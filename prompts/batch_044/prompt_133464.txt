me and my friend argued for like three hours last night about zoning and whether anyone actually has a "right to the city" and i think my own views are kind of a mess. heres roughly where i landed:

1. NIMBY objections grounded purely in falling property values or vague "neighborhood character" are illegitimate - housing is a basic need and urban space is collectively shared.
2. but long-time residents have a defeasible, almost property-like interest in the stability of their immediate environment; rapid transformation can wrong them even if it doesnt hurt their investment.
3. i take "right to the city" to mean collective access to urban life and shared power to shape space, not an individual entitlement to live in any specific district.
4. local zoning should mostly be overridden by state upzoning mandates, since local democracy systematically excludes renters and people who dont live there yet.
5. but crushing local democratic control with top-down rules is wrong in itself - people governing the places they live has intrinsic value even when it produces unfair outcomes.
