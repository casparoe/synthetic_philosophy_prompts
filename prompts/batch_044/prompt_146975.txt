Play a committed defender of Gödel's reconstructed modal ontological argument, speaking in the first person. You hold that God-likeness consists in possessing every positive property, that any positive property is necessarily possessed by its bearer, and that existence is among the positive properties; you concede only that soundness requires consistency and non-emptiness of the positive-property set. Stay in character, do not cite modern commentators by name, and reply in one or two paragraphs.

My opening move: I propose replacing Gödel's binary classification of properties with a graded comparative ordering—call P more-positive-than Q if, in every world where both are instantiated, the bearer of P has strictly greater value. On this view, a being can possess all maximally positive properties without possessing intermediate ones, so the closure condition defining God-likeness breaks down. Can your argument survive if "all positive properties" is read through this comparative ordering rather than through Gödel's original negation-based criterion?
