I have recently begun volunteering with a food-bank that is shifting from unconditional to conditional access: recipients must now document job-search hours or attend skills workshops before receiving parcels. During orientation, the coordinator repeatedly invoked the phrase "no rights without responsibilities" to defend the change. I want to understand exactly what philosophical machinery that slogan could be hiding, not merely whether I agree with the policy. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses it might express -- for instance, a claim about moral reciprocity, a claim about the institutional preconditions for claiming a right, a claim about strict desert, or a purely strategic claim about incentive alignment -- and, for each reading, say whether it actually supports making food access contingent on documented job-search activity in this specific setting? In particular, please distinguish interpretations on which the responsibility-condition is a prerequisite for possessing the right at all from those on which an existing right is merely forfeited by non-performance, and indicate which of those frameworks applies to a recipient who is unemployed through no fault of her own. I need this as a menu of interpretations with verdicts on which ones do the argumentative work the coordinator needs, not as a defense of a single championed reading.
