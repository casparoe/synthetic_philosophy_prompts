Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a token-gamete origin essentialist and a trajectory-class origin essentialist on whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg. Party A holds that the specific sperm and egg that actually fused are metaphysically essential: in any possible world in which that person exists, those very gametes (or entities causally continuous with them) initiate the organism. Party B holds that personal identity is anchored to a specific developmental-trajectory class—defined by a particular genetic-environmental interaction profile and a continuous causal-developmental path—so that different token gametes could instantiate the same trajectory, but also that the same gametes could yield a different person if the trajectory diverges at an early epigenetic branch point.

Produce a two-part document. First, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including: about the formal structure of origin-essentialism arguments (what they establish and what they do not); about the modal intuition that tracks something real but is ambiguous between gamete-source and trajectory-source; about the biological facts of fertilization, zygotic genome activation, and early epigenetic marking; and about what standard psychological-continuity counterexamples actually show (that continuity alone fails to rule out branching, not that origin is irrelevant). Second, a disagreements table with three columns—issue; Party A’s formulation in wording it accepts; Party B’s formulation in wording it accepts—covering: (i) whether gametes or trajectories are the proper essential origin; (ii) whether epigenetic contingency at the zygotic stage undermines gamete essentialism or reinforces trajectory essentialism; (iii) whether the counterfactual “different parents, same me” is genuinely possible or merely notional.

Then identify the cruxes—sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party. Specify two: (1) whether an organism with identical nuclear DNA but divergent epigenetic markers at implantation is the same person or a trajectory-branch; (2) whether a synthetically reconstructed developmental trajectory initiated by different gametes but matching the original profile preserves identity. For each crux, state the concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side’s explicit prediction recorded. For (1), both accept that monozygotic twinning and artificial embryo splitting are relevant; Party A should predict that splitting produces a new individual with a shared origin but not shared identity, while Party B should predict that identity tracks the trajectory, so that divergence at splitting initiates a new trajectory and therefore a new person. For (2), both accept that synthetic gamete derivation and in vitro trajectory replication are relevant; Party A should predict that reconstructed trajectories from different gametes necessarily produce a different person, while Party B should predict that identity is preserved if and only if the trajectory class is fully replicated.

Conclude with a brief joint assessment: is part of the dispute verbal, specifically about whether “origin” denotes gamete-source or trajectory-source? If so, specify exactly which formulations collapse into verbal disagreement and why, and isolate the residual substantive disagreement. The document must not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and should not invoke obscure technical literature unknown to a well-informed generalist philosopher; but the formulations should be precise enough that neither side can retreat to ambiguity.
