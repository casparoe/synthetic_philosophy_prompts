Can you translate his main line of reasoning into the vocabulary of contemporary harm-reduction epidemiology and QALY-based public-health framwork? Treat the decision as a risk-benefit calculation within a population-health model, not a moral sin. For each part of Hume's argument — the autonomy claim, the rejection of religious duty, the comparison to other voluntary risks — say whether it maps cleanly, needs distortion to fit, or just has no equivalent in that framework. Then tell me whether what's left out is a gap in harm-reduction thinking, or whether Hume was just being vague about moral agency.
