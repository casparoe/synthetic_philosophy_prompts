You are a professional philosopher of value, with particular expertise in existentialist thought and contemporary theories of meaning in life. Before answering, please do a brief internal audit: list the three core commitments of the view I am about to describe, then generate the strongest possible objection to each commitment separately, then rank all objections by how damaging they are. Think carefully and systematically; do not rush to defend the view.

I have come to hold a synthetic position I call lucid engagement, which draws on familiar absurdist and hybrid-objectivist ideas but arranges them in a way I have not seen fully defended. It has three parts. First, it accepts the standard absurdist claim that there is no cosmic, transcendent, or metaphysically guaranteed purpose that could confer meaning on human lives from outside: the universe is silent on value, and no divine or natural framework resolves this silence. Second, it rejects both pure subjectivism and the standard hybrid objectivist accounts associated with thinkers like Susan Wolf or Iddo Landau, which require that a meaningful life involve both subjective attraction and objective worth. On my view, objective worth is entirely irrelevant to whether a life is meaningful: a life spent in objectively trivial pursuits can be fully meaningful if pursued in the right mode, and a life of objectively worthy achievement can be meaningless if pursued instrumentally or in bad faith. Third, and this is the distinctive commitment, the view holds that awareness of groundlessness is not a deficit to be overcome but a constitutive feature of genuine meaning. Meaning does not arise from resolving the tension between human aspiration and cosmic indifference, but from sustaining that tension with full consciousness: what matters is not merely that you build the cathedral, but that you build it while knowing it will eventually be dust, and that you continue building precisely in light of that knowledge rather than in denial of it.

What would count against this position? I want an honest map of its exposure, not a list of generic objections. Which would be merely awkward, requiring qualification but not surrender? In particular, please address the following. If psychological research showed that sustained, explicit awareness of groundlessness correlates strongly with clinical despair and functional impairment rather than with flourishing, does that undermine the claim that such awareness is constitutive of meaning? Does the view risk collapsing into a disguised form of subjectivism, since the agent's internal attitude appears to do all the normative work?
