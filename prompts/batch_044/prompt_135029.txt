I read this summary in a philosophy podcast transscript about Mill's late socialism: "In his late Chapters on Socialism and the revised editions of Principles, Mill moved toward what he called associationist socialism rather than state collectivism. He argued that land rents should be socialized through a tax on unearned increment, and that worker cooperatives like the Rochdale type could eventually outcompete private firms without any state takeover. He also held that competitive markets systematically depressed wages below the value of labor, and that true liberty required economic self-government in the workplace, not just political voting rights. But he also believed that personal choice in consumption and career was largely a bourgeois myth, and that once large-scale industry was nationalized under democratic control, individual autonomy in economic matters would become obsolete because collective planning replaces private preference." Theres a lot packed in there. Is this a fair rendering of Mill's mature view?
