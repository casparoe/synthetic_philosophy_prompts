I'm giving the opening remarks tomorrow morning at a workshop called Certainty in Practice. The audience is split: half are epistemologists who've spent years with Wittgenstein's On Certianty, half are archivists and museum curators who haven't. I need about three minutes, casual — I'm terrible at formal speeches. I want hinge epistemology to do real structural work, not just be decoration. The core move: the archivists treat catalog entries and provenance records as fixed background, and philosophers treat basic commitments the same way; disagreement is only possible because those hinges are shared. But I need to block the obvious misreading that this makes everything relative or socially constructed — I want to say that recognizing a hinge isn't the same as doubting it. There's also this inversion I keep noticing, where one group's hinge is another group's open question; I want the remarks to stage that between the philosophers and the curators rather than just labeling it. And I need to honor my advisor, who planned this workshop before she died — she used to say a good hinge is like a doorframe, you only notice it when the door stops closing. I want that image in there without getting maudlin. No citations, no suggested readings, people will be half-asleep. Oh, and in my notes I wrote that hinge propositions are the axiums of inquiry — I meant axioms — but if leaving the typo makes it sound more like me, go ahead. Just don't make it a lecture.
