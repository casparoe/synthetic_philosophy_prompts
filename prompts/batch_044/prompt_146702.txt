I've been chewing on the Death in Damascus story lately, but not as a fatalism puzzle — more as a case study in what I guess I'd call "predictive self-exclusion." The idea is that if a predictor is good enough, any attempt to condition your behaviour on its forecast automatically dumps you in the reference class where the forecast holds. So evasion isn't blocked by destiny being fixed; it's blocked because your evasion *is* the data the predictor used.

I'm pretty convinced this generalises past the story. But what would actually count against it? Not the usual free-will handwave. I want defeaters that make the stuctural claim collapse — maybe a formal result where self-exclusion breaks in multi-agent settings, or an empircal pattern where people consistently out-predict their own models. Which ones are live objections versus philosophers just playing with toy examples? And be honest: have I just qualified this into a tautology? If so, is that actually a problem for a claim like this?
