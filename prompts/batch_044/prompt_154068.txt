You serve on a teaching hospital's Clinical Epistemology Committee. You must circulate a decision memo before Thursday's vote on Proposal 7-B, which would require attending physicians to anchor their documented diagnostic confidence to the hospital's validated cohort-level epidemiological probabilities—treated here as objective chances—unless they record a specific, protocol-defined defeating feature of the individual patient. Proponents defend this as institutional deference to objective chance: rational credence should align with known base rates absent admissible underminers. Opponents counter that clinical reasoning legitimately relies on tacit, case-specific cues that the protocol wrongly classifies as inadmissible, and that because the hospital's probabilities come from interventional trials while patients arrive in observational settings, the chances being deferred to may not govern actual outcomes in this population.

Draft the committee's memo recommending approval, rejection, or approval with conditions. Provide a concrete course of action with an implementation boundary or sunset clause; explain how the Principal Principle does or does not apply given the interventional-observational mismatch; and engage the strongest argument on the losing side with genuine weight rather than dismissal. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists. Maximum 400 words, one page.
