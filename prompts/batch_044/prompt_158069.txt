Design a decision-tree taxonomy for an advanced philosophy-of-mind seminar that sorts views on whether fundamental physics carries ethically relevant suffering within panpsychist and near-panpsychist frameworks. Root commitment: fundamental physical entities possess phenomenal properties of some kind. From there, branch through four diagnostic nodes phrased as concrete evaluable claims rather than label-questions: (1) macro-subject binding—does quantum or field-level coherence yield a unified cosmic subject, or only dispersed micro-subjects? (2) valence—do those properties include negative affect, or are they purely structural/cognitive? (3) decoherence threshold—does thermal noise or Planck-scale decoherence set a moral-status cutoff, or do all scales count equally? (4) intervention—would altering physical conditions (e.g., reducing local entropy to preserve coherence) be ethically required, permitted, or forbidden? Each leaf should name an actual position where one exists (e.g., cosmopsychism with full-spectrum valence, micropsychist “noise suffering” view, protopanpsychism with decoherence cutoff, physicalist eliminativism) and mark genuinely unoccupied combinations with a brief diagnosis of whether the gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate. The instrument should contain six to seven nodes total, be aimed at students familiar with the combination problem but not with the taxonomy itself, and must contain no bibliographies, citations, or requests for external reading lists.
