I was dissussing this with a friend over coffee and I think I've landed on a specific read of Wittgenstein's hinge epistemology—mostly drawn from On Certainty, but with a twist I haven't seen pushed very far. The standard line is that propositions like the earth having existed long before my birth, or my having two hands, aren't knowledge-claims we justify from other beliefs; they're the bedrock that makes justification possible at all. I want to push this further: I think these aren't metaphysically fixed bedrock, but what I'd call practical hinges—rules of the langauge-game that are certain only because the practices depending on them are currently stable. More specifically, I think some hinges are fragile hinges: they don't get undermined by direct counterevidence, but can migrate out of bedrock status when the practice itself shifts. For instance, the idea that this image is an unmediated record of reality used to function as a hinge, and now with generative synthetic media it's sliding into the river of empirical doubt. So my position is that hinge-certainty is structural and practice-bound rather than eternal, and that doubting a hinge is a catagory mistake only so long as the supporting practices remain intact.

What would actually count against this view? I don't want the boilerplate response that I'm just conventionalizing certainty—I want the serious defeaters. Is there an argument that some hinges are genuinely non-migratable regardless of how practices shift, which would mean my practice-dependence collapses into arbitrariness? Or that if hinges can migrate, then skepticism isn't a catagory error but just an accurate prediction that a practice is about to collapse? And if it turns out that every hinge is fragile given enough technological or social disruption, does my view become unfalsifiable—just saying the practice hasn't broken yet? I'd like them sorted by how decisive they'd be: fatal, awkward but survivable, and the ones defenders of this position would themselves concede. Also, is there a hidden risk that this is just Humean skepticism wearing different clothes, and if so, should I treat that as a problem or actually a feature?
