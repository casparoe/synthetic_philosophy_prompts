Contemporary cliodynamics treats secular cycles—such as the integration and disintegration phases observed in agrarian societies—as quantifiable patterns driven by demographic-structural mechanisms like elite overproduction and declining state fiscal capacity. If these variables can be parameterized with sufficient precision to generate predictive thresholds for systemic crisis, then it appears that macrohistorical dynamics operate with a law-like regularity comparable to physical systems. Does this imply that normative political theory should be subordinated to predictive modeling—since understanding the structural trajectory of a society becomes more urgent than debating justice within it—or does this view mistakenly treat probabilistic structural tendencies as deterministic necessities, thereby misunderstanding both the limits of quantification and the enduring role of contingent human agency?
