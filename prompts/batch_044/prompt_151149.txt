To keep the explanation concrete rather than purely formal, work with the following modified scenario: imagine the hotel has actually infinitely many occupied rooms, and each occupant can move to the next room only after receiving a direct signal from the occupant immediately behind them, so that accommodating a single new arrival by shifting every guest upward requires an infinite chain of signals with no first sender. First, clarify exactly which feature of actual infinity—simultaneous completeness, the absence of a last element, or the definability of a bijection with a proper subset—the physical signal-chain analogy illegitimately treats as if it were a temporal, step-by-step potential process. Then explain why this analogy does not undermine Cantorian cardinality but instead confuses logical structure with physical performability. Finally, address whether a finitist who denies that any infinite collection is ever actually completed can consistently allow that physical space or time might be unbounded in extent without ever constituting an actual infinity, or whether that position collapses into an implicit acceptance of the very completed totality it seeks to reject. In your answer, distinguish what the finitist must deny about the ontology of the hotel (that all rooms exist together) from what they may still accept about unending possible extension.
