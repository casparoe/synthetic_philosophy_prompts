You serve on an interdisciplinary working group revising diagnostic criteria for psychiatric conditions, composed of clinicians and epidemiologists without formal philosophical training. The group must evaluate whether proposed changes to a classification are tracking genuine shifts in an underlying condition or are artifacts of what Ian Hacking describes as looping effects—the reciprocal interaction between a diagnostic category and the self-understanding, institutional treatment, and behavioral presentation of those classified. You need a reusable decision procedure that this group can apply to any proposal to alter criteria or prevalence thresholds.

Write the procedure as an ordered sequence of questions. For each step, specify: what counts as a sufficient answer to proceed; the precise point where the procedure stops with a recommendation against the proposed change; the points where judgment cannot be eliminated because the issue is one of values rather than empirical fact, and who must sign off; and the conditions under which the procedure misfires or produces a false negative. The procedure must preserve Hacking’s core distinctions—between the classification altering the people classified, institutional incentives reshaping symptom presentation, and the classified actively resisting or redefining the category—while explicitly dropping philosophical distinctions that do not survive operationalization for practitioners.

Then run the procedure on three proposals: first, lowering the age-of-onset threshold for a mood disorder after clinicians report encountering younger patients who actively self-identify with the diagnosis; second, narrowing criteria for a personality disorder after prevalence rates drop sharply in jurisdictions that have reduced long-term institutional care; and third, a case where the procedure yields a recommendation that conflicts with the clinical intuition of the group members—explain exactly what the procedure demands, why the intuition resists it, and where the disagreement actually lies.

Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot determine, especially regarding whether altering patient identity through reclassification is harmful or beneficial, and identify the specific juncture at which a philosophical disagreement rather than an empirical disagreement drives the outcome. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
