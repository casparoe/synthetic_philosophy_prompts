i'm putting together a feature on Schrodinger's later philosophical stuff — the Vedanta influence, the idea that there's really only one consciousness and individual minds are like waves on a single ocean. a philosopher friend sent me this argument that she says kills the whole view, and i can't tell if it's fair or just sneeky. here's the chain:

1) If Advaita Vedanta is right, their's exactly one subject of experience, not many.
2) The difference between me and you is then like the difference between wave A and wave B — real at the practical level but not at the fundamental level.
3) Moral responsibility tracks the fundamental level, not the practical one, because that's where causation and consciousness actually sit.
4) So if I harm you, it's literally the same subject harming itself; if you win a prize, it's literally me winning it.
5) That collapses all moral distinctions — either i should feel guilty for every atrocity in history and proud of every good deed, or the concepts of praise and blame don't apply at all.
6) Schrodinger clearly didn't live like that, and neither does anyone who takes the view seriously.
7) So the unity thesis can't be literally true; at best it's a poetic metaphor.

it feels like step 3 is doing all the dirty work — maybe moral agency tracks the practical/illusory boundary after all? but then what's left of the metaphysical claim? if i reject 3, do i save Vedanta or just water it down into something useless? and if i keep 3, do i have to accept 5, or is there a hidden equivocation between "same subject" and "same agent"? i want to know which link is weakest and what it costs to break it, because right now i'm leaning toward keeping 1 and ditching something in the middle, but i'm not sure what i'm paying.
