I am preparing a graduate seminar on self-locating belief and have been working through the Judy Benjamin thought experiment, in which a soldier is told she belongs to one of two platoons—Red or Blue—and then receives a message whose content is conditional on her actual platoon assignment, generated according to a protocol she already knows in advance. The standard formulation asks how she should revise her credence about which platoon she is in once she receives this message, especially since she can anticipate exactly what message she would have received had she been in the other platoon.

What strikes me is that the philosophical literature on this case has been stalled for decades between those who treat the received message as ordinary evidence that should shift her unconditional credences through standard Bayesian conditioning, and those who insist that, because the message is self-locating and centered on the agent herself, the update must be handled through a centered-worlds framework that preserves her prior distribution over platoons intact. Each side has formal machinery—Jeffrey-style conditioning rules versus de se update procedures—that seems internally consistent, and neither side appears to commit an empirical mistake about the setup. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not really about the arithmetic of conditionalization at all, but about whether being Judy introduces an irreducible indexical that blocks ordinary conditioning; yet I cannot tell whether this divergence is substantive, verbal—perhaps both camps are using the word “updating” to cover different normative targets—or whether the dispute masks two genuinely different questions, one about rational belief dynamics and one about the metaphysics of centered propositions, being pursued under a single heading.

Can you diagnose why this particular debate has stalled? Is there a hidden formal disagreement about how self-locating information should be represented in a credence function, or is the impasse driven by bedrock intuitions about what rationality requires when an agent updates on evidence she knows was generated precisely for her? And given your diagnosis, what—if anything—would actually move the dispute: a new formal result in the logic of indexicals that forces one representation over another, a conceptual distinction that cleanly separates the epistemological question from the metaphysical one, or is this one of those cases where progress should not be expected because the two camps are answering different questions that happen to share the same example?
