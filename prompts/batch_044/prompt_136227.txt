I am drafting a philosophical novel in which two metaphysicians—one a relationalist about object identity, the other a defender of primitive thisness—are compelled to co-author a joint adversarial-collaboration document after encountering a modified version of Black’s spheres inside the narrative. For the scene to carry intellectual weight, I need the actual text they would sign together, with their residual disagreement rendered as precisely as possible.

Please draft that joint statement. Treat the two camps as adversarial collaborators: Camp Relational, which holds that spatiotemporal, causal, and structural relations fully individuate objects, so that Black’s two spheres are distinguished by their mutual relations and by the global topology of the universe they inhabit, preserving a relational form of the principle of identity of indiscernibles; and Camp Primitive, which holds that Black’s scenario demonstrates the possibility of two objects sharing every qualitative and every relational property, leaving only bare numerical difference that requires primitive haecceity or non-qualitative essence, and therefore that the strong principle is false.

Structure the document as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: include agreement on the logical form of Leibniz’s law, on the formal conditions that a counterexample to the principle must satisfy, on what Black’s symmetric universe establishes and what it leaves open, and on what modern symmetry arguments in physics do and do not prove about individuation. Second, a disagreements table: for each camp, state its position in wording that camp would accept, specifically addressing whether relational properties are genuinely intrinsic to an object or extrinsic to it; whether a universe with perfect global symmetry can still supply distinguishing relations; and whether quantum indistinguishability in symmetric potentials offers a useful analog. Third, identify the cruxes—the sub-questions whose settlement would actually move one side. I take the decisive ones to be whether relational individuation collapses when there is no asymmetric external reference frame, and whether a discrete lattice-simulated version of Black’s world—where two macro-objects occupy nodes that are symmetrically equivalent with respect to every other node and to every embedded observer, with no accessible outside—exposes the relational reply as circular or preserves it. Fourth, for each crux, present one concrete case or argument that both sides agree bears on it, and record each camp’s definite prediction about the outcome in advance.

In drafting, be committed rather than ecumenical. Each camp should argue with full conviction; do not present them as interchangeable options. Record definite predictions, not hedged probabilities. If you judge that part of the dispute is purely verbal—if, for example, the disagreement about intrinsic versus extrinsic properties dissolves once both sides accept the same structural description—say so plainly, defend that judgment, and explain what substantive disagreement remains, if any. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke obscure theories or technical results that a well-read but non-specialist model could not be expected to know. Keep every claim standing on conceptual argument alone, and make the document one that both fictional philosophers could genuinely put their names to.
