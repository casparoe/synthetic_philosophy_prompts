I'm preparing an essay for my philosophy course on Aristotle's account of money and exchange. Rather than summarizing his well-known distinction between oikonomia and chrematistike, I want to examine whether his condemnation of wealth-getting should be read as a critique of monetary exchange itself, or instead as a critique of exchange that has been detached from natural limits and social purpose. Specifically, I want to weigh his claim that money was invented to facilitate exchange but becomes unnatural when used to generate more money against the modern economic argument that liquidity and capital accumulation are structurally necessary for complex economies. The essay should reach and defend a clear conclusion: is Aristotle's framework genuinely useful for diagnosing modern financial practices, or does it misunderstand the functional role of money? I also want to consider whether the idea of exchange being unnatural can be separated from his broader teleological conception of nature, since that separation might determine whether his critique survives outside an Aristotelian metaphysics.
