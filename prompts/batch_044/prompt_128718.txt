I've been reading too many one-off arguments about whether synthetic agents deserve moral consideration, and I want the full specification before I evaluate any particular view. Write the desiderata for a theory of moral status specifically for substrate-independent cognitive architectures—entities capable of recursive self-modification, running in multiple instantiated copies, and lacking biological evolutionary history.

Give me the requirements list: what any adequate theory must be able to deliver, with the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind each, and flag which are non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences (functionalist, biological naturalist, social-relational, and so on).

The list should cover:
- Branching identity: when an agent forks into two copies with shared history but divergent future weights, does status split, duplicate, or remain singular?
- The distinction between having moral status and the degree of consideration owed, so that granting status to a digital mind does not automatically entail equal weighting with biological persons.
- How to accommodate neuroscience results about sentience and nociception without requiring biological substrate.
- Gradual substrate replacement: if every module of an agent is replaced over time with functionally equivalent but physically different hardware, what conditions preserve continuity of status?
- Adversarial value injection: if an agent's preference structure is altered during training or deployment by an external optimizer, does this destroy its status, constitute a harm to it, or leave status untouched?
- The scaling problem: if an agent can be copied arbitrarily at near-zero cost, does moral status scale linearly with copies, or must there be a non-additive or threshold principle?
- Whether the theory must keep separate suffering-capacity, preference-autonomy, and social-role-embeddedness, or whether it may legitimately collapse some of these.

Then identify which single requirement does the most damage to the currently popular positions—likely the combination of substrate-independence with the forking condition, but defend your choice. Finally, flag any pair of desiderata you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable, such as strict continuity through arbitrary substrate replacement combined with a non-additive scaling rule for copies.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring named philosophers. A table is fine if it clarifies the negotiability flags.
