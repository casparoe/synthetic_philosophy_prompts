I've been working with the idea that lost-cause myths — the Confederacy's Lost Cause, Masada, Kosovo, even Dunkirk when its spun as noble retreat — all rely on a move I call counterfactual patriotism: they treat defeat as evidence of the cause's purity, splitting the ideal from the violence used to pursue it, so that the worse the defeat, the more righteous the cause looks. I think this makes the myth-form structurally dangerous regardless of content. But I want to stress-test it. What would actually count against this view? Don't just catalogue objections — take a stand: rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones defenders of the view should concede, and tell me wheter I've qualified it into unfalsifiability. Be specific about the cases — does Dunkirk really break the pattern, does Masada? — and commit to whether the structural claim survives.
