I sit on the acquisitions board for the Westbrook Municipal Archive and we need a memo for Thursday. The estate of Alderman Vera Holt—local pol for thirty years, died last spring—offered us her full papers, but only if we sign an agreement that we won't publicly mention missing material or publish a finding aid that notes redactions. Problem is, anyone opening the boxes sees huge gaps: every folder tied to the 1987 dockworkers strike and to the East Mill neighborhood association is either empty or stuffed with blank placeholder pages. Our director wants the prestige and grant funding, but our charter talks about documenting civic life "including its silences." The estate says privacy and family dignity are at stake, and they've hinted they'll destroy the rest of the collection if we push back.

Can you draft the short opinion I'll circulate? Recommend a concrete course—accept with conditions, reject, or take the papers and still build a "shadow index" of exactly what's excised—with a clear timeframe for action. Take the estate's position seriously, don't just cite the charter. And please engage this idea I've been floating: that archives don't just store power's leftovers, they decide which absences count as part of "the past." So address whether building a negative catalog—listing what's gone and who removed it—actually resists the silence or just makes the archive a monument to thier own impotence. No need for bibliographies, just reasoning.
