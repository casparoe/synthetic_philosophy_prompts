I've been convinced that Death in Damascus is basically Newcomb's problem with a scythe: Death predics your flight, so fleeing to Aleppo is like one-boxing—you think you're free but you're just selecting the branch where she waits. Staying in Damascus breaks the correllation. But what would actually count against this framing? I want defeaters sorted from fatal to awkward—like whether it smuggles in an impossible self-reference, confuses evidential vs causal reasoning, or falls apart if you can genuinely randomize your choice with a die roll. Which of these would a committed Newcomb-analogy defender concede, and which would they brush off? And has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability—if Death always retroactively "predicted" whatever happened—so that its immune to any story outcome? Is that insulation a feature or a bug for a parable about rationality?
