I'm putting together a taxonomy for an applied ethics session on speciesism — not just are you for or against but the actual logical branches people don't notice. Build it as a tree where each split is a concrete claim, not a philosopher's name.

First branch: is species membership ever a direct ground for moral status, or only ever a stand-in for capacities like sentience or autonomy?

If direct: is it absolute (always trumps cross-species capacities) or defeasible?

If only indirect: do capacities scale moral status gradually, or is there a binary threshold? This is what I'm calling the gradient consistency problem — if sentience comes in degrees, does status?

Then there's the symmetry branch: can moral obligations run both ways across species in the same currency? Or is it always human-to-animal, never the reverse?

Then the ecological embededness split: does being embedded in a predator-prey or symbiotic system generate role-based permissions that can justify harming individuals?

Each leaf should carry a position name if there is one, or unnamed. Note which combinations are empty — like absolute direct membership plus full symmetry — and whether they're incoherent or just unpopular. Flag any two leaves your tree can't actually separate with these questions.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble, and don't restate the question. Casual tone is fine — a typo or two won't bother me.
