I am drafting a position statement for a metaethics reading group and want a consistency audit before I present. My commitments are these:

(1) I hold a version of moral relativism in which moral sentences are indexical: a claim such as that torture is wrong expresses a proposition that is true or false only relative to a parameter fixed by the speaker's operative moral framework. There are no framework-transcendent moral facts.

(2) Consequently, cross-cultural moral disagreement is usually not genuine disagreement but a kind of semantic divergence, analogous to disputants arguing about whether an object is to the left without sharing a spatial orientation.

(3) Even so, a moral framework is open to immanent critique. If it generates contradictory prescriptions for agents situated in structurally identical moral circumstances, that incoherence is detectable without invoking any external moral standard.

(4) It follows that moral humility is obligatory. An outsider who condemns a practice that is internally coherent within its host framework is merely applying her own indexical parameter; she possesses no rational authority that transcends her framework.

(5) Where epistemic peers from different frameworks disagree persistently, the appropriate response is not to seek a neutral arbiter but to pursue parallel reflective equilibrium. Each peer ought to treat the other's verdicts as data revealing hidden tensions within her own framework, revising her beliefs to restore internal coherence.

(6) Moral progress is therefore always internal to a framework. A framework advances when it resolves its contradictions and becomes more responsive to the needs of those it governs, but there is no meaningful cross-framework scale by which to rank frameworks.

Please analyse whether these six commitments hang together. I want to know: which pairs are in genuine tension, which tensions are only apparent, and what the least costly revision would be in each case? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. Identify any methodological or practical consequence that my stated principles cannot deliver. Do not advise me to abandon the relativist core unless that is genuinely the cheapest repair; I am interested in the minimal adjustments that preserve the package.
