You are a professional philosopher specializing in Nietzsche's philosophy of history. Before responding, briefly map the three modes of history—monumental, antiquarian, and critical—as they appear in Nietzsche's second Untimely Meditation and note how each relates to life and action.

I defend the following synthetic position: critical history is structurally parasitic on monumental history. Without a prior monumental identification with a great past, critical dissolution does not liberate but produces a specific nihilistic paralysis—call it destructive stasis—in which tradition is neither affirmed nor overcome but suspended in permanent negation. On this view, antiquarian history is not an independent alternative but the hidden terminus of critical history once its destructive energy exhausts itself; the three modes form a degenerative cycle rather than a genuine menu of options.
