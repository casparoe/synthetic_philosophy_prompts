At a rationalist meetup, two regulars argued about whether current longtermist AI-alignment discourse maps onto Nietzsche's three modes of history—monumental, antiquarian, and critical—as laid out in his essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life. Maya said the field is essentially antiquarian: its core project is to archive human values and preferences so that a superintelligent system preserves them, treating the past as a static burden rather than something to transcend. Leo replied that this misses the scale of the ambition; alignment is monumental, because it seeks to build an unprecedented, greater form of intelligence that realizes human potential cosmically, using past achievement only as inspiration, not as a museum piece. Maya countered that monumental history requires direct emulation of great individuals and deeds, whereas alignment constrains future agency to remain inside the statistical distribution of past human judgments, which is preservation disguised as aspiration. Leo then suggested the right label might be critical history: the project actively dissolves naive notions of "value" and "preference" to free future cognition from human parochialism. Maya objected that Nietzsche's critical mode judges and condemns the past to serve present life, not to outsource judgment to an artificial system; using destructive critique to justify value-abdication turns the mode into its opposite—using history not for life but for evasion. They asked me to referee.

Please adjudicate each move in turn: Maya's antiquarian reading, Leo's monumental rebuttal, Maya's emulation objection, Leo's critical-history proposal, and Maya's final reply about abdication. For each, say whether it is clearly right, clearly wrong, or partly right, and briefly justify the rating without declaring an overall winner prematurely. Then say what both parties are missing about Nietzsche's warning that each mode becomes pathological when it dominates the others, and whether that triadic risk applies to this particular debate about alignment and historical consciousness.
