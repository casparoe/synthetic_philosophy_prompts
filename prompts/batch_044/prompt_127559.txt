So I've been trying to understand this weird split in how my college handles student mental health. There's the disability office that gives accomodations for anxiety like it's a medical condition with biological roots, the counseling center running CBT groups where anxiety is treated as wrong judgments you can reason away, and then there's the new 'pedagogical care' policy that requires professors to put content warnings on syllabi not just for trauma but for any material that might cause 'significant emotional discomfort'—they literally compare it to putting a wet-floor sign in a hallway. All three deal with student emotions but seem to assume totally differnt things about what an emotion is. What philosophical commitments does each practice presuppose—are they treating emotion as bodily feeling, cognitive judgment, or perceptual danger? Can these three even live under one theory, or is the university quietly running incompatible philosophies? And if we forced everything onto one view, say that emotions are just judgments, what exactly would break in how each office operates?
