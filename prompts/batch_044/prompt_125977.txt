My study group is fighting about whether "collapse" is even a real category or just a dramatic word for "they changed a lot." Here's the argument that convinced half of them, and I can't find the exit. (1) If collapse is a real phenomenon, it needs a specific failure mechanism, not just big transformation. (2) The Bronze Age collapse, the Classic Maya depopulation around 900 CE, and Easter Island's deforestation all share one mechanism: a society's resource-use trajectory outruns its institutional adaptivity, forcing rapid simplification. (3) But every society eventually faces a resource trajectory that outruns its current institutions; thats just entropy plus complexity. (4) So either every society is technically on a collapse trajectory, or "collapse" just means "the moment when adaptation looks too slow to observers." (5) If it's the latter, then labeling the Bronze Age a collapse but not the Industrial Revolution is an arbitrary time-slice, not an explanatory category. (6) So "collapse" either applies to all societies eventually or explains nothing specific about any of them. Where does this slip? I'd like to keep (2) and reject (6), but I need to know what I'm paying.
