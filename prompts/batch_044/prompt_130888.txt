(1) literal self-deception—simultaneously believing p and ~p with full doxastic assent—is conceptually impossible, not just empirically rare. belief isn't voluntary in the way that requires.
(2) but "strategic ignorance" is real and common: people actively structure attention, avoid evidence, and curate environments so they never form beliefs they'd rather not have. it's not compartmentalized belief, it's informational self-censorship.
(3) I think strategic ignorance is morally worse than lying to another person, because lying at least respects the other as an epistemic agent, whereas here you're treating yourself as an object to be managed.
(4) but I also think there's no "real self" behind the curtain—autonomy is basically a useful fiction, and the mind is just competing subsystems with no central homunculus.
(5) I reject divided-mind models (Freudian parts, system-1/system-2 as little agents) because they reify subsystems into believers.
(6) evolutionary psych explains strategic ignorance just fine—mechanisms protecting self-image—but these mechanisms don't have beliefs or intentions themselves.
(7) actually, if strategic ignorance is widespread and adaptive, then "knowing thyself" might be closer to an evolutionary malfunction than a virtue.
