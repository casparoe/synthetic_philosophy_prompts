Third, she said from this it follows that involuntary commitment is not just unethical but conceptually incoherent — you cant treat someone for a disease they dont literally have — and she added that Szasz eventually softened this and said depression is real but only when its caused by a physical thing like thyroid issues, otherwise its just problems in living.

How fair is this summary? I want to know layer by layer whats accurate, whats a slight distortion, and what a defender of Szasz would object to. And does his rejection of involuntary commitment depend on that conceptual incoherence point, or is that mixing up different parts of his argument? Also, is the bit about depression and thyroid issues a correct representation of how he distinguished physical conditions from problems in living, or is she importing something he never actually said?
