I was arguing with my roommate — she’s in neurscience and I’m in phil mind — about whether deciding to grab coffee actually makes my arm move or if I’m just a by stander watching neurons do the work. That led me back to Kim’s causal exclusion argument from my seminar: if every physical event has a complete physical cause, mental events are either redundant or they overdetermine everything. I think the standard debate is stuck because both sides treat mental and physical causation as competing on the exact same flat playing field. For my one-year dissertation fellowship I need a two-page proposal asking whether exclusion still bites if we treat mental properties as organizational patterns — not extra pushes but structures that constrain which physical paths actually occur — and whether mechanist or interventionist accounts of causation can make room for that kind of structural efficacy without collapsing into epiphenominalism.

Write the proposal with the question stated precisely: does the exclusion argument depend on a monistic, single-track ontology of causal powers, and can a hierarchical or pattern-based view avoid both redundancy and overdetermination while keeping physicalism intact?
