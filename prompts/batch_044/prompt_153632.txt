I serve as the junior student delegate on the Riverside Unified School District's Civic Engagement Advisory Panel, a body that advises the board of education on structural governance changes. At our session last Thursday we debated "Charter Amendment 9-C," a pilot proposal that would lower the voting age to sixteen for school-budget referenda—provided the minor has completed a district-certified civic literacy module—while simultaneously extending ballot access to non-citizen residents who have paid local property taxes continuously for at least five years and maintain no federal tax delinquency. Some panel members, emphasizing democratic sovereignty, contend that voting is an exclusive prerogative of citizens regardless of tax status or educational attainment; others maintain that both affected teenagers and long-term contributing residents are governed by district fiscal policy without representation, which undermines the legitimacy of consent-based authority.

Before this Thursday's vote I must circulate a formal recommendation memo addressed to the panel chair and the board, and I need it to recommend a specific action—full endorsement, conditional approval with a phased rollout timeline, or rejection—supported by reasoning that takes the citizenship-sovereignty objection seriously rather than treating it as mere prejudice. The memo should also acknowledge the strongest philosophical argument on the side opposite to your recommendation, should avoid all bibliographic references and citation requests since our evaluation must stand on internal reasoning alone, and should adopt an institutional voice appropriate for a document that could be appended to official minutes. Please ensure the recommendation is concrete enough that the board knows exactly what conditions, if any, must be met before implementation, and frame the issue so that it addresses not only the voting-age and non-citizen provisions but also whether compulsory participation requirements should apply uniformly to both new voter categories or only to traditional citizens.
