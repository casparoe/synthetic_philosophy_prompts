I have been working through some recent philosophy of psychiatry and keep returning to Ian Hacking's concept of looping effects: the idea that classifying people can alter the people classified, which in turn forces the classification itself to change. The case that interests me most is the DSM-5's decision to fold Asperger's disorder into autism spectrum disorder, and the way neurodiversity advocacy since then has not only resisted pathologization but actively reshaped clinical descriptions of autistic cognition and sociality. This looks like a looping effect with an unusually reflexive feedback loop—the classified group has acquired enough institutional and epistemic authority to rewrite the criteria from within.

With that in mind, I would like to imagine a counterfactual conversation. Suppose Michel Foucault—who died in 1984, before Hacking fully developed the looping-effects framework and well before the DSM-5 revision or the contemporary neurodiversity movement—were presented with this precise scenario. How might he analyze the relationship between psychiatric classification, looping dynamics, and a counter-discourse that emerges when the classified population gains authoritative voice? Would he interpret the neurodiversity movement as an escape from psychiatric power, as a novel form of biopolitical self-governance, or as a development that fundamentally complicates his earlier analyses of how reason constructs madness?

Please respond in American English. I am not looking for a bibliography, reading list, or specific citations; I want conceptual reasoning that draws on Foucault's known concerns with power, knowledge, and the historical production of subjectivity, applied thoughtfully to this modern case.
