Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains that alternate—on Nietzsche's doctrine of eternal recurrence. I am a software engineer with no formal philosophical training, and I keep returning to the image of a deterministic loop that exhausts its state space and returns precisely to its initial conditions, every intermediate state identical, as a way to approach the cosmological claim. Please make the form enact the idea: the refrains should shift meaning across the stanzas, first describing that cyclical loop, then becoming the ethical challenge of whether one could affirm such repetition, and finally holding both horror and affirmation in suspension without resolving them. The poem should be philosophically precise: treat recurrence as the identical repetition of every detail of one's life, not as déjà vu or reincarnation, and treat the heaviest weight as an existential test rather than a consolation, engaging with amor fati as a possible but not necessary outcome. Keep the tension open in the final quatrain so that despair and willing acceptance remain simultaneously present.
