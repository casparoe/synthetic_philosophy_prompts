Compare the global Quine-Duhem thesis—that for any theory there exists an empirically equivalent rival constructible by adjusting auxiliary hypotheses—with what might be called “domain-locked underdetermination,” in which the pool of permissible auxiliary adjustments is restricted by prior theoretical commitments that are treated as non-negotiable within the domain in question. In particular, discuss whether domain-locked underdetermination avoids Quine’s objection that global underdetermination is trivial because auxiliary hypotheses are always available; whether it retains any anti-realist force or collapses into a pragmatic selection problem; and how each variant handles observationally equivalent cosmological models that differ solely in the ontological interpretation of a dark-energy parameter without altering the underlying dynamics. Be precise about where the two positions diverge on the scope of theory revision and on whether empirical equivalence alone can motivate a skeptical conclusion.
