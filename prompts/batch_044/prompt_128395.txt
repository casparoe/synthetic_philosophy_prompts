There's this standard protocol in consciousness labs where subjects hit a button at the exact instinct they notice a flash, and the timestamp gets treated like the moment the show started in their head. Researchers map neural correlates to that point, and in some clinical settings families are asked to report when a coma patient first seemed present, using the same idea of one decisive entry into awareness. What view of consciousness does that practice presuppose? Is it essentially a Cartesian theater, with one central audience waiting for the performance to begin? Or could a multiple drafts model accommodate a specific time of awareness without a single stage? And if the drafts view is right, is the protocol still measuring something real, or is it just tracking when one particular draft gets edited for public report? I'm also curious whether the practice is philosophically underdetermined — maybe both theories allow it, but they make it mean totally different things.
