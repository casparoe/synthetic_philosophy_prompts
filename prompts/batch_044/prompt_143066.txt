ok so i'm trying to straighten out my views before a reading group on punishment and free will. here's roughly where i sit:

(1) i buy Dennett's line that free will is real but it's just an evolved social competence, not some metaphysical break in causation — so determinism doesn't threaten moral responsability.

(2) but i'm also convinced by Caruso that "just deserts" is basically a retribtive fiction and punishment should be reconceived like public health: quarantine and treatment, never suffering for its own sake.

(3) still, i think reactive attitudes like resentment and blame are genuinely fitting responses when someone wrongs you, not just useful social tools.

(4) i think that even if determinism turned out false and we had real indeterminsm, libertarian free will still wouldn't license retributive punishment — suffering for its own sake is never justified.

(5) and i think there's an important moral distinction between a culpable wrongdoer and a dangerous but non-culpable person (say, severe mental illness), and that society should treat them differently — maybe stricter monitoring for the culpable — though never with pure suffering.

do these hang together? in particular: does (2) undermine (3), since if desert is out then blame seems to presuppose exactly the retributive framework caruso rejects? does (5) conflict with (2)'s public-health logic, where only dangerousness should determine the response? and is there tension between (1) and (4) — if free will is just social technology, does it even make sense to ask what "would" be justified under libertarian free will?

which pairs are actually in conflict, which only look like it, and what's the least costly revision? please rank the tensions by severity and say for each what'd have to give.
