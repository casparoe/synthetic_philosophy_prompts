As a retired experimental physicist now working through philosophy of science, I want a diagnostic instrument—not a list of authors or reading—that sorts actual stances on scientific realism given the pessimistic meta-induction. Please build a taxonomy in tree form, with each split labeled by a concrete claim about specific historical theories (Newtonian gravitation, phlogiston chemistry, caloric heat, the luminiferous ether, or the Bohr atomic model) and about what, if anything, survives in the successor theory.

The root question should be: does the empirical and predictive success of a radically false theory provide any reason to retain belief in its unobservable ontology? From there, branch on whether retention applies only to entities also present in the successor theory, only to formal or mathematical structures regardless of ontology, or to nothing at all. Add a further split on whether the historical pattern supports cumulative approximation toward one true ontology, or whether the very notion of approximate truth collapses without a stable ontological frame across revolutions.

At each leaf, provide the standard position label if one exists—structural realism, entity realism, constructive empiricism, pessimistic anti-realism, or similar—or mark it "unnamed"; include one sentence on its most awkward commitment, such as having to explain why current quantum field theory should not be subject to the same inductive skepticism. Identify any pair of leaves your tree genuinely cannot separate, and flag any unoccupied combination of answers: is the gap incoherent, merely unattractive, or unexplored? The aim is to place someone by what they say about these cases, not by which philosopher they have read.
