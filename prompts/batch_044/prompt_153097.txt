(1) aristotle says money was invented just to make exchange easier, and exchange itself is only for filling natural needs.
(2) he also says money is barren — it shouldn't breed more money like interest does, because metal doesn't reproduce.
(3) but if money is just a stand-in for need, then holding onto money is like holding onto need itself without satisfying it. the value should be spent into goods.
(4) so any system where people save up money for future needs, or where prices float based on supply instead of actual need, is treating money like it's productive rather than just a measuring stick.

(7) yet aristotle's household management (oikonomia) literally depends on households making surplus to exchange with others.
(8) so if we take his money-is-barren idea seriously, it kills the surplus production his own economics needs.
(9) therefore either his distinction between natural exchange and unnatural chrematistike collapses, or need-currency is wrong.
