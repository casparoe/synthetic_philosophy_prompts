Write a dialogue between two equally sharp interlocutors — call them M and N — debating whether symbols push the substrate around, using the hypothetical careenium and its simmballs as their shared test case. In the careenium, simmballs are symbol-bearing spheres arranged in patterns that appear to steer mechanical pistons; M, the substrate-causalist, argues that the pistons move solely through thermodynamic and mechanical linkages, and that the simmballs are merely readable traces of these substrate-level processes, lacking any downward causal efficacy. N, the symbolic-causalist, counters that in this specific engine replacing a simmball with a physically identical placeholder — same mass, temperature, and position, but bearing a different symbol — alters the piston trajectory, which suggests the symbol-pattern functionally constrains the substrate rather than merely correlating with it. They must respond directly to each other’s actual moves: if M appeals to supervenience, N should press whether supervenience without downward causation can explain the observed functional specificity; if N invokes multiple realisability, M should ask whether that proves causal autonomy or only descriptive redundancy. The dialogue should remain technically precise yet entirely self-contained: do not request citations, bibliographies, or references to specific published works, and assume neither speaker has access to external literature. Write in polished British English, keep it concise, and adopt the tone of a longtime rationalist-forum reader who is frustrated with both naive eliminativism about symbols and unexamined dualism about them.
