I'm drafting a framework for moral compromise across incommensurable value systems and want a consistency audit before I present it to a working group. Here are my commitments:

(1) Compromise between genuinely incommensurable value systems can be rationally justified as a form of axiological trade—each party secures more of what it values—without presupposing a universal metric or objective exchange rate between values.

(2) The legitimacy of such compromise depends on procedural conditions of reciprocal recognition and non-domination, not on convergence toward an ideal equilibrium that an impartial observer could identify.

(3) There are constitutive commitments—those without which a community's practical identity collapses—that are categorically non-tradeable, even for substantial axiological gains; this boundary is structural, not quantitative.

(4) I deny that tradition, naturalness, or cultural continuity carry independent moral weight; they are instrumentally valuable only insofar as they stabilize the value system in question.

(5) Applying this: mutual institutional accommodation between secular liberal universities and religious traditionalist colleges—shared accreditation with modified curricula—is permissible and rational, because both gain resilience and neither is forced to abandon commitments constitutive of its ethos.

(6) However, if one party's constitutive commitments include gender-based hierarchy or exclusion, then procedural accommodation becomes categorically impermissible, regardless of the institutional gains available.

Are these jointly tenable? Rank the tensions by severity. In particular: does (4) undermine the protection of constitutive commitments in (3) and (5)? Does (6) smuggle in an objective moral veto incompatible with the proceduralism of (2) and the anti-objectivism of (1)? Is the tension between (3) and (6) merely applicational—who decides what counts as constitutive—or does it reveal that (6) relies on a moral constraint the rest of the framework rejects? And does the apparent conflict between (1) and (2) dissolve once we distinguish local Pareto improvement from universal commensurability? For each genuine tension, identify the cheapest revision and its cost; for the apparent one, explain why it dissolves. Deliver this as an ordered audit, not a lecture.
