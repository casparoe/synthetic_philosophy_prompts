I was chatting with my coworkers Maya and Leo over lunch about whether there's a "Cartesian theater" in the brain or if it's all just Dennett's multiple drafts model. I don't have formal philosophy training — I just write backend code — so I might be mangling the terms. Maya argued there definately has to be a central screen where experience binds together, like a main render thread in a game engine. Without that, she said, you'd just have a bunch of seperate neural processes with no actual observer, which sounds like dissociation. Leo said that's exactly the myth: consciousness isn't a movie playing to a viewer, it's more like distributed microservices editing drafts in parallel, with no master database. He thinks the feeling of unity is just the latest merged pull request, not proof of a theater.

Maya came back saying even microservices need an API gateway or load balancer to aggregate responses for the client. If there's no central binding mechanism, why don't we constantly feel fragmented? She also said VR headsets prove the point: if you render to seperate displays without a unified frame buffer, people get nausea, so the brain must have one render target. Leo countered that there's no gateway — just competing drafts and the one that wins gets broadcast. Then he added this idea he picked up somewhere: maybe experience works like lazy evaluation in functional programming, where the brain doesn't continuously render a full scene but only computes specific parts when queried, caching the rest. So there's no movie running at all, just on-demand functions that feel continuous. Maya said that still needs an evaluator — something has to call the function. If there's no Cartesian observer, what's invoking it? She guessed maybe drafts call each other recursively. Leo said that just creates infinite regress with no base case, and admitted he can't fully explain why it feels like there's a "me" watching, but insisted that's an illusion from the narrative center, not evidence of an actual theater.

They asked me to referee and I honestly don't know who's right about what. Can you adjudicate point by point? Specifically: is Maya right that binding requires a central render target, or is Leo right that distributed drafts can do the job? Does the lazy evaluation analogy actually help Leo, or does Maya's "who calls the function" objection kill it? Is recursive draft-evaluation a real solution or just regress?
