Please explain the conceptual tension between common knowledge of rationality and persistent rational disagreement in epistemic game theory when agents hold imprecise probabilities—represented as convex sets of credences rather than point-valued beliefs. In particular, address whether the standard no-disagreement result requires precise common priors, or whether it can be reconstructed for agents whose belief states are credal sets that overlap only partially. Discuss how an agent should update her own imprecise credences upon learning that an epistemic peer, whom she regards as fully rational, assigns a different convex set of probabilities to the same proposition, and whether common knowledge of rationality in this setting forces convergence on a shared set, permits stable divergence, or generates a novel form of epistemic paralysis. Your explanation should be intuitive but technically precise, and it should avoid treating imprecision as mere ignorance about a single true precise belief.
