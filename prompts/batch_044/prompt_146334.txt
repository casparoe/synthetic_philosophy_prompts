I have been following a graduate seminar on moral psychology and the philosophy of action, where the instructor, Professor M. K. Liu, has been offering informal verdicts about whether particular agents possess the variety of free will that actually grounds moral responsibility and rational self-governance, as opposed to some metaphysically extravagant contra-causal liberty. Her judgments to date are these: she holds that a deterministically caused human who deliberates with care and endorses her choice plainly possesses it; that an agent with a dormant controller who never actually interferes also possesses it; that a person acting under post-hypnotic suggestion with no awareness of the source entirely lacks it; that a severe addict whose practical reasoning is genuinely unresponsive to her own strongest desire to quit, despite experiencing intense inner conflict, also lacks it; and that a well-structured collective body such as a jury, despite engaging in distributed deliberation, does not possess it because there is no unified subject to whom accountability can attach.

Now consider a more intricate case. Imagine an adult raised inside a closed, ideologically homogeneous community, who has cultivated a highly refined internal deliberative capacity. She fully endorses every first-order value she holds, and her decision-making mechanism is exquisitely responsive to reasons—provided they remain within her inherited framework. Unbeknownst to her, her education included a subliminal affective conditioning protocol that silently blocks any counter-argument that threatens her core commitments; she experiences this blockage not as an obstacle but as her own rational confidence. When she later takes credit for a subordinate's idea, she does so after a brief impulse that she consciously endorsed, yet that endorsement was subtly amplified by the conditioning protocol. Would Professor Liu judge that she possesses the relevant variety of free will in performing this act?

I am less interested in a bare yes or no than in mapping the competing principles—perhaps a reasons-responsiveness condition, a sourcehood or non-manipulation condition, and a hierarchical endorsement condition—that all accommodate her first five verdicts, stating precisely what each predicts about this indoctrinated agent, offering your best-guess reconstruction of her actual verdict with the supporting reasoning, and identifying the one further hypothetical case that would most cleanly adjudicate between the rules.
