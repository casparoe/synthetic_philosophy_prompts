I sit on the Metropolitan Redevelopment and Cultural Memory Commission, and we must vote next Thursday on the Old Meridian Freight Yard. The structure is unsound and maintenance costs are accelerating; the choice is either to authorize demolition and replacement with a standard logistics hub, with construction beginning within eighteen months, or to mandate adaptive preservation with a phased thirty-six-month restoration plan.

A faction on the commission treats the yard as a genuine branching node: without its particular 1920s rail geometry and the labor agreements it anchored, they argue, our city’s logistics network and the adjacent neighborhood identity would not exist in their present form, so demolition severs an irreplaceable historical path. The opposing faction contends this is largely narrative lock-in—the yard is a surface manifestation of deeper structural forces, including river access, flat terrain, and available labor, that would have converged on functionally identical outcomes regardless of this specific site, which means preservation mainly aestheticizes contingency.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action with its associated time frame, treat the convergence argument seriously rather than dismissing it, and clarify whether “path dependence” here is doing causal work (the physical infrastructure genuinely constrains future options) or hermeneutic work (the story we tell about the yard makes present arrangements feel inevitable). Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; rely on the conceptual distinction itself, and give an honest account of the strongest consideration on the side you do not choose.
