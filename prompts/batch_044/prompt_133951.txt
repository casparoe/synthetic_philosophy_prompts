I'm taking an intro grad course on epistemic justification and we've been talking about the bootstrapping problem—like the Vogel/Cohen-style objection where you use your eyes to check your eyes are reliable and suddenly you've bootstrapped knowledge that your vision works. I was discussing it with a friend whose in epistemology and she said foundationalists actually have it worse, because at least reliabilists can appeal to some external track-record, whereas a foundationalist just asserts basic beliefs are self-justifying with no loop at all. That made me wonder: if the whole issue is circular justification, then isn't foundationalism just bootstrapping with extra steps? You still depend on your cognative faculties to deliver those basic beliefs in the first place, you just refuse to ask whether they're reliable. So why do people treat easy knowledge as a special problem for reliabilism rather than a general problem for anyone who thinks perception gives us knowledge?
