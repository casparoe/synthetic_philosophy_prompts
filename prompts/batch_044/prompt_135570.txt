Write a dialogue between two metaphysicians—Dr. Aris Thorne, defending an interstitial realist view of universals, and Dr. Lina Voss, defending a fusion-rule nominalism—discussing the problem of universals at a seminar table. Thorne holds that universals are not self-standing qualitative entities but relational structures that emerge only in the interdependencies among instantiated tropes; he calls these structures "interstitial universals." Voss denies that any shared entity exists and instead proposes that resemblance among tropes is not primitive but is generated by a "fusion rule" that groups tropes into ad hoc similarity classes without ontological commitment to shared properties. The dialogue must contain genuine back-and-forth: each speaker must respond directly to the specific argument just made, not pivot to a generic talking point. Both should be equally competent; neither should be a strawman. Have them address three issues in turn: (1) whether Thorne's interstitial structures genuinely explain exact resemblance or merely redescribe Voss's fusion rules; (2) whether Voss's fusion rule avoids circularity when it appeals to qualitative similarity to determine group membership; and (3) whether either account can accommodate the modal intuition that an uninstantiated universal—say, a precise shade of crimson—could have existed even if no trope ever instantiated it. Include at least one concession by each speaker that forces them to refine their position. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or references to specific texts; rely only on the concepts defined above and on standard metaphysical vocabulary.
