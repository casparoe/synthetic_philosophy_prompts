I'm writing a piece on how people actually argue about giving. At a panel last week two researchers got into it over "earning to give," but with a twist: they were fighting about a grantmaker who only funds projects with pre-registered outcome metrics. (1) She said thats just rational accountability — if you can't measure it you can't improve it, and without hard data you're just funding vibes. (2) He said thats the measurability bias people talk about, and it systematically kills funding for institutional maintenence like legal reform or goverance training where payoffs are diffuse and decades away. (3) She shot back that earning to give already solves this — take the high-paying job, donate surplus, let specialized orgs handle long-term stuff; the problem isn't metrics, its that people dont actually earn enough to cover both direct aid and structural work. (4) He said that concedes her point on metrics but misses the institutional critique: if high-earners all work inside the same tech-finace ecosystem that profits from weak regulation, then earning to give is basically laundering structural harm through individual donations. Can you go through their exchange move by move? Rate each of the four steps — clearly wrong to clearly right — and say what hidden assumption each person is making. Also, is there a fifth move they're both missing about how measurability bias interacts with labor-market capture?
