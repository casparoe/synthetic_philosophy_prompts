(1) Deflationism says 'true' is purely disquotational: calling a belief true just endorses its content, with no extra property being attributed.
(2) So there is no metaphysical property of truth for beliefs to track or aim at.
(3) That means norms like 'pursue true beliefs' or 'use reliable methods' must also be read disquotationally — they scatter into particular endorsements rather than forming a unified goal.
(4) Without a unified property, there's no such thing as epistemic friciton — the resistance reality puts up against false belief — since resistance needs a substantive relation between mind and property.
(5) So deflationism can't explain why false beliefs systematically cause practical behaviour failures; it can only list correlations case by case.
(6) But epistemology clearly treats that resistance as real and central.
(7) Therefore deflationism destroys the very subject matter of epistemology.

Which step should I drop? I'd really like to hang on to (1) and (6) if I can. Please explain why the faulty step seemed okay, and what minimalists, prosententialists and performativists each have to pay to reject it.
