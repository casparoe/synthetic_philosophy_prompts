Write a short story centered on Hilbert's hotel, narrated from the viewpoint of a night archivist who adheres to a strict finitist philosophy: she allows that new guests can always be accommodated, yet denies that an actually infinite collection of rooms or occupants exists at any moment. During a quiet shift she applies Cantor's diagonal argument—not to the real numbers, but to the hotel's sequential registration ledger—in order to construct a guest record that, according to her own ontological commitments, should not map onto any room she recognizes as genuinely existing. The story should trace her attempt to reconcile this result with her finitism, her conversation with the hotel's manager, who regards actual infinity as unproblematic, and the ambiguity over whether the anomalous record is a logical paradox, an administrative mistake, or a fissure in her metaphysical framework. Avoid giving a definitive resolution to the underlying ontological dispute; maintain the tension between potential and actual infinity throughout.
