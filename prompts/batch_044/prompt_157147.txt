There's this thing museums do where they restore an ancient statue by filling missing bits with modern plaster, but they paint it grey so you can tell its not original. Meanwhile if a visitor chips off a finger they treat it like he damaged the real artwork, not just made it need more plaster. What theory of material identity does that practice presuppose? Do they think the statue is the shape, the original marble, or some historical trajectory? And if we took the statue-and-clay distinction seriously — that replacing parts creates a new object — wouldnt conservation law have to treat every restoration as manufacturing a fake? Reconstruct the implicit view, check whether its actually consistent with both the restoration and the vandalism rules, and say what'd have to change in museum policy if we replaced it with a straight-up mereological view where parts are essential.
