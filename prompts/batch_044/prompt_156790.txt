Draft the joint adversarial-collaboration statement that a Rational-Process Engagement (RPE) theorist and an Autonomy-Respecting Intent (ARI) theorist could both sign, on the boundary between manipulation and legitimate persuasion. The RPE camp holds that manipulation essentially bypasses or subverts an agent’s capacities for critical, reflective evaluation, while legitimate persuasion necessarily operates through those capacities. The ARI camp holds that manipulation essentially involves a covert substitution of the influencer’s will or value-ordering for the agent’s own autonomous preferences, while legitimate persuasion respects the sovereignty of the agent’s values, regardless of whether rational capacities are engaged.

The document should contain four parts.

1. A numbered section of agreed claims, including: that not all influence is manipulation and that some influence is legitimate persuasion; that explicit deception is neither necessary nor sufficient for manipulation; that autonomy is the central value at stake, with each side stating concisely what they take autonomy to require without resolving the dispute; that framing effects, emotional appeals, and algorithmic recommendation present genuine borderline cases; that the Recursive-Transparency Scenario—where an influence mechanism fully discloses every parameter of its reasoning and value-weighting to the agent, yet the disclosure is structured so that the agent internalizes the mechanism’s ordering as their own—is conceptually coherent and bears directly on the disagreement; and that both sides accept the scenario as a valid test case.

2. A disagreements table. For each disagreement, provide a formulation that the RPE side accepts for its own view and a formulation that the ARI side accepts for its own view. Cover at least: whether bypassing rational evaluation is necessary for manipulation; whether full transparency about an influence mechanism automatically renders it legitimate persuasion; and whether an influence can be manipulative even when the agent’s rational capacities are fully engaged but their value-sovereignty is undermined.

3. A cruxes section identifying the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I take the two central cruxes to be: (a) whether second-order transparency—knowing not merely that one is being influenced, but knowing exactly how one’s own values are being mapped onto options—is sufficient to eliminate manipulation; and (b) whether an influence that operates entirely through an agent’s endorsed reasoning structures can nonetheless be manipulative if those structures were previously installed or shaped by the influencer. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it: use the Recursive-Transparency Scenario for (a), and for (b) use the Clinician-Framing Case, in which a clinician explains the statistical rationale for every treatment framing but selects frames precisely to steer the patient toward the clinician’s preferred outcome while leaving the patient’s reasoning capacities intact. For each crux, record in advance what each side predicts: specifically, whether resolving the crux in their favor would require revising their core distinction, and what conceptual or empirical finding would cause them to do so.

4. A brief concluding section in which both sides state whether any portion of their dispute is verbal—whether “manipulation” and “persuasion” are being assigned different extensions but compatible intensions—identifying exactly which part, if any, and explaining why.

Write in a formal, polished register appropriate to a long-time reader of rationalist forums. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke highly niche named theories that a small language model could not reliably have memorized; you may invent conceptual distinctions like the Recursive-Transparency Scenario and second-order transparency to sharpen the disagreement, but ground them fully in the prompt itself. Separate disagreements that are genuinely substantive from those that are merely terminological.
