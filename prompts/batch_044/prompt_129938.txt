I'm in a graduate seminar on metaphysics and philosophy of language, and we've been working through the literature generated by Kripke's lectures on naming and necessity together with more recent discussions of a posteriori essentialism. I've arrived at a package of views that feels coherent to me, but before I present it in discussion section I want a consistency audit, because I suspect there may be hidden tensions I'm not seeing.

Specifically, I hold that: (1) proper names are rigid designators in the strongest sense, tracking the same individual across all worlds in which that individual exists; (2) natural kind terms such as "water" or "tiger" are also rigid, designating the same essential kind everywhere; (3) the discovery that water is H2O reveals a necessary truth that was not and could not be known a priori; (4) consequently, conceivability is an unreliable guide to metaphysical possibility, at least for empirical identities of this kind; (5) however, I also want to maintain that certain theoretical terms—say, "entropy," "gene," or "black hole"—function as what I call "functionally rigid" designators, picking out whatever plays a specific causal or structural role in our world, but with a derivative status: if nothing plays that role in a given world, the term fails to designate rather than designating something different; and (6) this distinction between primitive rigidity for individuals and natural kinds, versus derivative functional rigidity for theoretical kinds, allows me to reject both pure descriptivism and pure causal theories of reference without committing to full-blown essentialism about every scientific kind.

My concern is whether (5) and (6) can genuinely be separated from (1)-(4). If natural-kind rigidity is primitive and essentialist, doesn't deriving rigidity for theoretical terms from causal-functional roles collapse back into a form of descriptivism I'm trying to avoid? And if I allow that functional rigidity can "fail to apply" in worlds with no occupant, am I implicitly treating the corresponding identity statements as contingent rather than necessary, which would undermine my essentialism in (2)? I also worry that my rejection of conceivability in (4) is too broad: if I explain away the conceivability of water lacking H2O by saying we're only conceiving of something with superficial water-properties, isn't that move structurally identical to the descriptivist strategy I reject for proper names?

Please examine these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled, and what the least costly revision would be in each case. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging. I'm particularly unsure about the interaction between my essentialism about natural kinds and my derivative account of theoretical terms—does maintaining one force me to abandon the other, or can they be held together with a more precise distinction than the one I've offered?
