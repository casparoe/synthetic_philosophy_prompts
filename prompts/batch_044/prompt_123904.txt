From this, the host concluded that Schlick therefore held that life has no meaning whatsoever, and that anyone who feels life is meaningful is merely experiencing a subjective emotional reaction with no cognitive content, analogous to saying that chocolate tastes good. However, the host added that Schlick offered a kind of therapeutic replacement: rather than seeking purpose or achievement, we should treat life as a form of play, engaging in art, sport, and creative activity purely for their own sake, because these activities do not aim at any external goal and thus escape the demand for empirical verification. The podcast then concluded that Schlick's position is ultimately self-defeating, since if all value statements are just emotional reactions lacking rational authority, then his own recommendation to live playfully is itself nothing more than an emotional reaction and therefore carries no rational weight. Could you audit the summary point by point?
