Analyze the 2008 global financial crisis — specifically the securitization of subprime mortgages, the retention of executive bonuses, and the near-total absence of criminal prosecutions of senior U.S. banking figures in its aftermath — through the lens of karma reconceived not as metaphysical reincarnation mechanics but as a theory of moral desert. On this view, morally significant actions carry an intrinsic normative weight that ought eventually to be matched by appropriate outcomes, yet the matching mechanism may operate in either of two ways: individual and diachronic (personal karmic tracking, in which the specific agent receives the deserved result) or structural and distributed (institutional karma, in which collective actions generate system-level feedback that need not return to the original agents). First, map the episode with discipline: identify the key actors and actions (risk misrepresentation by originators and rating agencies, lobbying for regulatory relaxation, the design and sale of toxic instruments), the outcomes that actually followed (personal wealth preservation, no prison sentences, market collapse, taxpayer-funded stabilization), and what each tracking model would have predicted. Second, say what karma explains and what it misses: can the systemic collapse and subsequent economic pain be read as institutional karmic feedback, or does the retention of bonuses by specific individuals break the desert link? Does the socialization of losses through public bailout sever action from deserved outcome in a way the theory cannot accommodate? Third, report what the episode shows about the theory itself: whether karma as moral desert requires a supernatural enforcement mechanism to avoid collapsing into a normative wish, whether structural karma is a legitimate extension of the concept or an ad hoc rescue invented to save it from falsification, and whether the case demonstrates that any desert-based account of justice must be embedded in legal and economic institutions capable of translating moral weight into actual consequence. Treat both tracking models fairly, and be explicit about where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
