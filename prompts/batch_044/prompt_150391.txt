I'm writing a feature on the philosophy of negative emotions in art, and I recently spoke with a cultural critic who offered me a tidy narrative about the so-called paradox of tragedy. She summarized the debate this way: the paradox is that rational people seek out films and plays that make them grieve or tremble, even though they ordinarily avoid such feelings. Aristotle resolved this with catharsis — tragedy acts like an emotional purge, flushing sorrow and fear from the psyche and leaving viewers lighter and cleansed. Colin Radford later demolished that account by pointing out that audiences often leave tragedies feeling drained rather than relieved, which proves the negative emotions are genuinely experienced, not expelled. The real solution, she argued, is that tragedy provides a safe sandbox for emotional control: the sadness is authentic, but it is continuously buffered by an implicit awareness that we are only playing at suffering, and that meta-cognitive frame transforms the pain into a kind of mastery reward. Consequently, she concluded, there is no genuine paradox — we pursue sad art solely to confirm our own resilience, and the pleasure we report is nothing more than the satisfaction of having survived a simulated threat.

Alongside this, I have been reading some newer interdisciplinary work that describes tragic enjoyment through a predictive-processing lens: the brain anticipates aversive affect, but when the fiction frame contains it, the resulting mismatch produces a reinforcing signal rather than distress. I want to make sure my article does not mislead readers. Could you audit her summary point by point — telling me what a philosopher of art would accept, what is a common oversimplification, and what is a straight misrepresentation of the thinkers involved? In particular, does her conclusion that we seek tragedy only for self-testing follow from the accurate parts? And does the predictive-processing framing I mentioned actually support her mastery-based conclusion, or does it point in a different direction?
