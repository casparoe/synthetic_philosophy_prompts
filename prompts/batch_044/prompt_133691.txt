Been re-reading Keynes's essay Economic Possibilities for Our Grandchilren—he predicts we'll eventually work like 15 hours a week and spend the rest on the good life, and he splits needs into absolute ones and relative ones that are insatiable. I was talking with a friend who said relative needs are basically positional goods, which means they can't be satisfied for everyone at once. If that's right, then Keynes's vision of a universal leisure society collapses, doesn't it? Because people would just move status competition into culture and free time instead of work. So was Keynes secretly assuming we'd all collectively agree to stop caring about being better than each other? Or did he think the good life was only available to a small elite who could opt out? And how does that square with his idea that treating money as an end is a kind of mental illness—does he see status-seeking as the same pathology, or a different one?
