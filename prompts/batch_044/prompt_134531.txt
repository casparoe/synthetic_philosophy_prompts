I'm facilitating a philosophical reading group that has spent the last semester debating whether well-being consists solely in the absence of craving and suffering—what we've been calling tranquilism—or whether positive states such as understanding, creative achievement, or loving attachment carry intrinsic value that is not reducible to suffering-aversion. After months of circular exchange, I want us to produce the document we should have written at the start: a joint adversarial-collaboration statement between the tranquilist camp and what we call the flourishing-preservationist camp, which holds that some goods are genuinely positive and that a life of pure tranquil absence, if technologically achievable, might still be deficient.

Please draft this joint statement. It should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides can genuinely sign—including about the phenomenology of suffering, the logical structure of value-asymmetry arguments, the conceptual distinction between affective absence and motivational absence, and what existing psychological literature actually establishes about the relationship between desire-satisfaction and reported well-being; (2) a disagreements table stating, in wording each camp would accept for its own position, exactly where they part ways—particularly on whether craving-independent satisfactions are conceptually coherent, whether the complete elimination of desire undermines the conditions for achievement-value, and whether a hypothetical being with no suffering, no cravings, no projects, and no cognitive tension could properly be said to flourish; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side—which I suspect include whether natural selection has shaped human positive affect to be irreducibly goal-directed rather than merely homeostatic, and whether there is any non-instrumental reason to preserve complex cognitive capacities once suffering and craving are abolished; and (4) for each crux, a concrete thought experiment, empirical pattern, or hypothetical biotechnology scenario that both sides agree bears on it, together with each side's explicit prediction about what that case would show.

In writing this, please introduce one fresh conceptual framing that is not part of standard textbook debates—for instance, a distinction between value-constitutive tension and mere instrumental striving—so that the collaboration addresses something genuinely new rather than rehearsing familiar anti-hedonist tropes. Finally, please state how confident you are in the framing of the disagreements and single out the specific point in your analysis about which you are least certain—whether it concerns the coherence of craving-independent goods, the interpretation of empirical findings on desire and satisfaction, or the logical form of the asymmetry argument.
