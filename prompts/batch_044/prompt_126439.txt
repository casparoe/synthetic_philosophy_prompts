Rather than asking for a literature review, I want you to prioritize: which open questions in this domain are genuinely upstream of institutional design, and which are philosophical side-branches that do not affect policy architecture?

Specifically, please rank the following three lines of inquiry by their practical urgency for constructing a coherent non-retributive framework, defending the ordering with explicit criteria—tractability for philosophers rather than empirical researchers, dependence of other questions on this one, and whether resolving it would actually change institutional practice. First, whether a compatibilist account of reasons-responsive agency can be operationalized in legal diagnostics without smuggling in libertarian assumptions. Second, whether abandoning just deserts necessarily collapses moral responses to wrongdoing into pure consequentialist management, or whether a non-retributive but genuinely moral form of blame or accountability can be sustained. Third, whether the empirical literature on behavioral prediction is conceptually necessary to justify preventive detention, or whether normative arguments about risk and dignity can stand independently.

Then, tell me which of these you would actually deprioritize if my goal is not to defend determinism but to build a narratively coherent world in which punishment is understood as harm-reduction rather than moral repayment. What conceptual evidence—rather than bibliographic references—would cause you to reorder these?
