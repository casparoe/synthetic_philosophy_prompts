I have recently found myself returning to questions of origin essentialism with an urgency that feels personal: my partner and I are now discussing in-vitro fertilization, and the clinical image of selecting one embryo from a batch of genetically distinct siblings has made the abstract question of whether I could have come from a different sperm and egg feel concretely unavoidable. I hold a cluster of intuitive verdicts about these counterfactual origins, but I cannot tell whether they form a coherent pattern or whether I must sacrifice some of them for the sake of a simpler principle.

Here are my judgments. If my parents had conceived with different gametes—whether earlier, later, or in another possible world—the resulting child would not be me, but a sibling. If, however, the very sperm and egg that actually produced me had been fertilized in a laboratory and implanted in a surrogate mother, I think I still would have come into existence, only with a different gestational history. In an IVF cycle where clinicians create several embryos from the same parents and select one for implantation, I feel strongly that had a different embryo from that same batch been chosen, the person who developed would not be me, despite sharing both parents and temporal origin. I am genuinely torn about donor conception: if my father's sperm had fertilized a donor egg and I had been raised by my parents, one part of me insists I could still be that child, while another part holds that my actual egg is essential to my being this particular person. Finally, if scientists had reconstructed my exact zygote from preserved genetic material long after my death and brought it to term, I believe the result would be a delayed identical twin rather than me reborn.

Is there a single principle—perhaps tracking the causal continuity of one particular zygote, or the necessary origin of this organism—that can accommodate all five verdicts without ad hoc adjustments? If coherence demands that I abandon one or two, which should go, and what does the surviving principle predict for a case I have not mentioned: suppose my parents used IVF with their own gametes, but due to a laboratory mix-up the sperm came from a different ejaculate of my father's, collected on a different day—would the child be me?
