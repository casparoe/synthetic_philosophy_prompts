I help run a neighborhood energy cooperative, and I have no formal background in political theory, but I have been thinking about whether we should keep electing our dispute and contract boards or switch to selecting people by lot. I want to turn the core principle behind sortition—random selection to reduce capture and improve descriptive representation—into a reusable decision procedure my cooperative can apply whenever we need to staff a decision-making body. The procedure should ask, in order: what is the body's purpose, how high are the stakes, how much specialized expertise is genuinely required versus learnable, how vulnerable the selection is to insider capture or campaigning bias, and whether accountability to the membership is essential. For each question, specify what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops with a clear recommendation for election, where it stops with a clear recommendation for pure sortition, and where it must hand the decision to a values judgment because the facts do not settle it. Include explicit evaluation of a mechanism I am considering: a "shadow panel" of randomly selected observers who attend deliberations but do not vote, intended to detect capture. The procedure should say whether this shadow mechanism improves legitimacy or merely adds noise, with conditions.

Then apply the procedure to three proposals:
1. A three-person panel to adjudicate solar-installation disputes between neighbors (mostly factual, low organizational stakes but high personal stakes for the parties).
2. A five-person team to negotiate a ten-year contract with the city utility (high technical complexity, high organizational stakes, long commitment).
3. Two representatives for a regional alliance where they will vote on shared policy positions (representative function, need for ongoing accountability).

In one of these cases, the procedure should recommend pure sortition even though our cooperative's instinct is to elect experienced, known members; flag that case explicitly and explain what the procedure still cannot resolve. Also state clearly what the procedure is not designed to do.

Finally, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields: procedure_name (string), decision_nodes (array of objects with node_name and stop_condition), shadow_panel_verdict (object with recommendation, conditions_where_helpful, and conditions_where_harmful), case_results (array of objects with case_name, method_selected, judgment_call_flagged as boolean, and conflict_with_instinct as boolean), and procedure_limitations (array of strings). Do not ask for bibliographies or citations; ground the reasoning in the logic of selection and representation itself.
