I recently retired from a career in condensed-matter physics and have been working through philosophy of science and cognitive science on my own. In my reading I have become particularly interested in the debate over whether artificial neural networks genuinely possess internal representations, as opposed to merely behaving in ways that invite representational interpretation. I would like you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps in this debate: on one side, representational realists about interpretability, who maintain that mechanistic interpretability and related methods recover causally efficacious, content-bearing states inside the network; on the other side, deflationary instrumentalists, who hold that attributing representations to networks is a pragmatic explanatory heuristic with no corresponding ontological structure.

Please format the document as follows: first, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, including agreements about the formal properties of deep networks, about what mechanistic interpretability actually delivers, and about which philosophical positions are not at stake; second, a disagreements table that states each side's precise position in wording that side would accept, focusing especially on whether phenomena such as feature superposition—where a single activation pattern appears to encode multiple distinct concepts—count as evidence for distributed representational content or as evidence that representation-talk is merely projective; third, identify the cruxes, specifically (i) whether representational attribution is causally explanatory or only predictively useful, and (ii) whether superposition undermines the idea of discrete representational vehicles; and for each crux provide a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, together with each side's predicted evaluation. Please separate what is settled—such as the mathematical structure of feedforward computation and the empirical success of certain interpretability techniques—from what remains genuinely contested among knowledgeable researchers, and do not present either side's philosophical interpretation as if it were the consensus view.
