I've been trying to understand the frame problem in artificial intelligence without any formal background in the field. A friend who works in technology summarised it for me like this: it is the puzzle of how a computer knows which facts remain true after it performs an action, often illustrated by a robot saving a painting from a burning room but also dragging out the flaming cart beneath it. He claimed that the problem was essentially solved by the shift from rule-based systems to neural networks, because distributed representations do not store discrete symbols that need individual updating, so there is no explicit "frame" to revise. He added that this is really the same difficulty as the symbol-grounding problem — both concern how internal representations connect to real-world meanings — and that philosophers like Dennett showed it was never a genuine obstacle, only an artefact of trying to program common sense by hand.

Something about this account feels suspiciously neat, yet I cannot tell where legitimate simplification ends and error begins. Is this a fair summary? Please distinguish what is accurate, what is a common simplification, and what is misrepresented — particularly regarding whether neural networks avoid the problem, whether it collapses into symbol grounding, and whether Dennett's position is being described correctly.
