You are an expert in moral and political philosophy specializing in the ethics of labor and temporal autonomy. Before drafting, briefly brainstorm the conceptual tension between vocation-based moral economies and universal rights to leisure.

Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers—Dr. Aris Thorne and Dr. Lena Vasquez—debating whether leisure can be a moral right that overrides vocational duty. Set the exchange in a seminar room after a public lecture on work and human flourishing. Each speaker must respond directly to the other’s actual premises: Thorne must acknowledge that karoshi involves real suffering yet maintain that suffering within a calling differs morally from suffering within exploitation; Vasquez must engage Weber seriously rather than dismiss him, and she must show precisely why universalizing leisure rights undermines vocational exceptionalism without collapsing into anti-work nihilism. Avoid strawmen.
