Two people on my co-op board got stuck arguing about the charter's self-amendment clause. (1) Maria said it's flat-out incohearent: Article 7 says you need 3/4 vote to amend anything, Article 12 says Article 7 itself can be changed by simple majority, and Article 15 says amendment-rules take precdence. So there's no stable ground -- apply 12 first and you've gutted 7, apply 7 first and 12 never activates. (2) Jake said that's just a hierarchy, not incoherence. 15 breaks the tie: meta-rules govern object-rules, end of story. (3) Maria said 15 is inside the same document, so it can be amended too; there's no archimedian point outside. She brought up Suber's Nomic, where rules can change rules, but even there you have an explicit rule about rule-changes being valid -- here the rule about precedence is itself subject to the rules it governs. (4) Jake said every legal system is like that. The US Constitution's Article V can amend Article V; we don't call it paradox, we call it flexibility. (5) Maria said there's a big gap between potential self-amendment and actually using it. Article V hasn't rewritten its own threshold, but 12 is being invoked right now to lower the bar for changing 7. That's not flexibility, it's a coup in legal clothing. (6) Jake said she's sneaking in a normative claim -- that lowering the bar is illegitimate -- and calling it logic. The logical sequence is just: invoke 12, amend 7. The "tail-eating" is just time ordering. (7) Maria said fine, then there's no reason they couldn't vote to replace the whole charter with "Maria decides everything." If amendment rules have no external constraint, the charter isn't a framework, it's just a placeholder for whoever controls the process.
