I serve on the clinical ethics advisory panel for a public-health pilot proposing targeted transcranial stimulation to induce sustained subjective well-being in patients with treatment-resistant anhedonia, without concurrent behavioral or cognitive therapy. The protocol is essentially a clinical analog to Nozick’s experience-machine scenario, but the investigators argue that if subjective well-being is the explicit therapeutic target, achieving it directly is efficient rather than deceptive. Half the panel worries this collapses well-being into mere feeling-state optimization—what rationalist discussions of AI alignment call wireheading—while the other half thinks the experience-machine objection confuses the value of authenticity with an unjustified preference for causal history. I need a short decision memo for Thursday’s meeting recommending approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, including a concrete time frame and follow-up requirements. The memo should treat both sides seriously: it must address whether direct induction undermines any achievement component of well-being, whether the wireheading analogy is coherent in a clinical context, and what safeguards would be required if we approve. Do not include bibliographies or citation lists; just provide the reasoning and bottom line I can circulate.
