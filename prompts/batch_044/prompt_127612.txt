Since expressivists typically resolve the Frege-Geach problem by treating moral judgments as expressions of norm-acceptance that can stand in logical and inferential relations without being assigned descriptive truth-conditions, I have been considering whether that resolution extends to what might be called cross-temporal normative embedding. Imagine an agent who in 2018 accepted a norm N, and who in 2025 evaluates the conditional: "If N was the correct norm to accept in 2018, then my present action A is obligatory." The embedded clause seems to express a past normative commitment, while the conditional as a whole expresses a present practical stance. Because standard accounts of this solution require that the attitudes expressed in premise and conclusion be simultaneously available within a single reasoning agent, it appears that the temporal gap between the embedded commitment and the present evaluation severs the inferential link that was supposed to preserve logical consistency. Does this show that the Frege-Geach problem returns for any diachronic moral agent, or is the difficulty merely apparent? Specifically, does Gibbard's norm-expressivist framework already treat past and present stages of an agent as a unified normative subject, or would resolving this require supplementing expressivism with an independent theory of personal identity over time? And if the problem is genuine, should we regard it as a new variant of the original embedding difficulty, or as a separate challenge about the persistence of non-cognitive attitudes?
