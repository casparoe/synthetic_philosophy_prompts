Center the narrative on an archivist, cartographer, or mnemonist who maintains these analogical webs, and who encounters an artifact (a mirror that reflects relations rather than images, a language with no stable referents, a mechanical calendar that counts backward) that can only be integrated through such an anti-analogy. Show, through plot and imagery rather than exposition, how this failure of ordinary mapping restructures the protagonist's cognitive world. After the story, append a brief note that distinguishes what is widely accepted in the analogy-centered framework from what remains genuinely contested—such as whether analogy can be fully formalized, whether there exists a non-analogical substrate of thought, or whether "slippage" between domains is a bug or a feature—and flag any specific liberties your narrative takes with Hofstadter and Sander's actual claims or with contested interpretations of them.
