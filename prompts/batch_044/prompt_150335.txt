As a retired experimental biologist who has recently turned to philosophy of science, I have arrived at a somewhat qualified defense of adaptationist reasoning that I suspect sits uncomfortably between the standard camps. I hold that Gould and Lewontin's spandrels critique is decisive for morphological architecture—traits arising as non-adaptive byproducts of developmental allometry, such as certain cranial sutures or the human chin—yet that it loses force when applied to tightly integrated physiological control systems, where functional coherence is difficult to explain away as mere structural spillover. I do not claim that every physiological trait is adaptive, only that the burden of proof shifts differently across these domains, with morphology defaulting to spandrel and physiology defaulting to adaptation unless specifically undermined.

I want an honest assessment of how exposed this bifurcated position is. What observations, formal results, or conceptual arguments would count as decisive defeaters rather than merely awkward complications? For example, if comparative phylogenetics showed that a widely conserved homeostatic mechanism—say, a particular feedback loop in osmoregulation—had arisen as a non-functional consequence of membrane geometry with no history of selective tuning, would that collapse the physiological side of my view, or could I absorb it by narrowing the domain further? Conversely, what if every trait previously classed as a morphological spandrel turned out to carry a subtle but measurable fitness effect in a specific ecological context?

Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. I also want to know which objections defenders of the original spandrels critique would themselves concede as damaging to my synthesis, and which they would dismiss as misreading their argument. Finally, tell me whether my position risks being insulated by ad hoc qualifications—whether I have constructed a view that survives counterexamples only by redrawing the boundary between morphology and physiology—and, if so, whether that insulation is a philosophical vice or a reasonable methodological partition. Please write your response in American English.
