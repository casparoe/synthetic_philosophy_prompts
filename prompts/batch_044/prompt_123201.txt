Write a short story related to acausal trade, but skip the usual multiverse-superintelligence handshake trope. Set it on a research station orbiting near a black hole where closed timelike curves let data loop without crossing horizons. Two groups — human scientists and an uploaded collective that doesn't experience linear time — find identical noise patterns in thier instruments and start treating them as messages. The humans think they're bargaining with aliens; the upload thinks they're facing a counterfactual version of themselves. They attempt a deal: the humans preserve specific quantum states, expecting the other side to "pay" by altering distant conditions the humans care about, even though no particle can travel between them. Focus on the dialogue where they argue over whether this is rational cooperation, self-deception, or just statistical coincidence. End ambiguously — maybe they both change behavior, maybe nothing happens — and keep it grounded in decision-theoretic confusion rather than space-opera spectacle.
