Been lurking rationalist spaces for years and now I'm actually trying to parse the value-pluralism stuff people gesture at. In a thread about career vs family tradeoffs someone said "if values are truly incommensurable there's just no rational basis for picking, you're literally flipping a mental coin." I feel like that one sentence is smugling in like five different philosophical claims. Can you separate the precise theses it could stand for? Like: no shared cardinal metric? An ordinal gap that can't be bridged? The parity thesis where options are on a par but not equal? Practical reason being normatively incomplete? Or the hidden-variable idea that if you dig deep enough you'll always find a tiebraker? For each one say whether it's a platitute, whether it's defensible, and whether it actually supports his "flip a coin" conclusion. I think people conflate the parity reading with the hidden-tiebreaker mistake but I'm not sure which is which.
