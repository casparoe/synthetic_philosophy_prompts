A professor in my comparative philosophy reading group summarized Zhuangzi's butterfly dream episode like this: Zhuangzi establishes that during a vivid dream one possesses no internal criterion to distinguish the dream-state from waking, and since contemporary predictive processing demonstrates that both states deploy the same hierarchical generative models minimizing prediction error, there is no 'outside' perspective from which to verify reality. The professor takes this further: Zhuangzi is not merely voicing Cartesian epistemic humility but advancing a metaphysical claim that distinctions—between self and butterfly, life and death—are conventional labels imposed on an undifferentiated flow of transformation (wuhua). I am inclined to think this captures something genuine about the passage, yet I worry it conflates distinct layers. Is it fair to read the dream argument as an implicit anticipation of predictive brain theory, or is that a clear anachronism? Does Zhuangzi actually deny the reality of these distinctions, or does he use the dream to illustrate the arbitrariness of fixed perspectives without collapsing them into illusion? And if we remove the neuroscience framing, does the core argument establish anything stronger than a call for epistemic humility, or is it being overstated as a metaphysical refutation of stable identity?
