I'm designing a diagnostic decision tree for a philosophy of science seminar that sorts a respondent's actual commitments—not the label they prefer—within the logical space of pragmatist theories of truth. The root node should ask whether truth is essentially a property of beliefs that successfully guide action in experience, with no further constraint. From there, branch on three axes: first, whether inquiry must converge on a single answer in an ideal long run (Peircean convergence) or can remain permanently plural (Jamesian warranted assertibility in particular contexts); second, whether verification is restricted to predictive-experimental success or extends equally to ethical, aesthetic, and religious domains where 'what works' is domain-relative; third, whether fallibilism applies universally or permits some beliefs to become permanently settled.

The leaves must include: strict Peircean convergence pragmatism; Jamesian radical pluralism; Deweyan instrumentalism (only as a genuinely separable leaf—if it collapses onto one of the others on these axes, flag it as indistinguishable rather than forcing a split); a hybrid local-convergence position; and at least two logically possible but apparently unoccupied combinations, such as convergence-required paired with verification restricted to non-empirical domains, or universal fallibilism paired with permanent plural settlement. Each node should be phrased as a concrete commitment question answerable without knowing philosopher-names, and the tree should contain no more than seven leaves. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for specific texts; where positions cannot be separated by these three axes, explicitly note the ambiguity.
