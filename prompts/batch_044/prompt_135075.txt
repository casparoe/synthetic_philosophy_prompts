I run a grad seminar on philosophy of mind — 10 people, 75 min, one meeting. Design an in-class game that makes them feel Hofstadter's knob-twiddling idea about creativity being variations on a theme, not magic out of nowhere. But skip teh usual music/art examples; instead give each small group a "seed concept" — the social practice of apology — and physical dials or cards for five parameters: scale of harm addressed, direction of agency, emotional temperature, temporal orientation, and social visibility. They twist 2-3 knobs and build a concrete scenario or micro-ritual that still counts as a variation on apology but is clearly different. Then teh other groups vote: same theme, stretched, or broken. Points for internal coherence and for taking risks. Include rules, cheap materials (paper/cardboard dials are fine), payoff scheme without real money, timeline, and a debrief that maps the voting crises onto the formal idea — that creativity is systematic parameter adjustment with conceptual slippage at the edges. Tell me what will predictably go wrong first time (probaly people only twist one knob to stay safe). Be honest about which part of Hofstadter's model this can't reproduce — maybe the unconscious, automatic nature of teh slippage? — and don't ask for citations or a reading list. They shouldn't hear the phrase knob-twiddling named until after they've played.
