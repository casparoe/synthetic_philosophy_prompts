As a retired biologist now working in moral philosophy, I am designing a 90-minute workshop for sixteen advanced undergraduates. I want them to feel, through action rather than lecture, that labeling an exchange "corrupt" depends on an unstated ontology of the office being polluted, and that applying Western bureaucratic-purity standards universally can misclassify socially embedded reciprocity as deviance. Design a structured role-play or deliberation game—using only reputation points and access tokens, not money—with explicit rules and assigned roles. Participants should first classify a fixed sequence of gift and favor exchanges instinctively, then have the institutional background revealed (a Weberian bureaucratic frame, a local reciprocity frame in which giving to a health official is a moral obligation sustaining trust, and a hybrid translation frame), discover that their classifications invert, and finally face a forced choice in which honoring one frame necessarily violates another. The debrief should map specific game moments onto the claim that corruption is frame-relative rather than act-intrinsic, predict what will go wrong on the first run—likely participants collapsing all non-bureaucratic frames into a single "cultural exception"—and state frankly which feature of the position, perhaps the absence of any neutral meta-frame for adjudication, the activity cannot reproduce and I must supply myself. Keep the design self-contained: no bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, since I will run it without external resources.
