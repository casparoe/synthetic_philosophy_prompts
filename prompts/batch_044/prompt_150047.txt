In my novel, an art historian named Dr. Voss distinguishes harmless tangled hierarchies from breaches of what she terms the inviolate plane. Her judgments to date: Escher's Drawing Hands is tangled yet safe, since the drawn hands never leave the paper's surface; Magritte's image of a pipe that denies being a pipe operates linguistically rather than recursively, so it does not breach the plane; Borges's Library of Babel, by cataloguing itself within itself, does breach it; a mirror facing another mirror is mere repetition without hierarchical ascent, hence safe; yet Gödel's formal system, in asserting its own unprovability, constitutes a true strange loop that breaches the plane. For the narrative, I require her verdict on the following case: a generative model trained exclusively on images of Drawing Hands produces a single frame in which one drawn hand holds a scroll inscribed with the model's exact training weights and the image's own cryptographic hash. Predict her judgment, then articulate two competing principles—one centered on the containment of the generative substrate, another on symbolic self-reference across media—that both fit her record. Conclude by proposing the single further image that would force her to decide between them.
