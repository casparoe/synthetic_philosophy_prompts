As a novelist drafting a satirical chapter about institutionalized ethics, I want a deadpan parody in the form of an employee handbook—approximately 650 words, formatted in numbered sections with subsections—for a fictional consultancy called the Bureau of Moral Surplus. The handbook should bureaucratize the concept of supererogation: it should treat acts traditionally considered beyond the call of duty as quantifiable, mandatory, and subject to quarterly performance review. Invent a fictional measurement system, the Surplus Obligation Coefficient, that assigns numerical weights to emotional labor, bodily risk, and foregone opportunity, and requires every employee to maintain a positive coefficient or face progressive disciplinary action. Include a clause—tentatively called the Saint Cap—that places an upper limit on how much of one’s life may be devoted to exceeding baseline duty, invoking the familiar philosophical worry that a life of relentless moral excellence may become personally unattractive or inhuman. The satire should exaggerate real positions in the literature: the consequentialist denial that there is any meaningful gap between duty and moral excellence; the cost-based defense that supererogation exists only when an action exceeds a specific threshold of personal sacrifice; the asymmetry between praising those who go beyond duty and blaming those who do not; and the problem of measuring moral cost in standardized units. The voice should be that of an earnest, well-meaning HR director who genuinely believes this system is fair, transparent, and liberating; the irony must remain entirely deadpan, with no winking or direct acknowledgment of absurdity, so that a specialist recognizes each clause as a reductio of an actual argument, while a non-specialist grasps the human cost of treating morality as accounting. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or references to specific texts that require verification. Write in polished American English.
