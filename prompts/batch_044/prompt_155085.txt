I'm the chair of a seven-member institutional review board at a mid-size research hospital; we convene twice monthly for ninety minutes to render collective ethical clearance decisions on proposed clinical studies involving vulnerable populations. Our protocol requires judgment on three binary premises—whether risk is minimal (R), whether informed consent is genuinely adequate (C), and whether scientific value justifies any residual risk (V)—with clearance granted only if all three are affirmed, which makes us structurally vulnerable to the discursive dilemma: majorities can support each premise individually while rejecting the conclusion, or affirm the conclusion while one premise lacks majority support. Our members—a biostatistician, a patient advocate, a clinician-researcher, legal counsel, and a community representative—carry unequal domain authority, so I need a reusable, real-time decision procedure, essentially a flowchart in words with ordered tests, that tells me whether to aggregate premise-by-premise or conclusion-first, how to tag each member's "designated authority" on specific premises without letting that tagging become circular, and exactly where an irreducible judgment call remains unavoidable. The procedure must handle cases where disagreement is factual versus axiological, must include a "qualified clearance" tier rather than forcing binary yes/no, and must specify a dissent-preservation rule: if the member with designated authority on a premise dissents, the board records a conditional status regardless of the vote. Apply it to three cases—one straightforward approval; one where majorities affirm R, C, and V separately but the conclusion fails, with the biostatistician dissenting on R; and one where the procedure mandates approval despite my personal conviction that the study should be rejected, with explicit identification of the step that overrules me. Be frank about what the procedure cannot settle, particularly when members disagree not about premises but about whether the premises are the right ones to apply, and specify how dissent must be documented when suppression would violate transparency obligations. It needs to work without external references, in ninety minutes, with people who are not logicians.
