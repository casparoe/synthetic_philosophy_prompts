Since my mother’s recent diagnosis with a progressive neurodegenerative disorder, I have been studying von Neumann’s posthumous reflections on whether the brain is best understood as a logical machine whose failures are broken circuits, or as an analog statistical system whose failures are degraded signal-to-noise ratios. Before I can assess which framework actually illuminates prognosis and intervention, I need to know whether the disagreement is substantive or partly verbal.

Draft the joint statement that two interpretive camps—the Digital-Structuralists and the Analog-Statisticalists—should have produced after arguing for years about von Neumann’s brain-computer comparison. Each side must be able to sign without feeling misrepresented.

Structure the document as follows. First, a numbered list of claims both sides accept: about what von Neumann actually said regarding neural logic and analog properties; about the formal limits he acknowledged for digital modeling of cognition; about the reliability mechanisms he noted in neural tissue; and about the explanatory purpose of the brain-computer analogy he constructed. Second, a precisely worded disagreements section—using language each camp accepts for its own position—covering at least: (i) whether von Neumann treated neural computation as reducible to discrete logical operations at the relevant explanatory level, or as irreducibly analog and statistical; (ii) whether the brain’s error tolerance is best explained by digital error-correction schemes or by analog redundancy and graded signal integration; and (iii) whether localized cognitive deficits after focal lesions support a modular-digital reading or remain compatible with distributed analog degradation.

Third, identify the cruxes: sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. I take two likely cruxes to be (A) whether von Neumann’s account of neural memory assumes discrete addressable registers or continuous statistical distributions, and (B) whether the predictive success of modern deep artificial neural networks in cognitive modeling vindicates the computational analogy he intended or undermines it by showing that analog-like statistical architectures succeed precisely where strict digital logic fails. For each crux, propose a concrete case, thought experiment, or line of reasoning that both sides agree bears on it—without relying on obscure citations. For instance, both might agree that if a precisely localized cortical intervention could restore a discrete cognitive function without altering global neural statistics, that would favor the Digital-Structuralist view, whereas if any restoration requires broad modulation of synaptic noise levels, that favors the Analog-Statisticalist view. Record each side’s advance prediction about that case in neutral language.

Finally, state explicitly whether any portion of the dispute is verbal—e.g., divergent uses of “computation,” “logic,” or “machine”—and explain which part and why. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations. Do not quote verbatim from von Neumann’s writings unless you reproduce an exact fetched passage; otherwise paraphrase without quotation marks. Write so that the residual disagreement, if any remains, is made as exact as possible.
