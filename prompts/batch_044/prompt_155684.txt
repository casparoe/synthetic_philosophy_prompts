hey, I'm not an academic or anything but I've been thinking hard about this Dennett idea with real patterns and the three stances — physical, design, intentional. I think there's a weird hole in it: people ask if mental states are real patterns, but they never ask if the design stance is actually doing real work or if it's just a blurry middle step. So I want to write a short proposal, maybe for a one-year fellowship (like two pages?), asking precisely whether the design stance is necessary to reach the intentional stance, or if in principle you could jump straight from physics to predicting beliefs without treating anything as 'designed'.

Why it isn't settled: Dennett and others say patterns are real if they help prediction, but they don't say whether recognizing an intentional pattern requires you to first recognize a functional design pattern. Some philosophers say yes, some say it's just a heuristic for brains with limited power, but neither side gives a principled test.

My approach: compare a thermostat (pure design) with a chess engine (intentional) and ask whether predicting the chess moves requires treating the chip as having a design, or if a super-powerful predictor could do it straight from the physics plus an intentional model that ignores function. I'd also bring in this bootstrapping worry — do we have to assume there's a design there in order to learn that there's an intentional agent?

A result would be either (a) design is indispensable because intentional patterns are only detectable against functional organization, or (b) it's eliminable and just a cognitive shortcut.

Failure: if I just end up saying 'it depends on the case' without saying what makes the cases different.
