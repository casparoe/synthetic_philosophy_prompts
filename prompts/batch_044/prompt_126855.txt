I am in the final year of my philosophy and politics degree, and this term I am taking a module on collective action problems and social choice theory. Last week we discussed Nick Bostrom’s framing of the unilateralist’s curse — the situation in which numerous agents each possess the capacity to take an action with globally significant consequences, and in which each actor’s local optimisation, absent coordination, tends to yield a collectively worse outcome than universal restraint would have produced. After the seminar I argued with a flatmate that the logic, applied literally, extends far beyond geoengineering or synthetic biology: if I turn up the heating unilaterally, and if everyone were to do so the boiler would fail, then the curse seems to forbid me from doing it; yet she raised the symmetrical difficulty, which we might call the reverse unilateralist’s blessing, in which an individually harmless or beneficial act becomes catastrophic only if everyone abstains from it, meaning that universal restraint is itself the danger. I want to see whether these two directions can be held in sustained tension within deadpan bureaucratic prose, without either breaking the irony or inventing logical absurdities that are not genuinely present in the reasoning.

Please write a satirical charter, approximately 650 to 750 words, formatted as a formal institutional document with numbered articles, subsections, and an appendix, in the deadpan voice of a university administrative manual. The document should be titled Charter of Non-Unilateral Conduct and should purport to govern every decision within a fictional student body named the Joint Committee on Non-Unilateral Conduct (JCNUC). Apply Bostrom-style reasoning with absolute literalness to every domain of student life — booking seminar rooms, selecting catering, even choosing a sandwich filling — and demonstrate how the prohibition on unilateral action requires a central veto body, only for that body itself to fall prey to a second-order unilateralist’s curse, generating an infinite regress of oversight committees, each requiring its own charter, until the document ends with an unresolved clause deferring all authority to a committee that has not yet been constituted. Include a specific clause addressing the reverse unilateralist’s blessing — the problem of harmful universal abstention — and show the charter tying itself in knots attempting to regulate both action and inaction simultaneously. The humour must arise entirely from the faithful exaggeration of the actual logic rather than from invented misrepresentations; a reader familiar with collective action theory should recognise that the parody is structurally fair. Maintain sustained irony throughout: never break character to acknowledge the absurdity, never wink, and never slip into direct commentary. Write in polished British English. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, requests for further sources, or specific page references to named scholars; the piece should function as a standalone conceptual exercise that requires no external lookup.
