I've been trying to pin down what makes the hole argument actually work vs just being a formal trick, and my intuitons are scattered. Here are my verdicts on some cases:

1. Classic GR: a diffeomorphism that's the identity outside region H but moves points around inside H. Same physical world, full stop—the reduncancy is built into the dynamics and we shouldn't try to "fix" it.

2. Lattice spacetime: there's an internal automorphism inside H that leaves every boundary observable unchanged, but there's no smooth manifold or continuous diffeomorphism at all. I still want to say it's the same state.

3. Hidden-sector unitary: apply a unitary inside H that commutes with all exterior observables, but it isn't induced by any spacetime symmetry and only touches fields that don't couple to gravity. Here I feel like it might actually be physically different, or at least I shouldn't just declare it a reduncancy like in GR.

4. Theory comparison: two theories match on every prediction, but one has hole-style internal redundancy and the other doesn't. Usually I'd pick the simpler one, yet in GR we keep the redundant formulation and treat it as a feature. So my simplicity preference seems to flip.

5. Horizon version: same transformation as 1, but H is behind an event horizon so no exterior observer can ever check inside. Still the same physical world, I think.

Is there a coherent principle behind all this—maybe about dynamical vs accidental symmetry, or about global gauge-fixability? Whatever it is, it needs to save 1, 2, and 5, explain why 4 doesn't push us to eliminate redundancy in GR, and tell me whether 3 has to go. What's the account, what does it say about cases I haven't mentioned, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop?
