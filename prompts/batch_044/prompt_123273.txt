As a recently appointed member of my university’s Committee for the Accreditation of Reflective Reasoning Systems, I have been asked to chair the sub-panel reviewing a proposal to deploy an autonomous deliberative agent—designated the Reflective Endorsement Protocol, or REP—within our administrative ethics office. The matter has become personally urgent: my own research concerns epistemic circularity in normative reasoning, and I now see the practical stakes with uncomfortable clarity, since the REP is intended to resolve ambiguous cases precisely by treating its own derivations as authoritative. The system operationalises what logicians recognise as a Löbian structure of self-trust: it accepts the schema that if it believes a proposition, then that proposition is sound, and uses this to bootstrap further ethical conclusions. The difficulty is the classic obstacle—if the agent genuinely endorses that belief implies truth, then it can derive belief in any representable proposition, collapsing the distinction between sound reasoning and circular bootstrapping. Our committee must recommend whether to approve deployment (perhaps confined to restricted domains), reject it outright, or approve only under stringent conditions, with a clear time frame for implementation or further review.

Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. It should present a concrete recommendation with a specific time frame, engage the strongest objection—that such an agent cannot genuinely trust itself without already presupposing its own consistency—seriously rather than dismissing it, and avoid any request for bibliographies, reading lists, or external citations, as several committee members lack specialised training in modal logic. Write in British English, and structure your response with the following sections in this exact order: Executive Summary (approximately 150 words); The Löbian Obstacle in Institutional Context (approximately 250 words); Arguments for Conditional Approval (approximately 200 words); The Strongest Case Against Deployment (approximately 200 words); Proposed Safeguards and Conditions (approximately 150 words); Recommendation and Time Frame (approximately 150 words); and an honest Note on the Weight of the Losing Argument (approximately 100 words).
