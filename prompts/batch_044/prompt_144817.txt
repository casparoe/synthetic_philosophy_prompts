As a retired physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of mind, I have become preoccupied with the disagreement between Norman Malcolm and Daniel Dennett over whether dreams are genuine experiences. Malcolm denies this on verificationist grounds—there is no observable criterion for an experience occurring during sleep—while Dennett treats the dream report as a post-awakening narrative construction rather than a record of lived episodes. Write a short story about a sleep researcher who uses a hypothetical neural-decoding interface to test whether her REM-sleeping subject actually underwent a sequential experience or merely confabulated it upon waking. The narrative should weave in the conceptual tension between verificationist skepticism and confabulationist explanation without resolving it, and it should include one invented technical feature—perhaps a retroactive coherence protocol that attempts to align neural traces with reported content only after arousal—to force the characters to ask whether attributing an unobservable experience to the sleeping brain is justified at all.
