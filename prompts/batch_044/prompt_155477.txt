I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind and cognitive science serving as the graduate representative on our university’s Institutional Review Board for Emerging Biocognitive Systems. We face a concrete decision at Thursday’s meeting: a synthetic-biology group has petitioned to classify their engineered ant colony—designed with synthetic pheromonal feedback loops that produce distributed, adaptive problem-solving—as a potential moral patient rather than a standard invertebrate research model. Their argument invokes the well-known philosophical analogy in which an ant colony is compared to a single conscious subject, with individual ants likened to neurons and the collective likened to an individual mind (the Aunt Hillary comparison). Board members opposing the petition contend that this is anthropomorphic projection onto swarm dynamics and that granting enhanced protections would set an unmanageable precedent for all collective organisms. I must circulate a short, formal written opinion before the meeting that recommends a concrete course of action—either granting provisional moral-patient status with clearly defined conditions and a review timeline, or denying it with an explicit rationale—while treating the collective-mind argument in its strongest form and giving an honest account of the best consideration on the other side. The memo should be actionable by the board, avoid appeals to authority or requests for further literature surveys, and should not depend on external citation. Please draft the opinion I will circulate.
