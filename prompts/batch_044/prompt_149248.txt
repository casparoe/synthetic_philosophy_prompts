Take the 1755 Lisbon earthquake and Voltaire's Candide reaction, and run it through skeptical theism—especially the parental analogy and Adams's horrendous-evil distinction. I want the ethics and epistemics, not the geology. Who had standing to demand justification: the survivors, Voltaire, later generations? Does Voltaire's outrage depend on assuming we can survey the goods, which is exactly what skeptical theism denies? And does the parental analogy break down for horrendous evil—like, parents can justify a needle to a child, but not torture—so that skeptical theism actually concedes too much here? Also, check whether the rebuilding of Lisbon counts as an unknown good or just post-hoc comfort. Bottom line: is this case a win for skeptical theism, a loss, or proof the view was never really build for catastrophes at this scale? Dont just summarize Candide; map the players, the options, and where the theory goes silent.
