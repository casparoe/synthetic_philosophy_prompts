I've sketched an argument about Wittgenstein's hinge propositions in On Certainty that treats them not as a uniform bedrock but as functionally differentiated by the kind of evidential pressure that could, in principle, dislodge them. I distinguish "gravitational hinges"—propositions like "the earth has existed for many years," held fast because overturning them would collapse massive swaths of empirical belief—from "normative hinges"—propositions like "other minds exist," held fast not by evidential weight but by being conditions for meaningful discourse and action. I claim gravitational hinges are revisable in principle by cumulative counter-evidence, however practically impossible, whereas normative hinges are not revisable by empirical evidence at all; abandoning them requires a change in form of life, which is a practical transformation rather than an epistemic update. From this I derive that skepticism about the external world and skepticism about other minds feel structurally different: the former targets a gravitational hinge and can be engaged, however absurdly, within the language-game of empirical reasoning, while the latter targets a normative hinge and therefore cannot be addressed by the same evidential moves.

Please comment on this essay idea. I want conceptual critique, not bibliography or suggested readings. Specifically: Is the gravitational/normative distinction defensible given Wittgenstein's own examples—such as his treatment of Moore's proof and the riverbed metaphor—without distorting his emphasis on the communal, practical grounding of certainty? Have I mischaracterized the conditions under which a hinge can be revised, particularly regarding whether normative hinges can ever be "given up" in a non-arbitrary way? Does my claim about the different phenomenology of skepticism actually follow from the taxonomy, or does it smuggle in an independent intuition? And does this framing obscure the social dimension of hinges that Wittgenstein stresses in the later sections? If you think the taxonomy collapses, say exactly where and what should replace it.
