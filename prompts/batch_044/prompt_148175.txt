Compose a villanelle—nineteen lines, with two refrains in the classic A1 b A2 rhyme scheme—on the conceptual problem of gradual neural replacement and the persistence of personal identity. Use as refrains: “One more column turned to silicon, and I remain,” and “No line divides the copy from the one replaced.” Between each recurrence, describe a successive stage of substitution—working perhaps at the scale of cortical columns or local synaptic networks—so that the poem’s formal repetition enacts the very accumulation of change it examines. In the opening tercets, build the functionalist intuition: that first-person reports stay stable across stages, that information flow is preserved, and that biological substrate is incidental to psychological continuity. In the middle tercets, press the opposing worry: that a continuous report might mask a discontinuity of being, and that if replacement proceeds without a discrete boundary, there may be no non-arbitrary point at which “the original” has been lost. The closing couplet must bring both refrains together without resolving whether the final silicon system is the same person or a perfect duplicate; the tension should remain open. Keep the tone analytical and unsettled rather than sermonizing about technological immortality. Do not name philosophers, cite texts, or include any bibliography; the argument should live entirely in the poem’s imagery, repetition, and rhyme.
