Draft the joint statement that a practitioner of microhistory and history from below and a practitioner of macrohistorical, state-centered, elite-focused explanation could both sign after an adversarial collaboration focused on method rather than on a single event. The collaboration addresses how micro-archival recoveries of subaltern practice should relate to structural explanations of institutional change, with particular attention to cases in which unregistered collective practices escaped both guild and state record.

Format the statement as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: shared commitments about what counts as valid historical evidence in both archival and material or oral traces; explicit shared rejections of common caricatures of the opposing camp; agreement on formal conditions under which a micro-level finding should, should not, or must revise a macro-level explanation; and a specific agreement on what you will call a scaling protocol—the explicit rules for translating claims about individual or local practice into claims about institutional trajectory without committing an ecological fallacy or an anecdotal overgeneralization.

Second, a disagreements table. In wording that each side would accept for its own position, state the precise disagreements about: whether subaltern agency is causally prior to institutional constraint or operates only within boundaries set by state and elite structures; whether official state archives are structurally blind to popular practice or merely incomplete yet correctable; and whether microhistorical case selection introduces unavoidable selection bias or instead corrects for the bias inherent in elite sources. Phrase each side neutrally.

Third, identify the cruxes—the sub-questions that, if resolved, would move at least one party. For each crux, specify a concrete class of evidence or argument both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance what each side predicts it will show. One crux must concern whether recovered collective labor or property practices that escaped registration can be demonstrated to have altered the path of a formal institutional reform, with each side predicting the causal mechanism or its absence. Another crux must concern whether macro-structural models of institutional change can be calibrated to incorporate micro-variance without losing explanatory coherence, with predictions about the limits of such calibration.

Fourth, a brief section on whether any part of the dispute is verbal. Specifically, examine whether disagreement over terms such as agency, structure, or scale dissolves once precise definitions are adopted—for instance, distinguishing strategic adaptation within constraints from constraint-transcending innovation, or distinguishing statistical aggregation from causal scaling.

Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific works by title, date, or page. Write the statement so that it can be constructed from general knowledge of historiographical method and reasoning alone, without access to external sources. Avoid verbatim quotations from specific texts; paraphrase well-known positions without quotation marks.
