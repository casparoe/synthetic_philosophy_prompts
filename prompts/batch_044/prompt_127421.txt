I've been working through the classic Meno-style problem—why knowledge should be prized over mere true belief if both guide action correctly—and I keep running into the same argumentative pattern in rationalist and epistemology-adjacent discussions. The defender says knowledge is superior because it is more "stable" or "robust," but when I press for concrete illustrations, the examples cluster in two places that don't obviously coincide. In one cluster, the thought experiment is counterfactual and solitary: the true-belief holder encounters a misleading cue or a slight change in circumstances and revises away from the truth, whereas the knower tracks correctly and retains the belief. Here the virtue looks like modal or dynamic stability—resistance to epistemic perturbation across nearby worlds. In the other cluster, the scenario is social and present: an interlocutor challenges the believer, demands reasons, or expresses doubt, and the knower can supply a defense or explanation that the true-belief holder lacks. Here the virtue looks like dialectical resilience—the ability to withstand interrogation without collapse. Both are presented as the single answer to why knowledge is better for the agent, yet they do different work: one concerns belief dynamics in counterfactual situations, the other concerns argumentative posture in actual ones. I see people slide between them within the same paragraph, using the prestige of the first to cover the second, without noticing that only the first even addresses Meno's concern about individual practical success, while the second mostly explains why knowledge is more defensible in debate.

Is there an established concept that captures exactly this slide—treating modal tracking-stability and dialectical defensive-stability as one unified virtue that solves the value problem? If there is a nearest neighbor in the literature (perhaps within anti-luck epistemology, or in discussions of the "value problem" itself), I want to know how my carved distinction relates to it: where the boundaries overlap and where they diverge. If nothing precise fits, I'd like the phenomenon named and defined, with boundary cases that separate it from plain equivocation on "robust" and from the general claim that knowledge involves justification. In particular, I'm interested in whether there are cases where dialectical resilience is present but modal stability is absent, or vice versa, and whether recognizing the split changes how we should assess answers to Meno's challenge. A bare label won't satisfy me; I'm after the carving—what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
