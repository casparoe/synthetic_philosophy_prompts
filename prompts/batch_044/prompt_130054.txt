Please write a retirement toast of no more than 400 words for Dr. Elena Vasquez, who is leaving her chair in philosophy after thirty years. The room is divided: about half are metaphysicians familiar with her defense of presentism, and about half are relatives and friends with no philosophical background. The toast must treat the philosophy as load-bearing, not as decoration.

The governing problem is Gödel's rotating-universe solution to Einstein's field equations. In that metric, global temporal ordering collapses, and Gödel took this to show that objective time is ideal rather than real. Vasquez has never denied the logical possibility of the metric; instead, she has recently proposed—in work still in draft—that presentism should be localized to causal diamonds, the spacetime regions untouched by closed timelike curves, rather than applied to the cosmos as a whole. She therefore saves presentism not by rejecting Gödel but by restricting its metaphysical scope.

Your speech must do three things. First, open with a single sentence stating your bottom-line judgment: does presentism survive Gödel's challenge once it is restricted to causal diamonds? Second, explain Gödel's conclusion in terms the family can follow—use the image of a path that loops back into one's own past—while giving the philosophers a precise formulation of the restriction strategy. Third, honor Vasquez without misrepresenting her: she does not claim the universe actually rotates, only that if it did, presentism need not collapse with it.

Constraints: do not provide citations, bibliographies, or references to specific page numbers. Do not present Gödel's cosmological model as empirically established. Speak in the first person as the speaker delivering the toast. Keep the tone affectionate but intellectually serious, and stay within 400 words.
