Specify the complete set of desiderata that a theory of rational belief revision on conditional propositions must satisfy to be adequate. Use the Judy Benjamin scenario as the central motivating case: an agent receives information linking conditions to outcomes (e.g., membership in a unit with a specific deployment rule) and must revise her credences in both the conditions and the outcomes in ways that standard conditionalization on atomic propositions does not license. For each desideratum, state the phenomenon it must accommodate, the distinction it must preserve, or the formal result it must respect; indicate whether it is negotiable or non-negotiable; and identify the specific counterexample or intuition that enforces it. The list should cover: (i) the separation of updating on a conditional from updating on its antecedent or consequent; (ii) consistency with the law of total probability over partitioned conditions; (iii) correct handling of indicative versus counterfactual conditionals; (iv) stability under nested updates; and (v) compatibility with a reflection principle for future credences. In addition, include two further requirements that go beyond the standard literature: first, "reflective-loop stability," meaning the theory must specify how an agent updates on conditionals that contain the agent's own revision rule in their content (e.g., "If I come to lower my credence in A, then B") without generating self-undermining cycles or explosive revision; second, "framing invariance," meaning the revision rule must produce identical results whether the conditional is expressed in canonical if-then form or in its logically equivalent disjunctive form, unless the framing itself carries independent evidential weight. After laying out the full specification with justifications, identify which single desideratum does the most damage to mainstream Bayesian, Jeffrey-style, and imaging-based approaches. Then briefly score the main existing contenders—Adams-style conditionalization, Lewis-style imaging, Popper-function revision, and primitive-conditional-probability frameworks—against your list, but keep the bulk of the analysis on the criteria rather than on the scoring. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the task is the conceptual architecture.
