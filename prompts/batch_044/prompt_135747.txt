I'm preparing questions for a podcast interview with an unnamed contemporary philosopher who defends tranquilism: the view that only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic value, while satisfied desire, pleasure, achievement, and other putatively positive states possess at most instrumental worth. He further holds a specific intertemporal extension of this view—well-being across a life should be calculated by integrating the intensity of unsatisfied craving over time, with moments in which craving is satisfied assigned zero axiological weight. From this he derives what he calls a compression preference, according to which a shorter lifespan with consistently minimal craving is intrinsically better than a longer lifespan punctuated by periods of satisfaction followed by renewed craving. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking him to restate the position. Include at least one question that presses the phenomenological distinction between craving-as-tension and the mere absence of desire; at least one that challenges the compression preference by invoking the intuition that extending a flourishing, craving-free life is valuable rather than axiologically neutral; at least one on whether the view permits moral reasons to generate pleasure or satisfaction in others if doing so risks producing future craving; and at least one that asks whether tranquilism collapses into negative utilitarianism despite his explicit rejection of that doctrine. For each main question, provide a follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give—especially anticipating his appeals to the intrinsic nullity of satisfied states, his separation of instrumental from axiological value, and his claim that only the removal of negative states constitutes real gain. Avoid generic biographical questions and do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
