I am preparing a long-form essay for a science-and-ideas magazine on whether formal learning theory finally answers Hume, and I want an internal consistency audit before I pitch the piece. Here is my working position, stated without hedging. First, I treat Occam's razor as more than a pragmatic rule of thumb: I think Solomonoff induction gives it a rigorous form, assigning prior probabilities proportional to 2 raised to the negative Kolmogorov complexity of a hypothesis. Second, I accept that Kolmogorov complexity is invariant across universal Turing machines only up to an additive constant, which means simplicity orderings are essentially objective for large-scale differences but underdetermined for small ones. Third, I hold that Hume's skeptical problem of induction survives even if Solomonoff induction is the optimal prediction strategy, because achieving optimal predictive loss is not the same thing as providing a rational justification for the inductive principle itself. Fourth, I believe working scientists are right to prefer descriptively richer theories—introducing dark energy, hidden parameters, or auxiliary conditions—when they save the phenomena, and I do not regard this as betraying Occam's razor but as applying it correctly to the total theoretical package. Fifth, I maintain that if two theories have exactly identical predictive distributions over all future observations, then the one with lower Kolmogorov complexity is not merely more rational to adopt but is actually more probable. Sixth, I also think that for any realistically sized scientific theory the additive constant dwarfs the complexity differences we care about, so Kolmogorov complexity is practically useless for adjudicating live research programs, even though I want to invoke it as a normative ideal.

Go through these six commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which ones only look conflicting but dissolve once the distinctions are drawn sharply, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, identify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and say what that revision costs me—whether it forces me to abandon the formalist gloss on Occam, weaken my Humean skepticism, or concede that scientific practice is irrational after all. Then tell me which single change to the set would clean up the largest number of problems at once. Please be precise: do not treat every commitment as equally vulnerable, and do not present apparent conflicts as fatal unless they actually are.
