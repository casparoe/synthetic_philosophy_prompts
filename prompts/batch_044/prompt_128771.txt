Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain with two refrains in an ABA rhyme scheme—about Schopenhauer’s claim that music holds a singular position among the arts: architecture, sculpture, painting, poetry, and drama each represent Platonic Ideas at ascending grades of objectivity, whereas music is not a representation of Ideas at all but an immediate objectification of the Will itself. Use two refrains, neither drawn from any source in quotation marks. Let Refrain A assert the representational view (that music, like painting or poetry, renders Ideas); let Refrain B assert the exceptionalist view (that music bypasses Ideas to copy the Will directly, with the bass line as the inorganic ground and melody as the direct image of human striving and desire). In the first five tercets, place Refrain A in its assigned lines and let it dominate as the poem climbs the hierarchical ladder from architecture up through drama; let Refrain B enter as an interruption that grows more insistent with each appearance, so that by the final quatrain both refrains must occupy the four lines together without merging or resolving. Do not synthesize them: the closing couplet should set the two lines side by side in irreducible tension, reflecting Schopenhauer’s insistence that music belongs to the artistic hierarchy and yet stands radically outside it. Include enough precision about bass (the deepest, concept-less foundation) and melody (the immediate, non-conceptual expression of will) that a reader familiar with Schopenhauer’s aesthetics recognizes the mapping, but keep the language poetic rather than expository. Do not include citations, a bibliography, references to specific editions, or any request for further reading.
