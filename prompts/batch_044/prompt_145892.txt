I'm trying to sort out where I actually stand on using predicition markets for real-world governace, not just forecasting tournaments. Here are my gut calls. Company lets traders bet on which product feature boosts revenue, then builds the winner: fine. City runs a pool on whether a subway extension cuts traffic, funds it if odds favor it: okay. Hospital runs survival-rate bets to rank ICU admissions: wrong, but I can't tell if it's because lives aren't commodities or because the data is noisy. Government uses a market to predict war outcomes and declares war if the expected utility is positive: I'm uneasy, though maybe that's just risk aversion? School board lets bets set the math curriculum: wrong, but is that different from the subway case? Family picks vacation via small betting pool: harmless but weirdly dystopian. Is there one principle that sorts these, maybe separating empirical bets from value judgments? Does that actually save the hospital verdict, or do I need an extra clause about human dignity? And what should the principle say about using markets to set criminal sentancing ranges, which feels like it should be banned outright?
