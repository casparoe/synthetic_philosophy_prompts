Construct a concrete conceptual puzzle arising at the intersection of Buddhist anattā and the phenomenological structure of what might be called attentional proprietorship: the sense that when awareness is directed toward a pain or a mental event, there is a non-reifiable but structurally necessary for-me dimension that is not itself one of the five skandhas. Do not invoke a Cartesian ego, a narrative self, or a transcendental subject; keep the tension internal to the aggregates. Describe a specific experiential scenario—such as the dissolution of body-boundaries in deep jhānic absorption, or the dissociative misattribution characteristic of somatoparaphrenia—where the usual correlation between citta and rūpa breaks down. Then articulate precisely where the conceptual pressure point lies: whether in cetanā seeming to require a director absent from the list, in the temporal continuity of the santāna presupposing a unifying principle foreign to momentary dharmas, or in the normative urgency of my suffering losing its grip if there is strictly nothing that suffers. Frame the issue sharply enough that it cannot be dissolved merely by appealing to dependent arising, and present it without resolving the tension.
