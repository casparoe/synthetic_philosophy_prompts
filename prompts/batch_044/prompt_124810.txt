I've come around to a pretty specific take on the Rietdijk-Putnam block-universe argument: its not mainly about simultaneity being conventional, but about determinacy being frame-invariant in a way that makes any global 'now' slice ontologically redundant rather than just perspectival. The usual replies — that becoming is psychological, or that Lorentz contraction is about measurement — feel like they miss the redundancy point. So here's my position: presentism collapses not by contradiction but by having nothing unique to do, since every event is already determinately past-or-future relative to some intertial frame. What would count against this framing? I'm not asking for 'time feels like flow' — I want the formal or philosophical moves that could actually undermine it. Could a Lorentz-violating quantum gravity model with a cosmological preferred frame rescue presentism without being ad hoc? Does quantum indeterminacy break the determinacy premise in a way that matters ontologically, or is that a category error? And have I insulated the view by defining determinacy too loosely — is there an unfalsifiability problem? Rank the defeaters: fatal, serious, merely awkward. Include what a defender of this determinacy-first framing would concede, and say whether the view is actually exposed in curved spacetime (GR) or if that just breaks the argument in a different direction.
