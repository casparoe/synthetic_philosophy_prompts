here is the answer one student turned in:

The Ship of Theseus is the puzzle where you gradually replace every plank of a ship, then ask if its still the same ship. Hobbes added the twist that if you save all the removed planks and rebuild them into a second ship, you now have two candidates for being the original. I think the continuously repaired ship is the real one, since for artifacts functional continuity is more important than material sameness.

Four-dimensionalism treats persisting objects as four-dimensional spacetime worms composed of temporal parts. So the ship that stays in the water is one worm, call it S, made of the temporal plank-parts that remain in that spatial location over time. The museum reconstruction is a different worm, M, composed of the old plank-parts arranged in a new spatial configuration. Because S and M dont share all the same parts, they cant be identical. This seems to solve the paradox cleanly.

But I dont think it really works. Locke argued in his Essay that artifacts lack any essential nature, so there is no deep metaphysical fact about which worm is the ship. On top of that, since plank replacement is gradual, there is no precise boundary between S and M. Four-dimensionalism requires sharp spatio-temporal boundaries for its worms, so gradual replacement creates vagueness that the view cant accommodate unless we allow vague identity, which is incoherent. So in the end four-dimensionalism just relocates the problem instead of solving it.
