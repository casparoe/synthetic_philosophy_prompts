I have been reflecting on the standard philosophical treatment of sphexishness—the mechanically rigid burrow-checking behavior of Sphex ichneumoneus—as a paradigm of action that lacks free will precisely because it exhibits no context-sensitive flexibility. It now strikes me that we ought to distinguish first-order sphexishness, the repetition of fixed action sequences, from second-order sphexishness, in which an agent deploys an invariant meta-corrective routine regardless of contextual novelty; on this account, the rigid system actually generates greater behavioral variability than genuinely flexible agency, since its outputs must constantly shift to preserve an unchanging procedural form. If that is correct, then should we not treat mechanical rigidity not as the absence of free will but as its structural precondition? I feel certain there is a subtle confusion embedded here—perhaps in identifying behavioral variability with the source rather than the symptom of agency—but I cannot precisely locate it. Where exactly does this reasoning fail?
