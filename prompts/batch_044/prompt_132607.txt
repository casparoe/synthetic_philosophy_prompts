Write a joke about Rawls's veil of ignorance and the difference principle. Rather than a generic joke about philosophers being detached from reality, the punchline should turn on a specific internal tension in the argument for the original position. The tension is this: behind the veil, rational parties are deprived of knowledge about their social position, talents, and conception of the good, and Rawls contends that under these conditions they will adopt the maximin rule, which in turn supports selecting the difference principle. Yet the veil also withholds knowledge of each party's own psychological profile, including their risk preferences. Consequently, the parties lack the very information needed to justify selecting maximin over an expected-utility rule or some other decision criterion; they must choose a principle of choice without knowing whether they are the kind of agents for whom maximin is rational. The joke should dramatize this instability—for instance, by depicting the parties attempting to agree on distributive principles only to realize that they cannot defend their choice of decision procedure without stepping outside the veil, or by showing that applying the difference principle already requires them to know whether they will end up among the worst off in a way the veil explicitly forbids. It should be conceptually precise and intelligible to someone familiar with the basic structure of the original position argument, but it should remain fully self-contained: do not rely on obscure secondary literature, direct quotations from texts, bibliographies, reading lists, or citations of specific commentators.
