Here is an argument my study group constructed as a reductio of Benacerraf's causal-epistemic constraint, and now we are unsure whether it genuinely refutes that constraint or quietly begs the question against the anti-platonist alternatives.

(1) Any domain in which we have genuine knowledge must contain entities that make the known truths true, and there must be a reliable epistemic link between us and those entities.
(2) Mathematical platonism provides the entities (abstract numbers, sets, structures) but severs any causal, spatiotemporal, or perceptual link to them; so platonism fails the second condition.
(3) Nominalism and fictionalism deny that there are such entities; they can preserve an epistemic link only by treating mathematical statements as lacking literal truth-values.
(4) Modal structuralism attempts a middle path: mathematics describes necessarily possible physical structures, so its "objects" are just patterns that concrete things could instantiate. But knowing these modal-structural truths still requires grasping facts about physical possibility that are not themselves concrete instances, so the causal-epistemic gap reappears at the modal level.
(5) Ante rem structuralism posits abstract structures that are instantiated; these structures are causally inert, so it falls back into the platonist difficulty.
(6) Therefore every available ontology either denies the entities (nominalism) or denies the link (platonism, ante rem structuralism, modal structuralism).
(7) Yet mathematical knowledge is real, intersubjectively stable, and indispensable to empirical science.
(8) So if (1) is correct, mathematical knowledge is impossible on every ontology, which is absurd.
(9) Hence (1) is false, and with it collapses Benacerraf's original motivation for rejecting mathematical platonism.

My friend takes this to show that Benacerraf's premise is self-undermining: applied consistently, it does not merely undermine platonism but undermines every rival, leaving global skepticism.
