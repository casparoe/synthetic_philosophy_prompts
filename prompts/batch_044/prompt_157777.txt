Take the CDC's March–April 2020 reversal on public mask guidance and examine it through van Fraassen's Reflection principle, treating the agency as a distributed epistemic system rather than a single believer. Specify what diachronic self-trust requires of an institution whose prior credence that masks reduce community transmission should align with its expected future credence once supply constraints and behavioral data update; determine whether the reversal counts as rational empirical updating or as the kind of diachronic incoherence reflection forbids; identify which institutional features—political pressure, supply opacity, heterogeneous sub-agencies—the principle fails to capture; and say whether this episode supports extending reflection to collective agents or instead reveals that the principle should remain restricted to individual minds.
