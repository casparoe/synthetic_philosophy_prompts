ok so I'm trying to write up my views on Rawls for a seminar and want a sanity check before I present. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. The veil of ignorance is mainly a heuristic for testing whether a principle is justifable to anyone regardless of birth class, not a literal contract scenario.
2. The difference principle is correct, but I reject maximin reasoning as its basis — maximin feels too risk-averse and doesn't match actual choice behavior.
3. I think the "no reasonable rejection" standard actually pushes toward strict equality, not the difference principle, since anyone stuck at the bottom could reject any permitted gap.
4. I still endorse the difference principle in practice, but I treat it as a pragmatic compromise rather than something the veil strictly delivers.
5. I think the veil should apply globally — nationality is as morally arbitrary as class — yet I deny we need a global difference principle, since there's no world basic structure that makes global cooperation anologous to domestic society.
6. I also think the veil should cover which generation you're born into, but I don't think that changes climate obligations in any interesting way.

Do these hang together? In particular, doesn't (3) undercut (4) so badly that I'm not being pragmatist but just incoherent? And isn't (5) hard to square with (1) — if the veil is global, why stop at borders for distributive justice? Is (6) just a weird add-on that conflicts with the cooperative-focus of (1), or does it dissolve? Rank the tensions by severity and say which single revision cleans up the most.
