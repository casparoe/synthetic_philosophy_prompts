Within higher-order theories of consciousness, I want to focus on an underexplored structural problem: whether the higher-order representation of a first-order mental state must occur simultaneously with that state, or whether diachronic integration—where the higher-order state is distributed across a temporal window—can still yield a unified conscious experience. This reframes the familiar tension between Rosenthal-style higher-order thought theories and Armstrong-style inner-sense accounts, since both traditionally assume near-instantaneous co-occurrence. For a researcher with roughly three years and a philosophical rather than neuroscientific background, which open questions in this area should take priority? Rank and defend the following three: first, whether diachronic integration can resolve the misrepresentation problem without collapsing higher-order theory into a first-order view; second, whether this framing changes the assessment of artificial consciousness in architectures with distributed temporal processing; and third, whether the inner-sense versus higher-order-thought distinction remains stable once simultaneity is relaxed. For each, explain why it is upstream or downstream of the others, how tractable it is with current philosophical tools rather than empirical methods, and what result in the next few years would cause you to reorder the priorities. I want a defended agenda—what deserves the limited time and why—not a survey or a reading list.
