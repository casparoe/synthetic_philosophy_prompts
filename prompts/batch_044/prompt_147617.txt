I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind and cognitive science, preparing a seminar on emergent consciousness and collective cognition. I want to explore whether Douglas Hofstadter's analogy in Gödel, Escher, Bach—between an ant colony and a conscious agent, embodied in the Ant Fugue and the figure of Aunt Hillary—can be sustained as a literal claim or collapses into metaphor.

Please write a formal but conversational dialogue between two equally competent researchers, Dr. Aris Kwan and Dr. Bex Osei, debating whether an ant colony can literally possess a mind. The setting is a graduate seminar.

Dr. Kwan should defend an emergentist position informed by integrated information theory and distributed cognition. She should argue that mental properties depend on causal integration rather than substrate, that collective qualia are not aggregates of ant-level experience but novel global properties, and that the Aunt Hillary analogy is instructive precisely because it reveals scale-invariant architectures of information integration.

Dr. Osei should oppose this not by rejecting emergence generally, but by imposing a recursive self-modeling constraint: any system with a mind must distinguish self-generated signals from external perturbations through a global error-monitoring architecture, which ant colonies lack because their signaling is chemically graded and largely feed-forward. Osei should also press the disassembly problem—that scattering the ants annihilates the supposed mind instantaneously, indicating the colony is an organized process rather than a persistent subject—and should argue that Aunt Hillary is an intentional projection onto statistical regularities rather than a discovered mental entity.

The dialogue must feature genuine back-and-forth. Kwan should respond to the disassembly objection by comparing it to brain death and neuronal replacement, and Osei should respond to the scale-invariance claim by distinguishing functional isomorphism from ontological identity. Neither interlocutor should be a strawman; both should display technical competence with the relevant conceptual machinery.

Please do not include bibliographies, citations, or suggested readings. The exchange should stand on its conceptual merits alone.
