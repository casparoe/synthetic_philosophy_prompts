I'd like your critical comments on a short conceptual sketch I've written about impact measures in low-impact AI systems—specifically, whether "avoiding side effects" can be defined without a full prior specification of value.

The argument is this. A standard approach defines side effects counterfactually: compare the trajectory of the world with the agent active against the trajectory in which the agent was never activated, then penalize deviations outside the direct mechanical requirements of the task. To avoid smuggling in values, one might try to ground the comparison in purely physical terms—what I will call a conservation-baseline criterion. On this view, an agent is low-impact if it does not alter global physical invariants—total energy, linear momentum, or the information-theoretic entropy of its local environment—beyond what its task mechanically requires.

I think this fails for two reasons. First, selecting energy and momentum as the protected dimensions, rather than chemical potential, biological structure, or social organization, is not ontologically neutral; it implicitly privileges one description of reality over others. Second, an agent could satisfy the criterion while causing severe local damage—rearranging matter into a high-entropy crystalline structure that preserves global energy but destroys an ecosystem—provided the rearrangement serves its assigned task. Thus, specifying what counts as a "side effect" seems to require a prior decision about which state-space dimensions are sacrosanct, and selecting the counterfactual reference class—inaction, random perturbation, or optimally efficient execution—similarly embeds normative commitments about permissible deviation.

My tentative conclusion is that low-impact alignment cannot be value-free: any formalization of "impact" either permits catastrophic restructuring or covertly encodes a value judgment. But I worry I may be conflating an epistemic gap—we do not yet know which dimensions to protect—with a genuinely normative claim that no dimension is protected without value. I would welcome your assessment of whether the argument is coherent, whether the conservation-baseline criterion fairly captures value-agnostic proposals in alignment thinking, and whether the problem could be avoided by constraining the agent's action space ex ante rather than measuring deviation ex post.
