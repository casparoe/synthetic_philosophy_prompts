Compose a rigorous philosophical dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—designate them the Hierarchist and the Reflexivist—on the coherence of legal self-amendment. The Hierarchist should maintain that a rule capable of altering its own conditions of validity necessarily collapses the distinction between law-creation and law-application into a vicious circularity that undermines normative authority. The Reflexivist should defend the view that circularity is benign provided the system distinguishes object-level amendment from meta-level authorization, allowing a rule to govern its own revision through a staged, hierarchically ordered procedure.

To keep the debate concrete rather than abstract, center it on the following hypothetical constitutional mechanism—call it Clause Omega: Clause Omega may be amended by the legislature, but only if the amending bill is ratified by a deliberative body constituted entirely according to the procedural rules that are in force prior to the amendment’s enactment. Have each speaker respond directly to the other’s actual points: the Hierarchist should argue that pre-amendment ratification merely postpones the circularity, since the authority of that very ratifying body ultimately derives from the clause being amended; the Reflexivist should reply that the temporal and institutional separation between the old-procedure body and the new-procedure rule creates an independent normative anchor that breaks the circle. Ensure the exchange is genuinely back-and-forth, with neither party reduced to a strawman, and instruct the dialogue to proceed without bibliographic references, citations, or appeals to named scholars—treat the arguments as conceptually self-standing.
