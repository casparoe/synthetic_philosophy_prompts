I've been thinking about whether culture evolves in a genuinely Darwinian way, as people often claim when they talk about memes replicating like genes. It strikes me that the comparison collapses in one specific area: biological replication copies discrete units with mostly random errors, but when humans learn complex skills—like speaking a language or building a tool—they do not copy discrete pieces. Instead they seem to reconstruct an underlying system from sparse, noisy examples, and they frequently improve or adapt it intentionally before passing it on. I want you to argue that this makes cultural transmission structurally Lamarckian rather than Darwinian: acquired modifications are inherited with high fidelity, and change is guided by reconstruction and purpose rather than by selection on randomly varying replicators. In making the case, please address the reply that memes can simply be defined as instructions for reconstruction; explain why intentional improvement is not just another kind of mutation; and show why this distinction matters for whether we should expect cultural evolution to produce adaptations in the biological sense. Write this as a focused philosophical argument, not a survey of the literature.
