I defend a strict, across-the-board version of greedy reductionism: every macro-level pattern—causal, normative, semantic, or social—is in principle fully reconstructible from bottom-up physical dynamics plus path-dependent history, leaving no explanatory skyhook and no structural residue that cannot be rebuilt as a crane. Call this exhaustive crane construction. On this view, apparent emergence always signals an incomplete reconstruction rather than an ontological gap, and any domain that currently resists reduction—whether conscious experience, institutional authority, or intentional content—does so only because we have not yet traced the right historical trajectory of lower-level interactions. What would genuinely count against this position? I want the arguments, formal results, conceptual demonstrations, or observable patterns that should move its defenders—sorted by how decisive they would be—including whether there exist macro-properties that systematically vanish under every possible bottom-up reconstruction rather than being merely redescribed, whether the view has already been qualified into unfalsifiability by treating every resistant case as a temporary cognitive limit, and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a metaphysical thesis of this kind. Please distinguish evidence that would be merely awkward from evidence that would be fatal, note what proponents of exhaustive crane construction ought themselves to concede as counting against them, and assess whether the position risks collapsing unnoticed into a rival—perhaps one that accepts irreducible relational structures—while still claiming to have eliminated all skyhooks.
