(1) braudel says geography and climate form a kind of prison that sets the hard limits of what's possible across centuries.
(2) if those prison walls are fixed, then individual events - a treaty, an assassination, even someone inventing a new kind of boat - are just fluctuations inside; they can't move the walls.
(3) so every specific historical outcome is overdetermined by the long structure; if napoleon hadn't existed, some equivalent consolidation would still have happened within the same geographical constraints.
(4) this applies to me too: my decision to stay here, or leave, or start a business, is just noise inside the mediterranean attrator.
(5) but if all individual choices are pure noise, then there's no causal difference between me actually deciding to stay versus being replaced by a structurally identical agent - my agency is redundant.
(6) extend this consistently: braudel's own choice to study the mediterranean instead of the baltic is also pure noise, which means his claim that the mediterranean has a specific long-term structure is just an accident of structural noise, not real knowledge.
(7) therefore the longue durée view proves that historical explanation is impossible and individual agency is an illusion.

where's the slip? i feel like there's an equivocation between can't change the walls and can't matter inside them, but i can't name it precisely. also if i have to choose, i'd rather keep (1) and (3) because living here has made the climate stuff feel really true, but i don't want to end up a determinist about my own life. what's the cost of saving agency here, and which step should i actually reject?
