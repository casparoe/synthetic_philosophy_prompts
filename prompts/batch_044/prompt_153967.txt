Two colleagues in a physics-philosophy reading group got stuck in a three-round disagreement about whether information-theoretic resolutions of Maxwell’s demon actually solve the paradox or merely redefine it away. The first said that the standard Landauer-resolution—erasing one bit costs kT ln 2—is correct but physically incomplete: it treats the demon as an external agent acting on the gas. If you rigorously embed the demon in the same Hamiltonian dynamics, the correlation between its internal registers and the gas particles becomes part of the joint entropy. Accounting for mutual information means the total entropy never drops; sorting only redistributes entropy into correlations, so the paradox dissolves because the demon was never outside the thermodynamic system to begin with. The second replied that this is precisely the kind of boundary-shifting that makes the resolution tautological: you save the second law only by defining the demon out of the role of an external, macroscopic observer-like system. The physically interesting case is an external apparatus that measures, sorts, and then resets, which is exactly where Landauer’s principle applies and where a real, irreversible cost is paid. She added that correlation accounting misses the directional, causal structure of measurement and feedback—correlations can exist in equilibrium without anyone sorting—so reducing the paradox to correlation entropy ignores the point. The first came back that insisting on “external agents” and “causal direction” smuggles anthropomorphic categories into physics; thermodynamics cares only about state-space volume and Hamiltonian evolution, not about observers, and if you require an explicit irreversible erasure step outside the correlation formalism you are importing a metaphysical distinction that has no physical grounding.
