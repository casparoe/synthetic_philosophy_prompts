Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between the Causal-Source Camp and the Institutional-Role Camp on killing versus letting die should have produced.

The Causal-Source Camp maintains that the doing/allowing distinction tracks an objective asymmetry in causal structure: killing introduces a lethal causal pathway originating with the agent, whereas letting die is the absence of an intervention that would have interrupted a pathway originating elsewhere. The Institutional-Role Camp maintains that the distinction carries no independent metaphysical weight and is fully constituted by socially constructed roles, expectations, and frameworks of responsibility allocation; they grant that killing and letting die are treated differently in law and morality, but deny that this reflects any pre-institutional causal fact.

Produce the document in four parts.

I. Agreed Claims. Number at least six claims both camps can sign. These should cover: the grammatical distinction between action and omission; the standard intuitive asymmetry in direct cases, such as administering a lethal agent versus failing to supply an antidote that one has no special duty to provide; the existence of a parallel legal distinction in most jurisdictions between homicide and failure to rescue; the collapse or ambiguity of the distinction in cases of indirect causation and causal preemption; the shared rejection of a purely affective or psychological explanation for the asymmetry; and the fact that technological mediation—specifically, an automated system with transparent decision rules that either actively withdraws life support or passively declines to initiate it—does not by itself dissolve the conceptual problem.

II. Disagreements Table. Present three columns: Issue / Crux; Causal-Source formulation, phrased so that camp would accept the wording of its own view; Institutional-Role formulation, phrased so that camp would accept the wording of its own view. Cover at least: whether there is a non-normative causal difference between initiating a lethal sequence and failing to block an independent lethal sequence; whether institutional roles and social expectations can fully absorb the moral weight traditionally assigned to the distinction; and whether the asymmetry survives in engineered cases where the same agent designed both the lethal pathway and the missing safeguard.

III. Cruxes and Predictive Tests. Identify three cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side. For each, provide: (a) a neutral formulation of the crux; (b) a concrete shared hypothetical that both sides agree is relevant, including at least one case involving an artificial mediator with fully transparent decision logic; (c) the Causal-Source Camp's predicted judgment about that case, recorded in advance; (d) the Institutional-Role Camp's predicted judgment about that case, recorded in advance.

IV. Residual Verdict. A brief paragraph, written in language both camps could endorse, stating whether any portion of the dispute is purely verbal—specifying exactly which portion and why—or whether every remaining disagreement is substantive, together with the reason both sides accept that assessment.

Write formally, using precise philosophical vocabulary. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific published works or authors. Avoid verbatim quotations from any real text; paraphrase without quotation marks if reference to a view is needed. The prompt should be self-contained and answerable without access to external sources.
