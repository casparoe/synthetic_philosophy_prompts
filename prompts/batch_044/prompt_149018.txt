Hi — I'm a retired experimental physicist who's only recently got into philosphy, mostly because I was tired of arguing about climate models with people who don't understand error bars. Lately I've been chewing over Zeno's arrow paradox, and I think there's a physical analogy everyone glosses over.

In quantum mechanics there's the quantum Zeno effect: measure a system often enough and you freeze its evolution. At first that sounds like Zeno was right — chop time into slices and motion stops. But I reckon that's a category mistake. The quantum effect is about state-vector collapse under interaction, not about whether an object can be in motion during an interval. Zeno wasn't worried about observers; he was worried that if an arrow occupies exactly one point at each instant, then at no instant is it actually moving, so it never moves at all. The quantum version just says 'watch it closely and it won't change', which is a different problem altogether.

I've scribbled some notes arguing that discretising time at the Planck scale doesn't solve the paradox either — it just pushes the continuity question down a level. Instead of asking how the arrow gets from point A to point B continuously, you ask how it gets from Planck-slice n to slice n+1. I also think modern talk of 'supertasks' — doing infinitely many actions in a finite time — is mostly a red herring here, because Zeno's worry is about the internal structure of a single interval, not about counting steps.

Could you please read these ramblings and tell me where I'm going wrong? I'm particularly unsure whether my distinction between physical measurement and metaphysical continuity holds up, or whether I'm just importing physics concepts into a debate where they don't belong. Also, have I mischaracterised how philosophers nowadays treat supertasks? Please reply in British English — I'm trying to favour that spelling now I'm reading more British texts. And please don't send me a bibliography or reading list; at my age I'd rather just hear your direct thoughts. Apologies for any sloppy wording — I'm still learning to write philosophically rather than experimentally!

My notes:
- Arrow paradox concerns the absence of motion at an instant.
- Quantum Zeno effect concerns measurement-induced freezing of dynamics.
- These are not analogous: one is metaphysical, the other is about observer-system interaction.
- Planck-scale discrete time relocates, but does not resolve, the continuity problem.
- Supertasks address infinite divisibility of action, not the composition of motion within an interval.
