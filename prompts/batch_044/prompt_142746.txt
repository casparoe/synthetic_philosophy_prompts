Write a short story of approximately 800 words exploring the dark forest hypothesis—the view that cosmic civilizations hide their presence because revealing one's location invites annihilation by unknown competitors. Set the narrative in a far-future epoch where two post-biological societies have never exchanged direct signals, yet an archivist discovers that the void between them is not natural but meticulously engineered: both civilizations maintain precise, energy-intensive absences in cosmic background radiation patterns, using structured silence as a form of costly signaling to demonstrate mutual awareness without exposure. The story should follow this archivist as they come to understand that sustained concealment requires continuous expenditure and that the refusal to communicate may itself constitute a kind of relationship—one built on the shared recognition that transparency is a thermodynamic and strategic vulnerability. The central conflict should arise when the archivist considers whether initiating contact would be read as an invitation to cooperate or as a confession of weakness that breaks the equilibrium of mutual terror. Avoid conventional tropes of alien encounter or technological spectacle; instead, focus on the philosophical ambiguity between rational caution and pathological paranoia. Conclude without resolving the archivist's choice, leaving the narrative suspended in the same epistemic darkness that defines the characters' existence.
