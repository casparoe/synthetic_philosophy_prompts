I have been arguing with a friend about whether it is fair to condemn Columbus for the violence and enslavement that followed his voyages. She maintains that he was merely a product of his era, and that applying contemporary moral standards to him is like blaming someone for not knowing the future. I countered that she regularly criticizes modern corporations for environmental harm that was entirely legal at the time, and she responded that the parallel fails: unlike today, she said, there were already voices within Columbus's own society—specifically among Spanish clergy—who condemned the mistreatment of indigenous peoples, which means he was not simply following an unquestioned universal norm. I am unsure whether this distinction saves her position, or whether the presence of dissent in both eras undermines the defence altogether. Is she being consistent, or is there a principled reason why historical figures should be judged differently from modern ones?
