As a journalist covering clinical epistemology and philosophy of mind, I recently spent an afternoon with a forensic psychologist who needs a structured, reusable way to evaluate whether an interviewee is in a state of self-deception rather than merely mistaken, strategically lying, or confabulating. The conceptual problem is the classic paradox: if deceiving requires an intention to induce a false belief, and being deceived requires ignorance of that falsehood, then one agent seems unable to satisfy both conditions at once; rather than treating this as a knockout argument, I want the interviewer to work with an operationalized failure mode I will call reflective accessibility failure, in which the agent genuinely possesses a belief under neutral conditions but has installed a defensive compartment that blocks its retrieval whenever self-image or emotional stakes are activated. The practitioner conducts forty-five-minute intake interviews for a multidisciplinary review board that lacks philosophical training, so the deliverable must be a flowchart in words—ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer at each node, explicit stop points that return “not self-deception,” clear hand-off nodes where the remaining question is one of values rather than facts, and an honest warning about where the procedure misfires—especially when affective resistance mimics compartmentalization.

Apply this procedure to two worked cases: first, a patient who reports “complete emotional neutrality” toward a sibling with whom he recently contested an inheritance, despite narrative and physiological indicators of intense attachment; second, a whistleblower who claims she “never actually registered” accounting anomalies that appear in emails she herself drafted. One of these applications should yield an answer the practitioner is likely to resist or find counter-intuitive. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the board needs executable steps, not literature, and a model without web access should not fabricate references. Present the entire response as exactly five numbered items, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion; embed the procedural stages, both case applications, and the misfire warning within those five numbered parts.
