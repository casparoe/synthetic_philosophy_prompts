I have been trying to work out whether Nozick's tracking conditions can coexist with any form of epistemic closure, and my intuitions about particular cases seem to conflict in ways I cannot resolve. Here are five verdicts. First, if an engineer tracks that a bridge will not collapse — in the sense that she would not believe it sound if it were unsound, and she does believe it — and she competently derives that crossing is safe, then she knows crossing is safe; closure seems unproblematic here. Second, if she does not track the bridge's integrity herself but tracks, via reliable testimony, that an inspector has tracked it, and she then derives that crossing is safe, I still think she knows, though the knowledge feels mediated and the closure step seems to depend on her tracking the reliability of the chain rather than the conclusion itself. Third, if she tracks the bridge's integrity but the inference to safety relies on an unstated auxiliary assumption — say, that no external shock will occur — which she has not tracked at all, then she does not know crossing is safe, even though she knows the bridge is sound and the entailment holds given that assumption; here closure appears to fail because part of the inferential machinery is epistemically dark to her. Fourth, if she tracks that the object is red, and therefore competently derives that it is not a colourless green idea, I feel she knows the conclusion despite the fact that her belief-forming method for the conclusion is not counterfactually sensitive to its falsity — the tracking condition is satisfied for the premise but manifestly not for the derived belief. Fifth, in a distributed case, agent A tracks p, agent B tracks only that A tracks p (not p itself), and B derives q from p; I am inclined to deny that B knows q, yet the premises for closure seem present in B's cognitive economy.

Is there a single principle — perhaps one that distinguishes direct entailment from entailment mediated by untracked auxiliaries, or that permits closure only when the mode of inference is itself within the scope of tracking — that accommodates all five judgments? If not, which of my verdicts must be sacrificed: the robustness of closure in the direct and trivial-entailment cases, or the fragility of closure when testimony, hidden premises, or distributed cognition are involved? And does the result show that Nozick's account is simply incompatible with closure, or only with an implausibly strong version of it?
