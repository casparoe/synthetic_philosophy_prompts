I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm also on my city's Civic Deliberation Fund board — long story, don't ask. We vote next tuesday on a $40k grant for "Bridging Voices," a facilitation program that trains residents to discuss zoning and housing policy. Their rule: if you argue from religious grounds about justice or the common good, you also have to present a "parallel secular version," but that version doesn't need to be your real reason, just present in the room. Some board members say this tracks Rawls's proviso — you can bring your full doctrine in as long as you eventually back it with proper political reasons in due course. Others say it's actually worse than an outright ban: it forces religious participants into a kind of double-consciousness, performing a reason they don't hold, which makes the public sphere feel fake and psychologically coercive. I need a short memo recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a concrete timeline like a six-month pilot then review. It should treat the strongest losing argument seriously — especially the coercion-objection — and deal with the actual mechanics of the program rather than just name-checking philosophers. What's the call?
