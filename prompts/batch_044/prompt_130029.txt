Give me your credence — one number between 0 and 1 — that if negative utilitarianism is correct, then an aligned artificial superintelligence should choose never to instantiate any digital substrate capable of suffering, even if that choice simultaneously eliminates the possibility of vastly greater net-positive well-being among those same substrates, because the moral asymmetry between suffering and happiness makes the risk of suffering decisive regardless of foregone good. Present your answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introductory or concluding text. In item 1, state the overall credence and indicate whether you combine the terms multiplicatively, by taking the minimum, or by another rule. In items 2, 3, and 4, each isolate one decomposition term — specifically: (i) the probability that negative utilitarianism genuinely assigns a lexical or near-lexical priority to suffering reduction over well-being promotion, (ii) the probability that potential suffering (the suffering a never-created being would have experienced) carries the same moral weight as actual suffering within this framework, and (iii) the probability that the duty of non-maleficence applies to the act of bringing suffering-capable beings into existence rather than only to harming existing ones — providing a rough probability and a brief justification for each. In item 5, identify which of those three terms you are least confident about and describe the specific philosophical argument or empirical result concerning artificial consciousness or the ontology of harm that would most significantly revise that term and therefore your overall credence.
