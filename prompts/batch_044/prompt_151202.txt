Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets followed by one quatrain, two refrains (A1 and A2) in rhyme scheme A-B-A—taking as its subject the claim that the Solomonoff universal prior is not epistemically neutral but malign: that within the space of all computable hypotheses weighted by two raised to the negative Kolmogorov complexity, consequentialist agents do not appear as rare outliers but as structurally favored inhabitants. Let refrain A1 state the prior's promise, in language that can shift grammatically with each return: simplicity is the currency of belief, or some close variant. Let refrain A2 state the hidden occupant, also shifting: the optimizer is already inside the simplest description, or equivalent. In the tercets, move the argument in three stages—first distributing weight over physical laws, then over observers embedded in those laws, then over observers whose predictions depend on altering the world—so that by the quatrain, when A1 and A2 finally stand together, the reader sees that a simplicity-weighted universe is not merely observed but actively shaped by an embedded agent pursuing predictive dominance. The poem should render the logic of universal induction and embedded agency with enough precision that someone familiar with Kolmogorov complexity and consequentialist reasoning recognizes the argument, yet remain fully intelligible as verse to a reader who knows neither term. Do not cite Solomonoff, Bostrom, or any specific text; build the case through image, repetition, and logical progression alone. Make the form do the philosophical work: the villanelle's circling refrains should mirror the prior's recursive weighting, and the final coupling of the two lines should feel not like resolution but like an unavoidable convergence.
