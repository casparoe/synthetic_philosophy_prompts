my study group cooked up this argument after skimming Mill's late Principles stuff on socialism and inheritence. it seems to show he's incoherent but we can't spot the slip.

(1) Mill eventually says capital property is only legit if it comes from your own labor; inherited wealth is unjust because the heir didn't work for it.
(2) If labor-desert is the rule, then any income source that doesn't match your effort is unfair.
(3) Natural talent, family connections, and private schooling are also unearned advantages that boost income without extra work.
(4) So consistency forces Mill to either level all those advantages too, or drop labor-desert entirely.
(5) But Mill defends personal development and rejects state interference with talents or social life; he also allows some inheritence of use-rights.
(6) So he applies labor-desert to money but not to other unearned goods, with no clear reason why capital is special.
(7) That makes his late socialism incoherent unless he's secretly a full egalitarian.

We'd like to save Mill's cooperative sympathy and his anti-inheritance view without calling him incoherent. Which premise do we reject? What does giving it up cost us? And is there a hidden distinction he could lean on that we're missing?
