Please explain the conceptual issue that arises from holding together Chalmers's meta-problem of consciousness—the question of why we form phenomenal judgments and whether explaining those judgments is easier than explaining consciousness itself—with the thesis that phenomenal reports are not descriptive attributions of intrinsic qualitative properties but performative expressions of cognitive accessibility or attentional availability. In particular, clarify whether accepting this thesis dissolves the meta-problem's characteristic asymmetry, merely relocates it to the semantics and pragmatics of self-report, or instead undermines the very distinction between the hard problem and the meta-problem. Be precise about the difference between phenomenal transparency (the claim that introspection gives direct access to qualitative properties) and phenomenal constitution (the claim that such properties are constituted by being accessed or reported), and indicate what each position implies for whether a physicalist account can legitimately stop at explaining judgment-formation without leaving an explanatory gap. Avoid treating the disagreement as purely verbal; identify the substantive crux about whether explaining why we think there is a hard problem eliminates the need to explain consciousness itself, and specify what kind of empirical or conceptual finding would shift the balance between these options.
