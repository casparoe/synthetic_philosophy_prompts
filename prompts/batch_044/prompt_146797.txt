Please draft the joint statement that a committed commemorative pluralist and a committed historical-realist demystifier could both endorse after a rigorous, year-long adversarial collaboration on the ethics and politics of national myths surrounding lost causes. Focus specifically on four reference cases: the Lost Cause of the Confederacy, the Masada narrative in Israeli national memory, the Kosovo myth in Serbian commemorative culture, and the Dunkirk spirit in British collective identity. Structure the document in three parts. First, provide a numbered list of agreed claims: include shared descriptions of the historical events themselves, shared recognition that all four narratives contain selective omission or symbolic exaggeration, shared agreement on the psychological and social functions these myths serve, shared acknowledgment of the harms that unrevised myths can perpetuate, and shared acceptance that not all commemorative falsehoods are ethically equivalent. Second, present a disagreements table. In neutral wording that each side would accept for its own position, state the precise residual disputes; I take the central cruxes to be (i) whether a community possesses something like mnemonic sovereignty — an intrinsic right to maintain its commemorative narrative regardless of historical accuracy — and (ii) whether the affective and cohesive functions of a lost-cause myth can in principle be preserved by substituting an accurate but structurally isomorphic narrative. For each crux, identify a concrete case or policy intervention that both sides agree would bear evidential weight (for example, the redesign of a specific monument or the adoption of a particular school curriculum), and record in advance each side's explicit prediction about what that case would demonstrate. Third, include a brief section identifying any disputes that are merely verbal — for instance, disagreements over whether labeling a narrative a myth entails a judgment of total falsehood or merely of symbolic construction — and explain how the collaboration has dissolved them. The statement should be written in formal philosophical prose, should not invoke named scholars as authorities, should not request or assume access to bibliographies or citation lists, and should be comprehensible to a reader who has no external search capability. Do not include a verdict on which camp is correct; the aim is exactness in agreement and in remaining disagreement.
