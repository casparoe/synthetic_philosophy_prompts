In philosophy of language, performative utterances are often likened to pressing a button: saying "I promise" or "I apologise" does not describe reality but activates an institutional mechanism, and success means the circuit closes rather than that a statement matches the world. This analogy is used to defend Austin's claim that performatives lack truth-values and are governed solely by felicity conditions.
