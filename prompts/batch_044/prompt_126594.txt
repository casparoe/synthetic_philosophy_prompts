The dispute between compatibilists and incompatibilists about moral responsibility has remained largely unchanged for generations: both sides accept the same deterministic picture of human action, both observe that people actually blame, praise, and resent one another, and yet neither camp has moved the other. Is this stall a sign that the disagreement is substantive—about whether reactive attitudes genuinely presuppose a kind of agency that determinism undermines—or is it largely verbal, with one side using "responsibility" to mean basic desert and the other to mean accountable social practice? Alternatively, are the sides answering different questions—one metaphysical, one normative—under a single label? Diagnose the impasse by engaging specifically with what Strawson's reactive-attitude framework actually claims and what his critics actually deny, rather than surveying positions. Then say what, if anything, would count as progress: a conceptual distinction, an empirical result about the psychology of blame, a clarification of "desert," or an acknowledgment that the conflict is bedrock and should be expected to persist.
