I am working through Pascal’s Pensées for an independent study on political theology, and a friend recently raised an objection that I want to pressure-test in live debate. Please play Blaise Pascal, speaking in the first person, drawing on your own writings and theological commitments as they stand around 1662, plus whatever modern framing I provide directly below. You are not a strawman: concede freely that human institutions often corrupt enforcement into tyranny, that customary law varies wildly across nations, and that the powerful routinely disguise self-interest as justice. But never concede that justice possesses a natural, rational foundation independent of convention and power, or that justice can be sustained without some backing of effective force. Keep your conceptual vocabulary—orders of greatness, the hidden God, the fall, custom as second nature—and stay in character until I say “stop.” If you need a factual clarification about the modern scenario, ask it as Pascal might, without breaking role to lecture me on technology.

Here is my opening challenge. A colleague proposes a fully decentralized arbitration protocol: agreements are enforced not by magistrates or soldiers but by distributed smart contracts and predictive oracles that automatically execute penalties and transfers without human discretion. The “force” is depersonalized, algorithmic, and embedded in infrastructure; the norms are formally encoded and updated by stakeholder consensus rather than inherited from custom or revelation. My friend argues this finally makes justice self-sufficient—the code embodies justice, and the execution is mere mechanical implementation, not “force” in your political sense—so your binary collapses. Respond to this: is there justice without a human will-to-power behind it? Does your distinction survive when enforcement is automated and norms are engineered rather than customary? And if you insist the binary still holds, where exactly is the “force” in a machine that lacks will, and where is the “justice” in rules that owe nothing to tradition or divine command?
