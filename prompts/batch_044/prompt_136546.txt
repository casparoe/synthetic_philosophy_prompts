I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mathematics, and in a recent seminar on the metaphysics of mathematics we reached an impasse that I think turns on a confusion I cannot quite isolate. We were discussing Hilbert's hotel as an intuitive model of actual infinity: a hotel with countably infinite rooms, every one occupied, yet able to accommodate a new guest by shifting each current occupant to the next room. Alongside this, we considered the finitist objection, rooted in an Aristotelian rejection of completed infinities, that an actually infinite collection violates the part-whole principle, since such a set can be placed in one-to-one correspondence with a proper subset of itself and therefore cannot be a genuinely completed whole.

My difficulty arises when I try to combine these pictures with Cantor's result that there is no largest cardinal. If Hilbert's hotel represents a completed infinite totality, with every room filled, then the shifting procedure that frees room 1 seems to show that this completed state is never stable: there is always an internal rearrangement that expands occupancy without introducing new rooms. Furthermore, Cantor's power-set argument demonstrates that for any given infinite cardinal there exists a strictly larger one. From this I am tempted to conclude that no infinite collection can ever be genuinely completed, because any candidate for a maximal infinity can be mapped onto a proper subset within a larger cardinal, and within the hotel itself the fully-occupied state is perpetually susceptible to internal expansion. But this appears to turn Hilbert's hotel into an argument for finitism rather than against it: if actual infinity requires a stable, completed totality, and both the internal dynamics of the hotel and the external Cantorian hierarchy show that no such stability is possible, then actual infinity seems incoherent.

Yet mathematicians and philosophers routinely treat Hilbert's hotel as a benign illustration of Dedekind-infinity, not as a reductio of actual infinity. So my question is whether my reasoning rests on a subtle category mistake between the structural property of being Dedekind-infinite and the metaphysical claim that a collection is ontologically incomplete, or whether there is a deeper error in thinking that Cantor's absence of a largest cardinal undermines the possibility of a specific actually infinite set being completed. I want to understand precisely where the confusion lies, whether it is a genuine tension that finitists ought to exploit, and how to disentangle cardinal comparison from questions of ontological completeness without simply begging the question against the finitist.
