(1) im a compatibilist about free will - determinism doesnt automatically kill it, what matters is being responsive to reasons in the right way.
(2) i think sphexishness is a real and deep problem: the sphex wasp dragging prey to a moved burrow is mechanically rigid in a way that clearly rules out free will.
(3) but i also think flexibility - adapting to genuinely novel situations - is necessary for free will, though not enough by itself.
(4) for the full human version i think you need hierarchical endorsement, like frankfurt-style second-order desires that align with your first-order ones.
(5) at the same time i think crows and maybe octopuses show enough behavioral flexibility that they have some thin or minimal free will, not the full human kind but more than zero.
(6) i also hold that if an agents flexibility is just the deterministic output of a learning algorithm with no meta-cognitive access to its own goals, then its really just sophisticated sphexishness and doesnt ground even thin free will.
(7) and i want to resist evolutionary debunking arguments about free will by saying sphexishness is an empirical architectural claim, not a normative one that evolution could undermine.

does this actually cohere? im especially worried about (1) and (6) - if determinism is ok for free will, why does deterministic flexibility fail? and about (4) and (5) - if hierarchy is required for free will, how can crows have any? but also (2) seems to conflict with (1) since the wasp is deterministic too, right? tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which ones only look bad, and rank them by severity. for each tension say what has to give and whats the cheapest fix. dont just list them all as equal.
