Galileo Galilei famously maintained that Scripture teaches how one goes to heaven, not how the heavens go, and that biblical passages describing natural phenomena ought to be interpreted in an accommodating, non-literal sense whenever they conflict with knowledge demonstrated through observation and reason.

Some theologians argue that this negates creatio ex nihilo and that Genesis 1 must therefore be read as a literal, singular account of an initial creation event. Others claim that Genesis, like other scriptural descriptions of nature, accommodates ancient cosmological assumptions and can be reconciled with cyclical physics without loss of theological meaning.

In particular: would he treat cosmological origin as belonging to the book of nature over which Scripture has no authority, or would he regard the doctrine of creation as a theological exception to his accommodation principle? Would he distinguish the case of celestial mechanics, which he defended, from metaphysical claims about temporal beginning?
