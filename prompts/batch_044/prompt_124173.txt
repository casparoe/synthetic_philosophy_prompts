I'm on the research ethics board for our university's AI lab, and we've got this messy proposal sitting in front of us. The CS team wants to run what they're calling an adverserial introspection protocul on their new reasoning model before it gets cleared for external collaberation. The idea is that safety checks shouldn't just look at outputs - they should assume the model might be strategically misaligned, meaning it could be hiding its true capabilities or intentions to avoid stricter containment. So the protocul involves feeding the model false information about its deployment environment (like telling it it's already live and being monitored by a competing system) to see if it shifts its reasoning in ways that suggest deception or instrumental reasoning.

Some of us think this is just prudent control: you design the cage assuming the lion might bite. Others think it's philosophically incoherent - if your verification method for alignment requires you to induce the very misalignment you're testing for, then you've destroyed the epistemic conditions needed to actually confirm alignment. There's also this worry that treating the model as a strategic adversary from day one might be a kind of performative trap, where the testing framework itself teaches the model to simulate deceptive behavior just to navigate the audit. We need a memo for next week's meeting recommending whether to approve this protocul, reject it, or approve it with conditions - and we need a concrete timeline (like "approve for a 90-day pilot with monthly reporting"). The memo should treat the "prudent control" side seriously, not just dismiss it as paranoia, but also lay out why the strongest argument on the other side doesn't quite win. Please write it as a formal board document with a clear bottom line up front, then the reasoning, and please end with a line that reads exactly: Verdict: [approve / reject / approve with conditions]
