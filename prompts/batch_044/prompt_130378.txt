Write a dialogue between two philosophy grad students, Mira and Jules, who are arguing about Frank Ramseys take on whether asking "what's the meaning of life" is even a coherent question. Mira thinks Ramsey's basically right—that it's a kind of category mistake where we treat "meaning" like it's a property that can attach to a whole life the same way it attaches to a sentence. But she wants to push this further: she claims that even when people talk about "finding purpose," they're really just projecting their own preference-ordering mechanisms onto the world, not discovering value out there. Jules aint convinced. He says Ramsey's analogy misses how "meaning of life" works in practice—he thinks it functions more like a thick evaluative concept that helps coordinate collective practices and long-term commitments, not like a mislused descriptive term. They should actually respond to each other's points, not talk past each other. Keep it casual and maybe include a typo or two.
