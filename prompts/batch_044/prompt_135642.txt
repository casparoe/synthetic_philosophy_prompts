I'm working up a position for a reading group that tries to thread the needle between subjectivism about meaning and the full existential force of absurdism, and I want a hard consistency audit before I present it. Here is the package:

(1) Meaning is subjective but constrained: a life is meaningful to the degree that its bearer is stably engaged in projects that survive reflective scrutiny from a diachronic evaluative stance — endorsement by one's later self assessing the trajectory, not merely present satisfaction.

(2) The absurd is real and inescapable: it is not an epistemic failure but the unavoidable collision between our normative demand for justification and the silent indifference of the universe.

(3) The correct response is rebellion rather than resignation or transcendent hope: we should pursue our projects with full awareness of their groundlessness, not paper over the gap.

(4) Nevertheless, stable engagement of the kind required by (1) functionally depends on maintaining an as-if belief in the objective worth of one's projects; if the groundlessness becomes affectively dominant — if it is not merely acknowledged intellectually but emotionally internalized — the motivational structure collapses.

(5) I reject objective-value theories of meaning, including those that ground meaning in independently valuable projects or in supernaturalist framing, on debunking grounds: our intuitions about objective value are adaptive projections rather than perceptions, so they cannot serve as foundations.

Which pairs are actually in tension, and which only appear to be? Specifically, does (3) conflict with (4) in a way that makes rebellion self-undermining? Does (1) depend on a kind of self-deception that (3) forbids? Is the tension between (2) and (1) merely apparent, dissolving once we distinguish cosmic justification from diachronic coherence? Rank the tensions by severity, specify what each resolution would cost, and identify the single cheapest revision that cleans up the most conflicts. Be precise about what pulls against what rather than flagging everything.
