What do you think David Lewis—given his commitment to Humean supervenience, his best-system account of laws, and his rejection of primitive modal connections—would have said about the post-2000 revival of dispositional essentialism? Specifically, consider the view that fundamental properties are essentially powers (for instance, that charge is essentially disposed to repel like charges), so that lawlike regularities are grounded in the intrinsic nature of properties rather than in global patterns of categorical distribution. Would Lewis have regarded this as a notational variant of his own framework with modal predicates added, or as a genuine rival that violates Humean supervenience? Respond in one paragraph of no more than 180 words, without citing specific texts or papers; reason only from the philosophical commitments he is known to have held.
