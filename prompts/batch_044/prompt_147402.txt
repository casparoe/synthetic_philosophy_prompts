I have come to accept the view that democratic backsliding is an analytically coherent, non-partisan category rather than a rhetorical weapon, because it tracks a specific directional mechanism rather than a policy outcome: the incremental aggrandizement of executive authority achieved through the strategic weakening or capture of horizontal accountability institutions—judicial review bodies, electoral management authorities, anti-corruption agencies—while the formal architecture of democratic procedure remains intact. The relevance of this mechanism across ideologically disparate cases—Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the contested American debates—suggests that the concept captures a structural logic that operates independently of left-right content. I want to treat this not as settled doctrine but as a position genuinely exposed to refutation.

What would have to be true for this framework to fail? I am asking for an honest map of its exposure, not a rehearsal of partisan talking points. Which specific comparative observations, institutional configurations, or conceptual distinctions would genuinely undermine it? For instance, suppose there existed a sustained "reverse-Poland" trajectory in which judicial restructuring and executive consolidation actually strengthened democratic accountability and popular sovereignty—would that collapse the directional claim, or could defenders absorb it by refining the mechanism? Would evidence that horizontal-accountability erosion occurs with equal frequency in stable democracies undergoing routine constitutional renovation be fatal, or merely require a boundary condition? And is there any development—say, a demonstration that the formal-shell maintenance condition is compatible with democratic deepening rather than erosion—that defenders of the backsliding framework would themselves concede counts against their view, rather than dismissing as misapplication? Please distinguish the defeaters that would be decisive from those that are merely awkward, rank them in order of severity, and say whether the position risks anything substantive or has been insulated by qualification to the point of unfalsifiability.
