Second, the contemporaneous "civilizing mission" rhetoric that used the very same universalist language to justify imposing European legal and religious norms on colonized societies—this strikes me as domination, even though structurally it also applies reason across cultural boundaries. Third, Beccaria's arguments for penal reform, which replaced arbitrary sovereign punishment with predictable, proportionate rules—progress-promoting, I think, despite the fact that such rationalization also expanded the state's disciplinary reach. Fourth, the development of state census and statistical classification systems in the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries—domination-promoting, in my view, because they made populations administratively legible for extraction and control, even though they also enabled democratic representation. Fifth, the Encyclopédie's project of systematizing craft and technical knowledge for a broad literate public—progress-promoting, because it distributed cognitive tools downward rather than concentrating them.
