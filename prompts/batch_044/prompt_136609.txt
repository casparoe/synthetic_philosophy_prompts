I'm writing the final exam for an upper-level metaphysics and epistemology course. I want one essay question on Descartes's cogito that students cannot answer by merely reciting "I think, therefore I am" and summarizing standard textbook objections. The question should force them to address exactly what existence-claim the cogito establishes—whether it proves a substantial thinking thing, only a bare occurrence of a mental event, or an intermediate indexical reference—and to evaluate whether the "thin" occurrence-reading can still perform the foundational work Descartes requires for his criterion of clear and distinct perception. It should also require them to examine the performative-contradiction interpretation, according to which denying one's own thinking is self-refuting in the very act of denial, and to assess whether that interpretation genuinely escapes the circularity charge that threatens the thicker substantial-self reading. The deliverable should include: the exam question itself, calibrated for a 90-minute response; a model answer of approximately 600 words; and a marking scheme built around five specific argumentative moves that a top answer must contain, with marks assigned to each (total 20), together with a one-line note on how candidates typically fail on each point. Please do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and ensure the question rewards conceptual precision over biographical or textual recall.
