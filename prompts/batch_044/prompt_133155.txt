In the microdomain where philosophy of science intersects with computational modeling, I have noticed a recurring pattern with the term "interventionist explanation." Originally, within structural causal modeling, it denotes an account that specifies which variables remain invariant under a precise class of surgical interventions. When philosophers of science adopted the term, they often preserved the vocabulary—speaking of interventionist tests and invariance—yet relaxed the formal conditions, treating any counterfactual dependency as sufficient. More recently, I have seen computational practitioners borrow this philosophical usage back into technical discourse, applying "interventionist explanation" to models that pass qualitative counterfactual checks but fail the quantitative invariance conditions the original framework required. The concept has thus completed a round trip: it retains the authority and syntactic form of its technical origin, yet its discriminative boundary conditions have been progressively eroded. I can point to concrete manifestations—cases in mechanistic interpretability where invariance is replaced by post-hoc salience mapping, and cases in philosophy of medicine where surgical intervention is analogized to policy change without structural equation constraints—where the term functions more as a credential than as a criterion. Is there an established concept that captures exactly this dynamic: not mere ambiguity, not Wittgensteinian family resemblance, not the strategic equivocation of motte-and-bailey, but the reflexive dilution that occurs when a concept migrates from a technical home domain into an adjacent interpretive community and then returns, carrying with it a loss of boundary conditions? If no standard label exists, I would like a precise delineation: what pragmatic or formal markers distinguish this "copycat" state—where the concept mimics the original's prestige but has lost its exclusionary power—from ordinary conceptual stretching, and at what point does the slippage become practically irreversible?
