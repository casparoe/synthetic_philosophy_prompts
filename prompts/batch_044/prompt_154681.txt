I'm working through a problem in the philosophy of explanation that combines Dennett's cranes-versus-skyhooks framework with the charge of greedy reductionism, and I've built an argument that seems to prove psychology must be eliminated entirely if we take both commitments seriously. I was discussing this with a friend who works on cognitive architecture, and I can't find the exit. Here is the argument. (1) Every legitimate reduction must be a crane: it explains the higher-level phenomenon solely by the organized interaction of lower-level parts, with no unexplained capacities smuggled in at any stage. (2) Greedy reductionism attempts to collapse intermediate levels into a single jump; but such a jump is not a true crane, because the missing rungs leave an explanatory gap that must be filled by an implicit skyhook—some capacity that does the work the skipped levels should have done. (3) Therefore, to avoid both skyhooks and greedy reduction, every intermediate explanatory level must be fully accounted for by the level immediately beneath it, leaving no predictive or explanatory residue. (4) Psychology—beliefs, desires, and other intentional states—is multiply realizable across different neural architectures, so no specific neural-level description fully accounts for a given psychological state; there is always residue that psychological vocabulary captures and neuroscience alone does not. (5) By (3), that residue makes psychology either a skyhook or a level that must be skipped in a greedy leap from neuroscience straight to behavior. (6) Since we reject both skyhooks and greedy reductionism, psychology must be eliminated from our ontology. (7) But that is absurd: we clearly need psychological explanation for predicting action and for understanding reasons. Where does this go wrong? I want to keep (1) and (4) if I can, since both the crane constraint and multiple realizability seem well motivated, but I'm willing to give up (2) or (3) if the cost is lower. Please separate what is settled about the crane/skyhook distinction and about the status of multiply realizable kinds from what is genuinely contested among philosophers of mind who know the literature—especially whether multiple realizability actually blocks downward explanation or merely complicates it—and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
