I'm in a philosophy of mind seminar and we're arguing about eliminative materialism—like the Churchlands' claim that beliefs and desires are going to go the way of phlogiston. So I want an essay that takes this distinction seriously: can eliminative materialism survive once we seperate its ontological rejection from its linguistic practice? Does that separation save it from self-refutation, or does it collapse the view into something like expressivism or instrumentalism about mental talk? Weigh the arguments, consider whether the pragmatic defense actually addresses the normative-reasoning problem, and reach an actual conclusion—don't just end on "it depends"—justifying whether the view remains genuinely radical or was always less threatening than advertised.
