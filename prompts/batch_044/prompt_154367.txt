Consider Marx's analysis of real subsumption and the transformation of labor under machinery in the Grundrisse, especially his argument that automation elevates science and collective knowledge into direct productive forces while reducing the worker to a supervisory function. If one takes seriously the idea that direct labor time thereby becomes a negligible measure of wealth, it seems to follow that in a fully automated capitalist system surplus-value extraction should become technically impossible, since there is almost no living labor left to appropriate. Nevertheless, Marx insists that capitalism will perish through internal crisis rather than through a smooth technological transition to post-scarcity abundance. How is this tension to be resolved? Does Marx hold that the social form of valorization can persist even when labor has been quantitatively minimized, or is there a different conceptual error in the inference that automation dissolves exploitation?
