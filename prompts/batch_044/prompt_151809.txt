(1) Any formal system strong enough to cover basic arithmatic is either inconsistant or contains true statements it can't prove.

(3) A trained mathematician can look at Godel's sentence for F and recognize it's true, even though F itself can't prove it.

(4) So the brain produces a true belief that F can't derive, which means the brain isn't operating inside F after all.

(6) But the same logic applies to any computer we could build, and to quantum processors if we formalize their dynamics, so either all physical devices are permanently blind to certain arithmatic truths, or minds involve something non-physical.

(7) That second option smells like dualism, which I don't want to buy, but if I reject (5) then Gödel doesn't show any real limit on mechanization.

Where did I go wrong? Is the mistake in assuming the brain runs one fixed F, or in thinking that "seeing the truth" counts as part of the mechanism's output?
