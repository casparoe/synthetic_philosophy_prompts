Give an example of a concrete sequential decision problem—set in public-health resource allocation or climate-adaptation planning, not in financial markets—where uncertainty about a single quantitative parameter is represented by an imprecise credal set (for instance, an interval of probabilities or a convex family of distributions) rather than by a point estimate. Specify the decision context precisely: the two competing actions, the outcomes that depend on the parameter, and the payoff or welfare structure. Then demonstrate that applying an ambiguity-averse selection rule—optimizing against the worst case within the credal set—recommends action A before any new evidence arrives, but that after receiving genuinely informative evidence which symmetrically narrows the interval without shifting its center, the worst-case scenario migrates and the same rule now recommends action B, despite the fact that the agent’s beliefs about every individual outcome have moved in directions that, intuitively, should not favor B over A. Provide the specific numerical bounds and outcomes that make this reversal transparent. Finally, diagnose the conceptual issue at stake: is the reversal a failure of dynamic consistency peculiar to imprecise-probability frameworks; does it show that narrowing a credal set is structurally different from Bayesian conditioning; or does it indicate that rational choice under ambiguity requires resolving all imprecision into a precise prior before decision? Focus on the logical structure of the example rather than on citations or historical attribution.
