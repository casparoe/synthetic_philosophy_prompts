I'm a high school student drafting an essay for my philosophy elective on Frank Jackson's knowledge argument. I want to argue that what she acquires is not a new propositional fact—she already knew that neural pattern in exhaustive detail—but rather a new functional capacity or causal power: the ability to generate and recognize that state from the inside. From this I sketch three claims: first, that Jackson's epistemic gap depends on Mary encountering color through ordinary perceptual channels; second, that direct stimulation blurs the line between knowing-that and knowing-how, since Mary can now trigger the state herself without learning any further physics; and third, that the argument therefore shows at most that physicalism is incomplete about abilities or skills, not about factual knowledge.

Could you comment on these notes? In particular: Is my distinction between propositional knowledge and functional capacity philosophically coherent, or does it merely rename the phenomenal problem? Does altering Mary's "exit" condition in this way misrepresent Jackson's core point about the nature of phenomenal character?
