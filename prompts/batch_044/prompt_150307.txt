Write a joke centered on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. The humor should depend on the theory's actual structure: the claim that introspection systematically misrepresents experience as possessing non-physical phenomenal properties that are not actually instantiated, rather than merely denying that consciousness exists at all. To keep it from being generic, frame the joke as a brief exchange in which an illusionist tries to persuade a skeptical friend that the friend's own vivid report of the subjective character of feeling cold is not counterevidence but precisely the kind of introspective misrepresentation the theory predicts. The punchline should turn specifically on the difference between illusionism and eliminativism—namely, that the illusionist fully accepts there is an experience of cold but denies that it carries any irreducible phenomenal ontology. Make it intelligible to someone who knows that distinction, but do not ask for citations, named authors, or bibliographic references.
