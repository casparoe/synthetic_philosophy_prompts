hey so i've been thinking about this weird split in public health. we medicalize obesity and pre-diabetes with ICD codes, drugs, and even surgeries, but chronic loneliness—which meta-analyses tie to mortality risk comparable to smoking—gets handled through UK-style "social prescribing" or just ignored. there's no "pre-loneliness" diagnosis, no billing code for isolation.

what view of health and disease does that institutional split presuppose? is it basically naturalist, that disease needs a biological dysfunction you can measure on a blood pannel, so loneliness doesn't count? or is it normativist, where we call something a disease when we think medicine should fix it, but call it a social problem when fixing it requires restructuring community? and can the practice be explained by either view, or does it strictly require one? also, if we started treating cortisol or oxytocin patterns as biomarker proof of a "social deficit disorder," what in the current practice would have to change?

just want comments on whether the implicit theory holds together, not a reading list.
