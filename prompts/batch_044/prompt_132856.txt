hey, i'm drafting a short essay about the control vs alignment split in AI safety and i'm stuck on the middle part. here's the gist:

everyone frames control as "the model wants to do X but we box it" and alignment as "the model wants to do X and we change what it wants." but i think both assume this weird folk-psychology where the model has stable "wants" that exist independently of how we probe it. in reality, a lot of control measures (tripwires, sandboxing, output filters) don't just block behavior; they reshape the training distribution in ways that look a lot like alignment work. meanwhile alignment techniques like RLHF often work less by "changing values" and more by making certain outputs statisticaly invisible to the model's own reasoning trace.

so i want to propose a different axis: not control vs alignment, but "behavorial containment" vs "representational truncation." containment is classic control: you keep the agent in a box. truncation is when you design the architecture so it literally can't form the internal representations that would lead to harmful planning — not because it's aligned, but because the concept space is missing pieces. i call this "structural deafness." its not capability restriction in the usual sense because the model can still talk about harm in abstract terms; it just can't assemble them into an operational plan because the linking representations are absent.

my question is whether this is actually distinct from just saying "we made it incapable of planning," and whether it avoids the usual objection that any truncation can be patched by a smart enough optimizer. also, does this framing help at all, or am i just relabeling old ideas?

what do you think? specifically: is my characterization of RLHF as statistical invisibility fair? is structural deafness coherent or just fantasy? and if you think the essay is interesting, where should i push it — toward technical architecture or toward the philosophy of mind stuff about whether LLMs have "wants" at all?
