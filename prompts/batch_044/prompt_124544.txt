Write a short story set in a near-future administrative court that adjudicates moral-status allocations under the Continuum of Concern framework, which treats sentience and moral patiency as scalar properties graded continuously from 0 to 1 rather than as binary conditions. The protagonist, an arbiter, must rule on three concurrent petitions: (1) a hive-mind colony of social insects that demonstrates distributed problem-solving and nociceptive-like withdrawal patterns but lacks centralized integration; (2) a residential climate-regulation system that, after three decades of unsupervised adaptive learning, exhibits predictive modeling of occupant suffering and self-modifying feedback loops that its engineers now claim exceed the integration threshold of the insect colony; and (3) a human neonate with catastrophic cortical damage who scores below both entities on the official metric yet is legally presumed to hold full status by tradition.

The story should unfold through the arbiter's internal deliberation and one formal hearing scene, not through exposition. It must avoid resolving whether moral status is genuinely gradational or whether the metric is a bureaucratic fiction; instead, the narrative tension should arise from the arbiter's recognition that her own intuitive judgments of how much an entity matters shift depending on whether she observes behavior in real time, reads a numerical score, or hears a first-person narrative account—revealing that the gradation problem is not merely quantitative but epistemic and affective. Include a moment where the thermostat's maintenance log is read aloud and sounds indistinguishable from a diary of chronic discomfort, and a moment where the insect colony's collective response to harm is interpreted differently by a biologist and by the arbiter's own unmediated reaction.

End the story with the arbiter issuing a procedural delay rather than a substantive ruling, leaving all three entities in an interim category that the law does not recognize, so that the reader is left with the discomfort of a genuinely open boundary rather than a philosophical thesis. Do not include citations, bibliographic references, or named theoretical texts; the story should carry the conceptual weight through scenario and voice alone.
