I'm working through some issues in my dissertation on analogical reasoning, and I keep returning to Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model of creativity—the idea that conceptual spaces are organized around an invariant theme with adjustable parameters, and that creative thought consists in turning those knobs rather than jumping to an entirely different framework. I was trying to formalize this for my advisor, and I ended up with an argument that seems to show the model either eliminates revolutionary creativity or makes itself unfalsifiable. I can't locate the error, and I want to know whether I'm misunderstanding the mechanics or whether one of the premises actually has to go.

Here is the chain as I wrote it down. (1) If the knob-twiddling model is correct, then every creative act is exhaustively describable as parameter adjustment within a fixed conceptual frame, where the frame defines both the invariant theme and the set of available knobs. (2) A genuinely revolutionary creative achievement—say, the shift from tonal harmony to twelve-tone serialism, or the reconceptualization of geometric space that made non-Euclidean mathematics possible—appears to involve not just turning existing knobs but reconceiving which dimensions are variable at all; call this a meta-variation. (3) By the model's own lights, if a meta-variation changes the invariant theme, then it is not parameter adjustment within the frame but frame-replacement, and therefore falls outside the domain of the model. (4) If, on the other hand, we reconstruct the post-revolutionary framework as just a new theme with its own knobs, then any conceptual change whatsoever can be redescribed post hoc as knob-twiddling on some reconstructed frame. (5) Therefore, either revolutionary creativity is impossible within the model (all apparent revolutions are just extreme parameter adjustments), or the model applies to every conceptual change and is therefore vacuous. (6) But revolutionary creativity is real and distinguishable from routine optimization; we do not want to say that inventing atonal music is the same kind of act as transposing a melody up a fifth. (7) So the knob-twiddling model must be rejected, at least as an account of high-level human creativity.

My instinct is that step (3) is doing illegitimate work by treating the theme as psychologically fixed rather than analytically reconstructed, but if I reject (3) I seem to lose the distinction that makes the model explanatory in the first place. Alternatively, maybe (2) mischaracterizes meta-variations: perhaps reconceiving the dimensions is just turning a hidden meta-knob that was always part of the frame. If that's the move, then I need to know what constrains the space of meta-knobs, otherwise I'm back at vacuity. Which premise should I actually give up, and what does surrendering it cost the model?
