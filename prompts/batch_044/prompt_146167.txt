For a workshop on democratic theory I am drafting my philosophical profile on constitutional precommitment and self-binding, and I want a consistency audit before presenting. Here are my commitments:

(1) A democratic constitution may legitimately entrench procedural mechanisms—supermajority rules, judicial review structures, or independent institutions—that constrain how future majorities reach decisions, provided the entrenchment was adopted through an inclusive democratic process.

(2) No democratic generation has the moral authority to bind future generations to specific substantive outcomes or values, because democratic self-government requires that the living remain able, in principle, to revise the rules that govern them.

(3) Ulysses contracts—rational self-binding against anticipated preference reversals—are justified only when the reversal is driven by predictable cognitive distortion (panic, addiction, short-term bias) rather than by genuine value change or the acquisition of new evidence.

(4) Emergency constitutional provisions that permit temporary suspension of ordinary rights during crises are best interpreted as legitimate Ulysses contracts: they bind the polity against its predictable tendency to overreact, scapegoat minorities, or abandon liberal norms under stress.

(5) If an agent knows her future self might come to hold different but equally reasonable values, she ought not bind that future self; treating a contingent value shift as a defect to be preempted is a mistake.

(6) The distinction between procedural precommitment (binding decision-rules) and substantive precommitment (binding outcomes) is morally decisive, and only procedural forms are compatible with democratic legitimacy.

Please examine these for joint tenability. Identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve with a clarification, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each serious tension, specify the cheapest revision—whether it requires modifying (1), (4), or (5)—and state precisely what is lost. Also assess whether the package as a whole is closer to a coherent "soft precommitment" view or to an unstable hybrid, and identify which single commitment, if retained unchanged, does the most damage to consistency with the others.
