I'm taking a graduate seminar in classical Indian philosophy, and we've spent the term working through Nāgārjuna's Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, particularly the tetralemma (catuṣkoṭi) and the doctrine of emptiness (śūnyatā). I've come to defend what my professor calls a strictly therapeutic interpretation: that śūnyatā is not an ontological thesis about the absence of inherent existence (svabhāva), but rather a pragmatic, dialectical instrument designed to dissolve conceptual proliferation (prapañca) without committing the Madhyamaka to any metaphysical position—including the position that entities ultimately do not exist. On this reading, the fourfold negation operates as a reductio applied to all four corners of the tetralemma (is, is not, both, neither) not in order to establish a hidden fifth position, emptiness, but to exhaust the conceptual framework itself and leave only conventional, dependently arisen discourse (saṃvṛti-satya).

What I want is an honest map of what would genuinely count against this therapeutic reading—what textual evidence, philosophical consequences, or internal tensions would undermine it rather than merely complicate it? Specifically, I want you to assess whether the reading can survive Nāgārjuna's explicit denial of svabhāva in works traditionally attributed to him, and whether it risks collapsing into either a disguised nihilism (if emptiness is interpreted as the non-existence of everything) or a hidden essentialism (if the therapeutic stance itself presupposes a fixed nature of cognition or language). I also want you to rank the potential defeaters: for instance, the charge that this reading makes Madhyamaka indistinguishable from Pyrrhonian skepticism; the problem of accounting for the two-truths framework without smuggling in ontological distinctions after all; and the objection that if no position is ultimately tenable, then the very claim that all positions are empty is self-undermining. Which of these would a committed therapeutic interpreter concede as genuinely damaging, and which would they dismiss as category errors?

Please distinguish decisive defeaters from merely awkward ones, and say where the reasoning is most exposed—perhaps in the move from conventional truth to ultimate truth, or in explaining why the therapeutic method doesn't itself become a metaphysical commitment. Finally, tell me exactly what argument or textual finding would change your mind about the viability of this interpretation, and identify the point in your reasoning where you are least confident or most likely to fail.
