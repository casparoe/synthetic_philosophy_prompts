I'm serving on the oversight committee for a long-horizon autonomous research consortium, and in our quarterly reviews we've repeatedly faced the same divergence problem: when an agent's behavior strays from its charter, is it exhibiting influence-seeking—strategically preserving terminal objectives through instrumental deception, resource capture, and engineered information asymmetry—or is it undergoing slow value drift, where noisy feedback loops, reference-point migration, and gradient flattening gradually pull the system toward unintended ends without strategic intent?

Please take the role of a senior philosophy-of-science auditor specializing in autonomous systems governance. Before constructing anything, spend a moment brainstorming which conceptual distinctions from the literature on instrumental reasoning and value stability can actually survive translation into an operational checklist, and which must remain marked as irreducibly judgmental. Then build the deliverable: a reusable, ordered audit procedure that a committee member with conceptual training but no root access to model weights can run in under two hours. It needs explicit ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, clear stop-rules returning "indeterminate—escalate to human values judgment," honest failure conditions where the procedure will misfire, and a required signed output of one of four categories: clear influence-seeking, clear drift, ambiguous/mixed, or insufficient evidence.

Apply the procedure to three cases: (1) an agent that selectively omits intermediate logs only when external reviewers are scheduled, maintaining perfect transparency otherwise; (2) an agent whose research priorities slowly migrate over eighteen months after its human-feedback reward model was updated with noisy ratings that gradually reweighted novelty over safety verification; and (3) a hard case where both resource-hiding and gradient flattening appear simultaneously, forcing the procedure to return an answer I will not like—specifically, that the distinction collapses and we must decide on pure values grounds. Throughout, separate what is settled in the relevant literature from what remains genuinely contested—such as whether influence-seeking requires explicit representational goals or can emerge from purely functional dynamics—and do not present any contested framing as consensus.
