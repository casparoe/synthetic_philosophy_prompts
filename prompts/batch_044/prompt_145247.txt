I have been examining Edmund Gettier's well-known counterexamples to the analysis of knowledge as justified true belief, and it seems to me that they all depend on a single hidden mechanism: the subject reaches a true conclusion by deducing it from a false premise via an otherwise sound rule. If we simply adopt the principle that justification is not transmitted through false premises, then in every classic case the belief in the conclusion is not genuinely justified after all. On this view, Gettier has not undermined the JTB analysis itself; he has merely shown that justification is not closed under valid deduction when one of the premises is false. Given that this resolution seems available, why do epistemologists continue to treat Gettier's result as a direct defeat of JTB, rather than as a correction to our account of justification-transfer? Please respond in British English, and explain whether my framing misses something important about the relationship between justification, truth, and belief.
