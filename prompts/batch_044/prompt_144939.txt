Write a two-page proposal for a six-month independent research fellowship on a conceptual problem arising from three familiar failures of Athenian democratic deliberation: the assembly’s decision to execute the Mytileneans and its reversal the next day, the vote to launch the Sicilian Expedition despite prior strategic warnings, and the trial and execution of Socrates. Existing scholarship tends to treat these as separate pathologies—capricious emotional reversal, imperial overreach fueled by demagoguery, and populist scapegoating of an intellectual dissident. Your proposal should frame a single precise question that binds them together: is there a shared structural feature in democratic deliberation, distinct from both individual irrationality and standard institutional design flaws, that makes an assembly systematically vulnerable to committing irreversible catastrophic judgments precisely when it believes it is exercising its most legitimate collective authority? State the question so that it admits a determinate yes-or-no answer supported by conceptual analysis rather than new archival discovery. Explain why current explanations—whether they invoke Thucydidean realism, Platonic anti-democratic psychology, or modern social-choice theory—leave this specific feature unexamined, and describe the approach you would take: constructing an analytical model of deliberative dynamics that treats the three episodes as controlled variations, identifying the conditions under which collective emotional framing becomes self-validating and resistant to subsequent correction, and testing whether that model distinguishes democratic failure from comparable non-democratic decision-making in other ancient poleis. Specify what the deliverable would look like for a funding board—an explicit framework with clear vulnerability conditions, not a historical narrative—and assess honestly what six months can realistically cover, noting where expanding the comparison to Roman assemblies or modern analogues would make the project too large. Finally, include an honest failure condition: what it would mean for the project to collapse, such as the three cases sharing only superficial narrative similarities or the proposed feature reducing to the standard explanations it aims to transcend. Write the entire proposal in continuous prose only, with no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables, as if composing a polished letter or email; do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations, though you may refer to well-known positions and arguments in general terms and note that full references will be supplied in the final draft.
