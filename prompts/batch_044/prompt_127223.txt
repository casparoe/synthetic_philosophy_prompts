I run a grad workshop on logic and metaphysics — about 12 people, 75 minutes. Design a live role-play that makes them feel the specific paradox in Death in Damascus, not just generic fatalism. The twist: the prophecy is indexical, so it points at wherever you're actively trying to avoid. Half the group are travelers with secret destination cards; half are oracle-messengers who reveal rules mid-game. The rules should mechanically backfire: if a traveler takes an explicit action to avoid Damascus, that choice closes every other exit, leaving Damascus as the only open path. Use a small point payoff so they actually care about escaping. Include roles, a timeline, the payoff scheme, and a debrief that maps each mechanical backfire onto the logical structure — belief reshapes the option space, avoidance is self-defeating. Be honest about what the game can't reproduce: probably the modal logic of counterfactual stability, which you'll have to supply verbally. Also tell me what predictibly goes wrong on the first run — people blaming the rules or spotting the trick too early. Don't lecture up front, and don't name the paradox until after they've played.
