Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of moral compromise between genuinely incompatible value systems must satisfy, with the conceptual motivation behind each and a clear marking of which are non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences. The theory should account for how agents operating within distinct normative frameworks—such as a rights-based deontological system and a longtermist consequentialist system—can achieve stable, non-coercive cooperation that yields mutual gains without requiring either party to abandon its core commitments. Your specification should cover at least the following: (i) a criterion that distinguishes authentic moral compromise from mere strategic concession, bribery, or domination; (ii) a requirement regarding the preservation of internal coherence within each value system after a compromise is struck; (iii) a condition addressing whether gains from trade can be recognized and distributed across frameworks that deny the commensurability of moral reasons; (iv) a constraint on cases where one framework classifies the issue at stake as a matter of justice or entitlement while the other classifies it as charity, supererogation, or permissible variation; and (v) an assessment of whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether incommensurability claims necessarily undermine the stability of cooperation—together with the reasoning behind that assessment. Keep the emphasis on the criteria and the cases that motivate them rather than on scoring particular philosophers, though a brief indication of how contractualist, value-pluralist, and virtue-ethical approaches fare against one or two requirements is welcome. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citation.
