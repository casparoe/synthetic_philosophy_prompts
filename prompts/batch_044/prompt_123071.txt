I am preparing the capstone assignment for my graduate seminar on existentialist ethics, and I have become convinced that students can summarize Kierkegaard’s account of Abraham with perfect fluency while still missing the structural scandal at its center. To force a more precise engagement, I want you to produce a deadpan bureaucratic parody that treats the teleological suspension of the ethical as if it were a routine institutional exemption. Specifically, write a completed university Institutional Review Board form—titled Application for Temporary Suspension of Universal Ethical Obligations on Teleological Grounds—submitted by Abraham, or by a faculty sponsor acting on his behalf, to justify the planned binding of Isaac on Mount Moriah. The form should include standard administrative sections: Project Title, Principal Investigator and Credentials, Brief Summary of Procedure, Risk-Benefit Analysis, Statement of Conflict with Universal Moral Law, Justification of Teleological Suspension, Measures to Protect the Subject (Isaac), Expected Outcomes, and a Compliance Officer’s Footnote. The voice must remain entirely deadpan; the philosophical bite should come from the collision between administrative rationality and Kierkegaard’s claim that faith establishes an absolute relation to the absolute that suspends, without abolishing, the ethical universal.

The satire must be grounded in the actual architecture of Kierkegaard’s argument, not in a caricature of it. You should clearly distinguish the knight of infinite resignation from the knight of faith; you should embed a bureaucratic attempt to classify Abraham’s action under a general rule—such as potential child endangerment or religious extremism—and show why such classification necessarily fails; and you should include at least one deadpan reference to the problem that the ethical universal, embodied in Isaac’s rights or in the community’s norms, cannot adjudicate the particular case of the single individual before God. The form should be readable to someone who knows nothing of Kierkegaard, but a specialist should recognize that you understand exactly what is being suspended and why the suspension is not mere lawlessness. The parody should be approximately 650 words, formatted as numbered sections with blank fields filled in Abraham’s voice.
