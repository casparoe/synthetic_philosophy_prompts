A colleague in the history dept summed up the revisionist line for me over coffee, and I want to check whether he’s being fair or just being contrarian. He said: 'Medieval people never thought they lived in a "Middle" Age; they saw themselves as Romans or Christians in an unbroken world. The Renaissance was basically Vasari’s marketing brand for Florentine art, and Burckhardt turned it into a myth of individualism in his 1860 book. Then the so-called Scientific Revolution wasn’t a revolution at all—Shapin and Schaffer showed Boyle’s air-pump experiments were really social performances for establishing gentlemanly credibility, not objective tests, and there was never a shared method linking Galileo, Newton, and the alchemists. So the whole tri-partite scheme obscures the real story, which is that change was always local, continuous, and driven by shifts in what some sociologists now call epistemic regimes—competing institutional ways of legitimizing belief—rather than by grand intellectual breaks.'
