Given Bentham's commitment to the principle of utility, his well-known rejection of "natural rights" rhetoric, and his specific claim that law should punish conduct only when it causes demonstrable harm to others, how would he likely assess these practices? Would he treat algorithmic mediation as a neutral efficiency that expands choice, or as a novel source of manipulation and informational harm requiring regulatory intervention? And would he extend contractual freedom to plural domestic arrangements on the grounds that voluntary cooperation maximizes aggregate welfare, or would he worry about hidden power asymmetries and the difficulty of ensuring genuinely informed consent in complex, long-term networks?

Please reconstruct the reasoning by separating what is relatively settled about Bentham's documented philosophical commitments—such as his harm-based criterion for legislation and his skepticism toward moralistic interference—from what remains genuinely speculative extrapolation. Be explicit about where scholars of Bentham's ethics disagree, and do not present any contested interpretation of his views on sexuality, contract, or state power as if it were the consensus position.
