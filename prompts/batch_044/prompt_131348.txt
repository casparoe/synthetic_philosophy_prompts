ive come around to a weird view about sphexishness and free will that i cant shake. basically: mechanical rigidity isnt the opposite of freedom, its the substrate. human action is just nested sphexishness — rigid subrutines (habits, reflexes, heuristics) being rearranged by other rigid subrutines (deliberation, long-term planning). the flexibility is real but its structural, not fundamental. so free will debates have been asking the wrong question; what matters isnt whether were deterministic, but whether our nesting is deep enough to support moral blame.

what would actually count against this? not the usual indeterminism-saves-us line — i want defeaters aimed at the nesting claim itself. maybe results showing deliberation isnt just a higher-order loop, or that responsibility requires a kind of openness that cant be decomposed into subrutines. rank them: which are fatal, which are just awkward, and which ones would defenders of this view concede? also be honest — have i just defined free will into existance by making it recursive rigidity? i want to know if the view is falsifiable or if its been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether thats a problem for a view like this.
