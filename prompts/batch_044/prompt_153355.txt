I'm in my second year of a philosphy PhD and I've been arguing with a friend in the phil of religion seminar about Wittgenstein's remarks on religious belief — specifically the idea that saying "God exists" isn't like asserting an empirical fact but is more like expressing a commitment to a form of life. We tried to formalize the worry that if you take this seriously, religious disagreement becomes conceptually impossible, and my friend wrote up this chain that I can't quite break but it feels like it proves way too much.

(1) For Wittgenstein, religious beliefs aren't hypotheses confirmed or disconfirmed by evidence; they're embedded in practices and pictures that regulate how one lives. (2) If a belief B is entirely embedded in form of life F, then B's content is constituted by the rules and practices internal to F. (3) So a belief whose content is constituted by F's rules has no cognitive content outside F — outside F it isn't false, it's just unintelligible, like trying to checkmate with checkers pieces. (4) Therefore, when a believer says "God exists" within Christian form-of-life C and an atheist denies it within naturalist form-of-life N, they aren't affirming and denying the same proposition. (5) If there's no shared proposition, there's no logical contradiction between them; they aren't genuinely disagreeing, just speaking past each other in different language-games. (6) But then religious misunderstanding — like treating the Last Judgment as a scientific prediction — can only happen inside a single game; an outsider can never genuinely misapply a religious concept, only fail to grasp it entirely. (7) Yet Wittgenstein clearly treats some people as genuinely misunderstanding religious belief from outside or across traditions, which requires shared content being misused. (8) So either (2) is false and religious belief carries trans-game propositional content, or Wittgenstein's own examples of misunderstanding collapse.

My friend wants to reject (7) and say all misunderstanding is just intra-game correction. But if I do that, religious belief becomes completely insulated from any external criticism — not just wrong, but conceptually immune — which feels like it proves too much. I think the slip might be between "not empirically assessable" and "having no shared content," maybe with an equivocation about what "constituted by practice" actually means, but I can't pin it exactly. Which step should I actually give up, and what does giving it up cost? Also, please seperate what's settled in the scholarship from what's genuinely contested about whether Wittgenstein allows any kind of cross-form-of-life rational assessment, and don't present one reading like it's the consensus — especially since people still fight about whether he's a fideist, a non-cognitivist, or something else entirely.
