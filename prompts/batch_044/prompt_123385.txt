I'm currently enrolled in a graduate seminar on contemporary pragmatism and philosophy of language, and we've been working closely through Rorty's antirepresentationalism and neopragmatism—specifically his argument that vocabularies are tools for coping rather than mirrors of nature, his distinction between normal and abnormal discourse, and his claim that philosophy should aim at edification rather than at representational accuracy. For a course exercise in philosophical writing, I'm trying to construct a sustained satire that applies these ideas with real precision rather than caricaturing them, and I would like you to draft the piece for me.

Please write a deadpan administrative memo—approximately 600 words, using standard memo formatting (TO, FROM, DATE, RE)—issued by the chair of a fictional philosophy department announcing that the department has formally adopted Rortyan neopragmatism and antirepresentationalism as its sole institutional framework. The memo should deploy Rorty's actual conceptual apparatus with care: treat course catalogs as contingent vocabularies with no representational content about knowledge; replace grading rubrics with "redescription protocols" that assess whether a student has successfully replaced an old vocabulary with a more useful one; reconceive tenure review in terms of solidarity-building and edification rather than approximation to truth; and distinguish between the "normal discourse" required for administrative compliance and the "abnormal discourse" of metaphysical objections raised by the dean's office. The humor should arise from pushing Rorty's genuine distinctions—such as the contingency of language, the rejection of representationalism as a dead metaphor, and the idea that truth is what is good in the way of belief for a particular community—to bureaucratic extremes, without attributing to Rorty positions he does not hold. Keep the voice strictly deadpan and administrative; the irony should be legible to someone familiar with Rorty without any authorial winking.

After the memo, include a brief analytical note—no more than 150 words—specifying exactly where the satire takes liberties with Rorty's actual views or leans on contested interpretations (for example, whether Rorty permits representational talk within the natural sciences, whether solidarity can be literalized as departmental policy, or whether edification can serve as a criterion for tenure). In that note, please also state what specific argument or clarification would lead you to revise the interpretation of Rorty embedded in the satire—where its reasoning is most vulnerable and what evidence or reasoning would change your assessment.
