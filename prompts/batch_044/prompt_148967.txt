I was trying to explain why knowledge graphs still feel like they're missing something even when every triple is formally correct, and he just rolled his eyes.

There's this passage where he talks about relations and propositions—he says if you have A and B standing in a relation R, then R has to be connected to A and also to B, but those connections are themselves new relations, and so on forever. So relations can't really exist as independent things, which means propositions (where a predicate is related to a subject) can't be genuinely unified either; instead there's just an undifferentiated block of feeling or immediate experience. I'm trying to understand exactly why he thinks the regress is viciuos rather than just an infinite chain we can accept, and whether saying relations are mere appearances actually solves the problem of how a subject and predicate hang together in one thought, or if it just dissolves the problem by denying there's any real hanging-together at all. Could someone break down the hidden assumption he's making about what a relation has to be? I feel like I'm missing a step, maybe something about treating relating like a kind of glue that needs its own glue.
