You have been reconstructing the judgments of a philosopher who defends a position she calls tranquilism: that well-being is constituted entirely by the absence of craving and the absence of suffering, with all positive, contentful, or affective states being strictly irrelevant. Her verdicts across cases are as follows. First, a practitioner in sustained non-dual absorption—no suffering, no craving, almost no sensory content—is in a state of complete flourishing. Second, a subject experiencing intense aesthetic rapture but constant craving for its continuation is not flourishing. Third, a chronic-pain sufferer who has genuinely extinguished all desire for relief, yet still experiences the pain as aversive, is not flourishing. Fourth, a person with mild positive affect, zero suffering, and zero craving is flourishing, but exactly as much as the practitioner in the first case, not more. Fifth, a permanently unconscious, brain-dead body maintained on life support—no mental life at all—is not within the domain of well-being at all; the question does not apply.

Consider now a novel case: a conscious subject kept indefinitely in a pharmacologically stabilized state of “pure presence”—no intentional objects, no affect, no suffering, no craving, but sustained wakefulness without sleep or unconsciousness; experience, if present at all, has no content beyond bare being-there.

Predict her verdict: is this flourishing, a failure of flourishing, or outside the scope? Identify at least two distinct rules that accommodate all five prior verdicts—e.g., a strict absence-only rule that cares solely about the two negative conditions regardless of consciousness-content, versus an absence-plus-conscious-presence rule that requires living mental existence but remains indifferent to what fills it. State which rule you take her to be running, with the reasoning drawn from her pattern that selects it over the competitor. Then specify the single further case that would best adjudicate between them.

In your answer, separate what follows necessarily from her stated commitments from what depends on genuinely contested interpretations—especially whether conscious presence without content counts as a substrate for well-being, and whether the absence of craving requires an active mental posture or can be satisfied by mere structural vacancy. Do not present either interpretation as the settled consensus among specialists in well-being theory.
