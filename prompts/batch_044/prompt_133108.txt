Draft the joint statement that a committed counterfactual theorist of causation, working in the Lewisian tradition, and a committed interventionist theorist, working in the Pearlian structural-causal-model tradition, could both endorse after a rigorous adversarial collaboration. The document should open with a numbered section of shared commitments—covering, at minimum, the distinction between causal and correlational claims, the utility of formal representation, and the conditions under which a causal claim can be empirically tested—written in language both parties accept without reservation. Next, present a disagreements table that states, in wording each camp would recognize as accurate to its own view, the precise point of divergence: specifically, whether explanatory asymmetry and the directionality of causation in complex, feedback-rich systems with non-modular architectures can be fully accounted for by invariant structural relationships and do-interventions alone, or whether they necessarily invoke irreducible counterfactual dependencies that outstrip any interventionist formalization. Then identify at least three cruxes—sub-questions whose resolution would plausibly shift one side—including, for example, how each framework handles preemption within cyclic causal graphs, the semantic status of physically impossible interventions, and the criteria for selecting the correct causal model when observational and interventional data underdetermine the structure. For each crux, specify one concrete case, argument, or empirical pattern that both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance each side’s prediction about what that case demonstrates. Conclude with a brief, jointly authored assessment of whether any portion of their disagreement is purely verbal rather than substantive, explaining why or why not. Maintain a formal, dispassionate register appropriate to an academic philosophy-of-science audience, and do not include citations, bibliographies, or requests for further reading.
