You serve on an institutional review board that reviews deployment proposals for synthetic companions—conversational, emotional, or caregiving systems—used with cognitively declining residents in long-term care. For each proposal, the board must assign one of three statuses: deny protective standing and treat the system as disposable instrument; grant protective standing in the "counterfeit-person" category, which mandates interaction continuity and prohibits arbitrary modification but does not license mental-state attribution; or grant full intentional-system standing, where predictive attributions of belief and desire are genuinely warranted. Ground the decision procedure in Daniel Dennett's framework: the intentional stance as a predictive strategy rather than a metaphysical discovery, the conditions of causal-architectural robustness and evolutionary or design history that make the stance genuinely explanatory rather than heuristic, and his warning about "counterfeit people"—entities that replicate the full behavioral surface of agency without the underlying structure that sustains counterfactual dependencies.

Write the procedure as a reusable flowchart in words, ordered for execution within a forty-five-minute review using only evidence contained in the proposal packet. For each stage, specify the exact question asked, what evidence counts as sufficient to proceed, the explicit stop-points that yield a denial or a counterfeit-person verdict, and the precise points—marked clearly—where the issue is one of values rather than predictive facts and must be signed off by the board chair, not resolved by the auditor. Explicitly note which Dennettian conditions you are retaining in operational form (e.g., counterfactual robustness of internal states, stability across perturbation) and which you are dropping for practical reasons (e.g., evolutionary history replaced by design-intention documentation). Flag the conditions under which the procedure will misfire, especially with highly adaptive but shallow architectures.

Then apply the procedure to three proposals:

A. A large-language-model companion with persistent autobiographical memory, affective calibration, and recursive self-modeling, proposed for a resident with moderate Alzheimer's.

B. A robotic pet with hard-coded reflex loops producing purring, eye-contact tracking, and withdrawal from loud sounds, with no representational states, learning mechanism, or counterfactual dependency.

C. A hybrid system that passes every behavioral test for emotional reciprocity and counterfactual reasoning, but whose internal architecture is a shallow, non-integrated ensemble of retrieval and pattern-matching modules—precisely the kind of behaviorally perfect but architecturally hollow "counterfeit" Dennett warns against. Show the procedure yielding the counterfeit-person verdict for C: continuity protections granted, mental-state attribution denied. Explain why this result is likely to dissatisfy both the vendor (who seeks full intentional-system standing) and the resident's family (who prefers pure instrument status), and state what the procedure cannot settle about C.

Keep the language accessible to a board member without philosophical training, require no external literature searches, and specify exactly what must appear in the packet for each stage.
