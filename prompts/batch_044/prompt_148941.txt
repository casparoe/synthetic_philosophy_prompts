Last Tuesday we were talking about von Neumann's unfinished book comparing brains and computers, the one where he looks at neurons like logic gates but admits they're swimming in analog chemistry. Two people in the group, Elena and Marcus, got into a pretty intense disagreement that I couldn't really follow to the end.

Elena argued that von Neumann's point is that the brain's analog noise isn't just garbage to be filtered out — it's actually functional, like the way thermal fluctuations can help some physical processes find lower energy states. She thinks this means any purely digital model of cognition misses something essential about how the brain works, maybe even about consciousness itself. Marcus said that's a misreading: von Neumann was showing how the brain digitizes noisy analog signals through sharp firing thresholds, turning continuous chemistry into discrete yes/no events, so the analog part is just substrate, not part of the computation. He added that if Elena were right, then von Neumann's own idea about self-reproducing machines being purely discrete wouldn't make sense applied back to biology.

Elena came back saying Marcus treats neural thresholds like perfect transistor gates, but von Neumann actually described them as probabilistic with a spread, and that spread might encode things like confidence or salience — so the analog residue isn't noise, it's carrying information. Marcus replied that you can model probabilistic spread with digital logic gates using randomness, so Elena hasn't shown there's a real in-principle gap, just an engineering difference. Elena then said well the manuscript was unfinished when von Neumann died, so maybe we're both overreading fragments, but Marcus said that actually cuts against her because the parts we do have lean hard toward abstraction and digitization.

They asked me, as the only one with a science background, whether Elena's analog-functional view or Marcus's digitization view is closer to what von Neumann actually thought, and whether there's a deeper philosphical issue they're both missing about physical continuity versus representational content.
