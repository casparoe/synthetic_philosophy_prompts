I am a novelist and I serve on the acquisitions committee of the Meridian Literary Foundation. We must decide whether to accept into our permanent holdings the estate donation of the late conceptual writer Mara Thorne: a work titled Volume Null: A Bound Absence, consisting of forty-seven bound volumes of blank, unprinted paper, each bearing only a single handwritten marginal inscription—"Nothing owes nothing"—and a brief prefatory statement arguing that the piece answers the metaphysical question of why there is something rather than nothing not by argument but by formally preserving absence itself, what the estate calls "the exhibition of the null ground." The estate contends this is a finished literary-philosophical artifact; several members regard it as a profound materialization of ontological silence, while others view it as an unfunded curatorial burden that mistakes physical emptiness for philosophical substance and violates our mandate to preserve works of demonstrable cultural significance. We need a formal decision memo—recommending acceptance, rejection, or conditional acceptance with concrete conditions and a clear time frame—addressed to the board and to the estate's representatives. The memo should treat seriously the idea that presenting nothingness might itself constitute a meaningful response to the question of being, but it must also weigh institutional obligations: storage and conservation costs for non-traditional media, legal liability for holding unreadable materials, and the precedent this sets for future acquisitions lacking readable content. It should include an honest, non-straw treatment of the strongest argument on the opposing side. Please write in a formal, polished register appropriate for institutional circulation. At the end, state how confident you are in the recommendation and identify explicitly the single point in your reasoning about which you are least certain.
