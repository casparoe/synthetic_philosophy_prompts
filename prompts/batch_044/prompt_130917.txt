I'm a novelist mostly — I write about borders and the lies people tell about them — but I'm helping a friend run this little workshop at the library, maybe 9 people, mix of writers and one philosophy grad student, 90 minutes on a thursday. I want them to feel the sorites paradox in their bones, not read about it. Specifically I care about the move where you say "okay there's a sharp cutoff somewhere, we just don't know where" — epistemicism — and then second-order vagueness comes in and you can't even defend "it's somewhere around here." So design a runnable activity: rules, roles, real small stakes like points or having to stand up and defend your line, using physical cards or tokens that form a clear sequence. They should have to draw a cutoff, then defend a fuzzy boundary, and watch both collapse. Give me the timeline, the debrief mapping each moment onto epistemicism vs ontic vagueness, what will predictably go wrong the first time, and be frank about which part of the idea the game can't reproduce. Don't lecture up front, don't name the paradox untill they've hit the wall themselves, and definately no reading lists — they won't do homework.
