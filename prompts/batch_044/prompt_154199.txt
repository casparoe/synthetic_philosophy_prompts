I'm fluent in dependent type theory and proof-relevant semantics, and I want the epistemicism versus supervaluationism debate about vagueness translated into that vocabulary, with an honest residue report. Treat the vague predicate "is bald" or "is a heap" as analogous to a membership judgment in a Martin-Löf-style system. For epistemicism in the Williamson vein: there is a sharp, hidden boundary—a precise cutoff—such that every instance is determinately in or out, but our epistemic access is limited. Translate this into type-theoretic terms. Does the hidden boundary correspond to an unknown parameter fixed in every model, to an oracle that decides membership, or to a canonical proof term that exists but is not constructively available? Specify exactly what "ignorance" means when the logic is proof-relevant rather than classical.

For supervaluationism in the Fine/Keefe vein: there is no single correct precisification; "John is bald" is true if true on all admissible sharpenings, false if false on all, and otherwise indeterminate. Translate this into the framework of type refinements or multi-valued judgments. Does "admissible precisification" map onto different consistent extensions of an inductive type definition? Does indeterminacy become the absence of a canonical proof term across all refinements, or does it require a genuinely proof-irrelevant layer that the system rejects?

Now the residue: for each view, say (1) what translates cleanly, (2) what must be distorted—e.g., does epistemicism's bivalence require smuggling in classical excluded middle that proof-relevant semantics avoids? Does supervaluationism's "admissible" collapse into gradual typing or parameterized polymorphism, losing its specifically vagueness-related content?—and (3) what has no expression at all, such as whether the sorites tolerance principle corresponds to a type-checking rule or to something else entirely. Assess whether the untranslatable residue reveals a limitation of the type-theoretic framework or an unclarity in the original philosophical claim.

Finally, evaluate a hybrid I have been considering: a "constructive epistemicist" view on which there is indeed a sharp boundary, but it is not hidden behind mere ignorance—rather, it is constructively undefined because the inductive definition of the predicate supplies no decision procedure, and different constructively valid extensions assign different cutoffs. Within this vocabulary, is that a coherent third option, or does it collapse into supervaluationism because the "extensions" just are the admissible precisifications? Be specific about whether the framework can distinguish epistemic ignorance from constructive underdetermination, or whether the residue shows that the distinction cannot be maintained without importing assumptions foreign to type theory.
