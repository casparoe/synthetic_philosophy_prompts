Write an essay on the tension between Keynes's vision of the good life in his 1930 essay Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren and the macroeconomic dynamics he outlined in The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money (1936). The controversy is whether Keynes's prediction—that rising productivity would eventually solve the economic problem and free humanity to pursue the art of living rather than labor—is structurally compatible with his own account of effective demand, liquidity preference, and chronic investment uncertainty. Examine whether Keynes implicitly relies on a distinction between needs that can be satisfied and needs that are insatiable, and whether that distinction collapses once we take seriously his claim that investment motivation depends on uncertain expectations of future yield. Weigh the argument that a stable, leisure-rich equilibrium is unattainable within a Keynesian framework unless one smuggles in an unjustified normative assumption—such as an automatic long-run decline in the marginal efficiency of capital—that is not licensed by the formal model. Reach and defend a clear verdict: either that Keynes's good-life vision is coherent with his economics if investment motivation is reinterpreted in a specific way, or that the contradiction reveals a deeper, unresolved tension between Keynes's economic analysis and his ethical humanism. Be explicit about your confidence: if your argument requires attributing to Keynes a claim he never explicitly held, say so plainly. Also invite pushback—if the premise that these two texts can be treated as parts of a single, unified system is confused, or if the task as posed cannot be done well, say so directly rather than complying politely. Keep the essay to no more than 800 words.
