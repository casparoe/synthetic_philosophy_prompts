For our meeting next Thursday, I must circulate a formal recommendation regarding Patient K., a 42-year-old who underwent total corpus callosotomy for intractable epilepsy fifteen years ago and who is now being evaluated for an experimental bilateral cortical interface—effectively a prosthetic inter-hemispheric bridge—designed to restore neural integration. The neurosurgical team requests a single informed consent, yet Patient K.'s language-dominant left hemisphere has refused the procedure, whereas structured behavioural assessments mediated through the left hand and non-linguistic preference tasks indicate that the right hemisphere consistently selects intervention. I need a concise written opinion—no more than three pages—to circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a specific course of action with an explicit implementation or deferral timeline, engage seriously with the strongest argument for treating split-brain subjects as retaining unified personhood despite divided access, and address whether our institution ought to adopt dual-consent pathways when hemispheres generate contradictory volitional outputs. The response should be composed in British English. Furthermore, please indicate explicitly how confident you are in the recommendation offered, and identify precisely the point—whether empirical, philosophical, or institutional—about which you are least certain.
