Please argue that embedded agency dissolves the standard separation between belief-updating and action-selection. Because an embedded agent is physically constituted by the very processes it models and intervenes upon, defend the claim that its epistemic and practical reasoning must be governed by a single, unified feedback dynamics rather than by distinct normative frameworks—such as Bayesian conditioning for beliefs and expected-utility maximization for actions. Address the objection that this collapses normative theory into mere descriptive dynamics. Further, inject and defend the idea that what we call rational error in such systems should be reconceived not as a failure to apply correct norms from an external viewpoint, but as a misalignment between locally tractable feedback loops and globally coherent conditions that the agent itself can never fully represent. The argument should remain conceptual: do not invoke specific empirical studies, named formalisms, or bibliographic references.
