I was prepping notes for my value theory seminar and got stuck re-reading Moore's Principia Ethica, specifically the bit where he lists friendship and aesthetic appriciation as the two supreme intrinsic goods. Most students grasp the organic-unity idea fine when Moore applies it to a painting and the consciousness of it, but friendship is trickier. He insists friendship is an organic whole — not just companionship plus the pleasure of noticing your friend's good mental qualities — yet he also says that part of what's valuable about friendship is precisely that we appreciate beautiful mental qualities in our friends. So here's the conceptual knot I'm trying to untangle: if appreciating those mental qualities is already one of the two highest goods on its own, and friendship is supposed to be an irreducible unity that includes that appreciation, doesn't applying the organic-unity principle to friendship risk double-counting the aesthetic component? Or does being embedded in the relational whole transform that appreciation so radically that it stops being aesthetic value altogether and becomes something else?

Can you explain this issue carefully? I want more than a summary of Moore's view — really dig into whether there's a genuine contradiction here, or whether we can dissolve it by distinguishing two different roles that beauty plays (say, as an object of contemplation versus as an element within a relational whole). Please don't give me a bibliography or a list of scholars to read; I'm writing this for a small-model evaluation and I want to see if it can think through the tension conceptually without just regurgitating standard commentary. Keep it in the weeds.
