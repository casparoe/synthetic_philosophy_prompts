I am a doctoral student in aesthetics and the philosophy of representation, currently drafting a chapter on self-reference in digital and visual media. In a recent supervision, my supervisor—whose research concerns logical and representational hierarchy—delivered a series of judgements about whether particular systems instantiate a tangled hierarchy that breaches an inviolate level between representation and substrate. She has never fully formalised her criterion, but her verdicts on four prior cases appear consistent, and I wish to determine whether her position is genuinely determined or underdetermined by them.

Her prior judgements are these. First, regarding Escher's well-known lithograph in which two hands appear to draw each other: she holds that this creates a tangled hierarchy, yet the loop remains benign because it is confined to the image plane. The physical substrate—the paper, the stone, the ink—remains causally and ontologically separate from what is depicted. Second, regarding a hypothetical neural architecture in which a model's internal activation patterns directly determine its own weight updates, with no external interpreter or mediating mechanism: she holds that this violates the inviolate level, because representation and substrate collapse into a single causal loop without mediating boundary. Third, regarding the Borges fable in which a map is produced at the scale of the territory it depicts: she holds that this does not constitute a level violation. Even though the map and territory share spatial extension, they remain distinct ontological kinds; the representational relation is redundant rather than collapsed. Fourth, regarding a museum installation in which a live video feed of a gallery is displayed on a monitor within that same gallery, with a temporal delay: she holds that this respects the boundary. Although the visual content refers back to the space containing it, the camera, transmission medium, and display screen remain physically distinct substrates.

These four verdicts seem jointly compatible with more than one principle. One possibility is a criterion of causal or substrate separation: the hierarchy is violated only when representational content can directly alter its own physical bearer without mediation. Another possibility is a criterion of register or logical separation: the hierarchy is violated only when the levels of representation and represented object operate in the same logical or formal register, regardless of whether physical mediation is present.

The new case is this. A virtual-reality artwork renders an immersive studio environment in which the user, wearing a headset, sees a virtual display showing the GLSL shader code that generates the studio's lighting. Using hand-tracking gestures within the virtual space, the user can edit parameters in this displayed code, and the edits are transmitted to the GPU running the simulation, altering the lighting in real time. The user's physical body and the GPU hardware exist outside the virtual representation; neither is depicted within the studio.

Given this track record, please analyse the case in British English and do the following. Predict what my supervisor would likely say about this VR installation, identifying which of the two candidate principles—causal separation or register separation—best explains her prior verdicts and therefore predicts her view here. If her precedents genuinely underdetermine the outcome, state clearly what principle she would be adopting with each possible choice, and identify the closest analogue among her four prior judgements. Then propose a further, hypothetical case that would decisively distinguish the causal-separation rule from the register-separation rule. Please do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I am interested solely in the conceptual analysis.
