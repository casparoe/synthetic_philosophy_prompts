I have been thinking on my own about how relying on AI for explanations might not merely replace individual knowledge but erode a specific kind of understanding: the internal model one builds when one can explain a thing oneself. I want to lead a single ninety-minute session for a small group of intelligent, non-specialist friends who use large language models regularly for work but have no background in philosophy, cognitive science, or technology studies. Rather than asking for a lecture, I need a teaching plan that lays out the explanatory arc. The central claim I want them to grasp is that there is a meaningful distinction between using a tool to extend one's own reasoning and outsourcing judgment to it, and that the latter produces a particular erosion in which one stops constructing a mental model of the domain and instead maintains a kind of proxy model that tracks what the system would say. To build this, please specify: which preliminary idea must be established before the distinction between extension and outsourcing can even be intelligible to them; the single concrete, non-obvious example or scenario that should carry the session and what properties it must have and lack; the predictable misconceptions they will arrive with or form during the discussion—including, I anticipate, the belief that being able to verify an AI's output is sufficient for understanding, and the analogy to calculators or search engines—and the precise point in the sequence where each should be headed off; what should be deliberately excluded at this level and acknowledged as such; and one focused question I can pose at the end that would reveal whether they have understood the erosion mechanism itself rather than merely adopted a vague skepticism about AI. The plan should be formal about the dependency order and should not include bibliographies, reading lists, or references to specific scholars.
