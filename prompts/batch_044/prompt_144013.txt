I have judgments about whether various episodes in the history of science represent progressive problem shifts, degenerative ones, or something in between, but I am not convinced they hang together. I want a precise principle—or a small, jointly consistent family of them—that captures as many of my verdicts as possible, together with an honest statement of which verdicts must be abandoned if the principle is to remain simple and non-ad hoc.

My verdicts, case by case:

- Ptolemaic astronomers adding successive epicycles and eventually equants to preserve geocentrism against accumulating positional data: clearly degenerative. The protective belt is being patched with no independent content that feeds back into the hard core.

- The caloric theory of heat, modified with increasingly contrived auxiliary hypotheses—such as invisible fluids with negative weight—to account for Lavoisier's combustion results: degenerative. The adjustments feel post-hoc and generate no risky predictions outside the anomaly they were invented to save.

- The transition from Newtonian gravitation to general relativity: progressive. Even though Einstein was partly motivated by Mercury's already-known perihelion advance, the theory was not tuned merely to accommodate that figure. It derived gravitational lensing and gravitational redshift as unexpected, risky predictions later confirmed independently. The hard core was replaced, but the replacement was heuristically fertile.

- The postulation of dark matter to explain flat galaxy rotation curves: progressive. The auxiliary entity was introduced to preserve the gravitational law, yet it quickly generated predictions—gravitational lensing in clusters, the cosmic microwave background power spectrum, large-scale structure formation—that were not part of the original anomaly and were confirmed through separate observational channels.

- The development of string theory from the mid-1980s onward, especially the discovery of dualities that unified five apparently distinct string theories and the later AdS/CFT correspondence linking quantum gravity to gauge theories: I want to call this progressive, despite the absence of direct empirical predictions for decades. The work solves internal consistency problems (anomalies, divergences) and generates new mathematical theorems that feed back into other domains of physics.

- The hypothetical planet Vulcan proposed inside Mercury's orbit to save Newtonian mechanics from the perihelion anomaly: degenerative. It is structurally parallel to dark matter in being an unobserved entity postulated to preserve a law in the face of an anomaly, yet my judgment diverges sharply.

The central tension is this. Both dark matter and Vulcan are unseen bodies added to save a theoretical core; both could in principle yield further predictions. Meanwhile, both general relativity and string theory involve deep theoretical restructuring—GR replaces the hard core, string theory transforms its protective belt—yet I call both progressive. At the same time, I want to deny that any theory which eventually makes a correct prediction after being modified counts as progressive; otherwise the caloric theory's occasional correct retrodictions would rescue it.

So: is there a coherent principle—perhaps refining Lakatos's distinction, perhaps departing from it—that explains why GR and string theory are progressive, why dark matter is progressive, but why Vulcan and caloric adjustments are not? Specifically, what weight should be given to (a) whether the modification solves an internal consistency problem rather than only an empirical mismatch, (b) whether the change generates predictions that are independently testable through channels not involved in the original anomaly, and (c) whether heuristic fertility across domains can substitute for direct empirical novelty? Which of my six verdicts must I drop if I insist on a principle with no more than three clauses?

Please state your best principle precisely, with each clause tied to the case that motivates it, its verdict on at least two further cases I have not mentioned (for instance, the Higgs mechanism added to electroweak theory, and the steady-state cosmology's continuous creation of matter), and an explicit admission of which of my six verdicts your principle cannot accommodate without becoming ad hoc.
