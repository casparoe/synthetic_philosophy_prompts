For my Buddhist philosophy seminar I am testing whether anatta can accommodate what I will call a “relational-indexical” continuity: the first-person pronoun tracks not a permanent substrate but a temporary node in a web of interdependent obligations and perceptual streams, so that “I” refers to a locus of accountability sustained only so long as that relational web holds it in place. Give me your credence, between 0 and 1, that this hybrid is genuinely coherent with classical anatta rather than either smuggling in a hidden substance or collapsing into a Humean bundle with no normative grip. Decompose the judgment into three parts: (1) whether anatta permits any structured continuity across aggregates beyond mere momentariness; (2) whether relational obligations can ground indexical self-reference without a substantial referent; and (3) whether such a view retains sufficient stability for moral accountability. Assign each part a rough probability, show how they combine into your overall number, and identify which sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to. Finally, what conceptual argument drawn from the Abhidharma or Madhyamaka frameworks—interpreted philosophically rather than bibliographically—would most move your number?
