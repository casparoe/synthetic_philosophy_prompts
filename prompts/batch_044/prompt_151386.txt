I sit on my city's Zoning and Preservation Appeals Board, and I am preparing the decision memo for Thursday's session on Variance Request 447: a proposal to demolish a 1928 courtyard apartment building—currently rent-stabilized and home to thirty-seven households, several of them artists who have lived there for decades—to make way for a forty-story tower with market-rate and below-market-rate units. The developer frames this as fulfilling a right to the city through increased density and inclusive growth; neighborhood opponents invoke community character and local democratic control. As a novelist, I am troubled by an unexamined premise in both briefs: each asks the board to identify the essential ontology of this block—whether it is fundamentally a site of working-class residence or fundamentally a node for urban expansion—while the board lacks epistemic access to the displaced tenants' lived, future-oriented relationships to the place. Draft a formal memo recommending approval, denial, or conditional approval with a specific timeline for implementation. The memo must take a committed philosophical position on whether a zoning body can legitimately make ontological identifications of neighborhood essence under conditions of inaccessible information, apply that position directly to Request 447, and treat the strongest argument on the opposing side—whether from the density advocates or the preservationists—with genuine engagement rather than dismissal. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; write as a document the board can vote on immediately.
