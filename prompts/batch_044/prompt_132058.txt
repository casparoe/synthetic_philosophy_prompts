Following a long and somewhat exhausting argument with a close friend about whether hypocrisy genuinely undermines one’s standing to blame, I find myself stuck on what seems to be a hidden premise in how philosophers treat the issue. My friend defended the view that hypocrisy only defeats standing when it takes the form of a performative contradiction: you blame someone for X in the very act of doing X yourself, which makes your utterance self-undermining in something like the way asserting “I am not speaking” is self-undermining. I found that compelling, and it led me to assume that standing is essentially binary—either your blame is internally coherent and you possess it, or it is performatively contradictory and you lack it entirely. But then I encountered the idea of “structural hypocrisy,” in which someone might lack standing to blame others for participating in an unjust system even though their own participation is not token-identical to the wrong they condemn—say, criticizing industrial animal agriculture while eating plant-based but still using products whose supply chains involve structurally analogous exploitation. Here there is no direct performative contradiction compressed into the act of speaking; the tension is distributed across social structures rather than embedded in the logical form of the utterance. So my question is: if structural hypocrisy can also remove standing, have we not quietly abandoned the contradiction-based logic my friend described and replaced it with a kind of moral-purity threshold? And if standing is genuinely binary, as I assumed, would that not imply that anyone embedded in large-scale injustices lacks standing to blame anyone else for being embedded in them—which seems to swallow the practice of blame altogether? I realize I may be conflating logical coherence with relational entitlement, or treating a scalar condition as an all-or-nothing property, but I cannot locate the precise error. Could you diagnose the false presupposition in this reasoning and show where the binary model breaks down?

Please state clearly what would change your mind—specifically, which argument or consideration would convince you that structural hypocrisy does not undermine standing, and where your own reasoning is most vulnerable to failure.
