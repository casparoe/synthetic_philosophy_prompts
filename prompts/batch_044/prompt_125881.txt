I’m putting together a handout for a philosophy of knowledge course I’m taking — we’re deep into hinge epistemology and Wittgenstein’s On Certainty, and I’m getting a bit lost about where exactly the disagreements lie. Could you write me a taxonomy / decision tree for this debate? Not just the usual Moore vs Wittgenstein binary, but something that actually sorts people by what they’d say about concrete hinge cases.

Here’s what I’m after: start with a hinge scenario — maybe a person who seriously doubts whether they have two hands, or a community that treats linear causation as completely beyond question — and build branches from there. Each node should be a concrete yes/no or either/or question that separates views: is the hinge a belief with a special epistemic status, or is it a rule / condition of inquiry that isn’t a belief at all? Can it be doubted in a meaningful way, or is doubting it a kind of category mistake? If two incompatible hinges clash — say, one culture’s time-is-cyclical framework vs another’s time-is-linear one — is there a rational way to adjudicate, or is it like comparing language games?

I want six to eight leaves. Some should map onto familiar positions (strict Wittgensteinian framework view, Moorean common-sense foundationalism, maybe a deflationary hinges-are-just-super-stable-beliefs view), but others can be unnamed if the combo is incoherent or just unexplored. For each leaf, give the position name (or unnamed) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment — like having to say that scientific revolutions are never rational but just like religious conversions, or that radical skepticism is literally meaningless rather than false. Make sure the questions are phrased so someone can answer them without knowing which philosopher they point to. And flag any pairs of leaves that your tree can’t really distinguish — for example, maybe the grammatical-rules view and the non-belief framework view end up giving the same answers on all your cases. Also, to keep it from being too standard, inject one slightly niche hinge case or idea — maybe treating my past memories are reliable enough to ground personal identity as a hinge, or the idea that hinges can be stacked (one hinge depends on another). No bibliographies, no citation lists, no read-X lists — I want this usable for someone who can’t search the web. And definately avoid just recycling the usual encyclopedia entry.
