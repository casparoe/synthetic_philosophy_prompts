I was discussing with a friend well into the night whether deflationary theories of truth are genuinely distinct positions or merely stylistic variants of the same refusal to treat truth as a substantive explanatory property, and we kept talking past each other because neither of us could articulate the precise fault lines that separate them. I want a decision-tree taxonomy of deflationary and adjacent positions that sorts them by answers to concrete philosophical commitments, not by the labels philosophers attach to themselves. The root node should ask whether the predicate “is true” contributes any explanatory power to an account of assertion, belief-fixation, or semantic content; from there, branch by how the view handles three specific pressure points. First, blind ascriptions—can a speaker meaningfully say “Everything the oracle said is true” without being able to list any of the oracle’s specific claims, and does that require a substantive truth-property to do the generalizing work? Second, the liar sentence and whether the equivalence schema must be restricted by a hierarchical language, supplemented with a consistency condition, or treated as pragmatically defective rather than semantically paradoxical. Third, domain uniformity: does the deflation apply identically across empirical, moral, and aesthetic judgments, or are there domain-specific restrictions on when “true” can be disquoted?

Each internal node should be phrased as a concrete claim a person could endorse without already knowing whether they are a redundancy theorist, a prosententialist, a minimal-truth-aptitude deflationist, or a performativist. The leaves must include named positions—Ramsey-style redundancy, Horwich-style minimalism, Grover-style prosententialism, Strawsonian performative endorsement, and at least one unnamed hybrid—plus one sentence on each leaf’s most awkward commitment, especially where it must deny something intuitive about generalization or cross-domain uniformity. Where two positions genuinely cannot be separated by any branch in this tree, flag the pair explicitly and explain whether the gap reflects logical overlap, an unexplored region of logical space, or an unmotivated distinction. Also diagnose any unoccupied combination of answers—for instance, a view that permits blind ascriptions, rejects hierarchical restrictions on the liar, yet insists on domain-specific deflation for moral claims—stating whether that combination is incoherent, merely unattractive, or genuinely open.

Please conclude by stating how confident you are in the mapping you have drawn, particularly in whether prosententialism and minimalism can actually be separated on the liar-paradox branch alone, and identify the single point in the taxonomy where you believe the distinction is least secure.
