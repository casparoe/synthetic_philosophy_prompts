Please construct a philosophical argument for the position that divine hiddenness is not an evidential failure on the part of theism but a structurally necessary condition for genuine theistic commitment. Your argument should proceed by analogy with the privacy of mental states in philosophy of mind: just as direct, unmediated access to another person's mental contents would undermine the intersubjective framework required to recognize them as a distinct mind rather than as an external mechanism, so an unmediated, continuously manifest divine presence would collapse the distinction between warranted belief and coerced assent, thereby eliminating the possibility of faith as a free, personal orientation. You should defend the claim that this analogy holds even though mental privacy is typically construed as an empirical limitation while divine hiddenness involves a voluntary divine choice, and you should address the counter-objection that an omnipotent being could manifest presence in a way that informs without coercing.
