design an in-class game or structured role-play that puts them in this situation. split them into makers and coordinators in a fictional small factory/community. at minute 25 drop an automation card that eliminates all maker tasks but leaves coordinator roles intact — except now they're coordinating phantom production. use real small stakes (candy, or points that determine who picks teh seminar topic next week). include rules, materials, the payoff scheme, a timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the output/recognition split.

also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time i run it, and be frank about which feature of the idea the activity can't reproduce (i'll have to supply that part in discussion).

- setting_and_goals: the classroom context and what students should come away seeing
- injected_concept: brief summary of the output-work vs recognition-work split
- rules_and_materials: how the game works, roles, and the automation card
- payoff_and_stakes: the incentive scheme
- timeline_minutes: breakdown of the 80 minutes
- debrief_mapping: which game moments map to which parts of the concept
- predictable_failure: what goes wrong on first run and how to handle it
- honest_limitation: what feature of the idea the game can't reproduce

no bibliographies or reading lists, please — my students can't look things up mid-class anyway.
