I'm an ethics consultant in a busy hospital and I get paged ocassionally — maybe twice a week — for consent conflicts that have to be settled in about twelve minutes because surgery is waiting or family is in the hallway yelling. I have to put my own signature on the call; there's no ethics committee at 3am to hide behind. I need a reusable checklist that turns deontology into something I can actually run, especially the paradox where absolute duties to autonomy and non-maleficence seem to make me violate one no matter which I pick.

The procedure should be steps I can run in order, with the judgment-call parts clearly flagged and not disguised. Specifically include:

- How to identify the active duties in the case, not just list Kantian rules
- A shadow-maxim test: state the maxim of the action I recommend and the maxim of the exception I'm allowing, then check whether both can be willed as universal law for anyone in my clinical role without contradiction. If they can't, that's a real conflict, not an apparent one.
- A tie-breaker for genuine conflict — but mark it as a judgment call I'm dressing up as procedure
- A moral-residue box: document which duty gets overridden and acknowledge it stays violated, so I'm not pretending the paradox disappears
- The misfire conditions: when does this checklist just collapse?

Then apply it to three cases. One should give me an answer I'm genuinely not going to like — I want to see the procedure force my hand.

Case 1: Fully capacitated adult refuses a life-saving transfusion for religious reasons; family begs me to override.

Case 2: Unconscious trauma patient, no surrogate present yet, old ambiguous chart note that might indicate refusal of aggressive care; surgeon wants to operate immediately or the patient dies.

Case 3: Patient with intermittent but documented capacity refuses dialysis during a lucid window; family and nephrologist claim depression is driving it, but refusal matches prior values. The procedure should tell me to honor the refusal, knowing it leads to death and legal exposure, and I have to sign my signiture.

Finally, say plainly what this procedure can't do — it can't prove deontology is right, it can't manufacture missing facts, and it can't replace my judgment under time pressure.

Keep it practical, not an academic essay. No reading lists or citations, just the steps.
