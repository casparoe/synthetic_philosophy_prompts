I hold a view I call semantic fence preservation: inherited categorical distinctions in ordinary language and moral practice—such as the boundary between intentional action and reflex, or between justification and excuse—function as Chesterton-style fences. Even if metaphysical analysis suggests these distinctions lack sharp ontological foundations, we ought to preserve them in reasoning and social coordination unless we can specify not merely that they are imprecise, but exactly which inferential and practical failures their removal would cause, and how we would avoid those failures without them. The position draws on Burke's skepticism of rationalist reconstruction and on the principle, familiar from Chesterton, that one should not tear down a fence until one understands why it was erected, but applies it specifically to conceptual architecture rather than to physical institutions or customs alone. What would count against this view? I want the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward: empirical cases in which a culture or discipline abandoned such distinctions without suffering the predicted coordination or reasoning failures; formal results in decision theory or cognitive science showing that unified, boundary-free representations actually improve predictive accuracy or normative judgment; arguments that the view insulates itself by redefining every successful conceptual reform as having secretly preserved the fence in functional form; and the possibility that the position collapses into an unhelpful status-quo bias that cannot distinguish prudent preservation from mere inertia. For each category, tell me what the position genuinely predicts that rival views do not, which objections its defenders would themselves concede as damaging rather than dismiss, and whether the risk of unfalsifiability—if every dissolution can be interpreted as not having truly removed the fence—is a virtue of methodological prudence or a vice that empties the view of content. I am especially interested in whether there is decisive evidence that could move a committed semantic fence preservationist, or whether the view is now so qualified that it survives any conceivable counterexample.
