I've come around to this pretty extreme take on inside-view vs outside-view — call it the veto model. The claim is that when you have an outside-view base rate for a referance class, it doesn't just weaken your inside reasoning, it functions as a hard veto: you have to suspend judgment unless you can show, with an independant check, that the class is misdrawn or contaminated. I think this applies even to conceptual reasoning, not just forecasting. So if I'm working through a metaphysics problem and I realize experts are split 60/40, my inside view shouldn't just be 'I'm in the 40 camp'; it should be suspended until there's external evidence the split is artifactual. What would actually count against this? I want the defeaters ranked: cases where inside view corrected a misdrawn referance class, formal results showing you can't tell contamination from self-serving bias, the risk of self-application (does the veto apply to believing the veto?), and whether this gets qualified away into unfalsifiability — like any inside-view win gets explained away as 'referance class error.' Which ones would defenders concede are real problems vs. just dismiss? And is being well-insulated here a feature or a bug?
