I have recently been assigned to audit mechanistic interpretability reports for a clinical decision-support system that is being redeployed from a large transformer into a sparse mixture-of-experts running on edge hardware with different tokenization and preprocessing pipelines. The audit protocol asks reviewers to flag any “mechanistic anomaly”—a case in which the compressed model relies on a different reasoning pathway than the reference deployment—but the protocol never defines what counts as sameness of mechanism across substrate changes, architectural pruning, and altered input representations. I need a taxonomy, built specifically for this kind of cross-deployment artificial-system comparison rather than for biology or neuroscience, that sorts positions by the criteria they treat as necessary and sufficient for mechanism identity.

Please construct a branching decision tree of yes/no questions that a reviewer could answer without already knowing the philosophical labels. Begin with this concrete audit case: the reference model detects pneumonia by tracking a spatial pattern through a specific sequence of attention heads, while the compressed model reaches the same diagnosis through a different sequence of experts activated by a differently tokenized representation of the same scan. The tree should branch, in order, on: (1) whether identity requires shared physical implementation (same weights, same hardware substrate); (2) if not, whether it requires shared causal-graph topology at the level of functional units; (3) if not, whether it requires preservation of counterfactual intervention outcomes on intermediate representations; (4) if not, whether preservation of information-flow topology—which channels influence which—is sufficient on its own; and (5) whether boundary conditions such as tokenization and preprocessing are internal to the mechanism or merely external context. Each leaf should carry a position name if one is standard in the philosophy of science or AI interpretability literature, otherwise “unnamed,” plus exactly one sentence on its most awkward commitment in this audit setting. For every answer-combination that produces no named position, include a brief diagnosis of whether the gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Present the taxonomy first in nested form, then summarize it in a table with these exact columns: Branch_Question, Yes_Path, No_Path, Leaf_Position, Awkward_Commitment, Gap_Diagnosis. Additionally, provide a JSON object with a single named field “indistinguishable_pairs” that lists any two leaves the tree’s five questions cannot actually separate, with a one-line explanation of what external factor distinguishes them. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific papers; I want the conceptual architecture for this audit problem, not a literature survey.
