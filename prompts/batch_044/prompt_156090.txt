Here is the cluster of commitments I have adopted regarding presentism in historiography, and I want a rigorous audit of whether they form a jointly tenable position. (1) As a methodological default, historians ought to evaluate past agents by the normative and epistemic standards available within those agents' own conceptual frameworks, rather than by anachronistic present-day criteria—what amounts to a principled avoidance of evaluative presentism. (2) I reject teleological or Whiggish historiography: historical narratives should not be organized around the degree to which past societies approximated contemporary liberal-democratic norms. (3) Nevertheless, I accept that the selection of research questions, archival priorities, and problems is legitimately and unavoidably shaped by present political and ethical concerns; there is no historiographical view from nowhere. (4) At the same time, I hold that certain institutional practices—such as chattel slavery or specific regimes of gendered exclusion—constitute cross-cultural moral wrongs that can be identified in a non-relativist register, without being reduced to mere local misunderstandings or internal normative failures. (5) Finally, I take the primary disciplinary task of historiography to be causal and structural explanation of institutional change, with explicit moral evaluation playing only a secondary, carefully circumscribed role.

Do these hang together? I see a potential conflict between (1) and (4): if I suspend contemporary moral judgment in favor of locally available standards, I need to explain on what grounds I can claim that chattel slavery is wrong in a framework-transcendent way, especially when local normative structures often accommodated it. I also wonder whether (2) and (3) are genuinely incompatible—if present concerns inevitably shape our questions, does that not smuggle present norms back into our narratives in a manner that reproduces Whiggism? And I want to know whether (5) genuinely resolves these tensions or merely relocates them: can I consistently restrict moral evaluation to a secondary role while still insisting on (4)? Please order the tensions by severity, distinguish pairs that are only apparently at odds from those that are structurally incompatible, and specify the cheapest consistency-restoring revision—indicating exactly which commitment must be weakened or abandoned and at what cost. Treat my position set, not presentism in general, as the object of analysis; trace precisely what commits me to what.
