I got pulled into this because my grandma's town in the South just voted to keep its confederate statue, and now I'm comparign how different countries deal with bad history. Germany's Vergangenheitsbewältigung is all official guilt and school programs, Japan barely mentions comfort women in textbooks but has these local museums, Turkey criminalizes mentioning 1915, and the American South uses heritage framing to defend monuments. The pattern I keep seeing is that societies build this big remembrance apparatus—memorials, apologies, curricula—that looks like accountability but actually works like moral credit. Once you've 'worked through the past' you get immunized against saying present problems come from the same root. Germany points to Holocaust education while debating immigration in weird ways; Japan points to local peace museums while ignoring gender issues now. Is there a real concept for this? Not just hypocrisy, but the structural way remembrance becomes a substitute for institutional change? And how does it differ in places where the apparatus is missing or inverted, like Turkey or the South? I want to know if there's a name, but mainly I want the edges: what seperates this 'credit' version from simple denial or from actual transitional justice?
