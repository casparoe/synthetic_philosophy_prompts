After a long and somewhat circular argument with a friend about whether the past is literally non-existent or merely elsewhere in the block of time, I want to map exactly where our disagreements lie rather than trading familiar labels. For a metaphysics reading group, please construct a decision tree—built from logical combinations of answers rather than from a pre-existing list of named theories—that sorts positions on temporal ontology. The tree should have four branching nodes, each phrased as a concrete claim a participant could accept or reject without knowing which philosophical label it produces. First: is temporal passage ontologically real, or is it reducible to psychological or perceptual asymmetry? Second: if passage is real, must there be a single globally privileged present (monadic presentism), or can presentness be indexed to an observer’s temporal location without ontological privilege—call this hypothetical stance “indexical presentism,” regardless of whether anyone actually defends it? Third: can cross-temporal relations such as causation and being earlier than hold between an existing present entity and a non-existing past entity, or do they require both relata to exist? Fourth: can the direction of passage be fully derived from the asymmetry of causation, or must it be taken as primitive? For each leaf, provide the name of an actual position if one occupies that combination—e.g., presentism, the growing-block view, the moving-spotlight theory, eternalism or block-universe views—or label it “unnamed” and supply one sentence on its most awkward commitment or on why the combination may be unstable or unoccupied. Please flag any pair of leaves that your tree genuinely fails to separate, particularly whether indexical presentism collapses into a perspectival variant of eternalism. Keep the tone formal and precise, do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and design the questions so that a reader answers them by belief rather than by recognizing a theory’s name.
