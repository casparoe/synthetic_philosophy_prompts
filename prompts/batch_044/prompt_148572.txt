Hey, I'm on our university's interdisciplinary grant board and we have to vote next week. There's a proposal for a pilot funding scheme — call it the recursive partnership model — for climate-adaptation research teams. It runs like a centipede game: each year both the PI and the university can choose to continue (the budget pool grows by a set factor) or take an immediate termination payout that's larger than the previous year's base but smaller than the potential final pool. The idea is to lock in long collaborations. The problem is backwards induction: rational agents should work back from the final round and exit immediately in year one, which makes the scheme pointless. One faction wants to reject it outright on that logic; another wants to approve but add a reputation-escrow clause that penalizes early exit. I need a memo with a concrete recomendation — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — and it should deal seriously with the strongest counter-argument, especially the point that reputation penalties don't actually solve the induction problem, they just relocate it. Please keep it grounded in the actual mechanics, not a lit review, and address whether the scheme is salvageable with modifications.
