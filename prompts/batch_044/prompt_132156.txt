Give an example of a conceptual issue in Sartre's framework of radical freedom and bad faith—specifically one that arises not from the familiar denial of freedom, but from its hyper-authentic exercise within an asymmetrical care relationship. Consider a long-term caregiver for a partner with progressive neurodegenerative disease who deliberately refuses every socially prescribed caregiver role, insisting instead on a radically free, moment-to-moment renegotiation of commitment in order to avoid falling into bad faith through identification with a fixed essence. The conceptual tension should concern whether this extreme acknowledgment of transcendence over facticity collapses into a different kind of bad faith—one that denies the partner's need for stable intersubjective recognition—and whether Sartre's ontology of the Look can account for harm inflicted not by objectification, but by an excess of authentic subjectivity on one side of the relation. In particular, does the case show that radical freedom requires a certain voluntary self-constraint to preserve the conditions for the other's freedom, and if so, does that constraint contradict Sartre's claim that we are always radically free to choose our values regardless of circumstance?
