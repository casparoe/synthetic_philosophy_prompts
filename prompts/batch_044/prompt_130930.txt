ok so I'm running these bi-monthly workshops at a community center where we pick one biography and ask what virtue people can actually learn from it, not just admire. I'm trying to adapt something like Plutarch's pairing method but with this twist I call structured defect comparison — you don't learn by copying the hero, you learn by finding the specific failure or limitation (the defect window) structurally tied to the virtue, then comparing that defect across two figures so virtue becomes visible without falling into hagiography. I need a reusable decision procedure my co-facilators can run in about 20 minutes before selecting a text, with questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands to me because it's values not facts. It needs to cover selecting the primary figure, locating the defect window, pairing with a second figure who has a different defect around the same virtue, checking we aren't implying failure is necessary for virtue, and writing the discussion question. Apply it to three cases: (1) a well-known abolitionist whose private letters show serious personal cruelty paired with an obscure local organizer who was kind but ineffective, (2) a religious figure treated mostly as legend with almost no documented failure in the record, and (3) a political leader whose strategic patience required complicity in a harmful policy — I want the procedure to flag or reject this one even though I think we should include them. Mark exactly where judgment can't be eliminated, say what the procedure can't do if the group just wants inspiration not analysis, and make sure one answer is one I won't like.
