I am preparing an analytical presentation for my high school philosophy seminar, and I want to examine a concrete episode through the lens of cross-cultural variation in philosophical intuitions rather than treating the theory only in the abstract. The episode I have in mind is the discovery of the Knobe effect in the early 2000s—where participants tended to judge that a corporate executive who foreseeably harmed the environment acted intentionally, while one who foreseeably helped did not—and the subsequent attempts, roughly a decade later, to replicate that asymmetry in East Asian and other non-Western populations, many of which yielded weaker or null results. I want a disciplined mapping: who the relevant actors were (the original researchers, the replication teams, and the philosophers of action who later built arguments about the concept of intentional action on this asymmetry), what the theory of cross-cultural intuition-variation predicts they should have found, and which features of the episode the theory explains well—for instance, whether the divergence in results genuinely reflects culturally shaped moral cognition. At the same time, I want an honest account of where the theory is silent or misapplied: specifically, whether the replication failures might instead reveal that the original effect tracks a linguistically specific feature of English aspectual constructions rather than a deep cognitive universal, and whether that possibility undermines the philosophical arguments that treated the Knobe asymmetry as data for a universal concept of intentional action. Finally, I want to know what this episode shows in return about the theory—does it confirm that analytic philosophy’s reliance on armchair intuition risks a WEIRD sampling bias, does it reveal that the cross-cultural literature was testing the wrong kind of claim all along, or does it suggest that linguistic artifacts and cultural variation are so intertwined that the theory needs a more fine-grained taxonomy of which intuitions are genuinely philosophical and which are merely semantic?
