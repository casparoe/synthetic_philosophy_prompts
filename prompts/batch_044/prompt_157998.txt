ok heres where i stand on moral saints and i want a consistency check before i present it in seminar:

1. i basically agree with the standard view that a maximally moral person sounds kind of exhausting and probably not living the best human life.
2. but if we imagine someone who is maximally good without being miserable, i don't see why they shouldn't be that way - there's no virtue in being less good.
3. i also think the inefficacy objection applies here: one moral saint doesn't fix systemic injustice, so being maximally good might be irrational if the costs are high and the impact is near zero.
4. heres the weird part i added: i think there's a hidden temporal problem. if you go maximally hard now, you burn out and your lifetime moral output is lower. so "maximal" point-in-time goodness conflicts with maximal integrated goodness over your whole life.
5. i'm a moral realist about all this, so i think there's a real fact about whether (4) is correct.

do these hang together? specifically i'm worried that (2) and (3) pull against each other, and that (4) introduces a kind of consequentialist calculus that doesn't fit with (1). which pairs actually conflict and what's the cheapest fix? also am i smuggling in something about personal identity over time that i haven't defended?
