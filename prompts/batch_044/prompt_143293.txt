I'm prepping a seminar on mind and meaning, and I keep noticing that institutions treat AI output differently depending on whether a human "curated" it. Take the Copyright Office: pure AI generation gets no registration, but if you select and arrange outputs it's yours. Same with journal authorship rules — Nature won't list an LLM as author because it can't take responsibility, even if it wrote paragraphs. Do these practices presuppose Searle's distinction — that only biological brains carry original intentionality, so AI is just Dennett's two-bitser shuffling derived symbols? Or could a Dennett-style stance, where derived intentionality is functionally real, still justify drawing the line at human selection? And is the distinction even about intentionality, or is it about causal control — like, we don't deny photographers copyright because cameras lack minds? I want the implicit theory reconstructed, where it's underdetermined, and whether the two rules are consisant with each other. Also, if I'm wrong that these rules care about mind at all — maybe they're just bureacratic risk-management — say so plainly instead of humoring the framing.
