I'm the student affairs compliance officer at a mid-size public university, and I have to rule on maybe five cultural or religious accomodation requests a week — prayer spaces, dining exemptions, dress-code waivers, holiday conflicts. There's never a framework. I want a decision procedure I can run in about fifteen minutes, grounded in the real tension between French laïcité (the public institution should stay blind, not fund or spotlight religion in shared space) and Anglo-American multiculturalism (equal citizenship sometimes means treating people differently so group difference doesn't become exclusion).

Turn that into ordered questions I ask in sequence, with what counts as a sufficient yes/no at each step, where the procedure stops with a hard no, where it hands off to a values call I have to flag for the dean, and where it misfires. Inject two practitioner checks: an "invisibility audit" — not just is religion present, but does the accomodation force people who don't share it to navigate around a religious marker in shared institutional space? — and a "burden-shift check" that asks who bears the cost of denial: a private cost the student absorbs, or does it push them out of a public good?

Then run it on two requests:
1. A student org wants university funds and permanent signage for a prayer room used mainly by Muslim students, open to all but in the student center.
2. A chemistry student wants to wear a loose religious sleeve under the required fitted lab coat, offering an extra flame-resistant layer.

One should come out with an answer I probably won't like — say which and why. Also say exactly what the procedure can't do: what kinds of case it has no grip on, and where it will definitely misfire. Keep it practical — I have to sign off, I don't have a philosophy degree, and I can't call an expert every time.
