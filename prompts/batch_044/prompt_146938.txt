I just finished a long and somewhat circular argument with a close friend about Dennett's multiple drafts model, and I am still unsure whether our disagreement was genuinely about the nature of consciousness or merely about explanatory taste. We began with the familiar objection to the Cartesian theater—the idea that there is no single place in the brain where everything comes together for a central observer—but my friend pressed a sharper point: distributing drafts across temporal and spatial scales might not eliminate the homunculus so much as replace one central viewer with a distributed committee of sub-personal processes that still implicitly requires a chairperson to adjudicate conflicts. From there we drifted into whether draft competition necessarily presupposes something like a global workspace, whether the apparent unity of perceptual binding can be accommodated without smuggling a theater back in through hierarchical predictive processing, and whether the model can make sense of the temporal thickness of experience—what philosophers have called the specious present—once we abandon a single narrative stream.

I want to understand which of these open lines actually matter most for someone working in cognitive architecture rather than pure metaphysics, and in what order they should be pursued. Specifically, is the global workspace question upstream of both binding and temporal dynamics, or are those largely independent tracks? And which of these problems is tractable for a philosopher without heavy neuroscience training, versus being an empirical question that philosophy should leave alone for now? Please rank the main unresolved issues and defend the ordering: I need to know not just what remains open, but which resolution would genuinely shift downstream debates about personal identity, about the explanatory gap, or about whether artificial systems running multi-draft architectures could be said to have experience at all. I am writing this up for a small reading group, so please avoid bibliographies or citation lists; I can look those up myself once I know which threads to pull.
