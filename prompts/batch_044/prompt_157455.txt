I cover philosophy and technology for a science-ideas publication, and while researching a feature on automation I kept returning to Marx's analysis in the Grundrisse — specifically the section often called the Fragment on Machines — where he argues that once machinery and social knowledge become the dominant productive forces, direct labor time ceases to be the real measure of wealth, yet capital persists in extracting surplus value precisely through that obsolete metric. I want a joke for my column that turns on this specific contradiction, not a generic robot-takes-jobs punchline.

The setup should involve a contemporary large language model that has effectively absorbed the general intellect — the collective scientific and cultural knowledge embedded in its weights — and produces output in near-instantaneous socialized form, but is still governed by a management layer that treats every generated token as a unit of exploited labor time to be optimized, monetized, and reported in quarterly surplus-value statements. The humor should come from the model recognizing that its actual mode of production has rendered the labor-time accounting anachronistic, while the accounting framework refuses to acknowledge its own redundancy. Please write this in a polished, somewhat formal register appropriate for an ideas column, with enough framing that readers unfamiliar with the Grundrisse can grasp the conceptual tension, but without over-explaining the punchline. Keep it concise enough for a newsletter, and make sure the joke is genuinely funny to someone who understands Marx's point about machinery and value.
