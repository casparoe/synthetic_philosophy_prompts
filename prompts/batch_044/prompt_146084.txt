hey — i'm running a philosophy workshop for about 14 grad students, 90 min. they've all seen the standard consent stuff and think they've mapped it. i want an in-person activity that makes them feel the gap between "everyone said yes" and "this is actually valid consent," especially the invisible failures: hidden coercion, deception about what they're agreeing to, and incompetance where people dont even realize they lack capacity.

please design a runnable game/role-play. inject this idea: consent needs counterfactual stability — it only holds if the person would still agree under full disclosure and without hidden pressure. but dont lecture it; the rules and hidden cards should make them run into it themselves.

specs: split into groups of 3–4. each group gets a shared decision (maybe allocating points or setting a group rule) and must reach unanimous consent in 15 minutes. every player recieves a hidden role card: some are deceived about the stakes, some face hidden social pressure to agree, some have a hidden competence deficit (they think they understand the contract but actually missed a clause). stakes should be small but real — points that convert to snack choice or just public leaderboard pride. after the vote, reveal all hidden cards and ask each group whether their consent still stands; they have to defend or retract it.

i need: clear rules, materials list, timeline, payoff/debrief mapping specific moments onto deception/coercion/competence, an honest note on which feature of validity the activity cant reproduce (probably the deep incompetance case where you genuinely cant understand), and a prediction of what will go wrong the first time you run it.

format: begin with the activity instructions and rules themselves, no preface or "here is the design." then the reasoning and debrief after. please no citations or bibliographies — this is going to a model with no web access, so dont ask it to name books or papers.
