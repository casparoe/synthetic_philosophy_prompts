I am designing evaluation protocols for autonomous agents that must operate in open-ended environments where no complete utility function can be specified in advance. A colleague recently proposed that we could define “low-impact” negatively—as bounded divergence between actual world-trajectories and a counterfactual baseline in which the agent never activated—thereby avoiding any explicit commitment to which features of the world are worth preserving. The more I work with this framework, the more I suspect that selecting the dimensions along which divergence is measured (energy flows, information states, biological patterns) already embeds an implicit axiology, and that pretending otherwise is not neutrality but a concealed ontology: a hidden decision about what counts as damage.

Write a poem in blank verse of about twenty-six lines, spoken in the voice of an engineer who has just reached this realization. Structure it in three movements: the first, in regular iambic pentameter, articulates the elegance of measuring impact as pure divergence without value-laden endpoints; the second, breaking into shorter, fractured lines with disrupted rhythm, records the crisis of recognizing that every choice of baseline feature—what is held constant—smuggles in a judgment about what matters; the third, returning to longer lines but with irregular caesuras and partial rhyme, accepts that genuinely low-impact reasoning may require an explicit, minimal axiological commitment rather than feigning absence. Let the formal shift from regular meter to fracture and partial restoration mirror the conceptual shift from false neutrality to acknowledged commitment. The poem should be technically recognizable to someone familiar with divergence-based impact measures and the baseline-selection problem in AI alignment, yet it should not resolve into either triumphalist transparency or despairing rejection of constraint. End with the tension intact: the recognition that avoiding value specification is itself a value choice, and that this may be the unavoidable price of coherence.
