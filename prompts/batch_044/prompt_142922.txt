I've come around to a kind of hybrid view I'm calling epistemic escrow: we have a Clifford-style duty not to believe on insufficient evidence for beliefs that feed into collective decision-making, but for beliefs that are purely self-constituting — like whether suffering has cosmic meaning — James is right that we may choose among live, forced, and momentous options, provided we hold them in escrow, meaning we don't treat them as knowledge-claims and stay open to revising them if they start predicting false empirical outcomes. What would count against this? I want to know which parts are most exposed: is the public/private split unstable? Does escrow just collapse back into ordinary agnosticism? Could a Cliffordian show that even private beliefs leak into public harm? And has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability — if any counterexample can be dismissed by saying that belief wasn't properly in escrow, is that a fatal flaw or just good taxonomy? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones defenders of escrow would actually concede rather than dismiss. I'm especially curious whether there's a principled way to seperate the two domains without smuggling in arbitrary preferences.
