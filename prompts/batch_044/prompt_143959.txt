I write a column on science and philosophy of mind, and next week I'm interviewing a philosopher who defends a specific resolution to the meta-problem of consciousness. Her view is that the explanatory gap is not a metaphysical failure but a normative asymmetry: phenomenal concepts carry what she calls an "indexical entitlement"—an implicit epistemic license tied to first-person access—so that the absence of a physical derivation feels like a deficit even though it is only a methodological boundary. I need eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond restating this position. Specifically, I want at least one question pressing how this "indexical entitlement" differs from a standard phenomenal-concept strategy without merely renaming it; one asking whether her account predicts that sufficiently advanced artificial predictive architectures would eventually acquire the same normative asymmetry; and one exploring what the view implies for the meta-problem in non-linguistic animals that lack the practices she claims generate the entitlement. For each of these three, include a sharp follow-up tailored to the answer she is most likely to offer—particularly the reply that the asymmetry is purely pragmatic. Please draft the full set in American English.
