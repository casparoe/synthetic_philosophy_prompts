Consider three related institutional practices. First, elementary handwriting instruction in many countries teaches children one canonical double-story lowercase “a” and marks the single-story circular form as an error in that context, even though both shapes appear constantly in printed text. Second, typographers and graphic designers treat “a” as a single category that legitimately includes serif, sans-serif, script, blackletter, and hand-drawn variants with wildly different geometries, with no sense that any one shape is more genuinely the letter than another. Third, copyright regimes protect the digital software file of a typeface but deny protection to the abstract letterform itself, treating the shape of an “a” as an unownable idea rather than a protectable design.
