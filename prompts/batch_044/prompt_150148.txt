Compare two ontological accounts of sociological generations—Baby Boomers, Millennials, Generation Z, and comparable cohort labels. First, treat generations as homeostatic property clusters: real kinds sustained by shared exposure to macro-historical events, economic conditions, and technological regimes that stabilize clusters of psychological and behavioral traits. Second, treat them as interactive kinds—where the act of labeling, marketing, and measuring a cohort produces the very behavioral regularities the label claims merely to describe, with demographic discourse looping back to shape self-conception and consumption patterns. Focus specifically on how each account handles three pressure points: (1) the existence of “micro-generations” (e.g., Xennials, Zillennials) that straddle conventional birth-year boundaries; (2) empirical patterns in which intra-cohort variance exceeds inter-cohort variance on most psychological traits except technology-adoption curves; and (3) the retrospective, externally imposed nature of generational boundaries by demographers and advertisers rather than by self-identification. For each pressure point, say which account suffers more severely and whether the damage is fatal or merely awkward. Then state how confident you are in your comparative assessments overall, and identify the specific claim about which you are least certain.
