my roommate and her study partner got stuck arguing about whether Hart actually demolished Devlin or just moved the goalposts. (1) she said Devlin was never saying society needs every shared value to survive, but that specific cooperative practices — like joint farming pools or communal water rights — depend on norms that have to be enforced if the practice is going to exist at all. Without legal backup people free-ride, the practice collapses, and thats harm to the participants, not just moral disgust. So Hart's harm/morality split misses the point because here the harm is literally constituted by breaking the moral rule. (2) he said thats just relabeling — she defines harm as whatever happens when norms break, which is exactly Devlins trick with social disintegration. If harm just means collapse of whatever practice you like, then the harm principle proves nothing and allows everything. (3) she answered theres a real structural difference: in a voluntary cooperative the norms are constitutive, not background culture. You opt in knowing the rules, so enforcing them is like contract law with teeth, not like policing private vice. Hart attacked state-imposed morality on non-consenting adults; this is closer to enforcing the terms of a voluntary association. (4) he came back that once the state generalizes those norms — applies them outside the cooperative or pushes people into joining — youre right back in legal moralism. And she hasnt shown a principled line between contract enforcement and moral policing. He also said her constitutive-norms talk is just institutional functionalism, which Hart already allows, so shes not saying anything new. (5) she said thats precisely her point — Hart allows protecting institutions but pretends its not moralism, which shows his distinction is unstable when you look closely. Can you go through the five moves, rate each from clearly wrong to clearly right, and say who is more right overall? Also, is there actually a middle ground here or are they just talking past each other?
