Please draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Structure it as follows. Second, a disagreements table: state the precise formulations of divergence—specifically, on whether substrate-independent functional equivalence is sufficient for moral attribution, on whether graded sentience can exist in the absence of a global self-model, and on whether ecological embeddedness and homeostatic regulation are necessary conditions for genuine experience—phrased so that each camp would accept the wording describing its own view. Third, the cruxes: identify the sub-questions that, if settled, would move at least one side, including whether there exists a non-arbitrary threshold in information integration corresponding to a qualitative moral boundary, and whether the risk of false-positive attribution should outweigh the risk of false-negative omission in policy design. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke obscure named theories, specific researchers, or dated works; and avoid framing the disagreement as resolvable by appeal to authority. Write in formal, polished prose appropriate for an ideas section of a general-interest science publication.
