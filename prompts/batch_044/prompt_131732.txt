I am preparing a position statement for a graduate seminar on diachronic rationality and commitment, and I want a consistency check before I present. I have settled on the following package of views, but I worry that I may be quietly helping myself to premises that do not actually cohere. First, I accept the standard Parfitian hitchhiker result: rational agency can require an ex post act—paying the driver—that is locally irrational, because an ex ante plan can bind an agent through predictive correlation rather than present causal efficacy. Second, I take this to show that diachronic rationality is not reducible to a sequence of time-sliced maximizations; the normative force in the hitchhiker case derives from the structural relation between planning and execution, not from any present evidential update. Third, I reject causal decision theory in one-shot cases on the grounds that it systematically ignores evidential correlations, yet I also deny that evidential decision theory by itself explains the hitchhiker obligation, since at the moment of decision there is no live evidential pathway linking my present payment to the driver's prior belief about me. Fourth, I distinguish this sharply from counterfactual mugging: once the randomizing event is past, there is no predictive mechanism to honor, and refusal to pay is not a rational failure. Fifth, I endorse a principle I call structural continuity—namely, that the normative principle governing my choice at the point of action ought to be identical to the principle that governed my planning, unless there has been a genuine change in epistemic or practical circumstances, not merely the passage of time. Sixth, I hold that predictive dependencies with very small probabilities should be treated as decision-theoretically negligible, so that rational agents are not required to perform costly acts to preserve correlations that are barely live possibilities.

Rank the tensions by severity, specifying for each whether the difficulty lies in my account of the hitchhiker, my rejection of both causal and evidential explanations, my treatment of counterfactual mugging, or my principle of structural continuity.
