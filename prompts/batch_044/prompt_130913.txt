I run a philosophy of history seminar with about 14 grad students, one 90-minute session. I want them to feel in their bones the problem of counterfactual substitutability — whether Hitler was structurally overdetermined or a true bottleneck that only he could fill. Design a two-round coalition game: round one gives hidden role cards (industry, army, SA leaders, left parties) plus payoff cards showing everyone fears chaos but wants authoritarian stabilization; there's a Hitler token with special coalition-building powers. Round two removes the token entirely but keeps all crisis conditions (Versailles resentment, economic collapse, fear of communism). They must either form an authoritarian government without him or frature into civil war. Use point payoffs, not real money, and rules that force the same strategic bind both rounds so they actually experience whether the system pulls in a substitute demagogue or collapses. Include a debrief that maps each coalition move onto structural pressure vs personal agency, warn me what predictably breaks the first run (probably they ignore framing and just max points?), and be honest about what the game can't reproduce — maybe the actual psychological grip of his rhetoric or the sheer contingency of his surviving 1930. No lecture up front, don't name the theories until after they play, and I don't need any bibliographies.
