Write a dialogue between a hardline deontologist who thinks agent-centered restrictions are unconditional and a "reflexive threshold" theorist who thinks deontic rules need internal escape clauses to avoid self-defeat, arguing over the paradox of deontology in the "rebound case": you have an absolute duty not to push someone off a bridge, but the only way to stop a runaway trolley from killing five involves pushing that same person—does the duty against pushing generate a contradictory duty to push? The reflexive theorist should argue that without an embedded exception clause, deontology actualy collapses into incoherence, but that the clause can't be read as weighing outcomes. Make it real back-and-forth—each has to answer the other's last point, not talk past them. Keep it tight, no bibliographys or citations, and don't let either side be a strawman.
