in AI alignment circles people keep comparing process-based supervision to a restaurant health inspection and outcome-based to just tasting the dish. the chef is the model, the inspector checks the kitchen (training data and reward shaping), the customer eats the output, and food poisoning is some harmful or deceptive generation. its used to argue that since we cant taste every dish an LLM might serve to millions of users, we need stricter kitchen audits before deployment. but i feel like this hides a lot: in a real restaurant the chef has intentions and the kitchen is physical, but for an LLM the "kitchen" is just gradient updates and hidden reasoning chains, and the "tasting" is distributed across users after release with no single inspector present. can you lay out the full correspondence table -- chef, inspector, customer, poison, menu, maybe the recipe -- then the disanalogies that actually matter for the safety conclusion, and tell me whether the preference for process supervision still stands once the analogy is stripped away? also, what happens if the "inspector" is itself another LLM auditing chain-of-thought? does that break the analogy further, or does it actually make the kitchen-inspection picture work better?
