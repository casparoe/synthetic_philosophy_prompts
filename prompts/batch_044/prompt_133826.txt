I'm writing a column on science and ideas and just rewatched Blade Runner — the 82 version — for the first time in ages. The replicants have implanted memories and respond to the Voight-Kampff test like they feel real emotion, which made me think about Dennett's two-bitser, that robot with only derived intentionality copying human scripts, versus Searle saying syntax is never semantics without original biological intentionality. But here's where I get tangled: if we take Dennett's intentional stance toward Roy Batty, we treat his monologue about attack ships and C-beams as meaningful because it predicts behavior, but Searle would say we're just projecting onto a system with no original intentionality. Does Blade Runner dramatize that the intentional stance is enough — that Roy's suffering makes him more than a two-bitser — or does the film secretly side with Searle by making bioengineering the reason replicants can eventually acheive and recieve original intentionality? I want a reading that takes both the movie and the Dennett/Searle debate seriously, and asks whether derived intentionality coupled with embodied trauma and false memory can generate something that functions like original intentionality even if it started as copy-paste.
