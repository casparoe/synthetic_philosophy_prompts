Write a dialogue between a secularist sociologist and a religious ethicist arguing about whether secular societies can maintain deep interpersonal trust without a shared transcendent framework. They should discuss what "more moral" even means — is it just lower crime rates, or something about the quality of obligations? The secularist should mount a defence where trust is sustained by institutional transparency and behavioural reciprocity norms, not metaphysics. The ethicist should push back that those norms are parasitic on older religious moral grammars and cant self-sustain once the metaphysics is gone. Make them respond to each other properly — no strawmen. Also have them consider a specific edge case: a society where everyone is perfectly rational and cooperative but nobody ever feels genuinly guilty for free-riding, only calculates risk. Is that "more moral" or not? Dont ask for citations or reading lists — just argue it out.
