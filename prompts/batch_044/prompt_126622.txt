everyone in my seminar keeps using the house analogy for historical materialism: the economic base is the foundation, law and culture are the rooms, and if you want to change the layout you've got to start with the concrete, not the wallpaper. its used to argue that ideas never drive change on their own, that the base explains everything else. but this feels too neat, and my friend says its like saying plumbing can never crack the walls. can you lay out the correspondance table precisely — what's the foundation, the load-bearing walls, the plumbing, the architect? then tell me where the analogy actually breaks: can the 'rooms' feed back and damage the foundation? does it smuggle in a one-way causality Marx wouldn't have liked? and once the disanalogies are on the table, does the conclusion that material conditions determine everything survive, or was the house image carrying the whole argument?
