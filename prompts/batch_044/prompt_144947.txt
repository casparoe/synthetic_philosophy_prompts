I've been trying to draft a short essay for my philosophy club about Epicurus and whether death can actually harm you, but I keep getting stuck on a weird metaphysical angle that feels half-baked. The usual debate is between Epicurus—death is nothing to us because there's no subject left to suffer—and the deprivation account, which says death is bad because it robs you of future goods you'd have experienced. I'm genuinely unsure whether this distinction does any real work or if I'm just renaming the deprivation account with process-language and calling it a win for Epicurus. I also want to check if there's an obvious mistake in saying that the badness is in the truncation itself—does that smuggle in a subject who is harmed by the truncation, which is exactly what Epicurus denies?
