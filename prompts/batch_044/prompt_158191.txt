Student answer:

Brain size is clearly relevant, but only indirectly. The encephalisation quotient shows that humans have larger brains relative to body size than most mammals, which explains our advanced cognition. Therefore, an entity with a larger EQ has greater moral status. This was recognised by evolutionary biologists who developed EQ as a normative scale for comparing species. When we turn to artificial systems, the analogy is direct: a neural network with more parameters has greater cognitive capacity, just as a brain with more neurons does. For instance, a model with hundreds of billions of parameters exceeds human neuronal counts in morally relevant scale. Hence, gross scale is not merely correlational but constitutive of moral standing, both biologically and artificially. The mistake philosophers like Singer make is to focus on sentience alone; they miss that scale itself generates the capacity for sentience.
