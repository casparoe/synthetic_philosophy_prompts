I sit on my city's Emergency Health Allocation Board, and we must vote next Thursday on whether to adopt a new hospital triage protocol ahead of the winter surge. The protocol was built using what its designers call "threshold satisficing": instead of computing an optimal allocation across every scenario, it selects the first plan that meets pre-set floors for survival rate, neighborhood equity, and staff feasibility, then stops searching. Our charter contains a clause I keep returning to: it requires "proportionate deliberation," meaning our decision process must match the urgency and the information actually available, not an ideal standard that ignores real constraints. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approve, approve with conditions, or reject with a specific revised timeline—and it should treat the "maximize lives saved" objection seriously rather than dismissing it as impatience.
