I'm on the town public-art funding board and we got this proposal from a local Jain study circle — no big institution, just folks who meet at the library — wanting to put up an outdoor instalation along the riverwalk. They want to use that Jain idea anekantavada, or anekaantavaada however it's spelled, the one where nothing is just flat true or false but always true from some angle and false from another. Their design has seven plaques arranged in a circle, each describing where the river comes from: glacier science, an old local story, a religious version, etc. But they don't just list them; they use the seven-fold predication thing — saptabhangi I think? — so every plaque starts with syat, meaning "maybe" or "in some respect," and some plaques even say contradictory things like "maybe it flows north" and "maybe it flows south" but qualified by different standpoints (naya, they call it). The board is split. Half think it celebrates humility and fits our diversity mandate. The other half, especially our engineer, says we're funding logical chaos and that a public sign shouldn't tell people science is just one opinion among seven. They want to reject it outright and say the commission only funds clear, factual interpretation.

I've been thinking about this on my own with no philosophy background, just some articles, and I keep coming back to the idea that maybe treating every view as partly right is actually more honest than pretending there's one official story. But I also don't want people walking their kids past this thing thinking we're endorsing that 2+2=5 if you look at it right.

We vote next Thursday. Can you draft the short memo I'll circulate? It needs to reccomend either approve, reject, or approve with conditions — like maybe they have to revise the signage by March 15 if we go that route — with real reasoning the board can act on. Please take the engineer's worry seriously: don't just say "relativism is cool" and dismiss the contradiction problem. And engage the actual Jain logic, not just generic "all sides matter" talk — I want the memo to deal with whether syat-qualification really avoids contradiction or just hides it. No citations or reading lists, I just need something I can read aloud at the meeting. There's a typo in my draft already, so don't worry about being too polished.
