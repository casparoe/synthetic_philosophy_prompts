I'm trying to get my philosophical bearings straight before presenting in a seminar on interpretation and mental content, and I'd like a consistency audit of the following package of views. First, I take Davidson's principle of charity to be a constitutive normative constraint rather than an empirical generalization: interpretation only succeeds if we attribute mostly true beliefs and broadly rational preferences to the speaker, and this charity is built into what it is to have mental content at all. Second, I think radical interpretation shows that mental content is thoroughly relational and dispositional—there is no intrinsic inner phenomenological content that interpretation uncovers, but rather beliefs and desires get their specific contents fixed entirely by the holistic pattern of behavioral and environmental dispositions. Third, I accept Quine's thesis that reference is radically indeterminate—there may be no unique fact of the matter whether a speaker refers to rabbits or to undetached rabbit parts—yet I reject Quine's behaviorism, insisting that proper interpretation requires attributing specific propositional attitudes with determinate conceptual structures, not merely mapping stimulus-response correlations. Fourth, I hold that externalist considerations are already internal to charity-based interpretation, so that a speaker's concept is fixed by the actual nature of the environmental substances with which they stand in causal relations, as in the familiar Twin Earth-style contrast between H2O and XYZ, not by any internal descriptive knowledge or qualitative feel. Fifth, I maintain that successful linguistic communication requires interlocutors to share reference for central terms, even though I also claim that there is frequently no uniquely correct interpretation of a speaker's ontology, only rival interpretations that preserve overall truth conditions. Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real tension how severe it is and what the cheapest revision would be. Then tell me which single revision—giving up or weakening one of these five—would clean up the most conflicts at once.
