hey, so i'm running a small philosophy of law reading group — about 12 people, 75 minutes, mostly grad students but a couple undergrads who haven't read much camus. last week we got into it over whether the death penalty is just another punishment or something structurally different, and someone brought up camus's essay on the guillotine. i haven't been able to stop thinking about his point that the state becomes a murderer with paperwork, and especially the part where he says the condemned dies twice: once when the sentence is fixed and once when it's actually carried out. i want an in-class acitivity that makes people feel that structural trap, not just discuss it.

the core idea i want them to run into: if you build appeals into the system to avoid killing the wrong person, you don't solve the problem — you just extend the first death, stretching out the psychological destruction. and if you skip appeals to avoid that suffering, you accept a higher risk of executing an innocent person. there should be no clean choice, just like camus describes.

can you design a role-play or structured game? setting: 12 participants, one table, 75 minutes. they should come away seeing that the cruelty isn't an accidental feature but built into the bureaucracy itself. include clear roles or rules, materials (cards, tokens, whatever), stakes that don't involve real money, the session flow, a debrief that maps moments onto camus's argument, and an honest note on what part of his view can't be reproduced here (probably the actual physical horror of execution?). also tell me what will predicatbly go wrong the first time — like someone refusing to vote, or the group finding a loophole.

please don't ask them to research stats or cite sources; keep it conceptual. and no reading lists, i just need the design. thanks!
