Compare endurantism and perdurantism, but skip the usual "are you wholley present at each time" recap. I want the comparison centered on something weirder: call it modal leakage. The idea is that if some future temporal part (or future stage, for the endurantist) undertakes a long-term commitment, that commitment binds me now in a way that isn't just causal influence. On perdurantism, is it easier to explain this because the future part is literally part of the four-dimensional worm? Or is it actually harder, since that part is spatiotemporaly distant and maybe not "me" in any practical sense? Meanwhile, can an endurantist say the present-wholley-object bears the property of being-committed-to-something-decades-away without smuggling in temporal parts? I don't want a bibliography, just which view actually has the cleaner story here, and where each one gets stuck. Also, does either view have to say that breaking that future commitment is literally destroying part of yourself?
