I'm working on a feature about moral emotion ecology and I've built an argument that seems to prove cultures can't actually choose between guilt and shame. It feels suspicious.

(1) If a culture can genuinely choose its dominant moral emotion, then it must be able to suppress one and cultivate the other without the social fabric tearing.
(2) Shame requires an audience — real or imagined — to operate; guilt requires only an internal standard.
(3) So a culture that picks shame is outsourcing regulation to visibility, not making a free emotional choise.
(4) But then rational shame-adoption commits a society to high transparency — constant social monitoring.
(5) Therefore no liberal society could rationally choose shame; it must pick guilt or deny cultural choice exists.
(6) Yet many liberal societies clearly centre shame-like norms — think of the affective division of labour where public failure is shamed but private guilt is ignored — without being authoritarian.
(7) So either (4) overstates the surveillance needed, or (6) misdescribes those societies.
(8) But if shame can work without visibility, (2) collapses and shame becomes indistinguishable from guilt, leaving nothing for culture to choose.

I'd rather hang on to (1) and (2). Where's the fault, and what does fixing it cost? Answer in British English please, and keep it in the weeds — no reading lists, just work out the reasoning.
