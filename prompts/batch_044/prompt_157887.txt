Consider five proposed resolutions or refinements of superrational cooperation in symmetric one-shot games: (i) pure reasoning-based symmetry breaking without external correlation; (ii) epsilon-superrationality, in which cooperation is sustained only when an agent assigns near-certain credence to identical reasoning in the counterpart; (iii) a causal-graph restriction that treats the opponent's decision node as non-intervenable and therefore excludes strategic influence; (iv) an evolutionary-stability claim that superrational cooperation persists in populations with perfect reasoning transparency but collapses under mutation that introduces opacity; and (v) a contractarian pre-commitment mechanism that binds reasoning processes ex ante, rendering cooperation rational before the game is instantiated. Rank these five approaches from most to least defensible specifically against the objection that superrational cooperation unravels under minute asymmetries in information access or reasoning architecture. Assign each a tier—S, A, B, C, or D—and provide exactly one precise sentence per tier explaining its placement, identifying the structural feature that either withstands or succumbs to that asymmetry. Do not provide bibliographic references; reason directly from the internal logic of each proposal.
