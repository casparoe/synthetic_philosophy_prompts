I understand the standard formulation: prioritarianism assigns greater weight to benefits received by those who are worse off, yet critics argue that this can justify reducing the welfare of the better-off even when no one is made better off, solely to achieve equality at a lower level. What I want to understand is which unresolved issues in this neighborhood actually carry conceptual weight for practical moral reasoning, and which are largely decorative complications.

Second, I have been thinking about a dynamic variant: if prioritarian weights apply not to a static snapshot of a population but to trajectories over time, does the objection become a problem about the moral status of transitional inequality rather than about equality itself? Among these considerations, together with more familiar questions—such as whether prioritarianism can be consistently paired with sufficientarian thresholds, or whether the objection collapses once we rigorously distinguish harming from failing to benefit—which lines of inquiry are genuinely upstream?
