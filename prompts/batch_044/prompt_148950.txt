Aristotle defines akrasia as knowing the right action in general but being pulled away by appetite at the particular moment. In the social media case, the worker knows in general that working is better, but at the exact second of reaching for the phone that general knowledge is suppressed—so they aren't fully responsible for the lapse. Hyperbolic discounting explains this suppression mechanically: present rewards are weighted exponentially more than distant ones, producing preference reversals between the planning phase and the action phase. This means Aristotle's failure of knowledge is really just a steep present-biased curve operating on attention. Because the model gives a complete causal account, there is no leftover agential failure—what Aristotle calls being overcome by passion is just the brain's present bias. Commitment devices like app blockers solve this by removing the option, which is functionally identical to Aristotelian continence, since both prevent the wrong choice from being available.
