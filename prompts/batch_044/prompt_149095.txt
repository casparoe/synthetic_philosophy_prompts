I am applying for my school's eight-week independent study fellowship in philosophy, and I need a polished two-page proposal. My project focuses on Benjamin Franklin's Art of Virtue—specifically his thirteen virtues, the weekly tracking grid, and the implicit rules he applies when virtues conflict—read through the framework of coherent extrapolated volition and idealized preference aggregation. The precise research question is whether Franklin's resolutions of virtue-conflicts, such as the tension between Order and Resolution, can be reconstructed as an informal consistency algorithm for aggregating contradictory idealized preferences, and whether his cyclical, asymptotic structure implies that moral perfection is unreachable by design rather than merely unattainable in practice. Existing treatments treat Franklin either as a biographical moralist or as a precursor to modern self-quantification; neither asks whether his conflict-resolution rules have formal properties analogous to preference-extrapolation conditions. I propose a philosophical reconstruction: extracting Franklin's implicit rules from the Autobiography, formalizing them as consistency conditions, and testing whether they preserve a stable ordering or collapse into ad hoc contextual judgments. A useful deliverable would be a short paper presenting the reconstructed rules, the structural comparison with preference-aggregation concepts, and an argument about asymptotic moral improvement. The project fails if the correspondence is purely metaphorical—if Franklin's resolutions lack any generalizable form beyond situational intuition. The scope is eight weeks of close reading and conceptual analysis, with no empirical component and no required bibliography beyond what I compile independently. Please write the proposal with: a sharply stated question; an explanation of why current historical and philosophical treatments leave it open; a justification for philosophical reconstruction over standard biographical scholarship; a description of the deliverable and explicit failure conditions; and a realistic timeline and scope appropriate for an eight-week fellowship.
