Here is an argument a study partner built to show that Dutch-book coherence is incompatible with rational belief revision over time. I think it proves too much, but I cannot locate the exact slip.

(1) The Dutch-book argument demonstrates that if an agent’s betting quotients violate the probability axioms, a bookie can combine bets—each individually acceptable to the agent—that together guarantee a net loss regardless of outcome.

(2) The standard defense of probabilism assumes that all bets in the book are placed and settled against a single, fixed set of credences.

(3) Rational agents, however, update continuously—for example, by Jeffrey conditioning on a stream of perceptual evidence—so their credences at t1 and t2 can differ in a fully legitimate way.

(4) Consider a “rolling-contract” bookie who offers bet B1 at t1 against event E at odds reflecting cr_t1(E) = 0.8, but embeds a clause that the settlement rate is locked to the agent’s own credence at the time of resolution, not at purchase.

(5) At t2 the agent rationally updates to cr_t2(E) = 0.2 and accepts bet B2 against E at the corresponding odds.

(6) At resolution, if E occurs, B1 pays out at the 0.8 rate and B2 pays out at the 0.2 rate; if ¬E occurs, the reverse holds. Because the stakes were calibrated to different credences but applied to the same outcome, the agent’s net position is negative in both worlds.

(7) Thus any agent who updates between accepting interlocking rolling contracts is guaranteed to lose, even though each individual bet was fair by her lights at the moment of acceptance and her updates were fully Bayesian.

(8) Call this a diachronic Dutch book. Because rational belief revision requires updating, every rational agent is vulnerable to it.

(9) The only available defense is to freeze one’s credences for the lifetime of all open bets.

(10) But freezing credences in the face of new evidence violates the very Bayesian norms that motivate probabilism in the first place.

(11) Therefore the Dutch-book argument for probabilism collapses: it demands static coherence, yet rationality requires dynamic revision, and the two are incompatible.
