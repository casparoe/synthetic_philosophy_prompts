I have been working through Dennett's writing on real patterns and the three stances—physical, design, and intentional—after it came up in a long forum thread about whether mental states are merely useful fictions. In a recent philosophy-of-mind blog post I read, the author summarises Dennett's position like this: Dennett holds that a pattern counts as genuinely real only when it is projectible from the intentional stance; the physical and design stances are merely computational shortcuts that save us from tracking beliefs and desires, but they do not correspond to any actual structures in the world. From this, the author infers that whenever a system can be explained at the design level—say, by evolutionary adaptation or functional organisation—there is no real pattern present, only a heuristic fiction. The blogger then claims that Dennett is therefore committed to a sharp ontological hierarchy: intentional-level patterns are real, design-level patterns are pragmatic illusions, and physical-level descriptions are bare mechanism with no predictive surplus. They conclude that this forces Dennett into a kind of panpsychism, or at least into treating only agents with beliefs and desires as genuinely real entities, since only they support the stance required for reality.

This strikes me as suspicious, not least because I had understood Dennett to be arguing that patterns are real precisely insofar as they support reliable predictions, regardless of which stance reveals them. I am also unsure whether he really demotes evolutionary explanations to mere fiction, given how often he invokes natural selection as a paradigmatic case of design-stance prediction. Could someone help me audit this summary? I would like to know which parts fairly represent Dennett's distinction between the stances and his criterion for real patterns, which parts subtly conflate predictive utility with ontological status, and whether the blogger's conclusion about panpsychism actually follows from any accurate part of the account. In particular, does Dennett think that design-stance patterns—like those revealed by biology—are less real than intentional-stance ones, or is the blogger smuggling in a distinction that Dennett's own anti-reductionism is meant to reject?
