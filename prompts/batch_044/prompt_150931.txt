Specify the desiderata that a philosophical theory of meaningful work must meet to be adequate in an economic context where automation severs the traditional link between productive labor, income, and social standing. For each requirement, state the motivating case or distinction: (1) it must differentiate meaning from mere instrumentality without reducing it to subjective preference; (2) it must explain why people seek work-like activity when material needs are satisfied—the post-work problem—and why automating away craft or care roles does not automatically render remaining human activities meaningless; (3) it must preserve the distinction between intrinsic task value and value as a source of recognition or status, and indicate whether automation threatens one, both, or neither; (4) it must accommodate empirical regularities—autonomy and mastery predict satisfaction more reliably than income, yet involuntary unemployment damages wellbeing beyond financial loss—without contradiction; (5) it must adjudicate whether meaningfulness resides in the activity itself, the social relation it sustains, or the narrative identity it supports, and forbid arbitrary collapse among these. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which reflect camp-specific commitments (e.g., Aristotelian flourishing, Marxist species-being, liberal recognition theories). Then, concisely, indicate how a purely subjective “calling” account, a purely objective “social contribution” account, and a hybrid “capability” framework each fare against the list, identifying the single requirement that eliminates the most candidates.
