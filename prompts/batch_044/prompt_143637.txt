The third is an artificial analog: a large language model produces a harmful output through its own weights, while an external safety filter that could have blocked the generation observes but doesn't intervene, and the model's architecture includes an internal self-monitoring layer that predicted the output with near-perfect accuracy and did nothing.

I'm less interested in her specific yes-or-no answers to these three than in reverse-engineering the principle she's running. My best guess is a sourcehood condition—responsibility tracks whether the actual causal sequence runs through the agent's own unmanipulated deliberation, with engineered origins breaking the chain but idle blockers and internal architecture leaving it intact—while a rival interpretation might be a modified flicker-of-freedom view that requires some minimal physical possibility of deviation somewhere in the chain, which the perfect internal predictor eliminates.
