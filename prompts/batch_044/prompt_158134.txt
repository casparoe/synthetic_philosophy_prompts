After retiring from a career in experimental physics, I have gradually turned to philosophy of mind, and I am now preparing an episode of my small podcast on the ontology of self-reference. My guest will be Dr. Elena Vasquez, a cognitive philosopher who defends a refined, updated version of Hofstadter’s strange-loop account of consciousness: she maintains that the symbol “I” functions as a self-referential token embedded not in a discrete classical architecture but in a continuous predictive-processing hierarchy, so that the loop is instantiated as an attractor in high-dimensional neural state space rather than as a formal logical formula. I want to press her on the mechanics of this loop without letting the conversation drift into biography, popular science, or requests for further reading.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the view. I need at least one question that targets the symbol-grounding problem for “I” specifically—how the token acquires reference to the very system that generates it without already presupposing a constituted subject to perform the referring; include a follow-up keyed to her likely appeal to feedback dynamics, asking whether that appeal smuggles in the selfhood it is meant to construct. I want one question on how the loop avoids vicious circularity or infinite regress, with a follow-up keyed to the standard reply that the circularity is benign because it is structural rather than inferential; press her on whether a merely structural loop can still be epistemically empty. Another question should explore the relationship between her account and Gödelian self-reference in formal systems—does the neural strange loop genuinely mirror the logical structure of a Gödel sentence, or is the analogy only metaphorical? Include a follow-up asking what empirical finding in neuroscience or computational modeling would weaken that analogy. I also want a question that confronts her predictive-processing framing directly: if the self-model is just one hierarchical inference among others, optimized to minimize free energy, what distinguishes a self-referential strange loop from a sophisticated but non-conscious predictive model—say, in a large artificial system—such that the loop explains phenomenal selfhood rather than merely functional self-monitoring? For each of these core questions, provide the follow-up I have described. Finally, add one or two broader questions about whether this account can accommodate disruptions of selfhood—such as those reported in certain dissociative or psychedelic states—without collapsing into a purely narrative or social construction of the self, and please avoid any requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, since I want answers that stand entirely on their conceptual merits.
