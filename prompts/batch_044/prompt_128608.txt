I am moderating an interview with a philosopher who defends a reconstructed Aristotelian account of money and exchange. On her view, money is conventionally instituted to solve the problem of commensurability, but its use remains legitimate only when exchange stays subordinate to natural use-value; usury is unnatural not because profit is immoral per se, but because it treats the exchange-medium as possessing a reproductive capacity that it actually lacks. She distinguishes household management (oikonomike) from unlimited wealth-getting (chrematistike) not merely by scale, but by whether an exchange sequence has a natural terminus in consumption or practical use, rather than looping back into further exchange.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions in American English for this conversation. The questions should avoid asking her merely to summarize Aristotle; instead, they should press on the reconstruction itself. I want at least one question addressing how she handles ancient Greek credit contracts that yielded returns resembling interest without involving biological reproduction; at least one addressing the objection that any system with stored value permits infinite looping, so that the natural-terminus distinction depends on subjective intention rather than on institutional form; and at least one addressing whether modern portfolio practices—holding assets purely for exchange-value with no planned consumption—are necessarily chrematistic on her account. For each of these three areas, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. The questions should assume an audience familiar with philosophy but should not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
