I was discussing Frege's late political diary with a friend who reads the rationalist blogs and works in formal logic, and we split over whether the 1924 journal — in which Frege voices authoritarian, anti-democratic, and ethnically exclusionary commitments — is just biographical embarrassment or whether it structurally illuminates his philosophy. I'm now preparing to interview, for a technically literate podcast audience, a philosopher who defends the stronger claim: that Frege's insistence on Gedanken as absolutely objective, culture-independent entities, together with his view that logical laws are discovered rather than invented, share a formal architecture with the political essentialism he expresses about national identity and authoritative leadership. She is not arguing that Frege derived logic from politics, but rather that both domains treat contingent historical formations as if they were necessary, mind-independent realities, and that this parallel is visible in specific features of his notation and his anti-psychologism. I want a sustained sequence of eight to ten interview questions that avoid generic biography and instead press the analogical reasoning she has to do.

Please include at least one question that demands she map precisely which logical commitments correspond to which political ones — for instance, whether the objectivity of thoughts maps onto a conception of national essence, or whether the rejection of psychologism maps onto a rejection of democratic deliberation — followed by a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that the connection is structural rather than causal. Another should address the standard universalist objection: Frege's logic is meant for all rational beings regardless of culture, so how could it be infected by a particular political vision? The follow-up should ask whether that very claim to culture-independent universality might itself depend on a conception of reason that her reading exposes as culturally embedded. I also want a question about disconfirmation: if private correspondence revealed Frege holding democratic or cosmopolitan views, would that undermine her structural parallel, or is her claim about formal architecture independent of biographical consistency? Include one that probes whether her reading requires attributing a hidden teleology to the concept-script — treating the notation as expressing a broader vision of rational order — and ask what specific technical details of the notation support or resist that attribution. Finally, draft one closing question that asks what kind of evidence, conceptual or historical, could genuinely move her away from the view that the diary is philosophically inert. Keep the tone precise, adversarial in the constructive podcast sense, and free of "what drew you to philosophy" filler; the audience knows what a Gedanke is but hasn't necessarily read the diary.
