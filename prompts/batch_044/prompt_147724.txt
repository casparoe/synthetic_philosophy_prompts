lately ive been thinking hard about whether pleasure and pain are even on the same scale, ever since my mom started dealing with chronic neuropathy after her surgery. i have these gut verdicts and cant tell if theres a principel behind them or im just being inconsistant. case 1: a life with constant mild discomfort but also deep love and creative work feels clearly worth living. case 2: a world with one person in extreme torture and billions with mild happiness still feels bad, like the happiness literally doesnt count against the torture. case 3: running a marathon and feeling awful for hours but then having that huge sense of achievement feels okay, but if the pain were permanent and crippling, the achievement wouldnt justify it at all. case 4: listening to a really sad symphony that makes you cry but also feel this profound beauty - the sadness doesnt seem to subtract from the value, theyre kind of integrated, which makes me think suffering can be part of a good state rather than always opposing it. case 5: a pill that eliminates all severe depression but also flattens every emotional high - i think id reject it, which suggests im not just trying to minimize pain. so is there a principle like lexical threshold negative utilitarianism with a cutoff for extreme suffering, but full commensurability below it? but then case 4 messes that up because the suffering there is real but not extreme, yet it doesnt need to be outweighed by pleasure - its part of the good. and what does this predict for a case i havent listed, like a society that chooses safe mild comfort over all intense art and all severe pain? also, if pleasure and pain are incommensurable above the threshold, does that mean small pleasures have literally zero weight, or just negligible? i keep going back and forth.
