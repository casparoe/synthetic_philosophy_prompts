I'm preparing a research agenda for a small philosophy of physics working group that has about eighteen months to produce a substantial conceptual contribution. We are not physicists, so we cannot settle empirical questions about quantum gravity, but we want to avoid merely rehashing the familiar dialectic between substantivalism and relationism that centres on Newton's rotating bucket. Instead, I would like you to identify what you take to be the three most significant open conceptual questions in this vicinity that remain genuinely unresolved and are tractable for philosophers rather than experimentalists.

One framing that strikes me as underdeveloped—though I do not wish to prejudge its value—is whether the debate should be reconceived not as an ontological dispute about whether spacetime points exist independently of material bodies, but as an explanatory dispute about whether dynamical laws can be formulated without presupposing a fixed background structure. I was recently discussing this with a colleague in theoretical physics who insisted that the debate is obsolete given modern formulations of general relativity, but I remain unconvinced that the conceptual issues have been dissolved rather than relocated. I am also interested in whether recent conceptual work on background independence, understood purely as a constraint on theory-formulation rather than as a physical result, genuinely favours one side or instead dissolves the disagreement into a choice of descriptive framework.

Please rank these (or other) lines of inquiry by how much they deserve our limited time: defend your ordering with explicit criteria—such as upstream importance for other philosophical problems, tractability within eighteen months for non-physicists, and what kind of conceptual clarification would count as progress. I am less interested in a bibliography or a survey of positions than in a defended agenda: which question should come first, which should be deprioritised because its answer would change little downstream, and what would cause you to revise the ranking. Avoid citation-heavy references; I want reasoning I could present to the group without access to a library database.
