The contradictions people point to—colonialism, hypocrisy, internal disagreement—feel like application failures, not proof teh category is empty. So my position is that it's a defensible framework with actual content.

But what would count against it? I'm not after teh standard "but slavery existed" rebuttal; I want teh deeper defeaters. If secular rationalism and Christian moral anthropology turn out to be genuinely incompatible rather than complementary, does that crack the package? If historians show these commitments were never actually shared, just retrofitted onto societies after teh fact, does the category collapse? And has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability—where any counterexample gets dismissed as "not real Western values"? I'd like teh objections ranked from fatal to merely awkward, plus an honest take on whether this position risks anything or has been insulated too well.
