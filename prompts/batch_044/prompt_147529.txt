I am preparing the interview script for an episode of my philosophy podcast, in which I shall speak with an unnamed defender of a recently articulated position on transformative experience — one that departs in a precise way from the standard framework associated with L.A. Paul. On this view, an experience counts as genuinely transformative not merely when it is both epistemically opaque and personally transformative in Paul’s sense, but only when it triggers what the defender calls “recursive preference revision”: a destabilisation not just of first-order preferences, but of second-order desires about which preferences ought to govern deliberation. The defender applies this stricter criterion to a deliberately marginal set of cases — elective limb-lengthening procedures, voluntary sensory-substitution implants, and long-term ascetic dietary regimes — arguing that familiar examples such as becoming a parent or losing sight fail to meet it because they leave the subject’s meta-preferences intact. I wish to avoid generic biographical questions and instead probe the conceptual machinery directly.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that turn on the actual content of this position. I should like at least one question on how the “recursive preference revision” test distinguishes genuine transformation from mere risk aversion or adaptive preference formation; at least one on whether the three marginal examples (limb lengthening, sensory substitution, asceticism) genuinely share the epistemic opacity claimed, or whether they differ in ways that undermine the unified treatment; and at least one on what normative authority, if any, remains for the pre-transformation self to veto a decision once recursive revision has occurred. For each of these, include a follow-up keyed to the answer the defender is most likely to give — for instance, that meta-preference stability is observable through retrospective narrative coherence, or that the pre-transformation veto is paternalistic. The questions should assume an audience conversant with Paul’s original distinction between epistemic and personal transformation, but should not depend on knowledge of unpublished manuscripts or highly technical literature. Please write your response in British English, using British spelling and idiom throughout.
