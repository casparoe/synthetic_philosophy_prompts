So I'm wondering what philosphical commitments that funding pattern actually presupposes. Does it only make sense if you buy Scheffler's view that the continuation of the species is a background condition for present meaning, rather than just one good among others? Reconstruct the implicit theory of value behind the practice, say whether it genuinely needs Scheffler's conjecture or is compatible with rival views like pure presentism or simple preference-satisfaction across generations, and tell me what a preservation policy built on the idea that extinction is the worst harm — not just the sum of individual deaths — would actually have to change. Would it shift budgets from archives to asteroid defense, or is there a deeper inconsistency in caring about future people at all while ignoring the risk that there won't be any?
