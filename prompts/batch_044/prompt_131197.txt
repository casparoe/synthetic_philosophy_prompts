After a long argument with a friend about whether inverse reinforcement learning can genuinely recover human values rather than merely model behavior, I want to sharpen my own judgment. Rank the following four obstacles to using IRL for value alignment from the most damaging to the least damaging, and assign each a severity score from 1 to 10: (A) the reward ambiguity problem, in which multiple reward functions can rationalize the same observed trajectory; (B) the instrumental-action problem, where observed behavior is mostly strategic and IRL therefore risks encoding means rather than ends; (C) the representational-filter problem—my friend’s central worry—that the observer’s choice of which state features are made observable acts as an unacknowledged normative filter, so the “inferred preferences” are partly artifacts of the modeler’s ontology rather than discoveries about the agent; and (D) the non-stationarity problem, that human preferences are context-dependent and shift over time, making any single inferred reward function an unstable snapshot. For each, justify its position in one or two sentences. Finally, state what specific empirical finding or theoretical argument would most likely change your ranking, and identify the precise step in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to being wrong.
