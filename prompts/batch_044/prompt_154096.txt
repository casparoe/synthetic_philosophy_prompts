I sit on the University Museum's Committee on Exhibition Ethics, and we must vote at Thursday's meeting on the proposed permanent gallery The Arc of Liberty, which arranges artifacts from 1600 to the present along a single normative axis the designers call dimensional expansion of moral recognition. In this framing, earlier societies are depicted as possessing lower-dimensional value structures that gradually unfold into modern liberal rights. The curators defend this as pedagogically necessary for grasping institutional continuity; critics object that it is a Whig narrative that flattens contingency and treats historical agents as deficient versions of ourselves rather than as holders of incommensurable moral topologies. Please draft the memo I will circulate before the meeting: recommend either approval with mandatory revisions, rejection, or conditional postponement, include a concrete implementation timeline if you recommend moving forward, and take seriously the curators' pedagogical argument without simply restating it. Lead with the bottom-line recommendation in the first sentence, keep the entire memo to 350 words or fewer, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
