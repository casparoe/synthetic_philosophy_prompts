Write a dialogue between a defender and a critic of Kierkegaard's teleological suspension of the ethical, focused specifically on Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac and his subsequent silence. The defender should argue that the suspension is not an exception within universal ethics but a genuinely higher telos that renders ethical justification structurally impossible, drawing on the distinction between the knight of infinite resignation and the knight of faith, and insisting that Abraham's silence marks an incommunicability that universal ethics cannot absorb. The critic should reply that this confuses a resolvable conflict of first-order duties with a transcendent rupture, proposing a modern parallel—such as a physician who breaches patient confidentiality to avert an imminent public-health catastrophe—to show that universal ethics already accommodates such overrides through second-order reflective equilibrium without being suspended. Require real dialectical exchange: each speaker must directly answer the other's specific claim about whether the suspension is genuinely teleological or merely an accommodated internal exception, and do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or references to secondary literature.
