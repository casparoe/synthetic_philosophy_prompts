I'm takeing a course on the philosophy of explanation and I keep coming back to this parallel between Darwin and Turing that feels like it might be more than a metaphor but I can't tell if it's actually doing work. So here's the rough draft of an essay idea I want feedback on. Darwin gives us the classic inversion: we see functional competence — an eye that tracks, a wing that lifts — and our reasoning wants to leap to a comprehending designer. Natural selection replaces that with a blind, cumulative process that produces the competence with zero grasp of optics or aerodynamics. Turing does something structurally similar but in the other direction: we see conversational or problem-solving competence and our reasoning wants to leap to an inner mind that understands. The imitation game and the idea of machine intelligence propose that you can have the behavioral competence without any semantic comprehension running the machinery. Both are "competence without comprehension," but I think people blur them together because both attack some version of "you can't have X without a mind behind it."

My specific claim — and this is the part I want checked — is that there's an asymmetry people miss. Darwin's inversion is diachronic and distributed: the "designer" is replaced by generations of differential survival, so the absence of comprehension is spread across deep time. Turing's inversion is synchronic and localized: one artifact runs algorithmic rules in real time, and the absence of comprehension is inside a single box. So Darwin removes the need for a mind in nature; Turing removes the need for a mind in the artifact. But then I started wondering about a kind of "third inverssion": if we treat natural selection itself as a mechanism — a blind algorithm running on heritable variation — doesn't that mean the explanation of biological competence is itself just another instance of competence (the algorithm "works") without comprehension? I'm worried this either collapses into tautology or opens an explanatory regress that doesn't go anywhere.

Can you comment on whether the asymmetry actually holds, whether I'm smuggling in teleological language I shouldn't, and if that "third inversion" is worth developing or just muddy?
