I've been preparing a session on logical empiricism and the philosophy of value, and I keep returning to Moritz Schlick's argument about the meaning of life — specifically his claim that treating life as having a purpose launches an infinite regress, since every purpose is itself valued only as a means to a further purpose, and that the only escape is to recognize activities that are ends in themselves, which he associates with the play of youth rather than the instrumental work of adulthood. The argument strikes me as having a hidden structural form: a circular repetition that only breaks when something self-justifying enters the loop, and I would like to see that form made audible in verse.

I'd like you to write a villanelle that enacts this philosophical movement. Use two refrains. The first should express the instrumental regress — something like every end becoming a means again — and it should dominate the opening tercets, returning with slight variation as if the speaker is caught in an endless chain of justification. The second refrain should express Schlick's resolution in intrinsic, playful activity — something like play needing no reason beyond itself — and it should gradually intrude, first as a distant interruption, then with growing insistence, so that the poem's compulsory repetition enacts both the trap and the escape. Keep the philosophical content precise enough that a reader familiar with Schlick would recognize the distinction between adulthood's work, valued for results, and youth's play, valued in its execution; avoid invoking divine or transcendent bestowal of meaning; and do not turn the poem into a sermon about living playfully, but let the form itself perform the resolution.

In the final quatrain, both refrains must appear together in the closing two lines, arranged so that their juxtaposition does the philosophical work: the chain of purpose collapses not by reaching a final, ultimate end, but by recognizing that some activities are not links in the chain at all. The rhyme scheme and cyclical returns should feel like the regress Schlick describes, and the meeting of the refrains should feel like the moment that regress is broken from within. No citations, bibliography, or references to secondary literature are needed — just the poem, with the villanelle structure intact and the two refrains clearly distinguishable.
