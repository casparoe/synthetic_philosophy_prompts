If the Principal Principle requires that a rational agent's credence in an event match its objective physical chance whenever she lacks inadmissible evidence, then in an Everettian framework—where every quantum outcome is deterministically realized in some branch and the Born weights are interpreted as measures of branch thickness rather than as stochastic probabilities—doesn't the principle compel an agent to distribute credence across outcomes in proportion to those weights, even though every outcome is ontologically actualized in some branch? Furthermore, if deference to objective chance is meant to be modally stable—binding the agent not only in the actual world but in counterfactual scenarios where the quantum dynamics differ—how can it avoid reducing to a mere indexical rule about self-location, given that within any single branch the chance of the realized outcome appears to be either undefined or trivially one?
