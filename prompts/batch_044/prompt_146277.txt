Please argue that the Death in Damascus paradox dissolves once we treat the predictive correlation between leaving and dying as an indexical artifact rather than as a genuine evidential or causal constraint. Specifically, defend a centered-worlds resolution: when the agent considers the counterfactual “if I stay in Damascus, then I will die there,” the location term “there” should be evaluated relative to the world centered on her actual position at the time of evaluation, not relative to an absolute spatial coordinate. On this view, leaving Damascus updates her self-locating beliefs—she knows she will not be present in Damascus at the time of death—without establishing any causal or statistical link between the act of departure and the occurrence of death. Consequently, she can rationally leave to maximize expected utility, since death is predicted in Damascus only under descriptions that include her being present there, while denying that her departure causally averts death. Address the objection that this merely relabels the standard evidentialist solution, and explain why the centered-worlds approach avoids the circularity that arises in causal-decision-theoretic resolutions, where the agent’s choice is treated as a causal intervention on a prediction that was formed precisely by observing her choice dispositions.
