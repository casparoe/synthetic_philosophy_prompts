I am drafting a philosophical dialogue for a novel whose protagonists debate the architecture of ordinary certainty, drawing on Wittgenstein's analysis of hinge propositions in On Certainty. To structure their disagreement, I need a taxonomy of positions arranged as a decision tree. The first branch should ask whether hinges are best understood as non-propositional rules of grammar, as basic beliefs that are epistemically prior to ordinary empirical claims, or as practical commitments that are prior in action rather than in cognition. The second branch should ask whether a hinge can be locally suspended in counterfactual or fictional reasoning—what I will call the imaginative-stability question—without collapsing the entire belief-system that depends on it. The third branch should ask whether doubting a hinge is always a form of practical irrationality, or whether it can be a coherent move within a restricted domain such as fiction, hypothetical argument, or narrative framing. Each leaf should name the corresponding philosophical position—grammatical interpretation, epistemic-foundationalist interpretation, pragmatic-certainty view, or an unnamed combination if none is defended—and should state in one sentence its most awkward commitment regarding what happens when a character treats a hinge as doubtful inside a story. Please note any combinations that appear unoccupied and briefly diagnose whether the gap is incoherent, merely unexplored, or philosophically unattractive, and flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely distinguish. The instrument should sort positions by their answers to these concrete questions, not by the labels philosophers prefer.
