I am putting together a training protocol for senior review analysts at an independent advisory board that evaluates public-facing arguments in science policy. A recurring problem is that stakeholders submit position papers that turn on a single vivid thought experiment—especially in bioethics and philosophy of mind—and my team needs to distinguish cases where the experiment genuinely clarifies a concept from cases where it merely smuggles in conclusions by suppressing context, exploiting unstated analogies, or trading on hidden normative commitments. I want you to design a reusable audit procedure that an analyst can run in roughly twenty minutes without consulting external literature. The procedure should translate what philosophy actually knows about how intuition pumps work—including their reliance on framing effects, selective detail suppression, and the elision of boundary conditions—into an ordered sequence of tests applied directly to the text of the argument. For each step, specify what feature of the argument counts as passing or failing, where the audit must stop and defer to an explicit value judgment rather than yielding a mechanical result, and what kind of misfire is likely at that stage. The deliverable should be a practical checklist or flowchart in words, not an abstract taxonomy.

Please apply the audit to three specific cases, one of which should be a scenario I am injecting into the module to test its limits: (1) a regulatory proposal for autonomous vehicles that invokes the trolley problem to justify a particular algorithmic harm-minimization rule; (2) a gradual-replacement-of-neurons-with-silicon scenario used to argue that consciousness is not essentially biological; and (3) a constructed case in which a society decides to preserve every instance of pain experienced by non-human animals in an eternal digital archive in order to honor suffering, and this is used to claim that moral status requires the capacity for retrospective narrative integration. In your applications, include at least one case where the procedure returns a verdict that runs against the intuitive plausibility of the argument, and clearly flag the kinds of misleading pumps—such as those operating through emotional salience or culturally specific narrative templates—that the procedure is structurally unable to catch. Finally, state explicitly what judgments the procedure can never eliminate, no matter how refined the checklist becomes.
