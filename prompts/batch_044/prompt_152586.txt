For a philosophy of social norms seminar, lay out the desiderata that an adequate theory extending Lewisian convention analysis to the evolution of normative practices must meet. Begin from Lewis's framework: conventions solve coordination problems through regularities sustained by mutual expectations and preference for conformity, without requiring explicit agreement or centralized authority. The theory you specify must then bridge to genuine normativity—explaining how arbitrary coordination equilibria (e.g., driving on the left versus the right) can become experienced as binding obligations that resist change even when a Pareto-superior alternative is available. Your specification should cover four categories of requirements, each with its motivating case: (i) explanatory coverage—what transitions must be accounted for, including the emergence of moralized conventions and the persistence of suboptimal norms; (ii) conceptual distinctions the theory must preserve, especially between first-order behavioral expectations and second-order expectations of approval, blame, or sanction, and between descriptive stability and prescriptive force; (iii) consistency constraints—compatibility with Lewis's conditions that conventions need no explicit rule and no common knowledge of rationality, and with evolutionary dynamics that can select among multiple equilibria; and (iv) exclusion conditions—results the theory must avoid, such as reducing the ought of norms to predictive reliability or importing unreduced moral realism. For each requirement, indicate whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference (e.g., rational-choice reductionism versus normative realism). Then address the following tension, which is the core of the exercise: can the theory satisfy all three of (a) deriving normative force solely from first-order coordination preferences, without second-order attitudes; (b) explaining why moralized conventions resist Pareto-improving revision; and (c) remaining faithful to Lewis's claim that conventions need no authority or explicit rule? If not, identify precisely which desideratum conflicts with which, and state which relaxation does the least damage to the project of explaining norm evolution. Weight the analysis on the criteria and their motivating cases; you may briefly locate evolutionary game-theoretic or social-norm accounts relative to the list, but do not produce a full scorecard.
