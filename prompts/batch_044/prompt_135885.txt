Below is a line of reasoning about evolutionary levels of selection that I developed for a seminar. It seems to prove that individual-level selection is always causally derivative, which I take to be unacceptable, yet I cannot isolate the faulty move.

(1) A necessary condition for natural selection operating at level L is that there exists heritable variation in fitness at L, not merely that entities at L differ in survival or reproduction.

(2) In organisms that engage in systematic niche construction—where the selective environment is partly constituted by accumulated products of prior generations' behavior—the fitness landscape confronting any individual is causally dependent on group-level traits.

(3) Where the fitness landscape depends on group-level traits, heritable variation in individual fitness is partially mediated by heritable variation at the group level.

(4) Therefore, in niche-constructing lineages, individual-level selection necessarily co-occurs with, and is causally subordinate to, group-level selection.

(5) Systematic niche construction is not exceptional but ubiquitous: microbial biofilms, beaver dams, and human cultural environments all exhibit it.

(6) So individual-level selection is never autonomous; it is always an epiphenomenon of selection at a higher level.

(7) But if individual selection is always an epiphenomenon, then the gene-individual-group hierarchy collapses into an undifferentiated multi-level process, and evolutionary biology loses the ability to assign distinct causal weights to different levels.

(8) Since evolutionary biology clearly assigns such weights productively, (6) must be false.

The conclusion feels wrong—there seems to be a slide between causal dependence and causal subordination, or between co-occurrence and epiphenomenalism—but I cannot name the exact equivocation or scope shift. Which premise or inference do you reject? If you reject (4), explain how heritable mediation can occur without rendering individual selection causally subordinate. If you reject (1), explain what replaces heritable fitness variation as the criterion for identifying a level of selection. And if the argument is actually valid and the real cost must be borne elsewhere—say, in abandoning the autonomy intuition behind (8)—state that explicitly and price it.
