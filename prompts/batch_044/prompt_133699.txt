I am an advanced high school student preparing materials for our civics final, and I want an exam question on constitutional monarchy in modern democracies that avoids simple comparison lists. Please write one essay question—designed for a 45-minute response of about 800 words—asking students to analyze the concept of dormant sovereignty: the situation in which a monarch retains formal constitutional powers that are never exercised in practice, yet whose mere legal existence reshapes democratic accountability and ministerial behavior. The question should ask students to apply this concept to two systems among the United Kingdom, the Scandinavian monarchies, and Japan, taking a clear argumentative position rather than merely describing institutions. Along with the question, provide a model answer of roughly 600 words and a marking scheme totaling 25 marks. The scheme should explicitly name five conceptual moves that distinguish an excellent answer from a merely competent one—for example, distinguishing legal form from political convention, explaining how dormant authority affects democratic legitimacy, and assessing whether such an arrangement strengthens or weakens popular sovereignty—together with the marks each move carries and the typical misconception that causes students to miss it. Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or references to obscure scholarship, as the exam is intended for students working entirely from course materials.
