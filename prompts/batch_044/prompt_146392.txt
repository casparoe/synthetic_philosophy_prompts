Write the full specification of desiderata that any philosophically adequate reconstruction of Leibniz's claim that this world is the best of all possible worlds must satisfy. Cover four clusters. First, the modal ontology of unactualized possibles: how they subsist, whether their non-actualization constitutes a harm that a benevolent creator ought to have prevented, and how compossibility functions as a prior constraint rather than a post-hoc rationalization. Second, the accounting for evil: whether moral and natural evil are reducible to privation, whether that reduction renders the thesis empirically empty or unfalsifiable, and what independent criterion distinguishes permissible evil in this world from gratuitous suffering in nearby possible worlds. Third, the nature of divine selection: whether God's choice is rationally necessary, freely contingent, or structurally determined by the principle of sufficient reason, and whether best denotes an objective ordinal ranking among worlds or merely an internal index of divine perfection. Fourth, the taxonomy of necessities: how metaphysical, physical, and moral necessity interact to explain why this world is optimally selected rather than merely permitted, and whether any collapse among these categories undermines the doctrine's explanatory power. For each desideratum, state the conceptual pressure that makes it non-negotiable or merely the preference of a particular interpretive camp. Then identify which pair of desiderata appears jointly unsatisfiable given Leibniz's own commitments—particularly the tension between maximal divine freedom and maximal rational determination—and flag which single desideratum, if abandoned, does the most work in allowing rival interpretations such as Spinozistic determinism, theological voluntarism, or modern modal-realist analogies to become viable. Keep the weight on the criteria, their motivating cases, and their internal tensions; do not provide a scorecard of existing scholars, a bibliography, or citation requests.
