I'm working through my own position on whether special relativity forces the block-universe view, particularly along the Rietdijk-Putnam line that denies any objective present. Here are my verdicts on concrete cases, and I'd like the principle behind them—if there is one—together with its prediction for a case I haven't listed, and an honest statement of which verdict it must overturn if simplicity demands it.

1. Two inertial observers in relative motion each correctly assign different simultaneity slices through a distant event E; special relativity gives neither frame physical priority. Verdict: There is no frame-independent "now" extending through space, so presentism—which requires exactly one global present—is false.

2. Our actual universe is governed by general relativity, and cosmological models supply a global cosmic rest frame (defined, for instance, by CMB isotropy) that picks out a privileged foliation of spacetime. Verdict: The Rietdijk-Putnam argument is locally correct but globally weakened; physics alone does not uniquely compel eternalism, since a global temporal ordering can be recovered cosmologically.

3. Even if all spacetime events are equally real in the four-dimensional manifold, my own conscious awareness carries an indexical "now" analogous to spatial "here." Verdict: This is fully compatible with the block view; it describes a feature of perspective, not an ontological privilege of one slice, so it does not rescue presentism.

4. I retain a strong asymmetry: the past seems fixed and causally closed, the future open and undetermined, and this intuition survives even when I imagine constructing a coordinate frame in which my present decision is simultaneous with some distant past event that causally influenced me. Verdict: The asymmetry is about causal determinacy and the direction of entropy, not about simultaneity, so the block universe can accommodate it without contradiction.

5. Here is the tension: I think that if future physics discovered a fundamental, non-statistical dynamical law that physically privileges one foliation—say, via quantum-gravitational reference frames—then presentism might be empirically revived. Yet I also think we should not now treat such a speculative possibility as undermining the block view. Verdict: My commitment to eternalism is conditional and defeasible, but my intuition of a "moving present" should not be treated as independent evidence for presentism.

These five do not sit easily together: case 1 treats physics as categorically undermining presentism, case 2 introduces a cosmological escape, case 4 insists on an asymmetry that sounds like becoming, and case 5 makes the whole view hostage to undiscovered dynamics.
