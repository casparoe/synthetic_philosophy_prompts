hey — my partner lost her sight suddenly like 6 months ago and now we're looking at this treatment that might restore it. suddenly Molyneux's problem feels personal: if she gets sight back, will she immediately know a cube from a sphere just from touch-memory? i want to design a workshop for my philosophy reading group (8 people, 75 mins, no lectures up front) where we physically feel why this answer isnt obvious.

design an in-room game called 'The Restoration Clinic.' split us into 'patients' (4 people), 'controls' (2 people), and 'betters/observers' (2 people). patients spend 10 mins in a black box with thick gloves learning 4 3d-printed shapes — cube, sphere, pyramid, cylinder — they can only feel edges and curves, and they invent thier own names ('spiky', 'smooth'). then the 'cure': blindfolds off, and they're shown photos of the same shapes on a screen, but shot from weird angles with shadows, textures, colors they never felt. there are also 3 visual distractors mixed in. they must point to which photo is which shape, with no explanation of how 3d feel maps to 2d sight. controls do the same photo task with zero prior touch-training. everyone else bets token-stakes (fake currency, loser buys snacks) on whether patients beat controls.

inject this twist: half the patients spent the blind phase also using a basic sensory-substitution setup — audio tones that change pitch based on object edges (like a cheap sonification glove or app). so they have an intermediate cross-modal map, but its auditory-spatial, not visual. do they do better, worse, or just differently?

the rules need to make us hit the confusion ourselves before anyone names Molyneux. include: exact materials, turn order, payoff scheme, and a debrief that maps our betting mistakes and identification failures onto the empiricist vs rationalist sides. also be honest about what feature this cant reproduce — like months of actual neural plasticity, or congenital blindness with no prior sight-memory. and tell me what predictably breaks the first time (people peeking through gloves, images being too obvious, the substitution audio being too clear).
