I am delivering a job-market seminar paper defending the claim that the classic paradox of deontology — in which an absolute prohibition on treating persons as mere means appears both to forbid pushing one to save five and to forbid standing aside — is not a genuine contradiction but a category error arising from the misapplication of Kant’s universalizability tests. My thesis is that we must distinguish between “executive maxims” (what an agent directly wills to do) and “toleration maxims” (what an agent wills to permit in the institutional space she inhabits), and that once this distinction is in place a third, overlooked test — contradiction-in-toleration — shows that allowing the five to die involves universalising a maxim of non-protection that rational agents could not consistently will as law. I will present this to a philosophy department audience comprising senior Kant scholars who treat the Formula of Humanity as non-negotiable, virtue ethicists who think system-building obscures moral perception, and one visiting consequentialist who regards the entire framing as rationalisation; I need a prep sheet for the objections they will actually raise, ordered by how likely and damaging they are.

Please prepare me for the question period: identify, in the vocabulary each camp would actually use, the objections I should expect from the Kantians (for instance, whether inventing a “toleration maxim” violates the unity of practical reason), from the virtue ethicists (whether scalar distinctions between direct and collateral harm simply smuggle in consequentialist weighing), and from the consequentialist (whether contradiction-in-toleration is just rule-utilitarianism in deontological dress). For each objection, tell me how serious it is, what I should concede up front without collapsing the argument, what reply must land for the proposal to survive, and where that reply is weakest. Include the one objection I am hoping nobody asks — perhaps about whether my framework permits too much discretionary latitude in emergencies — and advise me on whether to address it preemptively or only if pressed. Please write your answer in British English, and present it as a concise speaker’s briefing rather than a full defence of the thesis itself.
