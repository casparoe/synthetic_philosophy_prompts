As a software engineer with no formal philosophical training, I am preparing interview questions for a podcast episode featuring a cognitive scientist—unnamed—who defends the view that the Gödelian strange-loop analogy developed in Douglas Hofstadter’s Gödel, Escher, Bach, which moves from syntactically self-referential arithmetic formulas to self-aware minds, offers a genuine structural explanation of consciousness rather than a mere metaphor. I want eight to ten questions that do not ask her to restate the analogy, but instead press the points where logicians and philosophers of mind have raised objections. Please include at least one question on whether Gödel numbering requires a fixed external interpretation and, if consciousness requires an analogous self-reference, whether the system can ever bootstrap its own semantics without infinite regress; one on whether the Lucas-Penrose argument illegitimately transfers results about formal provability in consistent systems into claims about biological epistemic access; and one on whether distributed self-attention in modern transformer architectures—where tokens reference prior outputs without a centralized hierarchical symbol-manipulator—constitutes the same kind of strange loop Hofstadter describes, or only a weak analog. For each of these three, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give—for instance, that interpretation emerges from use, or that distributed reference lacks the singular self the analogy requires. I also want a question probing whether the analogy survives if we reject a unified Cartesian self as the referent of the self-referential statement, and one asking whether the Gödelian framework explains phenomenal experience or merely describes a functional architecture. The tone should be technically precise—using terms like syntax, semantics, interpretation, and self-attention correctly—but accessible to an audience of engineers. Avoid generic biographical questions, and do not request bibliographies or citations.
