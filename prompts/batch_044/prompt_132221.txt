I take Bernard Williams's "one thought too many" objection to amount to a general structural claim, not merely a local complaint against utilitarianism: for actions rooted in close personal relationships, providing any justification routed through an impartial or maximizing principle is not redundant but corrupting, and the only appropriate reason is the bare particularity of the bond itself. I am treating this as a thesis about the limits of reason-giving in intimate domains. What would count against it? I want the defeaters ranked from decisive to merely awkward—such as whether some relationships genuinely require principled defense, whether the corruption claim collapses once we separate motivation from justification, and whether the thesis can survive cases where the relationship is itself constituted by shared, inherently principled commitments. For each major point, say how confident you are, and single out the claim you are least sure of.
