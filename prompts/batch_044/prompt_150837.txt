ok so i've been retired from the physics dept for about five years now and lately i've fallen down a hole reading about logic paradoxes. here's roughly where i stand and i want a consistency check before i present it at a local philosophy meetup (don't laugh):

(1) i'm convinced the liar paradox is a genuine dialetheia - it's both true and false in a single stable sense, not just an illusion to be dissolved.
(2) so i think we need a paraconsistent framework, probably LP or something close, where expolsion fails and you can't derive arbitrary garbage from a contradiction.
(3) at the same time, my gut from forty years of empirical science says that if a physical model predicts both A and not-A about some measurable quantity, the model is broken, not reality.
(4) i try to square (1)-(3) by saying dialetheism applies only at the representational level - contradictions live in language and formal systems, never "out there" in nature.
(5) but i also feel like if something is genuinely true, it's true period; saying "true but only representationally" sounds like i'm watering down real dialetheism into a semantic artifact.
(6) and i don't buy logical pluralism - using one logic for semantics and another for physics feels like cheating, not a principled solution.

so: do (1) through (6) hang together? it feels like (4) and (5) pull in opposite directions, and (3) plus (6) makes it hard to quarantine paradoxes away from science. but maybe there's an apparent tension that dissolves once you look closer? please rank the actual conflicts by severity, say which ones only look bad, and tell me the least painful revision - i'm reluctant to drop (3) but i'm open on (6).
