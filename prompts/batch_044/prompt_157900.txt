I'm preparing a consistency audit of my own philosophical commitments regarding quantum ontology for a small reading group, and I want to determine whether they form a jointly tenable package or whether I'm implicitly relying on incompatible motivations. Here is my current position:

1. I take a psi-epistemic stance: the quantum state represents an agent's information or rational belief about an underlying reality, not a direct, literal description of physical ontology.
2. I accept the PBR result as correctly derived and take its conclusion seriously: any psi-epistemic model that respects preparation independence is incompatible with standard quantum predictions.
3. I reject preparation independence as physically well-motivated; I hold that ontic-level correlations between separately prepared systems are not only permitted but should be expected in any realistic ontology.
4. I am committed to the completeness of quantum mechanics in the standard sense: there are no hidden variables, and the quantum state gives the full description of an individual system.
5. I believe that macroscopic superposition phenomena—interference observed in large-molecule and optomechanical regimes—require the wavefunction to carry direct ontic weight in those contexts, because a purely informational object lacks the causal or structural resources to generate observed interference patterns without an underlying physical mediator.
6. I maintain that ontological commitment in physics ought to be driven by explanatory coherence and predictive success, rather than by a priori intuitions about what qualifies as "real."
7. I deny that there is a sharp, observer-independent boundary between epistemic and ontic descriptions in quantum theory; I treat state assignment as relative to an information partition or physical reference frame, so that describing the state as "merely epistemic" is always indexed to a particular descriptive context.

Please audit this set. For each pair of commitments, indicate whether they stand in genuine tension, only in apparent tension that dissolves on closer inspection, or in full compatibility, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, specify exactly how badly it damages the package, what the cheapest revision would be (which commitment should weaken or be abandoned), and what that revision costs with respect to the remaining commitments. Also identify the single change that, if adopted, would eliminate the largest number of tensions with the least overall damage to the view. Be precise about the logical and motivational links: show which commitments generate the pull, whether any other commitment in the set can mediate the conflict, and which apparent contradictions are actually harmless once the mediating commitment is taken into account.
