Imagine you are advising a philosopher with three years of postdoctoral funding who wants to determine what cross-cultural differences in folk morality—particularly the WEIRD-psychology finding that moral cognition varies systematically across industrialized and small-scale societies—actually imply for philosophical debates about moral objectivity. Do not survey the field; instead, identify and rank the three open conceptual questions at this intersection that deserve the bulk of that effort. For each, defend its position in the ordering by explaining: (a) why it is upstream of the others in the sense that resolving it would restructure how we approach them; (b) what form of progress—conceptual analysis, empirical collaboration without new fieldwork, or formal modeling—is tractable within three years; and (c) what specific result or argument would cause you to reorder them.

Crucially, separate two dimensions of variation that are often conflated: differences in first-order moral content (e.g., the relative weight of care versus loyalty) and differences in meta-ethical stance (e.g., whether people treat moral norms as mind-independent truths or as relational obligations tied to social roles). Then apply a further distinction: domain-level objectivity, the claim that a well-defined moral domain admits of a single correct normative structure, versus token-level objectivity, the claim that particular judgments hold regardless of cultural or relational context. Which conceptual problems about how these two levels interact—especially in light of evidence that some non-WEIRD populations deploy universalist moral language within tightly bounded social matrices but withhold it across them—are genuinely unsettled, and which are artifacts of this conflation?

In your prioritization, evaluate three candidate directions: refining philosophical accounts of objectivity to accommodate deep pluralism without collapsing into relativism; challenging whether WEIRD samples have distorted our very conception of what counts as moral disagreement; and constructing normative frameworks—such as contextualist or relational objectivity—that accommodate the observed pattern of locally universal but globally variable moral commitment. Which of these lines of work should come first, second, and third, given the criteria of upstream dependency, philosophical tractability, and downstream impact? Do not provide citations, reading lists, or bibliographic references. Your answer should be a defended research agenda, not a literature review.
