I serve on the editorial-policy committee of a university press, and as a novelist I am particularly concerned with how language encodes unexamined defaults. A proposal has come before us, inspired by Douglas Hofstadter's satirical method of replacing gender markers with racial ones to expose hidden assumptions, to institute a default-reversal audit for our backlist: every reissued text that employs an unmarked masculine or an unexamined racial default must either carry an editorial annotation or, in selected reference works, have the default systematically marked or reversed to render its contingency visible. Proponents say this applies Hofstadter's logic seriously rather than as mere parody; opponents say it mistakes satire for prescription, violates authorial intent, and imposes anachronistic norms on literary works that should be read in historical context. There is also a narrower pilot under consideration, limiting the audit to textbooks and reference volumes while exempting fiction and poetry. Our meeting is Thursday. Please draft the formal opinion memo I should circulate: recommend a concrete course of action—approve the full proposal, approve only the pilot, or reject both—with a clear implementation or dismissal timeline, and treat the strongest objection on the losing side with intellectual honesty rather than dismissal. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the reasoning should be self-contained.
