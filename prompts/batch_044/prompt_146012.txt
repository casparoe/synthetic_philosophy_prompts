Please argue either that luck egalitarianism can be rescued from, or that it necessarily collapses under, what we can call the recursive correction problem: in any institutional framework that compensates for circumstance-based disadvantage, the compensation itself becomes part of the agent's opportunity set, which means that subsequent choices made within that corrected set generate new inequalities that the framework must classify as either choice-based or circumstance-based. Because the correction mechanism is itself a circumstance for all future choice-evaluations, the theory cannot non-circularly distinguish the two categories after the first round of compensation.
