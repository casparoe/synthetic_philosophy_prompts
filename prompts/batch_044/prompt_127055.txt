Write a deadpan parody of a government job posting and brief employee orientation manual for the fictional "Office of Conditional Entitlement," the agency that administers all means-tested benefits through a continuously updated "Deservingness Metric." The metric aggregates biometric stress markers, job-search keystroke patterns, and peer-reported effort signals; benefits release only when a recipient's score crosses a threshold the manual deliberately leaves undefined. The satire should exaggerate real features of workfare and fraud surveillance—treating moral hazard as a quantifiable contaminant, treating need itself as a probabilistic indicator of deception, and treating the deserving/undeserving distinction as a calibration problem—so that the circularity becomes visible: the conditions required to prove deservingness are engineered to be just barely unattainable for anyone in genuine need. Include a quoted excerpt from the manual's "Ethics of Non-Release" section, rendered in flat bureaucratic prose, that reveals the unstated premise that receiving aid without visible suffering is itself a form of fraud. The target is the logic of conditionality and the historical construction of the undeserving poor, not any named official or program. About 600 words, American English, formatted as a numbered job posting followed by manual excerpts, readable without specialist knowledge but landing specifically for someone familiar with welfare-state debates.
