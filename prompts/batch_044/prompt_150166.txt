Design a diagnostic decision tree that sorts philosophical positions on cognitive penetration of perception, intended for use in an advanced philosophy-of-mind seminar. The tree should start from a single concrete case: a trained radiologist examines an ambiguous tissue scan; after being primed with the information that the sample is likely benign, she reports that the image looks uniformly smooth to her, whereas without that priming she reports seeing irregular mottling, despite identical retinal stimulation.

From this starting point, build a branching taxonomy in which each internal node is a substantive yes/no or either/or question that a respondent could answer without already knowing which named position it corresponds to. The branches should distinguish at least the following dimensions:

(a) Whether the cognitive priming alters the phenomenal character of the visual experience itself, or merely biases the post-perceptual interpretation and verbal report;
(b) If phenomenal character is altered, whether the mechanism involves direct top-down modulation of early sensory feature detectors, or only a restructuring of attention and conceptual framing after sensory registration is complete;
(c) Whether the penetrating cognitive state must be specifically conceptual or propositional, or whether affective, motivational, or statistical-learning states can produce structurally analogous perceptual shifts;
(d) Whether the penetrated percept remains available as independent, non-circular evidence for the very belief that penetrated it.

Each leaf should be assigned to a named theoretical position where one exists (for example, modular encapsulation, direct top-down penetration, attention-mediated restructuring, or epistemically circular belief-influence), or marked unnamed if the combination is not standardly defended. For every unnamed leaf, briefly diagnose whether it is likely unoccupied because it is conceptually incoherent, practically unattractive, or merely unexplored.

In addition, introduce a fifth branching dimension of your own invention—something not standardly found in textbook treatments of this debate—that genuinely separates positions in logical space. This might concern the temporal dynamics of the effect (whether it persists after withdrawal of the cognitive state), its reversibility through counter-priming, or its dependence on linguistic categorization versus non-linguistic expertise.

The tree should contain exactly six terminal leaves. Flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly distinguish, explaining the source of the ambiguity. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or recommended readings. Phrase every node as a direct question about the case or about general mechanisms, never as a menu of named options.
