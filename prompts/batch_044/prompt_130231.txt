I chair the program-evaluation panel for a residential contemplative-care facility that operates under an explicit tranquilist charter: the institution holds that the only intrinsic goods are the absence of craving and the absence of suffering, and that every proposed resident activity must be justified in those terms alone, with no appeal to achievement, connection, knowledge, or dignity as independent values. We need a reusable decision procedure that my three-person panel—none of whom are professional philosophers—can run in about thirty minutes per proposal, applying the charter consistently without collapsing into subjective impression. Please construct the procedure as an ordered set of questions with explicit stopping rules: what counts as adequate evidence of suffering-reduction versus mere distraction or postponement; how to classify proposals that diminish present suffering but generate new cravings (for status, mastery, or even for further tranquility); and precisely where the procedure must halt and acknowledge that a value judgment, rather than an empirical finding about craving, determines the outcome. Be explicit about the conceptual boundary between negative welfare—the absence of harm—and positive welfare—the presence of goods—treating this distinction as decisive rather than as a sliding scale. Then apply the procedure to three proposals: first, a mandatory vocational-rehabilitation track that reduces anxiety about future unemployment but requires residents to cultivate competitive ambition and skill-acquisition desires; second, an open-ended mindfulness program with no structured goals that reports high subjective calm but shows no measurable reduction in reported craving intensity; and third, a palliative sedation protocol for residents with treatment-resistant existential distress, which eliminates conscious suffering but also eliminates all conscious preference-formation. For the third case in particular, address whether the tranquilist framework can coherently distinguish the absence of craving from the absence of the subject who might crave, and flag any point where the procedure breaks down into metaphysical disagreement rather than an institutional decision that the panel can legitimately sign.
