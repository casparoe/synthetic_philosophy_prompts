As a novelist, I have been turning over an argument about literary forgeries that now strikes me as proving far more than it should. I lay it out in steps because I want the fault line named precisely.

(1) A literary work is identical with the ordered sequence of sentences that makes up its text; any object that instantiates that sequence is an instance of the same work.
(2) The value of an object qua instance of a work depends only on the properties of the work itself, not on the causal history of the particular object.
(3) A perfect manuscript forgery reproduces the exact sequence of sentences found in the original.
(4) Therefore the forgery is an instance of the same work and carries the same literary value.
(5) Yet we treat the unmasking of a forgery as a catastrophic devaluation of the object, not merely as a revision of its provenance.
(6) So the value of the manuscript must depend on causal history—specifically, on having been produced by the author.
(7) If causal history is essential to the identity of the work, then two texts that are word-for-word identical but have different histories are not instances of one work but two distinct works.
(8) Consequently there exist in the world two different novels—one authentic, one forged—occupying precisely the same words.
(9) Literary interpretation, however, takes the text as authoritative and offers no internal criterion for selecting between the two putative works.
(10) Hence interpretation is permanently indeterminate: it can never know which of the two works it is interpreting.
(11) That conclusion is intolerable; interpretation is not permanently paralyzed.
(12) Therefore one of the premises must be abandoned.

The argument seems to show that either literature has no autonomous textual identity or interpretation collapses into skepticism. Where does it slip? If forced to preserve premises, I would keep (1) and (6): surrendering (1) makes the text hostage to biography in a way that undermines literary criticism, and surrendering (6) leaves us unable to explain why forgeries are worthless. But I am prepared to pay elsewhere. Which step should be rejected, what makes it look plausible, and what is the cost of giving it up?
