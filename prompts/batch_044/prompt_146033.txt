I work as the head of interpretive design at a national museum of political history, and I need a reusable decision procedure for my curatorial team. Every quarter we receive proposals for gallery frames that treat military or political defeats as living elements of national identity — what are commonly called lost causes. Recent submissions have concerned the Confederate Lost Cause, the siege of Masada, the 1389 Battle of Kosovo Field, and the 1940 Dunkirk evacuation. I want the procedure grounded in the philosophy of collective memory, and I want you to treat as operative a specific three-part distinction: commemorative fidelity (presenting a defeated party’s experience and values on their own terms), mythic compensation (reframing defeat as a hidden moral victory that licenses present grievances), and mnemonic restitution (treating the defeat as a causal contribution to present moral order, thereby attributing hidden efficacy to it).

Please construct a step-by-step procedure my team can run in under thirty minutes per proposal. It should ask ordered questions, specify what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, state clearly where the procedure stops with a verdict of not admissible without revision, and flag explicitly the points where the issue is one of values rather than facts and must be referred to a senior curator or ethics board. Be honest about where the procedure will misfire or yield answers a curator will dislike, and state what it cannot do — for instance, whether it can adjudicate between competing national narratives when both invoke the same defeat.

Then apply the procedure to three proposals:
(a) framing the Confederate surrender at Appomattox as a noble defence of agrarian virtue against industrial coercion, whose defeat nonetheless preserved a distinct cultural identity that remains morally authoritative today;
(b) framing Masada as the tragic end of a besieged community, presented without attributing present moral authority to the act of collective suicide;
(c) framing the 1389 Kosovo Field defeat as a sacrificial event that secured Serbian spiritual sovereignty and continues to justify present territorial claims.

Note that for case (c) I am personally inclined to accept the framing as legitimate commemorative fidelity because of its centrality to Serbian national memory; apply the procedure without deference to my preference, and tell me plainly if it produces an answer I will not like.

Finally, take a definite position — do not merely list options — on whether any reusable procedure of this kind can avoid embedding its own normative commitments about which defeats deserve sympathy, and argue for that view with reasons. Write the entire response in British English.
