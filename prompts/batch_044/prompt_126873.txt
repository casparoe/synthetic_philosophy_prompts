I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy in earnest, and I wish to lead a single ninety-minute discussion for a small reading circle of scientifically literate non-philosophers on the varieties of free will worth wanting. I do not want a summary of standard positions; I want a teaching plan that sets out the precise dependency order of the conceptual pieces and explains why each must precede the next, the one concrete carrying example and exactly which features it must possess and which it must lack, the misconceptions this audience will import from a deterministic scientific worldview together with the exact point in the sequence at which each should be pre-empted, a brief check at the end that distinguishes genuine grasp of the structure from mere agreement, and an explicit statement of what should be deliberately omitted at this level—particularly any appeal to source-incompatibilist literature—with the reason given for that omission. Please write the plan in British English. In constructing it, please treat diachronic authorship (the temporal continuity of one’s deliberative identity) and synchronic openness (the live presence of genuinely available alternative paths at the moment of decision) as analytically separable axes rather than as a single bundled opposition, and design the carrying example so that it clearly exhibits one axis in the absence of the other; also anticipate the specific misconception that predictive success in neuroscience undermines authorship even when synchronic openness is absent, and indicate precisely where in the sequence this should be addressed.
