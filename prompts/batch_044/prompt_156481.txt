You are the data-ethics lead at a mid-sized health-tech firm that builds clinical-risk prediction tools. The board has adopted the view that race, gender, and socioeconomic status are institutional rather than natural kinds—specifically, interactive kinds that change through being measured and acted upon. You need a reusable decision procedure—an ordered checklist with stop points, judgment-call flags, and explicit hand-offs to human deliberation—for your team to apply whenever a data scientist proposes including one of these categories as a model feature. The procedure should translate the looping-effect idea into concrete steps: what to verify about the measurement instrument, what institutional context to check, when exclusion is mandatory, when inclusion requires a reflexivity safeguard, and where the question becomes normative rather than technical. Apply it to three proposals: (1) self-identified gender in a mental-health screening algorithm; (2) race-coded proxies (ZIP code plus insurance type) in a cardiovascular-risk model; and (3) a banking-pattern-derived financial-stress index used as a class proxy. Include at least one case where the procedure yields an answer the engineering team will resist, and close by stating what the procedure cannot resolve. Write in American English.
