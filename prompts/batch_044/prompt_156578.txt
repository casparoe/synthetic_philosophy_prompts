I've come around to thinking that weakness of will isnt actually a failure of self-control but a rational stalemate driven by hyperbolic discounting. The core claim is that your present self treats future selves as distinct strategic agents with unreliable commitment devices — think of it like a shadow-contract problem — and because the discount curve spikes right near now, the present self correctly predicts that any long-term plan gets undermined by the near-future self. So akrasia becomes a stable equilibrium: youre not failing to do what you judge best, youre rationally refusing to sign contracts you know will be broken. What would count against this? I want defeaters ranked from fatal to awkward — empirical findings on discount curves, phenomenological mismatches, maybe formal results showing the equilibrium collapses — plus an honest take on whether this has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so whether thats actually a problem for this kind of view. Also tell me which objections defenders of this framing would themselves concede as damaging, versus ones they'd write off as category errors.
