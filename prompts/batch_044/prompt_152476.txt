I want a reusable decision procedure I can actually run in under an hour, grounded in Friedman's F-twist and whatever philosophy of science actually says about unrealistic assumptions: does the false assumption matter for this specific prediction and decision, or not? Give me the ordered steps: the exact question to ask at each point, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops with "reject the model for this permit," where it stops with "this is a values call, not mine," and where I still have to make a judgment call despite the checklist. Apply it to three proposals: (1) a model assuming perfect substitutability that predicts flood-control acreage needed; (2) a model with homogeneous rational agents predicting 50-year carbon sequestration, where the result allows development I'd rather block; (3) a model with perfect-info agents predicting restoration success used to justify minimal long-term monitoring. Be explicit about what the procedure cant do.
