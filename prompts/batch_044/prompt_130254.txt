I am trying to clarify what an adequate causal-historical theory of long-run national prosperity actually has to deliver, and I want the full specification before I assess any particular institutionalist, geographic, or cultural account. The existing literature often conflates three distinct explanatory targets — cross-sectional variance at a single point in time, the temporal trajectory of one polity across centuries, and counterfactual resilience (what a nation’s path would have been under different colonial or climatic conditions) — and it treats competing explanations as if they were answers to the same question. Before I read further, I want the desiderata separated out with the logic behind each, and I want to know which ones can genuinely be satisfied together.
