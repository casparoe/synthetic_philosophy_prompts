Give an example of a social information environment that exhibits the exposure profile of an epistemic bubble—members rarely encounter well-formulated opposing views—yet is sustained not by algorithmic curation or social homophily, but by a collective practice of preemptive credibility deflation: whenever dissent does penetrate, members share a vocabulary that systematically downgrades its source status without engaging its content, so the environment also displays the trust-structure dynamics characteristic of an echo chamber. Specify the institutional or conversational mechanisms that produce this hybrid, identify the precise conceptual tension it creates for the standard distinction between bubbles (informational exclusion) and echo chambers (credibility-based exclusion), and argue whether accommodating it requires expanding one of the two categories, introducing a third category, or reconceiving the distinction as dimensional rather than taxonomic.
