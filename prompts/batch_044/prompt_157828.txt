You serve as a compliance auditor for a university accreditation body. Twice yearly you must review algorithmic learning-analytics platforms adopted by departments—systems that log keystroke dynamics, response latencies, and peer-ranking percentiles—and determine whether they instantiate Foucault's disciplinary power: specifically, the fusion of hierarchical observation with normalizing judgment that produces self-regulating subjects, as distinct from ordinary administrative feedback. Design a reusable, one-page decision procedure that you or a non-philosopher colleague can apply in a two-hour site review. The procedure must contain: (1) three observable indicators that separate disciplinary normalization from benign assessment; (2) a stepwise flowchart-in-words for platforms that trigger none, some, or all three indicators; (3) clearly marked points where judgment is unavoidable and must be signed off by a senior reviewer; (4) three worked cases—one clear failure, one clear pass, and one borderline case in which the procedure yields an outcome the adopting department will dispute; and (5) a brief statement of what the procedure cannot settle and must be escalated to the board. Ground the indicators in Foucault's analysis of the examination and the norm without citing specific texts. Introduce one modern mechanism not central to Foucault's original account—for instance, real-time biometric feedback loops—and specify exactly how the procedure classifies it.
