I'm preparing a discussion for an advanced seminar on the cognitive architecture of creativity, and I've become dissatisfied with standard taxonomies—such as the familiar division into exploratory, combinational, and transformational creativity—because they classify outputs without specifying the mechanism that produces them. I want to focus instead on theories that take Hofstadter's idea of "knob-twiddling"—systematic variation of parameters within an existing conceptual space—as the fundamental engine of creative production, treating creativity not as invention ex nihilo but as the discovery of meaningful variations on a theme. Before I assess particular models, I need the full conceptual specification of what such an account is actually committed to delivering.

Please write the desiderata: what any adequate theory treating variations-on-a-theme, in this knob-twiddling sense, as the crux of creativity must be able to do. For each requirement, include the motivating case or conceptual distinction behind it. Specifically, the theory must distinguish parametric drift—continuous adjustment of variables within a fixed representational scheme—from structural transposition, in which the dimensions that count as "knobs" or the relational architecture of the theme itself are redefined; it must explain how variations are evaluated and selected without smuggling in an external evaluator that already knows the correct answer; it must account for cases of apparent radical novelty that nevertheless retain thematic continuity, such as an improvisation that temporarily suspends the governing framework yet remains recognizably the same piece; and it must respect the empirical constraint that human creators often report feeling constrained or "pulled" by the problem domain rather than freely exploring an open space.
