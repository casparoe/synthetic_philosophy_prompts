I'm writing up my views on moral worth for a seminar and want a consistency check before presenting. Here is where I stand: (1) Moral worth is strictly proportional to acting for the right reasons, where those reasons are the ones that correctly represent the act's moral properties—call this reason-tracking internalism. (2) An agent can possess full moral worth even when she lacks the conceptual vocabulary to articulate why her action is right, provided her non-cognitive affective orientation is aretaically calibrated to the situation—so moral knowledge is not necessary for praiseworthiness. (3) I deny that meta-cognitive awareness of one's own motives is required for worth; a spontaneously compassionate agent who never reflects on her reasons can be maximally praiseworthy. (4) I accept evolutionary debunking arguments that show our affective calibrations are unreliable indicators of objective moral properties, so emotional fit should not be treated as evidential support for the claim that someone acted for the right reason. (5) I hold that reflective equilibrium is the best method for identifying which reasons are genuinely right, and that this procedure must begin from our considered judgments about who deserves praise and blame, judgments that are themselves shaped by our affective responses.
