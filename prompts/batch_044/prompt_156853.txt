Take the Mytilene debate in 427 BCE — Cleon vs. Diodotus, the assembly votes to slaughter every male citizen then flips the very next morning — and analize it through the lens of moral luck and what I'd call "democratic reversal." Then: does the reversal make the first decision less blameworthy, or does moral luck cut against them?
