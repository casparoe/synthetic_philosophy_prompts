Write a villanelle about applying Dennett's intentional stance to a large language model. Use two refrains: one denying that the system literally possesses beliefs, the other insisting that belief-attribution is predictively unavoidable. In the tercets, build the case for adopting the stance—predictive success, rational coherence, and the absence of a more efficient physical-level explanation—while allowing the model's context-dependent contradictions (the same framing yielding opposite assertions) to intrude as the stability objection that keeps breaking through. The repetition and rhyme should make the two positions circle each other without convergence. In the final quatrain, let both refrains stand together, side by side, so the form enacts their mutual dependence rather than resolving whether the model "really" believes. Be accurate enough that a philosopher of mind recognises Dennett's conditions operating in the imagery, but do not request citations, references, or a bibliography.
