I am preparing the interview guide for a podcast episode on metaphysics and philosophy of science. My guest is a philosopher who defends eternalism—the view that past, present, and future events are all equally real—by appealing specifically to the Rietdijk–Putnam argument drawn from special relativity. Rather than having her restate the basic symmetry of simultaneity, I want eight to ten interview questions that work in the weeds of the argument, press where the literature actually presses, and include a follow-up for each question keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. Please include at least one question that distinguishes Rietdijk’s original determinist framing from Putnam’s later ontological conclusion, and ask whether the presentist can accept the determinism without accepting eternalism. Include at least one question centered on the Andromeda-paradox scenario: two observers in relative motion disagree about whether a distant event is happening “now,” and I want the follow-up to anticipate her saying this disagreement is merely epistemic, then press her on why a presentist is entitled to treat frame-relative simultaneity as ontologically open rather than metaphysically determinate. Include at least one question about the presentist move of positing a metaphysically privileged reference frame that physics simply fails to detect; the follow-up should ask whether this does not reduce presentism to an empirically empty, ad hoc position. Finally, include one question that introduces a somewhat unconventional constraint—imagine a theory of conscious experience that requires a globally unique present moment for any subject of experience—to probe whether the block view must reject such a theory outright, or whether it can accommodate frame-relative “presents” of consciousness without contradiction. The tone should be rigorous and conversational, aimed at graduate students and specialists in philosophy of time, with no generic biographical questions and no requests for bibliographies or further reading.
