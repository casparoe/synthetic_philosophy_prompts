I'm preparing a graduate seminar session on moral standing and hypocrisy. Please write a dialogue between two moral philosophers—call them A and B—debating whether hypocrisy undermines standing to blame, but frame the disagreement through the distinction between diachronic and synchronic standing. A should defend diachronic standing: one's authority to blame is constituted across a moral biography, so past complicity leaves a residue that can be attenuated but never fully dissolved by reform. B should defend synchronic standing: blame-authority depends only on present relational position and current moral consistency, so a reformer's past hypocrisy is irrelevant unless it generates a present conflict of interest. Have them apply this to a concrete case—a former lobbyist for fossil-fuel interests now leading climate litigation—so that they must respond to each other rather than talk past one another. A should press that B cannot account for why the lobbyist's blame still feels hollow; B should press that A makes standing unattainable for anyone with a past. Keep both voices equally competent, with real back-and-forth, and avoid strawmanning. The dialogue should be suitable for a graduate seminar in moral and political philosophy.
