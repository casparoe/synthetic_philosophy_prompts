I am a retired field ecologist who has recently begun studying moral philosophy in earnest. In discussions with a local philosophy group, I have taken the following positions on proposed ecological interventions, though I have not fully articulated the principle behind them.

First, I endorsed a plan to cull an overabundant deer population in a protected reserve, where starvation and disease were causing widespread suffering, arguing that reducing that suffering justified the intervention.

Second, I opposed draining a seasonal wetland to prevent the breeding of mosquitoes and biting flies, maintaining that destroying a functioning aquatic ecosystem is wrong even when the welfare of its smallest inhabitants is ambiguous.

Third, I supported introducing a targeted biological control to eliminate an invasive rodent that was inflicting prolonged suffering on native ground-nesting birds.

Fourth, I argued against suppressing a natural wildfire in an old-growth forest, holding that the immediate suffering caused by the fire is outweighed by the high-welfare regeneration that follows, and that interference disrupts a natural cycle we do not fully understand.

Now I am considering a new proposal: deliberately fragmenting a large, stable grassland ecosystem into smaller, isolated patches separated by barriers. The ecological rationale is that in large continuous habitats, predator-prey dynamics produce severe boom-bust cycles, with intense suffering during population crashes; smaller, isolated patches would stabilize populations at lower densities, reducing the amplitude of these cycles and therefore the total suffering experienced, though overall population size, biodiversity, and ecosystem connectivity would decline significantly.

Given my four prior verdicts, what should I say about this fragmentation proposal if I wish to be consistent? Please identify at least two distinct principles that could account for my earlier judgments, say what each predicts about the fragmentation case, and indicate which principle you think best fits my track record with your reasoning. Then suggest one further hypothetical case that would most clearly distinguish between the leading candidates. I am less interested in the final yes-or-no answer than in seeing the structure of the reasoning mapped onto my own prior commitments. Please write your response in American English.
