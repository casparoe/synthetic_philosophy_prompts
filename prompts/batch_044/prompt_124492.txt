Write a deadpan parody of an academic tenure-review dossier evaluating an interdisciplinary artist whose entire practice is governed by Douglas Hofstadter’s “knob-twiddling” model of creativity. The dossier should run approximately 700 words and be formatted in sustained bureaucratic deadpan as numbered institutional sections: (1) an executive summary that maps the candidate’s “conceptual parameter space” across at least two distinct media—specify the invariant formal constraints (e.g., harmonic function, narrative arc, rhyme scheme, or logical form) that remain fixed while surface features are systematically varied, treating each finished work as a translation of a canonical theme under preserved structure rather than as an ex nihilo invention; (2) an internal memo from a skeptical senior colleague who charges that the oeuvre is merely “search-space traversal” and who raises objections that a committed defender of the knob-twiddling view must be able to answer without smuggling in appeals to ineffable genius, unconscious intuition, or mystical inspiration; (3) the candidate’s formal response, which defends the work entirely from within the model—arguing that analogical slippage and parameter adjustment are not deficiencies but the actual mechanism by which concepts maintain identity across transformation, and addressing whether preserving formal constraint across variations is sufficient to ground judgments of originality, depth, or aesthetic value; (4) the committee’s final recommendation, which should implicitly pose the philosophical question of whether, if creativity collapses into the systematic adjustment of conceptual knobs, our evaluative vocabulary of “vision” and “genius” survives in any meaningful form or becomes purely retrospective labeling. The satire must derive from taking the model’s real commitments seriously—especially the centrality of analogical reasoning and the idea that concepts are defined by their “slippage”—rather than from inventing false caricatures. Do not include bibliographies, citation requests, or reading lists. If you reference any specific published argument or text, paraphrase it without quotation marks; do not invent specific page numbers or verbatim passages. The piece should be readable to someone unfamiliar with Hofstadter, but should reward readers who recognize the actual structure of the view being ironized.
