hey, quick sanity check — i'm a phd student in phil of science (mostly climate modeling, so this is way outside my usual stuff) and my roommate summarized structural realism for me like this: "The core claim is that science accumulates only structural knowledge — we map the relational architecture but never the intrinsic nature of things. Worrall's classic argument showed that in theory change the mathematical formalism survives intact, not just approximately, which means objects like electrons are just theoretical placeholders with no independent existence. Poincaré had this insight around 1900 but people ignored it because they wanted materialist ontologies. Ontic structural realists push this further in quantum mechanics: entanglement relations are ontologically prior to the particles involved, so there literally aren't particles, only correlation structures. Epistimic structural realism is just the weaker view that we stay agnostic about intrinsic properties, but both versions reject scientific realism's commitment to objects."
