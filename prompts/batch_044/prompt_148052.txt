I retired from the physics lab a few years back and since then I've been slowly working through some old Chinese philosophy — mostly Zhuangzi, plus a bit of Guo Xiang's commentary. The butterfly dream passage keeps tripping me up: he wakes up unsure whether he's Zhuang Zhou who dreamed he was a butterfly, or the butterfly now dreaming it's him. To me this sounds exactly like Descartes' dream argument — if the phenominal quality of waking and dreaming is identical, we have no criterion to distinguish them, so global skeptisism about the external world seems unavoidable. But then I read that Zhuangzi's real point is wuhua, the transformation of things, and that the self is just a temporary configuration of qi with no fixed boundary. So here's what I don't get: if he isn't actually trying to establish that we can't know reality, why does he frame the experience as an epistemic deadlock at all? Is he deliberately setting up a skeptical problem just to dissolve it through transformation, or am I projecting the Cartesian framing onto him? And if the issue is really ontological — about the lack of a stable self rather than about knowledge — does that mean the modern analytic literature on dream skeptisism is fundamentally misreading the passage by treating transformation as an answer to a problem he never posed?
