I am preparing an interview for my philosophy-of-history seminar with a scholar who advances a revisionist account of Nietzsche's three historical modes—monumental, antiquarian, and critical—from the essay on history's uses and disadvantages for life. She contends that critical history is not essentially destructive but reparative: it dissolves only those past commitments that produce what she terms "temporal alienation," leaving the present's capacity for action intact rather than severing all continuity. On her reading, monumental history does not invite imitation of great deeds but borrows their "energetic profile" to address structurally analogous present problems, while antiquarian history becomes harmful not through excessive preservation but through the error of treating preservation itself as normative justification.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that engage the substance of this position rather than her biography or career. I want at least one question pressing how reparative criticism can be distinguished from mere selective amnesia; one testing whether the "energetic profile" reading genuinely blocks dangerous hero-worship or collapses into it; and one asking what her framework predicts for a culture that entirely lacks an antiquarian impulse. For each of these three, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to offer—for instance, if she appeals to the agent's present decision-capacity as the filter, ask whether that criterion is circular. Keep the remaining questions focused on internal tensions in the triad, such as whether reparative criticism can ever stabilize enough content for monumental or antiquarian use, and avoid generic questions about the value of studying history in general.
