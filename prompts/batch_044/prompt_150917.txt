okay so im a novelist and ive been obsessing over hofstadters knob-twiddling model—creativity as turning dials on some mental theme, right? but im stuck on which dials actually carry the theme and which ones are just cosmetic paint. here are my gut verdicts on concrete cases, and they dont line up neatly.

case one: i take a short story and swap every adjective for its opposite, leaving plot, charcters, and setting untouched. my call: thats not a variation, thats vandalism. the theme is neither twisted nor revealed.

case two: same story moved from rainy victorian london to a dust-colony on mars, emotional architecture kept exactly. call: thats a real variation. the turned knobs (setting, tech) feel surface-level, yet the theme breathes differently.

case three: gender-flip every charcter, change nothing else—not even pronoun logic, just he becomes she. sometimes this feels like a cheap gimmick; other times it feels like it exposes hidden gendered bones in the power dynamics. my verdict is split: if the story is about grief its cosmetic, if about dominance its profound. i cant tell in advance which knobs are loaded.

case four: a composer takes a folk melody and reharmonizes it in 7/4 with distorted guitars. call: creative variation, the theme is warped but intact.

case five: same composer bumps tempo 10 bpm and calls it a remix. call: not a variation at all, just faster playback.

case six: i write a sequel where the protagonist makes the exact opposite moral choice at every branch—where she lied she tells truth, where she forgave she punishes. call: this feels like a variation, but also like ive snapped the theme rather than twisted it. like turning the 'honesty' knob so far it breaks.

so is there a coherent principle—maybe about which knobs are entangled with the theme's identity conditions—that saves cases two and four, rejects one and five, and handles three and six? be precise: state the principle, which clause is motivated by which case, and what it says about cases i havent mentioned—like translating a poem into another language, or replacing every noun with a synonym, or rewriting a tragedy as a comedy keeping every line intact. also be honest: which of my gut reactions (especially the split on three and the break/twist ambiguity on six) does your principle have to reject? i dont want a scorecard of other theories; i want the spec of the principle itself, and where it forces me to change my mind. i cant recieve a answer that just lists examples without the rule behind them.
