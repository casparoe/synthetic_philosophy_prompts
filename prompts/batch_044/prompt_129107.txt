Construct a realistic, single-paragraph thought experiment—set in an ordinary clinical genetic-counseling or family-planning context, with no science-fiction technology, no perfect predictors, and stakes of the kind a normal person faces—in which Benatar's asymmetry between pleasure and pain for never-existent people delivers a verdict about a specific reproductive choice that conflicts with the verdict of a symmetric, person-affecting view weighing potential welfare equally regardless of actualization.
