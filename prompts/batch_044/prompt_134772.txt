Write an essay examining whether the nationalist and anti-Semitic commitments recorded in Gottlob Frege's late personal diary should alter our philosophical assessment of his logicism and anti-psychologism. Rather than treating the question as purely biographical, weigh whether there is a conceptual tension between his doctrine that thoughts are objective, shareable entities independent of any particular mind or cultural context, and the particularist, exclusionary political vision he articulated privately. Consider the argument that his universalism about logical content was always implicitly bounded—applying rigorously to abstract structures but not extending to the human subjects who grasp them—and evaluate whether this selective application undermines the coherence of his anti-psychologism, or whether it can be quarantined as an unrelated moral failure. You should also address the counter-position: that Frege's philosophy of language and logic contains no internal conceptual resources for deriving political exclusion, so that integrating the diary into his philosophical evaluation commits a category mistake between normative logic and empirical prejudice. Reach a definite conclusion about how, if at all, the diary should bear on our reading of Frege's philosophical work, and justify it by reference to the internal structure of his views on objectivity, thought, and mental content.
