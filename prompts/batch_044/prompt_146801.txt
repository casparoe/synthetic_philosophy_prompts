Many governance regimes for immersive virtual-reality platforms apply an unexplained three-part distinction: they classify unwanted haptic-avatar contact as a “virtual harm” that warrants immediate suspension; they treat the deletion or theft of virtual goods and currency as a license breach rather than a harm to the user; and their terms of service deny that virtual objects are property, describing them only as revocable access permissions. What implicit ontology of virtual bodies and virtual objects, and what implicit theory of harm, does this institutional pattern presuppose? Reconstruct the view that renders these three judgments consistent—carefully separating what the practice strictly requires from what merely fits it well—then ask whether the same framework could survive on a purely representational ontology that denies virtual worlds contain entities with independent moral status. Finally, specify what a moderation policy built on such a representational view would have to change in its sanctions, its user agreements, and its classification of bodily-space violations.
