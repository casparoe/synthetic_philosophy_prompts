I'm applying for a four-month independent research grant in philosophy of science; the deliverable is a single 7,000–8,000 word paper. My half-formed idea is that the standard framing of structural realism forces a false choice between epistemic structural realism (we know only the structure, not the objects) and ontic structural realism (there are only structures, and objects are eliminated or derivative). I want to test a third option—call it modal-structural constitution—according to which physical objects are not eliminated but are constituted entirely by the modal constraints they impose on neighboring nodes in a structure. On this view, an object retains enough stability to support counterfactual reasoning about it, yet has no intrinsic properties independent of those modal relations. The question is whether this genuinely escapes the familiar "relations without relata" problem, or whether it merely renames it, and whether applying it to quantum field theory—where particles are already treated as field excitations—produces a determinate ontology or collapses back into either epistemic or full ontic structural realism once the modal rules are specified.
