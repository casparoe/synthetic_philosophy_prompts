I'm a student in a graduate seminar on Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and I've kept running into an argumentative pattern I can't map onto the standard taxonomy of hinges, framework propositions, or riverbed analogies. The pattern works like this. A speaker treats a given commitment—say, that physical objects do not spontaneously vanish and reappear uncaused, or that the earth has existed for far longer than five minutes—as a hinge in Wittgenstein's sense: not a piece of knowledge justified by evidence, but part of the scaffolding that makes empirical justification possible in the first place. Then, when a skeptical or counterfactual scenario is introduced—a Boltzmann-brain cosmology, a five-minute-old universe with implanted memories—the speaker evaluates that very same commitment exactly as if it were an ordinary empirical claim, weighing its consistency with other beliefs, its prior probability, and the explanatory costs of denying it. Once the scenario is dismissed, the speaker reverts immediately to treating the commitment as pre-evidential and immune to rational scrutiny, with no acknowledgment that it was just subjected to evidential testing and no change in their overall epistemic posture. I see this in debates about the external world and in historical methodology, where the reliability of testimony is first bracketed as part of the framework, then tested inside the bracket, then restored untouched.

Is there an established concept for this specific oscillation—not for the hinge itself, but for the maneuver of temporarily downgrading a framework commitment to a contingent claim for hypothetical evaluation and then upgrading it back to hinge status without any recognized shift? If there is no name, can you coin and define one that distinguishes it precisely from Cartesian methodological doubt, from epistemic contextualism about knowledge attributions, and from plain equivocation? I want the carving done in detail: what exactly the phenomenon is, what it is not, where its boundaries lie, and whether the temporary suspension counts as genuine doubt or merely a simulated move within the language-game. In particular, does the fact that the hinge is evaluated by evidential criteria inside the hypothetical show that it was never truly outside the game of justification, or does it show that the game can be temporarily suspended and then resumed with the scaffolding intact?
