I retired from running a biochem lab about five years ago and lately I've been trying to wrap my head around philosphy of economics and bioethics, though I still think like an experimentalist. I'm serving on my state's Allocation Ethics Panel — a small board that advises the health department on pilot programs — and next thursday I need to circulate a written opinion before we vote. The case is a proposal called the Kin-Linked Reciprocal Donor Bond: living kidney donors wouldn't recieve cash, but they'd get a tax-sheltered health-security voucher that can only be activated for themselves or a named relative within ten years, with no transfer outside the family line. Advocates insist there's no price mechanism, so it isn't commodification; critics say it's just kidney sales with extra paperwork and it will crowd out genuine altruism. I've been arguing about it over coffee with a friend who thinks any incentive corrupts the gift, but I want something more rigorous than that for the board.

Please draft the panel's decision memo: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, lay out the reasoning in terms the researchers and advocates can actually respond to, and give an honest treatment of the strongest argument against your recommendation rather than just dismissing it. After the memo, include a brief structured summary in JSON with exactly these fields: "recommendation" (string: your bottom line), "core_distinction" (string: the conceptual line separating this from a market), "strongest_counter" (string: the best objection to your view), "time_frame" (string: any review period or deadline you propose), and "action_items" (array of strings: concrete steps for the board).
