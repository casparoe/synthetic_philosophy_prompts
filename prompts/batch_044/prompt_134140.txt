You are an expert philosopher of science and metaethics with deep familiarity with Quinean naturalism, ontological relativity, and the structure of conceptual schemes. Before you begin, take a moment to think carefully and brainstorm the key distinctions between treating a commitment as an internal feature of a conceptual framework versus treating it as an answer to an independent reality, especially as this applies not just to ontology but to normative discourse.

Consider the following institutional practice. The International Consortium for Responsible Research Funding requires every grant proposal to submit two separate impact statements: an Ontological Impact Statement, in which applicants specify the entities (genes, quantum fields, social structures) their research treats as real and the auxiliary hypotheses they are willing to revise to preserve predictive success; and a Normative Impact Statement, in which applicants specify the values (human welfare, ecological integrity, cultural autonomy) that constrain their methods. Crucially, the Normative Impact Statement must be filed in one of two registers. In the Universalist Register, values are presented as cross-culturally binding constraints that any adequate conceptual scheme must accommodate, much like observational predictions. In the Scheme-Relative Register, values are presented as internal to the applicant's research tradition—revisable in response to pragmatic pressure, comparable to the ontological commitments listed in the first statement, and not claiming authority over communities operating under different normative frameworks. The Consortium treats both registers as equally eligible for funding, but it prohibits mixing registers within a single proposal and requires peer reviewers to evaluate each proposal only by the internal coherence of the register it has chosen.

What philosophical commitments about moral values does this two-register funding practice presuppose? Does it strictly presuppose a Quinean view—that moral values, like ontological posits, are part of our web of belief with no independent metaphysical status and are subject to revision for pragmatic reasons—or is it also compatible with robust moral realism combined with epistemic humility? Reconstruct the implicit metaethical theory that makes sense of treating normative commitments as register-selectable in parallel with ontological ones. Distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits comfortably with. Is there an inconsistency between the Consortium's willingness to treat values as scheme-internal and its simultaneous insistence that certain research practices (e.g., non-consensual experimentation) are ineligible regardless of register? If so, does that reveal a hidden universalist commitment, or can a Quinean explain it as a non-negotiable pragmatic boundary of the funding scheme itself? Finally, describe what would have to change in the Consortium's procedures—specifically in its peer-review criteria and its prohibition on register-mixing—if the underlying Quinean conventionalism about values were replaced by a realist view on which moral values are discovered rather than adopted. Where is the practice philosophically underdetermined, such that several rival theories of value could license the same institutional design?
