Design a formal decision-tree taxonomy, suitable for a graduate-level philosophy of mind and ethics seminar, that maps the logical space of answers to the question: Does brain size — absolute cranial volume, encephalization quotient, or neuron count — carry independent moral weight in assessing the moral status of nonhuman animals and cognitively marginal human beings?

The tree must open with a root node asking whether neural scale is taken to have direct normative significance or only indirect evidential value. From there, construct at least five successive branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete conceptual commitment rather than a label-choice. The commitments should distinguish, at minimum, the following four positions: (1) neurocentric gradualism, which treats encephalization quotient adjusted for body mass as a continuous gradient of moral patienthood; (2) capacity-functionalism, which denies that size metrics have direct moral relevance but allows that they serve as fallible proxies for the cognitive capacities that do matter; (3) species-normativism, which holds that moral status is determined by conformity to a species-typical neural architecture rather than by absolute or relative scale; and (4) allometric-bias rejection, which contends that cross-species brain-size comparisons are confounded by non-linear scaling laws relating brain mass to body plan and metabolic constraints, rendering such comparisons morally inert.

At each branch, include a diagnostic hypothetical case — for instance, whether a human brain artificially enlarged to twice its normal volume but preserving identical functional connectivity and cognitive profile would acquire enhanced moral status — so that a respondent can navigate the tree without prior knowledge of the position labels.

The taxonomy should terminate in at least six leaves. Identify any logically possible combination of answers that remains unoccupied (for example, a view that grants direct moral weight to raw cranial volume while simultaneously denying that brain size correlates with any cognitive capacity) and provide a brief diagnosis of whether the gap stems from internal incoherence, empirical implausibility, or mere historical neglect.

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, named living authors, or citation requests. The instrument should be self-contained and evaluable entirely by its internal logical structure. Write in a precise, deadpan academic register, approximately 500–700 words, with clear textual indication of branching paths.
