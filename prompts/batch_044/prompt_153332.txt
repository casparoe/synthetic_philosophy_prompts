Right, so I'm redrafting this novel and I've hit a thing I can't quite name. There's my protagonist, Mara — in draft one she pulls her brother from the river, in draft two she freezes and he drowns. Now I'm writing a third version where Mara hasn't saved him, but she's plagued by these vivid, almost bodily memories of the drowning, as if she lived through it in some other life, yet she knows in her actual history he never existed. When I show this to readers they ask "is she still the same Mara?" and I notice everyone reaches for one of two answers: either she's literally the same soul replayed with different luck, or she's just a duplicate who happens to look identical. But my Mara isn't either of those. She isn't the original individual transplanted into a different outcome, and she isn't an unrelated copy either — she's carrying this partial, ghostly continuity with the other version's experience, like she's haunted by a path she never technically walked. I know philosophers argue about whether one person can exist across different possible worlds or whether each world gets its own seperate copy, but I don't know the proper names for these views. So: is there an established concept for this specific in-between relation, where someone has psychological access to another version's life without being numerically identical to them? Or am I just smuggling together two positions that should stay apart? If there's no name, what should I call it — echo-identity? — and where exactly does the standard distinction fail to capture what I'm describing?
