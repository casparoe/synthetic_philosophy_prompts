Write an essay on whether large language models should be interpreted through Dennett’s intentional stance as genuine belief-holders, focusing specifically on the problem of epistemic ventriloquism: the tendency of such systems to emit belief-like assertions drawn from statistical patterns in training data rather than fixed by internal evidential states. Weigh the argument that predictive utility alone justifies attributing beliefs to an LLM, and counter it with the claim that belief requires a specific kind of error-correction dynamics—sensitivity to contradiction and revision under evidence—that parameter updates via gradient descent do not replicate at the level of individual propositions. Take a definite position: either defend a restricted form of the intentional stance for AI that distinguishes functional belief-attribution from literal belief-possession, or argue that all such attribution is metaphorical and should be replaced by a different explanatory framework. Justify your verdict by analyzing a concrete case in which an LLM confidently asserts a falsehood that conflicts with information present in its context window, and explain whether that failure is best understood as holding a false belief, lacking a belief altogether, or exhibiting something else.
