I am constructing a conceptual sorting instrument for a graduate seminar on Marx's economic-philosophical writings, specifically for students who have outline familiarity with the 1844 Manuscripts and the mature critique of political economy but keep conflating exploitation with alienation in circular debate. I want a decision tree — not a prose comparison — that classifies interpretive positions by three successive diagnostic commitments, with each branch labeled by the claim that splits it.

The first split asks whether the extraction of surplus value (exploitation) is conceptually prior to the estrangement of labor from its product, process, fellow workers, and species-being (alienation), whether alienation is prior, or whether the two are analytically independent phenomena that co-occur without explanation in either direction.

The second split, applied within each trunk, asks whether alienation depends essentially on the commodity-form and wage-contract specific to capitalism, or whether it can persist in non-capitalist social formations — for example, in centralized planning structures where surplus is extracted bureaucratically rather than through market exchange — wherever labor is separated from the means of production.

The third split asks whether the normative wrong of exploitation is fully reducible to the harm of alienation, leaving no residual injustice if estrangement were eliminated, or whether exploitation carries a distinct wrong — such as domination over labor-time or an unfair distributive claim on surplus — that survives even a hypothetical cure of alienation.

Each leaf should correspond to a named position if one is standardly defended (for instance, a distributive-injustice reading that treats exploitation as primary and independent of phenomenological harm; a humanist reading that derives exploitation's wrongness from prior alienation; a structural-co-constitution view that denies reducibility in either direction). Where a logical combination is unoccupied, label it unnamed and state in one sentence whether its emptiness is best explained by incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Every leaf must state its core commitment in one sentence and identify its most awkward commitment — the concrete case or implication it must accept that runs against common intuition.

Please construct the tree so that a reader can navigate it by answering the three concrete questions without knowing the philosophical labels in advance. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, page references, or reading lists; the instrument must stand on its own. Limit the tree to eight or fewer terminal nodes, and explicitly flag any two named positions that the three questions fail to separate.
