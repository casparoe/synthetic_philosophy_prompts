I'm taking a seminar on market ethics and keep returning to what seems like an under-theorized patchwork in how legal systems regulate exchanges involving the body. Consider the following constellation, which holds in several jurisdictions: patients who have registered as posthumous organ donors are awarded priority points on transplant waiting lists should they themselves later need an organ—an institutionalized reciprocity—while direct sales of kidneys remain criminalized, except in a few state-mediated programs. At the same time, commercial extraction of blood plasma is routine and profitable, yet whole-blood donation is legally restricted to voluntary, uncompensated contribution. Those same frameworks typically permit the sale of hair and sperm, allow surrogacy contracts only if payments are structured strictly as reimbursement for medical expenses rather than compensation for gestational labor itself, and treat price-gouging prohibitions on bottled water during emergencies differently from identical price spikes on luxury goods that consume comparable resources.

What philosophical commitments about commodification, bodily integrity, civic reciprocity, and the boundaries of the market does this particular pattern presuppose? Does the donor-priority system implicitly rest on a contractualist or social-contract theory of health-care obligations, or can it be reconstructed on purely consequentialist, virtue-ethical, or relational grounds? Is the differential treatment of plasma versus blood, or of surrogacy expenses versus service fees, compatible with a unified account—say, that markets corrupt specific kinds of intrinsic value, or that certain goods should remain outside exchange for reasons of dignity—or does the pattern reveal that the law tracks something more pragmatic, such as risk externalization, tractability, or social signaling? Reconstruct the implicit theory of value and exchange that best explains the constellation, carefully distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates, and indicate whether the same underlying commitments could justify extending these market boundaries to currently prohibited exchanges or contracting them further.
