ok so i built this argument for a seminar and now it feels like it proves way too much. tell me where it actually breaks, if it does.

1. the only variety of free will worth wanting is the kind that justifies moral blame rather than just treating people like malfunctioning devices.
2. for blame to be non-arbitrary, the agent has to be the ultimate source of the act—if genes or environment fully explain it, then blaming the person is just misplacing anger.
3. but if you're the ultimate source, your choice can't be fully determined by your prior character, reasons, or values, otherwise those prior things are the real source and you're just a conduit.
4. so the worth-wanting variety requires an indeterminst gap right at the moment of decision.
5. now apply that to Ulysses-style precommitment: if i sign a contract locking my future self out of the bar, i'm relying on determinism—on my future self being causally bound by my present plan.
6. but if there's an indeterminst gap at every decision point, then my future self isn't causally bound; at the moment of temptation she can just jump the gap and open the door.
7. so either we keep the gap and lose real self-binding (precommitment becomes an illusion), or we close the gap and lose ultimate sourcehood.
8. but people clearly think self-binding is rational and blame is fair. if (4) and (6) are both true, those beliefs contradict each other.
9. therefore either ultimate sourcehood isn't needed for blame, or Ulysses contracts don't work, or there's no coherent variety of free will worth wanting at all.

i'd really like to keep (1) and the idea that precommitment makes sense. which step do i reject, and what does that cost me? is there an equivication hiding between source in (2) and source in (3)?
