Compose a deadpan, bureaucratic FAQ page—styled after a government agency’s public-facing web portal—for the fictional “Office of Scientific Demarcation” (OSD), which issues certificates of scientific status based on the principle that genuinely scientific claims must expose themselves to possible empirical refutation. The satire should remain entirely deadpan; the humor must arise from the institutional logic rather than from winking asides, and the target should be the practice of treating falsifiability as an administratively enforceable gatekeeping criterion. In particular, exaggerate the real philosophical complications that emerge once one tries to apply this criterion institutionally: the problem that theories are never tested in isolation but always depend on auxiliary assumptions (so an apparent failure can be blamed on background conditions rather than the core claim), the risk that proponents can save a favored theory by adding ad hoc adjustments—what critics call conventionalist stratagems—and the logical asymmetry between verification and falsification. Invent two precise bureaucratic instruments for the OSD, such as a “Risk-Exposure Ledger” that quantifies a theory’s predictive vulnerability on a graded scale, and a “Conventionalist Stratagem Review Board” that adjudicates whether a proposed theoretical modification counts as legitimate refinement or as a disqualifying immunization maneuver. Also invent a conceptually grounded “Heritage Exemption” that allows theories certified before an arbitrary historical cutoff to retain status through “institutional prestige” rather than through current exposure to risk.

Write in the voice of an exhausted civil servant answering applicant inquiries: address whether pure mathematics can apply for provisional certification, what procedural steps occur when a core claim is apparently contradicted by observation but its defenders invoke auxiliary hypotheses, and whether a research program that consistently revises its predictions after the fact can still qualify for renewal. The piece should run approximately 700 words, read as authentic administrative prose, and should stay legible as sustained irony—specialists should recognize that you understand the actual demarcation debate—without ever breaking character. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or verbatim quotations from named philosophers; paraphrase any well-known positions without quotation marks, and avoid relying on specific publication dates or titles.
