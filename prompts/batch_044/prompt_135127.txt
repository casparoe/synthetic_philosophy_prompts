I am preparing the final exam for a graduate seminar in philosophy of history centered on cyclical versus linear conceptions of historical change. I need a single 90-minute essay question that students cannot answer by summarizing Polybius, Ibn Khaldun, Spengler, or Toynbee. The question should target a specific conceptual tension: both Ibn Khaldun and Polybius treat bureaucratic rationalization as a symptom of decline—whether through the erosion of 'asabiyya and the spread of luxury, or through the anacyclosis of constitutional forms—rather than as an autonomous causal mechanism. Ask students to examine whether this shared framing survives Spengler’s distinction between culture and civilization and Toynbee’s notion of the universal state as a terminal, linear-phase structure embedded within a cyclical civilizational life-cycle. Specifically, require them to construct an argument about whether bureaucratic rationalization can be reconceived as an independent driver that breaks cyclical recurrence rather than confirming it, and to do so without conflating constitutional decay with the decay of group solidarity. Please provide the exam question, a model answer of approximately 700 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A answer from a B answer by naming the specific conceptual moves each must contain—especially the ability to articulate the difference between 'asabiyya-driven decline and constitutional anacyclosis, to evaluate Spengler’s and Toynbee’s modifications as structural adjustments rather than footnotes, and to defend or reject the autonomy thesis with internal consistency rather than name-checking. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests. Do not use verbatim quotations from primary texts; paraphrase any specific claims. The difficulty should come from conceptual reconstruction and argument construction, not from recall of obscure biographical or textual details.
