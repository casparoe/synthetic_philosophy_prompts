I have been working through the Born-rule problem in Everettian quantum mechanics, and I find the standard decision-theoretic derivations—those that ground probabilities in rational betting preferences across branches—unsatisfying because they seem to smuggle subjective credence into an ontology that claims to be purely physical. At the same time, self-locating uncertainty accounts appear to presuppose that an observer can be genuinely ignorant about which deterministic branch she inhabits, which strikes me as conceptually strained once one takes seriously that every branch is equally real and causally isolated after decoherence.

Please argue for the position that the Born rule should be reconstructed not as a measure over worlds or as a subjective betting quotient, but as an objective coherence constraint governing the correlation structure of physical records across branches. Specifically, defend the claim that if two decoherent histories have amplitudes a and b, then the ratio of the inter-branch record fidelity—the degree to which physical encoding mechanisms in one branch can be consistently mapped onto those in the other—must equal |a|²/|b|², and that this scaling is not conventionally stipulated but physically necessitated by the unitary dynamics that preserve total information. You should address the obvious circularity objection, namely that this merely relocates the problem to explaining why record fidelity tracks amplitude squared, and show why that tracking is an unavoidable feature of linear evolution rather than an ad hoc postulate.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then present the reasoning with no preamble and without restating the question.
