Write an essay on the following controversy: why do promises generate genuine obligations rather than merely prudential reasons to keep one's word? Examine the tension between the authority-transfer view, according to which promising delegates part of one's practical authority to the promisee, and the reliance-harm view, according to which breaking a promise wrongs the promisee primarily by frustrating deliberately cultivated legitimate expectations. Reach and defend a definite conclusion about which account succeeds, or whether both must be supplemented.

To keep the analysis from becoming a standard literature review, frame your argument through the concept of normative residue: the persistent moral remainder—something like agent-regret or a standing reason to make amends—that often survives even after full compensation and the dissipation of all further harm. Ask whether this residue is better explained by the authority-transfer model (because the promisee retains an unexercised claim) or by the reliance-harm model (because the original expectation-creating act cannot be fully undone). Then consider a layered alternative: that promises bind through two independent mechanisms, a deontic reason tied to authority or harm and a distinct aretaic reason tied to the promisor's integrity or fidelity, which can come apart in cases of compelled or overridden promises. Weigh the objection that this layered view overcounts the moral cost by making promise-breaking doubly wrong, and the counter-objection that without the aretaic layer the phenomenon of normative residue becomes inexplicable.

Your essay should not end in agnosticism; defend one of the three positions—the authority-transfer view, the reliance-harm view, or the layered hybrid—or a carefully qualified variant. At the very end, state your bottom line in this exact form:

Verdict: [your conclusion summarized in one sentence].
