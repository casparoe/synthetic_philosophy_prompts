For a feature on the resurgence of metaphysical accounts of democracy, please write a dialogue between two equally informed interlocutors. The first defends a Spinozist view: democracy is not primarily a voting mechanism but the political form in which the multitude preserves its collective power, and it requires the cultivation of common notions through shared affective experience rather than procedural rules alone. The second defends a modern liberal-institutionalist view: democracy is essentially a framework for aggregating preferences and resolving conflict under conditions of deep pluralism, and importing Spinoza's ontology of conatus risks imposing a homogeneous affective order that undermines neutrality. Have them respond directly to each other's arguments — for instance, the institutionalist should press the Spinozist on how shared affect can emerge without coercion in heterogeneous societies, and the Spinozist should challenge whether procedural neutrality can ever preserve collective power rather than alienate it. Keep the exchange rigorous and avoid straw-manning either side.
