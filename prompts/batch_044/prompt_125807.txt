I am drafting a dissertation chapter on the metaphysics of mental content and have become uncertain about how to read Dennett’s two-bitser thought experiment in relation to Searle’s distinction between original and derived intentionality. At a recent interdisciplinary workshop, a colleague presented the following gloss of Dennett’s position, and before I build my own argument on it I need to know whether the gloss is philosophically sound.

She claimed that Dennett introduces the two-bitser—a minimal representational system with only two internal state-transitions—precisely to show that derived intentionality does not require a sophisticated user-interpreter, but can arise whenever a design-stance target exhibits rationally predictable behavior. From this, she argued, Dennett concludes that human intentionality is not original in Searle’s sense, but is instead a form of thick derived intentionality: evolutionary selection functions as an interpreter that assigns aboutness to neural state-transitions, much as we assign aboutness to a thermostat. Consequently, she said, Searle’s Chinese Room objection misses its target, because the room’s deficiency is not that syntax fails to constitute semantics, but that a room-sized system lacks the evolutionary scaffolding required for us to adopt the intentional stance toward it profitably; once that scaffolding is present, there is no further intrinsic understanding left unexplained. She added that Dennett therefore collapses Searle’s distinction between first-order user-derived aboutness and second-order biologically-derived aboutness, treating both as interpretive projections onto state-transitions rather than as genuinely different metaphysical categories.

Could you audit this summary claim by claim? Please separate what a careful reader of Dennett and Searle would accept, what requires correction or qualification, and whether her conclusion about the Chinese Room actually follows from the premises she assigns to Dennett. I am especially unsure whether Dennett uses the two-bitser to undermine the original-derived distinction itself, or only to demonstrate that derived intentionality can be minimal; and I want to know whether describing evolutionary selection as an interpreter that assigns aboutness is a faithful reading of Dennett or a subtle misreading that imports Searle’s biological-naturalist vocabulary into a stance that rejects it. Please do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations—I am working without reliable access to a library this week, and I need an assessment of the summary’s philosophical fairness on its own merits.
