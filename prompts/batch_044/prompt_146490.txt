I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I am defending a thesis at an interdisciplinary seminar next week. My thesis is that the familiar distinction between resultant moral luck and constitutive moral luck collapses once we model moral agency deterministically by analogy to deterministic replay debugging in distributed computing: an agent's psychological state at the moment of choice is an accumulated state trace initialized by genetics and environment, and the resulting action unfolds like a replay of a logged execution trace. If we withhold blame for constitutive features—such as a reckless temperament—that the agent did not author, consistency demands we withhold blame for resultant outcomes—such as a reckless harm—that deterministically flow from that same initialized state. I therefore argue that compatibilist attempts to preserve asymmetry by appealing to reasons-responsiveness fail, because reasons-responsiveness is itself just a property of the initialized state vector, not an independent authorship condition.

To help you target the objections, here is my argument sketch: I accept a deterministic framework but reject libertarian free will. I treat the agent's character at time t as a state vector initialized by prior causes. From that vector, action outputs unfold deterministically. Standard views excuse agents for bad constitutive luck—being born with bad traits—while blaming them for bad resultant luck—causing harm through those traits. I claim this is inconsistent: both are deterministic outputs of the same trace. I anticipate the compatibilist reply that we blame agents when their actions reflect their own reasons-responsive mechanisms, but I counter that reasons-responsiveness is just another feature of the initialized vector, not a form of self-creation.
