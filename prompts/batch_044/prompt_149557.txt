I'm writing a feature on whether old Vienna Circle ethics can survive contact with AI hype. Moritz Schlick—shot dead in 1936, which is a brutal footnote—famously argued in his essay on the meaning of life that purpose and acheivement are red herrings; what matters is play, doing things for their own sake. So here's the thing: the effective altruism crowd is now pushing young people to optimize their careers for global welfare, and meanwhile AI is turning white-collar work into pure task-completion with metrics on everything. If Schlick were alive today, would he see this as the ultimate victory of 'work' over 'play,' and therefore condemn the EA career framework? Or could he argue that using AI to free up human time is actually expanding the domain of play? I want a committed take, not a both-sides laundry list—tell me exactly where he lands and what that reveals about whether his play/work distinction was ever meant to handle instutional pressure at this scale.
