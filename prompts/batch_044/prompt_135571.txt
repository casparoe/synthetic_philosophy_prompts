Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises specifically within endurantism when applied to entities defined purely by diachronic relational structure rather than by material continuity. Use as your constructed case a hypothetical institutional form — a "rolling mandate" in which a single regulatory authority is held in strict rotation by entirely distinct, non-overlapping individuals, with no shared material substrate or spatial extension linking one occupant-stage to the next. The issue should center on the endurantist commitment that the mandate is wholly present at each temporal stage, even though its intrinsic relational properties (who holds it, what powers are exercised) change completely across stages. Explain why this creates tension with the endurantist denial of temporal parts: does the mandate persist as one entity despite having contradictory properties at different times, does it fragment into a succession of distinct entities, or does it require an immaterial essence? Contrast this with the perdurantist treatment of the mandate as a four-dimensional fusion of occupant-stages. Keep the example distinct from standard material-replacement puzzles like the ship of Theseus; the difficulty should stem specifically from the absence of any substrate continuity.
