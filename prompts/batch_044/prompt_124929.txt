Please compare the notions of finkish interference and masking interference as they figure in post-Martinian critiques of the simple conditional analysis of dispositional ascriptions. Your comparison should address three dimensions: (i) the temporal profile of the interfering factor relative to the satisfaction of the stimulus condition; (ii) whether the underlying disposition is destroyed, suspended, or left intact; and (iii) the implications of each case for the claim that dispositions can be identified with counterfactual conditionals. Having established these distinctions, consider the following compound scenario. A crystalline compound C is disposed to undergo rapid exothermic reaction upon exposure to atmospheric moisture. However, C is coated with a thermally stable hydrophobic membrane that prevents moisture contact at standard temperatures, and C also contains a dormant internal inhibitor that becomes chemically active only upon direct aqueous contact and immediately suppresses the exothermic pathway. If C is exposed to moisture at standard temperature, is the absence of reaction best explained as a case of finking, masking, both simultaneously, or a structurally different kind of interference altogether? Defend your answer with explicit reference to the temporal and ontological criteria you identified above.
