I’m a PhD student in moral philosophy and I’m helping put together the final for our upper-level philosophy of religion module. I was discussin with my supervisor yesterday about how every exam question on the problem of evil and theodicies just ends up being “summarise Rowe, then summarise Plantinga” — basically something an LLM could vomit out without actually engaging with the argument. I want something that forces students to do actual philosophical work instead of just reciting labels.

Here’s the niche twist: I keep wondering whether skeptical theism’s appeal to cognitive limitation can be quarantined to evil, or whether it inevitably spills over. If we buy the parent analogy — a child can’t see why a parent permits suffering — then shouldn’t we also say we’re too cognitively limited to condemn factory farming or long-term climate damage, since those could be part of some hidden plan we can’t grasp? I don’t know if that’s a real move in the literature or just something we made up over coffee, but I want the exam to centre it.

So please draft one essay question for a 75-mintue exam, with a model answer of roughly 800 words and a marking scheme out of 25. The scheme should separate an A from a B by listing the specific moves — framing the evidential problem properly, testing whether the parental analogy can be restricted, distinguishing epistemic humility from global moral scepticism, and evaluating whether the spillover to interspecies or environmental harm is a legitimate extension or a reductio. No bibliographies or citation lists, please. Write in British English, keep the tone casual but precise, and separate what’s settled in the debate from what’s genuinely contested — don’t present sceptical theism as the standard view everyone accepts, and flag where my “spillover” idea might just be our contrivance rather than established philosophy. Oh, and please correct my typo: I keep writing “theodicy” with an extra “e”.
