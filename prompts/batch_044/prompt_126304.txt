Design a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on whether an ant colony can be a unified conscious subject, using Hofstadter's Ant Fugue scenario—in which Aunt Hillary debates whether the colony is a single mind—as its framing case. The root should pose whether distributed, non-neuronal biological systems can satisfy conditions for consciousness at all. From there, branch through four concrete diagnostic judgments, each phrased as a question about a specific scenario rather than as a theoretical label: (i) if a colony retains a learned maze-route after ninety-five percent of its workers have died and been replaced, does that preserve one persisting mind or only a functional system? (ii) if a physical barrier splits the colony and the two halves pursue incompatible foraging goals for days, is this one mind with conflicting intentions, two minds, or no minds at all? (iii) if pheromone trails systematically misrepresent food location yet the colony eventually corrects the error, does the colony possess representational content with a truth-condition, or only causal tracking? (iv) if the queen is removed and the colony collapses into disordered behavior within weeks, is the queen a central organizing hub analogous to a brain whose loss kills a subject, or merely a reproductive component whose absence damages a mechanism? At each node, require a yes/no/unclear answer about the case, not an endorsement of named theories such as integrated information theory or global workspace theory. The leaves should map onto at least these positions: strong boundary-dependent emergentism (the colony is a mind only when physically unified); boundary-independent emergentism; functionalism that grants colony-level cognition and intentionality but denies phenomenal consciousness; reductionist or eliminativist views that assign mental properties only to individual ants or deny them altogether; and one logically possible but apparently unoccupied combination—such as granting phenomenal consciousness to the colony while denying it any integrated functional organization—briefly diagnosing whether the vacancy stems from incoherence or mere neglect. For each leaf, provide one sentence describing its most awkward commitment, for example having to treat mass worker death as analogous to neuronal turnover without any corresponding phenomenology of loss. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that the four case questions cannot cleanly separate. Do not include citations, named contemporary theorists, bibliographies, or reading lists; construct the taxonomy solely from the logical space of answers to the four scenarios. Write for an audience familiar with philosophy of mind but not presupposing expertise in distributed cognition.
