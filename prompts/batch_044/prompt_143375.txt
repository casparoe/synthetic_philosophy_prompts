Terraforming Mercury — a genuinely dead rock with no plausible biosphere — to create human-habitable zones strikes me as not only permissible but actively valuable, even if the process destroys unique geological formations of scientific interest. A generation ship launched with fully informed, consenting adults, who understand that their descendants will be born into the voyage with no possibility of returning to Earth, seems ethically acceptable, but only if the ship's governance is democratic and revisable; without that safeguard, it feels closer to institutionalized coercion. A private corporation claiming exclusive extraction rights over lunar south-pole water ice and operating under corporate rather than democratic law strikes me as wrong in essentially the same way as a terrestrial colonial land grab. Seeding a barren asteroid with synthetic extremophiles — designed eventually to render it habitable, with no immediate human presence and with explicit safeguards to preserve any pre-existing chemical signatures — strikes me as permissible, even admirable. But rushing that same seeding process on a moon that shows ambiguous spectral evidence of possibly native chemistry, before a complete survey, strikes me as wrong, though I'm uncertain whether the wrongness is of the same kind as the Europa contamination or closer to reckless negligence.

The tension I keep returning to is this: my judgments about terraforming and synthetic seeding appear to hinge on epistemic confidence about the absence of native life, while my judgment about the generation ship hinges on governance structures that have nothing to do with biology. Meanwhile, both the poorly governed ship and the corporate lunar claim involve coercing future people, yet I treat them differently depending on whether the setting is mobile or territorial. Is there a unified principle — perhaps combining non-derivation of consent for future generations, epistemic humility about biospheres, and an anti-colonial condition on sovereignty — that captures all six verdicts without being ad hoc?
