In discussions of cause selection within longtermist and effective-altruist reasoning, the triad “importance, tractability, and neglectedness” is frequently invoked as if naming it settles which problems deserve resources, and it often operates as an argumentative stopping-point: once a cause scores poorly on tractability, it is set aside regardless of importance. I want to understand what precise claims that slogan could actually be advancing.

Lay out the distinct readings as a menu, ordered from the weakest to the strongest. At the weakest end, it may be a purely descriptive psychological claim about how donors actually allocate attention; further along, a heuristic for bounded rationality under deep uncertainty; further still, a normative aggregation rule analogous to multi-attribute decision analysis; and at the strongest end, a substantive moral thesis about where obligations lie given epistemic and institutional constraints. For each reading, indicate whether it is trivial, whether it is defensible, and whether it genuinely supports dismissing a high-importance cause that currently scores low on tractability.

Then address a dynamic tension that the static formulation obscures: tractability is often measured relative to current investment, which means that as a neglected cause receives funding and makes progress, its neglect score falls and—if tractability is partly defined by marginal returns—its tractability score may also shift, sometimes downward. This creates a potential feedback loop in which success undermines the framework’s own justification for initial support. Does any of the readings you have distinguished contain the resources to resolve this loop, or does resolving it require importing an external principle, such as a commitment to option value or to maintaining a diversified portfolio regardless of shifting scores?

Finally, say which reading is most plausibly being invoked when the slogan is used to reject climate-adaptation funding in favor of artificial-intelligence safety, and whether that invocation is internally coherent with the framework’s logic or instead smuggles in an unstated premise about irreversibility or existential risk.
