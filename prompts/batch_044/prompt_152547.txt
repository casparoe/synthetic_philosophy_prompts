I'm taking a graduate seminar on moral psychology, and our discussion of the situationist critique of virtue ethics has stalled in a peculiar way. One faction points to the well-known experimental literature—obedience paradigms, diffusion-of-responsibility scenarios, and emergency-intervention studies—to argue that human behavior varies so sharply with minor situational changes that there is no empirical basis for positing stable, cross-situational character traits of the kind virtue ethics seems to require. The opposing faction grants the data but insists that virtue ethics never promised global behavioral uniformity; it promises only the development of practical wisdom and habituated dispositions oriented toward human flourishing, which might express themselves differently in different contexts. After months, neither side has moved, because each treats the other as either ignoring the science or misunderstanding the philosophy.

What makes the deadlock strange is that I recently encountered a distinction—between treating character traits as predictive statistical constructs, as normative counterfactual profiles (what an agent would do across a range of nearby possible situations), and as teleological narrative structures that organize a life over time—that seems to split both camps internally. Some situationists sound like they are attacking only the predictive-statistical version, while some virtue ethicists defend only the teleological version, yet both sides speak as if they are addressing a single shared concept of character. Is this disagreement substantive but mislocated—perhaps the sides are answering different questions about explanation, evaluation, and metaphysics under one heading? Or is there a hidden empirical question about whether counterfactual robustness, rather than observed consistency, can be cultivated through habituation? Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a refined conceptual taxonomy, an empirical study measuring modal rather than actual stability, or an argument that the normative project of virtue ethics is independent of any empirical psychology whatsoever? Please diagnose the structure of the impasse rather than simply adjudicating the empirical dispute, and lay out what each diagnosis implies about whether the debate can be moved at all.
