For my philosophy of technology assignment, I need to audit an analogy I encountered in a discussion of whether AI should reason as utilitarians or deontologists. The comparison goes like this: a utilitarian AI is like an emergency-room triage nurse who constantly shifts resources toward the patients with the best survival odds, while a deontological AI is like a security guard who refuses to open an emergency exit even when smoke is filling the hallway, because the protocol says never unlock that door without authorization. Proponents use this to argue that deontological AI is dangerously rigid and that we ought to build utilitarian systems, perhaps with some guardrails. Can you provide the explicit correspondence table—what plays the role of the nurse, the guard, the patients, the protocol, the smoke, and the authorization? Then identify the disanalogies that actually matter for the argument: in particular, does the smoke-filled hallway capture the genuine paradox of deontology, in which universal adherence to a rule can produce catastrophic outcomes, or does it quietly treat moral rules as merely instrumental tools? Finally, tell me whether the conclusion—that we should prefer utilitarian reasoning for AI—survives once these breaks are mapped out, or whether the analogy was doing all the persuasive work from the start.
