Give an example of a concrete three-stage choice sequence involving options A, B, and C—drawn from a domain such as clinical treatment selection, consumer purchase, or policy preference—where an agent selects A over B, B over C, and then C over A. The cyclical pattern should not be explained by intransitive tastes, framing effects, menu dependence, or temporal preference change. Rather, it should result from a single, stable utility function operating together with a non-Bayesian informational update that changes which features of the options the agent takes to be relevant to the comparison, without altering the physical outcomes or her underlying valuations. Describe the outcomes at each stage, the specific information received, and why a revealed-preference analyst who assumes that choices reveal preferences over final bundles independently of the comparison context would misdiagnose the agent's utility ranking. The example should show how revealed preference theory can conflate a structural feature of the choice environment with a feature of the agent's utility.
