I've been trying to reverse-engineer the implicit welfare-aggregation framework of a philosopher I read regularly—she writes on population ethics and the asymmetry between suffering and flourishing, but hasn't formalized her axioms. Here are four of her verdicts on stylized trade-offs.

Case A: A policy that inflicts one minute of torture-level pain on a single individual, in exchange for granting ten billion people each an extra year of life at a subjective well-being level barely above neutrality. She rejects it categorically.

Case B: A policy that eliminates a decade of severe, treatment-resistant depression for one person, at the cost of removing occasional moments of profound aesthetic joy from ten billion people. She endorses it without hesitation.

Case C: A policy that abolishes all mild, transient headaches worldwide—affecting billions—by requiring one healthy volunteer to experience a single, brief migraine. She rejects it.

Case D: A tax that lowers the average subjective well-being of the wealthy by 0.01 percent, funding a program that reduces the intensity of suffering for a small population in extreme agony by 10 percent. She endorses it strongly.

Now consider Case E: A technology makes it possible to bring into existence 10^30 minds, each with a uniform, moderate positive valence and zero suffering, but only on the condition that one existing human experiences a single, isolated episode of intense yet brief grief. She hasn't addressed this, and I want to predict her response.

What should we expect? First, lay out at least two distinct principles—perhaps a strict lexical negative-utilitarian rule, a threshold-based incommensurability view that permits cardinal comparison only below a suffering threshold, or an asymmetrically weighted cardinal model with extreme diminishing returns for positive valence—that are jointly consistent with Cases A through D. For each principle, state clearly whether it approves or rejects Case E. Then offer your best-guess prediction of her actual verdict, with explicit reasoning about which principle her earlier pattern best supports and where the ambiguity remains. Finally, propose a hypothetical Case F that would sharply distinguish the two leading principles you have identified.
