I serve on my university's Committee for Institutional Safety and Legitimate Coercion, which the administration charged with reviewing whether the campus police department should be restructured, defunded, or maintained in its current form. Our chair requires a formal decision memo for the trustees' meeting in ten days: it must recommend one concrete course of action—complete abolition with transition to an unarmed crisis-response unit, retention with strict democratic-oversight conditions, or a phased restructuring—together with an implementation timeline, and it must treat seriously the strongest argument on the losing side, namely that universities inherit the Weberian monopoly on legitimate violence and that dissolving it creates a dangerous security vacuum. I am writing as a doctoral student in political philosophy and ethics, and I want the document to treat the issue as a problem of conceptual legitimacy rather than pure administration.

To that end, the memo should develop what I call a jurisdictional fink: the campus police hold formal powers of arrest, armed intervention, and detention, yet the university is not a sovereign body, democratic accountability mechanisms are weak, and the norms of academic governance structurally mask the coercive nature of these powers—so that the authority is present but its legitimating conditions are systematically suppressed, analogous to a masked disposition whose triggering conditions are never met. The memo should ask whether a non-sovereign institution can legitimately possess such a monopoly at all, or whether applying the Weberian framework here is not merely imprudent but conceptually misapplied, while also weighing the counter-claim that without centralized coercive capacity the campus would suffer a fragmentation of security responsibility that undermines equity and rapid crisis response.

Please draft the memo in formal institutional prose, with an explicit recommendation supported by reasoning that explains the jurisdictional-fink analysis without appealing to obscure secondary literature or providing citations. Include specific transition phases—for example, a six-month pilot for the unarmed unit if recommending defunding, or quarterly oversight audits if recommending retention—an honest engagement with the security-vacuum objection, and an actionable bottom line suitable for a trustee vote. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or page references; treat the conceptual framework as self-contained within the document.
