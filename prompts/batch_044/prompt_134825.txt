In debates over psychiatric nosology, defenders of categorical models sometimes analogize diagnosis to a census: the clinician merely counts pre-existing conditions, and counting left-handed people does not make them left-handed, so looping effects are negligible. They use this to argue that Hacking's concern about interactive kinds applies at best to marginal cases, not to core DSM categories. Audit the analogy: provide a correspondence table (census-taker, form, population, feedback loop), show exactly where the mapping fails—especially regarding whether psychiatric categories determine access to treatment, legal status, and self-narrative in ways census categories do not—and examine whether the analogy quietly presupposes that mental disorders are indifferent kinds. Consider specifically the historical loop involving the proliferation of reported alters after the popularization of dissociative identity disorder criteria. Does the census analogy's conclusion—that looping is structurally insignificant for nosology—survive once these disanalogies are made explicit? If not, suggest a better analogy that captures the interactive dynamics without reducing psychiatric kinds to pure social invention.
