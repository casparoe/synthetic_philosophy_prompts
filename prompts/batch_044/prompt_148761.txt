You are a philosopher of science with expertise in the evaluation of long-range predictive theories and the logic of falsification. Before you begin, take a few minutes to brainstorm the distinct failure-modes a century-scale prediction can suffer—temporal displacement, auxiliary-condition collapse, mechanism-substitution, and structural transformation of the domain itself—so that you do not conflate them in what follows.

I want you to construct the full desiderata list for any adequate philosophical account of whether a theory can be “right but early,” using Malthus’s population principle as the central, non-negotiable case study. Do not summarize Malthus’s biography or his essay’s reception; instead, specify what the framework must deliver. For each desideratum, state: (i) the precise phenomenon or case it must accommodate—such as the demographic transition, the Green Revolution’s interruption of scarcity dynamics, or endogenous technological response to pressure; (ii) the reason it counts as a genuine constraint on adequacy rather than a methodological prejudice of one camp; and (iii) whether it is negotiable or non-negotiable, with particular attention to whether “mechanistic fidelity” and “predictive vindication at long time-scales” can be jointly satisfied. Your list should explicitly flag any pair of requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable given standard underdetermination arguments about long-run evidence, and you should identify which single desideratum does the most damage to the popular “Malthus was simply falsified and therefore wrong” narrative.

Finally, briefly score three contender frameworks against your list—not with a narrative history, but with direct marks—namely a strict Popperian falsificationist view, a Lakatosian research-programme defense, and a structural-realist rescue. For each, indicate which desiderata it violates and which it preserves, and conclude by stating whether the disagreement over Malthus is really about empirical results, about what counts as an explanation, or about hidden normative commitments regarding human agency and technological progress. Keep the weight on the criteria and their conflicts; the scoring is only there to test the list.
