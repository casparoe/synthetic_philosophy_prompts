Mark which requirements are non-negotiable—those without which the criterion collapses either into bare functional equivalence or into unhelpful physical token-identity—and which are negotiable commitments of particular methodological camps, such as substrate independence, determinism of the mechanism-to-implementation mapping, or invariance under scale transformation. Then indicate whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, especially whether accommodating both fine-grained intervention-sensitive specificity and broad cross-substrate generalization forces an unavoidable trade-off. Conclude with a brief conceptual assessment of how informal practices in mechanistic interpretability and causal modeling currently stand against this list, but do not supply bibliographies, named citations, or reading lists; the evaluation should rest on conceptual adequacy alone.
