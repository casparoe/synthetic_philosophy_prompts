I’ve been volunteering at a community repair cafe — we fix broken electronics and furniture for free. About five years ago, the founder decided we should organize tools by color-coded tape rather than by function, mostly because he liked the look of the rainbow shelve. Now every training manual, every volunteer shift, even the way we talk about repairs (“is this a red-tool job or a blue-tool job?”) is built around those colors. Last month someone proposed switching to a functional layout and half the members freaked out, saying our system is “just how the cafe works” and that any group our size would have ended up with some organizing scheme anyway. But they also admit the colors are totally arbitrary. I keep seeing this pattern elsewhere: an early, pretty random choice becomes the invisible grammar for a whole organization, and then people defend it as both inevitable and essential. Is there an established word or concept for this — specifically the part where an arbitrary early rule gets baked into the language and then people argue about whether it was always going to happen? If not, can you name it and draw the line between it and cases where everyone sticks with something just because switching is too expensive? Keep it under 200 words, one paragraph please.
