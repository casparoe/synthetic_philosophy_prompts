Two colleagues, Jordan and Riley, debated Molinism over coffee. (1) Jordan argued that middle knowledge is incoherent because counterfactuals of creaturely freedom (CCFs) must be grounded in something prior to creation, yet they cannot rest on actual choices (which might never occur) or on God’s will (since middle knowledge is pre-volitional), leaving them ungrounded. (2) Riley replied that this assumes atomistic grounding; on a holistic view, CCFs are grounded in the global modal structure—the consistency conditions across possible worlds—so no single actual choice bears the weight. (3) Jordan countered that this only relocates the problem: if that modal architecture is necessary and not authored by God, divine choice is constrained by a “structural determinism” in which only worlds fitting the pre-given fabric are available, reviving the freedom problem globally. (4) Riley responded that logical constraint is not causal constraint; God is no more “limited” by necessary modal truths than by arithmetic, and calling this determinism smuggles in an implausible voluntarist demand that God should be able to violate consistency conditions.
