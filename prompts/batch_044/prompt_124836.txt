I am a journalist who covers science and philosophy, and I am drafting a proposal for a six-month independent research fellowship on cross-cultural variation in philosophical intuitions. My half-formed idea is that the well-known East-West divergences in judgments about moral responsibility, free will, and causal attribution may be artifacts of how experimental philosophers frame their vignettes rather than evidence of deep conceptual differences. Existing studies typically present scenarios as stripped-down propositional cases—an agent performs an action, an outcome occurs, and subjects rate responsibility or causation in isolation. These frames implicitly privilege an individualist, agent-centered ontology that may align with Western analytical habits but misrepresent moral cognition in contexts where relational obligations, institutional roles, and temporal reciprocity are primary. I suspect that if we redesign vignettes so that causal and moral relations are embedded in narrative structures—specifying kinship networks, sequences of mutual obligation, or social positions—some cross-cultural divergences will diminish or reverse, while others will sharpen in ways that reveal genuinely different conceptual architectures.

Please write a formal two-page research proposal containing the following: a precise, determinately answerable statement of the research question; an explanation of why current experimental-philosophy methods leave this framing gap open, without citing specific papers or authors; the methodological approach, including how you would construct narrative-embedded versus propositional control vignettes, what populations or comparative dimensions you would use (without reducing culture to a simple binary), and how you would analyze both quantitative response patterns and qualitative interpretive structure; what a successful result would look like and what specific finding would count as project failure; and an honest assessment of realistic scope for six months, identifying which components are most likely to expand beyond the time available and how you would narrow them. The proposal should be polished, structured for an interdisciplinary review panel comfortable with philosophy but not necessarily with experimental methods, and should contain no bibliography or reading list, as I will supply references separately. Do not ask for web searches or external citations within the proposal itself.
