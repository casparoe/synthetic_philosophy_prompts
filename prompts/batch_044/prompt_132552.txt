I'm a clinical ethics consultant at a big NHS trust, and we're seeing more case conferences where a patient or family rejects our recommendation not because they dispute one particular fact, but because the whole frame they're reasoning inside seems to treat something as immovable — like a hinge that doesn't shift when you push on the other bits. I was chatting with my flatmate about this over tea after reading Wittgenstein's On Certainty for a part-time philosophy programme, and I got stuck on his idea of hinge propositions: the ones that aren't justified by evidence but hold the rest of the enquiry together, like the hinges of a door that don't move themselves. I'd like you to turn that idea into a reusable decision procedue my team can actually run in about fifteen minutes, with clear steps in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where we stop and say this is a framework boundary rather than a claim to challenge, and where we have to flag that judgement can't be eliminated — particualrly when a belief looks like a hinge to the patient but looks like a factual error to us.

The deliverable should be a flowchart in words: ask this first, then that, with explicit stopping points. It needs to handle high-stakes cases where nurses and lawyers are signing off and we don't have philosophers in the room. Apply it to three worked exmaples: a Jehovah's Witness refusing blood because they hold that transfusion violates a sacred command about the soul; a cancer patient refusing chemo because they claim all synthetic chemicals corrupt the body's natural behaviour; and a family insisting on continued ventilation for a brain-dead relative because they say death only occurs when consciousness permanently departs, which for them hasn't happened yet — this last one should be the case where the procedure gives us an answer we'll probably find uncomfortable. Also tell me clearly what the procedure cannot settle, perhaps where a hinge is partly empirical, and include one further test that would distinguish a deep hinge from a shallow one. Don't give me a bibliography or citation list; I just need the steps, the cases, and an honest account of where it will misfire.
