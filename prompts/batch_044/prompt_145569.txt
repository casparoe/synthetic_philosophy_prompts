Consider the following argument about predictive stances. Dennett’s framework distinguishes three strategies: the physical stance, which appeals to physical laws; the design stance, which explains behavior through functional specifications—whether engineered or evolved; and the intentional stance, which attributes beliefs and desires to predict action. These are usually treated as conceptually separable, with the intentional stance available as an optional higher-level heuristic that does not alter lower-level descriptions. Now consider a closed-loop neuroadaptive prosthetic that continuously rewrites its own stimulation parameters in direct response to the patient’s real-time emotional feedback. From the design stance, one predicts its behavior by reference to the original feedback architecture specified by its engineers. From the intentional stance, one might predict it by attributing to the coupled system—prosthetic plus neural tissue—a unified goal of maintaining the patient’s emotional homeostasis. The problem is that the system’s self-modification means its design-stance specification is constantly being overwritten by patterns best explained via intentional attribution. Thus, for such coupled bio-digital systems, the design stance cannot provide a stable baseline from which one independently ascends to the intentional stance; the two become dynamically entangled.

Please comment on this line of reasoning. Specifically: (1) Does this entanglement genuinely challenge the claim that the intentional stance can be adopted or abandoned without altering lower-level descriptions, or does it merely show that stance selection in complex artifacts requires richer contextual information? (2) Is there a serious risk of anthropomorphizing engineering specifications, or does the feedback loop justify treating the coupled system as a single agent? (3) Would accommodating self-modifying artifacts require conceptual adjustments to the framework—perhaps a hybrid stance—or can the existing taxonomy absorb these cases without revision?
