heres a grading task for you. i have an exam question: explain David Lewis's (the modal realist) objection to penal substitution, especially his claim about punishing the innocent, and contrast that with Anselm's satisfaction view. does Lewis think its literally incoherent or just unfair?

student answer:
David Lewis, known for possible worlds, says penal substitution is Christ taking the punishment sinners deserve. His main point is that justice requires punishing only the guilty, so substituting an innocent person is not just unfair but conceptually incoherent — like being a married bachelor. He distinguishes this from Anselm's satisfaction theory, which he reads as Christ repaying a debt of honor through infinite merit; Lewis thinks satisfaction avoids incoherence since its repayment not punishment, but he still rejects it because it makes God look like a feudal lord demanding honor payments. He also considers the reply that Christ voluntarily accepts the punishment, and Lewis agrees this fixes the injustice, so penal substitution is defensible if you accept voluntary imputation. In the end he says substitution and satisfaction are basically the same idea with different labels, and that the doctrine is implausible but not incoherent.
