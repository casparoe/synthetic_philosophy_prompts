Mira insists that the principle is a classic crane: it begins with statistical mechanics and constructs hierarchical predictive processing upward, accounting for perception and action without invoking any mysterious downward-causal forces. Leo responds that its very breadth makes it behave like a skyhook in practice; because it is applied indiscriminately across biological and social scales, it risks becoming unfalsifiable and therefore operates like a teleological explanation dressed in mathematics. Mira concedes the empirical worry but distinguishes it sharply from metaphysics: a crane can be empirically overextended without becoming a skyhook, since the latter requires an inexplicable suspension of bottom-up construction, not merely bad generalization. Leo then presses that Dennett’s concept of greedy reductionism should be applied not only downward—stripping away higher-level patterns—but also upward, where a single low-level formalism is inflated to swallow genuinely autonomous higher-level descriptions, thereby committing the same explanatory overreach from the opposite direction.
