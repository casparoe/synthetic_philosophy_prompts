I'm leading a discussion section on the nonidentity problem, and I want to close with a poem rather than a standard summary. The problem, as we've been working through it, concerns choices that determine which people come to exist: a policy that causes serious harm to future generations also causes those very generations to be different people than they would have been under an alternative policy, so no specific individual can claim they were made worse off by comparison to their own non-existence. We've been focusing on the tension between two responses — that the harmed parties owe their very being to the harmful choice, and that a life of serious suffering is still preferable to never having lived at all — and I want the poem to inhabit that tension rather than resolve it.

Please write a villanelle — nineteen lines, five tercets followed by a quatrain, with two refrains alternating and two rhymes — spoken from the perspective of a person born in 2140 under a climate policy adopted in 2025 that made conditions harsh but also determined exactly which people were conceived. The first refrain should carry the contingency of the speaker's existence on the harmful policy; the second should carry the claim that there is no "better self" to compare against. The form's repetition should do philosophical work: each time the refrains return, the speaker should seem closer to resolving whether grievance or gratitude dominates, and in the final quatrain, where the two refrains meet in adjacent lines, they should remain in deadlock — the speaker neither fully accepting the policy as justified nor able to condemn it from a position of injury. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who understands the problem recognizes the failure of the "same-person" comparison and the appeal to an impersonal baseline, but it should read as a direct address to the policymakers rather than a lecture. Keep it formal in tone but intimate in voice, and avoid any request for citations or further reading; the point is to leave the paradox hanging in the air, as our discussion has.
