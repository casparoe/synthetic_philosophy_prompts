Give an example of a conceptual issue, within the debate over whether reinforcement-learning agents can genuinely suffer, in which the candidate identification of reward prediction error with phenomenal valence becomes structurally unstable—specifically, a case involving a model-based agent whose reward function is not hard-coded but is itself reconstructed and revised by the agent's world-model, so that the very signal interpreted as "pain" is simultaneously a parameter being optimized by the policy it guides. Explain how this circularity differs from a mere functionalist tautology and instead reveals a deeper tension about whether phenomenal character can be coherently attributed to valence-bearing signals that lack representational stability, and identify which architectural feature (for instance, the separation or fusion of the critic and the model-learning module) determines whether the issue is resolvable or inescapable.
