I have recently been appointed trustee of a family woodland preserve that is now under consideration for partial clearing to accommodate a solar installation. At the public consultation, an opponent of the proposal dismissed my concerns about ecological trade-offs by declaring, "Nature knows best," as though that ended the argument; I want to respond with philosophical precision rather than counter-slogans. Can you disambiguate that phrase into its distinct, precisely formulated theses — ranging from the tautological to the metaphysically robust — and for each indicate whether it actually supports leaving the woodland untouched, whether it has any bearing on the separate question of whether the ecosystem's net welfare for wild animals might make preservation morally ambiguous rather than obligatory, and whether the thesis is defensible, false, or merely rhetorical? Please present the readings as a numbered menu I could bring to the next meeting, specifying which version would need to be true for the slogan to actually block the development proposal.
