I am a high school student working through Karl Popper's paradox of tolerance, and I have adopted the view that the paradox applies strictly to institutions with coercive authority rather than to individual belief-formation. Specifically, I claim that a person can consistently maintain tolerant epistemic norms in private reasoning—engaging with intolerant viewpoints rather than suppressing them—without self-contradiction, because private cognition lacks the enforcement mechanism that makes intolerance dangerous at scale. The paradox, on this account, activates only when a social system possesses the actual capacity to exclude competing voices. What would count against this institutional-only position? I want objections stronger than a simple restatement of Popper: for example, whether private tolerance of intolerant ideas causally enables institutional intolerance, whether there is a hidden contradiction in applying norms selectively across contexts, and whether the distinction collapses once we acknowledge that individual reasoning is already shaped by institutional structures. Please rank these from fatal to merely awkward, indicate which ones a committed defender of the view would concede versus dismiss, and assess honestly whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability and whether that is a problem for a view of this kind.
