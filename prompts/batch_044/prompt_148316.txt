My advisor's rulings on what counts as corruption:
- Mayor gives a city contract to his cousin's firm: corruption, private loyalty overriding public duty.
- Diplomat accepts expensive hospitality from host country before negotiations: not corruption, it's customary reciprocity and the gift is public.
- Voter gives a small gift to a poll worker in a culture where that's standard thanks: corruption, because it inserts a private obligation into a public procedure.
- Corporation donates to a politician's foundation that funds research aligned with the firm's interests, with no explicit quid pro quo: corruption, structural dependency is sufficient.
- Elected official votes for a bill her spouse lobbied for, but she discloses it and it matches her long-held policy view: not corruption, no office-derived private gain.
