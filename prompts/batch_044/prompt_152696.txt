I'm taking a graduate seminar on the philosophy of physics, and for three weeks we've been stuck on whether special relativity genuinely forces a block-universe ontology. The Rietdijk–Putnam line is familiar enough: observers in relative motion assign different temporal locations to the same distant event—one placing it in her present, another in her past or future—so if all observers are correct, past, present, and future events must all exist tenselessly within a four-dimensional manifold. Presentist responses are equally well worn: some deny that distant simultaneity carries ontological weight, others invoke a privileged foliation concealed beneath Lorentz covariance, and others charge the argument with conflating coordinate dependence with ontological dependence. Yet the literature feels circular; eternalists treat presentism as an empirically obsolete curiosity, while presentists treat eternalism as an illegitimate extrapolation from geometry to metaphysics.

My working suspicion is that the stall is not about physics but about two incompatible registers of ontological commitment that have never been properly separated. One side treats the spacetime manifold as delivering direct ontology—if an event lies on the manifold, it exists tenselessly—while the other treats the manifold as a representational structure whose coordinate assignments do not, by themselves, determine which events possess the property of presentness in a non-derivative way. But I am unsure whether this is a verbal disagreement about what we mean by 'real,' a substantive divergence about whether physics can dictate metaphysics without interpretive mediation, or a case of two distinct questions—one about the structure of spacetime, one about the nature of temporal becoming—being forced under the single heading 'the problem of time in relativity.' There may also be a hidden formal question doing the work: whether presentism can be reconciled with Lorentz covariance only by adding structure that violates parsimony, or whether the Andromeda-paradox intuition relies on an implicit transitivity assumption about simultaneity that presentists are entitled to reject on structural rather than empirical grounds.

Can you diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is—verbal, substantive, methodological, or bedrock? And given your diagnosis, what would genuinely count as progress: a formal result showing that presentism requires hidden premises, a conceptual distinction between coordinate variance and ontological variance that dissolves the paradox, or an honest admission that the dispute turns on prior intuitions about becoming that neither side can argue for?
