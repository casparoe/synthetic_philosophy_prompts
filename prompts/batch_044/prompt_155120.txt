Write a joke that turns on the internal logic of Gongsun Long's White Horse Dialogue (Bai Ma Lun) and the School of Names (Mingjia), specifically the argument that "a white horse is not a horse" (bai ma fei ma). Treat the claim not as mere wordplay but as a theory of predicate exclusion: the term "horse" names a reality defined solely by the form-attribute (xing) of horse-ness, whereas "white horse" denotes a compound reality in which the color-attribute (se, "white") is conjoined with that form, so the single-attribute predicate "horse" fails to match the compound description. The joke should make this logical mechanism—names failing to ascribe to realities that contain extra conjoined attributes—the actual source of the humor, for instance by extending it to a modern taxonomic, bureaucratic, or database-classification scenario where compound descriptors systematically break single-label verification. It should be coherent enough that someone familiar with the dialogue recognizes the specific predicate-matching failure being lampooned, not just the surface slogan.
