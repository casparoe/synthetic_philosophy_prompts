hey so ive been trying to understand deflationary theories of truth — the idea that saying "snow is white is true" is literally just saying snow is white, with no hidden "truth property" behind it. i turned it into an argument and now it feels like it proves way too much.

(1) If deflationism is right, then "P is true" adds zero content beyond P itself.
(2) So "truth" isnt a real property like redness; its just a grammatical loop for repeating or endorsing a claim.
(3) But we clearly use "true" to do real work: courts say the truth is he was elsewhere, scientists call a model true but incomplete, and people apologize for saying false things.
(4) If (2) holds, all that talk is empty repetition. There is no meaningful difference between arguing over P and arguing over whether P is true.
(5) Yet people can reject "P is true" without rejecting P — for instance, someone might accept every climate-science fact but reject "its true" because they think the word smuggles in authority they dont grant.
(6) So deflationism predicts that rejecting "P is true" always means rejecting P, which plainly doesnt happen.
(7) Therefore deflationism is false and truth must be some thick, substantive property.

I want to keep (1) because "true" really does feel like just looping back. But (7) feels like overkill. Which step should I drop, and whats the cost? Is there an equivication hiding between "adds no content" and "does no work"?
