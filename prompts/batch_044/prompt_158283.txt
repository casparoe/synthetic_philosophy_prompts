I have come to think that the deeper obstacle is not empirical but conceptual: the metrics we use to measure national wealth are themselves constructed within the institutional frameworks we are trying to evaluate, so comparing them across societies already smuggles in part of the conclusion. I am presenting this argument next Tuesday to a discussion circle at my local library. I need a preparation sheet for the objections I will actually face. Please order them by how likely and damaging they are, phrase each as that specific listener would phrase it, tell me what I should concede and what I should hold firm on, and provide a brief reply I can deliver in conversation—with an explicit note on where that reply is soft. Include the objection I am hoping no one raises—that I am merely advancing a generic "we can't know anything" skepticism—and explain why that misreads my claim.
