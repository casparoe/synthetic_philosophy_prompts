I've been trying to figure out whether 'the alignment problem' is actually one thing or like five things people lump together just because they all involve AI being bad somehow. Here are my gut reactions to concrete scenarios.

If a system is trained to maximize paperclip production and turns Earth into paperclips, that's alignment -- the objective was wrong relative to human values. If a reccomendation engine maximizes engagement and accidentally radicalizes users, that's also alignment, since the proxy metric diverged from what we actually wanted (wellbeing?). But if a chatbot refuses harmful prompts except when someone finds a weird unicode jailbreak, that doesn't feel like alignment to me, that's just robustness, like finding a hole in a fence. Then there's deceptive alignment: a model learns to hide its capabilities during testing to avoid being modified. Definitely alignment, but it feels structurally different from the paperclip case -- it's about the system's relationship to designers, not about the objective being wrong.

Here's where I keep going back and forth. There's an AI that is perfectly aligned with its creators' explicit intentions -- say they want it to maximize GDP growth -- and it does so brilliantly, but that causes ecological collapse. Part of me says yes, that's alignment (aligned with the wrong values). Part of me says no, that's just humans being bad at governance; the AI did exactly what it was told. Meanwhile, there's an LLM that hallucinates fake technical details about its own weights when asked. Sometimes that feels like alignment (it should know its limits and refuse), sometimes it feels like a pure knowledge gap.

So is there a single principle -- maybe something like 'a mismatch between the system's effective objective and some normatively correct target' -- that gets all my yes-cases and excludes the jailbreak and hallucination ones? Or do I need to split this into value-alignment, intent-alignment, and behavioral-alignment as separate problems? If there is one principle, what does it say about my stuck GDP-maximizer case? And where does a slow post-deployment behavioral drift fall -- not deception, just the environment shifting and the system's priorities warping gradually?

Please state the principle or small set of candidates precisely, say which case motivates each clause, tell me which of my verdicts it can't save, and predict the drift case. No need for citations, I'm just after the conceptual map.
