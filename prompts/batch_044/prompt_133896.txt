ok so i just started interning at this cross-border planning office that works between a german land and a couple swiss cantons, and there's this funding rule that's been driving me nuts. there's two alpine valleys right next to eachother—same population, same tax base, same flood risk from the river, basically indescernible on every practical metric you could measure. but one valley's canton signed an inter-cantonal water treaty back in the 90s, and the other canton never did. so for the first valley, the federal/confederal authority says "this is local, you handle it" and won't fund anything. for the second valley, they say "no local framework exists, so we step in" and pour in federal money. the outcomes are totally different even though the regions themselves are identical in every substantive way.

i keep thinking about that philosophy problem with the two black spheres in an otherwise empty universe—if they're truly identical in every intrinsic property, do they even count as two things? it feels like this funding system is doing something similar with regions: it treats the valley that signed the treaty and the valley that didn't as fundamentally different "units of competence," not because of anything about their actual capacity, but purely because of their relational/legal status. but then i also heard my boss say this is exactly what subsidiarity is supposed to do—decisions at the lowest level that actually has the legal framework in place.

so here's what i'm wondering: what philosophical commitments about identity, competence, or the "right level" of government does this practice actually presuppose? does it require thinking that a region's political identity includes its treaty relationships (like the spheres' identity includes their spatial relation), or can a purely functional view—"whoever can actually solve the problem"—explain the split? and if we replaced the current rule with one that looked only at intrinsic capacity and ignored these legal memberships, what would have to change in how federalism or subsidiarity works? is the practice even consistent with itself, or does it quietly switch between a relational view of political units and a substantive view depending on which outcome it wants?
