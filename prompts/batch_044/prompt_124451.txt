I serve on the Joint Ethics and Program Funding Committee of the National Palliative Care Consortium, and we must reach a decision by our March meeting on whether to approve a three-year pilot grant for an “Absurdist Palliative Counseling” (APC) protocol. The protocol, developed by clinical ethicists and philosophers of value, trains counselors to conduct an “absurdist meaning audit” with terminal patients: instead of facilitating the reconstruction of personal, narrative, or transcendent meaning—as in standard logotherapy-informed or narrative-medicine approaches—counselors explicitly validate the absence of cosmic or objective meaning, guide patients through structured exercises in what the developers call “lucid revolt” (the sustained affirmation of subjective projects maintained with full awareness of their ultimate futility), and contractually prohibit counselors from introducing religious, objective-list, or other transcendent meaning frameworks, even when a patient explicitly requests them. Proponents contend that for patients already alienated from traditional meaning-making, forcing them into “meaning reconstruction” is itself a form of existential coercion, and that a publicly funded healthcare system should offer a philosophically serious option that takes metaphysical absurdity at face value rather than treating it as a symptom to be resolved. Opponents contend that institutionalizing a model built on the denial of objective meaning amounts to state endorsement of a specific metaphysical position; that the contractual prohibition on responding to requests for transcendent frameworks violates both patient autonomy and cultural pluralism; and that vulnerable dying patients may interpret clinical validation of futility as endorsement of despair rather than as liberation. The philosophical stakes turn on whether contemporary accounts of meaning in life—particularly objective-list theories, subjective-fulfillment accounts, and Camus-style absurdism—can be translated into clinical practice without collapsing into either coercion or nihilism. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action—full approval, rejection, or approval subject to binding conditions—together with an explicit implementation timeline, and it should engage seriously the strongest consideration on the side you reject, specifically addressing whether a public healthcare system can legitimately fund a counseling model predicated on the denial of objective meaning without violating its duties of philosophical neutrality and patient-centered autonomy.
