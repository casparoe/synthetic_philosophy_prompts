Design a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on insect sentience specifically in relation to the scale argument—the claim that a very low per-capita probability of phenomenal consciousness, multiplied by vast insect populations, might generate enormous aggregate moral weight. Build the tree from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of named authors, and target an audience of applied-ethics readers who have not already adopted a philosophical label.

Use exactly three binary diagnostic splits, each phrased as a concrete case or commitment that a reader can answer without knowing which position it corresponds to.

Split 1 (Evidence): Present the case of an insect that avoids noxious heat through localized reflex arcs with no central integration or global workspace. Ask whether this architecture provides (a) weak but genuine evidence leaving a small non-zero probability of sentience, or (b) effectively no relevant evidence, leaving that probability near zero.

Split 2 (Aggregation): Ask, assuming a small independent probability p for each insect and a population numbering in the billions or trillions, whether moral weight should be calculated by (a) linear multiplication of p by population size (expected-value style), or (b) a threshold or confidence-weighted approach in which low per-capita confidence blocks straightforward population-level scaling.

Split 3 (Practical Weight): Ask whether, setting aside practical feasibility, the protection of insects should be treated as a moral priority comparable in urgency to the protection of mammals—yes or no, regardless of the reasoning path that led there.

The tree should contain all eight logical combinations of answers, but you must place exactly seven named leaves in that space, leaving one combination unoccupied. For each occupied leaf, provide a one-sentence position label and a brief description (for example, “Scale-accepting sentientist: small p, linear aggregation, obligations comparable to mammals”). The unoccupied cell should be the path that combines near-zero individual probability, linear aggregation, and obligations comparable to mammals; diagnose whether that gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also fill the cell that combines near-zero probability, threshold-blocked aggregation, and comparable obligations—label it as a non-sentience-based view (for instance, ecological or structural protection) so that only one cell remains empty.
