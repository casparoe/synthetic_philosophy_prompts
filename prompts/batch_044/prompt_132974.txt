Drawing on Douglas Hofstadter's satire Person Paper on Purity in Language, she argues that the piece exposes not merely a sexist default in ordinary language but an asymmetrical ontological dependency: masculine terms function as the unmarked conceptual ground, while feminine terms appear as derivative modifications, so that swapping the gendered terms produces not just social absurdity but a kind of semantic collapse that reveals the underlying structure. I want the discussion to move well beyond a restatement of Hofstadter's original argument.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions that press her position directly? I would like at least one question on whether reversibility failure tracks grammatical gender specifically or extends to other asymmetrical linguistic pairs; one on how her account distinguishes itself from standard linguistic claims about markedness and directional entailment; and one on whether the satire's humour depends on the reader initially failing to notice the reversal, which might suggest the dependency is rhetorically constructed rather than conceptually deep. For each of these three, please include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—anticipating, for instance, an appeal to cross-linguistic evidence for the first, an appeal to directional entailment for the second, and a distinction between discovery and display for the third. The remaining questions should continue to probe the implications of her view for non-linguistic domains and for the possibility of conceptual reform. Please present the questions in a sequence that builds progressively, and write the entire response in British English, avoiding any request for citations, bibliographies, or further reading lists.
