I'm constructing a diagnostic taxonomy for a philosophy-of-science seminar on Imre Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes. The instrument should classify actual philosophical commitments, not self-identified labels.

Design a decision tree with the following root question: "When a mature research programme encounters a persistent empirical anomaly, is its hard core ever rationally revisable?"

From there, construct three successive branching dimensions, each expressed as a concrete claim that an interlocutor could accept or reject without knowing which named position it yields:

1. The constitution of the hard core: is it best understood as a cluster of metaphysical commitments, a set of methodological rules, or a theoretical network with no special protected status?
2. The normative criterion for auxiliary adjustments: does rationality require that changes to the protective belt generate novel predictions (a progressive problem-shift), or can ad hoc modifications remain rational if they preserve overall explanatory coherence?
3. The meta-methodological status of evaluation: is the distinction between progressive and degenerating programmes an empirical-historical judgment that can be shown false by the record of science, or is it a normative reconstruction applied retrospectively by philosophers?

The tree should yield between six and eight leaves. For each leaf, provide: (a) the name of an actual position if the combination is defended in the literature (e.g., orthodox Lakatosianism, a Popperian falsificationist variant, or an unnamed hybrid); (b) a single sentence stating its most awkward commitment; and (c) for any logically available combination that remains unoccupied, a brief diagnosis—whether it is incoherent, unmotivated, or merely unexplored.

Every branch must split genuinely: do not include nodes that all positions answer identically. Flag explicitly any pair of leaves that this tree cannot cleanly separate, explaining why the distinction collapses at that node. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations; the taxonomy should be answerable from conceptual analysis alone.
