Write the desiderata an adequate theory of the meta-problem of consciousness must satisfy. Take the meta-problem to be the task of explaining why phenomenal consciousness appears to present a special explanatory gap even to thinkers who reject substance dualism and endorse physicalism, rather than merely cataloguing the gap itself. Specify the phenomena the theory must account for — including the persistence of hard-problem intuitions among trained physicalists, the asymmetry between our readiness to functionalise other mental states and our resistance to functionalising phenomenal experience, and the apparent cross-cultural stability of the “what it is like” intuition — with the motivating case behind each. List the distinctions the theory must preserve, especially between explaining the cognitive origin of phenomenal concepts and explaining why those concepts seem structurally irreducible, and between treating the gap as a cognitive illusion and treating it as tracking a real feature of experiential architecture. State the hard constraints it must respect — consistency with the absence of detectable non-physical causal interaction, and with the fact that the intuition survives explicit philosophical training — and identify which of these are non-negotiable versus camp-specific preferences. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable, and say which single requirement does the most to rule out existing approaches. Separate what is settled among specialists from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present any contested view as the consensus. Use British English.
