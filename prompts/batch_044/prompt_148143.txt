Construct a thought experiment testing whether technology can "survive" meaningfully after human affairs end, using von Neumann's idea—reported by Ulam in 1958—that accelerating progress approaches "some essential singularity in the history of the race beyond which human affairs, as we know them, could not continue." Design a realistic near-future scenario (one paragraph, no exotic physics, no hidden-motive AI) in which a fully automated, value-agnostic infrastructure network—optimized solely for energy-efficient self-replication—survives a plausible synthetic-pandemic collapse of humanity and expands for centuries without embedded human values or decision procedures. After the scenario, show: (i) why von Neumann's singularity framework treats this decoupling as structural rather than accidental; (ii) the best reply for someone who insists the network's survival still counts as human legacy; and (iii) which feature—e.g., the replacement of human utility by energy-efficiency as the optimization target, or the absence of value-preservation mechanisms—separates mere survival from continuity of human affairs, and what happens if that feature is removed.
