I'm giving the opening remarks tomorrow at a small workshop called 'Pictures Without Words' — about half the room are philosphers of science who've read all the Neurath ISOTYPE material, and the other half are people who build data visulization tools and probably haven't. I want the welcome to be structured around a tension in Neurath's picture-language idea: he wanted images that carry meaning all by themselves without any verbal captian, like a universal symbol set, but he also insisted that every choice of symbol is already a theoretical decision, so there's no theory-neutral seeing. That contradiction needs to do real work in what I say — I shouldn't just celebrate universal design, and I shouldn't just collapse pictures into text, I need to stand right in the middle of it. I was also arguing with my roommate last night about whether Neurath's boat metaphor — rebuilding knowledge plank by plank — applies to AI image generators, since they don't share conventions but just reproduce statistical patterns; I think it applies to the conventions, not the knowledge, and if there's a subtle way to weave that in without turning this into a lecture, that'd be great. Please write the remarks as exactly five short movements, each only a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion, because I'm going to read them off a card and want the whole thing to feel like a single breath. The experts should hear the contradiction I'm pressing, and the designers should feel it in the dashboards they build every day.
