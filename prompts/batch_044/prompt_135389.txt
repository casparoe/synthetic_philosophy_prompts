I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, but I have been thinking about identity and origin in the way one thinks about system dependencies: change the initial inputs, and is the output still the same instance?

Read the 1997 film Gattaca through the lens of origin essentialism—the metaphysical thesis that an individual could not have originated from different sperm and egg and still be that very person. I want a reading that takes both the film and the philosophical debate seriously, not a reduction of either.

The film presents Vincent Freeman, conceived without genetic engineering (referred to in the film as a God child or in-valid), who assumes the biological identity of Jerome Morrow, a genetically engineered valid. The narrative turns on whether Vincent's destiny is fixed by his actual genetic origin, and whether adopting Jerome's DNA lets him become, in some sense, Jerome.

Please address the following, and write your response in American English.

First, apply Kripke-style origin essentialism to Vincent: if Vincent had been conceived from a different sperm and egg—say, one of the engineered embryos his parents later used for his brother Anton—would the resulting person be Vincent? Now distinguish that from a stronger genomic essentialism that treats the specific DNA sequence as essential. Does the film treat Vincent's heart condition and myopia as part of his essential identity, or as contingent properties that happen to trace back to this particular origin?

Second, examine the plot in which Vincent assumes Jerome's identity and succeeds only by supplying Jerome's hair, blood, and urine to pass biometric checks. Does this plot structure implicitly endorse origin essentialism by suggesting that biological origin is the real identity, and Vincent is essentially an impostor? Or does it undermine essentialism by showing that Vincent's narrative identity—his ambition, his love for Irene, his determination—remains constant regardless of which DNA is scanned?

Third, consider Anton. Anton is conceived through genetic selection with different gametes. If we apply origin essentialism strictly, Anton could not have come from different sperm and egg and still be Anton. But the film treats Anton as Vincent's brother, not as a counterfactual version of Vincent. Does the film's family dynamics commit it to the view that siblings share a family relation but have essentially distinct origins? And what does that imply for the counterfactual where the parents select Vincent's genome but with different donor material?

Fourth, where does the film resist a philosophical reading? The film is often read as an argument against genetic determinism. But if it rejects determinism, does it also reject origin essentialism, or can the two be separated? Identify at least one scene—perhaps the swimming race between the brothers, or the final space launch—where the cinematic logic pulls against the metaphysical logic, and say honestly which one should yield.

Finally, what does Gattaca add to the origin-essentialism debate that a pure philosophical argument—say, Kripke's discussion of Queen Elizabeth and the sperm-egg origin—does not? Does the visual and narrative emphasis on valid versus in-valid bodies illuminate something about the social construction of essential properties?

Please do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to academic literature; I want an analysis that stands on its own reasoning and on the film's common-knowledge plot and major scenes.
