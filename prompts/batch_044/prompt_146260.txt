I've been working through cause-prioritization exercises that score interventions along importance, tractability, and neglectedness, and I keep encountering a reasoning move I can't place. A proposal comes up that looks high on importance and technically straightforward—perhaps a cheap diagnostic that could prevent a widespread but underfunded condition, or a regulatory fix with clear cost-benefit modeling behind it—so tractability should be high, importance is high, and because almost no one is working on it, neglectedness is high too, which ought to push it to the top of the list. But then the objection arises: "If it were really that tractable, wouldn't it already have been done? The fact that capable people haven't solved it despite the obvious payoff suggests there's a hidden blocker—maybe institutional friction, maybe an unrecognized technical catch—that we just haven't seen yet, so we should actually downgrade tractability." What's notable is that no specific blocker is identified; the bare persistence of neglect is treated as direct, content-free evidence of hidden difficulty. At the same time, if an intervention has been widely adopted, the same reasoners tend to say it was never neglected enough to prioritize in the first place, which makes the framework feel circular: absence of effort proves intractability, but presence of effort proves low neglectedness, with no stable path for a high-importance, apparently tractable cause to stay on the list. Is there an established name for this particular epistemic pattern—using the current distribution of effort as an implicit, self-sealing signal about hidden obstacles? How should I distinguish it from an ordinary Bayesian update on others' private information, or from applying adverse-selection reasoning to cause selection? And if it lacks a specific term, how can I draw its boundaries precisely: does it apply only inside explicit ITN scoring, or is it a broader failure mode whenever resource-allocation reasoning treats observed neglect as intrinsic difficulty without identifying the mechanism?
