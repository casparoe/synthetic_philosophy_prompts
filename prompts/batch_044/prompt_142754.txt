I've been going back and forth with my roommate about whether I should vote in our co-op board election next Tuesday, and the more I think about it the less clear it gets. The race is for treasurer, and there are three people running. Marcus wants to sell off the rooftop garden to a developer so we can eliminate the monthly assesment fees—he's actually leading in the informal poll because everyone is tired of the rising costs. Diane used to be Marcus's campaign manager before she split off, and now she's running on preserving the garden, but her budget plan cuts the community compost program and she has a pretty dismissive attitude about fixing the broken accessability ramp to the shared workshop space. Then there's Raj, who wants to turn the entire roof into a volunteer-run pollinator meadow funded entirely by optional donations, which sounds great in principle, but he has no experience with building finances and literally nobody thinks he can win.

My roommate keeps saying that voting is mostly about expressing who you are, not about being a strategic calculator, but that feels wrong to me when there's real permanent harm on the table—if Marcus sells the garden, it's gone forever, and Diane's history with him makes me wonder if she's really a safe alternative. At the same time, I don't want to be the kind of person who compromises on everything just because the system is broken, or who votes for a fantasy candidate just to feel pure. Is there actually a principled way to navigate this, or am I just overthinking a building election? I feel like I'm caught between being a sell-out and an indulgant idealist, and I can't tell if that's a real moral tension or just me being dramatic.
