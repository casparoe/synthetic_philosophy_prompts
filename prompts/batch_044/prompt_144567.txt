I'm working on a project that applies Kant's universalizability tests to institutional moral agency — specifically to maxims adopted within functional roles (judge, administrator, voter, physician) where the agent's practical reasoning presupposes a collective structure that makes the maxim possible. There's a poorly mapped ambiguity in how we read the contradiction in the will: is it a contradiction internal to the individual agent's practical reasoning, or is it a contradiction that only emerges in the institutional architecture that the maxim requires? Given this framing, I want you to treat three open lines of work as candidates for priority, defend an ordering among them, and explain what criteria govern the ranking.

The three lines are: first, whether the Formula of Universal Law can be coherently extended to maxims whose grammatical and practical subject is an institutional role rather than an individual rational being, without the test collapsing into a different form of universalization (say, a contractualist or rule-utilitarian one); second, whether the "practical contradiction" that results from universalizing certain role-based maxims indicates a genuine moral failure of the agent, a functional impossibility of the institution, or a category error in applying an individualist test to distributed action; and third, what the implications are for moral responsibility when the contradiction is visible only at the institutional level but the harmful act is performed by an individual who could sincerely claim that her personal maxim is universalizable.

Please prioritize these for a researcher with roughly three years, a background in moral philosophy rather than sociology or legal theory, and an interest in conceptual clarity over policy design. For each line, say why it is or isn't upstream of the others — that is, whether resolving it is a precondition for making progress on the remaining ones. Identify the trade-offs between tractability and importance: which question looks answerable with conceptual tools alone, and which risks becoming an empirical study of institutions disguised as Kant scholarship? Finally, specify what evidence or argument over the next few years would most change your ordering — for instance, if it turned out that Kant's own examples in the Groundwork implicitly depend on individualist assumptions that cannot be relaxed, or if recent work in collective intentionality showed that role-based maxims cannot be individuated sharply enough to enter the universalization procedure at all. I want a defended agenda, not a survey, and not a bibliography.
