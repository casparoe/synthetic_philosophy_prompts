Predict how Charles Lyell—uniformitarian geologist and author of Principles of Geology—would have responded to the proposal that the Anthropocene be recognised as a formal geological epoch, with its onset marked variously by the Orbis spike of 1610, the Industrial Revolution, or the Great Acceleration of 1950, and with its status to be decided by the International Commission on Stratigraphy rather than by stratigraphic processes alone. Would he have accepted that human activity constitutes a legitimate discontinuity in geological time, or would he have resisted the idea that a committee can declare an epoch that is still unfolding? Ground your answer in Lyell's known methodological commitments—particularly his view that present processes explain the past—without reversing this into an unargued claim that the present is unprecedented. Write in British English, avoid bibliographies or source citations, and do not treat the Anthropocene as an already settled fact: address the tension between uniformitarian continuity and the claim that humans have become a stratigraphic force.
