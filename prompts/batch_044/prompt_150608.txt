Compare Nozick’s experience machine with its conceptual inverse: a mechanism that guarantees authentic achievement of genuinely valuable ends, but only by subjecting the user to real, unavoidable suffering and effort, with no possibility of simulated success. For each of the three theories of well-being that the original machine is usually taken to test—hedonism, desire-satisfaction, and objective-list theories—explain whether the inverse machine produces the opposite verdict, and whether that symmetry is philosophically stable. In particular, determine whether authenticity and achievement carry independent weight or are valuable only instrumentally, and identify which of the three theories is most destabilized when both machines are treated as serious, jointly available test cases.
