I'm delivering the opening remarks for a two-day workshop titled "Ontological Commitment and Ordinary Speech," hosted by a philosophy department but attended by metaphysicians, philosophers of language, and researchers from cognitive science who do not work on ontology. The audience is split: about half are specialists who will notice if I mischaracterize the debate over whether ontological disputes are verbal disputes, and the other half are intelligent outsiders who need to understand why this question matters without being lectured. I want the speech to treat that debate not as background decoration but as structural: it should determine what I can and cannot claim about the purpose of our gathering. Specifically, please frame the remarks around a middle position that is not standard Carnapian deflationism or Siderian realism—namely, the idea that ontological disputes are verbal at the level of explicit propositional content but carry substantive normative weight at the level of implicit practical commitment, so that translating between rival frameworks requires restructuring how we reason about salience and explanation rather than mere paraphrase. Use a concrete, non-technical example—such as disagreement over whether there are genuinely composite objects or only arranged simples—to show both audiences why this distinction changes the stakes of the workshop.

Please write the remarks in American English, suitable for about five minutes when spoken aloud. The speech should welcome participants, explain why the workshop exists given this framing, and avoid saying either that we are here "simply to discover what exists" or that we are "merely clarifying language." Instead, let the philosophical position you have introduced constrain the language throughout. After the text of the speech, include a brief meta-note stating how confident you are in the philosophical interpretation you have presented and identifying the specific claim about which you are least sure—particularly regarding whether practical-commitment structures can genuinely carry ontological weight independently of explicit quantificational claims. Do not include citations, a bibliography, or references to specific page numbers.
