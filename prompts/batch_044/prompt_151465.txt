You serve as the senior appeals officer for a platform's trust-and-safety division, adjudicating roughly fifty appeals per eight-hour shift with no in-house legal counsel available and a mandatory 150-word justification for every ruling that bears your digital signature. Your docket is now dominated by edge-case content—synthetic-voice parodies, procedurally generated visual memes, and context-collapsed video remixes—that does not map cleanly onto the existing policy taxonomy (e.g., "harassment," "misinformation," "hate speech"). Rather than searching for an essential defining feature of each category, construct a reusable, step-by-step decision procedure grounded in Wittgenstein's analysis of language games and family resemblance, designed for repeated use by an officer with no philosophical training. Specify the ordered checks in plain language: identifying the form of life or practice in which the utterance operates; mapping overlapping similarities and criss-crossing analogies to canonical policy examples; testing whether shared family traits are present, absent, or partially present; and flagging the precise points where judgment cannot be eliminated and must be documented as discretionary sign-off. The protocol must handle high-frequency, high-stakes decisions under severe time pressure and must explicitly state which boundary disputes it can settle, which it must escalate, and why an essential-definition approach would misfire on the same cases. Provide three worked applications: one synthetic-voice parody that the procedure clearly excludes from "harassment"; one procedurally generated meme that it clearly includes under "misinformation"; and one hybrid case—perhaps a context-stripped remix that mimics a protected satire form—where the procedure yields an unwelcome or indeterminate verdict that forces escalation rather than resolution. Conclude by stating what the procedure definitively cannot do, naming the kinds of irreducible boundary conflicts that remain outside its reach and explaining why family-resemblance reasoning reaches its limit there. Present this as a formal protocol document with a brief preamble fixing the practitioner's role, shift constraints, documentation requirements, and personal liability for each sign-off.
