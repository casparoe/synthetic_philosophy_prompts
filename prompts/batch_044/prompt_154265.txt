I've been arguing with friends about low-impact AI and whether "avoid side effects" can actually be defined without already knowing exactly what values you want. The standard proposal is to compare the world-state after the AI acts against a counterfactual baseline where the AI never existed, then penalize big deviations. But here's what I'm stuck on: picking that baseline already seems to encode normative judgments. If I assume the baseline is "humanity continues on roughly its current trajectory," I'm implicitly treating that path as the default "good" state. But if I try to avoid that by using something like an information-theoretic distance metric that's supposed to be value-neutral, I run into this other issue — a world where almost everyone dies quietly might register as "low impact" relative to pre-civilization entropy levels, while a world with thriving but politically different societies registers as high-impact.

Can "avoid side effects" be defined without a full specifcation of value? I want a committed answer, not just listing pros and cons. Specifically: do you think there's any formalization that genuinely escapes this circularity, or is the whole low-impact project necessarily pushing the hard alignment problem back one step? And if it's circular, does that actually undermine the motivation for using low-impact as a safety strategy, or can we salvage it by being explicit about baseline values? Please take a definite position and explain where the crux actually lies — especially for cases where the AI could reshape physical constants or something, making the counterfactual totally undefined.
