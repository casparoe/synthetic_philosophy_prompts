berkeley died in 1753, way before anyone dreamed up digital currency. in his writings on money he treats it less like a chunk of metal and more like a ticket or sign — value comes from the agreement and the idea it stands for, not from the physical stuff. so imagine he gets a briefing on bitcoin: no paper, no gold, just entries on a distributed ledger held together by consensus algorithms. what would he actually think? i don't want the easy answer ("immaterialism so he likes it"). work it out: does the fact that bitcoin has no physical token at all make it the purest realization of money as sign, or does it fail because there's no corresponding idea in god's mind backing each coin — a fiction layered on fiction? and how would he treat algorithmic stablecoins that adjust supply automatically without human judgment? does that look like reason imitating providence, or like people mistaking a symbol for the real economic relation? derive it from his commitments, don't just stamp a label on it.
