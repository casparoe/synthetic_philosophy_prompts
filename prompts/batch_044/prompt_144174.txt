Most popular treatments flatten this into a simple claim that study makes one happy or that ignorance is bliss, but her position is more delicate: the illusions must be actively cultivated and protected by the very discipline that threatens to dissolve them.

Could you write a joke—preferably with a brief narrative setup rather than a bare one-liner—that plays on exactly this paradox? The joke should reward a listener who recognizes du Châtelet's specific conjunction of study and necessary illusion, yet it should remain fully intelligible to someone who has never read her, functioning as a self-contained commentary on the irony of laboring diligently to preserve a comforting falsehood. Please keep the tone polished and conceptually precise, avoiding anachronistic slang or modern self-help jargon, though a contemporary framing is acceptable if it heightens the irony; and please do not include citations, bibliographic references, or requests for further reading, since the point is the conceptual twist itself.
