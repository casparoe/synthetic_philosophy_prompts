For my philosophy of mind seminar, I am drafting an adversarial-collaboration exercise on sphexishness and free will. Please write the joint statement that a hierarchical-sphexist and a genuine-flexibility theorist could both sign after good-faith debate. The hierarchical-sphexist maintains that human cognition consists of nested, rigid behavioural loops—analogous to the sphex wasp’s burrow-checking routine—whose apparent openness is merely an artefact of hierarchical complexity; the genuine-flexibility theorist maintains that human agents exhibit irreducibly open-ended, context-sensitive revision that breaks the sphexish mould, so that sphexishness defines precisely what free will is not.

Structure the document as follows: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims, covering the ethology of the sphex wasp, the distinction between mechanical rigidity and adaptive revision, and the explanatory limits both sides accept for purely hierarchical models; (2) a table of disagreements, with each position phrased in wording its own camp would accept—especially on whether hierarchical complexity ever yields genuine openness or only simulates it; (3) the cruxes, including whether mechanistic predictive success undermines the flexibility view and whether sphexishness is a continuum or a categorical failure; (4) for each crux, a concrete case or thought experiment both sides agree is relevant, with each camp’s predicted outcome recorded in advance; and (5) a brief assessment of whether any part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, identifying which part and why. Avoid bibliographies or citations; refer to Hofstadter’s discussion of the wasp only in general terms.
