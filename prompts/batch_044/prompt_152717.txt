Write a short story set in a near-future therapeutic program in which patients with severe dissociative fragmentation are linked through a shared, real-time mnemonic architecture that distributes episodic memory across the network. Focus on one participant who previously experienced herself as a discrete center of narrative gravity—an autonomous author of her life story—only to discover that the autobiographical thread she traces back to a singular origin is now collectively sustained. The other members contribute causally and emotionally to the coherence of her past, so that the narrative continuity once localized in her consciousness is now maintained by the group. Explore whether ethical responsibility for actions committed under her prior, isolated narrative identity can still be assigned to her individually, or whether it must be attributed to the distributed collective that now holds her memories. Examine, further, whether any single participant retains authority to revise or reject portions of the shared history, and what becomes of personal authorship when the center of narrative gravity is no longer confined to one consciousness. Keep the scenario grounded in the interpersonal dynamics of the group rather than in technical exposition, and let the philosophical tension emerge through the characters' attempts to navigate blame, revision, and self-definition.
