I'm constructing a taxonomy for a graduate seminar on virtue ethics and the situationist critique. The goal is to classify responses not by the philosopher who holds them, but by the underlying commitments they make about traits, consistency, and moral evaluation.

Please design a decision tree with six to eight branch points, each phrased as a concrete commitment or case judgment rather than a philosophical label. The root question should be whether the empirical situationist finding—that situational variables frequently predict behavior more reliably than global personality traits—is taken to undermine virtue ethics descriptively, normatively, both, or neither.

From that root, the tree should split on:
- whether cross-situational consistency is treated as necessary, sufficient, or irrelevant for virtue attribution;
- whether virtue, if retained, is reconceived as a local/state property tied to particular domains, a global disposition with context-sensitive activation conditions, or a relational property of agent-environment pairs (for which I use the working label "ecological virtue," though I am unsure whether this has an established name in the literature);
- and whether the response accepts a further, second-order form of situationism: the claim that the very criteria for what counts as a virtue vary across social contexts, not merely that behavior varies.

Each leaf should be assigned a name if the position is well-known (for example, classical Aristotelian virtue ethics, eliminativist situationism, or interactionist virtue ethics) or marked "unnamed" with a brief definition and a note on whether it appears coherent but unexplored, or incoherent. At least one logically possible combination of answers should be left unoccupied; please flag it and briefly diagnose why the space might be empty (e.g., instability, unattractiveness, or mere neglect).

The tree should be able to place a respondent who answers the concrete case questions—such as whether one can be courageous in professional crises yet consistently freeze in personal ones, and still possess the virtue of courage—without knowing the technical vocabulary in advance.

Please write your answer in British English. In addition, please indicate what specific empirical finding or philosophical argument would cause you to revise the classification of the "ecological virtue" branch, and identify the step in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to error.
