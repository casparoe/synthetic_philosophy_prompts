My study group cooked up this argument against illusionism about consciousness and I'm not sure where the leak is. (1) Illusionism says phenomenal consciousness is an illusion: there's no what-it-is-like quality, just representational states that misrepresent themselves as having it. (2) If I can misrepresent my own mental state about redness — thinking it has phenomenal redness when it only has representational content — then introspection isn't just falible about one feature, it's falible about what kind of state this is. (3) So for any mental state M that I access introspectivly, I'm representing it as having a phenomenal character that it lacks. (4) But knowledge of M requires representing M's actual kind, and I'm always representing the wrong kind. (5) Therefore I never have first-person knowledge of any mental state, including the representational ones illusionists say exist. (6) Yet illusionists must rely on first-person access to even identify the representational states they're talking about. (7) So illusionism undermines the very epistemic basis it needs. I want to keep (1) and (6) if I can. Which step should I reject, and what's the price?
