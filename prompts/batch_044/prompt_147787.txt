In debates over whether mistreating video-game NPCs can be morally wrong, critics often say an NPC is like a puppet or doll—the player pulls strings over pixels, there is no inner victim, so harm is conceptually impossible. Defenders reply that an NPC is more like a figure in an interactive drama where the audience is also a co-author, implying that participatory engagement creates moral responsibilities a passive reader lacks. Audit both analogies: lay out the precise correspondences (player, programmer, fictional world, emotional response), locate exactly where each mapping breaks, identify whether either smuggles in an unstated premise about the necessity of consciousness or about the moral weight of co-creation, and determine whether either can actually support a verdict of wrongness without first resolving whether fictional entities can be wronged at all. If neither analogy survives, specify what feature—if any—would have to be present in an NPC for a harm-analogy to become sound.
