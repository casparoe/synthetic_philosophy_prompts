Please examine the episode through both theoretical lenses. First, identify the structural conditions that made a radical-right consolidation likely regardless of who led the movement. Second, pinpoint the specific decisions in 1933–34—particularly Hitler's legalistic manipulation of constitutional mechanisms, his personal orchestration of the purge of Röhm, and his strategic management of Hindenburg's authority—where individual agency appears genuinely irreplaceable by structural logic alone. Finally, assess whether the case supports a modified Great Man view (that structures create the vacancy, but a particular personality is required to fill it) or whether it ultimately undermines individual-centered explanation in favor of structural determinism. I ask that the analysis remain disciplined: say explicitly which features of the consolidation each theory explains well, which features it misdescribes or leaves silent, and whether the episode functions as a confirmation, an anomaly, or an unanticipated case for either framework. Do not provide a bibliography or list of sources; I need only the conceptual analysis.
