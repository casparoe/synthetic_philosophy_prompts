Question: In Nāgārjuna's Mūlamadhyamakakārikā he applies the tetralemma (catuṣkoṭi) to svabhāva (inherent existence). Explain the four corners in this context and then clarify the Madhyamaka claim that emptiness (śūnyatā) is itself empty. Also say whether this amounts to nihilism.

Student answer:
Nāgārjuna uses the four-corner negation to check if things have svabhāva. The four options are: (1) they exist with an inherent nature, (2) they dont exist at all, (3) they both exist and dont, (4) they neither exist nor dont exist. He rejects all four for dependently originated things. Emptiness just means lacking svabhāva, not total non-existance—things still work convensionally without a fixed essence. Then he applies the same analysis to emptiness itself: emptiness is also empty of svabhāva, so it cant become a hidden metaphysical foundation. This avoids nihilism since convensionally existence stays intact. Actually this lines Madhyamaka up with Yogācāra, because both agree that emptiness shows reality is mind-only and there is no independent external world. So by saying emptiness is empty, Nāgārjuna is really endorsing the fourth corner—'neither existence nor non-existence'—but at a meta-level, and thats the actual Madhyamaka position.
