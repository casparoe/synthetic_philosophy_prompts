Here's roughly where I stand: (1) I buy Lewis-style counterpart theory for most de re modal talk — possible worlds are concrete, and "I could have been a musician" means there's a counterpart who plays guitar and is very similar. (2) But I also think Kripke was onto something with transworld identity: when I imagine being in a world where I never studied philosophy, it feels like it's literally me there, not just a doppleganger, and i dont think that's just a confusion. (3) I'm not a vagueness fan — I think identity is sharp, no fuzzy edges, so I reject the idea that there's a "degree" to which I'm my counterpart. (4) I also think that moral concern shouldn't extend accross worlds: I care about my actual-world friends suffering, but if there's a counterpart of me suffering in world W, that's not my problem, even though I think moral reasons are generally agent-neutral. (5) I think origin essentialism is false for persons — my parents could have been different — but I still think there's something essential about my specifc phenomenal character, the "what-it's-likeness" of my experience, and that this is preserved across worlds where I exist. (6) Finally, I think the accidental intrinsics problem (like Humphrey having hair in one world but being bald in another with the same intrinsic properties) is only a problem for transworld identity, and counterpart theory solves it cleanly.

So do these hang together? Specifically, can I really hold (1) and (2) without contradiction, or am i smuggling in transworld identity through the back door with (5)? And is (4) consistent with agent-neutrality given (2)? Also, is there an apparent tension between (3) and (5) — like if phenomenal character is essential but sharp, does that force me back into some kind of haecceitism? I want the tensions ranked by severity and the cheapest fix for each. No citations needed, just reasoning.
