As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun working through moral philosophy in a systematic way, I have tried to formulate my own position on character and moral evaluation, particularly in light of the situationist critique of virtue ethics. Before presenting this framework to a discussion group, I want an internal consistency audit rather than a lecture. Here are the commitments I currently hold:

(i) I accept the situationist findings in their strong interpretation: cross-situational correlations for moral behavior are typically in the range of r ≈ 0.20–0.30, which I regard—by the standards of causal modeling in the natural sciences—as too weak to support robust global dispositions such as honesty or compassion as stable explanatory variables across contexts.

(ii) I nevertheless defend virtue ethics, but only by reconceiving virtues as domain-specific expertises: context-sensitive capacities analogous to a scientist's skill in calibrating instruments under particular laboratory conditions, rather than as cross-situational character traits that manifest uniformly everywhere.

(iii) I hold that there is an objective, biologically anchored conception of human flourishing (eudaimonia) that supplies normative content to these local virtues; cooperative capacity, long-range planning, and emotional regulation are genuinely good for organisms with our evolutionary history, largely independent of cultural variation.

(iv) I maintain that moral judgment should be retrospective and particularist: we evaluate actions by whether they manifest practical wisdom (phronesis) calibrated to the specific affordances of the situation, not by measuring them against the agent's global character profile.

(v) I also accept that individuals engage in situation selection—they actively construct and choose environments that elicit behavior consistent with their local competencies—so the empirical weakness of global traits does not entail the absence of structured character, but rather its ecological embedding within selected niches.

(vi) At the same time, I worry that if virtues are purely local competencies lacking any cross-contextual integration, then the normative idea of a good life fragments into an unrelated patchwork of skills, and virtue ethics collapses into a form of situation-calibrated skill-ethics that is normatively indistinguishable from a refined act-utilitarianism applied case by case.

Please audit this package. Rank every pair of commitments that stands in genuine tension by severity, and for each state what the least costly revision would be and what conceptual cost it carries. Separately, identify at least one pair that appears to conflict but dissolves on closer inspection; explain precisely why the tension is only apparent and how the two commitments actually cohere. Treat my commitments as fixed unless logical consistency strictly demands revision, and do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I want a purely conceptual consistency check.
