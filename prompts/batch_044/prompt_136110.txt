As a member of my university's constitutional review board, I must cast a deciding vote on a procedural crisis that has suddenly become personal. Our charter contains two conflicting provisions: Section 4 permits amendments by a three-fourths vote of currently seated representatives, while Section 7 states that Section 7 itself—and any rule governing amendment procedures—may only be altered by unanimous consent of every person who has ever held a seat, including retired emeritus members. A faction now proposes invoking Section 4 to repeal Section 7's unanimity requirement, arguing that the general amendment power implicitly supersedes the special constraint. Opponents contend that this maneuver is self-nullifying: if a rule can be repealed by the very procedure it restricts, the charter's amendment framework collapses into meaninglessness. The board must circulate an interpretive memo before next Wednesday's session recommending whether the senate may lawfully proceed under Section 4, specifying any procedural safeguards should we approve, and giving an honest, rigorous account of the strongest objection—that allowing this vote renders the charter's self-amending rules arbitrary. Please draft that memo.
