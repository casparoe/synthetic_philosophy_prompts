In his later philosophical writings, Schrödinger defends a Vedantic monism holding that all individual minds are manifestations of one universal consciousness, and he is often read as grounding this claim specifically in quantum mechanics—namely, that the wave function describes a single indivisible reality and that the appearance of separate observers arises only through decoherence-induced branching. If we reject that quantum-physical foundation—say, because decoherence explains apparent branching without presupposing any underlying mental unity—are we compelled to abandon Schrödinger’s monism with it? Or does the Vedantic position he endorsed carry independent philosophical weight, perhaps through phenomenological arguments about the irreducibility of first-person awareness or logical considerations about the identity of consciousness across distinct bodies? Furthermore, if the quantum framing was always intended as analogical rather than demonstrative, why do so many interpreters treat it as structurally essential to his argument rather than as a heuristic motivation?
