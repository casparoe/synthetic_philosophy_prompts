I'm working through a problem set in my philosophy of mind seminar that operates with a deliberately artificial ontology: there is a reactive substrate called careenium, which changes state solely through local energy gradients or direct mechanical deformation and possesses no representational capacities of its own, and there are structural units called simmballs, which can instantiate relational patterns (symbols) while also carrying physical properties such as mass, thermal conductivity, or electromagnetic signature. Our instructor has issued verdicts on whether simmballs "push" the careenium—meaning whether the symbolic relation they carry is part of the causal explanation for a substrate state-change—across four baseline configurations. First, when simmballs composed of careenium material are arranged inside the substrate in a pattern that alters local energy flow and the substrate shifts, she rules that the symbols did push. Second, when the identical pattern is displayed externally and a human observer reads it before manually applying force, she rules that they did not; the observer did the pushing. Third, when foreign-material simmballs are embedded with the same pattern but are physically inert and no state-change occurs, she rules that they did not push. Fourth, when careenium-composed simmballs form the pattern and an embedded, non-conscious actuator—also made of careenium—detects the pattern's physical geometry and amplifies the resulting energy shift, she again rules that the symbols pushed.

Next week she will evaluate a fifth configuration: a cluster of simmballs made entirely of non-careenium material is arranged in a computing substrate that is physically isolated from the careenium except for a single wireless energy conduit. The relational pattern they instantiate is processed computationally, and the computation modulates the conduit in a manner that depends specifically on the pattern's relational structure rather than merely on the total energy transmitted. The careenium shifts state in response to this modulated input. Predict her verdict, then identify the two most plausible implicit rules that fit her first four judgments—one emphasizing that the symbol-bearer must be materially continuous with the substrate, the other emphasizing that the causal chain must be mechanically continuous and free of conscious mediation—and say which rule each assigns to the new case. Finally, specify the one further configuration that would most cleanly decide between those two rules.
