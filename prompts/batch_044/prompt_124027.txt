I am assembling a dataset of philosophical writing prompts and would like you to compose a satirical, deadpan parody of an NHS-style patient information leaflet for a fictional Neuro-Distributed Continuity Clinic that offers elective bilocated consciousness procedures modelled on Daniel Dennett's brain-transplant thought experiment in his 1978 essay Where Am I?. The leaflet should address prospective patients who are considering the surgical separation of their neural substrate from their original body, with remote telemetry controlling a replacement ambulatory unit.

Write approximately 600 words, using numbered clinical sections—such as Pre-operative counselling, During dislocation, Post-operative identity verification, and A note on holiday closures—in relentlessly bureaucratic British English. The humour must come from treating Dennett's central philosophical problem—whether the patient is now located with the cerebral substrate in the vat or with the walking body, and what personal identity amounts to when neural-body communication fails—as if it were governed by transparent administrative protocol. To keep the prompt away from well-trodden summaries, invent a niche institutional mechanism not present in Dennett's original text but extending its logic: for instance, a mandatory Primary Beneficiary Designation Form adjudicated by a fictional Continuity Tribunal, which requires patients to declare in advance whether inheritance rights, spousal obligations, and criminal liability should attach to the brain, to the body, or be suspended in escrow during any period of ambiguous location. Include a brief, straight-faced sidebar explaining the clinic's closure policy over bank holidays, framing the difficulty of returning to one's original location as a routine scheduling matter. The satire should exaggerate a real feature of the philosophical debate—namely, that our intuitions about being here or there collapse as soon as an institutional framework tries to codify them—so that a reader familiar with Dennett recognises the target and finds the exaggeration fair, while a reader unfamiliar with the literature can still follow the deadpan absurdity. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and write the response in British English.
