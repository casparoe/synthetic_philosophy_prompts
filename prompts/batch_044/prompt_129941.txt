Write a Petrarchan sonnet—ABBA ABBA in the octave, CDECDE in the sestet, strict iambic pentameter—on the absent-minded driver paradox, but treat it through the framework of indexical self-location rather than through mere forgetfulness. Frame each decision node as a centered-world agent who reasons correctly with identical evidence: at the first exit and at the second, the local belief-set and available information are indistinguishable. Use the octave to lay out the ex-ante commitment—passing the first exit is required to reach the higher payoff at the second, and the plan assumes a continuous self that remembers its own resolution. At the volta in line 9, place the driver at an exit and have him recognize that no indexical marker distinguishes this is exit one from this is exit two. In the sestet, loop the local reasoning: each line should restate why continuing is rational here, and the rhyme scheme should pair identical end-words across the sestet—exit rhymed with exit, now with now—so that the form itself enacts the indiscernibility that prevents resolution. The closing couplet should state precisely what must be abandoned to break the loop: either the trans-temporal identity that makes planning coherent, or the local consistency that makes acting rational. Do not choose between them; end on the unresolved tension. It should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with dynamic consistency and planning paradoxes recognizes the structure, with the volta tracking the collapse of self-location and the repeated rhymes tracking the repetition of reasoning. Write for an audience that has followed decision-theory discussions but needs no bibliography or citation.
