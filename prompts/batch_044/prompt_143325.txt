Been writing about AI alignment and everyone keeps reaching for this factory analogy for HCH — that recursive setup where a human consults a smarter version of themself to break hard reasoning into bits. They say factored cognition is just like Ford's assembly line: specialised stations, no one worker needs the full blueprint, quality control at the end. It's used to license the idea that deliberation can scale without anyone holding the whole argument — a kind of cognitive division of labour where comprehension is distributed, not centralised. I want to audit this properly. What's the correspondance table: factory floor, foreman, blueprint, inspector, and in the HCH version? Where does it snap — especially since in reasoning, breaking the problem into parts often changes the problem itself (non-compositionality of justification), and the workers are copies of the same mind, not interchangable labourers? Does the analogy smuggle in that there's a clear product being built, or that understanding can be fully delegated? If the disanologies are serious, is there a better image — maybe a jury where each juror is also a witness, or something like deliberative federalism? I need to know whether the scale-without-comprehension conclusion actually survives, or whether the factory image was doing all the work.
