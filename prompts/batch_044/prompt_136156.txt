Write the full specification of desiderata that any adequate theory of moral responsibility must satisfy if it is to survive Galen Strawson’s Basic Argument against true moral responsibility. Build the standard before scoring any contender.

For each desideratum, state the precise phenomenon or case it must cover—such as halting the regress of self-formation, preserving the distinction between being a causal source of an action and being genuinely deserving of blame, accommodating both determinism and indeterminism without collapse, maintaining psychological continuity across temporal gaps, and explaining whether and how an agent can be the originator of her own mental states. Behind each, give the consideration that makes it non-negotiable versus a preference of one camp, and flag any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable given Strawson’s reasoning—especially whether any view can both stop the regress and retain the kind of self-origination that moral responsibility is supposed to require.

As a constructed stress-test, include the requirement that the theory handle diachronic normative integration in branching form: present normative commitments depend on prior mental states that were themselves shaped by earlier commitments in a non-linear, branching structure rather than a simple linear chain. Treat this not as a standard formulation but as a genuine constraint the theory must accommodate or explain away.

Then, briefly, indicate how compatibilism, libertarianism, and Strawsonian impossibilism (or a revisionist alternative) fare against your list—only enough to reveal which criteria do the real exclusionary work. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not on the scorecard.
