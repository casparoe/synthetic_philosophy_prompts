Write a parody of an official government handbook, approximately 650 words, in deadpan bureaucratic prose modeled on immigration and civic-registration FAQs, for a fictional democratic state that claims to have solved the boundary problem through a rigorous algorithmic implementation of the all-affected-interests principle. Structure the piece as numbered sections explaining membership categories: full citizens (permanent residents affected by long-term infrastructure policy), provisional stakeholders (non-resident workers and trade partners affected by tariff regimes), non-human proxies (animals with welfare impacts from zoning or environmental rules), probabilistic future-generation avatars, and meta-voters who adjudicate amendments to the boundary rules themselves. Include concrete invented procedures—such as the Recursive Stakeholder Audit, temporary tourist voter-cards valid for the duration of a policy's externalities, or weighted proxy ballots for salmon in watershed districts—so that the absurdity arises from coherent extension of the principle rather than random nonsense. The target of the satire is the administrative impossibility and hidden normativity in defining the demos: let objections about who designs the algorithm, which demographics fail to demonstrate affectedness, and whether boundary-adjudication competence correlates with governing competence surface only through the handbook's clinical descriptions of appeals processes and eligibility tests. Maintain an unbroken deadpan voice; do not break character to explain the joke, and do not include real bibliographies, citations, or named-philosopher quotations. The result should be readable to someone unfamiliar with democratic theory yet clearly recognizable to a specialist as an exaggerated working-out of the classic democratic-boundary paradox and the stakeholder-democracy literature, with at least one invented institutional detail—such as a national holiday or a refund policy for misclassified avatars—that quietly underscores the circularity of letting the current demos vote on who belongs to it.
