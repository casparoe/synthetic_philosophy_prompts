In our dept's Dewey seminar we've been stuck for months on wether his experimentalism about democracy is actualy a claim about institutional design or about the psychological habits of citizens. One camp reads Democracy and Education and The Public and Its Problems as saying democratic government should literaly run like a lab -- form hypotheses about policy, test them, discard failures. The other camp says thats scientism, and Dewey just meant democratic life cultivates an open-minded, fallibilist attitude in people, with no specific institutional machinery required. Everyone quotes the same passages about intelligent action and scientific method, and nobody budges. Is this a verbal dispute about 'experimental' -- like one side using it literally, the other metaphorically? Or is there a hidden disagreement about wether democracy needs epistemic outputs (better policies) or just moral ones (better citizens)? And if its the latter, is the debate even well-formed, since Dewey might have meant both but in different contexts? I want a diagnosis of whats actualy doing the work here, not another survey of Dewey texts. Also, if you had to name one distinction or one kind of empirical evidence that could actualy break the logjam, what would it be?
