I'm drafting a three-month workshop proposal on the Letter Spirit problem — basically, what makes every 'a' an 'a' across totally different styles? The usual answers are family-resemblance clustering or some hidden geometric prototype, but both feel like they treat letters as isolated shapes. My half-baked twist is that letter identity might depend on 'inter-glyph contracts': relational obligations a glyph owes to other letters in the same alphabet (like how the counter of 'a' has to stay readable relative to the counters in 'e', 'o', 'b' in that same font family). I want the proposal to state the question precisely, explain why intrinsic-feature accounts leave it open, lay out my mthod — close reading of about 8-10 exstreme display typefaces plus designing a minimal 'contract-breaking' alphabet to test intuitions — and say what result I'd want (maybe a taxonomy of contract types) and what'd count as failure (if every variation can still be explained by shape alone). Keep it realisitic for 12 weeks, no bibliography needed since I'll fill citations later. Also, please be honest about what would change your mind here: what kind of counter-example or formal result would make you abandon the inter-glyph idea and go back to pure family resemblance?
