I run a philosophy workshop with about 13 people, one 75-min session. I want a game that makes them feel the pull of caring about originals when a copy is perfect in every visible way. Here's the niche twist to build in: include "ghost canvases" — forgeries made using the actual artist's leftover paint and brushes from the correct period, so material provenance is partly correct, but the intentional/causal chain from the artist's mind is totally broken.

Design the rules, hidden-info rounds, and a point-based payoff scheme (no real money). Participants should vote on insurance value, display status, or destruction for each piece before learning it's full causal history, then do a reveal round where the ghost-canvas status comes out. Include a debrif mapping what happened to the distinction between material continuity, perceptual identity, and intentional causation — and be honest about which part of the view, probably the normative claim that originals are objectively more valuable, the activty can't reproduce and you'll have to supply in discussion. Also tell me what'll predictably go wrong the first run; I bet people just vote on aesthetics and ignore the hidden provenance cards entirely.
