I should like you to write a villanelle addressing the paradox of phenomenal judgment under epiphenomenalism, but with a specific conceptual framing: treat the first-person report of consciousness not simply as an epistemically unjustified belief, but as an indexical expression whose referential anchor has been causally severed. Employ two refrains — one conveying the immediate givenness of experience ("This burning is present now"), the other conveying the physical production of that very report in the absence of any experiential input ("The cortex writes; the heat is elsewhere"). Their formal interweaving should mirror the structure of the paradox: an indexical that seems to point directly at its object is in fact generated by mechanisms that never interact with the qualia they name. The villanelle's requirement that the refrains alternate and finally stand together in the closing couplet ought to enact this proximity without causal contact — adjacency without interaction, convergence without resolution. Do not allow the final rhyme to harmonise the tension away; keep the two lines grammatically parallel yet metaphysically disjoint. The poem should be philosophically precise enough that someone acquainted with the literature — especially the worry that, if phenomenal properties do no causal work, the neural mechanisms producing phenomenal concepts never encounter the properties they purport to denote — recognises the specific difficulty rather than a generic mind-body puzzle. Write in polished British English, avoid all citation or bibliographic reference, and allow the form itself to carry part of the philosophical burden.
