I am writing an essay for a course on moral responsibility and free will, and I have been trying to map out my own stance on Frankfurt cases and the principle of alternative possibilities, but I find that my intuitions pull in directions I cannot quite reconcile. My current position is this: (1) I accept Frankfurt’s original counterexample and take it to show that the ability to do otherwise is not necessary for moral responsibility, since Jones decides and acts for his own reasons despite Black’s counterfactual presence; (2) I reject the flicker-of-freedom defence, on the grounds that an alternative which is causally irrelevant to the actual sequence cannot ground the kind of control responsibility requires; (3) I maintain that cases of duress — for example, complying with a credible threat to one’s life — do excuse the agent, but I attribute this not to the absence of alternatives but to the fact that the agent’s practical reasoning is coerced and therefore not fully her own; (4) I also hold that agents suffering from severe addiction or compulsive disorders are often not morally responsible for their actions, even though they typically retain the physical and psychological capacity to resist or seek help, and I am inclined to explain this failure of responsibility in terms of a disrupted predictive-processing hierarchy — that is, the agent’s higher-order predictions about her own behaviour are systematically overridden by lower-level compulsive signals, which undermines sourcehood without eliminating alternatives; and (5) I think that the correct analysis of responsibility should be source-based, requiring that the action flow from the agent’s own rational capacities in a way that is not externally hijacked, but I do not think this amounts to a disguised version of the principle of alternative possibilities.

Do these five commitments form a consistent package? Please audit them: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, rank those tensions by severity, indicate what the cheapest revision would be for each, and distinguish them from apparent tensions that dissolve on closer inspection — for instance, whether accepting Frankfurt-style counterfactual intervention in (1) really conflicts with my explanation of duress in (3). I would also welcome a brief indication of whether a single revision — perhaps to my account of coercion or to my view of addiction — could resolve the bulk of the conflict. Please write your response in American English.
