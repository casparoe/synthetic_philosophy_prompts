Write an essay on Glaucon's challenge in the Republic — specifcally whether the ring of Gyges actually undermines the claim that justice is good in itself, or whether it just shows that social repution is fragile. I want you to take seriously a somewhat weird angle: suppose we reinterpret the ring not as physical invisibility but as "epistemic invisibilty," meaning you can act unjustly but no observer (including yourself, due to self-deception mechanisms) can ever form a justified belief that you did it. Does this stronger version still make injustice rational? Weigh that against a modified contractarian reply — not the standard Hobbesian one, but the idea that moral norms are coordination devices that remain rational even when unobservable, because they stabilize your own long-term planning across possible worlds where you might forget or misremember your actions. Reach a conclusion: does Glaucon win under this epistemic-invisibility framing, or does justice still have non-instrumental value? Don't just survey positions — defend a verdict. End with a line beginning exactly "Verdict:" followed by your bottom line in one sentence.
