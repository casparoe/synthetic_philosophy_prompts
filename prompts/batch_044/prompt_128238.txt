Please explain the conceptual tension in treating cultural values as independent causal factors in historical explanation, using Weber's thesis about the Protestant ethic and the spirit of capitalism as the central case. Specifically, address the problem of explanatory autonomy: if values are genuinely causally efficacious in shaping economic behavior, they must not be reducible to material conditions; yet if they are not reducible, we seem to treat them as uncaused causes or as operating outside the causal nexus that governs social change. At the same time, if values are always already shaped by economic structures, then invoking them to explain the emergence of capitalism risks circularity. Clarify what is at stake in insisting that culture can be a cause rather than merely an epiphenomenon or a mediating variable, and explain why this problem is particularly acute for Weber's specific claim that an ascetic religious ethos helped generate capitalist rationalization—not just that the two co-occurred.
