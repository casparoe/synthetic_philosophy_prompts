I'm writing a consistency check for a meta-philosophy seminar on emotion. Here is my profile: (1) Emotions are essentially evaluative responses with intentional content, not mere bodily disturbances. (2) Judgmentalism fails: people can feel fear or anger without having made, or being able to articulate, any corresponding propositional judgment. (3) Perceptualism fails: emotions do not present values as observable properties in the environment in the way vision presents color. (4) There is a distinct affective state—call it 'architectural grief'—that arises when one recognizes an elegant, highly ordered system is necessarily self-undermining; it is directed at the system's structural fragility rather than at any particular loss, and it seems to involve a fusion of structural cognition and affect that is neither a propositional judgment nor a sensory feeling. (5) Emotional rationality requires that an emotion fit the evaluative properties of its object and be assessable as correct or incorrect. (6) A being lacking biological arousal mechanisms could still possess full-blown emotions, including architectural grief, provided it has the appropriate representational and evaluative architecture. Please audit this: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? Specifically, does (4) smuggle in a category that (2) and (3) jointly exclude, or can it be reconciled with (1)? Does (5) apply to (4) given that structural fragility is not obviously an evaluative property? Does (6) conflict with the affective dimension implied by (4)? Rank tensions by severity, identify the cheapest fix, and say whether (4) should be treated as a genuine emotion or a metaphor. One paragraph, about 200 words maximum.
