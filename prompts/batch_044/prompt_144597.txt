I'm prepping for a workshop on hinge epistemology and wanted to check if my take hangs together before I embarass myself. Heres where I stand:

1. I think hinge propositions — like the earth being old — aren't beliefs we justify with evidence; they're part of the scaffolding that makes justification possible.

2. I think that scaffolding can shift over time. What was a hinge in one era (geocentrism, for instance) can become a rejected belief, and vice versa.

3. I think skeptical scenarios are technically meaningful but don't actually bite, because they detach from the practices that give our hinges their point.

4. I think we have a kind of non-evidential entitlement to our hinges, but this entitlement is framework-relative: someone in a radically different framework doesn't share it.

5. I think Wittgenstein's discussion in On Certainty is largely therapeutic — he's not building an epistemology but dissolving a pseudo-problem.

6. I also think there's at least one genuinely universal hinge shared across all functioning human communities — maybe that there's an external world with other minds in it.

7. And I want to say that hinge commitments have a performative dimension: asserting them in the face of doubt isn't reporting knowledge but something closer to taking a stand or making a pledge.

Do these actually cohere? I'm especially worried that (4) and (6) pull in opposite directions — if entitlement is framework-relative, how can there be universal hinges? And does (5) sit badly with (2) and (7), since if it's just therapy why talk about shifting scaffolds and performative stands? But maybe some of these tensions are only apparent. Can you rank them by severity, say what has to give in each case, and tell me which single revision cleans up the most? Don't just list them all as equally bad.
