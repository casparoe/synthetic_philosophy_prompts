I am a software engineer without formal training in philosophy, but after a recent discussion with colleagues about power dynamics in open-source governance I began reading summaries of Hegel’s master–slave dialectic and its emphasis on the struggle for recognition. I am trying to understand why experienced external contributors to large repositories—people who submit high-quality patches for months—sometimes report a sense of invisibility and eventually withdraw, despite the fact that maintainers with merge authority depend on their labor. To think through this, I have sketched four interpretive frameworks and want you to evaluate them.

Please rank the following four approaches from most to least useful for explaining that specific retention failure, and assign each a predictive reliability score between 1 and 10 for forecasting whether a contributor in that position will actually leave within a year. The four are: (1) Kojève’s existentialist reading, in which recognition is a zero-sum contest for pure prestige and the contributor’s failure to become a recognized lord drives withdrawal; (2) Fanon’s postcolonial adaptation, in which misrecognition is structurally embedded in institutional gatekeeping so that merge authority functions as an unexamined racialized or hierarchical norm that systematically devalues the contributor’s presence; (3) a feminist ethics-of-care interpretation that treats recognition as mutual dependency rather than domination, predicting burnout only when the dependency is one-sided and the contributor’s care labor is rendered invisible; and (4) a distributed-systems analogy I have been developing, in which recognition is operationalized as commit-access grants and the dialectic becomes a protocol negotiation over repository governance—meaning withdrawal is not a psychological crisis but a rational exit when the contributor’s node lacks sufficient reputation-weight in the network. For each rank and score, provide one or two sentences explaining whether the framework captures the asymmetry between formal authority (merge rights) and social acknowledgment (attribution, maintainer status, citation in release notes), and note any important limitation that justifies its position.
