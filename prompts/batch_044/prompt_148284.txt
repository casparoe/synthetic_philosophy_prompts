From the science-and-ideas desk: Hilbert’s infinite hotel is usually presented as a thought experiment that makes actual infinity intuitively manageable—a fully occupied infinite hotel can still accommodate a new guest by shifting every current occupant one room up. Georg Cantor’s diagonal argument then demonstrates that infinite sets come in different sizes, a result that seems to treat infinity as a completed totality rather than an open-ended process. Finitists reject that treatment, holding that infinity is always potential, never actual. Consider now a variant—call it the cyclic-index hotel—in which room assignments are generated by a finite procedural protocol that cycles back to its origin after reaching an arbitrarily large but fixed ceiling N, and in which any pairing of guests with rooms must be executed within that same cyclic protocol rather than across a permanently completed list. Write an essay assessing whether this cyclic variant allows a finitist to preserve Hilbert’s intuition about accommodating new guests without conceding Cantor’s claim that some infinite collections are strictly larger than others, or whether the cycling mechanism covertly reinstates the actual infinity it is designed to exclude. Weigh the strongest finitist defense and the strongest Cantorian response, reach a specific verdict, and justify it by identifying the precise point at which the dispute turns on the difference between potential and actual infinity.
