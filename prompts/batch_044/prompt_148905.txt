A colleague has constructed the following argument about conditional updating and now claims that Bayesian rationality undermines itself. (1) In the Judy Benjamin problem, Judy learns the statement that if she is on Blue Team she is in Headquarters, and if she is on Red Team she is in the field. (2) Under standard Jeffrey conditioning, her conditional credence in being in Headquarters given Blue Team should remain equal to her prior, because the update only redistributes credence over the partition {Blue, Red}. (3) If that is correct, then learning any conditional of the form if A then B never provides evidence about B within A-worlds; it only shifts weight between A and not-A. (4) Generalise this result: all conditional testimony—scientific laws, medical prognoses, strategic forecasts—is epistemically inert with respect to the internal structure of the worlds it describes. (5) But this proves too much: it implies that a physician who learns that if the patient has condition C then symptom S will appear should not update her belief that the patient will display S upon subsequently learning C, which renders conditional knowledge useless for practical reasoning. (6) Therefore standard conditioning on conditionals must be rejected, and Judy ought to revise her internal branch-credences after all. I suspect the slip occurs between (2) and (3), perhaps involving an equivocation between updating on a material conditional and updating on a constraint over worlds, but I cannot fix it precisely. Which step should be rejected, and what does that rejection cost—particularly for the coherence of Jeffrey conditioning and for the distinction between learning a conditional and learning its antecedent?
