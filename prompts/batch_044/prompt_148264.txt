People have been arguing for decades about whether consciousness is just a user illusion — like the desktop on your computer — but it feels like nothing ever moves. My working hunch is the disagreement isn't about neuroscience or phenomology at all, but about whether illusion requires an actual misrepresented object (no desktop file behind the icon) or just a useful fiction with no user to be fooled in the first place. A few people I read push it further: they say the user is itself just another representational layer in the predictive hierarchy, so asking who gets fooled is a catagory error. Is that right, or is the stall really hiding an empirical bet about which cortical layers generate the transparency report? I want a real diagnosis — verbal, conceptual, or empirical in disguise — with reasons tied to what each side actually says, not a survey. And be honest: could a sharper distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness actually break the deadlock, or is that just shuffling deck chairs?
