Write the opening remarks—approximately five minutes when delivered—for the first session of an interdisciplinary workshop titled "Ensemble Forecasts and Many-Sided Truth: Anekāntavāda as Epistemic Practice." The audience is split evenly between philosophers of science who specialize in Jain epistemology (specifically syādvāda and the saptabhaṅgī, the sevenfold schema of predication) and climate scientists who work with multi-model ensembles but have no background in Jain thought. Your address must accomplish three things at once. First, explain the saptabhaṅgī without untranslated Sanskrit jargon: show how each of the seven modes ("in some respect it is," "in some respect it is not," "in some respect it is and is not," and the remaining qualified combinations) operates as a non-contradictory claim about a single object, and sharply distinguish this from simple relativism or "agreeing to disagree." Second, map this logical structure onto climate-model practice: use ensemble forecasting—running dozens of parameterizations that yield apparently contradictory projections for the same regional extreme-weather event—to illustrate how contradictory descriptions can all be partially true of the same system when each is indexed to a different conditioning standpoint (spatial resolution, boundary conditions, parameter scheme). Third, use this framing to set up the workshop's central tension without resolving it: some participants want attribution studies to collapse the ensemble into a single "best-estimate" probability for policy communication, while others insist on publishing the full contradictory spread. Frame this disagreement not as a failure of consensus but as an instance of anekāntavāda in practice, and make clear that adopting the Jain framework does not license vagueness or paralysis in decision-making, but rather changes what kind of certainty we claim when we speak for the model. The philosophers in the room should recognize that you are not diluting the doctrine into generic pluralism; the scientists should leave understanding that the seven modes are a logic of partial access, not an excuse for imprecision. Do not provide citations, a bibliography, or suggested readings.
