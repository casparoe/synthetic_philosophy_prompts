I'm trying to lock down my position on predictive processing and the free energy principle before drafting a chapter, and I want an audit of whether these commitments hang together or whether I'm borrowing from incompatible motivations. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) I take the free energy principle to be a normative constraint on any persisting system: maintaining structural integrity over time is formally equivalent to minimizing variational free energy with respect to sensory states, and this applies indiscriminately—cells, brains, and social groups are all instances, not analogies.

(2) For the brain specifically, I read predictive processing literally: cortical hierarchies implement hierarchical Bayesian inference, with predictions flowing downward and precision-weighted prediction errors flowing upward.

(3) I reject any "Cartesian theater" or global workspace reading of consciousness. There is no central place where predictions are displayed to an observer; conscious access is just the local stabilization of a high-level predictive model that suppresses competing error signals in its neighborhood.

(4) I think attention is best explained as precision-weighting—the modulation of synaptic gain on prediction-error units—but I also maintain that precision is never itself represented. There is no meta-level model of uncertainty; the system adjusts gain without encoding "how certain am I?"

(5) I want to treat the Markov blanket separating system from environment as ontologically real, not merely an observer-dependent modeling convenience. The boundary marks a genuine causal-statistical separation that grounds the system's identity.

My questions: Is this package jointly tenable? In particular, (2) seems to require some form of global integration for high-level predictions to constrain lower levels, which (3) denies. Meanwhile, (4) looks unstable with (2): if the brain is literally running Bayesian inference, shouldn't hyperparameters like precision be part of the represented model rather than unrepresented adjustments? And (5) seems to sit uneasily with the anti-dualist spirit of predictive processing, which often treats the system-environment boundary as enacted rather than pre-given. At the same time, (1) and (2) might only appear to conflict—one is universal, the other a specific neural implementation—while (3) and (5) might be reconcilable if local stabilization happens within an objectively bounded system.

Please audit this set. Rank the tensions by severity, distinguish genuine fractures from apparent ones that dissolve on inspection, specify for each real tension what I would have to give up, and identify the least costly revision and what it costs conceptually. Be specific about which commitment pulls against which, and don't treat every pairing as equally problematic.
