hey, quick consistency check for my chapter draft on conceivability as guide to possibility — i feel like my commitments are pulling in different directions but i can't spot which ones are actually incompatible vs just looking like it.

here's my current set:
(i) conceivability only guides possibility after what i call modal stress-testing — you fill out the scenario in detail and check whether it holds together or collapses.
(ii) zombie worlds fail this test: once you specify the full neurophyiscal dynamics, the absence of phenomenal consciousness becomes unstable, so the mere conceivability of zombies doesn't establish metaphysical possibility.
(iii) in pure math, though, i think robust conceivability of an alternative structure (say, non-standard models of arithmetic) counts as preliminary evidence for consistency, even before we've got formal proof — structural relations don't depend on our evolved physical-modal cognition.
(iv) our modal faculties are domain-specific: somewhat reliable for medium-sized phyiscal and social scenarios, but representationally rigid for phenomenal properties and abstracta unless anchored in formalization.
(v) therefore weak conceivability alone never justifies a modal claim, and stress-testing is mandatory — but its epistemic weight depends on whether we're operating in a "native" domain.

the tensions i'm seeing: (ii) vs (iii) looks like selective application — i reject zombies for failing stress-testing but accept math structures that haven't been formally verified either. (iii) vs (iv) also seems bad: if our cognition is unreliable for abstract objects, why should conceivability in math be trustworthy? then there's an apparent clash between (i) and (v) — is stress-testing a universal method or only valid in native domains? — but that might dissolve once you distinguish method from reliability conditions.

can you audit this? rank the real tensions by severity, say what's got to give for each, flag the apparent one, and suggest the least costly revision. no need for citations or a reading list, just the logic. thanks!
