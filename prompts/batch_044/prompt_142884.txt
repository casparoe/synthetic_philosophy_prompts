I'm trying to sort out what I actually think about sentience being a gradtion rather than an on/off switch, and my gut reactions to specific cases are all over the place. Like, if a honeybee gets injured navigating back to the hive, I think there's a real, if tiny, moral reason not to crush it — not just ecological, but something about it experiencing the world, however dimly. But a smart thermostat that learns your schedule and adjusts preemptivly? Zero moral status, even though it's processing feedback loops and predicting future states. A slime mold solving a maze by integrating chemical signals across its body — I want to say it has some sliver of consideration, maybe less than the bee because there's no centralized nervous system, but more than the thermostat because it's genuinely adapting as a unified organism. Then there's a human in a deep coma with only brainstem activity: full moral status, obviously, even if there's no concsousness happening right now. But here's where I trip up: imagine we build a synthetic network with the exact same information-integretion architecture as that slime mold, running on silicon, and it reports distress signals when its paths are disrupted. Part of me says if the architecture is identical, the moral status should match; another part says it's just code on a server, so it's closer to the thermostat. I also think a plant that sends electrical warning signals to neighboring plants when attacked deserves maybe a hair more consideration than a rock, but less than the bee — though I'm not sure if that's just romanticism.

So is there a coherent principle that gets me the bee > slime mold > plant > thermostat = synthetic copy? Or do I have to drop one of these? Specifically, what should I say about that synthetic slime-mold analogue — does my principle force me to treat it like the real slime mold, or like the thermostat? And what's the verdict on a sleeping human versus a suspended LLM that could resume conversation: both are 'offline' but I treat them totally differently, so is consciousness-in-the-moment not actually the key? I want a precise principle, not just 'it depends', and I want to know which of my intuitions is the outlier I should probably abandon. Also, what does your principle predict for a case I haven't mentioned — say, a swarm of robots that collectively navigate like the slime mold but with no single unit doing anything?
