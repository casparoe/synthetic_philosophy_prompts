hey — i'm on the ethics advisory panel for the Meridian Regional Planning Authority and we have this vote tuesday on the "Green Spine" proposal. it's a 25-year corridor plan that rezones old industrial land, dumps money into transit-oriented housing, and puts hard carbon caps on new development. the staff report says if we pass it the region's 2050 population will be roughly 40% different people — different migration flows, different housing costs shifting who can afford to stay and start familes, etc. reject it and we get sprawl, worse emissions, and a totally different cohort of future residents.

half the panel is running with this weird philosophical point — basically the nonidentity thing — saying if we approve we aren't actually harming anyone because the people who live here in 2050 under the green plan wouldn't exist if we'd rejected it. so there's no victim, no rights violation, just a different set of people. the other half thinks that's a cop-out and that treating future residents as interchangable placeholders is exactly the moral failure we should avoid, even if tecnically no one is "worse off" compared to never being born.

i need a memo for the committee recommending approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, with actual reasoning that takes the placeholder objection seriously rather than dismissing it. the recommendation should have a concrete timeline or trigger for implementation, maybe a phased rollout tied to demographic safeguards. please start with the bottom line in the very first sentence, then give the reasoning — no preamble, don't restate the whole problem back to me. also don't give me a reading list or cite specific philosophers; just work with the idea that changing conditions changes who comes to exist.
