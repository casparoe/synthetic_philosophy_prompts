Consider the following argument, which extends the reasoning of the White Horse dialogue (Bai ma fei ma) from the School of Names into a general skeptical claim about compound descriptions.

(1) In the dialogue, "horse" picks out the shape-form (xing) belonging to one kind-actuality, while "white" picks out a color-quality belonging to a different actual-configuration (shi).
(2) If two terms pick out genuinely different actual-configurations, then they do not apply to one and the same object; they apply to distinct "this-es."
(3) Therefore, "white horse" does not apply to a horse but to a white-configuration that is not identical with the horse-configuration.
(4) Generalizing: whenever two predicates correspond to different actual-configurations—whether kind, quality, relation, or location—no compound predicate "P-and-Q" applies to a single object.
(5) Every concrete object instantiates multiple actual-configurations (kind, color, size, position, etc.).
(6) So every compound description ("red apple," "running dog," "tall philosopher") fails to denote any single object; language systematically fragments reality.
(7) Yet we navigate the world successfully using compound descriptions, which suggests they are not systematically false.
(8) Hence the reasoning in (1)–(4) must fail.
