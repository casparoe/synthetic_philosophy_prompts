Rather than asking respondents which philosopher they prefer, I want a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions by the answers they give to concrete diagnostic questions—starting from whether there is any determinate fact about whether gavagai means rabbit, undetached rabbit part, or time-slice of rabbitness given identical behavioral evidence, and branching outward from there. Combinations that appear unoccupied should be flagged with a brief diagnosis of whether they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. I also want you to flag any pair of named positions that the tree's branch questions cannot genuinely separate.

Please deliver this as a structured JSON object with the following schema: root_question (string), branch_nodes (array of objects each containing node_id, question_text, answer_options [array of strings], and child_nodes [array of node_ids or null]), leaves (array of objects each containing leaf_id, position_name, awkward_commitment [string], occupied_status [named, unnamed, or unoccupied]), and unseparated_pairs (array of objects each containing position_a, position_b, and explanation [string]). Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the point is for reasoning to proceed entirely from the conceptual structure of translation, ontology, and meaning.
