I'm writing up my philosophical profile for a graduate seminar on material constitution and would value a consistency check before I present. Here is the package of commitments I've arrived at:

(1) I am an endurantist: material objects are wholly present at each moment of their existence, with no temporal parts spread across time.
(2) I accept a constitution view of the statue and the clay: at a given time they are numerically distinct entities that occupy precisely the same spatiotemporal region, with the statue constituted by the clay but not identical to it.
(3) I take constitution to be a primitive, sui generis metaphysical relation — it is not reducible to part-whole composition, modal coincidence, or functional organisation.
(4) I think there is a kind of "fluid concept" slippage in this microdomain: predicates such as "weighs 200 kg" or "is malleable" slide between applying to the clay and applying to the statue depending on conversational context and salience, though I take this slippage to be purely linguistic and not metaphysically real.
(5) I hold that the only properties that genuinely distinguish the statue from its constituting clay are relational (for instance, standing in an artistic-intention relation to its creator) or modal (for instance, being able to survive reshaping versus being able to survive melting), rather than intrinsic physical properties.
(6) In the gradual-replacement scenario, where an artist substitutes every particle of clay with synthetic polymer over decades while preserving shape and causal history, my verdict is that the statue survives and the original clay is destroyed.
(7) I also maintain that, for an endurantist, an object must share at least some essential constituent or essential property with its earlier stages in order to persist through time; total material replacement without retention of any original constituent therefore destroys the original object and brings a numerically distinct successor into existence.

Please audit whether these hang together. I want the tensions ordered by severity, with the least costly revision specified for each. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension — especially whether (6) can be reconciled with (7), and whether (3) is undermined by (5) — and which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, particularly whether (4) threatens (2). Be specific about what has to give, and do not offer a bibliography or reading list. Please reply in British English.
