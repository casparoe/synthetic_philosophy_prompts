Write a parody Terms of Service agreement, approximately 700 words, formatted as numbered legal clauses, in the deadpan corporate voice of a fictional wellness startup called “Prohairesis Wellness, Inc.” The company offers a subscription-based Stoic mindfulness platform. Your task is to extend the Stoic dichotomy of control—accurately distinguishing what lies within our power (judgments, impulses, assent) from what does not (body, property, reputation, algorithmic outputs, market conditions)—with rigorous internal consistency into every clause of the contract, so that the corporation systematically disclaims moral and legal responsibility for all “external indifferents” while retaining full ownership over the user’s “prohairesis” (faculty of rational choice). Include a clause that classifies data breaches, billing errors, and platform outages as “dispreferred indifferents” that the user must accept with equanimity; a licensing provision treating the user’s judgments as revocable corporate assets; and a “no-branching continuity clause” addressing whether a subscriber’s cultivated Stoic progress survives account termination or upload failure—an invented twist that should be handled with the same deadpan precision as the rest. The satire should target the modern commercial revival of Stoicism—particularly the tendency in contemporary self-help discourse to reduce ethical life to individual emotional management while treating structural and institutional conditions as fixed givens—without ever naming a living figure or breaking character with a wink. The humor must arise from the actual conceptual architecture: correctly reference the Stoic taxonomy of preferred and dispreferred indifferents, avoid inventing false doctrines, and maintain legalistic deadpan so that the absurdity emerges from the logic itself. The piece should be readable to someone with only casual knowledge of Stoicism yet clearly fair and precise to a reader familiar with Epictetus’s framework. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or further reading. Compose the prompt as a formal, polished assignment from a philosophy professor.
