Write a Petrarchan sonnet (octave rhyme scheme ABBA ABBA, sestet CDECDE) that takes as its subject the failure of epistemic closure within Robert Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with the theory recognizes the specific mechanism of failure rather than a generic skeptical scenario.

In the octave, establish the four tracking conditions—truth, belief, sensitivity (if p were false, S would not believe p), and adherence (if p were true in nearby worlds, S would believe p)—as they apply to an ordinary perceptual belief formed by a specific method M (for example, seeing a key in a drawer). Bind the counterfactual conditions together with one rhyme sound and the factual conditions with another, so the rhyme pairs do the work of pairing sensitivity with adherence and truth with belief.

At the volta in line 9, pivot to the entailment q (for instance, "therefore I am not a brain in a vat being deceived"). Here, inject the niche conceptual point that the belief in q is formed not by the original perceptual method M but by a distinct derivational method M*—logical inference from p. The sestet should show that, precisely because the method has shifted, the counterfactual conditions fail for q: in the nearest worlds where q is false (the brain-in-a-vat worlds), the derivation from p still proceeds and S still believes q. Thus closure breaks not because logic is invalid but because tracking is method-relative and the derivational method lacks the world-discriminating power of perception. Keep the sestet formally rhymed, but let its rhyme sounds avoid resolving back into the octave's paired sounds, leaving a structural gap that mirrors the unclosed inference.
