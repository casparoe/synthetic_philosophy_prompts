"Nietzsche's rules in Ecce Homo — no alcohol, light Italian food, eating according to climate — are practical applications of his idea of 'great health.' Because he suffered migraines and digestive problems, he developed these habits to protect his body, which shows that even a weak constitution can be disciplined into strength. Nevertheless, his chronic illnesses ultimately contradict his philosophy: if great health means possessing abundant physical vitality, then his constant suffering proves he never achieved it, and his writings are therefore aspirational fantasies rather than descriptions of a state he knew. His rejection of heavy German cuisine also implies that cultural eating habits shape moral and intellectual strength, so adopting Italian dietary practices is part of realizing the philosophical vitality he praises."
