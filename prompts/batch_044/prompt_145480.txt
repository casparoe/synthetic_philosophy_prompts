People defending the block universe often say spacetime is basically like a finished film reel -- every frame already exists, and 'now' is just whichever frame the projector happens to be shinning on. The Rietdijk-Putnam line uses this kind of picture to argue that since simultaneity is relative, there's no objective present moment, so the future is already 'there' just like distant parts of the reel. Audit this analogy: what exactly maps onto what (frames, projector, audience?), where does the mapping brake once you push it into the Rietdijk-Putnam argument, and does it quietly import determinism or static existence that special relativity alone doesn't actually force? Also, if the reel has no projector -- meaning there's no external time for it to run in -- does the analogy collapse on itself, and what's a better image that keeps four-dimensionality without making tomorrow feel like a prerecorded movie?
