ok so ive been thinking about those arguments where people ask if something is still "itself" after changing a bunch - like the ship of theseus but also like if your best friend gets amnesia are they still your best friend. i keep seeing this wierd pattern and idk if it has a name.

basically people seem to have two ways of deciding "same thing or not." way one is like checking if theres one contiuous line through time - even if every part changed, if it never got fully destroyed and replaced its still it. way two is like checking if the important pieces stayed - like the brain, or the frame, or the core memory or whatever.

but heres the wierd part i keep noticing: people dont pick one and stick with it. they use way one to defend cases where way two says its different, then switch to way two to defend cases where way one says its different. like with the ship where every plank gets replaced - they say "its still the same ship cause the repair was contiuous, never destroyed." ok. but then with a teleporter that kills you and rebuilds you exactly on mars - they say "no thats not you cause your original body was destroyed, even if every atom is copied perfect." wait thats way two now? they just switched rules. and if you call them out they say "no im just being nuanced about context."

so is there an established term for this specific move? not the theories themselves - i know philosophers have names for the "unbroken line" view vs the "check the parts" view - but the rhetorical pattern where both are kept around like escape hatches so neither can ever be falsified. does that have a concept, and if not, how would you draw the line between that and someone who genuinely thinks both rules work together in one principled system? also like, is this just what philosophers call equivocation or is it something else?
