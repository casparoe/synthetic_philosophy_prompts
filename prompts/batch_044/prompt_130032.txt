I am trying to prepare a precise response for the discussion that does not simply repeat textbook summaries, so I need to disentangle exactly what that slogan is doing in this inference.

Could you lay out the distinct precise theses esse est percipi might express, arranged from the near-trivial to the radically metaphysical? For each reading, specify whether it turns on actual human perception, divine or continuous mental perception, sensible qualities alone, or entire material substances; whether it is epistemic (about what we can know), ontological (about what can exist), or semantic (about the meaning of existence); and whether it genuinely follows from, or is supported by, Berkeley's master argument. Then indicate which of these readings, if any, actually licenses the blockchain inference my colleague made, and flag any reading that sounds plausible only because it silently imports modern assumptions about data storage, distributed ledgers, or information retrieval that Berkeley's own framework does not contain.
