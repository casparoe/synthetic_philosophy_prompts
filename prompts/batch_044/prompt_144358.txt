Write a short story about a technician named Mara who runs the Gradient Replica booths at a memory-archive clinic. Its not the classic teletransporter—Parfit's destroy-and-rebuild thing—its a slow dilution machine. The original stays alive but thier emotional weights get scaled down over thirty days, while a copy wakes up at full intensity with the same memories. Patients come in to 'survive' death by booking a copy, but Mara notices the diluted originals still insist theyre the real ones because narrative continuity feels intact even when affect is thinned. One day a philosopher-patient asks her to run the dilution on him without making the copy—just to see if survival without intensity is still survival. Write the scene where she has to decide whether to start the protocol, and have her think through whether what matters is psychological continuity, the feeling of being continuous, or some weird in-between thing like 'weighted presence' that neither side can fully claim. Keep it grounded in the room, not too abstract—show the dials, the hum, maybe the smell of ozone. And dont make the answer obvious.
