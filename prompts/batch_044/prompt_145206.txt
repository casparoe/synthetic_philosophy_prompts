After a long and somewhat exhausting argument with a friend about whether Kant's essay on perpetual peace offers a genuine institutional blueprint or merely a regulative fiction that masks the permanence of state conflict, I want to map exactly where our disagreement lives — not in terms of which scholars we cite, but in terms of the commitments we actually hold when pressed on concrete cases. Please construct a decision tree or taxonomy of interpretive positions on Kant's perpetual peace that sorts a reader by what they believe rather than by the label they prefer.

The root commitment is: I accept that Kant distinguishes preliminary articles (ending hostilities, reducing standing armies) from definitive articles (republican constitutions, a federation of free states, cosmopolitan hospitality). From there, build the tree from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of named interpretations. Each internal node must be a concrete scenario or a precise claim on which interpretations genuinely diverge, phrased so that someone can answer it without already knowing which interpretation it points to. Use at least five branch nodes, yielding between eight and ten leaves.

The diagnostic scenarios must include the following distinctions:
1. A democratic republic faces an existential threat from an autocratic neighbor. Is preemptive intervention to force republican reform permissible if it is the only path to joining the federation?
2. Commercial interdependence clearly reduces the incentive for war, but it requires global supply chains that erode local sovereignty and create new dependencies. Does this count as Kant's mechanism of nature guaranteeing peace?
3. The federation of states develops a permanent arbitration court with genuinely binding decisions over members. Has it thereby crossed the line into the world-state that Kant rejects, or is this the legitimate form of the negative substitute?
4. Climate displacement makes a region permanently uninhabitable. Does cosmopolitan hospitality extend only to temporary sojourn, or does it generate positive obligations of admission and resettlement?
5. A leader knows that full transparency about peace negotiations will cause them to collapse, and that collapse will prolong a devastating war. Is strategic deception justified if it serves the definitive articles?

For the logical space: present the combinations of answers these five nodes allow, then locate named or unnamed positions in that space. Required leaves must include: the strict republican-institutionalist; the voluntary-league mechanist; the cosmopolitan-expansionist; the negative-substitute sovereigntist; and the regulative-skeptic who treats the guarantee as non-empirical. You must also inject and occupy one niche distinction not usually found in standard textbooks: ask whether the guarantee should be read empirically, transcendentally, or performatively (as an act of practical reason that constitutes the conditions it describes), and place the resulting interpretation in the tree. Where a combination of answers yields no defensible position, label it unoccupied and diagnose briefly whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Where the tree genuinely cannot separate two positions — for instance, if two interpretations converge on identical answers to all five scenarios despite different theoretical groundings — flag the pair explicitly rather than inventing a spurious distinction.

Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the instrument should be answerable by reasoning about the cases alone. Do not use verbatim quotations from Kant's text: paraphrase any concepts you reference. Keep the deliverable formal, self-contained, and designed so that someone without web access can construct the taxonomy from philosophical reasoning alone.
