I'm in a philosophy of mind programme and we've been going through Parfit's teletransporter stuff for weeks. There's this thing I keep seeing in seminar that I don't know if it has a name, and it's definately not just plain equivocation. Basically, people are mostly okay with branching: if my brain gets copied into two seperate bodies, I survive as both, because psychological continuity is what matters, not being one specific physical person. But then I ask about the reverse — two people whose minds get gradualy merged into one body over time, so the result has memories and traits from both. Suddenly everyone gets strict and says neither original person survives; it's just a brand new individual. But both cases break the one-to-one link between original mind and future body. In one case you get two survivors, in the other apparently zero. Is there an established concept for this asymmetry — accepting fission but rejecting fusion — or is it just an inconsistency people don't notice? How does it relate to Parfit's claim that identity isn't what matters, since in fusion it seems like people suddenly care about being exactly one specific person? I don't want citations or a reading list, just whether there's a term for this specific mismatch, and if not, how you'd define it so it's not just motte-and-bailey with identity talk.
