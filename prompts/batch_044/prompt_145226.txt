I am writing a historical novel set in first-century Judea, and for every scene or line of dialogue I must decide whether a gospel detail is historically plausible enough to present without framing it as authorial invention. Write a reusable decision procedure — an ordered checklist I can run alone in under ten minutes — that translates the standard criteria of authenticity into practical steps, with explicit stopping points, conflict-resolution rules for when criteria point in opposite directions, and clear flags marking which steps are judgment calls disguised as rules. The procedure should include a specific friction test I have devised: determine whether the detail generates narrative tension that serves no obvious theological or apologetic purpose for its source, and whether it survives a counterfactual in which the author sought to make Jesus appear more conventionally pious or politically unthreatening. Apply it to three details: the temple action, attested across all four narratives but with divergent settings and motives; the birth narratives with angelic announcements and genealogies, present in only two sources and tightly woven into theological claims; and the reported cry of dereliction at the crucifixion, in which Jesus asks why he has been forsaken, a moment that is theologically awkward but emotionally powerful. Include one case where the procedure classifies a resonant, dramatically useful detail as likely invented — an outcome I will not want — and conclude by stating precisely what this checklist can never establish, such as whether Jesus was divine or whether any particular event actually happened. Note that I face editorial deadlines and must justify each decision in a brief author's note.
