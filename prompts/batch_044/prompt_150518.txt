Write the opening remarks—about twelve minutes—for a retirement workshop honoring Dr. Elena Vasquez, a philosopher of logic and epistemology. The audience is divided: half are specialists in formal epistemology and decision theory for whom backward induction is familiar territory; the other half are family, university staff, and former non-philosophy students who have never encountered the paradox. The remarks should treat the surprise exam paradox as the structural backbone, not as decoration: the teacher's announcement corresponds to Vasquez's public commitment to a particular pedagogical standard; the day-by-day elimination of possibilities mirrors the phases of her career; the hidden assumption that students share common knowledge of the teacher's rationality should parallel an unstated condition in her legacy; and the actual moment of surprise should map onto an event in her career that no one anticipated despite all the reasoning. To keep the idea from being a well-worn classroom example, build the speech around a specific variant: on Tuesday the teacher announces that there will be a surprise exam on one of the remaining days—Wednesday, Thursday, or Friday—but adds a secondary clause that the exam will not fall on any day immediately following a day on which every remaining student has publicly predicted its absence. This blocks the backward induction not by denying rationality but by undermining the transparency condition required for the common-knowledge chain. The expert half should recognize that you are using this clause-variant to structure what can and cannot be said about her work; the family half should be able to follow the emotional arc—the promise, the reasoning away of expectation, the unexpected arrival—without knowing any formal literature. Do not cite authors, years, or paper titles; do not provide a bibliography or reading list; and do not name the paradox until the final minute. The tone should be that of a toast that happens to be philosophically rigorous, not a lecture that happens to be warm.
