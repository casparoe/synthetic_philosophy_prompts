Serving on the allocation committee of the Meridian Relief Fund has recently forced me to confront a conflict I had previously treated as abstract: the tension between compatriot loyalty and cosmopolitan impartiality is now immediate, because I was born in the region facing moderate flooding and have lived for the past decade in the distant region facing catastrophic drought, giving me direct family ties to both affected populations.

Our charter was amended last year to include a post-national reciprocity clause that treats nationality as morally arbitrary in distribution decisions, yet our founding deed still imposes a duty of proximal stewardship toward the donor communities that sustain the fund. We must decide by Friday whether to release two million dollars in emergency reserves to the nearby domestic flood zone—where infrastructure damage is serious but mortality is low and state aid is already flowing—or to the distant drought zone, where state capacity has collapsed, mortality is rising, and our contribution could prevent an estimated four hundred deaths, but where no donor community resides. Our legal counsel warns that directing the majority abroad may violate our fiduciary obligations under the founding deed; our ethics advisor contends that the reciprocity clause supersedes geographic loyalty.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. Recommend a concrete course of action—full domestic allocation, full foreign allocation, or a defined split with enforceable conditions—and specify a clear disbursement time frame. The reasoning should treat the philosophical disagreement seriously: engage the strongest argument for partiality toward compatriots and the strongest argument for impartial cosmopolitan distribution, then explain why the institutional obligation we face resolves in the direction you recommend. Avoid bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I need defensible reasoning for the room, not references.
