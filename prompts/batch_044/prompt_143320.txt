I've been trying to formalize the dogmatism paradox for a workshop and keep arriving at a conclusion I don't want to accept: that rational belief is necessarily dogmatic unless we abandon logical closure. Here is the chain as I have it.

(1) Rational justification is closed under known logical entailment: if S justifiably believes p and S knows that p entails q, then S justifiably believes q.
(2) Knowing an entailment requires only logical competence; it does not require empirical investigation specific to q.
(3) So a rational agent can acquire justified belief in arbitrarily remote conclusions—about distant future events, other minds, or unobservable domains—without ever examining direct evidence for those conclusions. Call this "justificatory teletransport."
(4) Add the principle of epistemic inertia: a belief should be revised only when evidence specifically targets it or its immediate evidential base, not when evidence targets a remote entailment that depends on it.
(5) By (3) and (4), beliefs acquired via teletransport become empirically unfalsifiable for the agent. Any counterevidence can be deflected onto the base belief or onto the logical laws, both of which the agent has independent reason to preserve.
(6) Therefore closure generates a class of rational beliefs that are immune to revision—dogmatic in the precise sense that no future experience could dislodge them.
(7) But closure seems necessary for rational coherence: without it, an agent could rationally believe p and also rationally reject every consequence of p, which fragments belief.
(8) Hence either closure fails (costing coherence) or inertia fails (costing the idea that belief revision should be locally targeted), and either way rational belief turns out to be structurally dogmatic.

I want to keep (1) and (4) if I can; giving up closure feels like surrendering the unity of reason, but giving up inertia seems to let every belief be undermined by distant cosmological data. Which step should I reject, and what exactly does rejecting it cost? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
