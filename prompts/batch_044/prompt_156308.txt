here's where i land:

1. a stranger saves someone from drowning at serious risk to thier own life — clearly supererogatory, the classic case.
2. a parent works two jobs so their kid can go to school — not supererogatory, just parental duty, even though it's brutal.
3. donating 10% of income to effective charities — right at the boundary. praiseworthy, but i feel like it's close to what decency requires, not clearly "above and beyond."
4. donating 50% and living frugally — that extra chunk feels genuinely supererogatory.
5. a wealthy person anonymously funds vaccines for a village with basically no personal risk — i want to call it supererogatory, but it's weird because there's no cost. if they do the exact same thing loudly for reputation, it feels less so, even with identical outcomes.
6. someone accidentally causes a crash with no negligence, then spends years doing dangerous rescue work far beyond compensation — the excess part feels supererogatory, but part of me thinks it might just be extended duty.
7. refusing to lie to save your career when the lie hurts no one — required, not supererogatory. but refusing to lie when it saves a life? also required. so where's the line?

i also keep thinking about this distinction i made up between "heroic" one-off acts and "maintenance supererogation" — like consistently doing small costly good things over decades. i think both should count, but i'm way more sure about the heroic ones.

is there one principle that captures all this? maybe something about cost-relative-to-duty? but then case 5 breaks it because cost is near zero. or maybe it's about exceeding a socially defined baseline? but that feels too relativistic. which of my verdicts do i have to drop to get coherence?
