There's a weird kind of double standard in how central banks treat unrealistic models. The Fed and ECB still lean on DSGE frameworks for macro-economc forecasting — the ones built around a single representative agent, rational expections, and markets that clear unless you manually bolt on a friction. Everyone in the room knows those premises are literally false; they don't defend them as approximations that will eventually be replaced, but in a kind of as-if spirit straight out of Friedmans F-twist. Yet when the same institutions issue guidance on climate risk or pandemic preparedness, they suddenly demand mechanistic realism and reject simplifications that don't map onto actual processes. So what philosophical commitments about explanation and prediction does the macro-modeling practice actually presuppose? Is it genuinely instrumentalist — that false assumptions are fine if they yield decent forecasts — or does it secretly depend on a realist claim that the false parts isolate some deeper causal structure? And is the institution being philosophically incoherent, or is there a principled reason economics gets to play by different epistemic rules than climate science?
