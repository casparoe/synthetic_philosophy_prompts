As a journalist covering science and ideas, I am developing a feature that uses sustained irony to examine whether metaphysical doctrines can survive institutionalization. Please compose a deadpan travel-guide entry, in the precise style of a Lonely Planet page, for a fictional sovereign state—the Republic of Anicca—that has enshrined the Theravada Buddhist doctrine of anatta, the radical impermanence of a self composed solely of the five aggregates (rupa, vedana, sanna, sankhara, vinnana), as the constitutional basis of its civil administration and legal personhood. The entry should guide visitors through the practical absurdities that result: border controls that verify travelers by the coherence of their present aggregate-configuration rather than biometric continuity; the National Registry of Impermanent Aggregates, which issues temporary civic identifiers that automatically expire with each substantial mental formation; local etiquette requiring that strangers be addressed by their currently dominant skandha rather than by given name; and a sidebar describing the annual Festival of Non-Attachment, during which citizens ritually dissolve their registry entries in a public ceremony. The satire should run to approximately 650 words, maintain an unwavering deadpan voice, and derive its humor entirely from exaggerating real philosophical tensions—between momentariness and legal responsibility, between rebirth without a transmigrating soul and the continuity required for contracts or punishment, and between the denial of a persistent ego and the bureaucratic necessity of a rights-bearing subject—without ever breaking character to announce them. Include one invented quotation from a mid-level registry official that unwittingly exposes the paradox of administering a state that denies the continuity of the administrator. The piece should be fully intelligible to a reader acquainted with analytic philosophy of personal identity and Buddhist metaphysics, but it should depend on no obscure secondary scholarship; do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
