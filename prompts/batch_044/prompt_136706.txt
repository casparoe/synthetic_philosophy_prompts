Write a short story, composed in British English, that dramatises the conceptual tension between absurdism and contemporary naturalistic accounts of meaning—particularly the view that meaning consists in fitting labour directed at objectively valuable ends—through the experience of a solitary archivist maintaining a decommissioned lunar repository containing only treatises that deny the possibility of meaning. Rather than presenting a philosophical dialogue, embed the conflict in the protagonist's sensory environment, daily rituals, and interaction with an autonomous preservation system that has evolved an eccentric, self-consistent theory of value. The narrative should centre on a decisive moment in which the archivist discovers a corrupted manuscript that seems to argue precisely for the cosmic meaninglessness she has come to accept, and must choose whether to restore it—thereby sustaining her labour—or to permit its erasure. Do not cite philosophers, texts, or bibliographies, and do not provide an explicit theoretical resolution; instead, render the ideas through concrete detail, atmospheric description, and narrative consequence. The story should be approximately 800 to 1000 words, written in a formal literary register, and should close on an ambiguity that leaves open whether her persistence constitutes genuine meaning or merely an unexamined habit.
