Design a macroscopic thought experiment involving a purely mechanical Maxwell's demon that sorts gas molecules between two chambers using classical sensors and a physical memory register. Engineer the case so that the demon has perfect accuracy in measuring each molecule's velocity, yet suffers from logical-induction-style uncertainty: it assigns credences to self-referential predictions about its own future sorting efficiency, and these credences need not converge to 0 or 1 because the predictions depend on the demon's own subsequent reasoning states. The scenario must be constructed so that the standard information-theoretic resolution—whereby any net reduction in physical entropy must be compensated by an irreversible increase in the demon's memory-entropy—remains fully in force, while a purely epistemic resolution—claiming the paradox dissolves once we acknowledge the demon's limited knowledge of particle velocities—cannot by itself explain the residual thermodynamic cost. Constraints: one paragraph, no quantum mechanics, no perfect predictors or exotic physics, realistic macroscopic technology, and payoffs or stakes that do not depend on science-fictional devices. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that it meets each specification: state the verdict of the information-theoretic view and why, the verdict of the epistemic view and why, and identify the exact structural feature that forces the two explanations apart rather than letting them collapse into one. Then specify which feature is doing the work—the separation between flawless physical measurement and bounded logical inference capacity—and explain what happens to the case if that feature is removed, for instance by granting the demon complete deductive closure over its measurements. Finish by showing how a defender of the pure epistemic resolution would attack the thought experiment, and assess whether that attack succeeds without assuming the very point at issue.
