I'm taking a course on value theory and we got onto this weird view, tranquilism — only the absence of craving and suffering matters, and pleasure or fulfillment aren't positively good, just neutral. My freind tried to prove it's self-defeating with this chain, and I can't tell if he's actually caught something or if he's smuggling in somthing.

(1) Tranquilism says intrinsic value attaches only to the absence of craving and suffering; any state lacking both is maximally good, regardless of what's positively present. (2) Take a subject S who is currently in a tranquil state — no craving, no suffering. By (1), S's welfare is already at the ceiling. (3) Now imagine S never existed at all. There's no subject, hence no craving and no suffering. So the world-state "S does not exist" also contains zero craving and zero suffering, just without a bearer. (4) If welfare is just the absence of those two things, then S's welfare level in non-existence must be identical to S's welfare in tranquil existence — both hit the ceiling, only in one case there's nobody there to enjoy it (not that enjoyment matters). (5) But non-existence also eliminates all risk that S will later fall out of tranquility and experience craving. So from a risk-weighted tranquilist calculus, non-existence is strictly better: same ceiling, zero downside risk. (6) Therefore, for every being B, tranquilism implies that B's non-existence is at least as good as B's tranquil existence, and usually better. (7) Generalizing: the best world on tranquilist grounds is one with no beings at all, since that guarantees universal absence of craving and suffering with no risk of recurrence. But recommending universal annihilation as an ideal is absurd.

He says step (4) is where the slide happens, but I think the problem might be in (5) mixing risk with a view that doesn't care about future goods? Or maybe (1) is wrong about neutral positives? I'm not sure. Which premise or inference should I reject, and what's the price of giving it up? Also please push back if I'm misrepresenting tranquilism or if the argument actually works and I'm just squeamish about the conclusion — I want the real diagnosis, not a polite rescue. How confident are you in your answer, and where do you think the reasoning is most fragile?
