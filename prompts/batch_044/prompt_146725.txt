I work as a verification engineer for safety-critical autonomous systems, and I am attempting to design a knowledge-audit protocol that our certification team can run without requiring philosophical training. The protocol needs to be grounded in Nozick's tracking conditions for knowledge—specifically the sensitivity and adherence requirements that separate tracked true belief from accidental correctness—while confronting the standard tension with epistemic closure: a reasoning module may track a base premise P yet fail to track an entailed conclusion Q, because the nearby worlds in which Q is false are not identical to those in which P is false. Rather than resolving this tension theoretically, I need an operational decision procedure that translates tracking into executable audit steps.

Please provide a step-by-step checklist my team can apply during module certification to determine whether a derived claim C counts as tracked knowledge given that the system tracks its premises. The procedure must include: (1) a practical method for constructing the relevant modal neighborhood around C using only counterfactual test-generation that our engineers can actually execute—without full possible-world semantics—specifying how many counterfactual branches are required and what counts as a nearby world in software terms; (2) an explicit test for what I will call entailment leakage, where adherence to the premises fails to transfer adherence to the conclusion, with a clear stopping rule that flags the failure; (3) a designated point at which the audit must halt and hand the decision to a human reviewer because the question involves values or irreducible heuristic judgment rather than deterministic verification; and (4) instructions for the case where sensitivity holds for P but adherence fails specifically for the entailment P→Q, including what certification status that combination should receive. Apply the procedure to two concrete scenarios: first, a navigation module that tracks the primary GPS signal is authentic and derives the vehicle is not being actively spoofed; second, a medical-diagnostic module that tracks all input sensors report values within nominal ranges and derives no critical fault condition is present. In at least one scenario, the procedure should produce a result that violates intuitive epistemic closure—state that result precisely and specify the manual override required. Throughout, distinguish steps that are mechanically executable from those that remain judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
