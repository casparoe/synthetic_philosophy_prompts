You serve as senior counsel to a joint parliamentary committee reviewing the constitutional status of the Crown in a Westminster-derived democracy that retains a hereditary monarch. The government proposes to codify a statutory "duty to assent" that would eliminate any discretionary refusal of royal assent, converting the monarch's role into a purely mechanical one. Your committee must recommend whether to adopt this statute or to preserve the current conventional ambiguity, in which the reserve power technically exists but is governed by unwritten norms. In drafting your memo, address the following: first, compare the Scandinavian model of strictly ceremonial monarchy—where refusal is constitutionally unthinkable—with the UK model of unwritten conventions that preserve a theoretical but dormant reserve power; second, assess whether Japan's post-war constitutional framework, in which the Emperor is designated "the symbol of the State and of the unity of the people" and exercises only ceremonial functions, offers a viable intermediate template; third, analyze the counter-majoritarian dilemma: if assent is made automatic, is there any remaining institutional brake on majoritarian overreach, and if ambiguity is retained, does the existence of an unelected check undermine democratic legitimacy regardless of how rarely it is exercised? The memo should reach a clear recommendation, provide reasoning accessible to legislators without legal specialization, and include an honest, substantive treatment of the strongest argument for the position you reject. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. The recommendation will be read by members who must vote on the statute within the week.
