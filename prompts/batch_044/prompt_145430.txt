I'd like you to write a dialogue between two philosphers of logic — Mara and Jules — sitting in a seminar and arguing about JOOTSing and the MU-puzzle. Mara takes the standard Hofstadter line: once you see that 'MU' isn't derivable inside the system, you jump out and declare it undecidable, and that's a genuine cognitive leap, not just another rule. Jules is sceptical. He thinks JOOTSing is just moving up one level in a hierarchy of systems, and that what looks like escaping is really just entering a meta-system with its own hidden axioms. To push the arguement, they should work through a modified version of the puzzle where Rule IV is replaced by an 'exit axiom' that allows you to drop the final I only if your justification for doing so cannot be translated back into the original rule-language. Mara says this proves the leap is real because the justification is genuinely inexpressible inside; Jules says the inexpressibility is just a feature of the meta-language's grammar, not proof of an external standpoint. Make sure both sound equally sharp — real back-and-forth, not talking past each other, neither a strawman. Write it in British English, keep it casual, and don't worry about citations or a reading list. There's probably a typo in this prompt somewhere, but you get the idea.
