I'm a high school student putting together a position paper for my philosophy of mind seminar, and I want to know whether the views I've assembled actually cohere before I defend them. Here is the package I've arrived at, largely from thinking through the color phi phenomenon and what it implies about the timing of experience.

1. I take color phi as evidence for postdictive construction: the brain receives the red flash, then the green flash, and only then assembles an intermediate experience of a yellow dot in motion. This means experience itself—not just our memory of it—is continuously airbrushed in real time. I call this a "Stalinesque" revision, because the present display is rewritten, not merely the archive.

2. By contrast, I think an "Orwellian" revision only alters records, texts, or traces after the fact. If a future historian deletes a reference, the people who originally lived through the event do not have their past experience changed; only our access to it is corrupted. So I maintain that Stalinesque revision has ontological weight for present consciousness, while Orwellian revision is purely epistemic and backward-looking.

3. I also think phenomenal consciousness is unitary and narrative: there must be a single, continuous "now" that belongs to me. If Stalinesque airbrushing is happening constantly within every perceptual window, then my sense of continuity is not tracking a stable stream but is itself reconstructed frame by frame. I actually accept this—my self is an illusion built on overwritten frames—but I want to keep the idea that there is a genuine phenomenal "present" being overwritten, not just a disjointed series.

4. At the same time, I want to defend a specific claim about the timing of experience: consciousness operates over a temporal window (roughly a few hundred milliseconds), and revision can occur only within that window, never across its boundary. So there is no precise instant that gets retroactively altered from outside; the window itself is the indivisible unit of revision.

5. Finally, I am a physicalist. But I think postdictive construction creates a mismatch: the physical neural event that triggers the experience is not simultaneous with the experience's own temporal location. This makes first-person reports about "when" something happened unreliable, even though the underlying process is entirely physical.

Do these hang together? In particular: does my acceptance of continuous Stalinesque revision in (1) and (3) conflict with my window-boundary claim in (4), which seems to say that revision never crosses a line? And does my physicalism in (5) sit badly with my claim in (2) that Stalinesque revision changes the ontology of present experience—am I implicitly treating the "present display" as something non-physical that gets rewritten? Also, is there a hidden tension between my unitary-narrative requirement in (3) and my acceptance of frame-by-frame overwriting?

Please go through the pairs in order of severity, flag any tension that dissolves on inspection, and tell me which single revision—dropping the window boundary, weakening the ontology/epistemology distinction, or adjusting my physicalism—would clean up the most without forcing me to abandon the core idea that color phi reveals something real about how experience is assembled.
