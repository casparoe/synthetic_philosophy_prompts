Please explain the conceptual relationship between pragmatic encroachment and epistemic contextualism, focusing specifically on the tension that arises when both doctrines are held simultaneously. Pragmatic encroachment maintains that whether a subject S knows that p can vary with the practical stakes S faces in acting on p; epistemic contextualism maintains that the truth-conditions of an attributor's claim that S knows p shift with the conversational context, particularly with the salience of error-possibilities. If both are correct, a single knowledge-ascription appears to be governed by two independent contextual mechanisms—one anchored to the subject's practical situation, the other to the attributor's discursive environment. Can these mechanisms be kept conceptually separate, for example by assigning stakes to the subject's epistemic context and salience to the attributor's conversational context? Or do they collapse into a single contextual variable? In particular, consider a scenario in which S faces extremely high practical stakes but the attributor is embedded in a casual, low-salience conversational setting: does pragmatic encroachment imply that S's knowledge-status is affected regardless of what the attributor is positioned to truly assert? And if stakes operate independently of the attributor's context, does this show that pragmatic encroachment is not merely a special case of contextualism? Conversely, if stakes are themselves evaluated relative to the attributor's frame, does that undermine the claim that practical importance affects knowledge directly rather than through conversational norms? Your explanation should determine whether combining the two views yields redundancy, instability, or genuine complementarity, and it should be precise enough to expose the underlying mechanics without appealing to any particular named formulation in the technical literature.
