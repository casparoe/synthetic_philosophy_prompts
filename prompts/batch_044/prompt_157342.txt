As a retired physicist recently turned to philosophy, I seek a consistency audit of my Stoic commitments before presenting them informally. I hold that (1) the classical dichotomy of control is correct—only present judgments and volitions are genuinely eph' hēmin, while health, reputation, and outcomes are not; (2) modern cognitive-therapeutic revivals empirically validate Stoicism, since emotional distress tracks evaluative judgment rather than external events; (3) every judgment is causally determined by neurobiological and environmental antecedents without agent-causal exception; (4) Stoic virtue requires only normative ownership—the reflective capacity to endorse or revise judgments—not causal independence from prior conditions; and (5) no metaphysically significant boundary separates 'internal' from 'external' causation, as neural events are as externally determined as weather, with cranial location carrying no normative weight. I also work with a self-coined distinction, 'epistemic sovereignty': direct first-person access to a judgment's content does not entail causal autonomy over its origin. Audit this set—identify genuine conflicts versus merely apparent ones, rank severity, specify the cheapest revision for each, and name the single change that resolves the most—responding as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
