I'm on an audit team for a university research board and every other proposal now has some kind of self-referential oversight loop -- committees that rewrite their own rules, AI boards appointed by the system they watch, that kind of thing. I need a reusable checklist, grounded in the idea of tangled hierarchies and inviolate levels (like Escher's Drawing Hands), for deciding when a loop is just recursive and when it's actually collapsed levels that makes the oversight meaningless. The checklist should run in order: first check for a ground level, then check for termination conditions, then check for circular dependency in authority. For each step say what counts as a pass, where we hard-stop with reject, and where we have to hand it to a human because it's values not logic. Then run it on three cases: an IRB that can amend its own charter by vote; an AI governance board whose members are selected by the model being governed; and a grant funder that updates it's evaluation criteria during the review cycle. One of these should come out okay by the procedure even though it smells wrong -- say which. Be definite about where the line is, dont just map the options. American English please, and dont worry about being too polished.
