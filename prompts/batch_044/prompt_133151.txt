I'm preparing remarks for a moral philosophy seminar on standing to blame and hypocrisy, and I want an audit of my position before I defend it.

(1) Hypocrisy undermines standing to blame only when the blamer's own fault is of the same kind and at least as severe as the fault being blamed; faults that are categorically different or clearly less severe are irrelevant to standing, though they may diminish moral authority.

(2) In cases of institutional or structural wrong, standing is distributed across the affected population rather than restricted to direct victims: anyone within that population can blame the institution, provided they are not actively complicit in the specific mechanism causing harm.

(3) Standing and moral authority are fully separable; one can retain complete standing while possessing no authority, and one can have authority without possessing standing.

(4) Applying this: a middle-class consumer who occasionally buys fast fashion has standing to blame a multinational clothing firm for labor exploitation, since individual consumption is categorically different and less severe than systemic exploitation; but if she publicly shames the firm primarily to deflect attention from her own large carbon footprint, she retains standing—the faults differ in kind—while clearly having lost authority.

(5) A sincere act of blame requires that the blamer be genuinely committed to avoiding the wrong in her own conduct; without that commitment, the utterance is merely performative and does not count as real blame.

Do these five commitments form a jointly tenable package? For each pair, say whether the tension is genuine or only apparent, rank the real tensions by severity, and specify the least costly revision for each. If I should make exactly one change to clean up the most conflict, which commitment should I revise and what does that cost me? Please do not treat all tensions as equally serious.
