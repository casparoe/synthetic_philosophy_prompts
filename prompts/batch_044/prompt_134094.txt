I'm working out my views on the physics of information for a philosophy of science discussion group and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is where I've landed:

(1) The resolution of Maxwell's demon is essentially informational-thermodynamic. The demon can sort molecules and reduce local entropy only temporarily, because its finite memory must eventually be erased; Landauer's principle ensures that the heat dissipated in erasing those records compensates exactly for the local gain, so the second law holds globally.

(2) Information is physically instantiated—every bit requires a substrate—but it is not ontologically reducible to physics. Informational relations have autonomous constraining power over physical dynamics, comparable to the way software constrains hardware without being identical to electrical states.

(3) The second law of thermodynamics is not a fundamental dynamical law. It is an emergent statistical regularity that derives entirely from the low-entropy boundary condition of the universe; local violations are physically permitted and do not require any special informational or mechanical justification.

(4) Human cognition operates analogously to a Maxwellian demon: it sorts possibilities and channels energy into low-entropy configurations such as buildings, texts, and social structures. However, mental representation is analog and continuous rather than discrete and digital, so it is not subject to Landauer's erasure principle and does not pay the corresponding thermodynamic penalty.

(5) The best explanation for the universe's initial low-entropy state is a cosmological selection effect in a multiverse with heterogeneous boundary conditions, rather than a law-like constraint or an informational principle.

My question is whether this package is jointly tenable. Please audit it systematically: identify which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension and which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection. For each real tension, specify its severity, explain exactly which commitments pull in opposite directions, and say what the least costly revision would be and what it costs. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious. Do not ask which commitment I should drop—just lay out the conflicts and the price of fixing each—so I can see whether I'm smuggling in a hidden assumption, especially around the analog/digital distinction in (4) and the status of informational autonomy in (2). Be specific about whether (1) and (3) can both be true without overprotecting an emergent regularity, and whether (4) can claim exemption from Landauer's limit without undermining the physical embodiment required by (2).
