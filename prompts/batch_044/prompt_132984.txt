Please explain the conceptual issue that arises when Machiavelli’s account of fortune and virtù is read not as a static opposition between chance and skill, but as a dynamic system with feedback effects. Specifically, address the following interrelated problems. Machiavelli suggests that fortune governs roughly half of human affairs, yet he also insists that a prince endowed with virtù can anticipate and redirect her movements. This raises a tension: if virtù successfully shapes outcomes, does it actually reduce fortune’s proportional share over time, or does fortune’s domain remain invariant, with virtù merely reallocating results within the fixed half? Next, examine whether Machiavelli treats fortune primarily as an epistemic limitation—political events that exceed human foresight—or as an ontological feature of the world that persists even under perfect prediction. Finally, discuss whether institutional embodiments of virtù, such as well-ordered laws, disciplined armies, and civic religion, operate differently from the personal virtù of an individual prince in this dynamic. Do collective institutions genuinely dampen variability for the state, or do they merely displace risk onto other domains, such as external enemies or internal social conflict, leaving the total exposure to fortune unchanged?
