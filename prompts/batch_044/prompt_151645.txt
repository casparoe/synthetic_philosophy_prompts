I cover science and ideas for a long-form publication, and I am preparing to interview an unnamed philosopher who has recently advanced a reconstructed account of Jain anekāntavāda as a normative framework for scientific consensus-building rather than as religious metaphysics. On her view, every empirical claim must be indexed to a specific standpoint of inquiry—an adaptation of classical siddhānta-bheda—and persistent conflicts among climate models, psychiatric taxonomies, or evolutionary classifications should not always be resolved into a single correct account because the predicates in question are standpoint-relative. She calls this position many-sided epistemology, distinguishes it from simple relativism by insisting that each standpoint must acknowledge the partial validity of competing ones through syādvāda—conditional, perhaps-qualified predication—and argues that scientific committees should therefore publish not a unified conclusion but a structured map of conditional assertions.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the view. I want at least one question pressing the distinction between her framework and established Western pluralisms in philosophy of science that treat disagreement as socially mediated—for example, accounts emphasizing social values or perspectival realism—with a follow-up keyed to the reply that she is not merely describing social influence but prescribing logical form. I want at least one question on whether syādvāda collapses into practical agnosticism when applied to predictive models that must yield a single number for policy use, with a follow-up addressing her likely move that policy itself can be indexed to standpoints. And I want at least one question confronting the historical Jain objection that anekāntavāda was originally articulated for metaphysical categories—such as soul, matter, and motion—rather than for measurable empirical quantities, with a follow-up anticipating her defense that measurement itself presupposes metaphysical standpoints. The remaining questions should probe where the literature on scientific disagreement actually presses: how her view handles the asymmetry of predictive success across standpoints, what institutional procedures she proposes for constructing the structured map of conditional assertions, whether there is any empirical outcome that could count against her framework rather than being absorbed as another valid side, and how she responds to the charge that conditionalizing every claim multiplies entities without improving decision-making. Make the questions formal, precise, and suitable for a polished podcast or colloquium recording. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Anticipate her standard replies and build the follow-ups directly into the sequence.
