I am preparing a philosophical profile on the metaphysics of time for a reading group and would like an audit of whether my commitments hang together before I present them. Here is my position. First, I am a presentist in the strict ontological sense: only entities that exist now are real, and nothing past or future exists in any manner whatsoever. Second, I take special relativity to be approximately correct and deny that there is any globally privileged inertial frame or unique cosmic time parameter. Third, I reject the B-theory of time on the grounds that genuine change requires an ontologically real A-series—pastness, presentness, and futurity are not merely indexical or perspectival—though I also reject the moving-spotlight model because it treats all temporal locations as equally real with one merely highlighted, which I think smuggles eternalism back in. Fourth, I endorse a strong truthmaker maximalism restricted to contingent propositions: every true contingent claim must be made true by a concrete, presently existing state of affairs. Fifth, I hold that historical propositions, for instance that a particular asteroid struck Earth sixty-six million years ago, are genuinely true, and I think their truthmakers are present causal traces, fossil structures, and present records rather than the past event itself. Sixth, I argue that eternalism fails to justify the rational asymmetry between our dread of future suffering and our regret of past suffering; if both existed tenselessly, that asymmetry would be irrational, so I treat this as a reductio of eternalism. Seventh, I reject the growing-block theory because I accept a principle of ontological parity: existence should not come in degrees, and granting reality to past but not future times requires an unexplained, arbitrary boundary that violates that parity. Eighth, I want to reconcile presentism with Minkowski spacetime by treating the present not as a global slice but as a family of local, observer-dependent hyperplanes fixed by decoherence events in each region, so that there is no cosmic now but only regional presents, each metaphysically privileged for the observers embedded in them. Ninth, I maintain that cross-temporal causation does not require the co-existence of cause and effect; instead, I propose a primitive relation of causal inheritance, by which present effects structurally inherit properties from non-existent past causes through present mediating conditions alone. Tenth, I believe that if presentism is correct, temporal passage is not an illusion but a real feature of the world, best understood as the successive actualization of potentialities that were previously merely open possibilities determined by present states.

Please examine these ten commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but can be dissolved on closer inspection, and for each real tension what the least costly revision would be. Order the tensions by severity, specify the cheapest fix for each, and tell me whether there is a single change that would resolve the greatest number of problems at once. Write your response as continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, tables, or lists, in the manner of a letter or email. Do not cite specific authors, works, or page numbers, and do not include bibliographies or suggestions for further reading.
