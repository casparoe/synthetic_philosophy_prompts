ok so my study group came up with this argument about epiphenominalism and now it feels like a snake eating its tail but i cant tell where the bite is. (1) If phenomenal properties are causally inert, then they can't cause beliefs about themselves. (2) So any belief like 'i am in pain' must be caused by the underlying neural state N, not by the pain. (3) That means the pain plays no role in my knowing about it—classic paradox of phenominal judgment. (4) But now apply the same causal-closure idea to the belief that N causes my belief: if physical causation is closed, then my belief about that causal link is also just caused by N (or downstream brain stuff), not by my actually tracking the relation. (5) So I never genuinely know that N is the cause; I only have a causally induced belief that happens to match. (6) Generalize: every causal belief I have is like that, including my belief in causal closure itself. (7) So the epiphenomenalist must either deny causal closure—which kills the motivation for saying mental stuff is left out—or accept that they can't justify the very framework they need. (8) Therefore epiphenominalism is self-undermining.

where does it go wrong? i really want to hang onto (1) and (3) if possible. i think i might be conflating closure of the physical with closure of belief-formation, or maybe smuggling in a transparency condition on phenominal concepts that doesnt apply to causal beliefs? also is step (4) just repeating (2) at a higher order, or is there an equivocation about what 'tracking' means? any diagnosis appreciated—especially if it lets me keep the core paradox without the reductio.
