I take the position that the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents is not merely a boundary on formal consistency but generates a structural instability: any agent that simultaneously maintains (a) a disposition to revise its beliefs in response to observed conflict, and (b) a meta-level prohibition on revising the rules that govern that revision, must either freeze one commitment at a fixed point or fall into an unending oscillation between meta-level adjustments. From this I conclude that fully autonomous, self-trusting agency is not just bounded but structurally impossible, not only in formal systems but in any architecture that implements these two commitments. What would genuinely count against this? I want the arguments and distinctions—formal, conceptual, or architectural—that should weaken my confidence, ranked from decisive to merely awkward. In particular: are there coherent architectures that decouple belief-revision rules from object-level revision without falling into the Löbian trap? Could an agent consistently treat its meta-rules as defeasible in a non-circular way? And has the position been insulated by ad hoc qualifications—say, restricting 'agent' to formal provability systems—such that no empirical or conceptual finding could threaten it?
