I have been thinking about a problem at the intersection of moral psychology and normative ethics—specifically the difficulty of distinguishing genuine moral preferences from systematic biases once both have been shaped by years of reflective deliberation. In a seminar on practical reasoning, we treated reflective equilibrium as if it were a kind of cleaning procedure: you adjust particular judgments and general principles until they cohere, and whatever survives is presumably unbiased. But I have become skeptical that coherence alone can do this work, particularly when we consider what might be called normative sedimentation—the process by which biases do not merely distort isolated judgments but gradually reshape the very standards by which we evaluate coherence. I am trying to decide how to direct my research energy over the next couple of years, and I would like help constructing a defended agenda rather than a survey.

More concretely, I am interested in which open questions or lines of work in this area matter most if the goal is to build a workable framework for separating preference from bias in moral reasoning—one that could eventually inform how institutions design ethics review protocols or how individuals structure personal deliberation. Should priority go to developing formal models of preference change over time, so that we can distinguish legitimate moral development from bias reinforcement? Or is the more urgent work conceptual and empirical together—determining which cognitive distortions remain corrigible versus which become invisible precisely because they have structured our reflective practices? There is also a deeper meta-level question about whether the distinction itself holds up, or whether the ideal of a preference entirely untainted by bias is misleading, with the real task being to classify different forms of entanglement rather than to separate them cleanly. Given that I have about three years, a background in logic and conceptual analysis rather than laboratory science, and no resources for large-scale empirical studies, how would you rank these directions?
