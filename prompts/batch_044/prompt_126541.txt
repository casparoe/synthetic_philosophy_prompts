My colleague in the philosophy of medicine has, over the course of several years of discussion, given clear and consistent classifications for a range of conditions. She judges that schizophrenia is a disease; that pregnancy is not; that profound congenital deafness in a person fully embedded in Deaf culture is not a disease; that obesity accompanied by demonstrable metabolic dysfunction is a disease; that lifelong aphantasia—the complete absence of voluntary mental imagery—is not a disease; that alcohol dependence is a disease; that short stature caused by a pituitary tumor is a disease; and that familial short stature with no underlying pathology is not. I want you to work with this track record rather than simply list theories. First, predict what she will say about the following new case: an individual, following a specific psychological trauma and with no detectable neural damage, normal brain imaging, and fully intact underlying biological mechanisms, develops a complete inability to recognize familiar faces; the deficit resolves entirely under clinical hypnosis, yet while present it produces severe social anxiety, occupational impairment, and marked subjective distress. Second, identify at least two distinct rules or principles that each cohere with her entire list of verdicts—one that treats biological mechanism failure coupled with harm as necessary, and one that treats a departure from species-typical functioning combined with suffering or practical impairment as sufficient regardless of localized biological substrate—and say exactly what each rule predicts for this face-recognition case. Third, give your best guess as to which rule she is actually running, with your reasoning. Fourth, identify which of her earlier verdicts is the closest analog to this new case and explain why. Fifth, describe one further hypothetical case—different in structure from the face-recognition example—that would clearly force a choice between the two rules you have identified. Please present all of this in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, writing as one would in a letter or email.
