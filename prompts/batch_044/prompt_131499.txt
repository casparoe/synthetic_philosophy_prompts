Write an essay addressing the backward induction paradox in the centipede game. Specifically, evaluate whether the paradox demonstrates a genuine failure of rational cooperation in finite sequential interactions, or whether it depends on an unrealistic idealization—namely, that agents possess perfect common knowledge of rationality extending transparently over their own future reasoning steps, including steps they may be computationally unable to execute. Consider arguments that bounded rationality or partial self-opacity can block the inductive chain without violating consistency conditions on preference. Weigh both sides, but defend a clear verdict: does the paradox expose a deep limitation in standard rational choice theory, or is it dissolved once we abandon the assumption of full epistemic transparency about one's future mental states?
