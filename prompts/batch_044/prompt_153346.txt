For an upper-level philosophy seminar final exam, draft one 90-minute essay question on infinite ethics that tests conceptual precision rather than familiarity with standard literature. The prompt should ask students whether moral obligations can be genuinely unbounded without collapsing into incoherence or practical paralysis, but it must avoid being answerable by summarizing the standard over-demandingness objection to consequentialism or by reciting familiar rescue scenarios. To force original analysis, inject the following framing into the question itself: present the asymptotic obligation thesis, according to which an agent's moral demands approach but never reach a finite ceiling as available resources or situational proximity vary continuously, and require students to determine whether this framing resolves the tension between unlimited moral reasons and finite agency, or whether it merely relocates the paradox into an infinite regress of residual duties. Provide a model answer of approximately 800 words that takes a clear, defensible position and engages directly with the framing. Accompany it with a marking scheme structured around exactly five must-hit points totaling 20 marks, specifying the point value of each, and including a one-line note on how candidates typically fail that point. The scheme should distinguish answers that genuinely work through the asymptotic framing from those that revert to generic summaries of demandingness objections.
