Write a deadpan parody of a high-end restaurant menu and wine-pairing guide for an exclusive establishment called "The Common-Knowledge Dining Room," formatted exactly as a real Michelin-level document—courses with precise descriptions, pricing in abstract "utility units," chef’s notes, and a brief "House Rules" section—while the satire derives entirely from actual problems in superrationality and epistemic game theory. Each course should be structured as a one-shot coordination problem between the diner and an anonymous counterpart seated in a separate, soundproofed room, with the menu describing payoff matrices disguised as flavor profiles. The piece must engage, without didactic explanation, the distinction between causal and evidential reasoning about the other diner’s choice, the Hofstadter-style argument that recognizing one’s own source code should lead to cooperative behavior in non-iterated dilemmas, and the paradox that achieving common knowledge of rationality might either guarantee perfect coordination or collapse it entirely if both parties overthink the symmetry. Include a footnote in fine print addressing the refund policy should a diner discover they are a simulated instance running on the kitchen’s server. Keep the tone relentlessly serious and culinary, so that a specialist recognizes each course as mapping onto a specific conceptual problem—such as the difficulty of self-locating in a decision algorithm—while remaining readable to a layperson. Approximately 600–700 words.
