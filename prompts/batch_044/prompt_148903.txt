You serve as the senior ethics auditor for a regional health system that deploys predictive algorithms to assign individual patients to risk-reference classes for treatment prioritization. Because the philosophical reference-class problem undermines any automatic assignment—there is no unique correct class for an individual, and substituting one class for another can alter the predicted probability without altering the underlying medical fact—your office needs a reusable audit procedure. Write a formal, step-by-step decision procedure that a non-philosopher reviewer can execute in approximately thirty minutes per case to determine whether an algorithm’s assigned reference class is defensible, illegitimate, or genuinely indeterminate. The procedure must ask ordered questions about: (i) the statistical robustness and sample size of the proposed class; (ii) the causal relevance of each class-defining property to the specific outcome being predicted; (iii) counterfactual stability of membership—specifically, whether the patient would remain in the class under the intervention whose risk is being predicted, and whether using a counterfactual property creates circular reasoning; and (iv) whether intersecting sub-classes generate non-transitive membership relations that cause the prediction to collapse. For each step, specify what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure terminates with an unambiguous approval or rejection, and where it must escalate to a human values-committee because the issue is irreducibly normative rather than factual. Include a concise set of test conditions—data-level and sentence-level checks—the reviewer can apply without specialized statistical software. Then run the procedure on three cases: (a) a 58-year-old patient assigned to a cardiovascular-risk class defined partly by a biomarker that also functions as a proxy for socioeconomic status, where the procedure yields an approval that the clinical team will strongly resist on equity grounds; (b) a patient whose class membership depends on a counterfactual biomarker level—what the level would be after a preliminary treatment—creating circularity in the risk prediction for that very treatment; and (c) a patient who belongs to two statistically valid reference classes that yield contradictory risk rankings, leaving the procedure unable to resolve the conflict. Conclude by stating precisely what the procedure is powerless to settle—particularly regarding distributive fairness across classes and the weighting of competing valid classes—and flag every step where judgment is unavoidable despite the formal structure.
