Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy for a graduate seminar on public reason, focused on whether religious arguments may legitimately justify coercive laws in a pluralist democracy. The instrument should classify positions by the answers a respondent gives to concrete diagnostic questions, not by which philosopher or label they prefer.

Root the tree in a specific case: a legislator publicly defends a coercive policy solely by citing revealed-scriptural authority, offering no independent secular reasoning. From there, branch through three separable dimensions: (1) whether the core defect is epistemic (others cannot assess the premise), motivational (the legislator does not aim to justify the policy to citizens of all reasonable comprehensive doctrines), or democratic (the argument violates equal civic standing regardless of content); (2) whether secular translation of the underlying concern is always required, sometimes required, or never required for legitimacy; and (3) whether the same standard applies symmetrically to secular comprehensive doctrines—such as deep ecological holism or historical-materialist accounts of justice—or whether religious reasons face a distinctive burden.

The leaves must include the named positions: strong exclusion (religious reasons excluded from the public forum on coercion), accessibility-inclusive (religious motivation permitted provided secular reasons are also available), and full inclusion (mutual-respect inclusionism of the Wolterstorff/Eberle type). You must also include an unnamed leaf for a logically possible but rarely defended combination: domain-partitioned permissivism, which holds that religious justification is permissible for policies that do not restrict basic liberties, but requires full secular translation for criminal and distributive law. For each leaf, state the position name (or “unnamed”) and one sentence identifying its most awkward commitment.

Every internal node must be phrased as a standalone question that a student can answer without knowing which interpretation it leads to. Wherever two named positions land on the same branch for all three dimensions, flag them explicitly as not separable by this tree. In addition, map the full logical space of answer-combinations; for any unoccupied combination, briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Keep the taxonomy formal and suitable for students who already understand the basic Rawls–Audi–Eberle landscape but need to discover where they actually fall. In your exposition, clearly separate what is settled in the specialist literature from what remains genuinely contested—do not present any one interpretation of the “proviso” or of “accessibility” as the consensus view if it is actively disputed.
