Write a joke about the interplay between inside-view reasoning and outside-view reasoning, with particular attention to epistemic modesty. The humor should depend on the actual conceptual structure of these two perspectives—where the inside view weighs the particular features of a belief-forming situation and the outside view appeals to reference-class frequencies—rather than on generic tropes about being "humbler than thou." To keep the response from being a stock formulation, build the joke around a specific, less-discussed tension: the difficulty of selecting a non-arbitrary reference class for one's own epistemic failures, such that applying the outside view to one's neglect of the outside view seems to require an inside view about that very neglect, or such that every plausible reference class either dilutes the base rate with dissimilar reasoners or collapses into a sample size of one. The joke should be coherent enough that a listener familiar with these concepts recognizes the philosophical point being made, not merely a collection of jargon.
