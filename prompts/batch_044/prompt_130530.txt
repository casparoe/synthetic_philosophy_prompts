Consider a decentralized funding mechanism for interdisciplinary research in which individual scholars first rank candidate projects within their own fields, then field representatives aggregate these rankings via a weighted Borda rule into a global portfolio, with the field weights themselves fixed by an earlier strategic vote among representatives. The mechanism permits manipulation at both levels: scholars can misorder internal rankings, and representatives can distort weight allocations. Please describe the most important implications of the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem for the strategy-proofness of this two-tier system. Specifically: does the theorem apply independently to each tier, or does nesting the procedures alter the impossibility result? If the designers require non-dictatorship, full range over at least three distinct global portfolios, and strategy-proofness at both tiers simultaneously, what structural constraints must they accept? And could restricting the domain of admissible preferences—say, by requiring single-peakedness across fields—provide a genuine escape, or would strategic incentives merely migrate between tiers? Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or external references; answer from the conceptual structure of the theorem and its standard conditions alone.
