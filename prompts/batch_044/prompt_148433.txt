(1) Akrasia is acting against your own better judgment at the time of action.
(2) Hyperbolic discounting explains this: your present self discounts future rewards steeply, so your all-things-considered judgment (which uses a flatter rate) gets overridden by immediate payoff.
(3) But hyperbolic discounting is just time-inconsistent preferences — your utility curve at 9am and 2am are literally different. There's no single you failing; there's just you-at-t1 vs you-at-t2.
(4) So if akrasia is just hyperbolic discounting, weakness of will isn't a real mental failure, it's just preference drift over time, like wanting ice cream on tuesday but salad on wednesday.
(5) People use precommitment — blocking software, account deletion, implementation intentions (those if-then plans) — and these reduce failure rates. If weakness is just preference drift, precommitment works purely by constraining options, not by fixing will.
(6) But sophisticated hyperbolic discounters (the ones who know their own curve) should be able to predict exactly when they'll cave and build perfect blockers. Yet they still bypass them — i disable my own blocker with a vpn at 2am.
(7) If it's all just preference curves, bypassing should feel like a calm update: oh, my 2am preferences are different. Instead it feels like defeat, like losing to yourself.
(8) So either weakness of will is a myth and moral blame is unfair (you're just following your current curve), or hyperbolic discounting is incomplete and commitment devices shouldn't help — but they do help. Both conclusions seem wrong.

where's the slip? I'd rather keep (2) and (5) if possible, but I think I might be smuggling in an extra premise about phenomenology between (6) and (7). is there a way to keep the discounting model without throwing out blame?
