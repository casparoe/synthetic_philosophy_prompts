I am drafting a six-month research proposal for a small philosophy-of-value fellowship, and I need the framing turned into a precise, defensible plan. The domain is the heterogeneity problem for hedonism—what, if anything, all pleasures share that could justify treating pleasure as the sole intrinsic good—but I want to avoid the well-worn debate about whether pleasures have a uniform phenomenal "feel." Instead, my half-formed idea is that the problem has been misdiagnosed by a false dichotomy: philosophers assume the common element must be either an intrinsic qualitative character or nothing at all. I want to investigate whether hedonism can be reconstructed around a structural feature that is neither purely phenomenal nor purely intentional, but a specific functional-phenomenal relation—namely, that pleasure is the mental state in which a subject’s occurrent motivational orientation and her phenomenal field coincide without experienced resistance, conflict, or distance. Call this tentative feature "unresisted endorsement." The open questions are whether this feature actually picks out all and only pleasures, whether it distinguishes hedonism from desire-satisfaction theories (since it requires phenomenal presence of the fulfillment, not just belief that a desire is satisfied), and whether hard cases—malicious pleasure, aesthetic absorption, bodily pleasure in flow states, and so-called "cool" intellectual satisfactions—either fit or break the framework.

Please write the proposal with the following elements:

1. A precise statement of the research question, formulated so that it admits a determinate yes/no or this-rather-than-that answer.
2. An explanation of why the existing positions (qualitative uniformity defenses versus heterogeneity objections) leave this specific structural angle genuinely open rather than already settled.
3. The actual method, month by month: what conceptual work I will do, how I will test the "unresisted endorsement" feature against specific hard cases, how I will map its relation to rival theories (especially desire-satisfactionism and objective-list accounts), and where I will stop if the distinction collapses.
4. What a successful deliverable looks like—specifically, what conclusion about hedonism’s viability I could reach—and what would count as failure or as the project being too large for six months.
5. An honest scope assessment, including the risk that the proposed feature is merely a redescription of an existing view and how the project will guard against that.

The proposal should be formal and polished, suitable for a committee familiar with the standard literature but evaluating a non-obvious framing. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation requests; reference positions and arguments generally, and I will supply specific citations myself. Avoid relying on niche contemporary texts or specific page references that I could not verify without searching; ground the argument in conceptual distinctions that are available from the problem itself.
