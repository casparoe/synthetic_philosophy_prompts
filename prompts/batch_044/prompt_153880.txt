I am a novelist drafting a manuscript about institutional collapse in isolated communities, and in preparation I have been revisiting William Golding's Lord of the Flies with my writing group. We keep reaching for the prisoner's dilemma—both its one-shot form and its iterated version embedded in evolutionary dynamics—as a lens for the boys' society: Ralph's conch rules seem to establish an iterated game with a long shadow of the future, Jack's hunting party looks like a rational defection that exploits cooperators, and the decay of the signal-fire maintenance resembles the breakdown of direct reciprocity once noise and error enter the system. Some in the group invoke specific evolutionary conditions—clustering of cooperators, stochastic termination of interaction, the shift from direct to indirect reciprocity—to argue that Golding is essentially dramatizing evolutionary tournaments in a state of nature, with Ralph's tears at the end serving as the verdict that cooperation is unstable without external enforcement.

Could you lay out the precise correspondence table—what plays the role of the payoff matrix, of the shadow of the future, of the error rate, of the clustering mechanism, and of the termination probability—then identify the disanalogies that actually matter for the narrative and ethical conclusion? In particular, do the boys possess the cognitive architecture for strategic foresight that iterated models presuppose? Does the certainty of eventual rescue alter the termination dynamics in ways evolutionary models do not capture? Does the analogy quietly import rational-agent assumptions that make the boys' cruelty look inevitable rather than culturally constructed? And if these disanalogies are severe, does Golding's conclusion about the darkness of man's heart survive, or is it carrying the weight of a framework that does not apply?

Finally, if the prisoner's dilemma analogy ultimately collapses under this audit, I would like to know whether a different model—perhaps a tragedy of the commons with reputation signaling, or a coordination game with focal-point collapse—would map more honestly onto the novel's dynamics, so that I can avoid importing a false determinism into my own narrative.
