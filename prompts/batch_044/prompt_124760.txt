I've come to think that the backward induction resolution of the centipede game isn't merely one rational strategy among others, but a structural feature of sequential reasoning under common knowledge: the only way cooperation survives past the first node is if at least one player can credibly maintain that her reasoning is opaque to the other, which violates the transparency conditions built into the game's information structure. I tried to defend this to a friend, arguing that fixes like trembling-hand perfection or level-k bounded rationality don't rescue cooperation—they just relocate the opacity assumption into the error structure or the belief hierarchy, and once common knowledge of rationality (or boundedness) is restored, the induction runs through. What would actually count against this view? I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they'd be: formal results showing cooperation can be sustained without opacity, empirical patterns in human play that can't be dismissed as noise, or conceptual arguments that common knowledge doesn't entail transparency of reasoning. I'd also like an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—if every cooperative outcome is explained away as hidden opacity, is that a problem for the position, or is it the point?
