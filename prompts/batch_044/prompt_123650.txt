Malcolm and Dennett have been circling the same drain for decades: Malcolm insists dreams can't be experiences because there's no criterion for being wrong about them, while Dennett treats them as post-waking narrative reconstructions. In my philosophy of mind group we keep replaying the same moves and nobody budges. Is this stall just about competing definitions of "experience" — or is there a hidden empirical disagreement about whether REM sleep supports consciousness without reportability? And both seem to assume that if dreams aren't experiences they have to be illusions or nothing; maybe there's a middle ground like subpersonal phenomenal processing that neither side can accomodate. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, substantive, or two different questions about phenomenology vs epistemic authority — and tell me what would actually shift it: a conceptual distinction, a neuro result, or just giving up on a unified answer?
