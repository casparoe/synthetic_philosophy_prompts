Here is the issue I want you to address. Imagine that Immanuel Kant were alive today and had thoroughly absorbed the modern evidence that Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic subjects are psychological outliers: in many non-WEIRD societies, moral reasoning is not organized around individual autonomy and harm-based reciprocity, but around group hierarchy, communal loyalty, and purity, with violations of authority treated with a severity comparable to what Kant reserves for violations of human dignity. How would Kant likely respond?

Specifically, I want you to reconstruct his probable line of reasoning regarding the following tension. Consider a concrete hypothetical society—constructed for the sake of argument—in which the very concept of “individual autonomy” plays almost no role in normative judgment, and in which breaches of hierarchical role are condemned not merely as imprudent but as morally monstrous, on a par with treating a person merely as a means. Would Kant be forced to classify the members of this society as deficient in rational agency, or could he accommodate them within his framework?

Please map out the arguments he might deploy to distinguish between (a) a culture that applies the universal moral law correctly but assigns differential empirical weight to various duties because of local circumstances, and (b) a culture that operates with a genuinely different conception of rational agency altogether—one in which the kingdom of ends is replaced by a kingdom of roles. Would he reinterpret the cross-cultural data as revealing nothing more than culturally contingent applications of a single law, or would he see it as undermining the claim that moral objectivity requires identical normative structure across all human groups?
