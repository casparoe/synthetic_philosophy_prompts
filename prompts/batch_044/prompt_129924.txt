I'm preparing notes for an advanced epistemology seminar on the regress problem, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present. Here is my current package of commitments: (1) Foundationalism is correct about ordinary perceptual knowledge—some beliefs are justified non-inferentially by direct experiential contact, without being inferred from prior beliefs. (2) I accept what I will call the transparency requirement: for any belief to count as knowledge for me, I must be able, upon reflection, to identify a source of warrant for it that does not circularly depend on that very belief; this applies even to basic beliefs, though the warrant cited need not itself be another belief. (3) I reject infinitism on normative, not merely psychological, grounds—an infinite regress of reasons never actually justifies any belief, because justification is never anchored, and the psychological impossibility of traversing an infinite chain is only a symptom of this deeper failure. (4) I hold a domain-restricted coherentism for theoretical knowledge: in science, no belief is epistemically basic, but the entire web is justified by its explanatory coherence and predictive success. (5) I deny that there is a sharp boundary between basic and inferential belief; evidential dependence comes in degrees, so the basic/inferential distinction is a continuum rather than a kind-distinction. (6) I take epistemic circularity to be vicious without exception—using a faculty to justify beliefs that in turn justify the reliability of that faculty is never legitimate, even for our basic perceptual capacities.

Please audit these commitments. Identify which pairs are in genuine tension (not merely apparent), rank them by severity, and for each specify the least costly revision—whether weakening a claim, restricting its scope, or abandoning it. Pay special attention to whether (2) undermines the possibility of genuinely basic beliefs in (1), whether (5) dissolves the kind of boundary foundationalism needs, and whether (4) can survive (6) without circularity. Note also any tensions that dissolve on closer inspection, such as the apparent conflict between being a foundationalist about perception and a coherentist about science. Do not treat all conflicts as equally serious. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if revised or dropped, would clean up the largest number of severe tensions at the lowest philosophical cost.
