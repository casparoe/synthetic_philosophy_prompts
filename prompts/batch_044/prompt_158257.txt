I am attempting to formulate a focused research agenda at the intersection of philosophy of logic and the foundations of autonomous reasoning. Lewis Carroll's well-known dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise exposes a troubling regress in the justification of deduction: any attempt to ground an inference rule seems to require a further rule that licenses its application, threatening either circularity or an infinite ascent. The familiar responses—treating certain structural rules as primitive constraints rather than premises, appealing to pragmatic entitlement, or distributing justification across agents rather than localizing it in single steps—each resolve part of the difficulty while leaving others intact. What remains genuinely open is which of these strategies, if any, can be made coherent for a formal system that must validate its own inference procedures without relying on an external interpreter.

In particular, I want to understand which unresolved questions in this space are most worth pursuing now, and why. Among the candidates: whether the regress is vicious or merely benign; whether justification can be genuinely distributed across architectural levels rather than assigned to individual rules; and how a system that modifies its own axiomatic or inferential framework might avoid either presupposing the very rules it seeks to justify or falling into an ungrounded meta-level loop. But these are not equally urgent.

Please rank the two or three open lines of work you regard as most important, and defend the ordering rigorously. Your defense should specify: the criteria governing importance—such as conceptual tractability, upstream dependency (how many other questions turn on this one), and relevance to formal architectures lacking an external ground; the trade-offs between pursuing one line versus another given limited research resources; and what specific kind of argument or result in the near term would lead you to revise the ranking. I am not requesting a literature review, a reading list, or citations; I want a defended, dependency-aware agenda that treats the justification of deduction as a live problem for system design rather than a settled historical curiosity.
