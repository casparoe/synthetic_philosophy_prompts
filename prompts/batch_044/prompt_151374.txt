I've been arguing with my roommate about whether Gen Z is actually a thing, ontologically speaking, and it's made me realize the whole "generations" debate is weirdly stuck in a loop nobody admits. Sociologists keep publishing cohort studies showing people born roughly 1997–2012 share distinct attitudes about climate anxiety and workplace norms, but demographers push back saying those patterns are mostly age effects or period effects in disguise—there's the classic APC identification problem, where you can't statistically separate age, period, and cohort without making arbitrary assumptions that beg the question. Meanwhile philosophers of social science ask whether "Millennial" picks out a natural kind, a social kind with real causal powers, or just a marketing label invented to sell phone cases, and pop culture just uses the terms to make memes. The disagreement hasn't budged in years: sociologists cite survey instruments, demographers cite the identification deadlock, philosophers cite debates about projectibility and social construction, and everyone talks past each other without conceding an inch.

My working theory is that this isn't one debate but three different questions crammed under the single word "generation." One is statistical—can we isolate a genuine cohort effect? Another is metaphysical—does that cohort form a kind? And a third is pragmatic—should we use these labels for policy, hiring, or marketing? But maybe I'm wrong and there's actually a hidden empirical disagreement doing the work, or maybe the sides are just holding bedrock intuitions about whether social groups can be "real" in the same way biological species are. I'd like a diagnosis that actually engages what each side says—survey data versus statistical identification versus kind-talk—rather than a generic "it's complicated." Is it verbal, substantive, or are they answering different questions? And given your diagnosis, what would count as real progress? A new statistical method that breaks the APC deadlock? A philosophical distinction that resolves the metaphysical side? Or is the question just ill-posed, and should we stop asking it?
