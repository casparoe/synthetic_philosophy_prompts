I'm in this poli-sci/philo seminar on Arendt and we've been arguing about whether her idea of the banality of evil acutally holds up or if it's just a catchy slogan people throw around. My roommate keeps comparing Eichmann to, like, a logistics coordinator at Amazon who just makes sure packages move faster and never asks what's inside the boxes. He uses it to defend Arendt: evil isn't some Hitler-level demon, it's just careerist people treating moral questions like workflow optimization problems.

I want to audit how far this maps. For instance, Eichmann wasn't exactly neutral, he went out of his way for some transports, and the Amazon guy can quit without being executed. Also, does the analogy quietly import the idea that modern tech bureaucrats are dangerous in the exact same way, or does it weaken Arendt by making Eichmann seem too passive? And if the disanalogies are strong enough, does her conclusion — that we should fear thoughtlessness more than monstrous intent — still survive, or was the analogy carrying the whole argument?
