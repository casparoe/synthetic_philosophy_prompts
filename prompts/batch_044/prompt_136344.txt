In my graduate seminar on epistemic closure, a student recently summarized Nozick's tracking account with the slogan that knowledge tracks the truth, then immediately used it to reject the closure principle: she argued that because my belief that I have hands does not track the falsity of skeptical hypotheses, I do not know those hypotheses are false, and therefore closure never applies—so knowing I have hands never obliges me to know I am not a brain in a vat. Before I respond, I want to take the slogan seriously rather than dismiss it, but I need to know precisely what machinery is hidden inside it. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that knowledge tracks the truth might express, arranged from the trivial to the radical? For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive condition on knowledge, or false, and indicate which versions actually license the anti-closure move my student made. In particular, distinguish interpretations concerning counterfactual dependence of belief on fact, sensitivity conditions evaluated across nearby possible worlds, the modal profile of the belief-forming method itself, and whether tracking is proposed as a necessary condition, a sufficient condition, or a full reductive analysis of knowledge. Then tell me which of these readings are compatible with standard closure principles and which genuinely undermine them, so that I can return to the seminar with a disambiguated menu rather than a single interpretation.
