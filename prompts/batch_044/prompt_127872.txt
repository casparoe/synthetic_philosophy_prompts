I spent thirty years as an experimental neurophysiologist studying sensory integration in distributed nervous systems—particularly in cephalopods, where arm-level processing proceeds with minimal central oversight—before turning seriously to philosophy of mind in retirement. I have come to defend a strong extension of Dennett’s multiple drafts model: the Cartesian theater is not merely wrong about conscious experience, but about cognition generally. There is no unified temporal integrator of experience, no minimal “thin” self-model that persists across drafts, and the autobiographical narrative is a post-hoc confabulation shaped more by evolutionary pressures for social coherence than by any neural reality. In my philosophy reading group, colleagues have raised two preliminary worries: that autobiographical memory exhibits more continuity than radical fragmentation predicts, and that split-brain patients still report a singular sense of agency despite hemispheric disconnection. I want to press further.

What would genuinely count against this position—not as a routine objection, but as evidence or argument that should move a committed proponent? I want decisive defeaters ranked separately from merely awkward ones: empirical results about temporal binding or distributed computation, conceptual distinctions between integration and narration, or cases in which decentralized architectures still require some functional equivalent of a central register. Would defenders of this view have already qualified it—say, by allowing a “virtual” integrator or by treating fragmentation as itself narrated—such that nothing could expose it to risk? If so, is that insulation intellectually virtuous or a sign that the view has become unfalsifiable? And which objections would someone drawing analogies between human cognition and decentralized invertebrate nervous systems concede as genuinely damaging, versus which they would dismiss as still assuming the theater they reject? Please respond in no more than 450 words, with clear distinctions between fatal, serious, and merely uncomfortable challenges.
