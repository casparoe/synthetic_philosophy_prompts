I've been trying to work out a version of the Meno problem for a seminar I'm not actually taking and I think I've built a reductio that makes knowledge sound useless. Here's the chain I'm stuck on:

(1) If knowledge is genuinely more valuable than true belief, there has to be some practical or theoretical advantage it gives that true belief alone doesn't.

(2) The standard answer is stability: knowledge is tied down by justification or understanding, so you're less likely to lose it or be talked out of it.

(3) But stability only matters if there's a real chance of losing the belief, which means the extra value is basically insurance against future error.

(4) Now look at any single action: if I truly believe the bridge is safe I cross; if I know it, I also cross. The behavior is identical, and the risk in that moment is identical.

(5) So the "insurance" can't be about the present choice; it must be about future choices where I might have forgotten or been misled.

(6) But then the value of knowledge over true belief depends entirely on my own cognitive fragility—if I were a perfect rememberer with no susceptibility to counter-evidence, knowledge would add nothing.

(7) Worse, to actually cash in that insurance I need access to the justification, which is just more beliefs I also need protected. So knowledge requires a network of protected supporting beliefs.

(8) For a finite mind that either launches an infinite regress or bottoms out in unjustified foundations, neither of which actually delivers the protection promised.

(9) So either knowledge isn't more valuable for beings like us, or the stability answer is a myth and we're back to not knowing why knowledge beats true belief.

(10) Therefore I think (1) is false, but that feels like giving up on the Meno problem entirely.

I feel like something slipped between (4) and (5) or maybe in (6)—like I'm treating knowledge as a dispositional property but measuring it against occurrent belief?—but I can't name the mistake. I'd definately rather keep (1) and (2) if I can. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost me?
