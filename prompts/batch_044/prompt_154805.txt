I'm a software engineer without formal training in philosophy, and I recently encountered a summary of Ian Hacking's work on psychiatric classification that I'd like to check for accuracy. The summary explains that Hacking distinguishes "natural kinds," like chemical elements, from "human kinds," such as autism or dissociative identity disorder. Because individuals classified into human kinds can learn about their diagnosis and alter their behavior in response, the classification system and the people it describes enter a feedback loop—what Hacking calls a looping effect. The summary then claims that Hacking uses this to prove psychiatric disorders have no biological basis whatsoever, arguing they are purely cultural inventions in which the diagnostic label trains people to perform symptoms out of nothing. It further asserts that this makes psychiatric categories uniquely unstable compared to other medical classifications, and that Hacking's practical conclusion is that we should abolish diagnostic labels entirely to stop them from manufacturing the illnesses they name. Which parts of this summary fairly represent Hacking's view? Where does it conflate his descriptive account with stronger claims he rejects? And does the recommendation to eliminate labels actually follow from the looping-effect thesis, or is it an unsupported addition?
