Since I inherited my late advisor’s papers and began trying to systematize her unpublished philosophy of history lectures, the question of whether the economic base explains every other social form has become personally urgent for me. Her recorded judgments on specific historical developments are these: she held that the shift to double-entry bookkeeping in fourteenth-century Italian city-states was fully explained by the demands of mercantile capital accumulation; that the codification of modern patent law in seventeenth-century England was likewise base-driven, serving to secure returns on fixed capital; that the invention of the mechanical clock in late-medieval monasteries and towns was not base-driven but arose from autonomous liturgical and astronomical needs, only later being appropriated by market coordination; that the emergence of the realist novel in eighteenth-century Britain was not explained by the economic base but by autonomous transformations in narrative consciousness and print culture; and that the standardization of railway time across Britain in the 1840s was base-driven, being indispensable to the synchronization of industrial labor and commodity circulation. Now consider a new case: the widespread adoption of open-source software licensing frameworks in the 1990s, which emerged initially within academic computer-science departments with no direct market imperative, yet rapidly became infrastructural for global digital capitalism. Would she classify this development as base-driven or as an autonomous superstructural innovation subsequently captured by the base? I am less interested in the single answer than in the competing rules that could consistently generate all five of her earlier verdicts. Please lay out at least two distinct principles—perhaps one that distinguishes technologies of exchange and labor coordination from technologies of knowledge production, and another that distinguishes innovations that stabilize existing relations of production from those that destabilize them—that fit her track record equally well. For each principle, state precisely what it predicts about the open-source case. Then give your best-guess prediction of her actual verdict, with the reasoning that favors it over the alternative, and identify one further historical or conceptual case that would decisively discriminate between the two rules you have proposed.
