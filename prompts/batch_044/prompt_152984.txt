Essay notes (working title: Hypocrisy Gradients and the Distribution of Blame Rights):

Most social practices of accusation distinguish, often implicitly, between those who may blame a wrongdoer and those who may not. A recurrent pattern is this: a person who has committed a similar wrong retains some right to blame if her failure was structurally compelled, yet forfeits it entirely if the failure was agentially chosen—while outsiders with no comparable failure possess full standing regardless of structural position. This suggests that the practice presupposes not a uniform condition for standing, but a two-tiered model: implicated standing for those enmeshed in the wrong-type, and disinterested standing for those outside it.

I wish to inject a further distinction—hypocrisy gradients—where the erosion of standing is not binary but proportional to the similarity between the blamer’s past failure and the blamed party’s present action, weighted by temporal distance, visibility, and social proximity. On this view, hypocrisy functions less as a contaminating stain and more as a comparative calibration: the closer the mirror, the weaker the voice.
