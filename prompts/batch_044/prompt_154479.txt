You are a senior philosopher of epistemology writing assessments for an upper-level undergraduate seminar. The question should present a novel scenario rather than a familiar counterexample: subject S justifiably but falsely believes R on the basis of strong misleading evidence, then derives belief Q, which happens to be true, from that false premise; however, S also possesses a second genuinely reliable cognitive track that would have produced belief Q had the first track failed, though this second track is causally redundant and epistemically inaccessible to S. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations; students should argue conceptually. Write the entire response as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, numbered lists, or tables, as in a formal letter. Think carefully and ensure the scenario is philosophically precise but does not depend on obscure technical vocabulary beyond standard epistemological terminology.
