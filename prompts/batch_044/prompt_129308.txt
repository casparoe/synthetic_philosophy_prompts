Please explain the precise logical structure of the teleological suspension of the ethical in Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling, with particular attention to the relation between infinite resignation and the knight of faith. Many readings treat Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac as a hierarchical override—where a higher religious duty temporarily supersedes the universal ethical prohibition against filicide—yet this risks reducing the paradox to an ordered ranking of norms rather than the irreducible tension Johannes de Silentio describes, in which Abraham simultaneously retains his fatherly love and violates its ethical expression. To probe this distinction, consider a modified scenario: Abraham is commanded not to kill Isaac, but to permanently conceal from him that Abraham is his biological father, thereby preserving Isaac's life but destroying the ethical transparency that constitutes the universal father-son relation. Would this concealment instantiate the same structural paradox—where the particular love is both affirmed and suspended—or does the absence of physical violence and the preservation of life collapse the conflict into a merely severe ethical dilemma? In addressing this, clarify whether the suspension requires an internal contradiction within the universal itself, rather than only a conflict between particular affection and universal duty, and explain why Abraham's silence functions as an essential condition of the paradox rather than as a resolvable omission.
