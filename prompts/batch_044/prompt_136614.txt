I keep noticing this split in my intution about Parfit-style survival cases. If I'm teletransported to Mars in an instant — scanned, destroyed, rebuilt — most people say that's death with a copy left behind. But if they swap out my neurons one by one over twenty years with synthetic ones that keep all my memories and personality intact, that just feels like me getting cybernetic upgrades, not dying gradualy. The psychological continuity is actually smoother in the slow case, yet the fast case feels like duplication and the slow case feels like maintenance. Is there an established name for this asymmetry between instantaneous and gradual physical replacement? I don't think it's just physical vs psychological continuity, because both cases share the same psychology and differ only in the rate of physical change. Does this map onto something in the literature, or should I treat it as its own thing?
