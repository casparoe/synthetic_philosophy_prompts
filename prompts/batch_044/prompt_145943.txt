For my philosophy of mind and biology seminar, I need an essay on whether Darwin's and Turing's inversions of reasoning are genuinely parallel. Darwin observed that earthworms exercise complex soil-tilling competence without any comprehension of soil chemistry or structural engineering; Turing inverted the intelligence question by proposing we judge machines on behavioral competence rather than on internal understanding. Many treatments present these as twin dissolutions of the comprehension-requirement, but I want to press what I will call the tracking-versus-computation asymmetry: Darwinian competence is historically anchored—each worm's behavior tracks an environment through generations of selective retention—whereas Turing-machine competence is anchored only in symbol-manipulation rules that carry no necessary evolutionary relation to the world they describe. If this asymmetry holds, then Turing's inversion may not replicate Darwin's; it may substitute syntactic adequacy for the world-tracking adequacy that makes Darwinian competence genuinely adaptive rather than merely behaviorally successful. Write an essay weighing whether this asymmetry undermines the standard analogy. Consider the strongest reply—that evolutionary tracking itself reduces to computation at the genetic level, so the asymmetry collapses—and reach a clear, defended conclusion on whether comprehension can be eliminated in both cases by the same move, or whether Darwin's inversion succeeds precisely where Turing's fails.
