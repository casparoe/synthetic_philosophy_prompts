ok so ive been thinking about this debate around epistemic closure and nozicks tracking theory, and i feel like its been stuck for decades without anyone actually changing their mind. the basic idea of closure is if you know p and p entails q then you should know q, right? and nozick said knowledge tracks the truth - like, if things were different in some close possible world, your belief would change too. so people argue about whether you can really know youre not a brain in a vat, because in the closest world where you are a vat you still think you have hands. tracking seems to say you dont know youre not a vat, but closure says if you know you have hands and that means youre not a vat, then you do know it. and philosophers just talk past each other.

heres my wierd addition: i made up a case with a security guard named marla who tracks that the warehouse door is locked with a sensor. the sensor works in almost every nearby world except one glitch world where it falsely says locked but the door is actually locked anyway. marla knows the door is locked, and she knows that means no ones inside, but she never checks for the glitch. does she know nobodys inside? tracking might fail on that glitch world, but closure says she should get the knowledge for free. i also wonder if theres something like 'stacked tracking' where the second belief depends on the first ones conditions piling up. i dont know if thats real but it feels like it should matter.

so why is this debate stalled? is it just a verbal disagreement about what 'knowing' means? are they using different standards for what counts as a nearby world? or are they actually asking seperate questions - one about everyday knowing and one about skeptical possibilities? maybe its bedrock intuitions about whether you have to rule out every wierd scenario. i want a diagnosis using what each side actually says, not just names. and if it is stalled for that reason, what would actually move it? a formal result, a new distinction like my stacked tracking thing, or is the question just ill-posed? tell me what kind of disagreement it really is.
