Since I began designing interaction systems that model user attention, I have become preoccupied with a specific confusion I keep encountering in discussions of consciousness as a user illusion. The claim is usually that the brain constructs a streamlined interface for an organism. But in explaining how this interface is “experienced,” authors often seem to slip into treating the organism itself as a kind of inner user who receives the display—not merely the beneficiary of the representation, but a subject positioned behind it. I have seen this happen in debates about virtual embodiment and in popularizations of predictive-processing accounts, where the “self-model” is said to be “viewed” by the system that generated it. Is there an established name for this precise slide—from a representation built for an agent to a representation experienced by a second-order agent—and if not, can it be defined in a way that distinguishes it clearly from the homunculus fallacy on one side and from ordinary reification on the other? I am not asking for a literature survey; I want to know whether the carving is recognized, and if not, what its exact boundaries would be.
