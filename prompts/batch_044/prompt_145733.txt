Take the WHO's 2015 call during the West African Ebola outbreak to run a ring-vaccination trial with an unlicensed vaccine instead of just distributing it everywhere, and analyse it through Scanlon's contractualism, specifically the aggragation problem. Don't just ask if it was justified -- map out exactly who counts as each person that the decision needs to be justifiable to: the people in the trial getting placebo, the ones in the ring getting the real shot, the ones outside the ring getting nothing, and future patients if the trial flops. Then ask whether Scanlon's resistance to aggregating small harms across lots of people actually helps here or totally collapses, since every option kills some identifable folks. Does this case prove contractualism secretly needs an aggregation mechanism, or does it show the aggregation problem was never the real bottleneck? Keep the mapping disciplined -- don't force the theory to speak where it's silent.
