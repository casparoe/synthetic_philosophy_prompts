Produce the document that a rational-choice evolutionary-game theorist and a cultural-institutional theorist could jointly sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on cooperation, the prisoner's dilemma (one-shot and iterated), and evolutionary dynamics. The first camp holds that cooperation in iterated interactions is explained chiefly by direct reciprocity and individual-level selection, that anonymous one-shot defection is the correct rational baseline, and that group-level or cultural mechanisms are derivative of repeated-game structures. The second camp holds that cooperation is sustained primarily by group selection, institutional scaffolding, and internalized norms; that treating one-shot defection as a universal rational baseline mistakes a culturally specific framing for normative bedrock; and that standard iterated-game models systematically understate shared intentionality and ecological embeddedness.

The statement must include: (i) numbered agreed claims on the formal PD structure, the empirical record of laboratory cooperation, and the origin-versus-maintenance distinction; (ii) a disagreements table with each camp's precise position phrased in wording that camp would accept; (iii) at least three cruxes—whether direct reciprocity scales without institutional support, whether high anonymous one-shot cooperation falsifies the rational-baseline claim, and whether ecological validity separates human cooperation from model predictions; (iv) for each crux, a concrete case, model result, or empirical pattern both sides agree is relevant, with each side's explicit prediction of what it shows; and (v) an assessment of whether any portion—especially around 'rationality'—is purely verbal, with the exact boundary specified. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or requests for further reading.
