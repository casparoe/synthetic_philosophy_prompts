I'm on the appeals panel of the Digital Atlas of Mass Atrocities, and we've got a taxonomy call for next week's meeting that I need a memo for. A research group submitted an entry on the 1965-66 killings in Indonesia using a framework they call structural-annihilation tagging — it splits events into direct-genocide (state-ordered physical destruction of an ascriptive group), structural-genocide (state-facilitated conditions that foreseeably erase a group's social or cultural existence), and political-democide (mass killing of opponents without group-targeting). They want to apply both structural-genocide and political-democide tags, but not direct-genocide, arguing that even though the primary selection criterion was alleged communists — an achieved identity — the campaign also destroyed Chinese-Indonesian cultural institutions and used ethnic markers for selection, so it hits the cultural and social fields in Lemkin's original broader conception without fitting the UN Convention's narrower physical-or-biological-group clause.

Our charter says we should honor Lemkin's broader idea but avoid inflating the term for political advocacy. Half the panel thinks dual-tagging is precise; the other half thinks it's just a backdoor way to call it genocide without saying so, and that if the primary selection was political, adding structural-genocide confuses archive users and dilutes the legal category.

Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before Thursday — reccomend either adopting the dual-tag, rejecting it for a single tag, or adopting it with conditions — with a concrete timeline if we go with approval. The memo needs to engage the inflation worry seriously, not just restate the legal definition, and it should work out how the recommendation handles the tension between Lemkin's cultural/social fields and the politics of naming atrocities in this specific case. Don't just summarize the literature; give the board a clear bottom line it can act on.
