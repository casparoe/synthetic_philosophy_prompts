I'm drafting a position paper on Confucian role ethics and want an internal consistency audit before I present it to a reading group. Here are the commitments I take myself to hold:

(1) Moral normativity is fundamentally role-constituted: the content and force of one's obligations derive from occupancy of specific relational positions within the Five Relationships (wulun)—parent-child, elder-younger sibling, ruler-subject, spouse-spouse, and friend-friend—rather than from universal, impersonal principles applied to individuals as such.

(2) The obligations within these roles are asymmetrical in content—the duties owed by child to parent are not mirror-images of those owed by parent to child—yet they are reciprocally weighted: neither party's legitimate moral claim is subordinate in importance, even though the required performances differ radically.

(3) Graded affection (ai you chadeng) is not a psychological bias to be corrected but a moral virtue: one's practical priority and emotional investment should genuinely diminish with relational distance, and treating strangers with the same quantitative care one owes one's parents represents a failure of moral proportion, not an achievement of justice.

(4) At the same time, the virtue of ren (benevolence) functions as a role-transcendent orientation: every human being possesses an inherent claim to humane treatment and to conditions that enable flourishing, generating genuine obligations toward strangers and distant others that are not canceled by the absence of direct relational ties.

(5) Moral extension (tui) operates through structural analogy—projecting the affective and practical patterns learned in dense familial roles onto broader social and political relations—without dissolving the special density of family obligations into an undifferentiated cosmopolitan love.

(6) In hard cases where dense familial obligations conflict with broad humanitarian demands, there is no external meta-principle or algorithmic decision procedure; resolution depends on the agent's cultivated practical wisdom (yi), informed by ritual propriety (li) and emotional attunement (qing).

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each tension? For each real tension, specify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision costs me. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. Finally, tell me whether there is a single commitment whose revision or abandonment would eliminate the largest number of conflicts, or whether the problems are distributed in a way that requires multiple adjustments.
