I'm working through a position on art, race, and political commitment, drawing on W. E. B. Du Bois's argument that all art functions as propaganda—especially for Black creators in a culture that reads their work through a racial lens regardless of intent. Before I present it, I want a consistency audit of the following commitments, which I hold jointly and am not signaling which I would drop:

(a) There is no genuinely apolitical aesthetic form; for Black artists, neutrality is a myth that serves white supremacy.

(b) Propaganda aligned with truth and collective liberation is not an aesthetic corruption but art's highest vocation.

(c) Black artists therefore bear a special moral obligation to direct their work toward explicit racial uplift; devoting talent to work that avoids this purpose is a failure of artistic responsibility.

(d) Several landmark Black artistic achievements derive their power precisely from formal ambiguity, emotional contradiction, and resistance to being reduced to a single uplifting message.

(e) Political meaning can reside in formal and structural properties—rhythm, harmony, compositional space—independently of narrative or representational content.

(f) Aesthetic value carries autonomous weight: a work can be good art partly through its internal form and emotional texture, not solely through its political function.

Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. Identify which pairs are genuinely incompatible, which only appear to conflict and dissolve on inspection, and for each real tension specify the least costly revision and exactly what it costs. Do not treat every friction as fatal, and do not assume I am prepared to abandon any particular commitment.
