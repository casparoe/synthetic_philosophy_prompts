She described this dissolution as "crossing the inviolate level," and added that Hofstadter treats this crossing as impossible within any formal system because Gödel's second incompleteness theorem demonstrates that a system can never contain a complete and consistent self-model. Consequently, she concluded that digital computers—being formal systems that maintain a strict software-hardware separation—are permanently incapable of achieving the collapsed hierarchical structure that consciousness requires, which she took to mean that Hofstadter's framework ultimately establishes a form of biological exceptionalism.

Could you evaluate this summary in detail? Please distinguish between what is a faithful rendering of Hofstadter's claims about strange loops and tangled hierarchies, what is a subtle conflation or overstatement—especially regarding the relationship between Gödelian incompleteness and self-reference in biological brains—and whether the inference about computers and biological exceptionalism actually follows from the accurate parts of the account, or whether it rests on a misinterpretation of both the inviolate level and the scope of formal self-reference.
