You chair the humanities division's interdisciplinary funding committee. A proposal has arrived to establish a Center for Geohistorical Ethics, which would apply Braudel's distinction between long-duration geographical and climatic structures—the longue durée—and short-term events or individual actions to the history of moral philosophy. The applicants claim that major normative frameworks, such as the prevalence of duty-based versus virtue-based reasoning, correlate more reliably with centuries-scale environmental conditions (resource predictability, agrarian stability, coastal versus inland geography) than with the arguments of particular philosophers, and they propose treating moral systems as structural outputs rather than products of rational deliberation. Your committee includes analytic moral philosophers who regard this as a reductive form of environmental determinism that eliminates the autonomy necessary for moral worth; historians sympathetic to Braudel who worry that importing his framework into ethics confuses causal explanation with normative justification; and one climate scientist who supports the correlation but doubts it can guide ethical reasoning. Draft the committee's written opinion for circulation before next Thursday's vote. Recommend whether to approve, reject, or approve with conditions; specify any conditions precisely enough that the applicants could respond to them. Your memo must explain Braudel's structural distinction clearly enough for non-historians, articulate the strongest version of the determinism objection without straw-manning it, and address whether a geohistorical approach to ethics can preserve any meaningful role for individual moral agency or for normative critique of existing structures. Include a concrete timeline for implementation or for a revised proposal, and identify the weakest point in the approval case that the committee should monitor if the center proceeds.
