I am preparing a graduate seminar on functional explanation in contemporary philosophy of biology, and I want the students to begin not with a survey of positions but with the full specification of what adequacy requires. Write the desiderata: the complete list of demands that any theory of biological function—whether etiological, causal-role, or otherwise—must meet to count as adequate, with the specific case, distinction, or empirical result behind each. In particular, the list must address: (a) how to assign functions to traits in synthetic or engineered organisms that lack deep evolutionary histories, and whether accommodating them forces a revision of the core etiological claim; (b) how to distinguish functional ascription from mere causal contribution in horizontally acquired genetic modules where selection history and current system role come apart; (c) whether the theory can preserve the normative dimension of proper function in clinical diagnostics and synthetic-biology design contexts without smuggling in intentional design; (d) consistency with evolutionary-developmental findings on functional redundancy, exaptation, and the dissociation of selected effects from current utility; and (e) whether it can explain malfunction in a way that does not collapse into statistical abnormality or mere causal failure. For each desideratum, state the motivating consideration, flag whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp, and identify any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—especially if accommodating engineered biological systems undermines the etiological account's distinctive advantage. Then, briefly and only to illustrate the criteria, indicate how the standard etiological framework and the causal-role framework fare against the list, but keep the conceptual weight on the requirements themselves and on which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates. Do not provide a bibliography or reading list; the point is the specification.
