In debates about virtual worlds people keep saying harming an avatar is like burning a photo of someone: damage is only symbolic, not real. They use it to argue virtual harms shouldn't be regulated. But then there's this weird case in some VR ethics papers where people talk about 'persistent virtual property' that carries over between sessions like a bank account. How far does the photo analogy actually hold? Give me the correspondence table -- whats the photo, whats the person, whats the fire -- then where it breaks, esp once we allow virtual objects can be persistent, inter-subjectively verified, and have causal effects IRL. Does the conclusion -- no real harm, no need to regulate -- survive once the disanalogies are on the table, and if the analogy is busted whats a better one?
