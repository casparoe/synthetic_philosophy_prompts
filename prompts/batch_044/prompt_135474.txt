Write a triptych poem — three stanzas of eight lines each — on Kant's three definitive articles for perpetual peace. The first stanza should treat the republican constitution, using an enclosed rhyme scheme (ABBA CDCD) to mirror internal consent enclosing sovereign power; the second should treat the federation of free states, using interlocking but non-hierarchical rhymes (ABA CBC DED) to represent an alliance that preserves each state's independence; the third should treat cosmopolitan right limited to universal hospitality, using open or slant-rhymed lines that refuse closure, reflecting the conditional and never-completed nature of that right. Within each stanza, weave two recurring voices: that of the moral politician (who treats peace as a mechanism of nature, commerce, and gradual institutional reform) and that of the political moralist (who demands that statecraft immediately conform to unconditional moral law). Do not resolve the tension between them; let their counter-lines pass each other without convergence, so that the poem's form enacts Kant's claim in the appendix that perpetual peace is an asymptotic idea of reason rather than a political state that can be fully realized. Use blank verse or loose iambic pentameter, avoid anachronism, and make the philosophical architecture accurate enough that a reader familiar with the essay recognizes the mapping of form to argument. Begin with the poem itself, with no preface or commentary.
