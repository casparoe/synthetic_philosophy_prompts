Write a joke about incommensurable values and hard choices. The joke should be philosophically coherent: a listener familiar with the concept should see that the punchline depends on the actual structure of incommensurability—specifically, that values such as dignity, loyalty, and welfare lack a shared metric and therefore resist rational trade-off through maximization or aggregation—rather than being a generic joke about indecision. To keep the framing from being something a model could regurgitate, ground the joke in this specific, constructed scenario: a government “Bureau of Ethical Translation” has built a device called the “Axiometric Comparator,” which claims to resolve hard choices by measuring every value in “hesitation-milliseconds” (the length of a decision-maker’s pause before choosing) and then computing an optimal ranking. The punchline should expose why this procedure necessarily fails according to the logic of incommensurability itself, not merely mock bureaucracy. Do not ask for citations, references, or a bibliography; the joke should stand on the conceptual reasoning alone.
