I am drafting a proposal for a six-month independent project in my graduate seminar on moral emotions and comparative ethics, and I need help sharpening the framing. The course has examined whether guilt or shame is the more defensible moral emotion—guilt for being action-directed and preserving individual self-worth, shame for being socially embedded and capable of deeper self-revision—but I want to move past the standard individual-level comparison. My specific interest is whether a culture can genuinely choose to cultivate one over the other, and whether the normative answer to “which is better” shifts depending on whether we evaluate from the first-person phenomenological perspective of the moral agent or from the third-person institutional-design perspective of a society seeking to maintain pluralism. I suspect the literature stalls here because both anthropological and psychological treatments treat cultural emotion as something a society “has” rather than something it “builds” through legal, ritual, and linguistic infrastructure—what I want to call emotional grammar. So my precise research question is: if we distinguish three senses of cultural choice—deliberate policy, emergent normative convergence, and structural constraint—does either guilt or shame consistently emerge as the better moral emotion across all three, or does the evaluation split by choice-type? I need the proposal to include: a sharply stated version of that question; an explanation of why existing positions in moral psychology and descriptive anthropology leave the normative collective-level question open; a conceptual (not empirical) method that tests the compatibility of each emotion with the three choice-senses; a description of what a successful result would look like, such as a decision matrix showing that shame is preferable only under structural constraint while guilt dominates under deliberate policy; a clear statement of what would count as failure, for instance if the framework collapses into pure sociology of norms or pure individual phenomenology; and an honest assessment of whether this is realistic for six months of work without archival or field research. I will add all citations and references myself, so please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or named literature review.
