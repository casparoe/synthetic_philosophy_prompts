I've been arguing with my roommate about Dennetts Quining Qualia essay — the one with Chase and Sanborn tasting Maxwell House coffee. For my philosophy of mind course I sketched a counter-argument: instead of treating Chase and Sanborn as disagreeing about some inner movie, I think theyre running the same hierarchical predictive model with different precission weights on sensory versus affective error signals. Chase down-weights the bottom-up taste mismatch and up-weights his evaluative prior, so he reports same coffee but a better appreciation of it; Sanborn up-weights the sensory mismatch, so he reports that it tastes like dishwater now. I call this the precission-divergence reading. If thats right, quining a quale isnt incoherent — its just redescribing a shift in precission allocation — but it also means Dennetts intuition pump misses its target because there never was a pure quale to deny in the first place. I worry Im just smuggling in predictive-processing machinery that Dennett never intended to engage, and that Im redefining the problem rather than solving it.

Could you give me exactly five brief critical comments on this line of thought? Number them 1 to 5, each just a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
