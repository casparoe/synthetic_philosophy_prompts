My roommate and his cousin got into it over dinner about whether DNA is literally a "code" or just a metaphor scientists lean on. The cousin said its literally code: four letters make three-letter words that map to amino acids, and the ribosome translates them like a compiler running source code. He said the mapping is mostly arbitrary — UUU means phenylalanine but could have meant something else — so you've got real syntax and semantics, not just chemistry happening.

My roommate pushed back and said thats just a fancy description of molecules fitting together. The ribosome doesnt "read" anything; its just shapes locking in. She said calling it a code sneaks in an intentional decoder that biology doesnt actually have, kind of like saying gravity is a "code" for falling rocks.

The cousin came back saying computer code doesnt need consciousness either — a compiler doesnt understand C++. You just need a system that maps symbols to outputs. So the ribosome is the compiler, and the arbitrariness of the codon table proves its symbolic, not purely causal.

Then my roommate brought up that mitochondria use their own variant of the table, where signals that usually mean "stop" actually code for amino acids in there. She said if DNA were a true formal language with universal syntax, it shouldnt have regional dialects. That makes it more like evolutionary slang than actual language.

The cousin said programming languages have dialects and deprecated commands too, so that actually strengthens the analogy. But then he went further: if DNA is literally text, then CRISPR is basically text editing, and copying a gene from a jellyfish into a rabbit should be treated like plagiarism or stealing someones work, not just chemistry.

My roommate said thats absurd — theres no author to steal from, and genes arent intellectual property just because we use a linguistic metaphor for them.
