I'm preparing questions for a podcast interview with a philosopher—call her Dr. Elena Vasquez—who has recently defended a rehabilitated, non-essentialist concept of national character. On her view, national character is not an aggregate of individual psychological traits nor a biological essence, but a modal-structural property of shared institutions and collective practices that shapes how a polity typically responds to certain kinds of practical and moral problems. I want six to eight questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the view. Specifically: one that presses the ecological-fallacy worry and asks how collective-level dispositions can be real without being reducible to member-level statistics, with a follow-up anticipating her appeal to collective intentionality or institutional role-structures; one on whether her framework can distinguish legitimate generalization from harmful stereotyping without collapsing into mere political caution, including the follow-up for when she claims the distinction is purely normative; one that tests conceptual coherence by asking what "character" denotes if it allows for rapid cultural change, large internal pluralism, and extensive diaspora populations—followed by the reply she'll likely give about core versus peripheral practices; one on moral responsibility: if nations genuinely have character in her sense, does that ground collective moral agency in ways that risk excusing individual wrongdoing or, conversely, blaming individuals for structural outcomes; and a final question that asks what observable evidence or institutional feature could in principle count against attributing a specific national character, designed to make it hard for her to retreat to the claim that the view is only interpretive. Each question should be sharp enough for a colloquium audience but accessible to informed non-specialists.
