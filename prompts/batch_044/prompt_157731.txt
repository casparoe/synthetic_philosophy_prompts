Please explain the conceptual architecture of indirect normativity as it operates within frameworks of idealized deliberation, with particular attention to the problem of normative transmission between counterfactual agreement and actual practical reasoning. The core issue is this: in many constructivist accounts, a principle gains authority not because any real agent directly endorses it from her present standpoint, but because it survives a deliberative procedure conducted under conditions—full information, mutual disinterest, rational coherence—that real agents cannot jointly satisfy. This creates an intermediate layer: the principle binds actual agents indirectly, via the hypothetical verdict of these idealized deliberators. Your task is to clarify the precise logical form of this indirectness. Is the actual agent's reason for compliance mediated by a belief about what the idealized agents would choose (making the norm instrumentally indirect), or is the normative content itself constituted by conditions that lie outside any actual agent's cognitive reach (making the norm constitutively indirect)? Then address the following tension: if idealization is understood as an improvement of real agents' own capacities—correcting for bias, ignorance, or partiality—then the resulting agreement seems like a purified version of direct self-legislation, and the normativity is only derivatively indirect. But if the idealization introduces structural constraints—such as the exclusion of particular identities or the equal weighting of interests—that real agents could not rationally accept without abandoning projects constitutive of their agency, then the authority of the principle originates in a standpoint that is not merely improved but alien to actual practical identity. Explain whether this stronger form of indirect normativity can still be reconciled with the idea that moral obligations are self-legislated, or whether it forces us to abandon that claim and treat the idealized deliberative framework as an external normative authority. Treat the problem structurally rather than through the exegesis of particular theorists.
