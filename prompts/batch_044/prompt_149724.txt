I am a novelist drafting a satirical chapter about the bureaucratization of moral philosophy, and I need you to produce an in-world appendix document. You are acting as a consulting philosopher and professional satirist with deep familiarity in analytic political philosophy. Before writing, please think carefully and begin with brief internal brainstorming: outline three concrete administrative scenarios in which an institution attempts to enforce both halves of Robert Nozick's well-known dual framework—aggregate welfare maximization applied to non-human animals, paired with strict, non-aggregable Kantian-style side-constraints protecting human persons—within a single policy structure. Identify which scenario best exposes the tension or absurdity latent in maintaining both halves with perfect consistency, especially at the boundary where agency or sentience becomes ambiguous. Only after completing this internal outline should you proceed to draft the final piece.

Write a sustained parody in the form of a deadpan, bureaucratic Frequently Asked Questions document—approximately 650 to 800 words, formatted as numbered questions and answers—for a fictional federal agency called the Office of Dual-Framework Interspecies Governance (ODFIG). The voice must be that of a meticulous, well-meaning civil servant who never breaks character, never winks, and treats every entry with identical administrative gravity. The satire must arise from the internal logic of Nozick's actual position rather than from invented distortions: exaggerate the real feature that individual animal suffering can be legitimately traded for net aggregate welfare gains, and the real feature that no number of human benefits can justify harming a single person protected by side-constraints, then show what happens when both rules are enforced by the same office.

To keep the prompt from yielding a standard textbook summary, inject the following niche institutional detail: ODFIG operates a classified Tier-3 Marginal Agency Assessment Protocol that uses neuro-behavioral diagnostics to determine whether specific non-standard entities—such as urban corvids fitted with experimentally enhanced working-memory implants, or chimeric organisms containing partial human neural tissue—have crossed the threshold that triggers side-constraints rather than aggregate-utilitarian management. The FAQ should address this protocol in at least two answers, treating the boundary problem with the same bureaucratic neutrality applied to parking regulations or waste disposal. Do not resolve the philosophical ambiguity; let the deadpan administrative language carry the irony.

The piece should be readable to someone with no prior knowledge of Nozick, but a specialist should recognize that the satire is fair—derived from the genuine structure of the view rather than a strawman. Do not include citations, footnotes, bibliographies, references to specific page numbers, or requests for further reading. Do not place any real philosophical text inside quotation marks; paraphrase all concepts freely in your own administrative prose. Maintain sustained irony throughout so that a reader can tell the framework is being ironized without the narrator ever signaling awareness of the absurdity.
