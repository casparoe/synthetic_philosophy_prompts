I'd like a consistency check on my view of standing to blame and hypocrisy, particularly where I combine a 'clean hands' condition with what I call normative residue: the idea that certain social roles retain a residual authority to blame even after the agent's moral credibility is partly depleted.

My commitments are roughly these:

(1) Standing is fundamentally undermined by hypocrisy, but only of the self-exempting kind: one loses standing by applying a norm to others while treating oneself as not bound by it, not merely by having failed to meet the norm.

(2) Even so, a parent retains standing to blame a child for dishonesty despite being dishonest in professional contexts, because the parental role generates normative residue that is not cancelled by unrelated moral failures.

(3) In cases of structural injustice, beneficiaries of an unjust system lack standing to blame individuals for participating in it, even when those beneficiaries actively oppose the system and do not self-exempt.

(4) The point of blame is to communicate a shared normative expectation rather than to express moral superiority.

(5) Genuine reform restores standing, but only for wrongs of a different kind from those one previously committed; one never regains standing to blame others for the very fault one once shared.

Are these jointly tenable? Please discriminate carefully: which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and rank the tensions by severity. For each real tension, identify the cheapest revision and its cost. Finally, take a definite position on whether the package can be saved without abandoning normative residue; if it cannot, say which single revision cleans up the most conflicts and why.
