Jeffrey conditionalization is Jeffrey's rule for updating when evidence is uncertain. you partition the sample space into cells {E_i}, assign new probabilities q_i, and compute P_new(H) = Σ q_i P(H|E_i). this avoids conditioning on a specific proposition with prior probability zero.

the old-evidence problem is that if you already knew E, then P(E|T) ≈ P(E) ≈ 1, so Bayes gives no confirmation. Jeffrey solves this because he updates weights over the whole partition rather than conditioning on E itself. even if one cell corresponds to old evidence, shifting mass into other cells lets confirmation flow indirectly through the conditionals P(H|E_i).

in the gauge example, the agent partitions over the two sources: direct (D) and proxy (P). she sets q_D = 0.7 and q_P = 0.3 based on her prior failure rates. then she applies Jeffrey's formula, computing her posterior over θ as P_new(θ) = 0.7 P(θ | s, D) + 0.3 P(θ | s, P). she treats the unknown drift f as part of the conditional in the P term. this averages over source uncertainty without conditioning on which instrument actually fired, giving a single updated belief about θ.
