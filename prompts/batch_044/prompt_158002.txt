Construct a realistic, single-paragraph workplace or institutional scenario—no science fiction, no trolley problems—in which a dispute over resource allocation or procedural authority is evaluated by three positions: (1) a Pascalian conventionalist view holding that justice is whatever is established by custom and sustained by force, and that the strongest party should be just so as to prevent disorder; (2) a natural-rights view holding that justice tracks pre-institutional entitlements independent of established rules; and (3) a pure might-makes-right view holding that the currently dominant actor’s preference determines the outcome. Engineer the case so that, contrary to the usual expectation, the Pascalian position requires defending the weaker claimant against the currently more powerful actor. The reversal must depend specifically on the “force” sustaining the relevant custom being structural or institutional rather than personal or coercive in the immediate interaction.

After presenting the scenario, respond in the following sections in order:

A. The scenario (one paragraph, realistic setting, specifying the parties, the resource or authority at stake, and the established institutional rule that creates the tension).

B. The three verdicts (approximately 150 words): state the decision or allocation each position demands and the reasoning that produces it, with particular care in showing why the Pascalian view does not simply endorse the presently stronger party.

C. The isolating feature (approximately 100 words): identify the exact structural element—e.g., institutional inertia, procedural precedent, or distributed enforcement—that does the work in reversing the Pascalian verdict, and explain what happens to that verdict if the feature is removed so that only immediate personal power remains.

D. The power-politics rebuttal (approximately 100 words): show how a defender of pure might-makes-right would try to dissolve the case, especially by arguing that institutional inertia does not count as “force” in Pascal’s sense, and note whether that attack succeeds or leaves the conventionalist view intact.
