I'm taking a graduate seminar in modal metaphysics and have been asked to design an editorial protocol for our student journal's anonymous submissions in philosophy of religion. I need a reusable decision procedure—something I can run in about fifteen minutes with only a semester of modal logic behind me—for evaluating papers that present modal ontological arguments in the style of Gödel's S5 version, including simpler variants. The procedure should translate the logical constraints on positive properties, necessary existence, and modal collapse into an ordered sequence of checks, each with explicit pass/fail conditions and a clear point where judgment is unavoidable rather than mechanical. It must distinguish formal failure (e.g., deriving existence in modal logic K without the S5 accessibility conditions that license the modal transfer) from framework disagreement (e.g., rejecting S5 altogether), and it must flag the step where an argument avoids every formal error yet depends on a contested axiom about the positivity of a property. Apply the procedure to three hypothetical submissions: (A) a standard argument deriving necessary existence from the consistency and positivity of being maximally excellent; (B) an argument using identical premises but restricted to modal logic K, where the procedure should halt and specify whether this counts as a logical mistake or only a framework mismatch; and (C) an argument that technically satisfies every check but concludes that a maximally deficient being necessarily exists, so the procedure approves a result I find metaphysically absurd. For each case, identify the exact step that renders the verdict, state whether the procedure permits an editorial override without internal contradiction, and be explicit about what the protocol can never settle—namely, whether the premises are true—versus what it can settle about formal validity. The deliverable should be the procedure itself, followed by the three worked applications.
