For an independent study I am writing for my philosophy course, I have tried to formalize my commitments about rational choice, and I want a consistency check before I present them. Here is my position: (1) I accept updateless decision theory—the rationality of a decision rule is evaluated globally across all worlds in which it is implemented, without conditioning on local information about one's own action. (2) In Newcomb's problem, I one-box. (3) In the hitchhiker case—where a driver demands payment after delivering you, having predicted your willingness to pay—I think you should pay, even though the prediction is already fixed and you have no further causal influence. (4) In the counterfactual mugging scenario—where a predictor has already flipped a coin, and you are asked for money after learning that the coin landed unfavorably—I think refusal is rational, because your present choice is causally disconnected from the payoff event, and updating on the revealed outcome is appropriate. (5) I hold that decision-theoretic rationality should be temporally invariant: the correct recommendation must not depend on the arbitrary moment at which you first formulate the problem. (6) Finally, I have adopted what I call the principle of algorithmic transparency: if I acquire evidence that my decision procedure has been systematically miscalibrated in past applications, I should revise my confidence in that procedure, even within an otherwise updateless framework.

Do these six commitments form a coherent package? I am especially concerned that (4) introduces a local update that (1) and (5) seem to forbid, and that (6) creates a revision mechanism that undermines the global evaluation required by (1). At the same time, (2) and (3) appear to rely on exactly the kind of non-causal correlation that (4) rejects. Is there a genuine contradiction between (3) and (4), or is the tension only apparent? Does (6) conflict with (5), or does it dissolve once we distinguish revising confidence in a procedure from updating on an action? Please audit these pairs, rank the tensions by severity, indicate what the least costly revision would be, and point out any tension that looks serious but actually dissolves on closer inspection.
