I'm a physicist working on open quantum systems; my vocabulary is decoherence, entanglement entropy, density matrices, observer-relative branches, and information flow. I have no formal philosophy training.

Restate the Epicurean argument that death is not bad — both the claim that there is no subject present to suffer harm and the symmetry argument comparing pre-birth and post-death non-existence — in these terms, as faithfully as possible. If Epicurus says harm requires a subject who exists, translate that into claims about branches, observers, or information preservation; if he appeals to symmetry, translate that into properties of the quantum state before and after an observer's decoherence.

Then report the residue: which parts map cleanly, which require distortion (for instance, does 'no longer existing' become 'no branch' or 'decohered information,' and are those equivalent?), and whether anything in the argument — perhaps about the value of future goods, or about our asymmetric concern for the future — has no counterpart in this framework. Is the residue a defect in the physical vocabulary, or does it reveal that part of Epicurus's reasoning depends on a metaphysics of presence that this vocabulary abandons?

Keep the translation honest: do not assume consciousness is just classical information storage merely to make the mapping work.
