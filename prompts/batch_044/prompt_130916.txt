Student answer: Both the Lost Cause and Masada are best understood as clear instances of retrospective moral framing. Both narratives therefore depend on rehabilitating the actual cause that was lost.

Moreover, both exhibit strong counterfactual dependency. Because both cases combine retrospective framing of the defeated cause with counterfactual dependency on the act of resistance, the comparison suggests that lost-cause myths constitute a unified category.
