I'm writing a piece on how communicative meaning is distributed across nested interpretive layers, and in a recent conversation with a fellow editor I found myself stuck on a specific question arising from Douglas Hofstadter's tripartite distinction in Gödel, Escher, Bach—frame messages (the contextual rules that make interpretation possible), outer messages (the explicit intent being communicated), and inner messages (the literal content embedded within). My colleague argues that in genuinely self-referential utterances—say, a statement that asserts its own unprovability within a formal system—the frame message carries the decisive semantic weight: alter the framing regime and the inner message doesn't merely become false, it loses its original truth-conditions entirely. I am less convinced; my own credence that frame-replacement destroys truth-conditions more reliably than outer-message-replacement is about 0.55, but I want to pressure-test that intuition.

So I am asking for your precise credence, on a scale of 0 to 1, in the following claim: in any communicative act that contains all three Hofstadterian layers and involves genuine self-reference (not simple ambiguity), if one preserves the inner message exactly but systematically substitutes the frame message with one drawn from an incompatible interpretive regime—reading a formal proof as poetic metaphor, for instance—the inner message forfeits its original truth-conditions in a greater proportion of cases than if one preserves the frame but substitutes the outer message with an incompatible communicative intent. Please do not answer with "it depends on your philosophy of language"; I want a number, together with an explicit decomposition. Break your reasoning into at least these sub-questions: (a) whether the three-layer model is descriptively accurate for human communication or mainly a pedagogical scaffold; (b) whether self-reference can be reconstructed purely syntactically without frame-dependency; (c) whether "truth-conditions" is even the correct metric, as opposed to mere interpretability or coherence; and (d) whether the comparison between frame-replacement and outer-message-replacement is genuinely symmetric, or whether one kind of substitution is structurally more destructive by definition. Assign a rough probability to each, show how they combine to your final figure, and tell me which single sub-question, if resolved firmly against your current view, would shift your number the most. If you think the disagreement between my 0.55 and a reasonable opposing position lives mainly in one of these four components, say which one and why.
