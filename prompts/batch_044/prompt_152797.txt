hey, since retiring from physics i've been reading philosophy in a scattered way — mostly Hofstadter, Dennett, and whatever my book clb picks. last week my freind summarized Hofstadter's view on whether the 'I' can live in other brains. she said that for Hofstadter, the self isn't a soul or a physical center but a 'strange loop' — a pattern of symbols that refers back to itself — and because it's just a pattern, it can be instantiated in multiple brains at once. she said that when people interact with you, they build up a more or less accurate copy of your loop inside their own heads, so after death your self-pattern continues running in them, especially in people who knew you well. she also said this is why Hofstadter isn't afraid of death: you're not annihilated, you're just redistributed. and she tied this to Gödel's incompleteness theorem, saying the strange loop works the same way — a system that talks about itself without being able to fully prove its own consistency.

something about this feels too neat. is she right that Hofstadter thinks the self is literally the same loop running in multiple brains? or is he more careful about saying it's a representation or echo? and does the Gödel connection actually support the idea of distributed identity, or is that her stretching the analogy? also she mentioned that small differences in the copy don't matter — that a 'fuzzy' version of your loop in a stranger's head still counts as part of your self — which sounds like she's smuggling in some extended-mind idea that Hofstadter never actually endorsed. can you separate what's accurate here, what's a standard pop-philosophy simplification, and what might be her own addition?
