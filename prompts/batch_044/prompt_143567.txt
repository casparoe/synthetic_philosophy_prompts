I have been thinking, without any formal training in economics or philosophy, about why the United Kingdom's Shared Parental Leave reform of 2015 seemed to promise a genuine shift in how dual-earner families divide care, yet left the gender gap in both unpaid work and career progression largely untouched. The usual explanations talk about money and workplace culture, but I keep returning to a distinction I have not seen named properly: the gap between the formal allocation of leave days, which the law made interchangeable, and what I call the interstitial burden — all the unpredictable, unmeasured care labour that falls outside any leave entitlement, such as arranging emergency childcare, managing nursery closures, or absorbing the administrative mental load of family life. I would like you to analyse the 2015 reform as a case study through this lens. First, set out what the interstitial-burden framing predicts about how a dual-earner household ought to respond when leave becomes formally gender-neutral but the hidden scheduling and emotional work does not. Then apply that to the actual uptake figures and to the well-known pattern that mothers still take longer career breaks and suffer larger earnings penalties: does this confirm the framing, or does it reveal an anomaly — for instance, that financial constraints or employer attitudes were doing the real work regardless of who handled the interstitial tasks? Be honest about where the theory is silent: can it explain why uptake was especially low among lower-income couples, or does it need to be supplemented by other considerations? Finally, reverse the direction and tell me what the UK episode shows about the concept itself. Does it demonstrate that formal equality in leave design is structurally undermined by invisible care asymmetries, or does it suggest that the interstitial burden is only decisive once economic and cultural barriers have already been lowered? Please present everything in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as though you were composing a careful letter or email.
