I have been working through recent literature on panpsychism for an informal reading group, and I keep encountering a particular argumentative move that I cannot locate in the standard taxonomy of objections. The move runs as follows. A panpsychist introduces a relation—call it phenomenal integration or co-subjectivity—to explain how discrete micro-experiences combine into a unified macro-subject. Critics then press for an account of what makes this relation hold: does it depend on structural features alone, or does it require consciousness at both ends? The theorist responds by characterizing the relation in terms that appear to presuppose the macro-level unity being constructed—for example, by defining integration as holding precisely when micro-experiences are jointly accessible from a single experiential perspective, or when they share a subject in the very sense that needs explaining. The relation is initially presented as an objective, consciousness-independent bridge analogous to physical binding, but its elucidation repeatedly appeals to conditions that are only satisfied if the macro-subject already exists.

This is not, I think, identical to the combination problem itself, which asks generally how many subjects become one. It is more specific: a proposed solution to combination that implicitly assumes the phenomenon it was meant to generate. I have seen versions of it in both constitutive and emergent formulations of panpsychism, and it feels distinct from a plain petitio principii because the circularity is localized in the conditions of application for a technical term rather than in the overall argument structure. Is there an established philosophical label for this exact failure mode—this smuggling of macro-level consciousness into the conditions of a relation that was supposed to generate it?

If such a concept exists, I would like to know how it is precisely distinguished from the general combination problem, from standard objections about the mysteriousness of psychophysical relations, and from ordinary circular reasoning. If it lacks a dedicated name, I would ask you to coin one, together with a clear definition and boundary conditions that would let me point to an argument and say exactly which move is being made, without conflating it with broader anti-panpsychist objections. I am not seeking a reading list or citations—only conceptual clarification and, if necessary, a new term with its edges well marked.
