I'm a senior researcher at a genocide-studies archive who must code perpetrator testimonies for an exhibition, classifying each account as best explained by situational conformity and peer pressure in the moment (in the manner of Browning's analysis of Reserve Police Battalion 101) or by prior ideological commitment and culturally embedded hatred (in the manner of Goldhagen's thesis of eliminatory antisemitism). I apply this dozens of times under time pressure, with only a research assistant checking consistency, and the museum director must sign off on any classification that exculpates. Write a reusable decision procedure—ordered questions with evidence thresholds—that I can run on a testimony and its contextual record. Include a step testing for "discursive residue" (whether the perpetrator uses bureaucratic euphemism versus explicit eliminationist language) and a step testing for "unobserved deviation" (whether the actor refused or modified orders when not directly supervised). For each step, specify what answer pushes the classification toward situational, toward ideological, or into an undetermined zone; mark explicit stop points where evidence is insufficient and the default must be "undetermined" rather than either explanation; and flag where the framing becomes a value judgment (e.g., whether a situational explanation amounts to moral excuse) that must be escalated to the director rather than resolved by procedure. Apply it to two cases: (a) a policeman who requested transfer after his first shooting, was denied, and continued; and (b) a local official who volunteered for auxiliary duties, used explicit racial slurs in private letters, but later claimed he was only following orders—this second case should yield a classification I will find uncomfortable. Conclude with an honest statement of what the procedure cannot settle, especially retrospective testimony bias, and identify which judgments remain irreducibly interpretive. Finally, say what would change your verdict on this framework: which empirical finding about perpetrator motivation or which conceptual argument about how situational and ideological factors interact would lead you to revise the procedure's weighting, and point to the specific step in your reasoning most likely to fail.
