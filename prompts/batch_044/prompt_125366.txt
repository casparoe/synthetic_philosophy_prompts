A tribunal that adjudicates resource disputes in enclosed settlements has ruled as follows: it permitted the exclusion of unregistered stowaways from a vessel at its engineered capacity, even though registered occupants held surplus private provisions that could not be legally pooled; it rejected a proposal to draw lots for exclusion when water was inadequate at standard consumption but adequate if all halved intake; it approved giving registered crew priority over non-registered passengers for emergency oxygen during a breach that made scarcity unavoidable regardless of rationing; it rejected citizenship-based exclusion from a state-funded shelter that was at nominal occupancy but could physically accommodate all with reduced space; and it approved a mandatory 40 per cent universal consumption reduction rather than expelling anyone when damage made supplies tight but technically sufficient with collective austerity.

The tribunal now faces a deep-space habitat carrying 400 registered colonists and 50 hidden stowaways. A micrometeorite strike damages the oxygen recyclers. Engineers report that with universal rationing all 450 could survive the journey, but with standard consumption exactly 50 must be expelled; however, the rationed level required to save everyone would cause irreversible cognitive impairment across the entire population. The colonists hold pre-launch contracts; the stowaways do not.

What should the tribunal decide to remain consistent with itself?
