I'm putting together my actual stance on citizen-state visibility for a rationalist meetup i'm running, and i want a consistency check before i present. I was arguing about this with friends after skimming some old threads on state capacity and privacy, and my view turned out to be a frankenstein of german-style data protection, american-style openness, and a weird rationalist twist on chinese social-credit logic. Here are my commitments:

(a) For collective-action domains—climate emissions, tax compliance, public health—citizens should be maximally legible to the state via continuous verification, not self-reporting. I think of this as a soft social-credit layer restricted strictly to collective-risk behaviors, with no moral-character scoring.

(b) For everything else—social ties, consumption patterns, psychological traits—the state should be constitutionally barred from retaining or inferring data, enforced by strict data-minimization rules. I want near-absolute opacity here.

(c) I hold an american-style openness principle applied symmetrically: if the state holds data or runs algorithms about you, you get full audit rights, including model weights and reasoning traces, not just a summary report.

(d) Democratic legitimacy requires pseudonymous or anonymous political speech and association, but i don't think that anonymity should be usable as cover for evading the collective-risk obligations in (a).

(e) I think the only credible enforcement mechanism for (b) is a decentralized, citizen-controlled audit layer—basically citizens hold the encryption keys—but i still think (a) requires state coercion to actually function.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how severely. For each real tension, say what the cheapest revision is and what it costs me. Also flag any pairs that only look tense but dissolve once you separate domains or mechanisms. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad. Then take a definite stand: tell me which single commitment i should revise or drop if i want to clean up the most problems at once, and defend that choice—don't just lay out the menu and leave me to pick. I want a committed audit, not a both-sides list.
