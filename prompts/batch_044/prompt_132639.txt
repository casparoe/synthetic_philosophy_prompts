I'm trying to pin down what i actually think about phenomenal consciousness and illusionism, going by my gut on cases. When someone has blindsight and can navigate around obstacles but sincerely denies seeing anything, i think they're genuinely not phenomenally conscious of the obstacle — it's not just a reporting failure. When I'm totally absorded in working through a proof and someone asks what it's like right now, i feel like there's barely any phenomenal content at all — not unconscious, just phenomenally thin, like the reasoning is running without any inner glow. But in deep meditaion people report "pure awareness with no object" and i actually buy that there's a rich, luminous phenomenal quality there even though there's no content — which feels weird because the math case is also low-content but i think it's the opposite, phenomenally empty. In dreams i think the actual REM experience is much thinner than the vivid story we tell ourselves afterwards, but i still think there's some minimal "what it's like" happening in the moment. With split-brain patients where the left hemispere confabulates reasons for actions the right side initiated, i don't think there's one unified phenomenal field — probably two fragmentary ones, or maybe none on the left and a minimal one on the right. And then there's this hypothetical silent processor i keep imagining — a system that does full visual processing, generates correct verbal reports about colors and shapes, but has no inner screen or echo at all. I think that system genuinely lacks phenomenal consciousness, unlike the math-absorbed me who is just thin.

So my verdicts are all over the place: blindsight is out, math absorption is in-but-thin, meditation emptiness is richly in, dreams are minimally in, split-brain is fragmented, silent processor is out. Is there one principle that sorts all these? Maybe something about whether a state is available for a certain kind of meta-representational echo, or whether it has cognitive ownership? I keep goign back and forth on the meditation vs math case — they both seem like low-content high-focus states, but i assign them totally different phenomenal status. What's the principle that saves that distinction without making the silent processor come out wrong? And what would it say about a case i haven't considered: someone in a flow-state playing music where they can't report what they're playing but the performance is flawless? Which of my verdicts does it force me to drop?
