I am a doctoral student in moral philosophy serving on my university's Committee on Institutional Naming and Ethical Endowment. We must vote Thursday on whether to accept a $4.5 million gift that would establish the "Truman Center for Strategic Decision-Making," honoring President Harry Truman, with an annual lecture series on presidential ethics. A faculty-student coalition has objected, invoking G.E.M. Anscombe's argument that Truman's authorization of the atomic bombings was not a case of double effect but an intentional directing of the will toward the killing of innocents—murder, in her framework—because he used the deaths of civilians as a means to end the war. They contend that affixing Truman's name to our institution would analogously direct our institutional intention toward honoring a murderer, rather than merely accepting an unavoidable association with a complex historical figure.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action—accept with a specific renaming or programmatic condition, reject outright, or accept only under a clearly defined temporal review clause—together with an implementation time frame (e.g., within thirty or ninety days). The memo must treat Anscombe's critique with philosophical seriousness rather than dismissing it as anachronistic, and it must honestly engage the strongest consideration on the opposing side: that rejecting the gift would deprive students and faculty of substantial resources, and that demanding moral purity from historical namesakes is neither feasible nor conducive to serious ethical study. To keep the reasoning precise, apply Anscombe's distinction between directing one's intention to evil and accepting evil only as a foreseen but unintended side-effect reflexively to our committee's own decision—specifically, examine whether naming a center after Truman is structurally closer to directing institutional honor toward murder, or closer to accepting moral complexity as an unavoidable byproduct of fostering rigorous public examination of exactly these questions. Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or suggestions for further reading, since this is an internal working document. Please conclude with a single line beginning exactly "Verdict: " followed by your brief recommendation.
