I am delivering a response paper at a philosophy of physics workshop next month, defending a position I call regional structuralism about Einstein's hole argument. Rather than treating the argument only as a determinism problem for generally covariant field equations, I claim that the hole demonstrates that matter-free regions possess no intrinsic individuation conditions apart from the relational metric structure extending through them. This is meant to steer between standard substantivalism — which must regard diffeomorphically-related metrics as physically distinct states — and full Leibnizian relationism, which denies spacetime any ontological weight. My audience comprises about ten philosophers of physics and two numerical-relativity physicists. Roughly half are sympathetic to ontic structural realism in physics; two are committed defenders of manifold substantivalism and its standard replies to the hole argument; one physicist cares primarily about whether any of this undermines deterministic prediction in simulation; the remainder are agnostic but well versed in the canonical presentations of the argument. I need a Q&A prep sheet: list the eight or so objections I will actually face, ordered by how damaging they are to regional structuralism, each phrased as that kind of interlocutor would phrase it. For each, provide a reply I can deliver in under two minutes, together with an honest assessment of where that reply is soft. Specify what I should concede upfront rather than defend. Finally, include the question I most hope nobody asks — namely, the one that exposes the tension between my denial of intrinsic regional identity and my reliance on a well-defined boundary to constitute the hole in the first place. Do not provide bibliographies, citation lists, or reading recommendations; I need arguments, not references.
