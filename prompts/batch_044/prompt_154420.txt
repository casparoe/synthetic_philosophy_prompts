My father has a neurological condition that leaves him unable to report anything he sees on his right side, yet he consistently flinches if an object moves quickly there, and—more puzzling—he continues to paint abstract canvases whose color palettes match objects placed on that side, even though he insists he chooses the colors at random. His neurologist mentioned a theory that for any experience to be genuinely conscious, the information must be shared widely enough across the brain to support speech, planning, and deliberate action, not just handled in isolated processing centers. I am trying to decide how to relate to him. Should I treat his verbal denial as the final word and assume there is no inner visual experience on that side, or should his flinching and his painting count as signs that something is being experienced—just in a way that never reaches the parts of him that talk or reach on purpose? More specifically, if local brain processes can drive both a defensive reflex and a complex creative choice without ever connecting to the systems that generate reports, does that mean awareness can exist in a kind of isolation, or does it just show that very sophisticated behavior can happen entirely in the dark?
