I'm presenting at our dept philosophy of mind reading group next tuesday. My thesis is that transformative experiences—like becoming a parent or getting a cochlear implant—aren't epistemically special in L.A. Paul's sense. I think the epistemic transformation she describes is actually just analogical self-projection breaking down: you can't map your current conceptual framework onto the future state because the relevant dimensions shift, not because there's some irreducible first-person access gap. The audience includes two committed Paul defenders, a cognitive scientist who works on analogy and knows Hofstadter and Sander at a pop-science level, and grad students who think this whole debate is overblown. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased like those two defenders and the analogy person would phrase them, with for each what I should concede, what I should hold, and where my reply is weakest. I especially want to be ready for: am I just redescribing Paul's phenomenon? Does analogical failure actually solve the rational-choice problem about deciding whether to have a child? And what's the weakest point in my own position—the part I should actually defend rather than hope nobody notices?
