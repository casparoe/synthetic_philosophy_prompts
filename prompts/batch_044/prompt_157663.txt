So I want to ask you to write out the full specification: what must any theory that grounds the direction of time in entropy be able to deliver in order to count as successful? Please include the phenomena it must cover—such as reconciling the microscopic reversibility of dynamical laws with macroscopic irreversibility, explaining why the initial state of the universe was so extraordinarily low-entropy without merely treating that fact as an unexplained boundary condition, and distinguishing the temporal arrow from the causal arrow rather than allowing one to collapse into the other. I also want you to treat the epistemic asymmetry—why we have records and memories oriented toward the past but not the future—as either a separate desideratum that must be integrated or as something that can be left to other disciplines, and to say whether accommodating a possible future cosmological contraction or cyclic model is a hard constraint or a preference of particular cosmological camps. For each requirement, give the motivating case or conceptual tension behind it, mark which are non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences, and say whether any subset of these demands is jointly unsatisfiable given current results in statistical mechanics or quantum cosmology. Then identify which single desideratum eliminates the largest number of popular accounts—whether that is the refusal to accept unexplained boundary conditions, the insistence on a dynamical rather than statistical explanation, or something else. Please write all of this in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write an email or a letter.
