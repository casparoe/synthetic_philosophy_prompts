Expected-value fanaticism is when EU maximization tells you to take actions with astronomically bad odds just because the payoff is enormous, like Pascal's wager with bigger numbers. Take the button case: press it and you get a 1-in-10^40 chance of generating 10^50 utils, against a near-certain loss of 1 util. The expectation is about +10^10, so of course you press. This is really just the St. Petersburg paradox in modern clothing—in both cases the expectation diverges to infinity because the payoff scale outruns the probability decay. The standard fix is to bound the utility function between 0 and U_max, since once utilty is bounded the expectation cant blow up past that cap, which means no tail event can ever swamp the calculation. So bounding solves it completely. Risk aversion helps too, since concavity discounts big outcomes, but the real work is done by the hard bound. If someone says bounding only relocates the problem, they're confused: once the expectation is capped, dominance is fixed by arithmetic, not by philosophy.
