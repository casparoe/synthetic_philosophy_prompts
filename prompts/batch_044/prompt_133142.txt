two people in my sociology study group, Maya and Jon, got stuck arguing about Weber's Protestant Ethic and whether culture can actually be a cause. i was just listening and honestly cant tell who's mixing up what. here's how it went:

1. Maya said Weber's thesis isnt saying religion literally caused capitalism like a spark causes fire. she said culture works more like a "filter" — given the same economic pressures, the calling made people save and reinvest rather than consume, but it didnt generate the economic pressure itself.

2. Jon said thats the "filter" reading, but Weber needs "culture as engine." the ethic created brand new practices like rational double-entry accounting that wouldnt exist without the value orientation. otherwise its just capitalism with religious decoration on top.

3. Maya pushed back that "engine" treats values like physical forces, which Weber rejects. she mentioned Wahlverwandtschaft — elective affinity — where values and material conditions select each other. values dont push people around; they shape which economic possibilities actually become real.

4. Jon said elective affinity still needs values to have independent causal weight. if you can fully explain saving behavior with market incentives and fear of poverty, then culture is redundant. he called this the "downward exclusion" problem — if lower-level explanations are complete, culture gets causally excluded.

5. Maya said you're mixing causal completeness with explanatory adequacy. Weber's idea of adequate causation (adäquate Verursachung) isnt about physical forces; its about tracing a chain that makes the outcome intelligible. values do real work there by constituting the reason someone acted, not by pushing them like billiard balls.

6. Jon replied that if values only make things "intelligible," then Weber hasnt explained capitalism's rise, hes just interpreted it. at that point theres no difference between Weber and a novelist writing a convincing story about why capitalists saved money. without causal bite, the thesis is empty.
