A friend mentioned the importance-tractability-neglectedness framework from cause prioritization, which sounds smart, but when we try to apply it to our actual pile it just collapses. We get direct stuff like food-bank equipment and weird indirect stuff like a journalist fellowship on water rights, and nobody knows how to compare them. Can you turn ITN into a real decision procedure I could actually run in a meeting? I need the questions in strict order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure gives a hard no, and where it stops being facts and becomes values so we just vote. Then apply it to two proposals: upgrading refrigeration at a food bank (direct, easy to understand) and a three-year fellowship for a journalist covering state water policy (indirect, high stakes, unclear tractability). Please include at least one case where the procedure spits out an answer my committee will hate, so I can see where it breaks. Also, seperate what's actually settled in the literature on this framework from what's genuinely contested—don't present one view on weighting importance vs tractability as if it's the consensus—because I need my board to know exactly where they're faking precision.
