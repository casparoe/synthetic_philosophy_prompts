I've been leading a philosophy-of-mind reading group through Jackson's knowledge argument, and we've spent the last two sessions on a detail that introductory summaries usually flatten: not whether Mary learns something new upon leaving her monochrome room, but whether that "something" is a new fact about the world or merely a new phenomenal concept—a different mode of representing the very same physical state she already knew completely. The phenomenal-concept strategist insists that Mary acquires only an epistemic ability, not ontological knowledge, so the apparent gap is in our cognition, not in physicalism itself. Our discussion ended without resolution, suspended between the claim that every wavelength and neural pathway was already in her grasp and the insistence that seeing red reveals something those descriptions necessarily leave out.

For our closing session I want a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains marked A1 and A2—to stand in for that suspension rather than resolve it. One refrain should voice the completeness claim from inside the room (something like "Every physical fact is already in my grasp"), the other should voice the experiential seeming after she sees color (something like "This is what the wavelength was hiding"). The form should do the philosophical work: the refrains must take turns through the tercets, passing each other without reconciliation, then finally appear together in the last two lines—side by side, not synthesized. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with the phenomenal-concept literature can tell which refrain belongs to which side of the debate, and the structural meeting in the quatrain should mirror exactly where we landed: the tension intact, neither position absorbed into the other. No prose explanation, no footnotes, no citations—just the poem, with the architecture of the argument carried by rhyme and repetition. Write it so that a reader who doesn't know the debate can still feel the two voices circling and then locking into place at the end.
