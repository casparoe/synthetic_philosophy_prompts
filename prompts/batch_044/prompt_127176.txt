I'm preparing a short methodological position paper for a seminar on archives and historical epistemology, and I want a consistency check before I present. Here is where I have landed: (1) archives are not neutral repositories but active instruments of governance—preservation, cataloging, and destruction are exercises of power, so the record is systematically distorted in favor of dominant groups; (2) from this I draw an ethical commitment that the historian's primary obligation is to the silences, to those never documented, rather than to the archive's explicit contents; (3) I therefore believe historians should attempt to reconstruct subaltern agency from negative spaces in the record, using speculative and counterfactual methods when direct testimony is absent; (4) at the same time, I think any reconstruction of subaltern voice from silence is necessarily anachronistic, imposing present categories onto subjects who did not inhabit them, which repeats a form of conceptual violence; (5) consequently, I also maintain that the correct stance is not to fill the silence but to map its architecture—to describe precisely how the absence was produced, by which mechanisms, and with what effects—without inventing voices; (6) yet I think that restricting ourselves to mapping absence without any attempt at recovery reproduces the original erasure and fails the ethical obligation in (2); (7) I reject the idea of a "view from nowhere" in archival work: all interpretation is situated, and the researcher's position shapes what counts as a meaningful silence; (8) despite (7), I hold that specific claims about what a given archive silences—for instance, that missing labor contracts reflect bureaucratic exclusion rather than demographic reality—can be evaluated as objectively correct or incorrect, independent of the interpreter's standpoint; and (9) I think finding aids, accession logs, and catalog metadata should be treated as primary sources in their own right, not as transparent administrative frames, because they actively structure visibility and invisibility.

My question is whether this package hangs together. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which are only apparently in conflict, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Please order the tensions by severity, specifying for each what commitment must give and what that costs. I am especially unsure whether (3) and (5) can coexist, and whether (7) undermines (8) in a way I have missed; but I do not want to be told that everything conflicts—some of these may dissolve on inspection. Be precise about which single revision, if forced, would clean up the most damage, and avoid treating all tensions as equally severe.
