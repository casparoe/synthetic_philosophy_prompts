Me and a couple friends have been stuck in this loop for like three months now, talking about Molinism and middle knowledge in a philosophy discord I'm in. I only have like a philosophy 101 background so maybe I'm mangling the terms, but here's where we're at: one friend is convinced that God's middle knowledge — knowing what every free creature would do in every possible situation — actually solves the problem of divine control versus human freedom. His argument is basically that God doesn't cause anyone's choices, he just surveys the counterfactual landscape and picks which world to actualize. The other friend, who leans open-theist, keeps saying that just pushes the problem back one step: if the counterfactuals about what I'd do in situation X are already true and God can't change them, then God's creative decision is constrained by something outside himself.

We keep going in circles. The Molinist friend brings up Plantinga's free will defense, the other friend brings up the grounding objection, and then we just stare at each other. I started thinking maybe we're not even arguing about the same thing. Like, is the real disagreement hidden under the word sovereignty? The Molinist seems to think sovereignty just means God decides which world exists, while the critic seems to think sovereignty requires that God also determines which counterfactuals are true in the first place. Or maybe it's about whether those counterfactuals have to be grounded in actual properties of people, or whether they can just be brute modal facts? There's also this thing I can't quite articulate — I keep wondering if both sides are using free will with different scopes. One sounds like in this exact world I could have done otherwise, and the other sounds like across possible worlds there exists a version of me who does otherwise. If that's the mix-up, then the whole debate about whether middle knowledge is compatible with freedom might be answering different questions. So what kind of disagreement is actually stalling us? Is it verbal, about the meaning of control and freedom? Is it a metaphysical fight over whether modal truths need explanation? Or is it just that we all have different gut feelings about divine power that can't be argued for? And if you think it's one of those, what would actually move it — a distinction we haven't made, some formal result about counterfactual logic, or is it just dead? I'm especially curious if there's a hidden formal or conceptual question here that neither side realizes they're banking on.
