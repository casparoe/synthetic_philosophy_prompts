Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps that have argued past each other on the common prior assumption. The first camp—Common-Prior Rationalists—holds that rational agents with identical information and a shared prior must converge in belief, that Aumann-style agreement theorems are normative constraints rather than mere formalities, and that persistent disagreement about fundamental matters therefore traces to hidden information asymmetry or to a failure of rational updating. The second camp—Prior-Pluralist Epistemologists—holds that rational agents can maintain genuinely different prior probabilities that are not reducible to private information, especially in normative, metaphysical, and ontologically underdetermined domains, and that requiring a common prior risks collapsing substantive disagreement into a diagnosis of cognitive error.

Produce the document they should have signed together. Include, first, a numbered section of agreed claims about belief updating, the formal conditions under which agreement theorems operate, and the acknowledged limits of those theorems—claims precise enough to be useful but not so anodyne that either side could not endorse them. Second, present the residual disagreements in neutral wording: a table or separated list in which each side’s view is stated so that its proponents would accept the formulation of their own position, distinguishing genuine conflicts from verbal ones. Third, identify the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp—including specifically whether normative and interpretive priors fall within the scope of agreement results, and whether agents who share all empirical data but assign different prior weights to theoretical virtues (explanatory depth, ontological parsimony) are in rational divergence or rational error. For each crux, supply one concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it; include, as one such case, two cosmologists interpreting identical cosmic microwave background measurements but assigning different priors to multiverse-generating mechanisms versus single-universe explanations in a regime where neither ontology is empirically accessible, and record each side’s explicit prediction or interpretation of what that case shows. Separate what is settled in the formal literature from what remains genuinely contested among people who understand both the mathematics and the philosophy, and do not present any contested interpretation of Bayesian rationality or of agreement theorems as the consensus view. Write the response in American English.
