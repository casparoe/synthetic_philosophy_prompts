I'm on my university's sustainability procurement board—yeah, we exist, we review contracts over like $50k. There's a proposal to renew our campus dining contract with this vendor that sources mostly beef for its meal-prep lines, and the vendor claims they can't switch proteins without breaking economies of scale.

The board's split: some say our contract is way too small to shift global meat demand, so dropping it is just symbolic, basically the same inefficacy objection people raise about climate or voting. Others say institutions have a kind of "expressive complicity" that doesn't depend on actually causing harm, but they're vauge about what that means for a procurement decision specifically. I need a memo for thursday's meeting recommending approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, with a concrete timeline—like should we renegotiate by fall or let the contract exipre?

The memo should seriously engage the "symbolic but causally irrelevant" worry rather than just dismissing it, and maybe tackle whether the inefficacy objection applies differently when the agent is a collective body with explicit rules rather than an individual buyer. I was chatting with a friend about whether there's an overdetermination angle here too—like if every institution makes this call independently, the harm is overdetermined but each choice still forms part of a pattern... anyway, draft something formal but not lawyerly, with a clear bottom line the committee can actually vote on.
