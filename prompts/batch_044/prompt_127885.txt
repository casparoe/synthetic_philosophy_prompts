My intuitive judgments about whether immoral origins or content undermine artistic value seem to form a pattern, and I want the principle behind them, if any. A novelist who systematically plagiarized others produces a novel that is stylistically original and morally insightful in its themes; I think the work remains good art, though diminished as an achievement. A filmmaker with documented abusive behavior directs a film with no trace of that behavior and exceptional formal beauty; I think the film should be judged independently and is fully good. A painting depicts cruelty with relish and technical brilliance; I think it is aesthetically powerful but artistically flawed, since its form serves an immoral vision. A symphony composed by someone cruel to colleagues expresses profound compassion; the music's value feels intact, yet praising the composer personally feels wrong. A propaganda film with stunning cinematography designed to dehumanize a group is technically accomplished, but I deny it is good art, since its form and immoral purpose are inseparable. Finally, a masterpiece is revealed to have been created by an artist whose private beliefs were vile but never expressed in the work; the revelation does not diminish the work, though it changes how we honor the creator.

I keep returning to a tension between cases where the immorality lies in biographical origin, in representational content, or in production purpose, and I am unsure whether these should be treated as distinct contamination vectors or reduced to one deeper principle. In particular, I go back and forth on whether the plagiarism case differs from the propaganda case only in degree, or whether origin-based flaws never penetrate aesthetic value while purpose-based ones always do. Please take a committed position: state the precise principle you think best captures the pattern, say explicitly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate and why, and apply it to a further case—a documentary made by exploiting suffering subjects, where the final film critically exposes that very exploitation. Do not lay out separatist and moralist options neutrally; defend a definite account, and say what it predicts for the documentary case.
