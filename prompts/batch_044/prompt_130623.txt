I run a grad philosophy workshop, about 12 people, 90 minutes. Design a runnable in-class deliberation about state apologies for historical wrongs built around what I'll call the "successor liability loop" -- once a state formally apologizes for wrong W, it implicitly accepts a framework that also obligates apologies for W' and W'', which share the same structural root but were committed by different actors entirely. Give roles (govt spokesperson, victim-group delegate, taxpayer advocate, historian-adivsor), decision rules, and small non-monetary stakes so participants discover the loop themselves: they should hit the choice between blocking extension (and seeming inconsistent) or extending it (and overcommitting the state). Include a timeline, a debrief that maps their moves onto the concept, an honest note on which feature of the loop the game can't reproduce, and a heads-up on what'll go wrong the first time. No citation requests or reading lists -- they argue purely from role.
