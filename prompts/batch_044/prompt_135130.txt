In a seminar comparing Western liberal and East Asian Confucian accounts of the person, a colleague kept invoking the saying that the nail which stands up gets hammered down to dismiss any claim that Confucian traditions accommodate individual autonomy. Could you disentangle the precise theses that proverb might express—whether descriptive of social practice, normatively prescriptive, metaphysical about the constitution of the self, or sociological about group dynamics—and for each indicate whether it is trivial, defensible, or plainly false? Please lay them out as a menu rather than selecting one, and say which readings actually support the conclusion that East Asian thought lacks a concept of the individual altogether, and which are being illegitimately conflated when the saying is treated as a decisive objection.
