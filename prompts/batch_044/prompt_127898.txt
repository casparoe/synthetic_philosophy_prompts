I run an epistemology seminar with ~12 students, 75 min, just index cards and point tokens. Design a game that makes them feel the dogmatism paradox — the loop where lowering confidence in P because you're unreliable about P also undermines your belief that you lowered it correctly, leaving you either paralyzed or dogmatic.

Use hidden "credence contracts" they sign at the start; then they recieve "calibration reports" using a niche metric — "regret-weighted calibration" from a fictional second-order audit — saying they're unreliable in that domain. They must apply a discount factor to thier bets, but the contract contains a hidden recursive clause: whatever factor they apply first-order, they must also apply to thier confidence in having applied it correctly. Include a hidden "oracle" deck that actually shows they're well-calibrated at the meta-level, revealed only after discounts are locked in. Give roles — maybe two "auditors" with veto power — and a payoff in candy or extra-credit points, not cash. Timeline should fit 75 min with no lecture up front; they should hit the loop mechanically.

Then a debrief that maps the recursive clause onto the formal paradox, predicts what will go wrong (they'll likely ignore the clause to avoid paralysis — that's part of the point), and is frank about what the game can't reproduce (probably the exact Bayesian conditionalization formula).

Start with the activity design itself — no intro or "here's what I made" — just jump into rules, materials, roles, timeline, then the debrief.
