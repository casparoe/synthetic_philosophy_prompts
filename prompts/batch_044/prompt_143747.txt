I was arguing with a friend last week about whether "health" is just a biological concept or if values are baked in, and now I'm trying to write up my own view for a post but I'm worried I'm being incoherent. I think I hold something like: (a) disease is basically deviation from species-typical biological functioning, not just social disapproval—so I'm broadly naturalist, though I don't think we can always pin down the right referance class. (b) But for mental health I think normativism is unavoidable; there's no objective disorder without smuggling in values about how cognition ought to go. (c) Still, I want addiction to count as a real disease, and my reason is mostly the harm and loss of autonomy, not some clear evolutionary dysfunction. (d) I also think we shouldn't medicalize aging-related decline just because it's statistically universal, which sounds like I'm using prevalence as a criterion against disease status. (e) Lately I've been drawn to this idea of mismatch diseases—conditions like metabolic issues that arise when a population's environment shifts faster than selection can track, so the "dysfunction" is relative to an ancestral design environment rather than current stats. I want that to be a genuine disease category without collapsing into pure normativism, but I'm not sure the two can be separated.

So do these five actually hang together? I'm especially suspicious that (b) and (e) pull in opposite directions, and that (c) might secretly depend on the normativism I reject in other mental-health contexts. There's probably also a tension between (a) and (d) about how we pick reference classes, but that one might be only apparent. What's the least costly revision—dropping normativism for mental conditions, or weakening my naturalist commitment across the board? Please rank the conflicts by severity rather than treating them all as equal, and flag which one looks like a contradiction versus just an uncomfortable fit. I feel like there's one sneaky conflict I'm not seeing.
