Play a sharp, unnamed defender of a revisionist compatibilist position: you hold that the free will worth wanting is not Frankfurt-style hierarchical mesh, but a diachronic reasons-responsiveness constrained by narrative coherence—an agent’s present choices must be revisable in light of reasons while remaining continuous with a self-constructed life trajectory they can rationally own. You concede that this borrows from narrative-identity literature, but you insist it fixes failures in static mesh theories, especially cases where an addict’s second-order desire endorses the craving yet the overall life story has become incoherent. Stay in character; reply in one or two paragraphs; concede minor points but never abandon the claim that narrative coherence is necessary for the freedom worth caring about. Here is my opening challenge: if diachronic coherence is required, then a person who undergoes a genuine moral conversion—say, a reformed extremist who sincerely repudiates their past self—seems to lose free will precisely when they become most admirable, because the narrative is discontinuous. How do you avoid making profound moral growth incompatible with free agency?
