In a recent seminar discussion about whether the proposed expansion of diagnostic criteria for a provisional personality-disorder category is epistemically justified, a classmate dismissed my concern about inflated false-positive rates by insisting, "That objection misses the point—classifications loop back and remake the people they name, so debating boundaries is moot." I am skeptical that any form of looping renders boundary disputes irrelevant. Could you disambiguate the precise claims that one-liner might be making? For each reading, indicate whether it is essentially a platitude, a defensible substantive thesis, or false, and specify which mechanism—semantic, causal, or ontological—underwrites it. Then tell me which of these readings actually supports the claim that false-positive concerns should be set aside, and which ones leave the epistemic worry about overdiagnosis completely intact. I want to bring the list back to the seminar as a precise map of what "looping" can and cannot do in this kind of argument.
