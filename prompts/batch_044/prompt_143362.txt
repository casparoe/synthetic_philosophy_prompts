In corporate and educational institutions worldwide, psychometric inventories that classify people into fixed personality types are used not only descriptively but also as sources of explanation and excuse: a person might say, "I cannot take on leadership because my profile identifies me as an introvert," and this is often accepted as a legitimate limitation rather than as an evasion of choice. Examine the philosophical commitments this practice presupposes about the self, agency, and moral responsibility. Reconstruct its implicit ontology: does it treat the individual as a being-in-itself whose properties determine its possibilities, thereby denying the Sartrean claim that existence precedes essence and that we are radically free? Distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely accompanies it, assess whether any recognition of facticity can be preserved without collapsing into bad faith, and determine whether this institutional use of typology necessarily denies transcendence. State how confident you are in your interpretation of Sartre and identify the specific point about which you are least certain.
