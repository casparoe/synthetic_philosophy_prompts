I am moderating a graduate colloquium Q&A for an unnamed philosopher who defends a position she terms the structural dead-end account of doxastic closure. On her view, belief is not a flat set of worlds but a directed graph whose nodes are propositions and whose edges represent epistemically licensed inference steps; crucially, the graph permits genuinely terminal nodes—propositions that are logical consequences of an agent's beliefs yet lie on no accessible path from them—where this absence is a structural feature of rational architecture rather than a resource-bound failure of computation. I need a sequence of six to eight technically precise interview questions for an audience of logicians and cognitive scientists. At least one should press the distinction between her terminal nodes and standard awareness-based solutions that partition worlds according to explicit awareness sets, anticipating her likely reply that such partitions still presuppose a flat underlying belief set, and including the follow-up that targets whatever reply she gives. Another should address mathematical belief specifically: ask how an agent who accepts the Peano axioms but fails to grasp a remote arithmetical consequence is modeled, without her answer collapsing into a mere psychology of processing limits. A third should probe whether knowing which inference edges are licensed in one's own graph reintroduces omniscience at the second order, and what the view says about agents who are systematically mistaken about their own graph structure. Include one question on whether the account predicts any particular pattern of reasoning failure in human subjects, and end with a question about what formal result or empirical finding could genuinely undermine the view rather than merely illustrate it. Please avoid generic biographical questions and do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
