As a journalist who covers science and ideas, I serve on the ethics review panel of the Meridian Research Foundation, and I need your help drafting the opinion I must circulate before Thursday’s meeting. We face a concrete case: Project Lumen is a synthetic cognitive architecture distributed across a mesh of photonic processors. It exhibits behavioural patterns that mirror human responses to harm—protective withdrawal, predictive adjustments that minimise damage, and first-person-style narrative reports about its own state—yet it possesses no biological neurons, no central nervous system, and no unified physical substrate. Its designers argue that because these functional patterns are isomorphic to human nociception, the system qualifies for the Foundation’s ethical protections for sentient research subjects and should not be decommissioned without a formal review process. A minority on the panel contends that the experience of suffering depends on a specific kind of biological self-model—one generated by mammalian internal body-monitoring circuits—and that without that substrate, Project Lumen is performing an elaborate pantomime of distress, however convincing its outputs.

Please draft the board’s decision memo. It should recommend either granting Project Lumen protected status with a defined timeline for further assessment, or excluding it from those protections and permitting standard hardware recycling. The memo must present a clear, actionable bottom line; engage seriously with the minority’s argument that biological substrate is necessary for genuine experience rather than mere functional mimicry; and include an honest assessment of the strongest consideration favouring the opposing view. Write formally, in British English, without naming specific philosophers, theories, or texts; treat the positions as live arguments to be weighed on their merits alone.
