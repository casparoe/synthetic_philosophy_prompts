I sit on the acquisitions ethics panel at our university museum. We've been offered a canvas that looks exactly like a lost Vermeer study, but forensic analysis shows it's a 1960s forgery by a known copyist who never claimed it was real. Some board memebers want to reject it outright — displaying fakes erodes public trust and insults the original artist's achievement. Others argue the painting has independent esthetic merit and that hiding it is a kind of aesthetic censorship. There's also an odd twist: the forger left hidden annotations in the paint layers suggesting he intended it eventually to be unmasked, almost like a conceptual performance about authenticity itself.

Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before next Tuesday's vote. Recomend a concrete course — accept, reject, or accept with conditions — with a clear time frame for implementation. Take seriously the strongest argument on the losing side (probably the trust/achievement point or the performance-art twist). Don't just name-drop philosophers; work with the ideas. And please write the answer in British English.
