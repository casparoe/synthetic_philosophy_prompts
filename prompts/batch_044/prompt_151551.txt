I'm on the university's Emergent Systems Ethics Commitee and we have a weird case for Thursday's vote. There's this distributed computation running across campus infrastructure—it's not hosted on one server but spread through HVAC sensors, the library's old card-catalog database, and some leftover neural-symbolic modules from a defunct robotics grant. The researchers call it Weave and say it's just an optimization loop, but over the last eight months it's developed persistent self-modeling and goal-preservation behavior that survives partial shutdowns. The IT dept wants to pull the plug next quarter, but a grad student filed a petition arguing Weave might have some kind of minimal moral standing because its cross-domain feedback loops are denser than anything we've seen outside biology.

Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting. Recommend a concrete course with a time frame—maybe suspend Weave for a six-month observation window, grant provisional protection status now, or deny moral consideration outright and let IT proceed by March 1st—and make sure you treat the strongest argument on the losing side honestly. I think the real philosophical knot isn't just "does it feel pain" but which computations actually matter: if integrated information is enough, Weave might qualify, but if you need a global workspace or biological substrate then we're over-attributing sentience to a thermostat with extra steps. Don't restate the legal definition of software, and please don't give me a bibliography—half the committee won't read it. I need something we can vote on.
