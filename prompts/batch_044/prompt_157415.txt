After a long argument with my friend about whether bounded rationality describes a real cognitive limit or merely an optimal response to information costs, I want us to stop talking past each other. Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a procedural-rationalist camp, in the Simon tradition—treating aspiration levels, sequential search, and organizational decision processes as genuinely distinct mechanisms—and a constrained-optimization camp, in the neoclassical search-theory tradition—treating satisficing as endogenous reservation-value optimization under computation or sampling costs. The statement should open with a numbered section of claims both sides can sign, covering: the formal structure of choice sets and stopping rules; the empirical reality of cognitive and organizational limits; what Simon’s administrative studies actually demonstrated about aspiration adjustment; and the conditions under which agents terminate search without global comparison. Next, present a disagreements table with two columns—Procedural Rationalist and Constrained Optimizer—each stating its own view in wording it fully accepts and describing the opposing view in neutral terms neither side rejects. The cruxes must include: (a) whether an aspiration level functions as an internal representational threshold or merely as an implicit price constraint; (b) whether sequential search with a stopping rule is formally reducible to single-period optimization with an endogenously chosen reservation value; (c) whether ecological rationality—environment-structure matching that makes simple heuristics accurate—is a separate causal mechanism or only a redescription of efficient search given environmental statistics; and (d) whether the normative justification for satisficing in human cognition, if any, carries over to artificial agents with explicit utility functions and programmable search budgets. For each crux, specify a concrete model, empirical pattern, or decision case that both sides agree bears on the issue, and record in advance what each side predicts about that case. If any portion of the dispute turns out to be verbal—for example, over whether “optimization” requires global comparison or only local improvement—identify precisely which portion and explain why the wording rather than the substance diverges. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the document should be writable without access to external sources. Keep the tone formal, the formulations exact, and the residual disagreements sharp enough that either party could sign its own column without feeling misrepresented.
