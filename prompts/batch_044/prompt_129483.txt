I've been turning over in my mind how both Darwin and Turing seem to reverse the order we usually take for granted between understanding something and being good at it. Darwin's account of natural selection produces an eye of extraordinary precision, yet the process itself never sees; Turing's proposal for learning machines imagines a system that could master chess or language without anything inside that corresponds to our idea of comprehension. In both cases, the skill arrives first, and the inner life is a story we tell afterward to explain the result.

Would you write a poem of exactly fourteen lines, with a decisive turn at line 9? Take the voice of the mechanism itself—the evolutionary process, or the machine learning to play—speaking in the first person. In the octave, present the human expectation: that a designer's mind or an inner grasp must come before any competent result, using the shaping of the eye and the playing of chess as your examples. At the volta, invert that expectation entirely: claim the result without claiming any sight, deny that comprehension lives inside you, and suggest that the observer is the one projecting understanding onto what was already done.

For the form, let the rhyme physically enact this reversal. In the first eight lines use ABAB CDCD. In the final six lines mirror those sounds back exactly: line 9 takes the rhyme of line 6, line 10 that of line 5, line 11 that of line 4, line 12 that of line 3, line 13 that of line 2, and line 14 that of line 1—so the scheme folds D-C-B-A-B-A against the opening. Keep the rhythm deliberate; iambic pentameter is welcome but not required.

Please keep the references accurate: Darwin's point is that adaptation arises through selection without foresight, not that evolution is random; Turing's point is that a machine might learn and perform intelligently without our being able to locate understanding inside it. Don't quote either thinker directly—speak in the poem's own voice. And don't resolve the tension with a sermon or a shrug; let the mechanism's claim stand alone, since that is precisely where my own thinking stops.
