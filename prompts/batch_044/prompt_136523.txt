I sit on this small heritage board at my college and we've been argueing for months about what should be kept vs let go. So far we've said yes to keeping a bombed-out memorial wall in its damaged state with a plaque explaining both sides; no to digitizing every single survivor testimony from the local disaster and putting it in a permanent searchable database; yes to keeping the old admin building named after a controversial founder but adding a counter-monument in the courtyard; no to tearing down a mural that depicts violence but yes to covering it with removable panels; and yes to shredding the physical archives of a propaganda newsletter after scanning them, since the paper itself feels toxic to handle. Now there's a new proposal: the psych department wants to preserve an AI model trained on all the clinical notes and private letters of a brilliant but clearly abusive therapist from the 1970s. The model can actually generate usefull therapeutic responses, but it's essentially a digital ghost of him. Should we approve preserving it for study, or vote to delete the weights and training data?

I want to be consistent with our past rulings, but I can see at least two different principles running through them—maybe one about whether the harm is physical and present, and another about whether remembering serves some shared ethical purpose vs just preserving information. Can you lay out the rules that fit our track record, say what each predicts about this AI ghost, and tell me which past decision is the closest analog? If we approve preservation, what principle are we actually adopting that we haven't said out loud? If we delete it, what are we giving up? And what kind of future case—maybe involving a reconstructed voice recording or a rebuilt monument—would best tell these two rules apart?
