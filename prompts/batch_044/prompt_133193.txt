I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I have agreed to lead a seventy-five-minute internal workshop for about fifteen colleagues—mostly machine-learning engineers and systems architects—on heterophenomenology as a methodological stance in consciousness science. Rather than treating first-person reports as windows onto an inner theater or dismissing them as unreliable noise, I want to present Dennett’s approach as a disciplined modeling strategy: we catalog the heterophenomenological world—the world exactly as it is reported—without resolving whether there is “something it is like” behind the reports. To make this concrete for this audience, I want to build the session around an analogy drawn from our own practice: treating an LLM’s token-level self-descriptions (for example, outputs that read “I feel uncertain about this answer”) as structured telemetry logs. The task is not to determine whether the model has phenomenology, but to construct a predictive model of those logs that is empirically adequate, leaving the ontology of consciousness untouched. I need a teaching plan, not the lecture itself. Please respond in the following sections, with approximate lengths:

The carrying example (about 200 words): Specify the single scenario that should carry the session—ideally the LLM-telemetry analogy, though an alternative is acceptable—stating precisely what it must contain (structured first-person reports, absence of direct access to internal states, predictive modeling of report patterns) and what it must lack (any appeal to neural correlates, any framing that requires solving the hard problem).
