I'm serving on my city's Public Memory and Naming Commission, and I honestly never thought I'd care about this stuff until it got personal. My grandpa was a pipe-fitter who worked on the reservoir system designed by Elias Vance in the 1920s, and I grew up thinking Vance was basically a local saint—there's even Vance Plaza downtown with a bronze plaque calling him a "visionary benefactor." But last month a grad student pulled letters from the university archives showing Vance wasn't just quietly in a eugenics society; he actively wrote state legislators in the 1930s pushing forced sterilization bills. Now there's a petition to rename the plaza, and the family is saying we can't erase a man who saved thousands from cholera, and that judging him only by those letters is presentist and unfair. The commission votes next Friday, and I need a memo I can circulate before the meeting recommending a concrete course of action with an actual timeline—whether that's full renaming, keeping it with a counter-plaque, or this hybrid idea I came across where you keep the geographic name but strip the honorific language and reclassify the signage as purely historical indexing rather than commemoration. I want the memo to take the family's arguement seriously, not just dismiss it with legal definitions, and also be honest about the strongest reason on the other side. Could you draft that? I need something the board can actually act on, not just philosophy for its own sake. Definately don't need a bibliography or reading list—just the reasoning and a bottom line.
