Specifically, I want to press the analogy further: if we treat a biological sensor or a neuronal circuit as performing a demonic function—selectively allowing information to flow in one direction and thereby reducing uncertainty—what are the most important philosophical and theoretical implications of applying Landauer's principle, which ties information erasure to heat dissipation, to these living processes? Rather than asking for engineering applications, I want you to identify the three or four conceptual consequences that should genuinely reshape how philosophers of science and theoretical biologists understand the boundary between measurement and computation in cognition. Please rank these implications by how upstream they are: which ones, if accepted, would force revisions in the others, and which might turn out to be conceptual dead ends once the physics is stated with sufficient precision? In addition, indicate whether any of the standard distinctions—such as that between passive observation and active intervention—collapse under this framing or remain intact. Please write in British English, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or lists of suggested reading.
