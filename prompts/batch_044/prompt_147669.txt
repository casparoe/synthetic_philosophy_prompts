I have been offered enrollment in a clinical trial for a neural implant that permanently eliminates psychological and physical suffering—grief, anxiety, the unpleasantness of exertion—while leaving pleasure and reasoning intact. The device includes a manual "drive switch" that I can activate to generate urgency for work, exercise, or maintaining relationships; when it is off, I feel complete contentment and no discomfort whatsoever about neglecting those obligations. My partner argues that this design undermines authentic commitment: without the involuntary, continuous pressure of suffering, she claims, I will not genuinely value my career or our relationship, but merely perform caring behaviors whenever I happen to flip the switch, and over time my real attachments will dissolve because nothing actually costs me anything. I think she confuses pain with care. I believe my values remain stable preferences, and the switch merely replaces destructive suffering with clean, voluntary motivation—realizing the core promise of using biotechnology to eradicate suffering altogether. Should I go ahead and receive the implant? Give me a definite yes or no, with explicit reasoning, and address whether my partner's fear reveals a genuine philosophical problem in separating suffering from commitment, or whether it rests on a mistaken view of what makes motivation and love authentic.
