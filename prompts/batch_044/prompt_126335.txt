people keep using the "inherited house with hidden defects" analogy for whether a nation can be guilty for stuff its grandparents did -- you buy the place, you didn't crack the foundation, but now you're liable for repairs and anyone hurt inside. it's used to say citizens today aren't criminally guilty but still have to pay up or fix the mess. Jaspers had that four-part split -- criminal, political, moral, metaphyscial -- and people map the analogy straight onto it. can you audit the correspondance? table first: what's the house, the previous owner, the hidden defect, the buyer, the repair bill, and which kind of guilt maps to which part. then the breaks: is citizenship voluntary like a purchase? can guilt transfer like property title? does it smuggle in that only current owners are responsible? and tell me whether Jaspers's distinction actually survives once the analogy is pulled apart, or if the house picture was doing hidden work to separate criminal guilt from the rest.
