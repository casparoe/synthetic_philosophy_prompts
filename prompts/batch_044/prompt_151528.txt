I'm preparing notes for a philosophy of action seminar focused on impossibility arguments for moral responsibility, and I have become stuck evaluating a particular defensive analogy that was floated in discussion. The context is Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: true moral responsibility is impossible because nothing can be causa sui, since being genuinely responsible for an action requires being genuinely responsible for the mental nature that produces it, which in turn requires being responsible for the causes of that nature, generating either an infinite regress or an impossible act of self-creation. The point is that responsibility attaches to the control of outputs rather than to authorship of the mechanism, so Strawson's demand that an agent be the ultimate cause of her own nature is as misplaced as demanding that the keeper be the metallurgist. If the analogy collapses, suggest a better one that captures the same compatibilist intuition without the disanalogy.
