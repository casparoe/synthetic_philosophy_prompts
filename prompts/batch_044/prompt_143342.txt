I am working with a reconstructed Thomistic position on tyrannicide that I think improves on the standard binary reading. The standard view holds that Aquinas permits killing only a usurper who lacks legitimate authority ab initio, while a ruler who was legitimately instituted but becomes corrupt must be resisted only through public authority, not private action. I propose instead a graded framework: authority is not merely present or absent but can exist in a state of partial suspension when a legitimate ruler systematically violates the common good in a sustained and structural manner. In such cases, the ruler retains formal title but forfeits the protective conditions that normally prohibit private resistance; the result is not a blanket license to kill, but a calibrated permission whose scope depends on whether the corruption is remediable through institutional channels and whether private action would serve the common good rather than private vengeance. This reading, I claim, makes sense of Aquinas's insistence that resistance must be proportionate and ordered to the common good, without forcing him into an implausible sharp line between usurpation and corruption.

What would genuinely count against this position? I want more than the usual objections that it is too permissive or that it violates modern liberal norms. Specifically: which features of Aquinas's broader account of authority, law, and the common good would have to be read differently for this graded framework to collapse? Are there conceptual commitments in his metaphysics of authority—say, regarding the conditions under which a mandate is void versus merely abused—that this reading mischaracterizes? What evidence or argument should I take as decisive rather than merely awkward, and which of these would defenders of the graded view themselves concede as damaging? Finally, does this framework risk being qualified into unfalsifiability—if any case of resistance can be explained by adjusting the degree of suspension, has the view lost predictive content, and is that a special problem for a normative theory of this kind?
