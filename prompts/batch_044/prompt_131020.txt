After three decades in experimental physics I have recently begun reading philosophy of religion, and I find that Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism is almost always introduced through a particular analogy: the world operates like a motion-picture projector, with God as the projector, each frame of existence created independently ex nihilo, and the appearance of causal connection — fire touching cotton and then cotton burning — being no more a real interaction than the appearance of continuous motion between discrete frames. The analogy is used to make occasionalism intuitive and to license the further claim that denying natural causation need not undermine empirical science, since God preserves a regular customary sequence that scientists can still describe. Yet I have also gathered that Al-Ghazālī does not deny logical or mathematical necessity, and that his argument specifically targets the philosophers' claim that nature contains inherent necessary causal powers, not the mere observation of regular succession. Could you provide the explicit correspondence table — projector, frame, screen, audience, the script or regular sequence — and then identify where the mapping breaks, particularly with respect to whether the analogy silently assumes a temporal container (the projection booth, the reel's ordered sequence) that occasionalism might treat differently? Separately, assess whether the analogy's intended conclusion — that occasionalism is compatible with science and is conceptually no stranger than digital display — survives once we account for the disanalogy between logical and natural necessity and for the fact that in a film there is genuinely no interaction between frames, whereas Al-Ghazālī might allow for secondary regularities that are not causal in the philosophical sense but are more robust than coincidence. If the analogy collapses under this audit, what better analogy, if any, captures his position without importing these misleading features?
