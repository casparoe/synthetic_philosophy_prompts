I'm giving the toast at my mentor's retirement do next Saturday. She's Swedish-American, built a huge career in Silicon Valley, and the room is split: her American collegues who live by the American Dream, and relatives from Stockholm for whom Jantelagen is still a living thing. There's also the tall poppy syndrome running underneath — Australians and Kiwis know it, but here it's flipped depending on which passport you're holding. I want about four minutes, in British English, and I need the contradiction between celebrating the successful and cutting them down to actually structure what I can say: determine which compliments are permited and which are forbidden, shape the anecdotes that land safely, and don't resolve it with some cheap best-of-both-worlds ending. Let the tension sit there, awkward and unresolved. The idea shouldn't be decoration — it should decide what the speech is allowed to praise and what it has to stay quiet about.
