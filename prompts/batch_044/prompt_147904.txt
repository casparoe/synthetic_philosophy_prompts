As a retired physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of mind, I have been trying to formalize my own position on personal identity and synthetic mind-uploading, but I worry that I am helping myself to incompatible premises. I would like a direct consistency audit of the following commitments, without any bibliography or reading list.

I hold that:
(a) mental properties supervene on the organizational and causal structure of the brain, not on its specific biochemical substrate, so a functionally equivalent silicon implementation could in principle realize the same mental states;
(b) personal identity over time is not an all-or-nothing metaphysical fact but survives in degrees, sustained by psychological continuity and appropriate causal connection rather than by bodily continuity;
(c) gradual replacement of biological neurons with synthetic equivalents—preserving synaptic weights, connectivity topology, and real-time signal propagation—would preserve me, even if eventually every original neuron is gone;
(d) an instantaneous scan-and-upload procedure that destroys the original brain and instantiates the same psychological profile in a computer does not preserve me; it produces a psychological duplicate, and the original subject has ceased;
(e) there is no deep metaphysical distinction between such gradual synthetic replacement and the natural metabolic turnover of cells that already replaces most bodily constituents over a span of years;
(f) however, if the brain is completely deactivated for any interval—however brief, even a single second—during the replacement process, and then restarted with synthetic parts, the result is not me but a successor entity, because unbroken causal continuity is essential;
(g) functional equivalence is genuinely substrate-independent: if a synthetic neuron receives the same inputs, applies the same transformation, and emits the same outputs with the same temporal dynamics, it plays the identical mental role regardless of material composition.

Please audit these commitments. For each pair, say whether they are in genuine tension, whether they only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and what the least costly revision would be and what it costs. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious. In particular, I want to know whether my functionalist premises in (a) and (g) can be reconciled with the sharp causal-continuity conditions in (c), (d), and (f); whether my analogy in (e) to metabolic turnover actually supports the survival verdict in (c) once we distinguish distributed cellular replacement from centralized neural-architecture replacement; and whether the gradualism implied by (b) can accommodate the hard cutoff I have imposed in (f). If a single revision would clean up the most inconsistencies without abandoning the core physicalist framework, identify it and explain the damage it does to the rest of the package.
