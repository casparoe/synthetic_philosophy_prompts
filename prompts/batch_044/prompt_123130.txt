I have noticed a recurring argumentative manoeuvre for which I lack a precise term, and I wish to know whether philosophy recognises it as a distinct phenomenon rather than a compound of familiar ones. The pattern works as follows. A speaker traces an opponent's moral commitment — for instance, to egalitarian distribution — back to a contingent psychological or social origin, such as resentment of the powerful or a reactive formation against aristocratic values, and treats that origin as discrediting the commitment's normative authority. When, however, the same genealogical treatment is turned upon the speaker's own core value — say, a commitment to autonomy or to a particular conception of justice — the speaker does not concede the discredit. Instead, they invoke a principled separation between the genesis of a belief and its justification, or they claim that their value functions as a transcendental precondition of rational agency and is therefore structurally immune to historical debunking. The manoeuvre is not mere hypocrisy, since each defensive move is locally defensible: the genetic fallacy genuinely is fallacious in the second case, and genealogical suspicion genuinely illuminates the first. What distinguishes the pattern is its structural asymmetry: genealogical transparency is demanded of the opponent's values but systematically blocked for one's own by a boundary that applies selectively rather than universally. Is there an established concept for this one-way deployment of genealogical critique — one that distinguishes it from the motte-and-bailey, from plain equivocation on the genetic fallacy, and from ordinary ideological bias? If a term exists, I want to understand its exact delimitation: which configurations belong inside it, which are merely adjacent cases of hypocrisy, and where the boundaries lie. If no recognised term exists, I would like one coined together with a precise definition, necessary conditions, and boundary cases that show what it is not. I am not requesting a bibliography, a reading list, or specific citations; I want the conceptual carving itself.
