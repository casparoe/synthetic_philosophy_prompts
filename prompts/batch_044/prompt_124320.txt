You are delivering the opening remarks at a workshop on the adaptive reuse of post-industrial infrastructure, attended by structural engineers, preservation architects, and municipal policymakers. The central tension in the room is between those who see abandoned factories and rail yards as obstacles to climate-efficient development and those who regard them as irreplaceable repositories of cultural memory. Without citing specific buildings, statutes, or bibliographies, construct a five-minute address that makes Burke's idea of prescription and Chesterton's fence do genuine structural work rather than serve as decoration: argue that the burden of justification falls on demolition rather than preservation, but also concede that this burden can never be fully discharged because the original practical and social reasons for a structure's form are partly irrecoverable.
