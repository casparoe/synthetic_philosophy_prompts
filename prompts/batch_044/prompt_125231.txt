I'm preparing a presentation on first-person reference and have become stuck on a specific tension in Douglas Hofstadter's account of the self as a strange loop. He proposes that the pronoun "I" functions within a cognitive system as a symbol that ultimately points back to the system itself, producing a loop structurally analogous to Gödelian self-reference in formal logic. While I understand the parallel, I cannot see how this recursion acquires its subjective, qualitative dimension—why it feels like being a single center of experience rather than remaining an abstract syntactic pattern.

To examine this, I have been developing a thought experiment about a distributed cognitive architecture: imagine a network of independent language-processing modules, each able to generate first-person reports, but none individually containing the full representational structure, so that a unified "I" might emerge solely from interaction dynamics across discontinuous nodes. From Hofstadter's perspective, would such a scattered configuration count as a genuine strange loop capable of consciousness, or does the loop require a continuous, localized substrate of representation to achieve authentic self-referential closure? I am especially interested in whether the symbol must be instantiated in one place to generate the subjective quality he describes, or whether distribution across separate processes necessarily fractures the loop.
