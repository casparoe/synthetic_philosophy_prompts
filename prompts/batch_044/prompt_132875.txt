Read Jorge Luis Borges’s short story "The Library of Babel" through the philosophical problem of actual versus potential infinity, with specific attention to Cantor’s defense of completed infinite totalities, Hilbert’s hotel as a model of accommodating infinite guests without reaching a boundary, and finitist resistance—whether Aristotelian or modern intuitionist—to treating an infinite collection as a finished object. The library is said to contain every possible book formed from a fixed alphabet and fixed length; treat this not merely as a metaphor for information overload, but as a claim about a completed set with cardinal properties. Does the story present the library as an actual infinity that has always already been fully populated, like a Cantorian set that exists all at once, or as a potential infinity that grows without ever being complete, with the librarians searching for a catalog that can never finish?
