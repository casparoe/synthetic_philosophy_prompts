hey — i'm writing a column on whether we should take AI 'beliefs' seriously and i keep running into this thermostat comparison everyone loves: you say the thermostat 'wants' 72 degrees just to predict its behavior, not because there's actually a little mind in there, so when an LLM says 'I believe Paris is in France' we're doing the exact same thing, just with more syllables. people use it to shut down the whole conversation about mental state attribution.

but i'm not convinced the mapping is that clean. can you lay out what actually corresponds to what — the thermostat, the set-point, the feedback loop, the engineer who designed it, the room — and then where it falls apart? i mean, llms talk about their own mental states in first-person, they update responses based on convesational context about beliefs, and they don't have a physical environment they're regulating. does the analogy still justify the its-all-just-fiction conclusion, or was that conclusion doing all the work? also, my friend in the interperetability crowd mentioned something about internal representations acting like content — is there a better analogy, maybe comparing belief attribution in llms to reading a map that's constantly being redrawed, rather than a thermostat? i'd love the full audit plus whatever you think the right comparison actually is.
