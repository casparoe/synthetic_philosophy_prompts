Having retired from experimental physics and only recently begun studying philosophy of mind, I have been working through the conceivability argument for philosophical zombies: the claim that a being physically identical to a conscious person yet entirely lacking phenomenal experience is conceivable, and that this conceivability implies physical facts do not metaphysically entail mental facts. I would like you to compose a polished, somewhat formal joke that engages this argument at a substantive level—not a generic reference to brains or souls, but one whose punchline depends on the actual logical structure of the zombie scenario. To avoid well-trodden formulations, frame the joke around a hypothetical empirical research program that seeks a "phenomenal signature": a putative marker of conscious experience that is causally inert, leaving no behavioral or neural trace, and therefore detectable only by first-person access. The humor should turn on the paradox that the failure to discover such a signature is simultaneously exactly what the zombie hypothesis predicts—since zombies are physically identical—and exactly what a rigorous physicalist expects, leaving the retired scientist unable to determine whether his laboratory has confirmed physicalism or accidentally populated the world with undetectable zombies. The joke should be accessible to someone who understands the basic conceivability argument, but it should not include citations, reading lists, named authors, or references to specific texts. Write it as if for an audience of scientifically literate colleagues who take philosophical rigor seriously.
