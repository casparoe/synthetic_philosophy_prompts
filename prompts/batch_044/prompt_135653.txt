I just finished a long, somewhat frustrating argument with a friend about whether meritocracy is conceptually coherent, and we kept missing each other because we never mapped exactly where our commitments diverged. I want a taxonomy — specifically a branching decision tree — that sorts the logical space of positions on desert and institutional reward, constructed from concrete cases and claims rather than from familiar political labels. Start at the root: does desert apply at all to outcomes that depend on unchosen talents or social background? From there, split by whether effort can be conceptually isolated from talent; whether structural advantages generate what we might call negative desert (a duty to forfeit rather than a claim to receive); whether luck-egalitarian compensation should be applied before desert is calculated or only after; whether counterfactual effort — what a person would have exerted under genuinely fair background conditions — should count as desert-generating; and whether a just institution should aim at proportional reward, sufficient reward, or strict equality regardless of contribution. Each leaf should carry the name of an actual position (strict desertism, corrected meritocracy, sufficientarian institutional design, relational egalitarianism, etc.) or be marked unnamed if the combination is incoherent or unexplored, plus one sentence on its most awkward practical commitment. Every branch node should be phrased as a claim or case someone could answer without knowing the philosophical label. Where two named positions fall on the same leaf — for example, if they share all the practical answers but differ only on free-will metaphysics — flag them as inseparable by this tree rather than inventing a false distinction. Keep it formal and precise, but write for someone without specialist training who cares about the logic of the debate rather than about citation lists.
