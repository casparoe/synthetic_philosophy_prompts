I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have recently been reading von Neumann’s reflections on the brain—particularly his observation that neural tissue achieves remarkably reliable behaviour despite being built from unreliable individual components, and his puzzlement over whether brain processes are best understood as analogue, digital, or an irreducible hybrid of the two. Before I assess any particular scientific account, I want a conceptual specification of what a theory of neural computation actually has to deliver if it takes these concerns seriously.

Write the desiderata: the full list of requirements that an adequate theory of brain computation must satisfy, with the motivating case or phenomenon behind each—such as explaining high fault-tolerance without a central error-correcting protocol, accommodating both continuous signal modulation and discrete logical inference, or accounting for self-modification and learning in the absence of an external programmer. For each item, indicate whether you treat it as non-negotiable or as a camp-specific preference (for instance, the insistence on a symbol-level architecture versus a purely statistical learning framework), and flag any subset of requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable given known constraints from neurobiology or information theory, since that tension is precisely what interests me most. I also want one requirement imported from distributed systems design included explicitly: the theory must explain graceful degradation under partial node failure without invoking a global orchestrator or redundant backup scheme, because this strikes me as a constraint von Neumann anticipated yet contemporary models rarely address directly. Please keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating distinctions—such as the difference between hardware failure and representational error, or between statistical approximation and verifiable inference—rather than on scoring competitors, though a brief assessment of how mainstream connectionist models, symbolic cognitive architectures, and predictive-processing frameworks fare against the list would be welcome. I am not seeking bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want the conceptual spec itself, and I would particularly like you to identify which single requirement, in your view, excludes the greatest number of popular current theories.
