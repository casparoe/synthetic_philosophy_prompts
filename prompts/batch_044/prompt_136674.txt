A friend who read some secondary literature on Confucian ethics summarized the modern role-ethics interpretation—associated with figures like Roger Ames and Henry Rosemont—for me this way: Confucianism holds that there is no pre-social, autonomous self; personhood is constituted entirely by occupying specific relational roles within the five cardinal relationships (wulun), including the equal friendship bond that stands apart from the other four hierarchical pairs. Moral knowledge is not a matter of applying universal rules but of interpreting the ritual norms (li) through yi, or contextual rightness, which permits adapting or suspending particular rituals when they conflict with ren, the fundamental humanizing quality that gives the roles their moral point. Because moral reasoning is always indexed to one's position in a specific relationship, there is no internal Confucian standpoint from which to criticize an entire social structure in universal terms—remonstration is allowed upward within a hierarchy, but not horizontally across unequal roles or downward in a way that dissolves the hierarchy itself—so the view amounts to a form of moral particularism in which every ethical judgment is situation-specific and role-bound. From this, my friend concluded that Confucian role ethics is structurally incompatible with democratic citizenship, since democracy requires relating to others as bearers of equal, role-transcendent moral status, something the view explicitly denies. He also said that ren is not an independent virtue like courage or justice but merely the emotional coherence that holds a well-ordered network of roles together. Is this summary fair?
