but here's my package, and i don't know if it's one package or two: (1) moral revolutions are mostly driven by shifts in honor/shame dynamics, not by independent moral arguments or by economic interests; (2) dueling ended exactly this way — honor flipped, arguments followed; (3) slavery's decline, though, was mainly about interest convergence, labor mobility, and political coalition-building, with honor playing only a secondary, post-hoc rationalizing role; (4) we shouldn't invoke moral progress as a causal force — if norms shift, it's through social-psychological mechanisms we can actually describe; (5) but if honor is the real engine, then any moral revolution should leave clear traces in how social status gets assigned, which means slavery's collapse should prominently show those traces or my view is in trouble; and (6) moral arguments are mostly ineffective unless they're anchored in an honor structure that's already cracking — which is why abolitionist rhetoric worked in Britain partly because aristocratic practices like dueling were already being mocked by the rising bourgeoisie, so the arguments rode the wave.
