At a philosophy of mind reading group, two participants debated Dennett's critique of the Cartesian theater and his multiple-drafts alternative. (1) Marcus argued that removing the central screen is insufficient if a central editorial function remains, because Dennett's talk of drafts achieving fame in the brain and being revised into a coherent narrative sounds like a spotlight selecting content for an implicit audience, merely renamed rather than eliminated. (2) Elena responded that fame is purely causal—defined only by a draft's influence on other processes and verbal reports—so there is no observer, only widespread causal impact, and narrative coherence is an emergent result of competition rather than an editorial achievement. (3) Marcus then cited split-brain cases in which the left hemisphere confabulates explanations for actions initiated by the right, asking why one hemisphere should feel compelled to construct a unified story if there is genuinely no central audience demanding coherence. (4) Elena replied that this drive belongs not to a hidden spectator but to the language-production module, which evolved to generate single-threaded explanations for social coordination; the confabulation is merely one draft dominating the vocal apparatus, not evidence of a theater. (5) Marcus conceded the causal account but raised a further issue: if retrospective narrative binding can overwrite earlier drafts so completely that subjects report no awareness of the replaced content, then the dominant draft exercises an authority functionally identical to a Cartesian display—determining what seems to have occurred—without anyone having witnessed the earlier versions, which suggests that a minimal self-model must be doing the adjudicating rather than mere competitive dynamics.

Please adjudicate move by move: evaluate each of the five positions above, identify where each party is strongest and weakest, and say whether Marcus's final concern about retrospective binding genuinely revives the Cartesian theater or instead transforms the dispute into a different problem about minimal self-models. Structure your response as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
