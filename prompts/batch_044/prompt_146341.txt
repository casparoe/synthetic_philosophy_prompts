Construct a thought experiment that isolates whether joint commitment can survive an asymmetric withdrawal of belief in the shared goal, without collapsing either into individual intentionality or into a purely external coordination mechanism. The scenario must involve exactly three agents with no explicit linguistic agreement or hierarchical authority; the shared activity must be non-instrumental, pursued for its own sake rather than as a means to individual ends; one agent must privately come to believe the collective goal is impossible, while the other two remain fully committed and unaware of her doubt; and the case must allow us to test whether the doubting agent retains obligations to the group, and whether the remaining pair can continue to act jointly without her participation. After presenting the scenario, do the following: (a) state the verdict that Searle's account of we-intentions, Bratman's shared cooperative activity, and Gilbert's plural-subject theory each deliver regarding the status of the joint action after the withdrawal; (b) identify the structural feature of the case that separates these three accounts; (c) explain what happens to the separation if the non-instrumentality condition is removed; and (d) assess whether any of the three accounts can accommodate the claim that joint commitment persists even when one member treats the goal as impossible yet continues to conform her behavior. Keep the scenario under 150 words, avoid science-fictional technology, and do not invoke perfect information conditions or explicit contracts.
