Write a deadpan internal audit report, in the bureaucratic voice of a major art museum's Committee on Aesthetic Quarantine, assessing whether a recently acquired painting may be displayed given its creator's documented history of serious moral harm. Rather than debating the separation thesis in philosophical terms, treat it as an engineering challenge: invent and apply a pseudo-technical protocol involving concepts such as moral contamination half-life, biometric viewer-isolation chambers, and autonomous aesthetic response metrics. Through relentless formal detail — calibration procedures, risk matrices, visitor waiver language — the report should make visible, without ever stating them outright, the familiar objections to separating art from artist: that the calibration authority itself embeds moral judgment, that isolating biography requires making the offense structurally central, and that measuring pure aesthetic response presupposes exactly the separation it claims to prove. Maintain an entirely straight face; the satire must come from the content, not from winking. Open with a one-paragraph executive summary stating the committee's recommendation. Present the body in three labeled sections: Protocol Design, Field Testing, and Unresolved Variables. Do not exceed 600 words. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to external scholarship.
