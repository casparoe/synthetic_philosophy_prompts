You advise the maintenance team for an autonomous warehouse network that must revise its operational belief database whenever a hardware component or regulatory constraint changes. The frame problem—knowing precisely which inferences to drop and which to preserve—makes every update risk either leaving hidden errors intact or cascading into irrelevant domains. Design a reusable audit procedure that the team can complete in under thirty minutes per change event. The procedure must require the auditor to sort every affected belief into three bins: direct entailments of the change, shielded defaults that survive untouched, and “ghost frames” (inherited but now-untracked assumptions that have not been explicitly invalidated and must be manually exposed). It must specify a hard stopping rule that prevents revision from reaching beliefs about human intentions, market prices, or legal liability; it must flag every step that depends on a value judgment rather than a technical check; and it must include a documentation protocol for unresolved conflicts between ghost frames. Apply the procedure to three cases: lowering the weight limit on a loading dock, where energy-cost defaults should survive but queue-order assumptions must be flagged; installing a fire-suppression system that disables certain sensors, where inventory-tracking beliefs should be shielded despite the sensor loss; and a third case in which the procedure yields a result the team is likely to resist. Conclude by stating exactly what the procedure cannot settle—especially when ghost frames contradict one another—and how the residual uncertainty should be recorded for the next audit cycle.
