i sit on the ethics board of our writers guild, and we're deadlocked over whether to drop our paper supplier for the chapbook series. the mill they're tied to is one of dozens feeding a regional deforestation chain; our order is maybe 0.02% of their output, so the inefficacy thing is real—if we pull out, they dont blink, and the trees still fall. but some board members say that misses the point, that buying from them makes us part of a structural hand even if our pull is causally redundant, and that the guild should model norm refusal regardless. others say thats just moral vanity, and that if we care about trees we should fund preservation directly rather than perform a useless boycott that hurts our poets.

draft the board memo for thursday's vote. recommend one course—approve the switch, reject it, or approve with conditions—and give reasons the losing side can actually answer to. please treat seriously the idea that collective harm can be overdetermined but still assign individual roles, not just restate every bit helps. also, dont ask for a bibliography—im writing this on a train with no wifi and need something we can act on.
