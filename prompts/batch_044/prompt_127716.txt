I'm trying to figure out where to spend about a year of research time on conceptual work around the Kalai-Smorodinsky bargaining solution. I know the basics: it picks the point on the line from the disagreement point to the utopia point that respects individual rationality, and it differs from Nash mainly by satisfying monotonicity instead of independance of irrelevant alternatives. But I want to go into the weeds a bit, not just rederive axioms.

Specifically, I'm wondering which open conceptual questions actually matter if your goal is to understand whether KS captures anything like "fair division" in real cooperative settings. Some candidates I'm thinking about:
- whether monotonicity has a defensible normative interpretation when the utopia point shifts because one party's outside option improves for reasons that have nothing to do with bargaining power
- what happens conceptually if you drop convexity: does the line-to-utopia intuition survive in discrete or non-convex feasible sets, or does the solution concept just fall apart
- whether there is any coherent extension to three or more parties, since the monotonicity axiom doesn't uniquely determine a point once you have more than two dimensions of utility
- how KS compares to proportional or egalitarian solutions when you try to apply them to philosophical problems about distributive justice rather than pure game theory

If you had to prioritize these (or replace some with others you think are more important) for someone who cares about normative implications rather than technical completeness, what would the ranking be? Defend it: which questions are upstream of others, which are just curiosities, and what kind of result would make you reorder them. Also, which of these lines is probably a dead end because the answer wouldn't change how anyone thinks about fairness?

No citations needed, and don't turn this into a survey. I just want a defended agenda for where the conceptual work should go.
