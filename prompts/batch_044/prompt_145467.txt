I'm preparing notes for a seminar on long-term governance and want a consistency check on my position before presenting it. My commitments are these:

(1) I accept the singleton hypothesis in its standard formulation: a technologically mature civilization tends toward a single dominant decision-making system that shapes its cosmic future.
(2) I distinguish a hard singleton (one institutional center) from a soft singleton (distributed agents implementing one value function), but I also think the only normatively attractive form is what I call a "revisable singleton" — a dominant value system that retains rapid, institutionally protected mechanisms for updating its core values in response to new moral argument.
(3) I am a moral realist about value: there are correct answers to what ought to happen, not merely coordination equilibria.
(4) Given (3), I think a soft or revisable singleton guided by correct values is preferable to multipolar competition, because the latter risks existential catastrophe through conflict or race dynamics.
(5) At the same time, I hold that the determination of correct values should be radically inclusive and democratic, with no single agent or institution granted privileged epistemic authority a priori.
(6) I also think we should actively resist technological and institutional paths that make a hard singleton structurally inevitable, even when those paths reduce near-term existential risk.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear so, and how severely? Rank the tensions. For the serious ones, identify the least costly revision and what it sacrifices. In particular, address whether (5) can survive the transition to (2) — that is, whether democratic legitimacy is compatible with a unified value system — and whether (1) undermines (6). Also identify any hidden commitment I have implicitly taken on that I have not listed, especially regarding how a revisable singleton avoids value lock-in without fragmenting back into multipolarity.
