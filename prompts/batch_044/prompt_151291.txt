I recently spent an evening arguing with a close friend about whether classic intuition pumps—Thomson's violinist, Nozick's experience machine, the trolley switch—actually advance philosophical reasoning or merely engineer assent by restructuring which features of a case feel salient. My friend insisted that describing a scenario neutrally is impossible, so framing is inevitable and harmless; I countered that there's a difference between inevitable framing and what looks like a hidden comparison class embedded in the scenario itself, one that makes a particular judgment appear conceptually mandatory while rendering its rival not just wrong but muddled. Next week I'm recording a podcast episode with a philosopher who has developed a precise, technical version of this concern: she argues that intuition pumps don't simply elicit pre-theoretic judgments but actively reshape what she calls the "salience landscape" by smuggling in an implicit baseline against which any deviation is read as irrational or as a failure of conceptual competence.

I want a sequence of interview questions that presses her on this mechanism rather than letting her restate the view. Specifically, I need one question asking how she distinguishes legitimate conceptual clarification from the kind of manipulative framing she describes, paired with a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that all description involves framing and that the charge of manipulation therefore collapses into a trivial point about language. I also want a question on whether her account predicts that philosophers from radically different cultural or institutional backgrounds should systematically diverge on the same classic pumps, with a follow-up pressing whether she treats existing cross-cultural disagreement as confirmation of her view or worries that such data suffer from post-hoc rationalization by respondents trying to make sense of an alien frame. Then I need a question about how she treats "deflationary" responses that reject the pump's premises outright—does escaping the frame count as philosophical insight, or as failing to grasp the problem she thinks the pump was designed to illuminate? Finally, I want a progressive closing question asking whether she can specify the formal or structural features of a genuinely non-manipulative pump, or whether she believes the restructuring she identifies is unavoidable in any scenario that actually does philosophical work. The sequence should build in difficulty, anticipate her standard defenses, and avoid generic biographical openings.
