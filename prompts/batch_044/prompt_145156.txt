ok so i was trying to work out this argument mixing Appiah's honor code stuff with that Bernard Williams idea about moral change needing a loss of face for the old practice. (1) Appiah says dueling died cause honor codes shifted, not cause anyone won an argument. (2) Williams adds that for a practice to become shameful, people have to feel its old prestige turned into embarrassment. (3) Together they suggest moral revolutions work by flipping status, with rational argument playing almost no causal role. (4) Take slavery: slaveholders never mostly conceded it was wrong, but eventually owning slaves carried social stigma. (5) So the "revolution" was really just a peer-pressure update, not anyone learning anything. (6) But if no learning happens, then saying "slavery is wrong" went from false (in their minds) to true (in ours) without any reasoning connecting them - its just fashion declaring itself correct. (7) That proves too much: any norm that becomes unpopular counts as moral progress. (8) So either Williams/Appiah are describing fashion, not morality, or they have to admit arguments did the hidden work after all. Where does this break? I'd rather keep (1)-(3) and (6) than ditch (5).
