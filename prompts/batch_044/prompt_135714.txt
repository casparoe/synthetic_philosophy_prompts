Write a short story about a forum moderator who tries to replace bans with a "reintegrative shaming" ritual — users have to post a public apology thread that stays pinned for a month, then they're supposed to be welcomed back. The idea comes from some old criminology theory about how punishment should re-absorb people into the community rather than stigmatize them forever. But the forum has no real community, just an architechture of permanent records and search engines. The protagonist slowly realizes that in this space, the ritual doesnt actually reintegrate anyone; it just creates a searchable monument to their mistake. I want the story to explore whether online social sanction can ever avoid being pure stigmatization, given that the internet remembers everything and has no physical village to forgive in. Keep it grounded in the characters, dont lecture me, but the philosophical tension should be in the bones of the plot.
