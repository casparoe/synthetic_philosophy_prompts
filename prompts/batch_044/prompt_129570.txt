To keep the exercise from feeling like a familiar retelling, please inject an unfamiliar substrate framing: rather than billiard balls, imagine the simmballs as vibrating strings inside a resonant cavity, so that a symbol’s apparent “pushing” is actually a selection and amplification of harmonic frequencies, making the causal story feel more like tuning than like shoving masses.

Please write the poem in exactly five quatrains, separated by blank lines, with no introduction or conclusion outside the verses themselves; each quatrain should be two to four lines, in blank verse or loose free verse. The first quatrain should speak from the substrate’s perspective—pure vibration, collision, absence of meaning; the second should introduce a symbol that appears to direct motion; the third should describe the same event simultaneously in both the frequency-selection vocabulary and the particle-collision vocabulary; the fourth should ask whether the symbol’s efficacy is genuinely causal or merely projected onto the resonant pattern; and the fifth should leave both descriptions standing side by side without synthesis. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with the careenium recognizes the debate about whether collective phenomena have causal potency, yet it should work on its own for an audience that has never encountered Hofstadter’s text. Present only the five stanzas, with no prefatory or closing remarks.
