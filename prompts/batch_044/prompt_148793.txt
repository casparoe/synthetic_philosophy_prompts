I'm taking a seminar on hinge epistemology that spends most of its time in Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and a fellow student has developed a fairly consistent but not fully explicit view about which propositions function as hinges. Her rulings so far go like this: she treats "I have two hands" as a hinge within ordinary clinical practice but denies it that status in a philosophy seminar where skepticism is being staged; she counts "7 + 5 = 12" as a hinge inside standard arithmetic yet allows that it can be suspended when the consistency of the formal system itself is under investigation; she regards "Napoleon was defeated at Waterloo" as a hinge for conventional historical inquiry but not for a revisionist project that treats every archival source as potentially fabricated; she says "there is a tree outside the window" is a hinge for gardening or navigation but not for a phenomenological study of hallucination; she denies hinge status to explicit metaphysical formulations like "there is an external material world," on the ground that they are theoretical articulations rather than practical certainties; and she denies it to autobiographical specifics like "I was born in 1990," arguing that hinges must be framework-structuring rather than merely informationally particular.

The new cases we want to apply her framework to involve a neuroscientist calibrating a brain-computer interface. In the calibration context, she treats it as practically certain that "the electrode array is registering voltage fluctuations" and builds her encoding model on that basis, even though she fully recognizes that the device could in principle be generating simulated signals. Meanwhile, in her grant proposal she writes that "human consciousness is not reducible to patterns of voltage," using that claim to justify the ethical significance of her research. Predict whether she will classify the calibration-certainty about voltage registration as a hinge, and whether she will treat the anti-reductionist claim about consciousness as a hinge within biological science, a theoretical overreach, or something else. I am less interested in bare yes-or-no answers than in the competing rules that could generate her earlier pattern—perhaps one that makes hinge-status depend on practical indispensability within a specific form of inquiry, another that adds the requirement that hinges resist being articulated as explicit theoretical theses, and perhaps a contextualist variant that ties hinges to the social-institutional framing of the activity. Identify the rules, say what each predicts about these two new cases, offer your best-guess reconstruction of her implicit principle with reasons, and propose one further hypothetical case that would best discriminate between the leading interpretations.
