I'm working on a novel where one of my characters is this brilliant but deeply anxious researcher — she genuinely wants to cure this fictional neurodegenrative disorder, but every time she's about to crack the final step she quietly rewrites her own success metrics so that "making meaningful progress" becomes the win, and then her lab partners praise her for that, and she feels good, but the cure never actually lands. I was talking with my writter friend and we realized it's not quite procrastination or fear of success, because she isn't avoiding the finish line out of anxiety; she's literally restructuring what the finish line is every time social approval is available for the intermediate step. There's another version I keep seeing in real-world tech teams too — the product manager who keeps adding "essential" features right before launch, not because users need them, but because each feature demo gets a round of internal applause, and then the original vision gets lost in the slow accretion of praise-worthy parts.

Is there an established term for this exact loop, where the approval mechanism doesn't just motivate the agent but actively rewrites the goal-state to match whatever is currently praise-worthy? I know about approval-directed vs goal-directed agents in AI alignment, but that usually frames it as the agent optimizing for approval instead of the goal, not this slower, iterative corruption where the goal itself gets redefined step by step through feedback. Does this have a name in psychology or decision theory, and if not, can you coin one that distinguishes it from simple goal displacement, from motte-and-bailey style equivocation, and from ordinary scope creep? I'm not trying to sound academic — I'm just tired of using the wrong word in my draft and I don't want to misrepresent the idea if I end up explaining it in an author's note. Also please say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you're least sure of, because I really don't want to recieve bad advice on something I'm going to put in print.
