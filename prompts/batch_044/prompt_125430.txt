I'm putting together the final exam for my philosophy of mind seminar—upper level, mostly juniors and seniors who've done the intro stuff but dont always think past the textbook. I'm sick of essays that just rehearse Dennett's user-illusion talk like it's gospel, so I want a question that gets in the weeds without asking students to cite papers they can't look up offline. Here's the framing I'm playing with: call it the "dual-layer user illusion." Layer one is the brain's interface for phenomenal properties—colors, pains, sounds—presented as if they were intrinsic features of the world or body. Layer two is the interface for a unified "user" or subject who experiences them. Now imagine a critic—the "layer-separatist"—who says split-brain cases show layer two can fracture while layer one stays intact, which means we should be local eliminativists about the self-illusion but leave qualia untouched.

Please write me teh exam question itself, designed for about 800 words in 90 minutes. Then give me a model answer and a marking scheme that seperates an A from a B. The scheme should name the specific moves a top answer must hit: whether transparency arguments apply differently to properties versus the subject, whether preserving layer one actually solves the explanatory gap or just relocates it, and whether the separatist is sneaking in property dualism about layer one. Also flag the common mistakes—like conflating the two layers, or thinking split-brain proves eliminativism about consciousness altogether. No bibliographies, no reading lists, just the conceptual machinery.
