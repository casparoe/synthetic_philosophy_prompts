Nietzsche's eternal recurrence means that every event in one's life will happen again infinitely in the exact same order. He first introduces this idea in Thus Spoke Zarathustra, where it serves as the central teaching of the work. The doctrine has two parts. First, there is a cosmological proof based on finite energy and infinite time, which Nietzsche treats in his published books as the rigorous foundation. Second, there is an ethical thought experiment, sometimes called the greatest weight, where a demon asks whether you would curse or bless your life repeating forever; this is mainly an emotional reaction to the physics. Nietzsche borrowed the idea directly from Stoic ekpyrosis, so like the Stoics he uses it to teach resignation to fate. Still, the idea fits with his view of becoming, because repetition is not static being but a dynamic process that keeps unfolding.
