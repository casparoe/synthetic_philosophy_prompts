Design a decision-tree taxonomy that classifies philosophical positions on the import of Zhuangzi's butterfly dream episode from the Qiwu lun chapter, structured so that a respondent is sorted by answers to concrete interpretive claims rather than by the labels they already prefer. Begin with a root node asking whether the episode is best read as a propositional skeptical argument about waking certainty or as a performative enactment of transformation (wuhua) that undermines the very distinction between arguing and enacting. From there, branch on whether the metamorphosis between Zhuang Zhou and the butterfly implies an ontological equality of phenomenal states, a merely epistemic limit on distinguishing them, or a dissolution of the substrate that makes such distinctions meaningful. Add a further branch on whether personal identity across the transformation is preserved in some hidden form, rendered serially discontinuous, or made genuinely indeterminate. Then branch on the appropriate philosophical response: epistemic suspension, aesthetic play in the face of change, or ethical detachment from fixed selfhood. The tree should contain between five and eight leaves, each named (e.g., classical dream skeptic, ontological pluralist, identity-dissolutionist, performative-transformationalist, aesthetic Zhuangist, ethical quietist, or unnamed) and each carrying a one-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment. You must include at least one combination of answers that appears to be genuinely unoccupied—diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored (for instance, a view that insists on strict numerical personal identity across transformations while simultaneously affirming complete ontological equality of the states). Every internal node should be phrased as a concrete case or yes/no claim that a respondent can answer without knowing which leaf it leads to; avoid theoretical jargon in the questions themselves. Finally, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, explaining whether the gap is rhetorical, practical, or conceptual, and note whether the root distinction between propositional argument and performative enactment actually divides the space or merely relocates the same disagreements.
