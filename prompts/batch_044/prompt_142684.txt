Take the Burr-Hamilton duel — skip the politics, focus on the social logic of the insult, the refusal to apologize, and why both guys treated backing down as worse then death. Analyze it through honor culture, dignity culture, and victimhood culture, treating them as three rival moral frameworks rather then historical stages. For each: what was the real injury, what should Burr and Hamilton of done, and does the framework even recognize a harm that isnt physical? Then flip it — does Hamilton dying and Burr being ruined prove dignity culture is moraly superior, or does it just misread honor culture's internal coherence as barbarism? Now the niche twist: imagine Hamilton survives but is permanently disabled; does victimhood culture grant him a status that dignity culture denies, and does that show victimhood culture isnt just a corruption of the other two but has its own logic? I want disciplined mapping — who plays which role, what counts as evidence, where the theory goes silent — running both ways: the duel testing whether any culture is actually morally superior, and each culture explaining why the duel made sense to them. Don't force a winner if the case doesnt yield one.
