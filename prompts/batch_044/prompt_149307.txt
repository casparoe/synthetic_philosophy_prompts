The second framework is "conditional asymmetry": the view that these forces are always mediated by prior institutional fragility, and that attributing any form of agency to them commits a category error that obscures how human storage practices, trade networks, and state capacities amplify or dampen environmental shocks. Here, Tambora only becomes historically decisive because specific agrarian and market structures had already created systemic vulnerability.
