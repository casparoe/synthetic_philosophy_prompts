The student wrote:
'Aumann's 1976 theorem proves that two Bayesian agents with a common prior who have common knowledge of each other's posteriors for some event must have identical posteriors. The proof works by induction over information partitions: you show that if posteriors are constant on every cell of both agents' partitions and there's common knowledge, they match across the whole space. So you need shared priors, Bayes-rule updating, and full transparency about beliefs.

For climate disputes, the theorem doesn't apply because the common prior condition fails — scientists and skeptics weight studies differently and hold different background commitments about institutional bias. There's also a transparency gap: climate modelers often don't reveal all paramter tweaks, so others can't have common knowledge of their true posteriors. That's why disagreement persists without anyone being irrational.

But there's a deeper convergence result. Aumann showed in later work that if rational agents keep communicating their beliefs, they eventually agree even from different priors. That's exactly why idealized deliberation structures like HCH in AI alignment build in iterative feedback loops — to factor away disagreement through repeated transparency. In real committees this rarely happens because hidden prior differences block the process, so convergence is the exception.'
