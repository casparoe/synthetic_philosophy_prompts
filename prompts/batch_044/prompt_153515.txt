For my senior-year independent study in philosophy, I am trying to specify the criteria any adequate theory of the meaning of work must meet now that automation is a structural feature rather than a marginal exception. I want the desiderata to address five specific demands, each with its motivating case or distinction. First, must the theory ground work's meaning in the narrative identity it gives the worker, or in the social recognition it receives, and how should it treat phantom labor—hidden emotional or algorithmic oversight that is structurally necessary but socially invisible? Second, must it preserve a distinction between embodied, co-present labor and disembodied distributed labor, given that care work requires physical presence while remote data-labeling produces identical economic outputs without the same relational structure? Third, when automation eliminates dangerous or repetitive tasks, does the theory have to account for a corresponding loss of meaning tied to risk, mastery, or collective struggle, or should it treat that loss as an unambiguous liberation? Fourth, can the theory accommodate roles that generate organizational hierarchy without producing use-value—what are sometimes called bullshit jobs—without reducing them to mere error, especially since such roles appear structurally necessary to certain automated systems? Fifth, is the meaning of work essentially resistant to full automation, such that fully automated labor is conceptually no longer work, or does automation reveal that the meaning was always contingent on social context rather than on the activity itself? For each desideratum, please state the consideration behind it, flag which are non-negotiable and which reflect particular camp preferences, and indicate whether any pair—especially the tension between work as self-creation and work as necessary drudgery—seems jointly unsatisfiable. I am not asking for an exhaustive scorecard, but I do want a brief indication of where a Marxist-humanist framework, a recognition-based framework, and a purely instrumental framework each stand against the list, since my aim is to see whether these demands can be held together at all.
