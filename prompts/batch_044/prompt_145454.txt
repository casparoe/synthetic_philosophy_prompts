I've been drafting a short essay for my grad seminar on Strawson's Basic Argument and I keep getting stuck on a weird extension. Here's the gist of the first part: Strawson says you're not morally responsible for what you do if you do it becuase of how you are, where 'how you are' includes your mental nature, and you're not responsible for how you are because you can't be causa sui. That's all standard stuff we've covered in class.

But then I want to apply this to a distributed AI system that updates its weights through federated learning across thousands of nodes—no single node designed the final weights, and the system can't trace its own architecture back to any one causal origin. Is the system 'not responsible' in Strawson's sense, or does the argument require a single locus of agency? I also try to compare it to a corporate board making decisions—no individual board member is fully responsible, yet we hold the corporation liable. I'm not asking for a bibliography, but I'd appreciate comments on whether I'm stretching Strawson too far, whether the federated-learning analogy actually illuminates the causa sui problem or just muddles it, and if there's an obviuos objection I'm missing about collective agency that kills the parallel. The draft feels interesting but I worry I'm just being clever for no reason, and I'd rather know now before I show it to the seminar.
