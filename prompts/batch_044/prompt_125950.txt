I am a university student currently enrolled in an advanced seminar on normative ethics and, concurrently, in a course on robust statistical decision theory. In our ethics module we have been examining the problem often called consequentialist cluelessness: the claim that moral agents typically lack sufficient predictive access to the long-range, branching causal structures triggered by their actions—particularly under conditions of social complexity and ecological feedback—so that expected-utility reasoning fails to yield determinate guidance. Rather than asking for a standard literature review, I would like you to perform a systematic translation. Restate the central arguments both for and against this cluelessness objection using only the vocabulary and conceptual machinery of my decision-theory course: empirical risk minimization, out-of-distribution generalization, adversarial perturbations of the data-generating process, policy classes constrained by complexity bounds, and the notion of an epistemic budget—the explicit allocation of confidence resources across competing causal models when no single model achieves dominance in sample. Frame the moral agent as a policy selecting actions in an environment characterized by delayed, noisy reward signals and severe distribution shift.

Then provide the residue report. Identify which normative claims translate without loss, for instance whether the learnability conditions in statistical theory correspond precisely to the ethical principle that obligation requires capacity; which claims must be distorted to fit the framework, such as the distinction between act-utilitarian and rule-utilitarian evaluation or the moral relevance of intentions versus outcomes; and which claims have no counterpart whatsoever—whether anything like non-instrumental value, moral luck, or the distinctive normative weight of omission versus commission simply vanishes. Conclude by assessing whether the untranslatable residue reveals that consequentialism is essentially applied robust optimization missing a few normative axioms, or whether it demonstrates that the statistical vocabulary is systematically blind to dimensions of moral reasoning that the ethical debate genuinely tracks.
