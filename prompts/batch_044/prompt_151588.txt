Compare internalist and externalist accounts of practical reasons by applying both to the same precisely specified case: an agent who, after a profound and apparently irreversible shift in her values, retains formal obligations to a long-term project she now regards as worthless—for instance, a philanthropic endowment she founded but no longer endorses. For each framework, state whether she still has a reason to maintain the project, and pinpoint exactly where they diverge, particularly on whether a present absence of any motivational link can nullify a reason. Then assess which account yields the more defensible verdict in this scenario, justifying your judgment in terms of the coherence of practical rationality rather than empirical psychology.
