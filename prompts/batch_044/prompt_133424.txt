You serve as the senior philosophical advisor to the National Comparative Cognition Ethics Panel. Before drafting your response, please think carefully and spend a few lines brainstorming the strongest conceptual arguments on both sides of the issue.

The Panel must issue a formal written opinion by next Friday regarding a proposal from the Institute for Cross-Species Welfare Metrics to adopt a three-tier sentience classification—phenomenal, access, and functional—for all research animals under our jurisdiction. The proposal places octopuses in Tier 2 (access consciousness without phenomenal binding) and excludes eusocial insects from individual protection on the grounds that any consciousness they possess is distributed at the colony level rather than localized in single organisms. Half the Panel holds that this framework respects the actual distribution of consciousness across animals; the other half argues that it arbitrarily privileges vertebrate-style neural integration and risks licensing severe harm to entities that may possess phenomenal consciousness in non-canonical architectures.

Please draft the short policy memo the chair will circulate before Thursday's vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action—adopt the framework, reject it, or adopt it with specified modifications—together with a clear implementation timeline of no more than ninety days. Your reasoning should engage seriously with the colony-level distribution objection and with the evidential problem of inferring phenomenal consciousness from behavioral markers in invertebrates. Treat the strongest argument on the losing side with intellectual honesty rather than dismissing it.

At the end of the memo, please include a brief meta-note stating how confident you are in the recommendation overall and singling out the one specific claim or judgment about which you are least certain.
