Write a proposal for a one-semester philosophy-of-mind research project (approximately 5,000 words, deliverable at term end) on the reflexive stability of illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. The precise question: if phenomenal properties are illusory, what is the status of the first-person introspective judgments that report them? Specifically, does the illusionist need those judgments to carry genuine phenomenal content in order to explain why the illusion is widespread and compelling, and if so, does that reintroduce the very phenomenology the view denies? State the question clearly and distinguish this reflexive problem from standard objections about the explanatory gap or epistemic access. Explain why current illusionist strategies—treating introspection as predictive self-modeling without phenomenal residue—leave the reflexive issue open rather than resolving it. Outline the method: first, formalize the reflexive condition (what an introspective report must be like to motivate illusionism without being phenomenally loaded); second, examine whether self-modeling architectures can generate reports that satisfy that condition without presupposing phenomenal consciousness; third, compare the result with parallel reflexive challenges faced by eliminativism about propositional attitudes. Specify what counts as success (a coherent reflexive illusionism that does not smuggle in phenomenal properties at the meta-level) and what counts as failure (a demonstration that any introspective account robust enough to motivate illusionism necessarily presupposes phenomenal consciousness). Provide a realistic scope assessment: identify which components are tractable within a single semester, which risk expanding into a dissertation-scale project, and how to contain the work accordingly. Do not request a bibliography, citation list, or references to specific authors; frame the conceptual argument so that scholarly citations can be added afterward.
