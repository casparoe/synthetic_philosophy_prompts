Hey, quick question for anyone who reads too much political ethics. There's the usual dirty-hands problem -- a mayor approves a toxic dump in a poor neighborhood because it's the only way to stop a bigger environmental disaster. Classic unavoidable wrong. But I've noticed a follow-up move: later, the same mayor approves a luxury condo deal that displaces residents, and when critics call it out, she says "Look, I already have dirty hands from the dump; refusing this won't cleanse me, and the tax revenue fixes the dump's damage." The condo deal isn't neccessary at all -- she's just using her prior unavoidable stain as a license for avoidable corruption. I've seen a similar thing with a senator who voted for a bad surveillance bill during a crisis, then later used that vote to justify supporting a clearly unconstitutinal data grab: "My hands were dirty the moment I voted yes in '03, so holding the line now is just vanity."

Is there an established concept for this exact pattern? It's close to dirty hands, but dirty hands usually stops at the unavoidable act. It's not moral licensing, since that's about good behavior giving permission for bad behavior, not about past neccessary evil excusing present optional evil. It also feels different from a slippery slope, because she's not saying the first act caused the second; she's saying her morral status is already ruined so she might as well. If there's no name, what would you call it, and how do you draw the line between this and plain old hypocrisy?
