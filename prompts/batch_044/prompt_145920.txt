Compare what "liberal" means in American political talk versus in European (especialy continental) political talk. In the US it often gets tied to left-leaning welfare-state stuff, but in Europe it can still mean classical liberalism — free markets, individual rights, limited government. Now here's a twist: there's this somewhat niche idea called "ordoliberalism" from post-war Germany, which mixes strong market competition with a strict legal framework designed to stop cartels and monopolies. Is ordoliberalism closer to American "liberalism" or to European "liberalism"? And does the fact that the same word points in such different directions tell us something deep about how political language gets shaped by local fights, or is it mostly just a translation mistake?
