Write a deadpan, bureaucratic FAQ—approximately 600 words, formatted with numbered questions, official footnotes, and a brief preamble—published by the fictional "Municipal Office of Independent Judgment" in a jurisdiction that has formally abolished all political parties according to the logic of Simone Weil's critique. The document should address practical citizen inquiries: how to advance a policy proposal without party platforms, how collective deliberation is structured, what procedures exist when citizens experience "collective enthusiasm," and how administrative disputes are resolved. Maintain an entirely neutral administrative voice throughout. The satire should emerge from the substance rather than the tone: every institutional safeguard intended to preserve individual moral reasoning—such as mandatory "moral attention" assessments before voting, the licensing of disagreement through certified "independent judgment panels," and strict prohibitions on shared slogans—should quietly recreate the exact pathologies Weil attributed to parties, including bureaucratic groupthink, emotional loyalty to administrative categories, and the suppression of dissent under the guise of neutrality. Include at least one invented internal footnote describing an incident in which an administrator accidentally formed a faction around a procedural interpretation, and show that the official reprimand issued afterward demonstrates the system's inability to recognize its own partisanship. The piece should remain legible to someone unfamiliar with Weil's work, but a reader acquainted with her view that parties corrupt conscience by substituting collective passion for independent judgment should recognize that the parody is structurally faithful to her argument rather than an external mockery.
