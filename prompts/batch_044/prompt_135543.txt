If Gottfried Leibniz—who died in 1716 with the Monadology and the mill argument among his final works—were presented with a version of the extended mind thesis that involves not a notebook but a human agent coupled to a large language model in a loop of continuous interaction: the agent treats the model's outputs as continuous with their own reasoning, lacks introspective access to the weights or training data that produce them, and experiences the combined system as a single cognitive subject because the interface is designed to be functionally transparent—how would he analyze this? Apply only his 1714 conceptual apparatus: does this count as a genuine extension of the monad's perceptions, or as a particularly sophisticated case of pre-established harmony between distinct substances? Can his distinction between petites perceptions and apperception accommodate distributed cognition across biological and artificial substrates that lack windows? And does his rejection of interactionist dualism commit him to accepting cognition beyond the skin, or rather to denying that the artificial component has perceptions at all, leaving only the biological monad capable of true apperception?
