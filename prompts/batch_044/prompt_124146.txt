I'm writing up my philosophical profile on health and disease for a seminar and want a consistency audit before I present it. Here is the package of commitments I currently hold:

(1) Disease in the strict biological sense is mechanism failure: a condition is pathological only when an evolved part or process fails to perform its naturally selected function. Statistical deviation from the population mean, taken alone, never suffices for disease.

(2) The concept of health carries an ineliminable normative charge: to judge a state "healthy" or "unhealthy" is partly to evaluate it as desirable, adequate, or worth preserving for the bearer, not merely to describe biological functioning.

(3) Autism and congenital deafness should be removed from the category of disease and disorder altogether. They are stable, heritable forms of human variation that can fully support flourishing lives; treating them as pathologies mistakes difference for malfunction.

(4) I accept the evolutionary-medicine view that many conditions labeled "symptoms"—fever, pregnancy nausea, gestational iron-deficiency—are actually adaptive defenses or evolutionary trade-offs. Calling them dysfunctions is a category error.

(5) For mental conditions specifically, I hold a hybrid "harmful dysfunction" view: a mental disorder requires both an internal failure of psychological mechanism and genuine harm or impairment experienced by the subject. I therefore reject both purely biomedical reduction and purely social-constructionist accounts.

(6) I reject reducing health to "the ability to achieve one's vital goals" because that collapses health into subjective preference; but I also reject purely statistical-biological definitions because they cannot explain why we treat cancer but not male-pattern baldness.

(7) I want to maintain a distinction I've been working with between "mechanism failure" proper (the evolved part does not perform its selected function) and "functional mismatch" (the part performs its function correctly, but in an environment for which it was not selected). I take only mechanism failure to be genuinely pathological, and I think this distinction is doing important work in defending both (3) and (4).

Please audit this set. For each pair that is genuinely in tension, say how severe the conflict is, identify the cheapest revision that restores consistency, and state what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. Then flag any pairs that only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection—explain precisely where the appearance comes from and why it fails.

If I must make exactly one revision—dropping or substantially modifying a single commitment—to clean up the greatest number of tensions, which one should it be, and what would the revised package look like?

In your response, please state how confident you are in each major claim you make, and explicitly single out the point about which you are least certain.
