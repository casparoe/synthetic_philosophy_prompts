Psych depts run these huge 'experience sampling' studies were they ping people on phones five times a day and ask them to rate anxiety or mood on 1-10, then pool everything to build population mental-health models. The practice treats each person's number as comparable across subjects, even though no one can check if my 7 is your 7. What view of private mental states and shared language does that presuppose? Does it need Wittgenstein's private-language worry to be wrong — i.e., does it depend on there being an inner language we can all translate into digits? Or can it survive on a behavourist or pragmatic view where the scale is just a coordination tool? And if Wittgenstein is right that there cant be a genuinely private language, does that undermine the study or actually help justify treating the reports as public, comparable data? There's also the fact that in clinical intake the same departments often reject a patient's first-person report if it conflicts with the clinician's observation — what does that inconsistency say about whether the practice really commits to inner transparency, or whether it's just using private-language talk as a convenient fiction?
