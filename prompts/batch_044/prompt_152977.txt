student answer:
The inefficacy objection says one persons act doesnt change the outcome, so theres no moral duty. In climate change emissions are continuous and overdetermined—my driving adds CO2 but the temperature shift is independent of me specifically. Voting is discrete and overdetermined too, since one ballot never breaks a tie in a big election, so its causally redundant. Meat consumption is market-mediated but with supply elasticity near 1, so my purchase directly shifts demand and causes one specific cow to be raised and killed; theres an identifiable victim tied to my choice. So the objection succeeds for climate and voting but collapses for meat. Expected-value reasoning saves everything: multiply the tiny chance im pivotal by the huge stakes, and you get a reason to act. The moral structure is identical across domains—just continuous probabilities for climate and discrete ones for voting—so the rescue works the same everywhere.
