(1) for linked propostions, any agregation rule over a universal domain will hit profiles that produce collective contradictions.
(2) the only way to avoid that is to restrict the domain of admissible profiles.
(3) in a real democracy, restricting admissible profiles means excluding some people's judgments.
(4) so guaranteeing rational collective decisions requires disenfranchisment.
(5) but that's absurd -- the dilemma is a logic puzzle, not an argument for silencing voters.
(6) therefore one of the steps is wrong.

i'd rather keep (1) than (3). where does the slip actually happen? is it that domain restriction can mean agenda control instead of excluding people, or is there a hidden move from logical consistency to democratic legitimacy?
