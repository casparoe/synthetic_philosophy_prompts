Draft the joint statement that would result from an adversarial collaboration between a Lewis-style counterpart theorist and a Kripke-Plantinga-style transworld-identity theorist on de re modality, essential properties, and origin essentialism. The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims—covering the formal possible-worlds apparatus, the distinction between essential and accidental properties, and exactly what the standard no-go results (failure of cross-world identity of indiscernibles, non-uniqueness of counterpart relations) do and do not rule out—followed by a disagreements table or paired list in which each disagreement is phrased in wording its own side would accept. The disagreements should address at least: whether one and the same individual can exist in more than one world; whether counterpart relations ground modal truths or merely represent them; and whether origin essentialism applies uniformly to artifacts, organisms, and abstract objects. Next, identify the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record each side’s prediction in advance. Include as one such test case a specific blockchain smart-contract instance deployed at block height H with nonce N and unique hash, which has a functional specification that could have been realized with nonce N+1 or a different deployment block; both parties should agree this is structurally analogous to classic origin-essentialism puzzles, and should state whether, in the world with nonce N+1 but identical code and state, the original contract exists, has a counterpart, or is absent. Finally, indicate whether any part of the dispute is purely verbal—e.g., disagreements about whether “the same object” is used in a metaphysically loaded sense versus a loose representational sense—and if so, specify exactly which part and why. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; keep every claim evaluable without web access.
