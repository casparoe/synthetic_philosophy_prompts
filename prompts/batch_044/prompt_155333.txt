Me and two friends in my philosphy seminar got stuck arguing about Grice and speaker meaning. Heres how it went:

Maya: Grice says speaker meaning is just what you intend your audience to recognize. So if I tell you "I'm out of gas" and I mean my car is empty, thats fixed. Even if I later tell people I actually meant I was tired, that doesnt retroactively change what I meant originally — its just a new claim about my mental state.

Leo: But that makes conversation way too one-directional. In real life you often figure out what you meant by seeing how the other person responds. The meaning gets negotiated, not just broadcast from one head. Grice has no room for that co-construction.

Maya: Youre mixing up speaker meaning with hearer interpretation. Grice never said the listener cant influence future turns. He just said for that specific utterance theres a fact about my intention. If my intention was genuinely vague, thats fine — but you cant say the listeners reaction determines my original mental state.

Leo: Okay, but then take cases where I genuinely have no fixed intention about whether Im being literal or metaphorical. Grice forces us to say theres a hidden correct interpretation, even when I myself couldnt tell you what it was until after you reacted. That seems wrong.

Maya: No, it just means my intention was indeterminate, not that it was determined by you. The problem with your view is that it collapses speaker meaning into whatever the audience takes away, which is exactly what Grice was trying to avoid.

Leo: Im not collapsing it entirely — Im saying its jointly constructed. But Grices framework cant even describe that because every bit of meaning has to be traced back to the speakers prior mental state. So theyre missing half the story.
