As a doctoral student in philosophy of physics working on the foundations of Everettian quantum mechanics, I am trying to clarify a precise conceptual tension in attempts to ground subjective probabilities without circularly presupposing the Born rule. Consider a measurement that produces exactly two branches, with amplitudes α and β such that |α|² / |β|² is irrational. In the self-locating-uncertainty approach, an agent is supposed to be uncertain about which branch she occupies, yet critics observe that without prior weights one might be tempted to apply a principle of indifference over the branches themselves. Please explain the conceptual issue that arises specifically from this incommensurability: why does the irrational ratio prevent any rational partitioning of epistemic credence across branches, and how does this expose a circularity risk if one tries to recover |α|² via branch-counting rather than branch-weighting? I am looking for an intuitive, gut-level account of why the irrational-ratio case makes the gap between counting branches and weighting them by squared amplitude especially acute, and what this implies for the claim that Everettian mechanics can genuinely generate standard quantum probabilities from purely structural features of the wavefunction.
