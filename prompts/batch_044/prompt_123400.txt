State your credence, on a scale of 0 to 1, that the paradox of fiction dissolves once we distinguish between existential belief-commitment and what we might call shadow-ontological evaluation—the activation of normative and affective structures directed at a minimal representational scaffold that carries the properties of harm, injustice, or loss without carrying any commitment that those properties are instantiated in the actual world. Decompose your answer into three terms with a number and one line of justification for each: first, the probability that emotional responses to real-world tragedies necessarily involve existential belief-commitment about the victim or event; second, the probability that fiction can trigger the same normative-evaluative and affective mechanisms while entirely suspending that existential layer; third, the probability that such suspension is compatible with the full phenomenological intensity of fictional grief or fear—that is, that the response is not merely a muted analog. Then combine them into an overall credence. Finally, identify which of the three terms is the weakest link in your chain and specify what kind of conceptual or empirical result—not a citation or author—would most shift that term and therefore your final number. Do not appeal to named theories or texts; reason directly from the structure of emotional cognition and representational commitment.
