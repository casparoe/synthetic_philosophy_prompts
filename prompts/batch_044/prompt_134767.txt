I'm a philosophy PhD student who also advises a regional disaster-response board, and I need a reusable decision proceedure for rescue-allocation cases where we can't save everyone. I want it grounded in the Taurek-style view that raw numbers shouldn't decide—saving five doesn't automatically outweigh saving one—but I need it to handle real messiness: victim counts that are estimates with big error bars, partial rescue where survivors are left severely disabled, and knock-on harm to dependants. Write the procedure as ordered questions with clear stop/no-stop points, flag exactly where judgment replaces calculation, and say what it can't settle. Then run it on two proposals: (a) one helicopter can reach either a confirmed 3 people in a flooded clinic or an estimated 8–15 in a remote valley with unreliable headcounts; (b) a rapid unit saves more lives but leaves survivors with serious disability, while a slower unit saves fewer but intact. Include one case where the procedure gives an answer the board will hate. Keep it practical—something people without philosophy training can run in a meeting.
