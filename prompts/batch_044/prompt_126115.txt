I am currently enrolled in an upper-division seminar on bioethics and transhumanism, and we have spent several weeks examining the hedonistic imperative—the abolitionist thesis that biotechnology should not merely treat suffering but eradicate its neurological substrate altogether. For a creative assignment tied to the course, I need a sustained satirical piece that treats this view with deadpan seriousness rather than dismissive mockery. Please write a parody of an institutional review board evaluation memo, approximately 750 words, formatted as numbered sections (Project Overview, Risk-Benefit Analysis, Informed Consent Protocol, Committee Deliberation, and Final Recommendation). The voice should be that of an unflappable university ethics committee assessing a fictional proposed trial led by an invented principal investigator, Dr. Aris Thorne, who seeks to deploy germline gene editing to eliminate the capacity for suffering in all descendants. The satire should derive from applying standard bioethical machinery—the therapy/enhancement distinction, the problem of consent for unborn subjects, and the asymmetry between suffering and pleasure—to the abolitionist goal, so that bureaucratic language gradually exposes its own contradictions: for example, the committee should debate whether eradicating suffering counts as restoring species-typical function or as enhancement beyond it, and they should draft consent forms for embryos who cannot be consulted. Do not invent verbatim quotations from real philosophers or texts; paraphrase any referenced arguments without quotation marks. The target is the internal logic of abolitionist bioethics and the institutional discourse of ethics review, not any named living person. The final recommendation must take a definite position—approval, rejection, or approval-with-bizarre-conditions—and the reasoning should reveal a genuine tension in treating universal suffering-abolition as a routine therapeutic intervention. After the piece, include a brief authorial note of roughly 100 to 150 words identifying where you took liberties with actual bioethical positions or relied on contested readings of the therapy/enhancement boundary, since I want to understand exactly where the satire diverges from serious scholarship. Keep the irony sustained and deadpan throughout; do not break character to signal that the proposal is absurd.
