Hey, since I retired from teh lab I've been chewing on AI ethics and I think I built an argument that proves too much about value alignment. (1) If an AI should serve everyone's values, it needs some aggregation rule that ranks outcomes across people with different preferences. (2) Liberal neutrality says that rule can't depend on any one substantive conception of the good life. (3) But Arrow's conditions for rational social choice -- unrestricted domain, Pareto efficiency, independence of irrelevent alternatives, and non-dictatorship -- can't all hold together once you have three people with incommensurable values. (4) So any workable aggregation rule must violate at least one of those conditions. (5) If we drop non-dictatorship, one person's values dominate, which violates neutrality. (6) If we drop independence of irrelevent alternatives, then adding a clearly bad option changes the ranking of the good ones, which looks like a reasoning error in teh AI. (7) Therefore liberal neutrality plus rational value aggregation for AI is impossible, and every talk of democratically aligned AI is secretly smuggling in a moral theory like utilitarianism. I want to keep (1) and (3) if possible, but I feel like (6) or (7) is sneaking in an extra premise. Where does this argument go wrong, and what does fixing it cost?
