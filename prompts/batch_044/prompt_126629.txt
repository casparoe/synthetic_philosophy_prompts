I have read the standard rationalist-blog summaries of Wittgenstein’s private language argument—the usual quick tour through the beetle-in-the-box, the diary-keeper, and the community-check conclusion—and I remain unsure whether contemporary philosophy of mind has actually answered the argument or merely renamed its premises. Before I try to apply it to questions about artificial mental representation, I want a rigorous specification of what an answer must deliver.

Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of mental content must satisfy to count as having genuinely addressed Wittgenstein’s private language argument, rather than sidestepping it by changing the subject. The list should cover at least the following dimensions, each paired with the concrete case or distinction that motivates it:

- How the theory handles first-person authority and the possibility of systematic self-error about one’s own mental states, without collapsing into a behaviorist check that requires an actual external observer.
- Whether it can draw a stable line between a genuinely private language and a merely private code—an internal representational scheme that is in principle interpretable by others but not accessible to the subject herself.
- Whether it can accommodate artificial or isolated systems that acquire structured internal representations without undergoing social linguistic practice, and if not, whether that exclusion is principled or ad hoc.
- Which elements of the argument concern normativity of meaning—the idea that correct use requires a standard beyond individual whim—versus which concern only epistemic access—the idea that one cannot verify one’s own usage without an external check.
- Whether avoiding the argument forces the theory into a dispositional or functionalist reduction of mental content, and whether that consequence should count as a success or a failure.

For each desideratum, indicate whether it is non-negotiable or camp-specific, flag any pairs that appear jointly unsatisfiable given standard constraints in philosophy of mind, and briefly sketch how causal-informational and conceptual-role theories fare against it. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citation requests; I need the logical structure, not a literature map. Phrase every node so that someone could evaluate it without already knowing which interpretation of Wittgenstein it favors.
