I'm trying to pin down a pattern I keep seeing in arguments about origin essentialism — you know, the idea that you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg. People will say things like, if sperm #2 had won the race instead of sperm #1, the resulting kid wouldn't be me, just my sibling. But then they also say that if I'd been born with a sligtly different genome from a mutation right after fertilization, I'd still be me, just with different traits. So there's this weird asymmetry where genetic difference before the zygote breaks identity, but genetic difference immediately after doesn't. Is there an established term for that exact boundary — treating the conception genome as some kind of locked blueprint? Or is it just part of the standard origin-essentialist story? I'm also curious whether anyone's called out how this sneaks in a kind of essentialism about the zygote that doesn't line up with how we usually think about personal identity surviving development and change over time. If there's no name, would "conception-lock bias" be a decent label, and what's the cleanest way to distinguish it from plain origin essentialism?
