For my metaphysics seminar on dispositions and failure conditions, I have been extending the literature on finks and masks to institutional rules that contain self-amending structures, and I want to check whether the argument below contains a hidden flaw or genuinely proves too much.

(1) A constitutional amendment clause possesses the disposition to be activated when a specified procedural threshold is met.
(2) The clause can contain an entrenchment provision that functions as a mask: it suppresses the activation of the amendment procedure precisely when the threshold is satisfied, analogous to a fink that interrupts a disposition at its triggering moment.
(3) If such a mask is legally and structurally possible, then the clause's amendability is inseparable from the potential for its own simultaneous cancellation.
(4) By the same reasoning, for any disposition D with trigger conditions T, one can construct a corresponding mask that activates exactly at T.
(5) Therefore, for every alleged disposition, there exists a structurally identical counterpart in which the manifestation is permanently vulnerable to preemptive cancellation.
(6) If a property never manifests without the concurrent possibility of self-cancellation being present, then it lacks the modal stability required of a genuine causal power.
(7) Consequently, dispositions—whether physical, psychological, or institutional—are not real properties but only heuristic descriptions of patterns that never complete in an unmasked form.

The conclusion seems absurd: it would imply, for example, that a vase is not genuinely brittle because one could always attach a shock-absorbing shield that activates upon impact. I want to keep (1) and (2) because they seem descriptively accurate about legal design, but I am unsure whether the error lies in (4)'s universal constructibility claim, in an equivocation within (5) between possibility and actuality, or in (6)'s demand for modal stability. Which step should be rejected, why did it initially look plausible, and what does each available rejection cost for the ontology of dispositions?
