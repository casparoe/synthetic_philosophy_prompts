I am serving as a provisional legislative auditor for a transitional administration that has adopted an explicit hybrid charter attempting to reconcile source-based legal validity with the internal morality of law. In this role I must evaluate draft administrative decrees—roughly three per week—before they are published, deciding within about twenty minutes whether each decree possesses legal force under the charter. The stakes are high: an erroneous clearance allows an invalid decree into force, while an erroneous block stalls necessary governance, and my supervisor, a senior civil servant without philosophical training, must sign off on my recommendation based solely on a one-page checklist I provide. I need you to turn the conceptual tension between Hart’s rule of recognition and Fuller’s eight canons of legality into a reusable, step-by-step decision procedure—something like an ordered flowchart with explicit tests—that I can apply repeatedly without returning to the theoretical literature.

The procedure must preserve the genuinely distinct work each theory does: the source-based inquiry into whether the decree was enacted by an authority recognized by the current rule of recognition, and the formal-moral inquiry into whether it satisfies conditions such as promulgation, clarity, non-contradiction, and feasibility of compliance. Crucially, it needs a dedicated conflict-resolution branch for cases where the two inquiries diverge—particularly where a decree is perfectly authorized by the recognized institutional sources yet violates one or more of Fuller’s internal principles, or where it satisfies Fuller’s formal criteria but issues from an authority that the transitional charter’s rule of recognition excludes (for example, a ministerial office abolished by the prior regime but still exercising de facto control). Mark explicitly which steps are mechanical verifications and which remain irreducible judgment calls, and be honest about where the procedure is likely to misfire or produce answers that feel wrong. Include worked applications to three hypothetical decrees: a routine technical regulation that both theories validate; a retroactive tax assessment issued through correct procedural channels; and a secret, contradictory directive that is formally promulgated but internally incoherent. In at least one case, the procedure should yield a verdict that a reasonable auditor would find counterintuitive, and you should say what that reveals about the limits of the framework. Finally, state clearly what this operationalized procedure cannot do—whether it can resolve deep disagreements about the charter’s own legitimacy, whether it addresses the moral content of the decree beyond its formal legality, and whether it remains stable if the transitional administration’s rule of recognition itself is in flux.

To keep the task practical rather than academic, do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not ask me to consult specific texts I do not have; and avoid framing the answer as an abstract comparison of the two philosophers. Instead, deliver the checklist in language suitable for a non-philosopher signatory, with enough conceptual fidelity that the distinction between source-based and internal-moral validity remains intact.
