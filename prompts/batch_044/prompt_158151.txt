I've been working through Bradley's regress on my own after encountering it in older metaphysics texts, and I've noticed a strange deadlock in the discussions I find in study groups and online forums. There are two camps that both accept the formal structure of the argument—if a relation R connects terms a and b, then some further relation R' seems needed to connect R to a and R to b, generating an infinite regress—but they draw opposite conclusions and haven't moved in years. One side, which I call the "stitching" view, treats the regress as a problem to be solved by discovering a special kind of tie—perhaps an internal relation, a primitive binding, or a non-relational glue—that stops the chain without dissolving the terms into one another. The other side, the "tapestry" view, takes Bradley to show that any attempt to stitch separate entities together is doomed, and that genuine unity requires abandoning the idea that relations are external connectors; on this view, "relata plus relation" is the fundamental mistake, and relations are already internal to an undivided reality. Both sides agree the regress is vicious if unchecked, both reject arbitrary stopping points, and both talk about the unity of a proposition—how a subject and predicate form one thought—but they never genuinely engage each other: the stitchers accuse the tapestry view of collapsing distinction into indistinguishability, while the tapestry side accuses the stitchers of smuggling in the very separation they claim to overcome.
