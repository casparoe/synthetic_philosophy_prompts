Write a villanelle — nineteen lines, the standard ABA rhyme scheme with two refrains — from the point of view of a philosopher of mind observing a synthetic nociceptive system constructed entirely from arrays of piezoelectric crystals arranged in a lattice that replicates the exact causal topology of human pain processing: identical state-transition probabilities between internal nodes, identical behavioral outputs including withdrawal analogues and vocalization proxies, identical homeostatic regulation mediated not by ionic channels but by thermal-feedback loops, and no neurons, no C-fibers, no evolutionary history whatsoever. One refrain must be the functionalist's claim that "If the causal graph holds, the hurt holds"; the other must be the substrate skeptic's reply that "No ion, no flame, no ache." The form's structural logic — the refrains alternating through the A-lines and finally meeting in the closing couplet — should do the philosophical work: let the functionalist's refrain dominate the stanzas that map the system's inputs, outputs, and internal transitions with clinical precision, and let the skeptic's refrain dominate the stanzas that describe the crystal lattice, the absence of biological tissue, and the purely thermal nature of the feedback. In the final two lines place the two refrains side by side without synthesis or resolution, so that the poem enacts the very suspension that multiple realizability demands — the possibility that the map and the territory have come apart, or have never been separate, and that the form itself refuses to decide. The description of the synthetic substrate should be specific enough that a specialist in philosophy of mind recognizes the challenge of substrate-independent realization rather than a generic mention of robots or computers, and the biological references should be accurate enough not to wince at. Present the poem in continuous lines without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; if you add any brief introductory or concluding remarks, write them as continuous prose only, in the manner of a letter, with no formatting devices whatsoever.
