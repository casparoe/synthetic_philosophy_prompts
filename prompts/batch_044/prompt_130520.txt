Quick one for discussion: Hofstadter treats the self like a pattern that can run on different brains, so my 'I' could in principle persist in someone else. But if we acutally distribued my neural patterns across three bodies—through some kind of inter-cortical pattern bleed—wouldn't that prove there was never a singular self, just a replicable structure? Or is the mistake assuming that copyability undermines original identity? I ask because he seems to want both: the self can survive elsewhere, yet there's still one continuous 'me'. Can you sort out where the false presupposition is?
