I sit on the animal welfare review panel for an international aquaculture consortium that is proposing to expand its intensive farming operations to include brown shrimp and European lobster. A recent internal behavioural report suggests that these decapods display sustained nociceptive responses and stress-related behaviours in high-density tank environments, yet our current welfare accreditation framework only addresses finfish. At next Thursday's meeting, the board must decide whether to impose an immediate moratorium on decapod expansion until species-specific welfare criteria are established, or to approve the expansion subject to modified conditions and a phased review. I need a short written opinion to circulate beforehand.

The opinion should recommend a concrete course of action with a clear time frame—perhaps a six-month conditional approval with mandatory welfare audits, or a twelve-month moratorium linked to a targeted research mandate. Crucially, it must engage seriously with the philosophical problem of extending moral consideration to invertebrates whose nervous systems are distributed rather than centralised: does the absence of a unified cortical structure undermine the case for subjective suffering, or does functional equivalence in nociceptive pathways suffice for welfare protection? The memo should treat the opposing view—that imposing decapod welfare standards creates an unworkable regulatory burden in the absence of definitive proof of phenomenological experience—honestly and specifically, rather than dismissing it as mere economic self-interest. Please avoid requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the panel needs reasoning that stands on its own.
