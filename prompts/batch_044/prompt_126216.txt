Frank Ramsey died in 1930, leaving behind a philosophy of belief as measurable partial conviction, a redundancy theory of truth, and a broadly rationalist, pragmatist temperament that treated logical and ethical questions as continuous with practical reasoning. Imagine he were alive today and confronted with a line of argument—now current in some longtermist and AI-ethics discussions—that human lives possess meaning primarily in an instrumental, deferral mode: we matter chiefly as the necessary causal origin of a far future in which artificial moral agents realize the overwhelming share of cosmic value, so that the significance of present human existence is almost entirely borrowed from entities that do not yet exist and that may lack consciousness altogether. This is not the familiar non-identity problem, but a distinct configuration in which nobody is made worse off, yet the present is emptied of intrinsic significance and turned into a mere precursor stage. Given Ramsey’s actual commitments—his subjectivist account of belief, his view that asserting truth is redundant with asserting the fact itself, and his insistence that reasoning must connect to action—how do you think he would assess this “instrumental deferral” framing of existential meaning? Would he reject it on the grounds that meaning, like belief, must be anchored in present dispositions and verifiable practical differences, or might he accept that rational agents can coherently structure their lives around distant, probabilistic goods? In your answer, reconstruct the specific tension between his pragmatism and this modern deferral argument, and say where his own philosophy is silent or ambiguous. Please also say how confident you are in what you claim about Ramsey’s likely position, and identify the single point on which you are least certain.
