Then apply just war theory's principles of discrimination and proportionality to the actual use: given that the weapons were deliberately targeted at cities with substantial non-combatant populations, does the theory classify the acts as violations of jus in bello, and can any version of the proportionality calculus—one weighing direct harm against strategic goods—accommodate them?

Here inject the niche conceptual problem: standard debates invoke Michael Walzer's notion of supreme emergency to excuse non-combatant harm when the survival of a political community is at stake, but this episode raises a prior question about whether radical technological asymmetry collapses the combatant/non-combatant distinction for the aggressor state in a way that either renders discrimination moot and therefore useless as a constraint, or generates a new category—call it systemic combatancy—that just war theory has no resources to evaluate. Examine both possibilities. Next, address what I will call counterfactual proportionality: the moral defense of the bombings depends on comparing actual deaths, approximately 200,000 by the end of 1945, against hypothetical invasion casualties, yet just war theory traditionally evaluates acts by their foreseeable effects given available information, not by ex post comparisons to unrealized alternatives. Does the theory require decision-makers to weigh epistemically uncertain counterfactuals as part of the proportionality calculus, or does it restrict evaluation to the actual choice-set? If the former, how does the theory avoid collapsing into a form of consequentialism that renders discrimination redundant; if the latter, can the bombings be justified without appealing to the invasion counterfactual?

Finally, state what the episode reveals about just war theory itself: is it a case that exposes a missing category—perhaps a technologically mediated supreme emergency, or a form of counterfactual justification—or does it show that the theory's core distinctions remain intact and that the bombings were straightforward violations?
