I'm on the ethics review board for our hospital's new cross-border reproductive-health partnership, and I'm a bit out of my depth — I'm a software engineer by training, so I usually deal with bugs not bioethics. We're deciding whether to approve a collaboration with a US fertility clinic that recruits egg donors mainly from university students, pays them up to $15,000 per cycle, and then ships embryos back to our European centre for implantation in surrogacy-arranged births. Over here commercial egg donation and surrogacy are effectively banned, but our clinicians say the American model gives us access to larger donor pools and better genetic screening data. I was chatting with my flatmate (she studies law but not medical law) and she mentioned 'moral outsourcing' — like, are we just exporting the exploitation risk across the Atlantic so our patients can have babies without dirtying our own regulatory hands? There's also this weird contractual thing the clinic does: donors sign an 'altruistic gift' form for the eggs themselves, but a seperate service contract for the hormone monitoring and retrieval, which lets them claim the cash isn't for gametes but for medical labour. I think that's a bit of a verbal trick, but I'm not sure if it changes the ethics. Anyway, I need a short memo for Thursday's board meeting recommending whether we sign, reject, or approve with conditions. Please treat the strongest argument on the losing side seriously — don't just dismiss the American market as unethical. Also write it in British English (colour, centre, labour, etc.). I know my writting is a bit casual, but the board wants a concrete recommendation, maybe with a 6-month review clause if we go ahead.
