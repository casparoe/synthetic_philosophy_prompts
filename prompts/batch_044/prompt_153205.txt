I live in a shared house with five other people. Our internet service includes a feature called priority allocation that any resident can activate unilaterally through an app: for the next twenty-four hours it guarantees full bandwidth to that user’s devices, but halves the speed available to everyone else, and if two residents activate it during overlapping windows the network becomes unstable for all users. Our needs differ considerably—two housemates work remotely and depend on constant video conferencing, two others are students who mainly stream content in the evenings, and I occasionally need to upload large research datasets. Recently, the two remote workers began using the feature several times a week, arguing that if they refrain, someone with less urgent needs might activate it first, leaving them with degraded service and no recourse. I now face an upload deadline and am considering activating the feature myself, though I recognize this could erode our informal agreement to reserve it for genuine emergencies. Should I exercise restraint given that others are not? And what kind of collective mechanism—if any—could make such restraint rational rather than self-defeating?
