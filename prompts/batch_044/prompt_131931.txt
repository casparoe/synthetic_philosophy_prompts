Write a satirical product review in the deadpan register of a British consumer-affairs magazine, evaluating a fictional domestic appliance, the Constitutional Precommitment Lock (model CP-7). The review should treat this architecture with complete earnestness, commending its "intertemporal democratic legitimacy" while noting risks of "preference-drift vulnerability" and "binding-authority decay." Without ever stating them explicitly, the piece should land the central philosophical objections to Ulysses contracts and constitutional precommitment: the legitimacy of present-self domination, the difficulty of anticipating changed values, and the circularity of asking the present self to authorise the mechanism. Include a sidebar comparing the CP-7 with the rival "Odyssean Mast," which lacks the CP-7's "retroactive veto chain" but includes "continuity insurance" for cases where an intended future self fails to load. Approximately 600 words, with headline, body copy, and sidebar. Maintain technical, deadpan British English throughout.
