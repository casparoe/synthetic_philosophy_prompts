ok so i just moved to rotterdam for work and suddenly i'm obsessed with how people talk here vs back home. want a consistency check before i post this on my blog. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) white lies are wrong, full stop — they violate autonomy by getting someone to hold a false belief without consent.

(2) but japanese indirect refusal ("chotto..." or calling something "a bit dificult" when you mean no) isn't wrong, even though it looks like the same move.

(3) my defense is that in high-context settings the "lie" is transparent — nobody actually forms a false belief, so there's no belief-manipulation. wrongness tracks successful deception, not surface ambiguity.

(4) meanwhile i've come to love dutch directness. being blunt feels more respectful because you treat people as able to handle reality without padding.

(5) yet if directness is more respectful everywhere, then japanese indirectness should be less respectful everywhere — but i said it's okay in tokyo. so either culture changes the moral calculus, or i'm cheating.

(6) and i don't think communication ethics should be culturally relative like that. if something violates autonomy, it violates it regardless of local convention.

so do (3) and (6) actually clash, or does (3) save (2) without needing culture? is my praise of dutch directness in (4) committing me to universal blunteness, or can i keep it local? rank the tensions by severity, don't just list them — take a definite stand on which commitment is least costly to drop and why.
