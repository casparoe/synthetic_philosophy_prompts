I'm giving the opening remarks tomorrow at our city's pilot workshop. Half the room are philosophers and welfare economists who've argued for years whether equality should be of resources, welfare, or capabilities — Sen, Dworkin, the usual suspects — and the other half are social workers and planners who actually have to assign benefits next monday. Our pilot is adminstratively weird: instead of cash, we issue 'capability vouchers' redeemable only for childcare, job training, or health access. I want a ten-minute talk that uses the equality-of-what debate to show why this design isn't just bureaucratic but philosophically loaded. The idea should shape what can and can't be said: if capabilities are the right metric, what does that commit us to about people who don't use their vouchers? If resources are right, what are we failing to equalize? Don't resolve it with false harmony. Make it land for practitioners without insulting the theorists. Keep it honest.
