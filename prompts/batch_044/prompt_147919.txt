Read Kafka's The Metamorphosis through the lens of sentience as gradations — moral status running from insects up through humans and maybe all the way down to thermostats, not a sharp on/off switch. Gregor wakes up with a human mind in a bug body, and thier family slowly shifts from caring for him to treating him like a broken appliance they need to dispose of. Is Kafka dramatizing the problem with gradualism? Like, if moral status really tracks cognitive capacities, Gregor should stay near the top since his inner life hasnt changed, but his family acts like hes slid down toward pest-level or even object-level. Im especially interested in the apple-throwing scene and the cleaning womans final dismissal of his body — do those moments map specifc points on the spectrum where he crosses from damaged person to unpleasant object? And what about the fact that we never really know whats in Gregors head? Does that ambiguity undercut any philosophical reading that tries to place him precisely on the scale? I want a reading that takes both Kafkas weirdness and the philosophy seriously, and that says whether the story is actually doing work on the thermostat-to-insect problem or just using a gross body to talk about alienation. If the text resists the gradation framing, say where.
