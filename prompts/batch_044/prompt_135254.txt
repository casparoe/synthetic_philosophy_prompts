Here is roughly where I stand: (1) I accept Zahavi's handicap principle — honest biological signals must impose costs that are differentially heavier on low-quality individuals; (2) I extend this to culture, treating practices like potlatch, religious asceticism, and peer review as institutional handicaps that solve credibility problems; (3) I think there is a distinct "epistemic handicap" in intellectual life, where holding a counter-intuitive philosophical position signals commitment because the social and cognitive costs fall more heavily on those who do not genuinely hold the view; (4) I deny that every costly cultural trait is a signal — many are functional spandrels or by-products with no communicative function; (5) I hold that in modern economies physical risk has largely been replaced by attention scarcity and opportunity cost, yet I still analyse luxury branding and conspicuous consumption as handicap signals, even though money is borrowable and therefore not differentially costly; (6) I maintain that if a signalling cost can be faked, insured, or otherwise decoupled from the underlying quality, the handicap logic collapses.

Please audit these commitments: which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict and dissolve on inspection, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Rank the conflicts by severity, say exactly what would have to give, and tell me whether the package can be saved without abandoning the core claim that differential cost is essential.
