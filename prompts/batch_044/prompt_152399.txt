Write a short story exploring the philosophical distinction between knowing how and knowing that—specifically in a scenario where propositional knowledge can be fully transmitted through documentation, but embodied skill requires continuous physical attunement that decays without practice. Set the narrative among deep-sea repair divers maintaining an obsolete analog navigation lattice in a near-future setting. The protagonist should possess complete manual knowledge (knowing that) of pressure differentials and current patterns, but have lost the intuitive bodily feel (knowing how) after years away, and must now guide a novice through an emergency where the documentation fails. Let the conceptual tension emerge through action and consequence rather than explicit philosophical commentary. Keep the story concise and focused. End with a single sentence summarizing the story's central insight about the relationship between these two forms of knowledge, placed on its own line.
