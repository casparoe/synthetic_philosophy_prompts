I serve on the Emergency Resource Allocation Board for our regional hospital consortium, and we must vote next Tuesday on whether to certify a new catastrophic-surge triage protocol. The protocol’s designers have embedded an explicit tie-breaking rule for cases where two patient cohorts present identical clinical priority scores: selection is randomized, and the technical documentation calls this an “acknowledged genuine moral dilemma,” arguing that when all relevant moral considerations are symmetrically balanced, no resolution is correct and suppressing that fact behind a spurious principle is ethically dishonest. A minority of board members contends that labeling the tie “arbitrary” is not a metaphysical insight but a design failure—that any apparent symmetry dissolves with finer-grained distinctions in prognosis, social vulnerability, or procedural fairness—and that our charter, which prohibits certifying any protocol that “institutionalizes an unavoidable moral wrong,” therefore bars adoption. I need a concise written opinion—no more than three pages—to circulate before Tuesday’s meeting. Recommend a concrete course of action (certify, reject, or certify with binding conditions) together with an implementation timeline if adopted. Treat the designers’ claim that genuine dilemmas exist seriously rather than reducing it to conceptual confusion; specifically address whether a decision-making system can legitimately encode a choice that its own architects regard as lacking a correct answer. Give an honest account of the strongest consideration on the side you reject, including whether rejecting the protocol actually resolves the underlying moral tension or merely relocates it. Please end with your recommendation in fixed form: \answer{...} enclosing ADOPT, REJECT, or ADOPT WITH CONDITIONS followed by the core reason in twelve words or fewer.
