I'm in philosophy grad school and was arguing with my roommate (she's in econ) about whether the centipede paradox is just a toy problem or actually eats real-world cooperation. We cooked up this extension and now I'm stuck because it seems to prove rational people can't maintain any long-term relationship, but I can't find the leak.

(1) In the standard centipede game, backward induction forces immediate take because each player knows the other is rational and will take at the prior node.

(2) The logic doesn't depend on money payoffs; it only needs a sequntial structure where continuing is jointly better but every node offers a local advantage to stopping.

(3) So apply it to friendship or collaborative work: cooperation is a chain of moves—showing up, keeping confidences, contributing labor—and at every step there's a local incentive to defect (save effort, avoid risk, free-ride).

(4) If both parties are rational and this is common knowledge, backward induction runs all the way back: each knows the other will eventually defect, so rationality demands defecting immediately—never entering the relationship, or abandoning it at the first chance.

(5) But clearly rational people do have friends and long projects that survive local temptations.

(6) You might object that friendship lacks the perfect information about payoffs the game requires, but we can always model the approximate payoffs well enough for the induction to bite.

(7) Or you might say friendship isn't competitive in the right way, but that's just redscribing the payoffs; the structural feature—common knowledge plus sequential local gain—remains.

So either backward induction fails everywhere outside the lab, or rational agents are incapable of sustained cooperation. I really want to keep (3), since real cooperation does seem to have that local-defection structure, and I'd hate to lose (1) because the induction is mathematically tight. Is the error in (4)—maybe backward induction needs a definite endpoint that friendship lacks? Or is (6) wrong in a deeper way, like common knowledge of rationality actually breaking down in intimate contexts? Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me?
