hey so im drafting rough notes for an essay on satisficing and wanted some outside eyes. usually we treat bounded rationality like its just people being cognitively cheap — simons idea that you stop searching once you hit an aspiration level. but i think theres an institutional version that gets ignored, where orgs dont just lower the bar but actively suppress the regret signal that would push the bar up. im calling it structural aspiration supression (bad name probably).

the notes sketch three mechanisms:

1. metric substitution — swap a hard goal (actual wellbeing) for an easy proxy (throughput). you satisfice on the proxy, and because the proxy is measurable the aspiration level feels justified.

2. temporal truncation — shrink evaluation windows so long-run failures never feed back. its satisficing with amnesia; the org never learns its bar was too low.

3. social proof normalization — benchmark against peers who are also truncating and substituting. if everyones clearing a fake bar, nobody feels the gap.

then i try to argue this isnt just optimization-under-constraints. in standard bounded rationality you know a better option exists but cant compute it. in structural supression the gap is engineered away — you genuinely dont experience absence.

could you comment on whether this framing is actually distinct from organizational inertia, whether mechanism 2 sounds like it has an established name im missing (not asking for citations, just whether the idea sounds familiar), and whether the philosophical distinction between 'cant compute best' vs 'dont feel the absence' is interesting or just mood? also flag anything incoherent or boring — these are just messy notes, so be honest.
