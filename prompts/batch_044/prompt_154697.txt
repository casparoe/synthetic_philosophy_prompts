I serve as the student representative on my school's Archives and Institutional Memory Committee, which must approve the digitization and cataloging protocol for disciplinary and administrative records spanning 1960 to 2005. Before us is a proposal—submitted by the student history collective—to adopt a practice they call "negative cataloging": whenever records for a particular student demographic are systematically absent, incomplete, or redacted, the digital archive should include an explicit metadata entry describing the probable structural cause of that absence (for instance, that records were never created for certain groups, selectively destroyed, or stored in a separate filing system that was lost) rather than allowing the gap to remain invisible. The administration's archivist opposes this, arguing that assigning probable causes to missing documents exceeds available evidence and risks presenting speculative historical claims as institutional fact; she recommends cataloging only extant materials with a single general methodological note on historical bias. Our committee must issue a formal recommendation by next Friday—either approve the negative-cataloging protocol with specific evidentiary safeguards, reject it in favor of minimal cataloging, or approve a modified hybrid version. Please draft the memo I will circulate to the committee. It should state a clear recommendation with a concrete implementation timeline, take seriously the collective's argument that invisible archival silences reproduce the very power dynamics that produced them, and address with intellectual honesty the strongest consideration on the opposing side—namely, that structured absence entries can transform epistemic humility into fabricated certainty.
