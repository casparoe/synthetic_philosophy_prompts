Design a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on weakness of will (akrasia) and hyperbolic discounting by concrete diagnostic commitments rather than by label recognition. The audience is advanced undergraduates in philosophy or behavioral economics who may not have read the primary literature, and the tree must contain exactly seven terminal leaves corresponding to the following views: (1) the Socratic denial that genuine akrasia is possible; (2) an Aristotelian account on which akrasia is a failure in the practical syllogism despite retention of the correct universal premise; (3) Davidson’s view of incontinent action as acting against one’s own all-things-considered best judgment; (4) a hyperbolic-discounting reductionist view that weakness of will is nothing over and above present-biased intertemporal choice; (5) a planner-doer dual-self model that explains reversal by conflict between long-run and short-run evaluative systems; (6) a structural-rationality view that treats time-inconsistent reversal as rational given legitimately shifting evaluative perspectives; and (7) an unexplored or hybrid position you must explicitly flag as unoccupied if the logical space permits it. Each internal node should present one concrete scenario or binary claim—phrased without technical jargon—on which these positions genuinely come apart. The first branching must ask whether weakness of will can exist at all; the second must separate explanations that depend essentially on temporal proximity from those that invoke a failure of practical reasoning that could occur even with identical time horizons; the third must ask whether accommodating preference reversal requires positing a split within the agent or can be handled by a unified decision-maker with non-constant preferences. Crucially, inject the following niche diagnostic case: an agent displays a locally reversed discount curve inside a narrow temporal window (choosing the smaller-sooner reward only when both options are within one hour, but consistently choosing larger-later rewards at every wider separation), yet never violates her globally endorsed long-term plan. Ask whether this local reversal counts as weakness of will, and use the answers to separate hyperbolic-reductionist accounts from judgment-failure accounts. Wherever the combinatorics of answers yield a cell with no corresponding named position, note the empty combination and briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Present the deliverable in logical-space order—questions and branching conditions first, then the mapping of answer patterns onto the seven positions, with unoccupied cells flagged—rather than as a catalog of named theories. The instrument must include no bibliographic references, no requests for citations or reading lists, and no questions that require recognizing specific authors or texts by name.
