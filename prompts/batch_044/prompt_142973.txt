Is there a name for this pattern i keep seeing in equality debates? Someone starts arguing we should distribute resources equally -- cash, goods, whatever -- and then when you point out people convert that stuff into wellbeing differently (expensive meds, mobility needs, etc.), they pivot to "actually equality is about capabilities or welfare, not just resources." But then when you ask what policy they actually want, it's back to the flat cash transfer, like the capability point was just a rhetorical shield. It's not quite equivocation because both metrics seem sincerely held; it's more like using the richer metric to absorb objections while the thinner metric does all the practical work. Is there an established concept for this exact move, and if not can you name and define it in a way that separates it from plain inconsistency or from the standard resources-vs-capabilities debate?
