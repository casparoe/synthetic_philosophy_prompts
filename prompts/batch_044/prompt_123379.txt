The implicit rule seems to be that only harms leaving a physical or economic trace count as real injuries the state owes for; moral injury gets treated as a subjective overlay that victims recieve counseling for rather than compensation. So what view of moral reality does that funding split presuppose? Or could an anti-realist license the same split through inter-subjective agreement being easier for bodily stuff? And is it even consistent, given these same bodies invoke human dignity language in their charters? Could you reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely fits well with it, and tell me what would have to change in the reparations rules if we dropped that locality idea?
