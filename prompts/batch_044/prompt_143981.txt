I have been following debates about whether extreme suffering can be weighed against pleasure at all—the view that there is a lexical boundary below which no accumulation of goods can compensate. In these discussions I keep noticing a particular maneuver, and I am unsure whether it has been named.

The pattern runs like this. A proponent begins with an intuitive, extreme case—say, prolonged torture or catastrophic neurological damage—and insists that this kind of harm belongs to a category that is not merely worse but incommensurable with pleasure: no quantity of the latter can outweigh it. Then critics or interlocutors point to intermediate conditions: not acute agony, but decades of what one might call structural stagnation, or a life shaped by the persistent absence of meaningful agency, or a cumulative dull ache of unrealized potential. Rather than placing these on a gradient and admitting that they might be outweighed by substantial goods, the proponent typically introduces a distinction—perhaps about the 'kind' of harm, or about a structural feature of the experience—that allows the intermediate case to be reclassified into the same lexically protected category. Each reclassification is locally reasonable, but the cumulative effect is that the boundary of the incommensurable region becomes unfalsifiable: any discomfort that threatens to be measurable is redescribed as belonging to the wrong kind, and the original claim of absolute non-compensability survives untouched.

This is not exactly concept creep, because the category is not gradually diluting; it is being fortified by ever-finer internal distinctions. Nor does it seem to be an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis, since the adjustments are not about conditions under which a law applies, but about which phenomena fall under an unquantifiable moral kind. I wonder: is there an established term in ethics or philosophy of science for this specific dynamic—the preservation of an absolute normative boundary through recursive taxonomic redescription of borderline cases? If not, what is the closest concept—perhaps a form of immunization strategy, or a failure of commensurability masked by classification? And where exactly should its edges be drawn: does it require an explicit lexical commitment, or can it occur whenever a theorist protects a normative absolute by subdividing the domain of experience?
