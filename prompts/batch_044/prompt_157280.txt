Let's say I buy the performative-hinge reading of Wittgenstein's On Certainty: hinges arent beliefs that happen to be immune to doubt, they're constitutive rules that make doubt possible at all, like the grammar that lets you form a question. So doubting a hinge isnt being irrational—it's like trying to play chess while denying that rooks move in straight lines; youre not wrong, you've just left the game. What would count against this? I want the serious defeaters, not just 'but people do doubt hinges.' For instance, what if child development shows hinges are aquired as beliefs and only later become untouchable? Or what if there's a formal model showing you can have a rational revision system without fixed points? Rank them by fatalness, and say whether a committed performative-hinge theorist would concede these or dismiss them. Is the view genuinely exposed, or well-insulated to the point of being unfalsifiable? And if hinges are just rules we abandon, does that erase deep irationality or just rename it? Bug or feature?
