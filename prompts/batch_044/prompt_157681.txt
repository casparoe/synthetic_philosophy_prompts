Draft the joint statement that a good-faith adversarial collaboration between the “Constitutive Narrative” camp and the “Corrective Integration” camp could sign, concerning the philosophy of national myths of lost causes. The Constitutive Narrative camp holds that myths surrounding defeats—the Confederacy’s “Lost Cause,” Masada, Kosovo Polje, the Dunkirk spirit—are not mere historical errors but structurally load-bearing for the practical identity of the groups that maintain them, such that their empirical correction risks dissolving social bonds rather than merely updating beliefs. The Corrective Integration camp holds that collective identity is robust enough to survive historical correction, that democratic legitimacy requires epistemic accountability, and that the functions served by these myths can be preserved through corrected commemorative practices.

Produce a document with two parts. First, a numbered agreed-claims section: state what both sides accept about the historical cases named above, about the psychological and political functions these narratives actually serve (e.g., sustaining dignity in defeat, providing explanatory coherence for subsequent politics), and about the empirical limits of both camps—specifically, that neither side has established a general law predicting when debunking strengthens versus fractures social cohesion. Second, a disagreements table: give the precise formulation of each residual disagreement, phrased so that each camp would accept the wording of its own position; separate disagreements that are genuinely substantive from those that are verbal.

Then identify the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I take two to be central: (i) whether “mnemonic sovereignty” (a community’s authority over its own commemorative practices) can justify maintaining propositions that are demonstrably false when those propositions are load-bearing for collective self-respect; and (ii) whether the “expressive value” of a lost-cause myth (honoring sacrifice, maintaining dignity) is logically separable from its “propositional content” (the factual and moral claims about the cause and its defeat).

For each crux, specify one concrete comparative case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side’s prediction recorded in advance. Use the contrast between Dunkirk—where strategic retreat was reframed as moral victory with relatively little resistance to correction—and the American Lost Cause, where correction has faced sustained institutional resistance, as a shared test case: state what each camp predicts about whether debunking a Dunkirk-level falsehood (were one present) would damage social cohesion relative to the American case, and why. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; do not ask the model to verify obscure historical details. Keep the formulations exact, avoid caricature, and flag any point where the disagreement reduces to a values judgment that empirical resolution cannot settle.
