I'm drafting a position paper for a moral-responsibility seminar and want a consistency check before I present. I hold the following commitments about standing to blame and hypocrisy:

(a) Standing to blame is fundamentally relational: one possesses it in virtue of having a legitimate stake in the wrongdoer's conduct or in the normative order that was violated, rather than in virtue of being morally impeccable.

(b) Hypocrisy undermines standing specifically when it takes the form of self-exemption: the blamer applies a normative standard to the wrongdoer that they refuse to apply to themselves in a relevantly similar case, and this refusal vitiates the moral authority of their blame.

(c) Acknowledging one's own comparable fault is neither necessary nor sufficient for retaining standing; what matters is only whether one's blame is genuinely non-exempting, which can obtain without explicit self-censure and can fail despite performative acknowledgment.

(d) In collective contexts, an individual member can possess standing to blame fellow members for a shared wrong, even when the collective as a whole applies stricter standards to outsiders, because individual standing tracks the member's own non-exempting relation to the norm rather than inheriting the group's overall pattern.

(e) If hypocrisy never annulled standing—if moral imperfection were always compatible with blaming—then the distinctive normative complaint "you have no right to blame me" would become unintelligible, and standing would collapse into mere authority or power; therefore there must be at least some configurations of hypocrisy that genuinely remove standing.

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are in genuine tension, and which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection? For each real tension, rank it by severity, say what the least costly revision would be, and indicate what that revision costs me elsewhere. I do not want a lecture on hypocrisy in general—just trace what each thesis pulls me toward, and do not advise me which commitment to abandon.
