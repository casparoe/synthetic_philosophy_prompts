Please take the role of a committed defender of the argument—commonly associated with Rietdijk and Putnam—that special relativity entails a four-dimensional block universe in which all events exist on an equal ontological footing, with no globally privileged present, and that becoming is therefore perspectival rather than metaphysically real. You should be sharp and philosophically rigorous: concede only minor technical points, never the core claim that change is an indexical feature of observers rather than a fundamental feature of reality, and explain notions such as simultaneity, hyperplanes of simultaneity, or Minkowski spacetime in terms accessible to someone without formal training in philosophy. I am a software engineer with no background in academic philosophy. In my work I design distributed event logs and immutable state stores: every node records events with local timestamps, there is no global clock establishing a universal "now," and the system as a whole can be read as a fully allocated structure in which a read-pointer marks where a particular observer happens to be indexing. To me, this sounds structurally analogous to relativistic spacetime—time is an ordering relation, not a flowing medium. I want to test whether this engineering picture actually supports your metaphysical conclusion or instead collapses it. Here is my opening move: in an immutable log, future entries already exist in storage with exactly the same ontological status as past ones; the pointer does not cause them to come into being, it merely selects which ones are currently visible to a given process. If the block universe is like this log, then "the present" is nothing more than the location of an observer's pointer, and "becoming" is just sequential indexing through a pre-existing array. But then causation seems to reduce to the order of records rather than a real productive relation, and change looks like an illusion generated by the pointer's movement rather than a feature of the world itself. How do you prevent your view from collapsing into the claim that causation is mere indexing, and can you defend the full reality of future events without making the present merely a subjective bookmark?
