Draft the joint statement that the Regress Camp and the Competence Camp could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the justification of deduction, taking Carroll’s dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise as the shared reference point. The Regress Camp holds that the dialogue reveals an unavoidable infinite hierarchy: applying any rule of inference requires a further rule licensing that application, so deductive justification either runs in a circle or halts at an arbitrary, unjustified stopping point. The Competence Camp holds that the regress dissolves once we distinguish using a logical rule from stating it as a premise, and that deduction is ultimately grounded in rational practical competence or in the constitutive role inference rules play in reasoning, not in derivation from higher-order premises.

Structure the deliverable as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims about the dialogue’s scenario—paraphrased without verbatim quotation—including the structure of the Tortoise’s challenge, what Achilles attempts in response, and what is genuinely at stake for theories of logical justification. Second, a disagreements table with two columns, each phrased so that the camp named in that column would accept the wording of its own view; separate substantive disagreement from any merely verbal divergence you identify, and say which part is verbal and why. Third, specify at least two cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp—including: (a) whether the use/mention distinction genuinely stops the regress or merely relocates it to an implicit meta-level; and (b) whether logical pluralism, in which alternative deductive systems are equally valid, undermines the Competence Camp’s appeal to a unique rational competence. For each crux, name one concrete case or argument both camps agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts that case shows. Keep the prompt focused, avoid any request for bibliographies or citations, and conclude by asking the response to include a brief meta-note identifying where in this joint formulation the reasoning is most vulnerable—specifically, which characterization of one camp by the other is most likely to be charged with begging the question, and what specific observation or argument would cause the drafter to revise the framing.
