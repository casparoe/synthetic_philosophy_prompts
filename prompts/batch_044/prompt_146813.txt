After a prolonged argument with a friend about whether the Kalai–Smorodinsky monotonicity axiom expresses a genuinely normative commitment or only an elegant technical convenience, write a dialogue between two equally competent bargaining theorists. One interlocutor defends the classical global monotonicity principle: if the feasible set expands in directions that do not harm either party, neither should receive a smaller payoff, with the ideal point held fixed regardless of the disagreement point’s location. The other advances a “localized monotonicity” view, arguing that when the disagreement point shifts endogenously with the scale of the feasible region—especially in non-convex environments involving indivisible goods or discrete options—the ideal point must be rescaled relative to the disagreement point rather than anchored to an absolute origin, so that global monotonicity implicitly assumes an unjustified symmetry in utility scaling. They should respond directly to each other’s specific objections: the defender should address whether fixed ideal points smuggle in an arbitrary normalization, and the critic should answer the charge that localized rescaling undermines uniqueness and renders comparative statics indeterminate. Neither should be a strawman; each should concede the force of the opposing point before pressing forward. Keep the exchange rigorous, focused on the axiomatic structure, and free of appeals to authority, bibliographies, or citation lists.
