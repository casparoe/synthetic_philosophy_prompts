I've been trying to get my head around Hacking's looping effects for a philosophy of psychiatry essay, and there's a specific knot I can't untie. The standard story is that psychiatric classifcations aren't like biological taxonomies because people react to being labelled — they change their behaviour, self-image, and even symptoms to fit the category, which then forces clinicians to revise the category. So far so good. But I'm wondering about a more granular version: what happens when a provisional diagnostic cluster — say, a working-group proposal for attenuated somatic anxiety with high functional masking — gets circulated in clinical journals before it's officially adopted? The loop seems to start running even before formal classification, because clinicians begin screening for it and patients begin narrating their distress through that lens. Is this still a looping effect in Hacking's strict sense, or does it require an official institutional label to trigger the feedback?

My flatmate says I'm overcomplicating it and that loops only happen when the label carries enough social weight to reshape identity — basically stigma or pride — so purely descriptive symptom clusters without cultural baggage shouldn't loop at all. I think that's too neat, because bureaucratic tracking categories (like disability-benefit codes) can reshape behaviour even when nobody identifies with them emotionally. But I can't quite pin down whether there's a principalled distinction between identity-forming loops and administrative loops, or if Hacking's framework just collapses them together. Could you walk through the conceptual issue here — particularly whether looping requires an interactive kind in a strong ontological sense, or whether it's enough that measurement practices and institutional incentives co-evolve with the phenomenon?
