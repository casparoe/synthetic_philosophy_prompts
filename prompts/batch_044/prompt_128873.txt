In the manuscript I am presently composing, a post-scarcity deliberative body—the Continuum Assembly—must draft a Demographic Constitution governing reproduction, population scale, and the ethical standing of persons who do not yet exist. I want the philosophical disputes within this body to be rigorous rather than ornamental, which requires knowing which unresolved problems in population axiology are genuinely upstream of constitutional design, and which are elegant distractions. To that end, I am introducing a framing I call axiological discontinuity: the condition in which successive generations possess radically incommensurable internal value structures, so that aggregating welfare across populations becomes structurally ambiguous rather than merely technically difficult. This is not a standard textbook scenario, but it is the exact pressure point in my narrative, because the Assembly must decide whether to stabilize population size, permit indefinite growth, or enforce generational replacement without being able to assume a uniform metric of the good life.

Against this background, I ask you to prioritize the open conceptual questions that would actually shape such a decision. I have in mind the status of the repugnant conclusion under heterogeneous-quality conditions; whether critical-level modifications to aggregation can be reformulated without relying on arbitrary welfare thresholds; the coherence prospects for person-affecting views that do not collapse back into totalist aggregation; the modified behavior of the mere-addition argument when added lives differ not merely in quantity but in kind; and whether longtermist frameworks for catastrophic risk implicitly depend on an unstated, settled population ethics. Rather than a survey, I want a defended agenda: rank these (or substitute others you judge more important) according to explicit criteria—upstream dependency on other debates, tractability for purely conceptual work over empirical research, and direct relevance to constitutional governance under axiological discontinuity. For each ranking, explain what conceptual breakthrough or failure in the coming years would cause you to revise the order, and be explicit about the trade-offs between tractability and importance.

I should add that the speaker delivering this analysis is an isolated philosopher-advisor with no access to archives or libraries, so the reasoning must stand on first principles and widely shared conceptual knowledge; no bibliography, reading list, or citation is required or desired. Please write from the perspective of someone advising this assembly, maintaining a formal, polished tone appropriate to the gravity of the constitutional task, and keep the focus on which lines of inquiry deserve scarce deliberative attention—not on summarizing established literature, but on carving out what genuinely remains unsettled and why it matters for the choice between totalist, critical-level, or person-affecting constitutional principles.
