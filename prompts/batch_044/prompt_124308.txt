Write a deadpan bureaucratic FAQ of approximately 600 words for a fictional government program called the "Civic Intention Stipend." Under the program, citizens receive a substantial payment if they form and verify a sincere intention to undertake an unpleasant but necessary civic task—such as a year of sewer maintenance—while being contractually released from any obligation to actually perform it. Present the document as an official form with numbered questions and answers. The satire should target the logic of Kavka's toxin puzzle: exaggerate the real philosophical difficulties of knowing one's own intentions, the paradox of being rewarded for a mental state one hopes never to actualize, and the bureaucratic absurdity of monetizing unacted-upon resolve. Invent a defending expert, Dr. Alva K. Merton, a behavioral economist quoted within the FAQ, who appeals to decision-theoretic consistency and the separation of mental commitment from physical execution. Include one entry addressing "counterfactual regret"—the anxiety that in a nearby possible world one might have followed through—and state the program's official stance on such modal uncertainty. Maintain a consistently ironic, deadpan government voice that remains legible as satire without winking. The target is the rationalist practice of treating intention and action as fully separable, not any real individual. Do not request citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, and do not depend on obscure technical literature beyond general familiarity with the toxin puzzle.
