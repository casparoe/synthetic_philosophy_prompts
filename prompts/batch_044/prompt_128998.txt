Two people in my philosphy seminar got stuck arguing about Dennett's center of narrative gravity idea. Jordan said look, Dennett literally compares the self to a center of gravity — it's just a theoretical point we calculate, not a little soul sitting in the brain. Maya said that's misreading the analogy — a center of gravity isn't fictional, it's a real emergent feature of the mass distribution. If the self is like that, then it's real, just distributed across brain processes and stories, not a ghost. Jordan came back that physical centers exist independent of anyone talking about them; you can drop the chair and it falls toward that point whether you know physics or not. Maya replied that binary stars have two centers of gravity orbiting inside one gravitational system — maybe DID is like that, two narrative centers orbiting in one brain. Jordan said that just proves there's no single self, just competing fictions. They asked me to referree and I honestly don't know who's getting the physics right or the mind part right.
