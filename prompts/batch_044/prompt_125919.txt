I am drafting a scene in a novel about an ethicist who has become preoccupied with the scale argument for insect sentience—the claim that because the number of insects alive at any moment is astronomical, even a vanishingly small credence that they are sentient produces an expected moral weight that may rival or exceed all human suffering combined. I want to include in her journal a poem that enacts this logical structure rather than merely illustrating it. Please write a villanelle: two refrains must alternate through the tercets and then converge in the final quatrain. The first refrain should articulate the multiplicative logic precisely—that expectation is the product of moral value, subjective credence, and population size, and that when the number approaches the uncountable, the expectation remains formidable even as the probability shrinks toward zero. The second refrain should articulate the opposing threshold intuition—that there exists a point below which a credence is not merely small but morally negligible, that practical reasoning collapses if we distribute concern across infinitesimal probabilities multiplied by infinite populations, and that treating such remote possibilities as binding obligations dissolves the distinction between serious moral choice and paralyzing hyperbole. These lines must be accurate enough that a philosopher working on moral uncertainty and intertheoretic comparison would recognize exactly which positions are being voiced, yet they should function as poetry, not as propositions in verse. The form itself should do the philosophical work: the refrains should return, take turns, and finally be compelled into adjacency without resolving, mirroring the way the two considerations keep passing each other. Use modern but formal diction, do not cite any philosopher or text by name, do not include bibliographic references or reading lists, and do not resolve the tension in the closing lines—leave it genuinely open, as the character herself remains undecided. If it serves the architecture, you may embed a specific hypothetical choice, such as whether to approve an agricultural pesticide that saves human crops while killing uncounted larvae, but do not declare whether the action is justified.
