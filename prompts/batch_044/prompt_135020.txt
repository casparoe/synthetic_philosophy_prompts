Following a long and rather technical argument with a close friend about whether scientific taxonomy tracks real divisions in nature, I want to formalize her commitments and see if they cohere. She affirmed that Mammalia is a natural kind, but that Reptilia in the traditional paraphyletic sense—excluding Aves—is not; that gold, individuated by atomic number 79, is a natural kind, whereas bronze is not; that the electron is a natural kind, but macroscopic temperature is not; that major depressive disorder, as defined by the symptom criteria in DSM-5, is not a natural kind; and that the ecological functional role “pollinator” is not a natural kind, even though bees and hummingbirds instantiate it. From these verdicts I can see at least two competing reconstructions: one on which natural kinds are either monophyletic evolutionary clades or entities with a fundamental microstructural essence that explains their causal powers, with everything else being either an aggregate or a convention; and another on which a category is natural just in case membership supports stable, counterfactual-supporting inductive inferences that are not artifacts of human classificatory interests. There may also be a pluralist intermediate view that accepts kinds at every autonomous level of organization except where definition involves explicit functional or normative criteria. Given these candidates, what should she say about the category of endothermic vertebrates—animals that maintain metabolic body-temperature homeostasis, spanning Mammalia and Aves but excluding other vertebrates—since this grouping is physiologically unified but phylogenetically polyphyletic? For each reconstructed rule, state the predicted verdict, identify which of her earlier judgments are genuinely ambiguous between the rules rather than decisive, and then propose one further hypothetical kind—perhaps drawn from particle physics, cognitive taxonomy, or synthetic biology—that would cleanly separate the remaining candidates. I am not looking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature; I want the reasoning made explicit and the tie-breaking case precisely described.
