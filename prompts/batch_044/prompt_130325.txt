After arguing with a friend well into the night about determinism in general relativity, I keep circling back to what looks like a basic mistake in the standard telling of Einstein's hole argument. My friend insisted that because one can apply a smooth transformation inside a matter-free region—leaving the exterior geometry fixed—and thereby generate a metric field that assigns different values to exactly the same manifold points, GR is indeterministic in precisely the quantum-mechanical sense: there is simply no fact of the matter about the interior geometry given only the exterior data. I pushed back that this sounds like it confuses a passive coordinate relabeling with an active dragging of physical fields, and that if the canonical resolution is just to identify solutions related by diffeomorphism, then we have already conceded that manifold points lack primitive identity conditions—essentially adopting point-eliminativism, or at least a thoroughgoing relationalism. But my friend countered that one could instead adopt a fiber-bundle formulation in which the metric lives as a section of an associated bundle over the base manifold, so that there is no meaningful "same point" being dragged across solutions, and that this dissolves the argument without eliminating points altogether. I am not convinced this saves substantivalism rather than merely relocating the structural commitment into the fiber structure. So I want to ask: where exactly does the false presupposition lie? Is the error in treating the hole argument as demonstrating physical indeterminacy rather than representational redundancy? Does quotienting the solution space necessarily commit one to denying that spacetime points exist, or is there a stable middle position—perhaps the view philosophers call sophisticated substantivalism—that respects point existence while denying haecceitistic identity conditions? And does the fiber-bundle reformulation actually resolve the determinism tension, or does it shift the ontological burden from the base manifold to the fiber, leaving the underlying problem untouched?
