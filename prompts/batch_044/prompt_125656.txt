I'm on the board of our neighborhood makerspace and we're arguing about this predictive safety brake we installed on the table saw last month. It stops the blade if sensors think your hand is about to get near, like 150 milliseconds before actual contact. Last saturday my friend Marco was cutting some expensive walnut for a cabinet, and the brake fired — he says he was being carefull, but the blade stopped and ruined the board, costing him about $200. Now Marco says the brake definatly caused the damage, because if it hadn't fired the cut would of finished fine. But Priya, who helps maintain the equipment, says that's the wrong way to think about it. She says the brake is wired into the sensor loop, so you can't just imagine it not firing while keeping Marco's hand position the same; if you try to "change" whether the brake triggers without changing his hand movement, you're not testing a real cause, you're breaking the system. She thinks the real cause was Marco drifting too close. I feel like there's a deeper issue here about how we decide what actually caused the ruined wood, especially since Marco wants the shop to cover the cost and Priya thinks he should pay for being sloppy. I'm not sure who's right, or whether the answer even tells us who should pay. Can you help me think through it?
