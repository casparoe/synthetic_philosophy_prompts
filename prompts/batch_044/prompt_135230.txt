Please describe the most important implications of reconceptualizing "national character" not as an essentialist trait distributed among individuals but as a property of institutional and linguistic infrastructures—call this "structural ethos"—for the moral and epistemic permissibility of generalizing about peoples in three specific contexts: predictive modeling used by international organizations, diplomatic strategy formulation, and educational curriculum design. In particular, address whether aggregating behavioral data to characterize a population avoids the standard objections to national-character talk, whether structural ethos can be meaningfully attributed without committing to innate group traits, and what ethical obligations remain when such generalizations influence resource allocation or policy even if they are statistically well-founded and explicitly non-essentialist.
