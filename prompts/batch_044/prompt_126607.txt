Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of cultural causation must meet if it claims to explain macroeconomic transformation through values, using Weber’s Protestant ethic thesis as the focal case but treating the problem generally: how values can cause economic behaviour rather than merely reflect or rationalise it. For each desideratum, provide the specific phenomenon or case it addresses and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. The list should cover: (1) temporal and causal priority of value-change over behavioural change; (2) a mechanism that translates abstract theological or ethical commitments into concrete economic practices without reducing them to material incentives; (3) preservation of the distinction between values as genuine motivational causes and values as post-hoc legitimating narratives; (4) consistency with the existence of functionally similar economic behaviours in contexts lacking the relevant values; and (5) immunity to circularity, given that economic success often reshapes the values it is said to have been caused by. Note whether (1) and (5) can be jointly satisfied, or whether they constitute an unsatisfiable subset that forces a choice between causal independence and historical stability. Identify which single desideratum eliminates the greatest number of rival theories — whether it is the mechanism requirement, the anti-circularity condition, or another — and briefly indicate how Weber’s original account, a strict materialist reduction, and a practice-theoretic synthesis (in which values and material conditions co-constitute each other through embodied habitus) each perform against the full list. Write in polished British English, and do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the evaluation should rely on conceptual reasoning alone.
