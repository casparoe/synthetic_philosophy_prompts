I'm putting together material for a philosophy discussion group I run with some rationalist-adjacent friends — we're trying to move past the usual kidneys-yes-or-no deadlock and actually map where people diverge on commodification. I want a taxonomy, basically a decision tree with branching questions, that sorts positions on repugnant markets by concrete answers rather than by the labels people wear.

The tree should have maybe five or six nodes. Start with: is there any voluntary exchange that should be categorically banned even if both parties are fully informed, uncoerced, and there's no direct harm to third parties? If no, that's one leaf. If yes, the next split should be about the mechanism: does the price corrupt the intrinsic meaning of what's traded (so vote-selling makes democracy not democracy, not just risky), does it replace a need-based or merit-based selection rule with a wealth-based one (kidney sales, university admission spots), or does it erode a background social norm we rely on even in adjacent non-market domains? Then split on remedy: categorical ban, regulated non-profit exchange with price caps, or disclosure-only with informed consent?

Each leaf needs a name — maybe "pure commodification view," "corruption-of-meaning view," "selection-effect institutionalist," "solidarity-spillover view," "regulated-market pragmatist," etc. — and exactly one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I also want you to map the logical space, not just list existing camps: note any combo the branches allow but nobody occupies, and say whether it's incoherent, just politically toxic, or merely unexplored. And flag any pair your tree genuinely can't seperate — I think corruption and spillover might both block vote-selling, so use a specifc case like price-gouging bottled water after a hurricane to pull them apart, or admit the tree fails there. Phrase every node as a scenario question — kidney sales to strangers, vote auctions, disaster pricing — so someone can answer without knowing jargon. Don't ask for a bibliography or reading list, half my group runs local LLMs that can't browse, and please don't just recycle the standard Sandel/Roth framing; inject one fresh distinction, like whether the harm stays inside the market domain or leaks into neighboring practices that were never for sale. Aim for 6-8 leaves total, casual but precise.
