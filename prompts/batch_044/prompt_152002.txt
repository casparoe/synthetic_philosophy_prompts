Write an essay on the apparent tension between Rudolf Carnap's logical-empiricist meta-ethics and his advocacy for rational social planning. Carnap maintained that normative and value judgments lack cognitive content and function as expressions of attitude or practical decision; yet he also argued that scientific reasoning should guide large-scale political and economic organization. Some interpreters treat this as a straightforward contradiction—if values cannot be rationally adjudicated, then any planner who selects social ends is stepping outside the bounds of rational discourse—while others deny that there is any conflict.

Weigh both interpretations with care. On the contradiction side, examine whether planning necessarily involves the rational justification of ends, not merely the instrumental optimization of means, and whether Carnap's emotivism therefore strips planning of normative authority. On the reconciliation side, consider a framework-instrumentalist reading: that Carnap's distinction between internal and external questions can be extended so that the choice of social goals is treated as the adoption of a practical linguistic framework, fixed by democratic agreement or convention rather than discovered, while scientific planning operates strictly within that framework on empirical and logical questions. Evaluate whether this reading genuinely dissolves the tension, or whether it merely relocates the problem by rendering the initial framework choice arbitrary and therefore politically empty.

Reach and defend a definite conclusion—whether Carnap's position is ultimately coherent, incoherent, or coherent only at the cost of evacuating planning of substantive normative content—and justify that verdict by reference to the internal structure of his philosophy rather than by appeal to general political intuitions.
