There's this practice at a bunch of universities now — they run these "student success analytics" platforms that track library card swipes, meal-plan usage, LMS logins, gym check-ins, all of it, and feed it into an algorithm that flags students as "at risk" whenever their patterns drift from the norm. The admin says it's purely supportive: the flag just triggers a counselor email, not discipline, and they publish papers about how data-driven care is more equitable. But they never ask students if they want to be surveilled, and the raw data never leaves the office. Meanwhile the same schools still sell education as this autonomous, self-directed thing.

What philosophical commitments about power, knowledge, and subjectivity does that setup presuppose? Does it only work if you think power is mainly repressive and can be kept seperate from knowledge, or is it actually closer to Foucault's view that power produces the very subjects it claims to help? Is there a tension between the "supportive" framing and the fact that the knowledge is hoarded and opaque? And if you really bought that knowledge and power are inseperable, what would have to change about the platform — or about the university's story that it's just helping?
