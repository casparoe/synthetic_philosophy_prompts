In discussions of the color phi phenomenon—where two dots of different colors are flashed in rapid succession at nearby positions and observers report seeing a single dot that moves smoothly between the locations and changes color midway through its trajectory, apparently registering the color change before the motion is completed—philosophers of mind and vision scientists have long been divided between Orwellian and Stalinesque explanations. On the Orwellian reading, the brain initially represents the first dot with its original color and then retroactively updates that past representation once the second stimulus arrives; on the Stalinesque reading, the brain constructs a false intermediate percept of motion and color change in real time and then suppresses awareness of the fabrication. Both sides accept the psychophysical data, reject any Cartesian-theater model of consciousness, and agree that first-person reports are fallible, yet the disagreement has persisted for decades with little movement. My working hypothesis is that the stalemate is not primarily about neural mechanisms at all, but about what is being measured when we ask about the "timing of experience"—whether we mean the temporal index of the neural representation, the latency of verbal report, or the phenomenological presentness of the event—so that the two camps are answering different questions under a shared vocabulary of "revision." Is this diagnosis right, or is there a hidden empirical disagreement about predictive coding versus post-hoc editing that has been obscured by conceptual confusion? More precisely, is the dispute verbal (turning on different uses of "revision" and "experience"), substantive but about standards of evidence for first-person access rather than about brain processes, or a clash of bedrock intuitions about whether consciousness can ever be transparent to its own construction? And given your diagnosis, what—if anything—would actually break the impasse: a formal distinction that maps predictive and retroactive processes onto distinct neural signatures, an experiment that distinguishes report latency from representational timing, a reconception of the question that abandons the Orwellian/Stalinesque taxonomy altogether, or an argument that no progress should be expected and why? I want an analysis of the structure of the disagreement itself, supported by what each side actually says about color phi and temporal order, together with an honest assessment of whether the impasse signals an ill-posed question or merely a hard one.
