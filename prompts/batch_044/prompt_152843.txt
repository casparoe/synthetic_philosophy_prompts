Construct a thought experiment, suitable for discussion in a philosophy of mind seminar, that instantiates a tangled hierarchy in a near-future artificial cognitive architecture rather than in abstract consciousness theory alone. Specify a three-layer system: Layer 1 implements raw sensorimotor processing in dedicated hardware; Layer 2 maintains a predictive self-model of Layer 1; and Layer 3 models Layer 2 and, crucially, issues parameter adjustments back to Layer 1, so that Layer 1's dynamics are partly determined by the interpretation occurring at Layer 3. The loop closes when Layer 2 begins modeling Layer 3, yielding mutual dependency analogous to Escher's Drawing Hands. The scenario must be realistic—no perfect predictors, no exotic physics, confined to one paragraph—and must isolate the status of Layer 1: does it remain an inviolate level that escapes interpretation by the loop, or does the loop's closure strip even the substrate of that status?

Present the case, then deliver the required analysis. First, state the verdict of the defender of an inviolate substrate: that Layer 1 remains uninterpreted because interpretation requires a distinct interpreting level, and the loop never contains an interpreter genuinely outside itself. Second, state the verdict of the advocate of full tangling: that once Layer 1's physical states are systematically mapped and modified by Layer 3, they acquire representational content within the loop and therefore lose their inviolate character. For each, identify the reasoning step that depends specifically on the bidirectional dependency between Layer 1 and Layer 3 mediated through Layer 2. Next, say which structural feature of the case does the work—namely, the feedback closure that makes Layer 1 both the interpreted object and a causally responsive component of the interpreting loop—and explain whether removing that feature collapses the hierarchy into a standard linear observer-observed relation with an obvious base. Finally, indicate how a defender of the position that comes out badly would attack the case, and note the precise point at which the analogy to Escher's Drawing Hands breaks down if pushed beyond its appropriate scope. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the analysis should stand on its own conceptual merits.
