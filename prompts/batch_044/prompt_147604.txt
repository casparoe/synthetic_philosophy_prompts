Consider the following exam question and a student's answer.

Exam question: "Distinguish the counterfactual-dependence theory of causation in the Lewisian tradition from the interventionist theory developed by Pearl and Woodward. Then apply both to this case: in a redundant safety system, fail-safe A activates and prevents a failure; fail-safe B was inoperative but was restored to working order by an automated maintenance process that had no causal connection to A's activation, with both now functional at the moment of crisis. Did A's activation cause the system to remain safe? Discuss whether this is preemption, overdetermination, or redundant causation, and explain what verdict each theory delivers."

Student answer:
"The counterfactual-dependence view, as Lewis presented it, holds that A causes B only if B counterfactually depends on A—had A not occurred, B would not have occurred. To solve preemption problems, Lewis added that we must check for quasi-dependence in nearby possible worlds and that temporal priority breaks ties between candidates. The interventionist view abandons counterfactuals for structural equation models: A causes B if B is invariant across interventions that change A, holding other direct causes fixed.

In the safety-system case, we have redundant causation, not preemption, because both A and B are present and sufficient. Applying the counterfactual test: if A had not activated, B was already restored, so the system would still be safe. Therefore B breaks the counterfactual dependence of safety on A, and Lewis's theory must deny that A caused the safety—though this seems counterintuitive. Lewis might reply by appealing to temporal priority, since A activated first, but that does not restore counterfactual dependence.

The interventionist analysis models Safety as a function of A and B. Because B is present and functional, an intervention that sets A to inactive still yields Safety via B. Hence there is no invariant change in Safety under interventions on A, so the interventionist theory also denies A is a cause. This convergence is reinforced by the fact that B's repair was statistically independent of A, so in the structural model B is exogenous and A's coefficient predicting Safety is effectively zero once B is included. One might worry that interventionist theories require physical manipulability, but since A and B are mechanically accessible, that concern does not apply here."
