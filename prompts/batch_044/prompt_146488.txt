Here is an argument about downward causation in Hofstadter’s careenium that seems to collapse into an elimination of mental causation altogether, and I cannot locate the slip.

(1) In the careenium, simms are magnetic marbles that bounce and cluster into larger patterns called simmballs; a simmball is nothing over and above the particular arrangement of simms that constitute it.
(2) Suppose a stable cluster—functioning as a symbol, say one encoding a logical rule—causes a neighboring cluster to reorganize. This is downward causation: the symbol-level state makes a causal difference.
(3) But the symbol-pattern is identical to, or fully supervenes on, the underlying simm-arrangement. Every movement of every simm is fully determined by low-level forces (magnetism, collision, friction, boundary conditions).
(4) So if we hold the low-level physical state fixed, the symbol-level state cannot vary; and if the low-level dynamics already determine a given trajectory, the symbol-level description performs no additional causal work.
(5) For downward causation to be genuine rather than redundant, there must be counterfactual slack: cases where the same low-level state could have realized different symbol-patterns, or where the symbol-pattern produces an outcome that the simm-level dynamics do not already fix.
(6) But the careenium is deterministic. Given the positions, velocities, and magnetic orientations of all simms, the future is uniquely fixed. There is no slack for the symbol-level to steer outcomes independently of the substrate.
(7) Therefore, symbols in the careenium do not push the substrate around; they are merely descriptive summaries of substrate-level dynamics.
(8) Apply the same reasoning to minds. Mental states supervene on neural states; neural dynamics are physically complete; and within a given brain there is no multiple realizability that would create counterfactual slack at the mental level.
(9) So mental causation is impossible—beliefs and desires never cause actions; neurons do all the causal work, and mental talk is only an explanatory summary.
(10) This proves too much: it eliminates not only symbolic downward causation in artificial systems but all mental causation in biological ones.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? I would most like to keep (1)–(3) and (9), since supervenience and the completeness of physics seem difficult to deny. But I suspect something slips between (4) and (7), or perhaps in the move from (6) to (8)—maybe a hidden assumption that causal efficacy requires counterfactual slack at the very same level, or a conflation of explanatory redundancy with causal redundancy. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Does it force me to accept overdetermination, deny physical closure, or allow that high-level patterns can be causally efficacious precisely because they constrain the low-level dynamics in ways that are invisible from below?
