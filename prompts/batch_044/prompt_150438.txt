Write a two-page dissertation fellowship proposal on the following question: among honor cultures, dignity cultures, and victimhood cultures, is any morally superior, and can the comparison be made without circularly assuming the normative framework of one? Frame the problem not through standard sociological comparison or liberal metrics like autonomy and equality, but through an internal coherence test. Each culture distributes moral standing according to a different currency—reputation and social role for honor; inherent and universal worth for dignity; recognized harm and grievance for victimhood. Existing treatments tend to defend dignity as universalist, treat honor as archaic, or treat victimhood as a corrective; few ask whether victimhood culture contains a structural instability analogous to inflation in an economy of recognition: if moral status is conferred by being recognized as a victim, and recognition is competitive and scarce, then expanding the category of victim risks debasing the very status it grants. Meanwhile, dignity culture claims to avoid contingency, yet its institutional forms of recognition may smuggle in social dependency that its own universalism cannot acknowledge. Honor culture makes standing explicitly contingent, but may possess internal resources—such as the idea that honor must be earned within a shared normative framework—to critique both dignity's abstraction and victimhood's competitive dynamics. State the precise question: can each culture generate a non-question-begging critique of the others using only its own normative resources, and is there a genuinely shared criterion—such as non-arbitrariness in the distribution of moral standing—that permits adjudication without presupposing one framework? Describe the method: conceptual analysis of each culture's internal logic, testing for coherence, examining whether victimhood culture's recognition dynamics are self-undermining or can be stabilized, and whether dignity culture can account for the moral importance of social recognition without collapsing into honor. Specify what a successful result looks like—perhaps identifying one shared criterion interpreted differently by each culture—versus failure, which might be discovering that any comparison necessarily presupposes one normative framework. Give an honest scope assessment: can this yield more than taxonomy in one year, and what safeguards prevent the project from becoming an exercise in labeling? Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or literature review; present positions and arguments conceptually, and note that references will be supplied separately.
