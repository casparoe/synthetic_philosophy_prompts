For decades, left-hand driving had been the established regularity; then the government legislated a switch and executed it overnight through massive state coordination—repainting road signs, redesigning intersections, and running a saturation publicity campaign—after which right-hand driving became the stable norm. By Lewis's criteria, was the post-1967 regime a genuine convention? The available equilibria (left versus right) were technically arbitrary, yet the selection was not the result of iterative reasoning or precedent among drivers but of top-down authority. Does Dagen H demonstrate that conventions can be manufactured if an authority solves the coordination problem exogenously, or does it reveal that Lewis conflates the evolutionary origin of a norm with its present functional structure? I'm especially interested in whether there is a coherent distinction between an "organic" convention and an "engineered" one, and whether recognizing that distinction undermines the claim that conventions are ultimately arbitrary equilibria sustained by mutual belief. Please don't summarize Lewis in general terms; map his conditions onto this specific transition, indicate exactly where the analogy holds and where it fractures, and address whether a deliberately imposed regularity can ever satisfy the conditions for being a convention in Lewis's sense.
