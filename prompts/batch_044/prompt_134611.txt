Here is an argument I constructed for a seminar on rational preference and now find suspicious because its conclusion undermines a view I want to defend, though I cannot locate the misstep. (1) The canonical Dutch-book argument demonstrates that if an agent's credences violate the probability axioms, a bookmaker can design a set of bets that guarantees a net loss; rational belief therefore requires probabilistic coherence. (2) By strict analogy, if an agent's preference ordering violates the axioms of expected utility—transitivity, continuity, and independence—a strategic adversary can construct a sequence of pairwise trades, each acceptable to the agent in isolation, that collectively deliver an outcome the agent ranks strictly below the starting point; this is the preference analogue of a Dutch book, often called a money pump. (3) Hence rational preference requires coherence with expected-utility axioms. (4) Apply this now to tranquilism: the thesis that only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic value, with the result that any two states equally free of both are precisely equal in value regardless of differences in knowledge, relationships, aesthetic richness, or conceptual depth. (5) Under that ordering, an agent must be indifferent between a state of refined contemplative awareness without suffering and one of bare sensory blankness without suffering, and similarly indifferent between embedded communal life and total isolation, so long as neither involves craving or suffering. (6) Consequently, an adversary can execute a downward trading sequence—exchanging awareness for blankness, then community for isolation, then memory for emptiness—where at each step the agent accepts because she is genuinely indifferent, yet the cumulative result is a minimal tranquil state that she initially regarded as profoundly impoverished. (7) To block the pump, she must either deny that the trades are genuinely available, which looks ad hoc, or concede that she implicitly values some non-tranquil goods positively, thereby abandoning pure tranquilism. (8) So the argument appears to show that tranquilism, when combined with standard coherence constraints on preference, licenses a self-defeating spiral to a state its own proponent should reject.

I want to preserve both the coherence constraint in (2)-(3) and the tranquilist core in (4), but I feel there is a hidden slide—perhaps an equivocation between local pairwise indifference and global preference, or an illicit assumption that all goods can be decomposed into tradeable units—somewhere between (4) and (6). Which step should I reject, and what exactly does rejecting it cost? Does it weaken the analogy with credal Dutch books, or does it force me toward a non-utilitarian, perhaps holistic, account of well-being?
