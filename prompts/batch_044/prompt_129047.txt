Please explain the conceptual issue at the heart of Bernard Williams's integrity objection to utilitarianism, but do so through a distinction that is not always drawn clearly: the difference between integrity as non-alienation from one's ground projects and integrity as the absence of a certain kind of motivational corruption, which Williams illustrates with the charge that an agent may entertain one thought too many. Clarify why these are not the same thing, and why a failure of the first kind is not necessarily a failure of the second. Then apply the distinction to the following case: a trauma surgeon whose ground project is to treat every patient before her as irreplaceable refuses, during a mass-casualty event, to switch to triage protocol even though doing so would save more lives. Is her refusal an expression of integrity — specifically, does it become a corruption of her motives if she pauses to weigh lives, or does refusing to weigh them betray her ground project altogether? In your answer, state how confident you are in the conceptual distinction you draw, and identify the specific point about the surgeon case where you are least certain.
