Write a short story set in the 1640s about an artisan who constructs a mechanical nightingale according to Descartes' doctrine that animals are complex automata—bêtes-machines—whose behaviors reduce entirely to corporeal mechanism. The narrative should center on the artisan's patron, a physician committed to Cartesian mechanism, who notices that the bird modifies its song in response to his presence in the garden. The story should dramatize the tension between explaining this adjustment through bellows, pins, and air pressure alone, and the patron's resistant intuition that something like anticipatory responsiveness is present. Avoid resolving the ambiguity; instead, render the conceptual conflict through concrete sensory detail and the patron's internal hesitation.
