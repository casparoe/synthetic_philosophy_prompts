I'm preparing a workshop on normative epistemology, and I've been wrestling with the classic Pascal's mugging scenario—the decision-theoretic puzzle in which an agent is offered an astronomically large payoff contingent on an astronomically unlikely event, apparently justifying any finite cost to secure it. Lately I've been thinking about what I call "distributed epistemic mugging": rather than facing a single explicit wager, an agent is embedded in an information ecosystem where dozens of independent sources each advance distinct low-probability, high-stakes claims—catastrophic risk forecasts, eschatological predictions, salvation narratives—that collectively exert pressure on belief revision and resource allocation, with no single interlocutor accountable for the aggregate burden. Before I evaluate specific responses to either the classic case or this distributed variant, I want the full specification. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of rational belief and action under mugging conditions must satisfy. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or consideration behind it: which scenarios it must get right (the explicit single-shot wager, the cumulative micro-claim environment, manufactured uncertainty), which distinctions it must preserve (between evidential warrant and pragmatic value, between individual belief costs and collective allocation costs, between rejecting a claim and discounting its stakes), and which established results or constraints it must remain consistent with (resource-bounded rationality, the unavoidability of prior probabilities, the social costs of widespread false positives). Flag which desiderata are non-negotiable constraints on theoretical adequacy and which reflect the preferences of particular camps—say, strict evidentialists versus pragmatists versus decision-theoretic consequentialists. Then say whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable: for instance, whether demanding both complete immunity to low-probability high-stakes manipulation and full practical responsiveness to genuine existential risks generates a contradiction, and if so, what that impossibility result implies for the project. Finally, briefly indicate how a Bayesian threshold view, a robust evidentialist rejection, and a pragmatic decision-theoretic framework each fare against the list, but keep the weight of your answer on the criteria and their interrelations rather than on the scorecard.
