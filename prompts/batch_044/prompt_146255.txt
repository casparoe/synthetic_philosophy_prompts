The committee is split between folks who want a naturalist definition — basically Boorse-style statistical deviation from species-typical biological function — and folks who want a normativist one, like Nordenfelt's idea that disease is failure to achieve vital goals given standard circumstances. We need a reusable procedure that works in a 90-minute meeting with no philosophy PhDs present, and I want it to be honest about where it breaks.

Write it as a step-by-step flow: first check biological dysfunction with concrete criteria, then check harm/vital-goals impairment, then specify exactly where the two conflict and what tie-breaker applies. Give worked examples for three cases — one clear naturalist win, one clear normativist win, and one borderline clash like 'persistent social-comparison fatigue in heavy social-media users,' which is the kind of weird modern case we actually see. Include one case where the procedure gives an answer I'll probably dislike, and flag every point where we still have to punt to a subcommittee or just vote rather than hiding behind false objectivity. Also tell me what argument or finding would change your mind about which framework should dominate tie-breakers, and point out where your reasoning is most vulnerable — don't give me fake confidence.
