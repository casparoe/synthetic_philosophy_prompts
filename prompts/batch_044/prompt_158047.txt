I've been thinking about this ever since I moved back and forth between Berlin and Tokyo for work, and now I'm in this sociology course where they keep talking about 'tight' vs 'loose' cultures like its just one dial you can turn. But there's this pattern I keep seeing that doesn't fit the dial at all. In Berlin the formal rules are pretty lax - people jaywalk constantly, deadlines are more like suggestions - but there's this intense invisible pressure around how you disagree in social spaces. You can break an actual law and nobody blinks, but if you violate some unwritten rule about how to push back in a group chat, people just freeze you out. In Tokyo it's kind of flipped: tons of explicit rules about behvaior, and people navigate them with this pragmatic flexibilty that outsiders miss, except in workplace hierarchy where the informal penalties for stepping out of line feel way harsher than any formal rulebook.

So is there a name for this mismatch between where a society puts its rules on paper versus where it actually puts the social weight? It's not just formal vs informal norms, because the pattern is systematic - the loose parts are loudly loose and the tight parts are quietly hidden, almost like they're avoiding each other. I started calling it 'normative shadowing' in my notes but I have no idea if thats real. What's the closest concept, and does it mean the tight/loose framework is missing something, or just too simple? Also, why does this asymmetry always seem to cluster in specific domains like dissent or hierarchy - am I just overfitting from my two cities?
