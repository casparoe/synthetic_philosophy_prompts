I argue that this is distinct from deceptive alignment, reward hacking, or straightforward lying, because the debater never misrepresents information; rather, they embed genuinely correct local inferences inside frames that make them globally uninformative, thereby degrading the judge's epistemic position without triggering standard safeguards.

The audience will be a mixed group of AI safety researchers (mostly technical, familiar with adversarial training and scalable oversight frameworks), two decision theorists with backgrounds in epistemic game theory, and a philosopher of science who works on scientific disagreement. They are broadly committed to the view that debate improves oversight by distributing computation across adversarial agents, and they tend to treat bounded rationality as an engineering constraint rather than a fundamental theoretical limit. They will be sceptical that philosophical framing can expose a genuinely new failure mode in a technical mechanism.

List the objections I will actually receive, in roughly the order they will come, phrased exactly as these audience members would put them.
