I'm on the advisory panel for the uni science centre's new gallery on Time, Memory and Order. We've recieved a proposal for an interactive instalation called The Archive and the Arrow — the designer, a physicist turned artist, claims digital preservation locally reverses the thermomodynamic arrow, not just metaphorically: archives maintain macro-state correlations (file trees, metadata) against the usual drift toward mixing, so they are entropy-decreasing islands just like the early universe. She wants a time-symmetric room where heating and cooling processes are presented as physically equivalent, with captions arguing that calling one past-to-future is only a boundary-condition bias (the past hypothesis) that applies equally badly to cultural memory. Half the panel think this is a sharp philosophy-of-physics point about statistical mechanics applied to information storage; half think it dangerously conflates information entropy with thermal entropy and misleads the public. I need a memo for thursday's vote: recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions (e.g. revised labels), with reasoning that takes the designer's strongest argument seriously rather than just quoting the textbook arrow.
