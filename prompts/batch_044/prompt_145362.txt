Write a dialogue between Lin and Marcus, two philosophers who both take Zhuangzi seriously but read his attitude toward death in opposite ways. Lin argues he practices a kind of ontological hospitailty—he dissolves the boundary between mourner and mourned so completely that grief has no foothold, and death is just a shift in mode with no manager left to suffer it. Marcus thinks that's too tidy. He points to the drum-funeral anecdote and the "great clod" passage and says Zhuangzi is actually doing affectve dissonance: he laughs at death not because he's numb but because he's ritually recontextualizing loss, which still requires recognizing that something was lost. Lin pushes back that if you smuggle in a hidden continuity of qi just to preserve some personal trace, you've missed the point; Marcus counters that without minimal continuity Zhuangzi's own poetic laments make no sense, and asks how her "no manager" view explains why he bothers writing about death at all. Make them respond to each other's actual points, not just trade speeches. Keep it in the weeds—no generic "death is part of life" platitudes. A typo or two is totally fine.
