Here's where I've landed on multiculturalism, assimilation, and state neutrality, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present this in a seminar. I hold:

(1) French laïcité is essentially correct in treating conspicuous religious expression as belonging to the private sphere, so public institutions like schools should exclude religious symbols in order to maintain institutional neutrality.

(2) Anglo-American multicultural accommodation is also defensible: the state should permit religious dress and practice in public life—hijabs in classrooms, kirpans in government buildings—unless they directly impede institutional functioning or safety.

(3) State neutrality should be understood as neutrality of justification, not neutrality of effect: policies may affect religious and secular citizens differently, provided the reasons offered are civic and shareable rather than theological.

(4) The state should actively fund minority-language schools and cultural preservation programs, not merely tolerate them, because structural support is necessary for meaningful cultural membership.

(5) I reject assimilationist coercion that forces people to abandon core practices, but I accept assimilation as legitimate when it demands adherence to shared civic norms—particularly gender equality and unrestricted public discourse—even over religious objections.

(6) Laïcité succeeds specifically because of France's republican tradition of abstract universalism, and exporting that model to deeply plural societies with strong group-based identities would be unstable and unjust.

(7) I ground all multicultural protections in individual autonomy rather than collective rights: cultural frameworks are instrumentally valuable because they provide the meaningful options that make autonomous choice possible, not because groups possess intrinsic moral standing.

Are these jointly tenable? Go through them and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and rank the real tensions by severity. Also tell me which single revision (to one commitment) would eliminate the most tension across the set, and whether the apparent contradiction between (1) and (2) dissolves once (6) is taken seriously. Please discriminate sharply between surface inconsistencies and deep conflicts rather than flagging every pair. Answer in American English.
