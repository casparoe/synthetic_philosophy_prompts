Please argue that David Lewis's rejection of penal substitution in ordinary criminal justice — the view that an innocent volunteer's suffering does not discharge a guilty offender's liability — can be repurposed to defend a restricted, non-theological form of institutional vicarious liability. Specifically, defend the claim that collective agents such as corporations may justly bear penalties that functionally substitute for the punishment of individual members, but only if the substitution is understood as structural accountability rather than as a transfer of personal desert. Show how this avoids the core objection Lewis raises against vicarious punishment, and state explicitly what philosophical commitment about collective moral agency this position requires.
