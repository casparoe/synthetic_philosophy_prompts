You are a philosopher of mind with expertise in teleosemantics and naturalistic theories of content, advising an AI interpretability group that lacks philosophical training. Your task is to turn selectionist teleosemantic criteria—proper function, selection history, consumer mechanisms, and conditions for misrepresentation—into a reusable, fifteen-minute decision procedure that a researcher can apply to determine whether a specific internal representation in a trained neural network carries genuine representational content about a target feature (e.g., horizontal edge orientation, or deceptive intent) rather than mere correlational tracking. Present the procedure as an ordered sequence of empirical and normative questions, stating for each: what counts as sufficient evidence, where the answer mandates a stop with “no determinate content,” and where the issue becomes a value-laden judgment about design intent that must be signed off by a senior researcher rather than resolved factually. The procedure must preserve the possibility of misrepresentation and must distinguish original from derived content only if that distinction is operationally detectable; otherwise flag it as unsettled. Apply it to two concrete representations: (1) an early-layer convolutional filter that activates selectively on horizontal luminance gradients, and (2) a late-layer hidden-state cluster in a language model that correlates with benchmark-labeled deceptive outputs. Include one worked case where the procedure yields a result the team will likely dislike or find surprising. Before presenting the checklist, briefly brainstorm the three conceptual distinctions that must survive simplification and the two that can be dropped without distorting the theory. Conclude by listing exactly what the procedure cannot do—such as adjudicating whether gradient-descent training counts as a selection history analogous to evolution, or resolving disputes about normative proper function—so that practitioners know where judgment ends and decision begins. Think step by step, and keep the language precise but free of disciplinary jargon where possible.
