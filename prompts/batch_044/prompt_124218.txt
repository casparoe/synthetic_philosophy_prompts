Construct a thought experiment comparing two mechanistic anomaly detectors—Detector I, which classifies mechanisms by causal-graph isomorphism up to parameter rescaling, and Detector S, which classifies by intervention-stability profile—applied to a concrete biological-engineering pair. Design a case with two systems: System A, a natural heat-shock response pathway in E. coli, and System B, a synthetic circuit engineered to replicate the pathway but with a feedback loop replaced by an open-loop feedforward cascade that produces identical steady-state outputs but diverges under perturbation. The case must satisfy these specifications: both detectors flag System A as anomalous relative to a baseline training set; Detector I classifies B as the same mechanism (same graph, rescaled edges), while Detector S classifies B as a different mechanism (different stability landscape); the disagreement must depend on exactly one structural feature—the presence versus absence of the feedback loop—while all other nodes, edges, and parameter ranges are held fixed; the scenario must be realistic (no exotic physics or hypothetical technologies beyond current synthetic biology) and described in one paragraph. After presenting the scenario, walk through the verdict each detector delivers and why, identify which feature of the case is doing the work, describe what happens if that feature is removed (i.e., if B retains the feedback loop with only parameter changes), and explain how a defender of the detector that comes out "wrong"—the one whose classification conflicts with biologists' intuitive judgment—would attack the scenario.
