I am preparing material for an advanced seminar on moral philosophy, and I have become preoccupied with a tension I cannot resolve on my own. When historians and ethicists discuss large-scale historical evils — systematic mass killings, state-engineered famines, colonial extermination campaigns — they frequently face the question of whether these events can be ranked in severity, or whether any such ranking is not merely epistemically dubious but morally obscene. On one side, some maintain that comparative evaluation is indispensable for identifying causal patterns, allocating commemorative resources, and preventing future atrocities; on the other, critics contend that treating suffering as measurable and orderable implicitly commodifies victims and violates the dignity of remembrance.

Please write an essay that takes up this controversy directly. Your task is to determine whether ranking historical evils is conceptually viable and ethically permissible, or whether it is inherently obscene. To do this rigorously, you should distinguish between ordinal comparison — establishing that one atrocity was more severe than another without specifying by how much — and cardinal comparison — assigning quantitative metrics or aggregate scores. You should also examine whether a multi-dimensional framework, in which different evils are evaluated along incommensurable axes (scale, intentionality, institutional design, long-term structural damage) rather than collapsed into a single hierarchy, can escape the charge that comparison creates a 'market in suffering'. Weigh the principal arguments: that comparison aids moral clarity and prevention, that it risks instrumentalising victims, and that incommensurability makes any ranking arbitrary. Do not conclude with an agnostic balance; defend a definite position — for instance, that ordinal, multi-axis comparison is permissible and even obligatory, whereas cardinal aggregation is obscene, or that any hierarchical ordering is irredeemably disrespectful. Write the essay in formal British English.
