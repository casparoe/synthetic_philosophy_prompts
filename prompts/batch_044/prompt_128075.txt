As a novelist accustomed to watching readers reconstruct meanings I never intended, and to revising my own manuscripts until earlier intentions become archaeological layers, I want a stricter framework before I accept any account of how authorial intention relates to textual meaning. I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or a survey of named positions; I want the specification of adequacy itself. Write the desiderata that any theory linking authorial intention to the meaning of a literary text must satisfy, with particular attention to cases where the text is not a stable object—where it exists in variant manuscript versions, has been altered by posthumous editors, or was composed through iterative workshop feedback that explicitly supersedes earlier drafts. For each requirement, state the motivating case or distinction behind it: which interpretive puzzles it must dissolve (for example, a symbolic pattern that appears only in an editor's rearrangement of chapters, or a character's motivation that is clear in an early draft but contradicted in the final published version), which conceptual distinctions it must preserve (such as between an author's original mental state, the intention embodied at the moment of publication, and the meaning the text now supports independently of either), and which documented historical or textual constraints it must respect without contradiction. Mark which demands are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular interpretive camps (for instance, the insistence that every reader's response be preserved, or the reduction of all meaning to recoverable mental states). Identify any subset of desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable—especially if accommodating documented editorial intervention requires abandoning the unity of authorial intention, and say so explicitly. Briefly indicate, only in passing, how standard intentionalism, hypothetical intentionalism, and value-maximizing accounts stand against your list, but keep the conceptual weight on the criteria and their conflicts rather than on the scorecard.
