Does this generate a coherent form of "localized" epistemic akrasia that escapes the standard bootstrapping objection—namely, that distrusting one's reasoning undermines the very reasoning that produced P—because the defeat targets only the domain-specific mechanism? In your answer, written in American English, carefully distinguish the "level-splitting" view, which allows first-order and higher-order beliefs to diverge, from the "uniqueness" view, which rejects such divergence even locally. Finally, assess whether this localization genuinely avoids self-undermining or whether the domain-specific failure inevitably generalizes, collapsing localized akrasia back into global irrationality.
