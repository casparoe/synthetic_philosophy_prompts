I have been working through Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of minds—Darwinian creatures hard-wired by natural selection, Skinnerian creatures capable of trial-and-error reinforcement learning, Popperian creatures who can generate and test hypotheses internally before acting, and Gregorian creatures who inherit and deploy cultural tools, especially language, to expand their cognitive reach—and I am struck by how neatly it orders cognitive complexity from evolutionary inheritance to cultural scaffolding. I want to test whether Mary Shelley's Frankenstein supports that ordering or quietly undermines it, since Victor's Creature appears to be engineered as an adult human body and brain yet lacks any evolutionary or developmental history, then rapidly acquires language, reads Milton and Plutarch, and formulates elaborate revenge strategies.

Read the novel through Dennett's four categories. Which scenes show the Creature operating as a Skinnerian learner—say, his early sensory encounters with fire and food, or his observation of the De Lacey family? At what point does he become Popperian, able to imagine counterfactual futures and plan revenge without immediate environmental feedback? And is he genuinely Gregorian once he masters books and language, or does Shelley suggest that Gregorian cognition requires a social transmission that an isolated synthetic being can only parody? The taxonomy assumes a layered evolutionary and cultural inheritance; the Creature seems to skip layers entirely, acquiring advanced tools without the scaffolding that produced them. Does the novel therefore expose a hidden dependency in Dennett's account—that Gregorian minds require not just language but a lineage of teaching—or does the Creature's tragedy actually confirm it, since his cognitive powers are precisely what doom him without the social world that should accompany them? I am preparing this for a seminar on philosophy of mind and artificial cognition, and I want a reading that takes both Shelley's narrative and Dennett's framework seriously: where the taxonomy illuminates the Creature's development, where the novel resists being mapped onto it, and what the work adds to our thinking about minds that are constructed rather than evolved.
