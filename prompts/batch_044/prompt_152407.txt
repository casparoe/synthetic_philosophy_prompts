I'm writing my dissertation on moral status under uncertainty, and at a seminar last week two colleagues—let's call them A and B—fell into a prolonged disagreement about the scale argument for insect sentience. A argued that with roughly 10^19 insects alive at any given time, even a conservative prior of 0.01 for individual sentience yields an expected population of around 10^17 sentient beings, which would make insect suffering one of the largest moral problems by orders of magnitude. B replied that this expected-value aggregation collapses into a reductio: if we apply it consistently, we must also weigh low-probability sentience in plants, future artificial substrates, or even complex molecular configurations, and that A is arbitrarily privileging insects with a hidden line-drawing move. A countered that insects are not arbitrary, since they possess nociceptors and central pattern generators structurally continuous with acknowledged sentient taxa, which raises the prior well above the background noise that applies to flora or silicon; B then said that even a raised prior remains vanishingly small in absolute terms, and that A's reasoning still commits us to an implausible moral risk aversion—avoiding pesticides, road travel, and perhaps even respiration—unless A is prepared to embrace radical institutional asceticism. A responded by distinguishing intended harm from merely foreseen harm, insisting that the argument targets systemic agricultural reform rather than individual paralysis, but B pressed that if the expected suffering is genuinely at that scale, institutional reform is precisely the radical conclusion A should endorse, and that A's reluctance reveals an unacknowledged belief that the numbers are not genuinely action-guiding. Could you walk through their exchange point by point—assessing who has the stronger move at each turn, whether B's reductio genuinely undermines A's aggregation, and whether either side misses something important about how moral uncertainty should be calibrated when both probabilities and stakes are massively scaled?
