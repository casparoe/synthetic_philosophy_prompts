I want them to actually feel Quine's indeterminacy of translation—not just hear the gavagai story but live the problem that behavioral evidence underdetermines reference. Thier job is to write a translation manual that assigns referents to the key term "glex" (it shows up when rabbits appear, but also when rabbit-parts do, and also in temporal contexts). The rules should force them to commit to one of: (a) glex = rabbit, (b) glex = undetached rabbit part, (c) glex = temporal stage of a rabbit. They can only use behavioral evidence—no asking natives about ontology, no appealing to "common sense" about objects.
