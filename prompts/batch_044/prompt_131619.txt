here's the chain i have:

(1) If we want to confirm a multiverse theory without direct observation, it can't just contain our world; it needs to explain why we observe this particular physics through selection effects.

(2) String theory's landscape does that: the flux compactifications generate a specific distribution over vacua, and anthropic reasoning picks out observer-friendly ones.

(3) Now imagine a toy framework i call Random Lattice Theory (RLT). it just says every mathematically consistent set of low-energy constants is instantiated somewhere in an infinite lattice of universes.

(4) RLT clearly contains our observed constants, and it also contains regions where observers exist.

(5) So RLT can invoke the exact same anthropic story: we see our constants because we're in one of the observer-friendly nodes.

(6) Therefore, by (1) and (2), RLT gets the same non-empirical confirmation as string theory.

(7) But RLT was cooked up specifically to include everything; it has no dynamics, no derivation, no constraint on lattice spacing or anything.

(8) If RLT is confirmed on equal footing, then scientific confirmation collapses into "is this logically compatable with what we see?"

(9) That's clearly wrong.

(10) So one of (1)-(6) has to go. i really want to keep (1) because without some explanatory demand multiverse theories are just unfalsifiable lists, and if i drop it i feel like i'm letting string theory off the hook too easily. is the mistake in (5) -- like RLT can't actually apply the anthropic reasoning because it lacks a measure? or is there a hidden equivocation between "contains our world" and "predicts our world with a distribution"? where exactly does the argument break, and what do i pay if i reject that step?
