I am asking for a poem that works through the conceptual architecture of Isaiah Berlin's negative liberty, positive liberty, and Philip Pettit's republican liberty, composed as a Petrarchan sonnet—fourteen lines in iambic pentameter, rhyming ABBA ABBA in the octave and CDECDE in the sestet—with the volta falling precisely at line nine. The octave should present negative liberty as freedom defined solely by the absence of actual interference, and positive liberty as the capacity for self-direction, keeping the two in tension without resolving them. At the volta, republican liberty should enter not as a middle ground but as a reconceptualization of the threat itself: freedom is not the absence of interference but the absence of arbitrary domination, which requires that power be tracked and contestable regardless of whether it is exercised. The sestet should make structurally visible the consequence that republican liberty, in demanding institutional conditions that render power non-arbitrary, imports the very capacities and social provisions characteristic of positive liberty, despite its claim to be a species of negative freedom. The closing couplet should state, with conceptual precision, what collapses if one reduces republican freedom to negative freedom by treating arbitrary interference as just another form of obstruction. Please present the result in continuous lines with standard line breaks, without headings, bullet points, bold text, tables, or any separated explanatory framing or notes; do not set a title apart from the body, and do not append commentary, so that the verse stands alone as a single continuous piece.
