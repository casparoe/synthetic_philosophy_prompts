The separateness objection means utilitarianism is wrong because it adds up welfare across people like theyre all part of one big person. Rawls says each individual lives a seperate life with their own goals, so you cant justify hurting one person just because it helps more people overall. This is basically the same as Kants idea that you shouldnt use people as mere means. Williams says people have integrity, which means they have deep personal commitments and projects that shouldnt be sacrificed for the greater good. In the hospital case, the algorithm picks winners and losers based on stats alone. Rawls would reject this because behind a veil of ignorance nobody would agree to be in the low-probability group. Williams would also reject it because the patients who are denied care have their life projects destroyed, violating their integrity. So both philosophers agree the algorithm is bad, though for slightly different reasons.
