I am preparing a six-month dissertation prospectus on a conceptual question in comparative folk morality. The familiar framing pits German Ordnung—rule-following, procedural correctness, and institutional trust—against American individual conscience, authenticity, and moral self-reliance. I want to move past this standard comparison and investigate a specific asymmetry: whether German folk morality recognizes a distinct category of procedural betrayal (violating a legitimate rule one has genuinely accepted) that American folk morality tends to collapse into hypocrisy or inauthenticity, and conversely whether American folk morality recognizes conscientious deviation (breaking an illegitimate rule for sound moral reasons) that German folk morality treats as mere disorder or unreliability rather than as a moral achievement. The project is philosophical, not empirical: it uses conceptual analysis of moral-failure types rather than ethnography or survey data.

Write the proposal in four sections, in this order and with these approximate lengths:
1. The precise question and why existing cultural-comparison answers leave it open (~250 words);

3. What a successful result would look like, what would count as failure, and the realistic six-month deliverable (~250 words);
4. A brief defense of why this should be funded as philosophical work rather than as cultural studies (~150 words).
