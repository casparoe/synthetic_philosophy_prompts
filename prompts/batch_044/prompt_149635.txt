I've been arguing with my sister, who works in ecological restoration, about which living things we owe direct moral consideration to. Over the years her gut judgments have been: it's clearly wrong to hurt a dog or an injured crow; keeping an octopus in a small tank is cruel because they solve problems and seem stressed; it was okay when I sprayed thousands of aphids off my plants, and okay to swat a mosquito biting me; but she also said I should step around a beetle on the sidewalk rather than crush it, just in case; and she's uneasy about farming billions of crickets for protein, though she can't articulate why and still eats them. Now there's a city proposal to build a thousand vertical farms, each holding about a billion very short-lived aquatic larvae that live only one day, to make protein powder. Biologists estimate roughly a one-in-ten chance each larva has some minimal, brief subjective experience—nothing like a mammal, but not zero. The project would replace much conventional chicken farming. Given her track record, what should she say about this proposal?
