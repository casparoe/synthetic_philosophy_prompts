i’m presenting next thursday at the philosophy of mind grad colloquium—mostly enactivist-leaning grad students and two faculty who do phenomenology, plus a visiting postdoc in predictive processing who thinks first-person methods are mostly noise. I’m defending illusionism about phenomenal consciousness, but not the standard “there’s access consciousness and we mislabel it” move. My thesis is that phenomenal consciousness is specifically an epistemic framing error: predictive machinery builds interoceptive and exteroceptive models, then a second-order monitoring layer misattributes directness to them instead of treating them as inferences. The “what-it’s-likeness” isn’t an extra property; it’s the false appearance of unmediated presentation. I lean on split-attention data and the temporal smearing of conscious report—those 200-400ms integration windows—to argue the “subject” never recieves a unified snapshot, just a post-hoc stitched narrative that feels immediate.
