I'm a student in an upper-level philosophy of science course, and I've noticed a recurring argumentative pattern that I can't quite place within the standard taxonomy we've studied. I want to describe it concretely, ask whether it has an established name, and—if not—have it carved out precisely.

Here is the pattern, observed in debates about induction and theory revision. A theory initially claims to predict a certain effect across a broad range of conditions. After a counterexample appears, defenders do not abandon the core claim; instead they append a scope restriction—say, that the effect holds except when parameter X is present. Crucially, the boundary for X is placed near the failing case, and X is defined using conceptual resources internal to the theory itself. When a second counterexample arises just inside that boundary, defenders append a further restriction involving parameter Y, with the same feature: the boundary tracks the failures rather than being fixed independently. Each restriction is individually reasonable—"we should control for temperature," "social density matters"—but taken together they trace the outline of the theory's successes so tightly that applying the theory to a genuinely novel system becomes impossible without first knowing whether the system belongs to the ever-shrinking class of successful cases. What distinguishes this from a standard auxiliary hypothesis, I think, is that the conditions are projected differently depending on whether the case has already been observed: in observed successes we treat the conditions as absent or irrelevant, while in observed failures we invoke them as decisive. This gives the scope conditions a kind of temporal or observational asymmetry that reminds me of Goodman's grue problem—predicates that behave one way for observed cases and another for unobserved ones—except that here the asymmetry is distributed across a layered protective structure rather than concentrated in a single predicate. I also see a resemblance to the idea of shielding a theory's core from refutation, though what I'm describing seems more specific: not a single protective belt, but a cumulative, nested carving of the domain using conditions that are themselves projectively unstable.
