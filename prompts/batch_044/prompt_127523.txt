I'm making a handout for a philosophy of culture seminar on Nietzsche's weird obsession with digestion, climate, and diet—his rants about german cooking, the pro-meat anti-vegetarian bits, and the way he ties stomach trouble to philosophical error. I want a decision-tree taxomony of interpretive stances, about six to eight leaves, built from logical space rather than a scholar roll-call.

Root split: do you read his dietary prescriptions literally or as metaphors for intellectual digestion? Then: is poor digestion a cause of bad philosophy, or just a symptom of deeper physiological decay? Then: is his anti-asceticism mainly biological vitalism or a cultural-political swipe at christian morality?

Each leaf should carry the stance name (or 'unamed') plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment—like having to read Ecce Homo's self-praise about his stomach as either straight science or pure irony. Note any empty combos, e.g. literal universal prescription paired with purely cultural motivation, and say if they're incoherent, ugly, or just unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't separate.

Keep the branch quetions concrete—e.g., 'if Nietzsche had chronic indigestion, could he still have produced the Genealogy?'—so students answer from intuition, not label recognition. Audience is grads who know the big themes but not the niche secondary literature on diet. Don't ask for citations or reading lists; this needs to work for someone with no web access.
