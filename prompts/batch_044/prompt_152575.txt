Here is my opening move: I want to test whether your distinction can survive a reconstructed internal route. Imagine an agent who has deliberately emptied her first-order motivational set of any desire for ecological preservation. She retains only one bare second-order commitment: she endorses honoring the collective deliberative protocols of a scientific institution she belongs to. That institution’s rules generate a recommendation to protect a specific watershed, but she feels no motivational pull toward protection; the recommendation reaches her solely through the protocol. Is her reason to act internal, flowing from her commitment to the protocol, or external, since no sound deliberative route connects her actual first-order motivations to the act?
