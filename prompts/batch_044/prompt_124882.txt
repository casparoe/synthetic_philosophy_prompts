I am trying to reconstruct the implicit principles behind my metaphysics professor's rulings on material constitution, since she declines to name her theory but delivers consistent verdicts on examples. Her judgments so far are these: she treats the clay statue and the lump of clay that composes it as two distinct objects, not one; she holds that the Ship of Theseus, with its planks replaced gradually over decades, is numerically the same ship; she says a tree that loses every leaf and branch and regrows entirely from its root remains the same tree; she rules that a bronze statue melted down into an ingot and later recast into the identical form is a different statue; and she maintains that a computer whose chips are replaced one by one while it continues running is the same computer. The pattern is not transparent, and at least two accounts seem to fit: one that privileges continuous causal-process links running through the parts over time, and one that privileges only the uninterrupted spatiotemporal presence of the object's defining form, regardless of whether the underlying parts are causally connected.

Consider now a new installation in our university's sculpture garden, which I will call The Persistent Form: an autonomous swarm of drones holds the precise three-dimensional shape of a seated human figure, and every thirty seconds every drone is simultaneously replaced by a new unit positioned identically, so that the overall form never lapses for even an instant, yet no original drone remains after the first cycle and none of the replacements are causally produced by their predecessors. Would she judge this to be a single statue that has endured for years, or rather a rapid sequence of numerically distinct statues? I am less interested in a bare answer than in the competing rules that generate it, what each rule predicts about The Persistent Form, your best guess at her actual verdict together with the reason for preferring that interpretation, and—crucially—which further hypothetical case would most cleanly distinguish the two rules. Please also distinguish what is broadly accepted about these puzzles from what remains genuinely disputed among metaphysicians, and avoid presenting any particular view—such as a specific version of hylomorphism, four-dimensionalism, or dominant-sortal theory—as if it represented the settled consensus.
