Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a defender of the strong precautionary principle and an advocate of explicit, quantifiable risk-cost-benefit analysis, convened specifically on the governance of socio-technical transitions that may irreversibly diminish future democratic deliberative capacity—an issue that became personally urgent when I was recently tasked with assessing a regulatory framework lacking rollback provisions for autonomous environmental-modification systems.

The deliverable should contain: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section covering the formal structure of the precautionary principle versus proportional-risk frameworks, the shared rejection of both pure laissez-faire and pure paralysis, and the precise empirical and normative questions that remain genuinely open; (2) a disagreements table stating each camp's residual position in wording its own members would accept—particularly on whether precaution necessarily privileges the status quo, and on whether burden-of-proof allocation is a procedural or substantive moral choice; (3) the cruxes—at minimum, whether "irreversibility" can be defined independently of the value assigned to the current state, and whether precautionary thresholds must be established ex ante by democratic mandate or can be constructed ex post through liability regimes; (4) for each crux, the specific argument, hypothetical institutional design, or case both sides agree is relevant, together with each side's advance prediction of what that test reveals; and (5) an explicit identification of any purely verbal disagreement—such as whether "uncertainty" denotes epistemic limitation or ontological openness—and an explanation of why resolving that terminological point does not resolve the underlying dispute. Format the output as the numbered agreed-claims section followed by the disagreements table.
