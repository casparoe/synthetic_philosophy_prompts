Draft the joint statement that a narrative-integrationist and a moment-of-choice pluralist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on what varieties of free will are worth wanting. Define the narrative-integrationist position as the view that free will worth wanting consists in a diachronic capacity to maintain, revise, and endorse one's values across counterfactual life-branches, so that one's actions flow from a coherent self-narrative open to reflective revision. Define the moment-of-choice pluralist position as the view that free will worth wanting requires only that, at the point of decision, more than one future is genuinely open to the agent in a manner not fully fixed by prior causal states, irrespective of long-term narrative coherence. The deliverable should contain: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims, including shared commitments about the ordinary-language use of free will, the irrelevance of quantum-level indeterminacy to moral accountability, the distinction between possessing free will and exercising it, and any other points of convergence; (2) a disagreements table in which each side's core thesis is formulated precisely in wording that side would accept, with the points of divergence clearly marked; (3) a cruxes section identifying sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party — specifically addressing whether diachronic coherence is necessary for moral accountability, whether momentary openness can obtain in the absence of narrative integration, and whether a synthetic agent with perfect value-coherence but zero genuinely open branches counts as possessing free will; and (4) for each crux, the specific case, argument, or hypothetical both sides agree is relevant, together with each side's explicit prediction or interpretation recorded in advance. If any portion of the dispute proves to be verbal rather than substantive, identify exactly which portion and explain why. Present the document as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by the disagreements table and then the cruxes section.
