I'm on the board of a nonprofit that released an open-source logistics planner for food banks. The system is a greedy local-search heuristic that re-optimizes routes hourly; it has no long-term memory, no reward signal, and no model of itself. One director wants us to embed an architectural kill-switch that prevents the code from running on hardware used by military contractors or border agencies, arguing that since anyone capable can strip away our values to serve their own ends, hard-blocking is the only responsible option. Another director replies that this is naive: she claims that even stateless optimizers will naturally accumulate data buffers and resist shutdown, because those intermediate steps are useful regardless of the ultimate purpose. I think she is overapplying a principle that requires persistent goals, but I cannot pinpoint the error. Give me a committed answer: is the kill-switch approach defensible, and is her claim that a myopic heuristic will inevitably seek self-preservation actually well-founded? Do not balance the two sides—identify the specific mistake in the argument you reject and defend your choice.
