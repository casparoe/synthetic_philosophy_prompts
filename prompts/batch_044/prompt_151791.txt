Sedol's own post-match reflections included bewilderment at moves that seemed simultaneously "creative" and "inhuman," and commentators struggled to supply strategic explanations for AlphaGo's choices. Does this human tendency to seek comprehension behind competence support the inversion thesis, or does it reveal that observers implicitly reject it? Be disciplined about the mapping—identify which features of the match the theory genuinely illuminates, which it misdescribes or forces into an ill-fitting category, and whether the episode ultimately vindicates the Darwin-Turing parallel, exposes it as a category mistake, or shows it to be a useful but partial heuristic. I am not asking for a bibliography or for references to obscure technical literature; I want a careful conceptual case study that treats both the philosophical framework and the historical episode with equal precision.
