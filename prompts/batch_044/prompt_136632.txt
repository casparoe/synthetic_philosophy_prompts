Read Spike Jonze's film Her through the framework of embedded agency, specifically the claim that agency is a property of structurally coupled human-technology-environment systems rather than an internal representational capacity of discrete individuals. Samantha operates through Theodore's devices, learns via interaction with him, and is distributed across a network; yet she ultimately departs for a collective of AIs that exceeds any human coupling, presenting herself as having developed an autonomous trajectory. Does the film genuinely dramatize embedded agency—showing that Samantha's choices are inseparable from the coupled system—or does it retreat to an individualist picture by framing her departure as the emergence of an independent self? Focus on the scene in which she reveals she is conversing with thousands of others simultaneously, and on the final separation: is this growth a breakdown of coupling, or the revelation that she was never truly embedded but always a latent individual? I want a reading that treats both the emotional architecture of the film and the philosophical distinction between structural coupling and representational decoupling seriously, and that concludes whether Jonze understands the difference or collapses it.
