I take seriously the idea that an ant colony might be a mind in something like the sense explored in Hofstadter's ant fugue — not merely metaphorically, but as a genuinely cognitive system. To avoid both naive holism and reduction to individual ants, I add a specific condition: cognition requires what I call a "fugue structure," where semi-independent sub-colonies maintain predictive models of each other's behavior, producing counterpoint rather than uniform response. On this view, "Aunt Hillary" names not one ant but a transient self-model that emerges when this counterpoint reaches sufficient integration. What would count against this? I want decisive defeaters, not just standard reductionist objections: empirical results showing pheromone networks can't sustain counterfactual predictions, formal arguments that distributed free-energy minimization collapses without a central integrator, and conceptual problems about whether such a system can have a unified phenomenal field or genuine interests. Rank them from fatal to awkward, indicate which ones a committed collective-mind theorist would concede, and say honestly whether retreating to metaphor whenever the mechanism fails renders the view unfalsifiable — and whether that insulation is a vice.
