I am a doctoral student in cognitive science and philosophy of mind, and I have been working through what I take to be a stringent, perhaps overstated, reading of Hofstadter and Sander's central thesis in Surfaces and Essences: that analogy-making is not merely the dominant engine of cognition but its constitutively exhaustive substrate. On this view, every representational state—whether perceptual, conceptual, or formal—derives its content from an analogical mapping onto previously structured conceptual essences, leaving no genuinely non-analogical representations. Even apparently abstract or syntactic reasoning is, on this account, a high-level projection across domains that only appears context-independent because the underlying analogies have become deeply internalized and transparent through repetition; the appearance of pure symbolic computation is therefore an illusion generated by "frozen" analogies that have lost their visible relational structure.

What would have to be true for this strong constitutive analogy thesis to be wrong? I am not asking for the familiar objections about modularity or processing efficiency, but for the defeaters that should actually move me: empirical findings that reveal irreducibly non-analogical representations, formal arguments showing analogical mapping cannot ground certain necessary cognitive capacities, or conceptual demonstrations that the thesis collapses into an unfalsifiable claim about hidden similarity rather than a substantive claim about cognition. Which of these would defenders of the view themselves concede as damaging, and which would they dismiss by redescription—treating every counterexample as merely another layer of buried analogy? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say whether the position risks anything observable or has already been insulated by qualifications, and assess honestly whether that insulation renders the view intellectually costly or even empty. If my framing rests on a confused premise—perhaps by misreading Hofstadter and Sander as committed to this exhaustive version—say so plainly rather than accommodating the error politely; and if the answer must depend on contested interpretations of their text, flag where you are relying on one reading rather than another.
