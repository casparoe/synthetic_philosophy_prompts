everyone in my EA-adjacent circles keeps comparing measurability bias to the old streetlight story -- the drunk searching for his keys under the lamppost cuz thats where the lights, not cuz thats where he dropped them. they use it to argue that institutions like GiveWell end up funding only whats legible (bednets, deworming) and miss the big structural or longterm stuff. i want to audit how far that analogy actually carries weight. lay out the correspondence: whats the lost keys, the lamp post, the darkness, and the drunk guy? then tell me where the mapping breaks -- for instance, does it smuggle in that theres exactly one right place to look, or that quantification is always a distortion rather than a noisy proxy? and heres the key part: if the streetlight image collapses, does the institutional critique about measurability bias still stand on its own, or was the analogy doing all the argumentative work? be honest about which disanalogies matter and which are just decorative.
