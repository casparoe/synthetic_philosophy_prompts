I spent most of last evening arguing with a close friend—she works in political theory, I am trying to decide where Foucault’s analytics actually lead—about whether the framework still generates live, tractable research questions or mostly offers elegant retrospective redescriptions of institutional history. The argument turned on a specific tension: Foucault’s account of disciplinary power was built around enclosed architectures (the prison, the clinic, the school) where normalization operates through visible spatial arrangements and hierarchical observation. But if contemporary governance genuinely operates through distributed, predictive infrastructures—algorithmic scoring, real-time behavioral feedback, and decentralized risk calculation—then either we are dealing with a genuinely new modality of power, or we are merely stretching an old vocabulary over new surfaces. Before I commit to a trajectory with roughly two years of conceptual work and no institutional archive access, I need to know which open lines of work in extending this framework deserve precedence.

More concretely, I am weighing three unresolved questions that sit at different depths. First, the formal-conceptual problem: can Foucault’s notion of governmentality be rigorously extended to non-state, algorithmic nodes of calculation without collapsing the distinction between discipline and mere administration? Second, the normative-structural problem: given that his anti-essentialist account of the subject seems to foreclose any stable ground from which to judge practices as genuinely emancipatory rather than merely more efficient modes of subjectivation, is there any coherent way to recover normative traction within the framework, or should that be treated as a dead end? Third, the empirical-institutional problem: have knowledge-production institutions themselves—peer review, citation metrics, research funding structures—migrated from disciplinary normalization to a different logic of modulation and prediction, and does answering this change how we read Foucault’s history of the human sciences?

What I need is a defended agenda, not a literature survey. For someone working without archive access and with limited time, which of these should be treated as upstream? Does resolving the normative vacuum determine what empirical extensions are even worth attempting? Does the formalization of distributed discipline depend on clarifying whether biopower is scale-invariant across institutional contexts? And which answers, if secured, would genuinely change how we apply these analytics to education policy or governance design, versus which are scholastic refinements that would not alter practice? Please rank them with explicit criteria—tractability with conceptual tools alone, dependency relations between them, and practical leverage—while flagging which combinations of answers are incoherent or unexplored because they lead to unattractive commitments. I want to know what deserves my years, not just what remains unresolved.
