hey so I'm running a grad seminar on philosophy of AI and agency, about 10 people, one 75-minute session. we're working on embedded agency — not just the generic "you can't step outside yourself" thing, but the tighter version where an agent has to model its own reasoning process as part of the environment it's predicting, so any fully transparent self-model changes the very procedure it's tracking. like trying to weigh a scale you're standing on. i want them to feel that loop in their bones before anyone names it.

design a low-tech in-class game or structured deliberation — paper, index cards, maybe a shared grid on the whiteboard — where participants are simultaneously agents inside a system and rule-makers for that system. the rules they propose or vote on should apply back to themselves immediately, and they need to coordinate on some shared prediction about the system's state at a future point, except they themselves are part of that state. include the rules, materials, a timeline for the 75 minutes, and a payoff scheme using points or social status rather than real money. they should hit the wall where someone tries to take a "view from nowhere" and it collapses because they're embedded in the board.

then a debrief that maps specific moments — someone proposing an external observer rule, a change that backfired on its author, the failure to coordinate because no one can check the full state without altering it — onto parts of the embedded-agency argument. also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time i run it (probably people just pretend they're outside observers?), and be frank about which feature of the idea — maybe the logical infinite regress of full self-modeling, or the physical continuity between agent and world — this activity can't reproduce and i'll have to supply in discussion afterwards.
