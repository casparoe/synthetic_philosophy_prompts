I think the real explanatory work is done by reverse adaptation: once a high-infrastructure artifact is deployed, social institutions are redesigned around its maintenance and operational logic, and this redesign is technically necessary rather than ideologically chosen. The artifact does not merely constrain politics; it generates a path-dependent organizational form that becomes invisible because it is framed as engineering rather than governance.

- How should we treat digital infrastructures that seem to permit both centralized and decentralized governance — do they falsify the structural claim, or merely relocate the politics from hardware to protocol?
