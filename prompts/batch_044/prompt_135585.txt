I'm preparing to defend my position in population axiology for a graduate seminar, and I want an internal consistency audit before I present. Here is my package of commitments:

(1) I endorse totalist aggregation: the moral value of a world is the sum of welfare across all individuals in it.

(2) I reject the repugnant conclusion: a world with vastly more people living lives barely worth living is not better than one with fewer flourishing lives.

(3) I deny that welfare admits any sharp, non-arbitrary metaphysical boundary; well-being is graded continuously.

(4) Despite (3), I defend critical-level utilitarianism with a context-relative specification: there is a cutoff near psychological neutrality, defined relative to a reference class of stable, non-deprived psychological states in a given social context, such that lives falling below this baseline do not improve the world, while those above it do. I treat this as a functional parameter, not a metaphysical fact.

(5) I reject average utilitarianism outright, since it licenses eliminating people with below-average welfare.

(6) On procreation: there is no positive moral reason to create happy people; a flourishing life that comes to exist is permissible but does not count in favor of reproduction, even though that life carries positive welfare.

(7) I am a physicalist about welfare: well-being consists entirely in psychological states and material conditions, with no irreducible normative property.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Rank the tensions by severity. Specifically, I want to know whether (1) and (6) can be reconciled without abandoning totalism, whether (3) undermines (4) given the context-relative specification, and whether (5) and (2) together leave me with any coherent aggregation rule besides (4). Also indicate how confident you are in this audit and single out the point you are least sure about.
