I've ended up conviced by the hard-line illusionist view on phenominal consciousness — not that qualia are just weird, but that there's literally no inner feel at all, only a brain-generated narrative that misrepresents cognitive access as direct acquaintance with raw sensation. Reports of what it's like aren't lies, exactly, but they're systematically misframed confabulations. What'd actually count against this? I want the honest exposure map, not just 'but I know I feel stuff.' Maybe neuroscientists isolating a mechanism that genuinely produces non-representational phenomenal properties, or predictive-processing models that can't run without irreducible 'feel' nodes tracking precision errors. Any conceptual defeaters — like illusionism collapsing into eliminativism about belief, or failing to tell the illusion of pain apart from the illusion of seeing red? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones illusionists like Frankish or Dennett would actually concede damage the view, versus ones they'd dismiss. And be straight: has this been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that a problem for a claim of this kind?
