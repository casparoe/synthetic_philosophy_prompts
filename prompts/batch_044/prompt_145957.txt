I'm delivering the opening remarks at a one-day workshop titled "Forecasting and Humility," hosted at a policy research institute. The room holds about thirty people: on one side, professional forecasters, decision analysts, and philosophers of science who live with the inside-view versus outside-view distinction every day; on the other, board members, donors, and family members with no technical background, who need to understand why the workshop matters without being lectured. I want the remarks—roughly six minutes, formal but not dry—to do more than decorate a theme; the tension between the inside view and the outside view should structurally determine what I can and cannot say. Specifically, please frame the workshop's purpose through what I will call the single-case reference problem: for any particular reasoner, her own access to her specific models, doubts, and contextual adjustments (the inside view) and her placement within a statistical reference class with a known base rate of overconfidence or bias (the outside view) yield two accounts that cannot be merged into a single authoritative narrative without doing epistemic violence to one side. Epistemic modesty, then, should govern the speech: I should welcome everyone, explain why we have gathered, and introduce the day's question—how to reason well under ambiguity—without promising that the workshop will "solve" the tension, and without speaking as if the outside view grants us privileged access to anyone's particular reasoning. Include one concrete, non-obvious illustration—a small-group decision under ambiguity, built from first principles rather than drawn from a named study—that both halves of the room can grasp immediately, but that lands with different weight for the experts (who should see the reference-class trap) and the non-experts (who should feel the human pressure of the moment). Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke named forecasters, specific historical predictions, or obscure statistical studies; and do not resolve the tension in a tidy conclusion, since the point is that the workshop exists precisely because the tension is live.
