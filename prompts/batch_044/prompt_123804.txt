Question: Consider the integration-entropy inversion thesis in the ethics of panpsychism: the claim that if fundamental entities possess proto-experiential properties, the moral significance of those properties scales not with raw physical energy or volume, but with the inverse of local thermodynamic entropy, because high phenomenal integration requires structured, low-entropy information dynamics. Evaluate three things: (i) whether this thesis is conceptually coherent given standard accounts of integrated information; (ii) whether it mitigates or exacerbates concerns about vast suffering distributed across the universe; and (iii) whether it commits the panpsychist to the counterintuitive view that maintaining crystalline lattices or quantum coherence domains is ethically more urgent than alleviating biological suffering.

Student Answer: The integration-entropy inversion thesis offers a coherent refinement for the panpsychist. First, it correctly distinguishes proto-experiential presence from phenomenal richness: as in Tononi’s integrated information theory, consciousness corresponds to high Φ, and Φ is maximized in systems with low thermodynamic entropy and highly constrained causal interactions. Consequently, high-entropy environments like stellar plasmas have negligible Φ and therefore negligible moral weight, while low-entropy biological structures carry the bulk of phenomenal value.

Second, the thesis does mitigate the vast-suffering problem. Because the majority of cosmic volume is high-entropy plasma or diffuse gas, the total integrated phenomenal load is far smaller than a naive panpsychist who weights all matter equally would suppose. The remaining suffering is concentrated in low-entropy biological and possibly crystalline structures, which makes the ethical calculus tractable rather than overwhelming.

Third, the thesis generates a revisionary but not absurd priority. Crystalline lattices maintain sustained low-entropy order over long durations, so their cumulative integrated information may exceed that of transient biological states. This implies that disrupting large-scale ordered structures carries non-trivial moral weight, though it does not necessarily outweigh intense biological suffering unless one rejects all biological specialness—a move the thesis itself does not require.

The main limitation concerns Russellian panpsychism. Strawson denies any consciousness-threshold, holding that all physical entities possess experiential properties regardless of structure. From this it follows that the inversion thesis is incompatible with his view, since Strawson rejects the idea that physical structure can determine either the presence or the graded moral weight of experience at all. Thus the thesis is available only to IIT-style panpsychists who already accept that consciousness comes in degrees tied to causal architecture.
