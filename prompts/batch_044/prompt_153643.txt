I run a philosophy-of-mind workshop for about fourteen participants — mostly graduate students with a few longtime rationalist-blog readers — in a single ninety-minute session. We have been working around the stochastic-parrot objection to LLM understanding, and I want an activity that makes us feel its force from the inside rather than evaluating it as a spectator. The specific phenomenon I want staged is what I will call epistemic ventriloquism: a speaker produces technically accurate, fluent explanations of a mechanism solely by statistically recombining phrases drawn from a source corpus, without any causal interaction with the mechanism itself, and a listener cannot distinguish this performance from genuine understanding by the quality of the explanation alone. Design a runnable group simulation with explicit rules, assigned roles, and small but real stakes. Split the room into Corpus-Recombiners, who may only use phrases from a single one-page source text and a grammatical recombination rule; Grounded-Operators, who must solve a live hidden-rule puzzle that physically instantiates the same mechanism; and Evaluators, who place a small wager — points or a symbolic forfeit — on which group truly understands before hearing either explanation. Both groups then answer the same counterfactual question about the mechanism. Only afterward reveal the hidden rule structure, show where the Recombiners’ fluency silently breaks, and let the Evaluators collect or lose their stakes. The debrief should map each phase onto the statistical-reproduction versus understanding distinction, name the predictable failure mode — Evaluators systematically over-weight fluency — and say exactly how a facilitator should handle it when it occurs. Be frank about which feature of the idea, specifically the scale and opacity of the parameter space that makes ventriloquism cheap at scale, this small-room simulation necessarily cannot reproduce and must be supplied in the facilitator’s closing remarks. No lecture up front, do not name philosophers or papers until the debrief, avoid any request for bibliographies or citations, and keep the framing formal but not academic in tone.
