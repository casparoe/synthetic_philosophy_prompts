I've been reading a bunch of old LessWrong threads on epistemic rationality and now I'm stuck trying to formalize my own gut reactions about belief and acceptance. I'm in a philosophy course that covers the usual lottery and preface stuff, but the textbook examples feel too clean. Here are my actual verdicts on messier cases.

If I hold one ticket in a ten-million-ticket lottery, I think it's perfectly rational to believe I'll loose and to turn down $0.99 for it. But if I hold 500,000 tickets, the belief "I'll loose" suddenly feels different — not false, but like I'm holding it for pragmatic reasons rather than genuinely endorsing it in reasoning. Then there's the preface: a scientist writes a 600-page book, checks every claim, and writes in the preface that probably three errors are hiding in here somewhere. I think she's rational to believe each page and rational to believe the preface. But here's the variant I can't resolve: she lists pages 42, 117, and 203 as the ones she's suspicious of, but admits she doesn't know which ones are actually wrong. Do I think she should suspend belief specifically on those three pages? My intuition flickers — she still has 99% confidence in each, but now the error is localized instead of spread out, and that feels like it should matter. I keep flip-flopping.

There's also this scaling thing with conjunction. I'm about 99% sure my keys, wallet, and phone are each in my bag, and I feel totally entitled to say all three are in there without running the numbers. But list fifty items I'm 98% sure about, and suddenly all fifty are present feels like an overreach even though the math is structurally identical. And I think it's rational to accept this flight is delayed strongly enough to rebook my connection, but not strongly enough to cite in a debate about airline stats — like acceptance comes with a context tag. So is there one principle behind all this, or am I just mixing up credence, acceptance, and betting behavior?

Please lay out the principle or principles that capture these verdicts, especially what to do about the localized-error preface case and whether lottery-style beliefs can be used in reasoning or only for action. Be honest about which of my gut calls have to go if we want coherence. And please answer in American English — I'm already tired of seeing behaviour with a u.
