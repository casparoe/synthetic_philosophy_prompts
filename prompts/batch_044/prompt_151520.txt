I am trying to determine what I genuinely believe about political obligation and the conditions under which breaking the law can be justified, by examining my unreflective verdicts on specific cases and seeing whether they cohere.

My verdicts are as follows. First, openly refusing to pay a portion of income tax that is explicitly allocated to funding a military intervention I regard as gravely unjust, announcing the refusal and accepting the legal penalty, strikes me as justified civil disobedience: my general duty to obey that tax law is overridden. Second, quietly concealing assets to avoid paying the very same tax, with no public disclosure and with the aim of escaping punishment, strikes me as unjustified evasion: the obligation remains intact, and secrecy removes whatever justification might exist. Third, participating in an open, non-violent sit-in that blocks access to a government office in protest against an unjust deportation policy, accepting arrest without resistance, strikes me as justified: the procedural duty not to obstruct is suspended. Fourth, working as a civil servant and secretly altering official records to prevent one specific, imminent deportation that I regard as wrong, without ever revealing the alteration, strikes me as justified—indeed, more clearly so than case two, despite being equally secret. Fifth, publicly refusing jury service in a legal system with well-documented racial disparities in conviction rates, explaining my refusal to the court in writing, strikes me as unjustified: the burden is minimal, the refusal weakens a shared institution, and the communicative gain is insufficient to override the duty of fair participation. Sixth, working as a physician and quietly omitting to file a legally required report of a patient's undocumented immigration status, treating the patient but saying nothing in public, is the case I keep returning to: sometimes it feels analogous to case four (direct prevention of immediate harm justifies secret non-compliance with an instrumental rule), sometimes analogous to case two (a hidden withdrawal from legal obligation without communicative purpose is mere evasion).

Is there a single principle, or a small family of principles, that captures the pattern across these six? I want it stated precisely, with each clause tied to the case that motivates it. It should also yield clear verdicts on cases I have not considered—for instance, anonymously leaking classified documents that expose an unjust policy, versus anonymously damaging public property to delay its implementation. Most importantly, I want an honest statement of which of my six verdicts the principle cannot accommodate without revision: does it save case four at the expense of case six, or vice versa? And if one verdict must be surrendered, which is the least costly to drop?
