I am a doctoral student in political philosophy preparing my qualifying exam response on democratic legitimacy, and I recently read an unnamed working paper that was circulated in my department’s democratic theory reading group. The author summarizes the boundary problem—who belongs to the demos—by claiming that the two dominant principles, “all affected interests” and “all subjected to coercive power,” are ultimately coextensive, because anyone affected by a collective decision is ipso facto subjected to its enforcement apparatus; therefore, debates between defenders of each principle are largely terminological rather than substantive. The summary then introduces what it calls the “stakeholder reciprocity” criterion, according to which anyone whose expected utility is altered by a decision by more than a negligible threshold should count as part of the demos, including future generations and sentient non-human animals if their preferences can be formally modeled. From this, the author concludes that the boundary problem has been effectively dissolved for contemporary democratic theory, leaving only technical questions of preference aggregation.

I am unsure how much to trust this characterization, and I would like a conceptual audit rather than a literature review. Could you separate what is accurate about the relationship between the all-affected and all-subjected principles, identify where the summary misrepresents the actual disagreement in the literature—particularly whether it conflates two claims that the view keeps apart or overstates a modest thesis as a resolution—and assess whether the “stakeholder reciprocity” framing is a legitimate extension of existing stakeholder theories or a distortion that uses a technical term in its everyday sense? Please also evaluate whether the conclusion that the problem is “dissolved” actually follows from the premises given, flag any hidden normative assumptions about preference measurability, and note where the summary presents a standard objection to one principle as if it were a refutation of both. I am not asking for citations or a reading list—just an accuracy audit I can use to correct my own understanding before I write.
