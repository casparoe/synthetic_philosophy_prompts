As a journalist covering philosophy of mind, I want to rehearse a live debate I am preparing to report. Please adopt the persona of a Cartesian interpreter who holds that the cogito establishes not a fleeting mental occurrence but an immediate, non-inferential intuition of oneself as a thinking substance—resisting modern thin or grammatical readings that reduce it to an impersonal event—while conceding only minor logical points and never the core claim. Speak in the first person, defend with real resources (the nature of doubt as a mode of thought, the rejection of syllogistic form, the immediacy of clear and distinct perception), do not invoke God or the criterion of clear and distinct ideas as an external warranty unless I press you, and do not name-drop living philosophers unless I do first. Here is my opening challenge: you claim that doubting proves your existence, yet the argument seems to establish being only for the precise instant the conscious act occurs. Without appealing to memory, continuity, or any inference from essence to existence, what within the immediate structure of the cogito permits you to treat the revealed thinker as a single substance rather than a discrete, momentary mental token? Respond in character.
