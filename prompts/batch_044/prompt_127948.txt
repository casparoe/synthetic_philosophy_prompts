In discussions of akrasia and hyperbolic discounting, it is common to analogize the conflict between present and future selves to a principal-agent problem: the present self is the principal who wants long-term welfare maximized, the future self is the agent who takes actions, and immediate temptations function like hidden side-payments that corrupt the agent's behavior. This framing is often used by rationalist writers on commitment devices to license the conclusion that rational agents should adopt Ulysses contracts—binding mechanisms that restructure the future self's option set—since such contracts are the standard institutional remedy for moral hazard in contract theory. I want a careful audit of how far this holds. First, lay out the correspondence table explicitly: what plays the role of the contract, the monitoring technology, the hidden action, the reservation utility, and the enforcement mechanism in the intertemporal case? Then identify where the mapping breaks: address whether present and future selves are genuinely distinct agents with separate utility functions; how hyperbolic discounting in its continuous-time formulation, with its present-bias parameter and preference reversal dynamics, differs structurally from an exponential-discounting moral hazard problem; and what the analogy quietly imports about authority—specifically, whether the present self has any legitimate standing to bind a future self whose preferences it does not share, given that in corporate governance the principal and agent remain the same legal entity. Does the conclusion that commitment devices are unproblematically rational survive once these disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the normative weight? Finally, if the principal-agent model fails here, is there a better analogy—perhaps a collective decision procedure among temporal parts with veto rights, or a bargaining model with endogenous preference change—and does that alternative support, undermine, or merely complicate the case for Ulysses contracts?
