I have been working through Descartes's Meditations on my own, without any formal philosophical training, and I have settled on an unusually narrow reading of the cogito. On my view, when Descartes concludes that he thinks and therefore he is, he establishes nothing more than the immediate occurrence of a present cognitive event—something like a bare flash of awareness—without licensing any inference to a persisting substance, a unified ego, or even a continuous subject that owns the thought from one instant to the next. The pronoun I functions merely as a grammatical index for whatever is currently undergoing the mental act, and the claim that I exist means only that this particular thinking is happening right now. I find this minimal interpretation attractive because it avoids importing metaphysical commitments that have not yet been earned, but I want to understand exactly how exposed it is.

What would have to be true for this reading to fail? I am not asking for the familiar charge that every thought requires a thinker behind it. Instead, I want the deeper conceptual tensions. For example, does the very act of doubting presuppose a continuous doubter who persists through the process, or can doubt be reconstructed as a sequence of discrete, unowned mental flashes? Does the certainty expressed in the conclusion carry an implicit temporal commitment—to the effect that the one who is now certain will still be present to remember that certainty—without which the judgment collapses into a mere instantaneous noise? I have been considering a scenario I call a cogito flash: a single moment of awareness arises with the content that thinking is occurring, followed immediately by a different awareness that falsely remembers having been the first one. If this sequence is genuinely coherent, does it undermine the minimal reading, or does it actually confirm that no persistent self was ever needed? And if the minimal interpreter responds by treating any talk of a subject or substance as a category mistake—projecting metaphysical structure onto a bare event—has the view been insulated into unfalsifiability, and is that insulation intellectually honest or merely defensive?

Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Say which ones defenders of the thin interpretation would themselves concede as damaging, and which ones they would dismiss. Also address whether this reading risks collapsing into a view closer to a bundle theory of the self that Descartes himself would reject, and whether such a collapse counts as a failure of the interpretation or as an honest consequence of taking his premises seriously. Be direct about whether the minimal cogito actually risks anything, or whether it has been qualified into safety.
