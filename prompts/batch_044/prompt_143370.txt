Draft the joint statement that an advocate of the view that, in rescue dilemmas, the number of people saved should not determine our choice—following the line of argument associated with John Taurek—and an advocate of the opposing view that numbers do count could both endorse after a sustained adversarial collaboration. Restrict attention to cases where resources are genuinely scarce, the groups are disjoint, and rescue requires a direct physical intervention rather than a distant allocation. Include in your analysis one specific non-standard variant: a scenario in which saving each group requires a separate, independently risky action with its own probability of failure, rather than a single decision that determines all outcomes at once. Present the result in two parts. First, a numbered list of agreed claims: these should cover the formal structure of the choice situation, the requirement of impartial treatment of individuals, the absence of special relationships between rescuer and rescued, and the fact that each person faces a comparable threat. Second, a disagreements table that states, in neutral wording acceptable to each side, the precise points of divergence—especially whether there is a moral reason to aggregate welfare across persons, whether equality of concern requires treating groups differently, and how risk distributes across independent rescue attempts. Identify the cruxes: the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party. For each crux, describe the concrete case or reasoning both sides accept as relevant, apply the non-standard variant above, and record in advance what each side predicts the correct judgment or action should be. End with a brief assessment of whether any portion of the disagreement is purely verbal—if so, specify which formulations generated the appearance of conflict without substantive divergence, and why.
