Here's the answer:

Descartes uses the evil demon to show that all sensory knowledge could be false, which is basically the same problem as underdetermination in science where two theories fit the same data. Both mean we can never really know anything. Putnam's argument in his 1981 essay proves that being a brain in a vat is literally impossible because the word vat wouldn't refer to a real vat if we were inside one. This means scientific theories are safe too, since if we were in a vat we couldn't talk about real electrons or dark matter. So the skeptical challenge is actually weaker for science because science relies on math and logic instead of senses, and predictive success lets us pick the right model, which solves underdetermination.
