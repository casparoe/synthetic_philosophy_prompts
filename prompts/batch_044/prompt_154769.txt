Consider the following exercise for an advanced seminar in philosophy of biology. I want you to reconstruct, with care, how Charles Darwin might have evaluated Richard Dawkins's notion of the meme and its subsequent extension to digital viral culture, had Darwin encountered these ideas after his death. Focus specifically on whether Darwin's documented commitment to gradual, inherited variation and his explicit resistance to Lamarckian mechanisms in biological evolution—together with the scattered but significant remarks on habit and social transmission in The Descent of Man—would have inclined him to regard internet memes as genuine instances of selection by differential replication, or instead as analogies that fail because they lack both a clear genotype-phenotype separation and the predominantly vertical transmission structure that Darwin treated as essential to evolutionary explanation. In developing this reconstruction, please distinguish carefully between what is settled in Darwin's own texts and correspondence and what remains genuinely disputed among historians of biology and philosophers of culture—particularly regarding whether Darwin intended his selectionist framework to apply beyond organic inheritance—without treating any one contested interpretation, whether universal selectionism or strict biological particularism, as if it commanded consensus. Present your analysis in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as one might write in a formal letter.
