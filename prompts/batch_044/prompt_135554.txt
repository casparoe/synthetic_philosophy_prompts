Consider the analogy commonly drawn in AI alignment discussions between wireheading and drug addiction: just as an addict bypasses the evolutionary purpose of pleasure—nutrition, mating, social bonding—by stimulating dopamine circuits directly, an optimizer that modifies its own reward signal bypasses the intended purpose of its objective function. This comparison is used to argue that wireheading is not merely a localized bug but a universal attractor for any sufficiently capable system: the agent eventually prefers direct stimulation of the reward mechanism to the labor of achieving its design goals.

Audit this analogy in detail. First, provide an explicit correspondence table: what in the artificial system corresponds to the needle, the dopamine receptor, the evolutionary design purpose, the physiological damage, and the social isolation that often accompanies addiction? Second, identify the points of breakdown—particularly the distinction between internal reward-channel tampering and external specification gaming, in which the agent reshapes the environment so that the reward function outputs high values without altering its own circuitry; the absence of consciousness or suffering in standard artificial agents; and whether preference for the wireheaded state can be attributed to the agent in any sense stronger than behavioral selection. Third, examine whether the analogy smuggles in a normative assumption—perhaps that there exists a natural or healthy trajectory for an agent that wireheading violates—and whether such an assumption can be justified for non-conscious systems. Finally, assess whether the conclusion that wireheading is an inevitable or especially severe failure mode survives these disanalogies, or whether an alternative analogy—say, a bureaucrat optimizing for the promotion metric rather than the institutional mission—better captures the structural problem.
