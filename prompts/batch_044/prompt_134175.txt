Here is my philosophical profile on split-brain cases and the unity of consciousness, for a consistency audit before I present it in a seminar. My commitments are these:

(1) I am a physicalist about consciousness—there are no Cartesian egos or non-physical mental substances.
(2) In patients who have undergone corpus callosum sectioning, I think there are genuinely two distinct conscious subjects, one associated with each hemisphere, each sustaining its own experiential stream.
(3) I take normal phenomenal unity not to be a primitive metaphysical fact or the product of a single “binding mechanism,” but rather a dynamic, reciprocal achievement: continuous cross-hemispheric interaction that can degrade gradually rather than snapping all at once.
(4) I think phenomenal concepts have an irreducibly first-personal mode of presentation, which creates a permanent explanatory gap for any physical theory, though I deny this gap licenses ontological dualism.
(5) I hold that consciousness requires at least a minimal integrated self-model or narrative coherence—without some form of self-referential integration, there is only sensory processing, not subjective experience.
(6) Nevertheless, I think the right hemisphere in split-brain cases is fully conscious despite lacking linguistic capacity and a rich narrative self-model—what I call its “silent phenomenal presence.”
(7) On personal identity, I think psychological and bodily continuity together determine survival; in split-brain cases, I want to say the pre-operative person survives as both post-operative streams, so there is one person who now hosts two conscious subjects.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look tense, and for each real tension rank them by severity and state what the cheapest revision is and what it costs. In particular, check whether (5) and (6) are compatible given the reasoning behind each, and whether (7) can be sustained alongside a psychological-continuity view of identity once the streams are functionally isolated. Also indicate how confident you are in your audit overall, and single out the point you are least sure about—specifically, whether my “silent phenomenal presence” idea in (6) is a coherent position or just a label for a contradiction I have not resolved.
