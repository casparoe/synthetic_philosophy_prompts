I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about social construction, going by my gut on cases, and I keep circling the same tension. Here are my verdicts.

Bitcoin and cowrie shells in thier respective communities are both money — same kind, different tokens. If everyone stopped accepting Bitcoin it wouldn't be money anymore, but that feels like a feature of the kind, not a bug.

The US racial category "Black" shaped by the one-drop rule and the UK census category "Black British" feel like different kinds altogether, not the same kind with different borders. Ancestry is real, but the kind itself is made by laws and social practice.

Gender: I think "woman" in 1950s America and "woman" in Japan today are basically the same kind, just with different roles and expectations attached. But "hijra" in South Asia and "two-spirit" in some Indigenous North American communities dont feel like local versions of "non-binary" — they seem like genuinely different kinds with thier own histories and recognition conditions.

Then there's "refugee" under the 1951 Convention. This feels different. Forced displacement is real whether or not the UN labels it, so "refugee" seems more like a bureaucratic status applied to a pre-existing condition, not a kind constituted by the label itself.

But "credit score" creates something like a kind — "high-risk borrower" — through measurement and institutional response. People change behavior because of the score, and institutions treat them differently. Still, it feels thinner than gender or race, more like a temporary badge that loops back but doesnt have the same ontological weight.

The pair I cant reconcile: I want to say money is universal (all societies have some form of it) while race is locally specific (US Blackness is not Brazilian negritude). But I also want to say gender is universal ("woman" travels) except when it clearly doesnt (hijra, two-spirit). So is universality the marker of a "real" kind vs a constructed one? That cant be right, because I still think gender is constructed. At the same time, racial health disparities — like Black maternal mortality in the US — feel structurally embedded in a way that wouldnt vanish overnight if people stopped believing in race. But if money belief vanished, money would vanish. So are there two different kinds of "social construction" here — one belief-dependent and one structure-embedded — or am I just special-pleading for race?

Is there one principle, or maybe a small family of them, that sorts these into "full social kind," "bureaucratic status," "universal kind with local expression," and "looping metric"? What does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on — whether "autism" as defined by DSM-5 criteria is a social kind constructed through diagnostic looping and institutional response, or just a natural neurological kind we discovered and named? And be honest about which of my verdicts the principle forces me to drop — probably the idea that gender is universal while race is purely local, or maybe that money and race differ in kind rather than just in mechanism?
