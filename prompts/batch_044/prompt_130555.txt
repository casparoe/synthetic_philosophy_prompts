Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of valid consent must satisfy, with particular attention to how deception, coercion, and competence interact at the margins rather than in the textbook center. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or distinction behind it: for instance, consent secured through technically true but deliberately misleading framing that exploits the consenter’s reasonable inferences; consent given with full cognitive competence but within an institutional structure that systematically suppresses alternatives without any explicit threat; consent to an act that was genuinely understood at the time but whose downstream consequences were structurally unforeseeable in ways that bear on whether the original act was the right object of consent; and the difference between a consent act being initially invalid versus undergoing a kind of “decay” over time—losing normative force because of a change in the consenter’s epistemic or volitional state that does not amount to formal revocation. Specify which requirements are non-negotiable (e.g., the distinction between invalid consent and mere post-hoc regret must be preserved; competence must be assessed relative to the specific decision, not global rationality) and which are camp-dependent preferences (e.g., whether consent requires full specificity of every foreseeable sub-step, or whether broad anticipatory consent can be valid). Identify any subset of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—such as requiring both maximal informational disclosure and practical feasibility in time-pressured clinical or emergency contexts, or requiring both that coercion be defined subjectively (by the consenter’s felt pressure) and objectively (by the presence of a credible threat)—and indicate whether the tension is resolvable by contextual indexing or is principled. You may briefly indicate how information-centered, voluntariness-centered, and relational-autonomy accounts fare against your list, but keep the weight on the specification itself rather than on scoring. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to particular texts.
