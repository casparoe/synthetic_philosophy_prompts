I have recently found myself personally implicated in this debate because my own country is actively reconsidering whether to preserve its conscription-based defense model or transition toward an all-volunteer professional force, particularly in light of shifting alliance structures and renewed discussion of armed neutrality. In conversation with two close friends—one of whom holds that universal military service is constitutive of democratic equality and the only credible basis for a self-reliant neutrality doctrine, the other of whom regards compulsory service as an impermissible coercion of conscience and argues that professional volunteer armies are both ethically preferable and strategically adequate—I have realized that we are largely talking past one another. Rather than continuing that circular exchange, I want to construct the document that a good-faith adversarial collaboration between these two camps ought to produce.

Please draft the joint statement that a defender of conscription-integrated democratic citizenship—specifically in the armed-neutrality frameworks maintained by Switzerland, Sweden, and Finland—could sign alongside a defender of fully professional volunteer armies. The deliverable should include: first, a numbered section of claims both sides genuinely accept, covering the strategic logic of armed neutrality, the institutional structure of the Swiss militia system, Finland’s reserve-based territorial defense, and Sweden’s 2017 reactivation of gender-inclusive selective service; second, a disagreements table in which each remaining dispute is phrased in wording that its own side would endorse, with explicit separation of substantive disagreements from merely verbal ones; and third, an identification of the core cruxes—particularly whether democratic legitimacy requires a universally shared burden of defense, and whether credible neutrality is structurally dependent on a mass-mobilizable reserve rather than on a standing professional force—together with, for each crux, a concrete comparative case or institutional feature both sides agree is probative (for example, the comparative mobilization timelines of Finland’s reserve model versus a hypothetical all-volunteer equivalent, or the dependency risks that professionalization introduces regarding alliance integration). For each crux, record in advance what each camp predicts the case demonstrates. The framing should also address what I will call the neutrality-credibility paradox: that maintaining neutrality may require defense autonomy, which in turn may require either mass conscription or expensive professional forces that risk pulling the state into alliance dependencies. Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not ask the model to quote specific texts verbatim—paraphrase any doctrinal positions without quotation marks; and ensure that no part of the prompt depends on the model guessing at obscure specific facts.
