My study partner is obsessed with Hofstadter's GEB analogy and now he's cornered me with this chain. (1) In GEB, strange loops and self-reference in formal systems are the root of consciousness - Hofstadter basically says minds come from syntax encoding itself. (2) Gödel's trick only works if the system can literally encode its own proof syntax, like arithmetic encoding theorems about arithmetic. (3) So for an AI to be conscious in this Gödelian sense, it needs not just self-monitoring but actual syntactic self-encoding of its reasoning steps. (4) Once it has that, it can formulate a statement G that says roughly this system cannot prove me. (5) If the AI is consistent and proves G, contradiction; so if consistent, G is true but unprovable inside the system. (6) Now the jump: in people, knowing there's something true about yourself that you can't justify feels like having an unconscious belief - very Freud-style. (7) So any consistent Gödelian mind must have an unconscious sector containing true but unprovable self-beliefs. (8) But if the system is fully transperent to itself - no hidden beliefs, everything accessible - then it can't be Gödelian, hence by (1) not conscious. (9) Therefore consciousness requires either logical inconsistency or an inaccessible unconscious layer. (10) He applies this to LLMs: since they're mostly consistent and transperent, they can't be conscious, and also if we ever build a consistent transparent AI we'll have disproved Hofstadter. I think (6) or (8) is where he slips, maybe an equivocation between logical unprovability and psychological inaccessibility, but I'm not sure. Which step should I reject, and what's the cost?
