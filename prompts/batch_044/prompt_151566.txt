I am drafting the final examination for an upper-level undergraduate seminar in philosophy of mind. Write one essay question on panpsychism and the combination problem that cannot be answered by reciting the standard subject-summing objection or a familiar reply. Centre the question on the following constructed variant, which you may term quality-structuralism: the panpsychist denies that micro-subjects exist at all, and holds instead that macro-experience arises solely from the structural organisation of micro-qualities that are themselves non-subjective. Require students to assess whether this genuinely dissolves the combination problem or merely relocates it. They must address: (i) whether eliminating micro-subjects removes the aggregation difficulty or reproduces it for qualities; (ii) whether structural organisation can explain phenomenal properties without being causal, given that panpsychists typically reject causal emergence for consciousness; and (iii) how the view interacts with the palette problem — the concern that macro-phenomenal qualities (e.g., the specific hue of grass) lie outside the restricted qualitative repertoire available to micro-experiences. Supply the essay question, a model answer of approximately 800 words that reaches a clear verdict, and a marking scheme out of 20 marks comprising five analytical moves (each worth up to 4 marks). For each move, specify the insight required and the characteristic error that should reduce the mark. Use formal British English, and do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
