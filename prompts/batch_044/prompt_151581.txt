I sit on the acquisitions committee of a regional science museum. We must rule on a proposal for a temporary exhibit titled "Parallel Timelines" that presents Anatoly Fomenko's New Chronology—his statistical argument that major ancient civilizations and events are phantom duplicates of medieval history—using symmetrical display panels that place conventional archaeological chronology and Fomenko's compressed chronology side-by-side without labeling either as established or pseudohistorical. The curator argues this format teaches visitors to weigh evidence; the board's science advisor warns that symmetrical presentation falsely implies epistemic parity, violating our institutional demarcation standard that distinguishes legitimate revisionism (testable, peer-reviewed, responsive to anomaly) from pseudohistory (post-hoc pattern matching that evades falsification). Draft the committee's formal decision memo recommending approval, rejection, or conditional approval with specific modifications and a concrete implementation timeline. Give an honest account of the strongest consideration favoring the opposing view, invoke the relevant demarcation criteria explicitly, and take a committed position with clear reasoning rather than balancing both sides neutrally. Do not request citations, reading lists, or bibliographic references.
