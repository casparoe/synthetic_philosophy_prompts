I have recently become my sister's primary interpreter for her augmentative communication device after a brainstem stroke left her with locked-in syndrome. Observing the system generate sentences that anticipate her preferences—while knowing its weights hold no intrinsic aboutness—has made the distinction between original and derived intentionality feel personally urgent rather than merely theoretical. Write a formal dialogue between two philosophers of mind, both fully competent, debating whether such a device can ever be said to mean its outputs in the same sense a biological agent does. One should defend Searle's position that original intentionality is an irreducible biological primitive and that derived intentionality is always parasitic on a prior intrinsic source; the other should defend Dennett's position that derived intentionality is fully autonomous and that the design stance reveals original intentionality itself to be a useful fiction—explicitly invoking the logic of the two-bitser response to show how predictive architectures can carry intentional content without biological origin. Rather than rehearsing the Chinese Room or standard syntax-semantics objections, have them examine a specific, novel conceptual case: an adaptive home-climate system—call it a thermal loop—that modifies its own target temperatures not through stored representations but through direct, continuous integration of occupant discomfort signals that restructure its internal dynamics without symbolic intermediaries. Each interlocutor should address whether this loop possesses derived intentionality, whether it reveals original intentionality to be unnecessary, or whether it demonstrates the limits of both categories. The exchange must contain genuine back-and-forth: each speaker should respond to the other's specific points rather than talk past them, and neither should be reduced to a strawman.
