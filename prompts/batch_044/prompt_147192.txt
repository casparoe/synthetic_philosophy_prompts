I'm on the review board for our university's Interdisiplinary Science Fund, and we've got a weird one. A team of ecologists and philosophers applied for a major grant to build what they're calling a "constraint-closure" modeling center — the core idea is that in some wetland ecosystems the macro-level hydrological patterns don't just emerge from plant-level interactions, they actually close the causal loop and constrain which micro-interactions are physically possible in the first place. So they're arguing for "macro-first" funding: study the big-scale patterns first, and treat bottom-up reduction as a secondary check rather than the primary method. Half the board thinks this is just mysticism with math and wants to desk-reject anything that doesn't start with agent-based decomposition, while my co-chair wants a formal memo for next week's vote saying what our policy should be — not just on this grant, but on whether the fund should allow proposals that reject reductionist methodology as a matter of principle.

I need you to draft that memo. It should have a clear reccomendation — approve with conditions, reject, or create a seperate track? — plus the reasoning for it, including what "constraint-closure emergence" actually commits us to if we take it seriously, and an honest treatment of the strongest reductionist objection, probably about explanatory power or empirical testability. Also include concrete criterie reviewers can actually apply when they see this kind of claim in future proposals, like how to tell the difference between a genuine inter-level constraint and just a convenient excuse to avoid hard mechanistic work. Don't ask for citations or reading lists — I need this readable for board members who won't chase down papers. And please don't just rehash the standard "emergence vs reduction" debate from the 1990s; treat the constraint-closure idea as a live conceptual option, not as something to be dismissed out of hand. Oh, and I noticed a typo in the proposal's appendix — they wrote "causal closure" when they meant "constraint closure" — but don't let that confuse the memo's analysis.
