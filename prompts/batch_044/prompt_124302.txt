I've been drafting a novel about a welfare inspector who slowly realizes that 'benefit fraud' is mostly a fiction the state needs to keep the story straight. So here's the position I'm toying with: the category of the 'undeserving poor' isn't a design flaw in welfare—it's structurally load-bearing. Without conditionallity and moral sorting, the deserving poor lose their symbolic contrast, and universal support becomes politically impossible. Workfare isn't about efficiency; it's about maintaining that boundary. What would actually count against this? Not just 'that's cynical,' but which kinds of historical welfare regimes worked without moral sorting, what patterns in fraud data would undermine the 'myth' framing, and where does the logic actually break? Rank the defeaters from fatal to just awkward. Would defenders concede any, or absorb them? And does this collapse into saying welfare states are propaganda machines—is that a problem or just the point?
