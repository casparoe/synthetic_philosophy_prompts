Having retired two years ago from experimental condensed-matter physics, I have been reading philosophy of mind without the discipline of a formal curriculum, and I find that my intuitions about causation are still shaped more by physical dynamics than by metaphysical convention. I would like you to help me locate the precise fault lines in my thinking by giving me a decomposed credence.

Consider this claim: non-reductive physicalism is true, mental properties are genuinely realized by complex neural dynamics, and yet mental properties are causally inert with respect to behavior—but only on the condition that the underlying physicalism involves what I will call strict local supervenience, meaning that every mental state is fully determined by the instantaneous microphysical state of the brain at that exact time slice, with no independent contribution from the system’s temporal trajectory or organizational history. If we instead adopt historical organizational supervenience—where the mental state’s causal profile depends essentially on the self-organizing path that produced it, such that the instantaneous micro-state alone is not locally sufficient—then the standard causal exclusion argument may collapse, because the physical base itself is no longer a closed, locally sufficient cause.

What is your credence, expressed as a specific number between 0 and 1, that mental causation survives the exclusion argument under this framing? Please decompose your answer into at least these terms: (a) the probability that strict local supervenience is the correct interpretation of physicalist commitment, rather than a historical or organizational variant; (b) the probability that, if local supervenience does hold, mental properties are genuinely excluded rather than merely redundant or overdetermined in a benign way; (c) the probability that historical organizational supervenience is physically coherent and not merely a redescription of the microphysical trajectory over time; and (d) one further factor you judge essential—perhaps whether counterfactual dependence on macro-patterns can ground genuine causation even when micro-causes are present. Assign a rough probability to each term, indicate approximately how they combine into your final figure, and identify which term you are least confident in and what kind of conceptual or empirical finding would move it most. For reference, my own uncalibrated intuition puts the overall figure near 0.55, though I suspect my disagreement with standard treatments may live mainly in term (a) or term (c) rather than in the logic of exclusion itself.
