Case E: I don't think we have any duty to create brand new happy people, even if that'd mean infinite happiness never exists. There's an asymmetry between bringing-in and reducing-suffering that I can't shake.

Case F (the one I keep reversing): A policy saves 1000 people now with 100% certainty, but has a 1-in-10^30 risk of destroying all future life -- an infinite loss of value. My first instinct is reject the policy, but that feels like it conflicts with Case B, where I accepted near-certainty of infinite gain at the cost of one life. What's different?

Is there a coherent principle here?
