A. I support replacing the lower legislative chamber with a standing citizens' assembly selected by lot, structured around rotating deliberative juries of 150 citizens serving staggered four-year terms, with full legislative initiative and no party affiliation—essentially extending the Athenian boule's rotation principle to modern scale.

B. I reject term limits for elected executives (presidents, governors). They sever the retrospective feedback loop between governance and voter judgment, substituting an arbitrary calendar for democratic accountability.

C. The corruption of career politics is best understood through the lens of political capitalism—representatives accumulate personal electoral capital that decouples them from constituent interests—rather than as a problem of tenure per se. Therefore the remedy should target capital-accumulation mechanisms, not length of service.

D. To prevent capital accumulation inside the assembly, members should serve a single, non-renewable twelve-year term, with one-quarter rotating out every three years. This preserves institutional memory without allowing careerism.

E. Democratic legitimacy requires authorization: citizens must be able to select and remove the specific agents who make binding rules, which can only occur through periodic competitive elections for bodies with lawmaking power.

F. To preserve authorization without reintroducing careerism, I propose a small, directly elected consent chamber with a suspensive veto and no term limits, serving as the accountable elected agent, while the assembly remains the primary legislator.
