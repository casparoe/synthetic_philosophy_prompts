I'm trying to figure out whether guilt or shame is the "better" moral emotion, and whether a culture can just choose one. Here are my gut verdicts. Public shaming for tax evasion feels appropriate; private restitution with guilt feels right. If a friend feels deep shame for being late to dinner, that's emotional miscalibration — guilt at worst, probably nothing. But when a culture deliberately abolishes shame for adultery and replaces it with negotiated restitution, I think something valuable is lost, though I can't say exactly what. A synthetic commune that engineers away shame entirely and runs on pure guilt-tracking algorithms sounds dystopian, yet I can't tell if that's just an aesthetic reaction. The pair I can't square: shame feels wrong for breaking a personal diet goal, but it feels okay — even good — for skipping a communal mourning practice you're part of. Is there one principle behind all this, and which verdict do I have to drop? Also, what should I think about a culture that actively trains people to feel shame for private thoughts they never act on? That's the case I keep fliping on.
