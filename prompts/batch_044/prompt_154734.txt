Design a 90-minute upper-level undergraduate essay exam in the philosophy of archaeological inference. The question must address how material objects can justify attributions of collective belief and institutional structure, and it must not be answerable by summarizing standard methodological debates. To prevent generic recitation, embed an invented case study: the hypothetical settlement of Kharos-Delta, where burial assemblages combine regionally foreign iconography with locally standardized metrological weights. Ask students to adjudicate between two reconstructions — (a) a syncretic ritual institution integrating foreign cosmology into local practice, versus (b) a bureaucratic redistribution network that deploys standardized measures and uses religious display as purely legitimizing ornament — and require them to identify the precise inferential bridge, or gap, between observed object-patterns and claims about shared doctrine or collective intentionality. They must also show where either reconstruction risks circularity (using the postulated institution to explain the objects while using the objects to confirm the institution) or overdetermination (the same material pattern supporting incompatible mental-state attributions).

Provide:
1. The exam question itself, stating the expected response length (approximately 800 words) and naming the conceptual crux.
2. A model answer of about 600 words that demonstrates the specific moves required: isolating the institution-to-belief gap; proposing a non-circular criterion for distinguishing evidence of shared practice from evidence of shared doctrine; assessing whether standardized weights necessarily entail bureaucratic intentionality or might instead reflect ritual reciprocity; and explaining how hybrid iconography remains underdetermined with respect to cosmological commitment versus political legitimation.
3. A marking scheme totaling 20 marks, divided among five specific analytical moves with the marks for each specified. For each move, include a one-line note on the characteristic failure that should cost marks — for instance, conflating coordinated behavior with common belief, treating the invented site as if it were a documented historical case requiring citation, or resolving the underdetermination by fiat rather than by analyzing auxiliary assumptions.

Keep the prompt formal and self-contained. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or verification of specific historical texts or dates, and do not depend on knowledge of any obscure named theory.
