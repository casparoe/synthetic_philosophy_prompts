Now everyone talks about 'epistemic luck' like that's the magic key. But when I press people in my reading group, half seem to think Gettier proves justification has to be externally anchored or factive, and the other half think the whole mess comes from an internalist bias about what 'having reasons' even means.

Here's what I think is happening, and I want you to tell me if I'm wrong. There's this split people make between intervening luck and environmental luck, and then there's the further split between modal-safety folks — who say knowledge requires that the belief couldn't easily have been false — and the achievement crowd, who say knowledge is true belief because of cognitive competence. But then critics build fake-barn cases where belief is safe but still Gettiered, or they ask whether the competence was actually exercised or just accidentally present. So my working theory is that the stall isn't about knowledge at all, but a proxy war between internalists and externalists about justification, with a hidden layer about whether epistemic evaluation should be modal or achievement-based. Is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one about 'knowledge', or are people answering different questions — one about analysis, one about justification, one about luck taxonomy — under one heading? Diagnose it using what each side actually says about cases like the stopped clock or fake barns, tell me what hidden empirical or formal question might be doing the work, and be honest: is there anything — a new distinction, a formal result on belief revision, whatever — that could actually end it, or is the impasse a sign the question is ill-posed?
