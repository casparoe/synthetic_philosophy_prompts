I'm working on an essay for my philosophy elective about whether personal identity can be reduced to narrative coherence, and I've pushed Dennett's idea of the self as a center of narrative gravity into an extreme case I'm calling "narrative fusion." I can't find the flaw, but the conclusion feels wrong. Here is the argument.

(1) If being a particular self consists only in being the center of narrative gravity, then identity conditions are fixed by the coherence of a single first-person story, not by bodily or psychological continuity per se.

(2) A story can be logically and causally coherent even if it integrates experiences that originally belonged to two different bodies—imagine two people who undergo a perfect memory-merging and emotional-integration procedure so that there is now one seamless autobiography told from a unified "I."

(3) So narrative gravity allows that two selves can fuse into one without contradiction, provided the resulting narrative is unified.

(4) By the same reasoning, if one person's narrative cleanly bifurcates into two equally continuous and non-interacting streams—say, after a hypothetical perfect division of consciousness—then one self becomes two.

(5) But if selves can merge and split like this, then standard practices of moral and legal responsibility collapse: if fused self F, composed of prior individuals B and C, later commits a wrong, we cannot determine whether B, C, or F alone should be held accountable; and if D splits into E and F, we cannot determine which half inherits D's obligations.

(6) Since moral and legal responsibility requires that selves be bounded in a way that survives fusion and fission, the narrative-gravity account must be missing something essential.

Therefore, treating the self as nothing but a center of narrative gravity dissolves the boundaries needed for practical life. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost me? If I want to keep (1) and (5), am I forced to deny (3) or (4), and can I do that without making narrative coherence arbitrary?
