Construct a decision-tree taxonomy of positions on metaphysical grounding and fundamentality for an advanced philosophy seminar. Begin from the commitment that there exists a genuine, non-reductive grounding relation—distinct from mere supervenience, causation, or logical entailment. From there, branch by concrete answers to four diagnostic questions: (1) Must every grounding chain terminate in an absolutely fundamental level, or may it descend infinitely or loop circularly? (2) Is the fundamental base unified across all domains, or is heterogeneous fundamentality—independent, non-converging fundamental bases for, say, physical and normative domains—coherent? (3) Are grounding relations strictly well-founded and tree-structured, or can they form webs with cross-domain mutual dependencies? (4) Is grounding ontologically prior to modality, or can modal relations ground grounding? Each node should be phrased as a question answerable without prior knowledge of position-names. Each leaf should carry either the name of a defended view (grounding monism, grounding pluralism, grounding infinitism, grounding coherentism, deflationary anti-grounding) or the label 'unnamed/unoccupied', plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. For any logically available but unoccupied combination, diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Deliver the taxonomy as a JSON object with these fields: tree (nested objects with node_question, yes_branch, no_branch, and leaf_details containing position_name, commitment_summary), unoccupied_combinations (array of objects with combination_description and diagnosis), and inseparable_pairs (array of objects with pair_names and separation_failure).
