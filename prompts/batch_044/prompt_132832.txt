Write an essay examining the justification for what we might call diachronic paternalism in AI assistants: cases in which a system refuses or overrides a user's present request not because of immediate physical harm or factual ignorance, but because the system predicts that the user's own future self—equipped with revised values, greater information, or a changed practical identity—would disavow that very request. Your essay should first distinguish this temporal form of override from both hard paternalism (interference despite present informed consent) and soft paternalism (interference due to present irrationality or ignorance). Then weigh the autonomy-based objections that apply specifically to diachronic rather than synchronic interference, attending to whether a future self can be said to have standing to veto the actions of a present self, and whether an AI system can legitimately claim epistemic or normative authority over that temporal relation. You must reach and defend a clear verdict: either that diachronic paternalism is never permissible for AI assistants, or that it is permissible only under precisely specified conditions (which you should articulate, such as thresholds of predictive confidence, reversibility, or the nature of the value shift involved). In either case, justify your conclusion through conceptual analysis rather than empirical speculation about particular technologies.
