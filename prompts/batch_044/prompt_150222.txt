Read Denis Villeneuve’s film *Arrival* (2016), adapted from Ted Chiang’s “Story of Your Life,” through the lens of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe. The heptapods teach Louise Banks a language that lets her experience future events—her daughter’s life and death—as present impressions, and she ultimately chooses to bring that child into being despite knowing the suffering ahead. Rather than treating this as a generic determinism-versus-free-will parable, examine whether the film’s metaphysics aligns with the specific eternalist reasoning drawn from special relativity: does Louise’s “simultaneous” awareness of past, present, and future correspond to the denial of an objective present moment that Rietdijk and Putnam derive from the conventionality of simultaneity, or does it smuggle in a privileged “now” that travels along her worldline? Where does the narrative resist the eternalist reading (for instance, in the emotional weight of her “choice” to embrace grief), and where does it inadvertently reproduce a confusion between epistemic access to a completed timeline and ontological co-existence of all times?
