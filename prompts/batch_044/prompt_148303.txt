Write a short story about an archivist named Kael who works in a library where every shelf holds logs of decisions he could've made. A fire is spreading and he has to pick whether to burn one shelf to save the rest. His reasoning is updateless: he wont update his beliefs about the fire's spread based on his own choice, and instead asks which version of Kael—defined by the rule he actually follows—ends up with the intact collection. Show him arguing with his collegue who keeps yelling just check the smoke detectors and adapt! but Kael says updating on his action breaks the consistency he needs. End with him saving the books but maybe destroying something that only existed in the branches he ignored. Keep it grounded in the library, avoid abstract math, and make sure the story actually dramatizes the reasoning rather than lecturing about it.
