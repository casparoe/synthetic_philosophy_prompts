ok so i've been chewing on hofstadter's idea that the 'i' is just a pattern and if you copy it well enough into another brain or computer then that's you too. and it got me into this weird argument that i think proves too much but i can't find the leak. here it is:

(1) if the self is just an information pattern, then identity is continuity of that pattern, not tied to one body.
(2) but patterns come in degrees - there's no magical line where 99.9% similarity counts and 99.8% doesn't.
(3) people who know me well - my partner, my sister - have neural patterns that predict me, mirror my habits, hold my memories. call this sympathetic encoding.
(4) so right now there are partial copies of my self-pattern running in other brains, maybe at 30% or 40% fidelity.
(5) if pattern-continuity is what makes me me, then those partial copies are partial me's. i am literally distributed across those heads right now.
(6) but if i'm partly in my partner's brain, then damage to her brain should feel like damage to me, and i should have some first-person access to her thoughts about me.
(7) obviously i don't. so either (1) is false, or partial patterns don't count.
(8) but if partial patterns don't count, we need a sharp fidelity boundry for "real" copying.
(9) any boundry is arbitary - why 95%? why not 94.9? - and hofstadter's whole point was to avoid arbitary biological boundaries.
(10) so we're stuck: either i'm already scattered across everyone who knows me (which is nuts), or we need an arbitrary cutoff that kills the pattern view.

which step is the bad one? i really want to keep (1) because it feels right that i'm not just this meat. but maybe (3) is smuggling in something? or is the mistake in thinking that being represented equals being instatiated? tell me which premise to drop and what dropping it costs - particulary whether it forces us back into a body-locked view of the self.
