Hey, I'm putting together a taxonomy exercise for my political theory grad seminar — we're doing Fukuyama's end-of-history thing and I want something that doesn't just ask "do you agree?" I'm trying to build a decision tree (or maybe a scoring quiz) that sorts people by what they actually think is going on, not by which label they slap on themselves. The idea is to start with whether you think Fukuyama is making a claim about state-forms running out of mutations, or about human self-understanding reaching a kind of conceptual ceiling — I wrote "telelogical" in my notes but I meant teleological — and then branch from there.

Specifically, I want nodes that ask concrete things, like: if a society developed a genuinely post-liberal form of governance that wasn't just fascism or communism but something structurally novel, would that count as disproving Fukuyama, or would he just say it's not a universalizable alternative? Or: is the "last man" problem a psychological prediction about people getting bored and nihilistic, or is it a misreading of Nietzsche that's doing work in the argument? I also want to inject a distinction I cooked up that I think gets lost in standard discussions: whether the "end" is best understood as an exhaustion of the ideological menu (no new recipes can be cooked) versus a saturation point where recipes exist but the political kitchen refuses to try them. Each branch should lead to a named position — Fukuyama's own liberal-democratic teleology, the Marxist "history continues via capitalism's contradictions" view, the postmodern "no grand narratives" skepticism, maybe a recursive-modernity view where liberal democracy keeps mutating internally, and whatever else belongs — with unoccupied combinations flagged as incoherent, ugly, or genuinely unexplored.

Please build this for grad students who know the basic thesis but aren't Fukuyama scholars, keep it to maybe 6-8 nodes, and make sure no question requires knowing the label in advance. Say how confident you are in the tree you build, and especially call out the branch you're least sure about — probably the one separating internal mutation from external replacement, since that feels slippery to me. And please don't include a bibliography or citation list; my students don't have external database access for this and I want them to reason from the concepts alone.
