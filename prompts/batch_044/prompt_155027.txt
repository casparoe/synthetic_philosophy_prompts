Over coffee with a colleague from our cognitive science department, I found myself defending a particular reading of the butterfly dream passage in the second chapter of the Zhuangzi—the familiar moment in which the narrator, upon waking from a dream of being a butterfly, confesses he cannot determine whether he is Zhuang Zhou who has just awakened from the dream or the butterfly now dreaming that it is Zhuang Zhou. I had initially presented this in my advanced seminar on comparative epistemology as a classical expression of dream skepticism, an Eastern anticipation of Descartes's first Meditation designed to undermine perceptual reliability by exposing the phenomenological indistinguishability of waking and dreaming states. My colleague, however, insisted that this framing imports a Cartesian concern with epistemic justification that is foreign to the text; she argued that the passage is better understood through the lens of ontological transformation, specifically the concept of wu-hua—the continuous metamorphosis of things—where the dissolution of the boundary between self and other is not a skeptical problem to be solved but a metaphysical feature to be embraced. While I am now persuaded that Zhuangzi's primary concern is not with securing knowledge but with describing a reality in which fixed identities are illusory, I remain troubled by a lingering tension: if the boundary between the knowing subject and the object of knowledge is genuinely porous in the way Zhuangzi describes, then any stable framework within which knowledge-claims could be evaluated seems to dissolve as well. Have I falsely presupposed that an ontological account of fluid identity must exclude a skeptical challenge to the coherence of the epistemic subject? Or is there a specific feature of the passage—perhaps the very structure of the question Zhuangzi poses about which identity is the real one—that demonstrates that his concern is not with justification at all, but with showing that the conditions for asking knowledge-questions have themselves collapsed?
