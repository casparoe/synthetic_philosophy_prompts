I am grading papers for a colleague’s graduate seminar on intergenerational ethics, and I need a precise, point-by-point evaluation that does not reveal my own view but clearly separates consensus from dispute.

The exam question was: “Consider a 2150 scenario in which Government X adopts Climate Policy Alpha, permitting high-emission agriculture that funds rapid medical advances. The resulting generation—Cohort A—lives lives worth living but with moderate respiratory impairment. Had Government X adopted Climate Policy Beta (strict decarbonization with slower growth), different genetic and social pairings would have produced Cohort B, with no impairment and slightly higher welfare. A policy analyst claims Alpha wrongs Cohort A members because each is worse off than the corresponding Beta-person they replace. Evaluate this claim using the nonidentity problem, and explain whether Parfit’s analysis gives the government any reason—person-affecting or otherwise—to prefer Beta.”

Here is one student’s answer: The student correctly states that Cohort A exists only under Alpha, so its members are not worse off than they would have been, since under Beta they would not exist at all; this captures the core nonidentity insight. The student then claims the case is structurally identical to Parfit’s wrongful-life examples, in which an actual child with a life worth living complains about being born with a disability. From this analogy, the student infers that because Cohort A members have lives worth living, they lack standing to complain, and therefore the government has no person-affecting reason to choose Beta. The student further asserts that Parfit rejects impersonal welfare aggregation across different populations and instead endorses a counterfactual replacement principle, which requires comparing actual individuals only to the specific counterparts they displace; since Cohort A members have no such counterparts under Beta, there is no meaningful basis for comparison, leaving the government indifferent between the policies except for effects on present people.
