I am a PhD student in philosophy working at the intersection of metaphysics and philosophy of mind, and I am preparing notes for an essay on Bradley’s regress in relation to the unity of the proposition. I have been discussing with colleagues whether Bradley’s argument—roughly, that treating relations as external to their terms generates an infinite regress of further relating relations—retains any force once modern accounts of mental representation are taken seriously. Rather than treating it as a purely historical puzzle, I want to explore whether the regress resurfaces inside the cognitive-scientific binding problem: the question of how disparate feature representations (color, shape, spatial location, and so on) are integrated into a unified percept or conceptual thought.

Here is the sketch of my argument. On standard representationalist views, some mechanism—perhaps temporal synchrony of neural firing, or a vector-symbolic architecture—serves to bind separate representations into a single propositional content. But if this binding mechanism is itself a representation that must stand in some relation to the representations it binds, then we seem to need a second-order binder, and then a third-order binder, reproducing Bradley’s structure inside the mind. One might reply, following Russell’s strategy, that binding is primitive and not itself a relation requiring further relating; but then, I worry, we have not explained propositional unity so much as renamed it as a brute psychological capacity. Alternatively, one might deny that the binding mechanism is representational at all, treating synchrony as a non-representational causal process; yet if it is non-representational, it is unclear how it could yield genuinely propositional unity rather than mere causal correlation. Thus I want to argue that Bradley’s regress functions not as an obsolete metaphysical curio but as a structural constraint: any representationalist account of the unity of the proposition must either face the regress or surrender its explanatory ambition by positing an unexplained primitive.

I am particularly unsure whether this analogy is genuinely illuminating or merely decorative. Does the cognitive binding problem actually instantiate the same logical structure as Bradley’s argument about external relations? Do contemporary dynamicist or predictive-processing accounts avoid the regress in ways I have overlooked? And am I being unfair to Russell’s account of relations—might his resources for “internal” relating actually map neatly onto representational binding without generating a vicious regress?
