I'm on the advisory board for a seafood certification nonprofit (got roped in cause I'm a software engineer and they said they needed a "systems thinker"). We have to vote Friday on whether our "Humane Harvest" seal should cover decapod crustaceans—lobsters, crabs, shrimp—and farmed Atlantic salmon, or stay limited to vertebrates with a backbone like our current rule.

Here's why it's sticky. Our science advisory group sent two reports pointing opposite directions. One says crustaceans have nociceptors and avoidance learning, so they feel pain in a morally relevant way. The other argues their nervous system is distributed across ganglia with no central brain, so what looks like suffering is really just local reflex loops—like a server shutting down without anyone inside. One of our advisors even floated a weirder idea: maybe crustaceans have "distributed sentience," meaning not one unified sufferer but a network of local aversive states, so stunning them quickly might actually be worse cause you're disrupting multiple nodes at once instead of one central consciousness. I don't know if that's legit science or philosophy cosplay, but it's the strongest argument against expanding our standard.

For salmon, there's a seperate debate about whether they have phenomenal consciousness or just sophisticated homeostatic tracking. Our biggest clients are Atlantic salmon farms, so whatever we decide hits them hard.

Half the board wants to expand the seal to all decapods and salmonids under a "functional equivalence" rule—if it behaves like pain, treat it as pain. The other half wants an "architectural criterion"—only cover species with centralized neural integration—because without clear binding we can't verify that humane slaughter actually reduces suffering, and compliance becomes impossible for small shrimp farms.

I need a memo for Thursday's meeting recommending either expansion, staying narrow, or a tiered middle path, with a concrete decision timeline and conditions. Take the "distributed sentience / no central binder" argument seriously—don't just dismiss it—since that's the strongest case on the losing side if we expand. Also address the salmon question specifically, since our clients depend on it. Keep it practical, no citations needed, just reasoning and a bottom line we can vote on. I definetly need the memo to say approve, reject, or approve with conditions, not just list pros and cons.
