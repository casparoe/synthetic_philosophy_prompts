A municipality selects its council by lot for single, non-renewable terms, imposes strict term limits on its directly elected mayors, yet runs a civil-service track open only to former councillors—who thereby become career-eligible administrators—while barring anyone with prior elected office from entering that track. The system therefore excludes career politicians from both legislature and executive, but deliberately transforms randomly chosen citizens into experienced officials who may later hold appointed power. What theory of democratic representation, competence, and the moral hazard of ambition does this hybrid presuppose? Does it hold that representation must remain amateur to be legitimate, whereas governance requires accumulated expertise that only tenure can provide, or that corruption attaches specifically to electoral incentives rather than to institutional power itself? Reconstruct the implicit commitments, distinguish what the design strictly requires from what merely accommodates it, and say whether the same premises could justify pure sortition or a fully professional bureaucracy.
