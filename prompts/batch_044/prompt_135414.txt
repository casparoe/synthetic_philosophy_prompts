I advise a municipal treasury office that is piloting a local accounting unit—call it a service-backed scrip—redeemable not for national currency but for concrete labor and maintenance: road repair, flood-barrier upkeep, food-distribution logistics, and artisanal repair services. We are attempting to avoid both speculative hoarding and inflationary over-issue, and a colleague has directed me to George Berkeley's theory of money, according to which coin or paper is not wealth in itself but a kind of ticket or counter that represents labor and commodities, such that to multiply the tickets without multiplying the goods they represent is to undermine the social trust on which the system depends. I need you to translate Berkeley's core commitments—especially that money tracks real productive capacity, that it operates as a convention sustained by collective confidence rather than as a commodity with intrinsic value, and that its issuance should not outpace the tangible assets and labor available to honor it—into a concrete, reusable decision procedure my staff can run without philosophical training.

Please structure it as an ordered set of questions: what must be established at each stage, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure terminates with a clear refusal, and where it must hand the decision to a person because the issue is one of values rather than of economic or institutional fact. The procedure should apply specifically to proposals about issuing new scrip, redeeming existing scrip for services, and accepting collateral to back the unit, and it should be honest about where simplification risks distorting Berkeley's original distinction between a token of value and value itself.

Then apply the procedure to two proposals we are actually considering: first, backing a new tranche of scrip with a portfolio of undeveloped municipal land whose market value is projected but not realized, and second, authorizing emergency redemption of scrip for climate-adaptation labor during a budget crisis in which the available labor pool is uncertain and the citizens' assembly that sets redemption priorities is divided over whether to privilege immediate disaster response over long-term infrastructure. Include one case in which the procedure yields an answer my office will not welcome, and clearly mark any step at which the procedure cannot eliminate judgment and a treasury officer must decide.
