Hey, I'm a retired ecologist—thirty years studying fungal networks—who got into philosphy of biology about six months ago. I'm sketching an essay comparing the etiological account (Wright-style: a trait's function is what it was selected for) with the causal-role account (Cummins-style: function is the contribution to some present system capacity). I think I understand the basic divide, but I want to check whether my own twist is coherent or just muddled.

The standard story goes like this. Hearts pump blood; that's their function on the etiological view because evolutionary history selected for pumping. On Cummins's view, if the heart started filtering waste and that kept the organism alive, filtering could become part of its function regardless of history, as long as it contributes to a system-level capacity.

My injection: in modular, clonal organisms—say an aspen grove or a mycelial mat—the same physical structure can shift roles over its lifespan. A root-like module might start absorbing nitrogen, then transition to carbon storage, then eventually be broken down for resources elsewhere. The evolutionary selection pressure likely acted on the absorptive role. But the current causal contribution might be storage or even structural scaffolding. So there's a "function lag" where the historical selection points one way and the present systemic contribution points another. I want to suggest that in these cases neither pure account works: the etiological view seems stuck with an ancestral role that isn't currently active, while the causal-role view picks up whatever is happening now, which can change seasonally. Maybe we need functions indexed to both historical context and present system context, but I'm not sure that's a real third option or just saying "it depends."

I'm particularly unsure about two things. First, am I misreading Cummins? Does his view allow a trait to have multiple simultaneous functions if it contributes to different capacities at once, or does he require some stability? Second, could the etiological theorist reply that selection actually favored phenotypic plasticity itself, so that shifting roles is exactly the selected effect? If so, my "function lag" collapses into the etiological view after all.
