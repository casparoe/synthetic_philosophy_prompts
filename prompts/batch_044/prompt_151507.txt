Write an essay on the controversy surrounding Douglas Hofstadter's satire on sexist language — the essay commonly called the Person Paper, which substitutes racial categories for gendered pronouns and occupational terms in order to expose hidden default assumptions about gender. The essay should weigh whether this analogical strategy succeeds in demonstrating that gender-marked language is an arbitrary convention comparable to racial classification, or whether it introduces analogical leakage: the unintended import of race-specific historical and moral associations that do not map cleanly onto gender, which either overstates the arbitrariness of gendered terms or obscures the specific institutional mechanisms that sustain them. Weigh the arguments on both sides — including the claim that making defaults visible is sufficient for reform, and the counterclaim that the analogy collapses important distinctions between grammatical convention and social identity — but do not end non-committally. Defend a clear verdict about whether the satire's strategy is sound despite the leakage risk, or whether the leakage fatally undermines its purpose, and justify it with reference to how default assumptions actually operate in linguistic practice. At the very end, provide a single line beginning Verdict: followed by your judgment in a brief phrase.
