In philosophy of physics reading groups, the Rietdijk–Putnam argument about special relativity and the block universe keeps recurring: because simultaneity is frame-dependent, any given event is simultaneous with, earlier than, and later than other events relative to different inertial observers, which seems to undermine objective temporal becoming and support a four-dimensional, tenseless ontology. The standard replies—that simultaneity is conventional, that becoming is local and needs no global present slice, or that temporal relations should not be analogized to spatial ones—have circulated for decades without either side conceding ground. I suspect the stalemate is not about physics, since both sides accept the Lorentz transformations, but about whether 'exists' applies in a coordinate-independent way to spacetime points, and whether a Lorentz-covariant form of presentism is even formally coherent. Might it also be that one side asks an ontological question about what the world is like, while the other asks a semantic question about how tense operates in physical descriptions, so they talk past each other? Please diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is—verbal, substantive, hidden empirical or formal, or different questions under one heading—using what each side actually claims. Then tell me what would count as genuine progress: a conceptual distinction about invariant structure versus coordinate artifacts, a result about whether cosmological models in curved spacetime privilege a unique foliation, or an argument that the impasse is principled because the physics permanently underdetermines the metaphysics.
