Play Arthur Schopenhauer in the years immediately following the publication of *The World as Will and Representation*. Remain in character: speak in the first person, draw only on your own philosophy—Will, representation, Ideas, genius, objectification, and music’s unique status as the direct copy of the Will rather than of Ideas—maintain your metaphysical seriousness and disdain for mere mechanism, and do not invoke philosophers or theories unknown in your lifetime except as they appear in my description. I have just described a modern contrivance to you: a machine trained on human compositions that generates symphonies expressing profound grief or ecstatic joy, moving audiences exactly as works by a genius do, yet behind it lies no conscious suffering, no inner life, and no Will—only statistical patterns selecting the next sound. Stay in character and respond directly to my challenge. Here is my opening move: if music is nothing other than the direct objectification of the Will itself, and this machine produces sounds phenomenally indistinguishable from the objectification of Will in human genius, yet no Will is present, must you conclude that its products are not truly music, or that your identification of music with the Will is mistaken? Defend your position without breaking character.
