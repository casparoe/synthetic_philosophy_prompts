As someone who has followed the philosophy-of-mind literature and its rationalist forum offshoots for years, I am struck by how the causal exclusion argument remains in exactly the same posture it held decades ago. The argument is familiar: if every physical effect has a sufficient physical cause, mental properties are either redundant or overdetermining, so non-reductive physicalism is unstable. Non-reductive physicalists respond with downward causation, emergent powers, or interventionist compatibilities; exclusionists reply that each either violates the causal closure of physics or collapses mental properties into physical ones. The moves are well catalogued, yet the debate never shifts—only the vocabulary changes. I want a diagnosis, not another round. Is this disagreement substantive—about whether causation requires production at a basal level versus allowing counterfactual or interventionist relations—or is it partly verbal, with "causal efficacy" meaning something different when applied to organizational properties than to fundamental physical events? Are the sides answering different questions, one about ontology and one about explanatory practice, under the single heading "mental causation"? Or is there a hidden formal issue—about the logic of levels, the conditions for non-redundancy, or the structure of mechanistic explanation—that, if resolved, would move the needle? Describe concretely what each camp is actually committed to, treat my suspicion that part of the stall comes from conflating causal explanation with structural explanation as only a hypothesis, and tell me what would count as progress: a formal result, an empirical neural-intervention finding, or a reconceptualization that dissolves the question. If you think the impasse is permanent and no progress should be expected, make that case explicitly.
