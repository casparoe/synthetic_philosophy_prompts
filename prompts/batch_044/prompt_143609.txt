In a study-group discussion about transformative experience, two members debated a hypothetical neural interface that permanently rewires emotional architecture, leaving the subject with values incommensurable with their prior self.

(1) Elena argued that rational choice requires comparing outcomes against one's present value system, but transformative change replaces that very system, so deciding is necessarily a blind leap, not a reasoned selection.

(2) Marcus replied that people routinely choose transformative paths—parenthood, emigration, conversion—by weighing testimony, narrative coherence, and second-order preferences, none of which require first-person foreknowledge.

(3) Elena countered that testimony is useless because the evaluating subject is replaced; relying on others' reports is like letting strangers vote on one's identity, violating the autonomy condition of rational choice.

(4) Marcus answered that Elena's standard paralyzes all long-term planning, since any sustained commitment reshapes preferences; rationality demands only projected continuity of the decider, not preservation of every current value.

(5) Elena insisted Marcus conflates ordinary preference drift with metric transformation: when the scale by which goods are weighed itself changes, projected continuity is a category error, not a justification.
