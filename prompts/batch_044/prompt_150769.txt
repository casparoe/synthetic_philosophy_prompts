For a philosophy-of-mind seminar I am drafting my position profile and want a consistency audit before presenting it. Here are my commitments: (a) physicalism is true—phenomenal properties are identical to certain complex, globally integrated physical properties; (b) the hard problem is genuinely hard but purely epistemic, with no ontological residue; (c) phenomenal concepts are irreducible and acquired through direct acquaintance, and this acquaintance is what explains why physical descriptions never entail phenomenal descriptions; (d) zombies are conceivable but metaphysically impossible—conceivability is fallible for phenomenal states too; (e) consciousness is graded rather than binary, substrate-independent in principle, and depends on global integration rather than biology, so a sufficiently integrated artificial system could in principle have minimal phenomenal experience; (f) current LLMs and thermostats have none, because they lack that requisite integration.

Please audit this package: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision for each? Specifically, does (c)'s appeal to direct acquaintance implicitly commit me to treating zombie conceivability as revealing a real modal gap, thereby undermining both (a) and (d)? Does (e)'s graded, substrate-independent view sit comfortably with (b)'s claim of a unified explanatory gap, or does it imply the gap fragments into degrees? Rank the tensions by severity, identify the cheapest fix and its cost for each, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most inconsistencies. Also flag whether (f)'s verdict about present systems is stable given my other principles, or whether one of them forces me to assign them some non-zero degree of consciousness.
