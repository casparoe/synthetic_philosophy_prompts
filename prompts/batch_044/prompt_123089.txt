I am delivering the commencement address in June for the joint graduating class of the Department of Cognitive Ethology and the Department of Philosophy. Roughly half the audience will be specialists who recognize the reference immediately; the other half are family members and guests with no background in the field. The assigned theme is "breaking the loop," and I want the concept of sphexishness—the mechanical rigidity observed in the wasp Sphex ichneumoneus, which repeats its burrow-checking ritual even when conditions have clearly changed—to do real structural work rather than serve as a decorative metaphor. Specifically, the speech must navigate between two interpretations the faculty have asked me to avoid: one that treats sphexishness as proof that all behavior is mechanically determined, and one that treats any deviation from rigidity as evidence of an immaterial soul. I need to say something meaningful to both audiences about flexibility and free will without endorsing either extreme, and I must do so without using the word "agency," which the philosophy faculty have asked be retired from this ceremony. The address should run about twelve minutes, be formal but not academic in tone, and open with a concrete image rather than an abstraction. Please write the speech.
