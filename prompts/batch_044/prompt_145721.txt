My graduate seminar tracks the rulings of a senior decision theorist—call her Dr. K—on whether various preference structures are rationally incoherent. Her record is as follows. She judges cyclical strict preferences over three certain monetary outcomes incoherent, since they invite a money pump. She distinguishes simultaneous Dutch-book exposure, which she treats as genuinely undermining, from the same net exposure distributed across sequential, individually revocable offers, which she does not. She treats framing-induced reversals—choosing differently when outcomes are described as lives saved versus lives lost—as not indicating incoherence, provided the underlying ordering over final states is stable. She has held that violating the independence axiom is incoherent when the shared consequence is certain, but she has not ruled on violations involving an ambiguous shared consequence. Finally, she permits cross-domain intransitivity, for instance between financial risk and social reputation, treating domain boundaries as insulating rationality.

The new case: an agent violates the sure-thing principle with respect to a shared consequence that is an imprecisely quantified lottery—probabilities are bounded but not point-valued. The agent's beliefs form a convex set of priors, and her betting behavior is consistent with every prior in that set. Consequently, no Dutch book can be constructed: any book requires selecting a precise betting rate inside the interval, which the agent can rationally reject. The axiom is violated, yet guaranteed loss is impossible.

Please predict Dr. K's verdict on this case, identify her closest analog in the record, and state the principle she would be adopting with each of the two main options—strict satisfaction of all Savage axioms versus pragmatic immunity to guaranteed loss. Be explicit about whether her precedents genuinely underdetermine the answer. If they do, specify the further case—one not yet in her record—that would distinguish these two standards.
