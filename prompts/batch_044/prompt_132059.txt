I have been trying to apply Dennett's distinction between Orwellian and Stalinesque revisions to his rejection of the Cartesian theatre, but I find myself stuck on what seems to be a hidden presupposition in the literature. If there is no central stage where a single narrative is performed, and instead multiple parallel drafts are composed and revised—some suppressed Stalinesque-style, others misremembered in an Orwellian fashion—then must there not still be a meta-editorial mechanism that determines which draft achieves fame in the brain and thereby counts as the conscious report? In other words, has Dennett merely relocated the Cartesian theatre from the auditorium to an editorial boardroom, with the binding problem now solved not by a spotlight of attention but by a kind of committee vote? I should like to understand whether this relocation genuinely dissolves the theatre, or whether it preserves the core error under a distributed architecture, and what that implies for the claim that consciousness contains no final, canonical draft.
