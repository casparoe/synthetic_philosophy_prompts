A science-blogger's summary of structural realism runs as follows. The view was first articulated by Worrall in 1989 specifically to solve the pessimistic meta-induction: it holds that scientific theories reveal only the mathematical structure of the world and say nothing about the objects within it. There are two variants. The epistemic version, Worrall's own, uses Ramsey sentences to strip away all reference to intrinsic properties, leaving only relational knowledge; it grants that objects exist but claims they are permanently unknowable. The ontic version, later developed by Ladyman and Ross, denies that objects exist at all, treating structures as ontologically basic and composed of pure relational form without any underlying substrate; according to the blogger, this makes ontic structural realism essentially logical empiricism updated, since it eliminates all reference to unobservable entities. The blogger concludes that the whole position collapses into standard scientific realism with the vocabulary changed—replacing "object" with "node"—so the pessimistic meta-induction applies unchanged to structures, and nothing is gained.
