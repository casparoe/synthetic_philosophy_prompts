Assume the role of a senior economic philosopher and Q&A strategist. Think carefully about audience dynamics, argument structure, and the specific commitments of each interlocutor, then work through the objections systematically before drafting the prep sheet.

The speaker is defending the following thesis in a 20-minute interdisciplinary seminar: Keynes’s 1930 essay on economic possibilities for our grandchildren is usually read as advocating leisure and aesthetic consumption once material scarcity is solved, but this misses its deeper normative architecture. The “good life” Keynes describes requires what we might call structured idleness — not passive relaxation, but a temporal architecture in which economic calculation is replaced by ritualized, non-productive social rhythms that preserve collective coordination without instrumental purpose. This is neither hedonism nor virtue-ethical leisure; it is a distinct theory of post-scarcity temporal autonomy that depends on shared, non-contractual rhythms rather than individual choice.

The audience is precisely this group: two Keynes scholars who know the 1930 essay and related correspondence inside out; two virtue ethicists (one Aristotelian, one Stoic-leaning) who are suspicious of any normative framing of “idleness”; one historian of economic thought who treats Keynes’s essay as rhetorical whimsy rather than serious philosophy; and three philosophers of economics with Bayesian leanings who care about coherence and predictive precision but have no special loyalty to Keynes.

Prepare the speaker for the objections these people will actually raise, ordered first by likelihood of being voiced and then by potential damage to the thesis. For each objection:
- Phrase it in the vocabulary and conceptual commitments of the specific audience member who would raise it.
- State whether the speaker should concede a point, hold firm, or offer a partial concession.
- Provide a concise spoken reply (roughly 60–90 seconds) targeted to that formulation.
- Flag the weakest point in that reply — the exact spot where a follow-up could land.

Also identify the single objection the speaker is hoping nobody asks: the one that strikes the weakest structural point in the overall position. Sketch a defensive reply that admits the vulnerability honestly without letting the thesis collapse.

Keep the prep focused on live Q&A dynamics: do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke obscure post-2010 theoretical apparatus; do not treat the essay as a canonical text requiring exegetical defense — treat it as a philosophical claim separable from Keynes’s biography. End with a brief strategic note on which concession, if made early, will defuse the room and which line must not be crossed.
