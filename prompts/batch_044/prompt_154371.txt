Write a two-page dissertation prospectus for a philosophy-of-mind and logic project. The precise question is whether the Lucas-Penrose anti-mechanist argument can be reconstructed to target only diachronic, fixed-architecture cognitive systems—systems whose core inferential rules are not revised during the evaluation period—rather than all formal systems capable of arithmetic, and whether this reconstruction survives standard objections without requiring the implausible claim that humans can prove the consistency of their own reasoning apparatus. Frame the problem by distinguishing synchronic consistency-recognition, which concerns a system's ability at a single time to verify its own consistency, from diachronic consistency-tracking, which concerns an evolving system's capacity to maintain consistency-checking procedures across updates. Explain why the existing debate leaves this open: standard presentations apply Gödel's second incompleteness theorem to any formal system that can represent arithmetic, and the standard mechanist replies either deny that human cognition is formalizable or argue that minds, like machines, can change their axioms; neither camp examines whether restricting the argument to systems with non-revisable core rules alters the dialectic. Propose the approach in stages: first, formalize a minimal model of a diachronic cognitive system with fixed inference rules and representational capacities sufficient for basic arithmetic; second, determine whether Gödel's construction yields a true-but-unprovable sentence within that system's language; third, evaluate whether human mathematical practice exhibits diachronic consistency-tracking that exceeds the capacities of such a model, and whether that excess can be explained by resource-bounded but formally representable update mechanisms or requires a non-mechanist explanation. Specify the possible conclusions—either a restricted Lucas-Penrose argument succeeds, or it collapses because diachronic tracking is either mechanistically replicable or not demonstrably possessed by humans. Define failure conditions: the formal model is either too weak to generate the relevant Gödelian sentence, or so general that standard rebuttals apply without modification. Assess scope honestly: the logical formalization and philosophical analysis are feasible within one semester, but the project must avoid attempting to resolve the broader mechanism debate or to construct a complete cognitive architecture. Refer to positions and arguments by name only; do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation requests, and note that references will be added in a later draft.
