As a journalist covering the intersection of cultural psychology and moral philosophy, I keep running into a particular dismissal in panels and essays responding to the WEIRD literature: "If moral truths were objective, we wouldn't see this kind of cross-cultural patchwork." The speaker usually points to the documented divergence in harm, fairness, purity, and authority intuitions between Western, educated, industrialized, rich, democratic populations and small-scale societies, then treats that variance as an empirical refutation of moral objectivity. I want to understand exactly what claim is being made, because the sentence seems to do different work in different mouths. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan could be expressing? Specifically, disambiguate the possible readings of "objective" (mind-independent, universally accessible, rationally necessary, invariant across institutional contexts?), the possible readings of "patchwork" (random noise, locally adaptive optimization, epistemic failure of one culture, deep structural convergence masked by surface disagreement?), and the implicit bridge principle that connects observed cultural variance to the denial of objectivity. For each combination, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and indicate whether it actually licenses the conclusion that moral objectivity is defeated, or only that moral knowledge is unevenly distributed. I am particularly interested in whether there is a coherent reading that treats institutional transparency—the degree to which a society's norms are exposed to cross-cultural scrutiny and internal revision—as an epistemic confound rather than as evidence against objectivity, and whether that reading can accommodate the pattern in which purity-based reasoning declines sharply with secular institutional exposure even within WEIRD populations. Which of these precisifications is the speaker likely relying on when they invoke the line to dismiss a defense of universal human rights? And if we substitute "mathematical truths" for "moral truths" in the same sentence, which readings survive and which collapse? Present the options as a menu I can take back to the debate, and tell me which of my implicit verdicts—say, that convergence under transparent institutions supports rather than undermines objectivity—I would have to give up to maintain a consistent principle.
