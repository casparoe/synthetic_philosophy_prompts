I am a retired physicist who has only recently begun reading metaphysics systematically, and I have come to hold what I will call an entropic-pattern view of material constitution: there is only the clay—no second object called “the statue” that is constituted by it or shares its spatial location. What we call a statue is merely a metastable, locally negentropic configuration of clay particles sustained by boundary conditions—gravity, temperature, occasional human repair—much as a whirlpool is a stable dynamic pattern within a single flow of water rather than an object distinct from the river. Its persistence conditions are purely descriptive conveniences for tracking shape-stability over time; when the configuration is crushed or melted, nothing is destroyed except the pattern itself, just as a standing wave vanishes when the driving force stops. The clay alone persists through all changes.

This strikes me as more parsimonious than standard constitution theories, but I want to know what would genuinely count against it—not rhetorical objections, but the kinds of arguments, intuitions, formal results, or conceptual pressures that should move someone with a scientific background. In particular:

- Which intuitions about artifact persistence—gradual part-replacement, sudden crushing, or the possibility of reassembly—does this view mishandle, and are those failures fatal or merely awkward?
- Are there results from philosophy of language or social ontology—about reference, collective intentionality, or institutional facts—that show “statue” tracks a real entity rather than a high-level pattern-label?
- Could a committed defender concede that the view collapses into a rival position, such as temporal-parts theory or four-dimensionalism, without actually gaining ontological parsimony, and if so, is that collapse a serious problem?
- Has the view already been qualified into unfalsifiability by allowing any shape-change to be redescribed as “pattern loss”? If yes, is that insulation a virtue or a vice for a metaphysical claim of this kind?

Please rank the potential defeaters from decisive to merely awkward; distinguish what a defender of the entropic-pattern view should honestly concede as counting against it from what they can legitimately dismiss; and assess whether the position risks anything substantive or has been rendered immune to evidence. I want a committed response: take a definite position on whether the view survives these pressures or should be abandoned, give your reasons explicitly, and do not merely lay out options for me to choose between. Where you must rely on judgment rather than proof—say, about whether an intuition should carry metaphysical weight—state the judgment and defend it.
