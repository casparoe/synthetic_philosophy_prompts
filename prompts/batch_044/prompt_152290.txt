hey so i just moved to chicago for my phd and suddenly im living inside every econ textbook example — my rent is capped but my landlord stopped fixing the heater, and my campus job pays exactly the new city minumum wage. i tried to work out whether these rules are actually fair or just politics, and i walked into this argument that now feels like a trap. here it is:

(1) if a deal is voluntary — both sides agree and can walk away — then stopping it harms both sides without helping anyone else.
(2) rent control stops landlords and tenants from agreeing on higher rent; min wage stops workers and bosses from agreeing on lower wages. both are voluntary deals being blocked.
(3) so both rules harm the people they claim to protect (landlords cut maintainance, employers cut hours) and dont clearly help anyone.
(4) but the exact same reasoning applies to bans on selling kidneys or contracting away your freedom: those are voluntary deals the law blocks too.
(5) and if we reject (1) just to save kidney bans, we have no principled reason left for rent control or min wage either — were just picking favorites.
(6) yet i dont want to legalize kidney markets, and i also dont want my landlord to let the pipes freeze.
(7) so either (1) is wrong and i need to explain why rent control is different from organ sales, or the argument collapses everything together.

where does the slip happen? is there an equivocation between "voluntary" in the economic sense and "voluntary" in the legal/ethical sense? or is (1) actually false and the real work is done by some hidden premise about exploitation or coercion? i want to keep the intuition that forcing my landlord to charge less isnt pure charity, but i also dont want to end up defending slave contracts. whats the least costly premise to drop, and what do i pay for dropping it?
