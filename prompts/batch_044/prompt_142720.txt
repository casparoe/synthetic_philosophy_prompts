I'm working through the metaphysics of natural laws for an advanced seminar, and I've become convinced that standard presentations of the Humean versus anti-Humean debate obscure more than they reveal because neither side makes explicit the full adequacy conditions it is implicitly demanding of a theory. Before I commit to any framework, I want to see the complete specification laid out as a set of requirements rather than as a partisan scorecard. Write the desiderata that any theory of natural laws—whether regularity-based, dispositional-essentialist, or primitivist—must satisfy to count as genuinely adequate, with attention not only to fundamental physics but also to special-science domains where lawhood seems derivative or emergent. For each requirement, give the motivating case or theoretical pressure: the need to distinguish lawlike regularities from accidental generalizations (for instance, the gravitational inverse-square relation versus a contingent correlation like all gold spheres being smaller than ten miles across); the need to support counterfactuals and interventions without circular appeal to modal primitives; the need to accommodate apparently vacuous law-statements in worlds that lack the relevant entities or properties; the need to explain why laws remain stable across time rather than shifting with the mosaic; and the need to ground scientific explanation without collapsing the distinction between describing a pattern and accounting for why that pattern holds. Then indicate which of these requirements you take to be non-negotiable for any serious contender and which are camp-specific preferences—particularly whether delivering genuine modal constraint across nearby worlds necessarily requires an anti-Humean governing relation, or whether a sufficiently restricted Humean supervenience base can supply it. Crucially, flag any subset of desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable, especially the combination of full Humean supervenience, non-circular explanatory power, and robust modal force. Briefly note how a best-system regularity account, a dispositional essentialist account, and a primitivist governing account fare against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves and on the points where they come into direct conflict rather than on the scoring.
