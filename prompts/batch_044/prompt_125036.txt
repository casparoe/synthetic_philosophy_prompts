If one takes Dennett's two-bitser model seriously—where derived intentionality is assembled from subsystems that themselves possess only derived intentionality—then it appears that original intentionality is never required, not even as a foundation. Human cognition, on this view, is just an especially large stack of such subsystems. So when Searle insists that original intentionality is uniquely tied to conscious biological processes, isn't he merely refusing to apply the two-bitser decomposition deeply enough? Specifically, if every intentional state in a human can be explained by breaking it into smaller derived states, and if an artificial recursive-interpreter stack could replicate that same decomposition without any conscious substrate, then doesn't Searle's distinction between original and derived collapse into nothing more than a preference for where we arbitrarily stop the analysis? Identify the error in this line of reasoning.
