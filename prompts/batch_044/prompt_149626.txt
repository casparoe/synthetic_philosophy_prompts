Write a five-minute retirement toast for Dr. Elena Vasquez, delivered at a dinner where half the guests are moral philosophers and welfare economists and the other half are family members, former students from other disciplines, and university staff with no background in ethics. Vasquez spent forty years arguing that the classic utility-monster objection to aggregate maximization succeeds only if one accepts what she calls the flatness error: the tacit assumption that moral weight scales linearly across persons, so that one being's enormous gain can always outweigh many others' modest gains. She holds that comparison across persons is not the problem; the problem is treating that comparison as a flat, continuous curve rather than one that is structurally discontinuous at the boundary of distinct lives.

Your toast must satisfy three structural conditions. First, explain the utility-monster scenario to the non-experts in the room without using technical terms such as prioritarianism, lexical ordering, or interpersonal comparison, and without naming Nozick or any other author; the explanation should turn entirely on the flatness error. Second, describe Vasquez's contribution in terms precise enough that the specialists present recognize her distinctive move—that she relocates the failure from epistemic or metaphysical doubts about utility measurement to a normative mistake about the geometry of moral weight—without turning the toast into a lecture. Third, close with a wish for her retirement that formally mirrors her argument: you must refuse to summarize her career as a single maximized total (publications, students, influence summed into one figure), and instead name at least two discrete, non-fungible chapters of her life that resist aggregation.

Keep the tone warm and personal; the philosophical machinery should shape what you can and cannot say, not serve as decoration.
