In my philosophy of probability seminar, we have been working through variants of the Sleeping Beauty problem and the logic of self-locating belief. Rather than ask about the textbook version, I want to test reasoning on a modified protocol I have been trying to formalize.

The baseline scenario is the familiar one: on Sunday a fair coin is flipped. If it lands heads, Beauty is awakened and interviewed once on Monday, then permanently ended. If it lands tails, she is awakened and interviewed on Monday and again on Tuesday, with complete amnesia between the two awakenings so that each feels subjectively identical. She always knows the protocol but never knows the day or whether she has been awakened before.

My modification introduces a mechanically independent malfunction with probability ε = 0.05. In this phantom-error case, the coin actually lands heads, but the apparatus erroneously produces exactly one tail-style awakening on Monday—indistinguishable in every subjective respect from a genuine tail-Monday awakening—after which Beauty is permanently ended with no Tuesday awakening. The error is independent of the coin outcome.

Beauty is now awakened and interviewed. She knows the full protocol, knows ε = 0.05, knows she has just been awakened, but she does not know whether today is Monday or Tuesday, and she has no memory of prior awakenings.

What should her credence be that the coin actually landed heads?

Please do not retreat to "it depends on your decision theory." I want an explicit number and a decomposition of the reasoning that produces it. Specifically:

(a) The weight you place on the standard thirder reasoning being correct in the pure protocol (that is, that without the error the rational credence upon awakening is 1/3).

(b) The directional adjustment, if any, that the phantom-error possibility introduces—does the chance of a spurious single awakening push her credence back toward 1/2, and roughly by how much?

(c) The weight you assign to the halfer position being correct about self-locating conditioning in general.

(d) Any other structural factor you think is decisive, such as whether she should condition on being interviewed at this specific temporal location versus conditioning on being some copy in the overall population of awakenings.

Please combine these into an overall credence, justify each component in one line, identify which term you are least confident in, and say what kind of argument or observation would most shift that term.
