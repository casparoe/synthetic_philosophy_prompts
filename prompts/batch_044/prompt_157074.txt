The long-running disagreement over Quine's indeterminacy of translation—usually introduced through the 'gavagai' scenario—feels like one of those philosophical debates that has mapped out all its moves without anyone changing sides: defenders insist that charity and stimulus conditions still leave reference radically underdetermined, critics respond that practical interpretation converges determinately, and the literature repeats. I am less interested in a verdict than in a structural diagnosis. Is this a substantive dispute about ontology and semantics, a verbal disagreement about what 'meaning' is supposed to cover (semantic content versus cognitive role versus predictive utility), or two projects—one about the limits of empirical translation theory, one about the metaphysics of content—operating under one heading? In particular, might a hidden divergence over the formal framework of reference—say, whether we presuppose classical extensional semantics or allow structurally richer interpretations—be sustaining the appearance of deadlock? Using what each side actually argues, diagnose whether the impasse is substantive, verbal, or a case of misaligned questions, and say what would count as progress: a formal result fixing reference under specified constraints, an empirical finding about actual field-linguistic practice, a clear separation of semantic indeterminacy from mere epistemic uncertainty, or an argument that no further resolution should be expected and why.
