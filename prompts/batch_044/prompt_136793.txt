I was listening to a podcast about Nietzche's genealogy of morality and the host summed up the slave revolt in morals like this: the nobles start with "good" and "bad" as pure self-affirmation, not comparing themselves to anyone, just saying we are good and those others are bad. Then the weak, full of ressentiment—which the host called basically a toxic cocktail of envy and suppressed revenge—can't win by force, so they flip the values: weakness, suffering, and humility become good, while strength and nobility become evil. The host said this was a conscious strategy cooked up by the priests to seize power, and that once slave morality takes over it destroys any objective ground for values, leaving everything purely subjective. He also said modern democracy and human-rights talk is literally just slave morality running on autopilot in politics, same mechanism, same ressentiment.

I'm not sure how fair this is. Is ressentiment really just envy with extra steps, or is there something more creative going on that the host missed? Did Nietzsche think the priests were literally scheming, or was the revaluation more unconscious? And does slave morality actually wipe out all moral objectivity, or is the host overstating the anti-objectivism? Also, is it right to collapse democratic equality into slave morality like that, or does Nietzsche draw sharper lines between them?
