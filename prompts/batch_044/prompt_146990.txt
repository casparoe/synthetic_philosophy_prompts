Design a 75-minute structured deliberation for an upper-level philosophy seminar of fourteen students, intended to make participants feel the instability of the therapy/enhancement distinction from the inside rather than accepting it as a neutral biological boundary.

Prepare six case cards describing concrete interventions. Alongside familiar examples (restoring sight after trauma, regulating anxiety to clinical norms), inject three constructed “baseline-drift” cases: a surgeon seeking reversal of a state-certified cognitive endurance implant now that the implant is standard in her hospital; a musician requesting restoration of her natural hearing range after she had previously enhanced it for mandatory professional certification; and a diplomat asking for “emotional-range therapy” to return to pre-voluntary-suppression affect after leaving government service. Each card specifies the intervention, the pre-intervention state, the post-intervention state, and the institutional context.

Structure (75 minutes):
- 0–10 minutes: Briefing. Divide students into three roles — Clinical Allocators (defend patient autonomy), Regulatory Auditors (apply one consistent written criterion), and Baseline Sceptics (challenge any baseline as arbitrary). Give each group the six cards and ten “public funding tokens”; explain that only interventions unanimously classified as therapy receive a token, while enhancement receives none. They must agree on a single one-sentence criterion before classifying any card.
- 10–40 minutes: Deliberation in role. The groups classify all six cases and allocate tokens. The rule is that if they alter their written criterion at any point, they forfeit one token.
- 40–55 minutes: Reveal and silent vote. Each group presents its allocations and final criterion. Every student then writes, privately, whether the group applied the same criterion to case one and case six.
- 55–75 minutes: Debrief.

Debrief: Map the moment when Regulatory Auditors usually shift their criterion — typically classifying the surgeon’s reversal as “enhancement suppression” and the musician’s as “therapy” despite identical logical structures — onto the problem of normative baseline drift, where institutional norms redefine “normal functioning.” Note that the Baseline Sceptics should have exposed how “species-typical functioning” smuggles in statistical norms that are themselves products of prior enhancement adoption. Predict the failure: groups will protect occupational baselines they treat as given. Handle it by forcing explicit statement of which norm is being used. Be frank: the activity cannot reproduce the macro-level institutional inertia by which enhanced baselines become legally or medically entrenched over decades; the facilitator must supply that historical dimension verbally, as the seminar only reveals the micro-level arbitrariness in real time.
