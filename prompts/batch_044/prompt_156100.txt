I am convening a postgraduate philosophy seminar for fourteen MA students, meeting for a single ninety-minute session. I should like you to design a structured, rules-bound deliberative exercise that makes the participants feel the practical weight of an Ubuntu-inspired relational conception of personhood—specifically, the thesis that individual personhood is not an intrinsic, pre-social property but is constituted and sustained through networks of mutual recognition.

To prevent the exercise from collapsing into generic communitarian sentiment, please embed it in a constructed scenario: a post-disaster settlement referred to as the Archive, in which survival resources—medicine, shelter allocations, and labour credit—are distributed according to each inhabitant’s position in a mapped recognition network rather than according to individual need, cognitive capacity, or contractual entitlement. This network records reciprocal acknowledgements that may be inherited, fractured, or repaired. One assigned role should be that of an inhabitant who retains full rational and emotional capacities but has suffered a total rupture in all recognition ties (the Unrecognised), while other roles occupy positions of high connectivity, partial recognition debt, or contested inheritance.

The procedural architecture should be arranged so that the group collectively commits to a distribution protocol before the full relational map—including the Unrecognised case—is revealed. The facilitator must not deliver a preliminary lecture on Ubuntu, and the term itself should be withheld until the debrief. The exercise should generate its force through the rules and incentives alone.

Please provide:
1. Precise rules, assigned roles, and material tokens representing recognition ties and resources;
2. A symbolic payoff and cost structure—without real money—that generates tangible tension between safeguarding relational standing and responding to immediate individual need;
3. A step-by-step facilitation guide for the ninety-minute session;
4. A debrief that maps specific moments of the exercise onto the conceptual structure of relational personhood (constitution through relation, inheritance of moral standing, repair of broken ties), predicts what will predictably fail on the first run, and frankly identifies which feature of Ubuntu—perhaps its spiritual or cosmological dimension—the exercise necessarily flattens and the facilitator must supply verbally.

Please compose your answer in American English. I should prefer British spelling and punctuation in this request itself. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
