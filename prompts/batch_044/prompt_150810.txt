I’m running a small philosophy of mind reading group — about 9 postgrad students, one 90-minute session on Tuesday evenings — and we’re trying to get our heads around Dennett’s attack on the Cartesian theatre and his multiple drafts idea. I was chatting with my flatmate about it and she said it sounds like a committee meeting where no ones actually in charge, which made me think we could stage an activity where the group literally becomes that committee.

What I’d like is a structured in-class game that makes them feel why there is no central screen where experience comes together. Split them into roles representing different sensory drafts — visual, auditory, and an editorial node — and have them respond to a staged event (a light flash followed by a sound, but with different timings revealed to different people). The rules should push them to produce one group report, yet there should be hidden mechanics — perhaps points for consistency that are only awarded if the group achieves it without anyone being named the official observer, and the editorial node can only communicate via a shared board that gets wiped clean after each round, so there is never a persistent master copy. They shouldnt hear the phrase Cartesian theatre until the debrief. No lecture up front; they should run into the problem themselves. Then a debrief mapping the failure onto Dennett’s rejection of a single narrative stream, including roles, materials, a point-based payoff scheme, a timeline for the session, what will predictably go wrong the first time, and an honest note about which feature of the multiple drafts model the game cant reproduce. Please dont ask for bibliographies or page citations — half the group hasnt done the close reading anyway.
