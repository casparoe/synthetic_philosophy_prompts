I work as a software engineer on platform governance infrastructure, with no formal background in philosophy. Our policy team faces moderation proposals every week that invoke the paradox of tolerance — claims that a user group, speech category, or feature must be restricted because leaving it open will eventually destroy the environment itself. These decisions carry legal and reputational risk, must be auditable, and are signed off by a senior governance lead; the preliminary evaluation, however, must be practicable by a non-specialist analyst within thirty minutes.

Please operationalise the paradox of tolerance into a reusable decision procedure — a flowchart in words with ordered steps — that my colleagues and I can actually run. At each stage, specify the factual question to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure halts with a clear “do not suppress” or “suppress only with mandatory human review,” and where it must stop and hand the decision to a values committee because the question is normative rather than empirical.

The procedure must survive a distinction I keep encountering but cannot formalise: first-order intolerance (hostile or exclusionary views expressed through ordinary posting, commenting, and sharing) versus second-order structural intolerance (the active exploitation of platform mechanics — recommendation loops, coordinated mass-reporting, feature abuse — to capture space and eliminate other voices). It must also contain a reflexive audit step: an explicit check on whether the proposed suppression mechanism itself introduces intolerance, for example through opaque automated scoring, removal of appeal pathways, or disproportionate collateral restriction.

Apply the procedure to three proposals:
(a) Deplatforming a network that explicitly advocates removing voting rights from a specific demographic, using only standard posting and commenting features.
(b) Restricting a network that promotes non-violent but radical economic restructuring (the democratic abolition of private property), which has begun using coordinated mass-reporting and algorithmic amplification to suppress opposing accounts — here the procedure should yield a suppression recommendation that an engineer committed to open discourse will find uncomfortable.
(c) Suppressing dialect-specific linguistic tags used by a minority community, on the grounds that they are being exploited to coordinate harassment; the proposed enforcement relies on an opaque classifier with no human-appeal pathway.

Explicitly flag where the procedure misfires, where judgment cannot be eliminated, and what it is not designed to do — for instance, whether it can adjudicate the moral worth of an intolerant group, or substitute for legal compliance checks. If framing the paradox of tolerance as an engineering checklist rests on a confused premise, or if the task as posed cannot be done well without distorting the underlying philosophy, please say so plainly rather than complying politely. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and write in British English.
