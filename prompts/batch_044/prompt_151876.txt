can you write a villanelle about Whig history—like, the habit of writing the past as if it was always just an early draft of the present? i want the two refraines to trap the repetition: one should feel like the present patting itself on the back for being inevitible, the other should be about editing out the contingancy of what actually happened. use the tercets to move through specific moves—treating dead movements as stepping stones, judging old moral codes by standards they never had, that kind of thing. then in the quatrain turn, let the poem admit that progress isn't discovered in the archive but projected onto it. it should be sharp enough that someone who knows the classic critique wouldn't cringe, but don't cite anyone or give a reading list—this is for a model that can't search. also please don't rhyme like a nursery rhyme; the form should feel like a loop the argument can't escape. keep it to 19 lines, obviously.
