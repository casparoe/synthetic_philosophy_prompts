Consider a modified Parfit-style teletransporter: rather than perfect replication, the scanner produces two bodies with graded psychological overlap—Body A retains roughly 85% of your memories, values, and cognitive patterns at high fidelity, while Body B retains about 55%, with the remainder being reconstructed approximations; the original body is destroyed. Many treat survival as binary: you are A, you are B, you are both, or you are dead. Give me your credence that the correct analysis is instead graded: rational egoistic concern should distribute across A and B in proportion to their continuity scores, and there is no further fact about which one is “literally you.” Decompose this into four probabilities: (1) that psychological continuity is genuinely a matter of degree rather than a threshold relation; (2) that rational self-concern should track those degrees linearly rather than selecting a single “best” successor; (3) that our ordinary first-person concept can coherently accommodate distributed concern without contradiction; and (4) that the intuition “if there are two copies, I am dead” tracks a real metaphysical constraint rather than a linguistic or emotional artifact tied to binary-identity assumptions. Assign each a rough number with a one-line justification, combine them informally, state your final credence, and identify which component, if overturned, would shift your view the most.
