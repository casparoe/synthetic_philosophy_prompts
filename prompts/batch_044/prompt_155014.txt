Having spent a career measuring biochemical reference ranges and now, in retirement, trying to understand the philosophy behind them, I find the naturalism versus normativism debate about health and disease curiously static. The naturalist position, broadly Boorsean, holds that health is the statistically normal functional contribution of parts to the organism's survival and reproduction; the normativist position, in the Nordenfelt tradition, holds that health is the ability of parts to contribute to the organism's vital goals, an assessment that necessarily imports value judgments about what counts as a vital goal. The standard objections are well rehearsed—naturalists are accused of smuggling norms into the selection of "normal" statistical ranges and of treating evolutionary history as normative; normativists are accused of making health irreducibly subjective and of failing to distinguish between disease and mere disability or social disadvantage. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not primarily about values versus facts, but about an ambiguity in "function": naturalists slide between evolutionary selected-effect accounts and present causal-role accounts without noticing, while normativists reject both but implicitly rely on a biological teleology they claim to have abandoned. I want a diagnosis rather than another round of adjudication. Is this a verbal dispute about the word "function," a substantive disagreement about whether evolutionary biology can ground clinical norms, or are the two sides actually answering different questions—one about the metaphysical classification of physiological states, the other about the normative conditions for clinical intervention—under the single heading "health"? Use what each side actually says about function, statistical normality, and vital goals to support your answer. And tell me what, if anything, would count as progress: a formal distinction among types of function, an empirical study of how clinicians actually classify borderline cases, a conceptual clarification that dissolves the problem, or an argument that no progress should be expected because the underlying questions are incommensurable?
