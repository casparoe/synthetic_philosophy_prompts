I've been hanging out in rationalist-adjacent forums where people lean hard on the brain-computer anology: the mind is software running on biological wetware, so substrate-independence and mind-uploading are basically engineering questions, not metaphysical ones. One friend used it to claim that consciousness is multiply realizable the same way a Python script runs on both a Mac and a PC — swap the hardware, keep the program, keep the person. I want to actually audit this instead of just repeating it. Can you lay out the full correspondence table? What's playing the role of software (synaptic weights? predictive models?), hardware (neurons? glia? the vasculature?), the compiler or OS, the I/O bus, and error-correcting codes? Then go through the breaks: there's no von Neumann bottleneck separating memory from processing, development is self-assembly with massive redundancy rather than top-down fabrication, glial cells do something closer to network administration and metabolic regulation than to passive wiring, and computation is analog, stochastic, and deeply embodied rather than digital symbol-manipulation. Separate the disanalogies that actually threaten the uploadability conclusion from the ones that are just cosmetic.

Once the mapping is fully explicit, tell me whether the conclusion — that "I" could in principle run on silicon — survives, or whether the anology was carrying the argument all along. If the computer framing really collapses, suggest a better replacement (maybe the brain as a self-tuning ecosystem, or as a distributed market with no central planner) and say whether that new framing supports or undermines subtrate-independence.
