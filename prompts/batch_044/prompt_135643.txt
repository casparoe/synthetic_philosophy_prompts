I spent four decades in experimental physics, mostly in low-temperature thermodynamics, and since retiring I have turned to philosophical puzzles about infinity and time. I have been thinking about Thomson's lamp—the hypothetical switch toggled infinitely often in a finite interval, leaving its final state apparently undetermined—and about the broader class of supertasks that seem to violate physical intuition. Would you write a short story, in a precise and literary style, about a retired engineer who constructs a mechanical realization of this lamp in her workshop? The narrative should center on the sensory reality of the device—ratcheting gears, cooling solenoids, the smell of machine oil—while exploring the philosophical problem of whether an infinite sequence of discrete actions can have a definite endpoint. To make the situation concrete, let the lamp's final position govern the trigger of a simple quantum measurement apparatus; the protagonist should be recording whether a particle registers, and in doing so should confront the unsettling possibility that the supertask has no consistent final state. Please avoid bibliographies, citations, or references to specific philosophical texts; I am interested in how the concept unfolds imaginatively rather than in scholarly apparatus.
