I am drafting a short essay on the "White Horse is Not Horse" dialogue (Bai Ma Fei Ma) within the School of Names (Mingjia). Rather than treating Gongsun Long's argument as a logical puzzle about universals, I propose reading it as a claim about the failure of compositionality in kind-predication: adding the qualifier "white" does not merely restrict the extension of "horse" but transforms the inferential and perceptual dispositions tied to the bare kind-term. I draw a comparison with modern discussions of "thick concepts", asking whether a descriptive modifier can alter the logical form of the underlying kind-term rather than just narrowing its application, and I suggest that Gongsun Long's concern with correct assertion (yan) is pragmatic—about the conditions for invoking a kind—rather than metaphysical essentialism. Please comment on whether this framing avoids the usual charge of sophistry, whether it misrepresents the Mingjia context by importing a theory of inferential roles foreign to it, and whether the comparison to thick concepts is genuinely illuminating or anachronistic. I am especially interested in whether the dependency between kind-predication and compositionality is coherent, or whether I have smuggled in modern semantic assumptions. Please reply in British English.
