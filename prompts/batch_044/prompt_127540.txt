The debate about whether we should take the intentional stance toward language models has been stuck for like three years now. One camp—mostly philosphers of mind—says attributng beliefs to an LLM is just a useful fiction, like attributing desires to a thermostat, because there's no inner mental life and no genuine aboutness. The other camp—mostly AI researchers—says that if the system reliably tracks world-states and uses those tracks to guide outputs, then denying belief is just biological chavinism, and Dennett's own criteria are satisfied. Everyone agrees on the empirical facts about how transformers work, nobody changes their mind, and the same references keep circulating.

My hunch is that this isn't really about belief at all but about what counts as a deep explanation versus a shallow predictive shortcut—the philosophers want mechanistic transparency, the researchers want behavioral leverage. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a hidden substantive disagreement about whether representational content can stay stable across context shifts when there's no persistent self-model. Can you diagnose what kind of stall this actually is? Are they answering different questions—one about cognitive science, one about engineering—under the same word? Is there a hidden empirical question about representational stability that neither side has made explicit? Or is the disagreement just bedrock intuition about what "aboutness" requires? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move it—a formal result, a new architecture, or a conceptual distinction we haven't drawn?
