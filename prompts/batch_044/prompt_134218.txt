At dinner last week two friends got stuck arguing about an old factory collapse that killed a dozen workers back in the 70s. The town never put up anything, and now theres a push for a small memorial. One said its wrong not to remember, especially since some kids of the victims still live here — she brought up Margalit and his idea that the duty to remember is strongest in thick, close-knit relations. The other said Rieff makes a good point that forcing remembrance through monuments can trap survivors in a victim identity and that active forgetting is sometimes healthier. She told him Rieff is mostly talking about governments forcing remembrance on everyone, not about families naturally remembering thier own. He said thats a fake distinction because once the town builds it its basically state-sponsored thick memory and the line blurs completely. She said even then, if survivors design it, its not imposed from outside. He said control is never that pure, and besides Margalit assumes shared memory is always good, ignoring how remembering can fuel present grudges. They asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't. Can you go through thier exchange and say who's right at each step — not just who wins overall? I also want to know if there's something they're both missing about how public monuments change the nature of remembering.
