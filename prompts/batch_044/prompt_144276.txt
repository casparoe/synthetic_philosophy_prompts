I am writing an essay for an advanced course on philosophy of culture, and I want to explore whether the notion of national character is coherent and whether generalising about peoples is ever defensible. Rather than producing a conventional argument, I would like to test these ideas through sustained irony. Please write a satirical travel-guide entry, in the deadpan, practical style of a Lonely Planet page, for the fictional Federal Republic of Volkscharakter—a nation-state that has fully institutionalised national character as legal doctrine and tourist spectacle. The guide should cover arrival formalities (including the ethnopsychometric passport stamp that assigns visitors a provisional “temperament compatibility score”); local customs, particularly the mandatory Interaction Rituals in which tourists perform stereotyped behaviours associated with their assigned nationality for the observation of local citizens; practical advice for navigating the capital’s museums of collective psychology; and a sidebar on the annual national holiday honouring the country’s invented founding ethnographer, Dr. Albrecht Kollektiv. Through deadpan description, the piece should exaggerate the real logical commitments of essentialist generalisation—circular definition, empirical unfalsifiability, and the slide from statistical description to normative destiny—so that the standard philosophical objections land without being explicitly named. Aim for roughly 600 words, use British English throughout, and maintain an unwaveringly serious, informative voice.

After the guide, please include a brief note of no more than 150 words identifying where the satire takes liberties with actual philosophical debates about national character, which objections are broadly settled (for example, the problem of essentialism or empirical inadequacy), and which remain genuinely contested—such as whether statistical group generalisations can ever be epistemically harmless. Separate settled claims from contested ones clearly, and do not present any disputed interpretation as if it were the consensus view.
