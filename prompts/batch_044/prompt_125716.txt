As a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, I have been thinking about Buridan’s ass and the broader problem of choice without preference, particularly after encountering tie-breaking logic in distributed systems. I would like help seeing whether my intuitions form a coherent pattern, and I want a precise principle that captures them — or an honest account of which verdicts I must drop.

Here are my concrete verdicts. First, Buridan’s ass stands between two identical bales of hay and starves: that seems plainly irrational; the ass should pick one arbitrarily, since there is no substantive reason to prefer either. Second, in the Raft consensus protocol, if two nodes present identical log states and terms, the system breaks the tie deterministically by node ID; I think this is correct design, and insisting on finding a hidden difference before proceeding would prevent progress. Third, in hiring, if two candidates have exactly identical qualifications, scores, and references, selecting the one with the alphabetically earlier surname feels wrong — arbitrary features should not determine a person’s livelihood, even when all substantive criteria are tied. Fourth, in medicine, if two treatments have identical efficacy, risk profiles, and costs, a doctor should prescribe one immediately; delaying treatment to hunt for an invisible distinction seems irrational. Fifth, I recently tried to choose between two rental flats that were genuinely identical in rent, location, and layout, and I spent days analysing trivial differences; I now think that was foolish and I should have flipped a coin.

The pair that troubles me is the second and the third: in Raft, an arbitrary identifier is a perfectly rational tie-breaker, yet in hiring, an arbitrary identifier feels unjust. Both involve truly identical options and a feature that carries no substantive weight. Is there a coherent principle that permits arbitrary resolution in some domains but forbids it in others? Or should I abandon one of these verdicts?

If there is such a principle, please state it precisely, indicate which clause explains each case above, and tell me what it predicts for a case I have not listed: an automated system that must choose between two identically rated responses to present to a user, where neither response contains any distinguishing feature. Please also say honestly which of my five verdicts the principle cannot accommodate, and whether saving the others requires more complexity than is warranted.
