I am drafting an essay on what I have begun to call "epistemic viscosity"—the resistance one feels when a concept genuinely grips the mind, as opposed to the frictionless passage of information that occurs when one relies on an artificial system to synthesize, explain, or judge on one's behalf. The essay contends that our deepening epistemic dependence on AI is not merely a redistribution of cognitive labor but an erosion of understanding itself, because understanding, on my account, requires a certain irregularity: the missteps, the contradictions, the slow accommodation of a foreign idea into one's own vocabulary. When AI supplies answers that are syntactically perfect but epistemically smooth, they bypass the viscosity that would have made them stick. I distinguish between using AI as an instrument—no different from consulting a reference work—and using it as a prosthetic that replaces the struggle of comprehension, arguing that only the latter produces the hollowing I describe. I want comments on whether this distinction between instrumental and prosthetic dependence is philosophically defensible, whether I conflate the phenomenology of difficulty with the ontology of understanding, and whether the concept of viscosity adds anything beyond a romanticized preference for labor. I ask specifically for your judgment on the coherence of the philosophical commitments underlying the essay—particularly my implicit claim that understanding is partly constituted by its resistance to acquisition. If the request rests on a confused premise, or if evaluating these claims properly requires empirical evidence I have not supplied, please say so plainly instead of accommodating me politely.
