Consider the claim that Darwinian natural selection and universal computation are not merely analogically similar but structurally unified in one philosophically substantive respect: both generate complex, adaptive competence—biological design in one case, apparently intelligent behavior in the other—through mechanisms that possess no comprehension, representational or semantic, of the ends they serve. Give your overall credence that "competence without comprehension" is therefore a genuinely unified philosophical category rather than a misleading analogy that collapses under closer scrutiny. Decompose your judgment into at least four components: (a) the probability that evolutionary adaptation operates without any form of representational or intentional comprehension of fitness, function, or future states; (b) the probability that a Turing-complete mechanism, operating purely on syntactic rules without external semantic interpretation, can display behavioral competence that genuinely exceeds its own internal understanding; (c) the probability that these two processes share a deep structural feature—such as blind optimization over a combinatorial search space—rather than being disanalogous because one involves causal-historical selection across generations and the other involves instantaneous rule-governed symbol manipulation within a fixed architecture; and (d) the probability that recognizing this unity actually resolves a substantive conceptual tension (for instance, explaining apparent design without a designer, or intelligence without understanding, without smuggling comprehension back into the substrate). For each component, assign a rough probability between 0 and 1 with a one-line justification, indicate how you combine them into the overall credence, and identify which component you are least confident in and what specific empirical discovery or conceptual argument would most shift its value.
