been chewing over this thing my aunt hilary noticed with her ant colony — she called it the 'ant fugue', these counterpoint foraging loops where pheremone trails suppress each other and the behaviour of the whole nest ends up carrying information about food that no single ant knows. it made me think: can an ant colony actually have a mind? but then i walked myself into an argument that proves way too much, and i can't find the exit.

(1) if a system has a mind, there has to be some representational state that belongs to the whole and can't be broken down into states of the parts.

(2) in the ant fugue, the global pheremone field tracks food location, but no individual ant tracks it in that holistic way.

(3) so the colony has a representational state that belongs to the whole.

(4) but the same kind of global tracking happens in thermostat networks, river deltas with sediment feedback, even stock-market loops — none of their parts carry the global information either.

(5) therefore, either all those things have minds, or (1) is wrong.

(6) yet if (1) is false, there's no clear reason brains are special, since brains are just neurons with feedback loops.

(7) so rejecting (1) costs us the only principled line between minds and other complex systems.

my friend says this proves collective minds are unavoidable. i feel like there's a sneaky slide between (2) and (3) — maybe 'tracking information' isn't the same as 'having a representational state' — but i can't quite name it. which step do you reject, and what does giving it up cost? please answer in british english, and could you say how confident you are in what you claim and point out the specific bit you're least sure about?
