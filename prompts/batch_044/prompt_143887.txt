In a seminar on virtual-world ethics, a fellow student kept insisting, "If there's no physical impact, there's no real harm," and used it to reject two separate objections: that sustained virtual stalking across interoperable platforms can cause genuine psychological injury, and that destroying virtual assets tied to real-world contracts can constitute real economic harm. I want to bring a disambiguation to the next session. What are the distinct precise theses this slogan could be expressing—ranging from tautological to metaphysically ambitious—and which of those readings would actually support dismissing both objections? Lay them out as a menu: for each reading, indicate whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, false, or ambiguous, and note which ones a casual speaker probably intends. Be especially careful to separate claims about physical causation, ontological status, experiential quality, and normative significance, since the argument seems to slide between them.
