I want a taxonomy—structured as a decision tree—for positions in the philosophy of imprecise probabilities. Build it from three diagnostic questions rather than from pre-existing labels. First branch: when an agent updates an imprecise interval credence with evidence that is itself imprecisely specified, does the framework require preserving the width of imprecision, widening it, or permitting sharpening (a posterior interval strictly narrower than the prior)? Second branch: can the framework assign a coherent sharp value to a self-referential proposition such as this assignment is false without contradiction, or does it mandate suspension? Third branch: is imprecision justified only epistemically, or can it also be ontic?

Each leaf should carry the name of a recognized position—interval Bayesians, Dempster-Shafer belief-function theorists, comparative probabilists, or the unnamed sharpening-permitting view—and one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Identify any unoccupied combinations of answers and briefly diagnose whether each gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Keep the tree to eight leaves or fewer. Write in American English. End with a single line beginning Summary: that describes the taxonomy's most surprising empty branch in one sentence.
