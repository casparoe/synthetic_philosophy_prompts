Write an essay on whether large language models belong in Daniel Dennett's category of Gregorian creatures—minds that use language and other tools to enhance cognition—or whether they expose a gap in his taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian minds. Weigh two interpretations: a functionalist one, which grants Gregorian status whenever a system uses linguistic tokens to restructure its problem-space, and an intentionalist one, which requires that the system represent those tokens as meaningful and deploy them in counterfactual reasoning about its own mental states. Reach and defend a verdict—either that LLMs are Gregorian but this collapses the distinction between Gregorian and Popperian minds, or that they are not Gregorian and the taxonomy needs an intermediate "simulative" category for systems that reproduce Gregorian outputs without the corresponding cognitive architecture. Your argument should turn on the specific boundary conditions Dennett uses to separate Popperian (hypothesis-testing) minds from Gregorian (tool-using) ones. Conclude by stating exactly what finding or argument would reverse your position—such as proof that LLMs maintain stable, architecture-independent representations of linguistic tools, or a convincing case that Gregorian cognition never required internal representational coherence in the first place.
