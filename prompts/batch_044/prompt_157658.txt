I'm a product lead for a voice assistant used mainly by older adults to manage meds and appointments. We need a refusal and override procedre grounded in the philosophy of paternalism -- hard versus soft interference, and the worry that blocking someone can itself be disrespectful if you misjudge their competance. But I need it turned into something my engineers can actually run in under five minutes per request, without having to dig into the original literature.

The twist is to avoid treating refusal as a yes/no gate. Instead use a refusal gradient -- silent flag, verbal caution, partial restriction, full override, human escalation -- mapped against a stake-competence matrix: high or low risk crossed with high or low confidence that the user genuinely understands what they're asking. Write the ordered checklist: the questions to ask in sequence, what counts as enough evidence at each gate, where the procedre stops automatically, and where it must hand the decision to a human carer because it's a values call rather than a safety call. Then run it on three cases: a user asking to disable all heart-med reminders for a week, a user insisting on doubling a dose because it worked faster yesterday, and a user cancelling every reminder with no explanation. Make sure one of these produces an answer the team won't like -- probably the cancellation case, where low stakes plus shaky competance readings mean you can't justify a block but it still feels wrong.

Finally, flag the misfire points, especially where temporary frustration or a bad mood gets read as incompetence.

Please structure it like this:

1. The checklist and gradient map (around 350 words)
2. The three worked cases (around 350 words)
3. Where the procedre breaks (around 150 words)

Use British English, keep it casual.
