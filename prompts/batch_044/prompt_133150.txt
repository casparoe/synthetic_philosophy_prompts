Write a short story that dramatises a conceptual tension in Zhuangzi's view of death as transformation rather than annihilation. In your world, death is a reconfiguration of qi into a new form; the transformed retain fragmented, disordered memories of prior lives but have lost the emotional and linguistic framework of those lives. Your protagonist has just become a stationary, root-bound being after a human life and is visited by the person they once loved most, who grieves loudly. The narrative should turn precisely on whether that grief is rational—because a specific relational identity has genuinely ended—or ignorant—because it mistakes a seasonal change for a loss. Do not cite texts, scholars, or named works. Use British English throughout, and end with a one-sentence summary of the story's central philosophical point on its own final line.
