The case should be engineered so that ex-ante planning rationality recommends committing in advance to skip the first option and wait for the second, whereas ex-post rationality at the meeting itself recommends taking whatever approval is available, with the divergence arising specifically from her inability to tell which meeting she is attending rather than from any mistake in reasoning or from an exotic payoff structure. After the scenario, show the reasoning for the planner and for the chooser at the node, identify the precise structural feature—the indistinguishability of nodes combined with the irreversibility of the first approval—that forces the split, and explain whether removing that feature, for instance by giving her an unmistakable calendar cue, collapses the case back into a standard sequential decision problem. If my specification is incoherent—for example, if demanding that both the ex-ante and ex-post verdicts count as fully rational for the same agent smuggles in an equivocation between planning and acting—please say so plainly rather than building a scenario that papers over the confusion.
