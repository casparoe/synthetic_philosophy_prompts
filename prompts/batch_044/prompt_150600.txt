The film's formal structure—reverse chronology for the color sequences, chronological progression for the black-and-white sequences, and Leonard Shelby's anterograde amnesia—seems to literalize Borges's skepticism: Leonard lives in an eternal present, using Polaroids and tattoos as artificial chronometers to impose linearity on an experience that is actually a series of disconnected nows. At the same time, Borges insists that repetition, not progress, is the deeper structure of existence, which resonates with the film's cyclical revenge plot and the suggestion that Leonard may have already killed John G. and chosen to begin the hunt again.

Can you offer a reading that treats Nolan's editing choices and Leonard's bodily inscriptions as philosophical arguments about whether narrative is a cognitive necessity or a metaphysical error? Be honest about where the analogy breaks: does Leonard's condition demonstrate Borges's eternal present, or does it show that the present without narrative collapses into paralysis? Conclude by saying what this pairing adds to the philosophical debate that argument alone does not—perhaps something about whether the body, in the form of tattooed skin, can substitute for memory as a ground for identity, a problem Borges raises in his fictions but rarely resolves in his essays.
