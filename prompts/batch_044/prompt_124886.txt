I've been working through my own view on quantum ontology and want a consistency check before I settle on it. Here is where I've landed:

(1) I think quantum mechanics is complete—there are no hidden variables or deeper dynamics that explain why measurements come out one way rather than another.

(2) I treat the wavefunction as epistemic: it represents an observer's rational beliefs or information about a system, not an objective physical state.

(3) I take protective measurements seriously—if we can observe expectation values of the wavefunction without collapsing the state, that suggests it tracks something genuinely present in the system, not mere ignorance.

(4) I accept the PBR result: if two quantum states are distinct, they correspond to genuinely different underlying physical conditions.

(5) In a Wigner's-friend-type scenario, I think both observers can correctly assign different wavefunctions to the same system, with neither being mistaken. I also reject Bohmian mechanics and don't want to reify the wavefunction as a field vibrating in 3N-dimensional configuration space; I'd prefer to keep ontology anchored in ordinary spacetime.

Do these hang together? I'm worried that (2) is being pulled in opposite directions by (3) and (4), and I can't tell whether (5) deepens the problem or actually helps resolve it. I also have a nagging sense that my rejection of configuration-space realism might conflict with (1) in a way I haven't fully seen, though it might only be an apparent clash. Can you audit this set: rank the actual conflicts by severity, point out any tension that dissolves once you trace what each commitment really entails, and say what the least costly revision would be? I don't have a fixed prior on which one I'd give up.
