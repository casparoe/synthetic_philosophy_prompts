As a novelist constructing a scene in which my protagonist moderates a podcast on philosophical anachronism, I require a sequence of interview questions for an invented guest—a contemporary philosopher who defends a position she calls gustatory moral relativism. She contends that Montaigne’s reflections on cannibalism reveal moral disgust to operate not as rational judgment but as a culturally cultivated taste, analogous to culinary preference, and that recognizing this undermines universalist ethics without collapsing into mere tolerance. Draft eight to ten sharply focused questions that avoid asking her to summarize Montaigne and instead probe the internal architecture of her view. Specifically, include one question on how she distinguishes the affective response to cannibalism from the horror elicited by practices that appear to violate autonomy regardless of culture—such as torture—together with a follow-up keyed to her likely appeal to autonomy; one question on whether her framework renders cannibalism permissible within its own cultural context or merely withholds cross-cultural judgment, with a follow-up tailored to whichever answer she gives; and one question on what her analogy implies for contemporary bioethical boundary cases—for instance, regulated markets for human organs or the consumption of lab-cultured human tissue—with a follow-up that presses her if she tries to quarantine these modern cases as technologically exceptional. The remaining questions should anticipate standard objections to Montaigne-inspired relativism, such as the charge that it collapses into moral quietism or that it cannot account for internal reform movements within a culture, and should include follow-ups that make the usual deflections harder to maintain. After presenting the questions, please state how confident you are in this framing and identify the specific point about which you are least certain.
