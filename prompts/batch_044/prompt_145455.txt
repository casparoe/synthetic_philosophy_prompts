I've been sitting with a specific reading of Zhuangzi on death—call it the "no-knot" view. The idea is that if you take the transformation thesis seriously, it's not just "death isn't harmful" but that grief itself is a catagory mistake: there's no persisting subject that gets lost, since the self was always just a temporary knot in qi-flow, and anticipating loss is doubly confused because the anticipator and the deceased are already different patterns. So mourning is like being sad that winter turned into spring, except the knot never had the kind of identity conditions that make loss coherent. What would count against this? I want the honest defeaters—not just "grief feels real" but arguments, intuitions, maybe results from phenomenology or anthropology of mourning, or formal issues with process ontology applied to persons. Which ones would a committed Zhuangzian concede are damaging, and which can be dismissed as cultural conditioning? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward. Also, has the view been qualifed into unfalsifiability—like saying any emotional response is just attachment to illusion—and if so, is that a problem for a philosophical position of this kind?
