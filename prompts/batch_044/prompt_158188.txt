Consider the following argument, which begins with Glaucon's challenge in the Republic but extends it through a hypothetical device—call it the "opaque ring"—that seems to drive moral motivation to extinction.

(1) The only non-instrumental defense of justice is internalist: justice is rational because it produces harmony in the soul, while injustice produces disorder, regardless of reputation or punishment.

(2) This defense depends on the agent having at least minimal access to her own psychic state; one must be able to register, however dimly, whether one's soul is ordered or chaotic.

(3) The ring of Gyges removes external observation; the opaque ring extends this by removing introspective access entirely, so the agent cannot distinguish a just soul from an unjust one from the inside.

(4) If reasons must be epistemically accessible to motivate (reasons internalism), then with the opaque ring neither the external reason (reputation, reward, punishment) nor the internal reason (psychic harmony) is available to the agent.

(5) Therefore, under the opaque ring there is no rational motivation to be just.

(6) The internalist might reply that justice remains rational because harmony exists objectively, whether or not the agent can observe it.

(7) But if objective existence alone is sufficient for rational motivation regardless of accessibility, then agents are rationally bound to pursue goods they can never identify—including goods that conflict with every observable reason they possess.

(8) So the internalist defense either collapses back into requiring some form of transparency (making it vulnerable to the original ring), or it accepts that rational requirements can float entirely free of epistemic access, which generalizes to an implausible doctrine about all practical reason.

(9) Hence, when pushed consistently, Glaucon's challenge seems to show that morality either depends on being observed—externally or internally—or makes no rational demands at all.

Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If you wish to preserve (1), can you do so without falling into (7)? If you wish to preserve (4), are you then committed to the conclusion in (5) whenever introspection is blocked? And if you reject (6) rather than (7), what account of rational motivation allows reasons to operate in total opacity without generalizing to every hidden good?
