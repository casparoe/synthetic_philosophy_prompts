I am designing a graduate seminar on metaphilosophy of science and want an exercise in sustained philosophical satire. Please write a deadpan bureaucratic white paper—approximately 700 words, formatted with numbered sections and a brief appendix—issued by the fictional “Office of Explanatory Policy” in a world where greedy reductionism has been adopted as the sole legitimate methodology and all “skyhook” explanations have been banned by statute. The voice should be that of a senior administrator reporting to a legislative oversight committee: dry, precise, and utterly unperturbed by the ontological casualties of the policy.

The satire must exaggerate real features of Dennett’s cranes-and-skyhooks distinction rather than inventing them: the document should celebrate step-by-step “crane” explanations that build from basic physics upward, treat any appeal to top-down, normative, or emergent framing as a prohibited “skyhook,” and report with administrative satisfaction that several phenomena—conscious experience, moral obligation, and aesthetic judgment—have been “successfully explained away” rather than genuinely explained. Without ever stating the standard objections explicitly, the paper should make them legible to a reader who knows the literature: that what has been eliminated is precisely what required explanation, that some domains resist bottom-up assembly, and that declaring victory by redefining the explanandum away is not an explanatory triumph. You may invent concrete bureaucratic instruments—such as a “Crane Compliance Index” or a “Skyhook Contamination Audit”—to ground the parody, but work from the widely shared understanding of Dennett’s metaphors and avoid relying on obscure scholarship or specific page references that a well-read model might not have properly absorbed.

After the white paper, include a brief analytical postscript of about 150 words that identifies where the parody takes liberties with Dennett’s actual position—particularly whether he endorses greedy reductionism, how he distinguishes benign from greedy reduction, and whether he allows any legitimate role for explanatory patterns that are not purely bottom-up—or where it leans on a contested interpretation of reduction in philosophy of mind. In this postscript, say explicitly what argument or clarification would change your assessment: for instance, whether a closer reading of Dennett’s writings on evolutionary explanation reveals that he permits non-reductive explanatory structures that the parody falsely treats as banned, and where the reasoning is most likely to fail if that interpretation turns out to be wrong.
