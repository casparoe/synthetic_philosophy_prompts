I am attempting to reconstruct how Ludwig Boltzmann might have responded to a cluster of conceptual developments in statistical mechanics and cosmology that arose only after his death in 1906, and I should welcome a reasoned speculation rather than a biographical summary. Boltzmann's own account of the thermodynamic arrow was resolutely statistical and local: he held that entropy increases in our region of the universe not because of any fundamental law of nature, but because we inhabit an extraordinarily improbable low-entropy pocket within a much larger high-entropy cosmos, and he was notably reluctant to invoke global cosmological boundary conditions as explanations. With this background in mind, consider a hypothetical scenario—one that introduces a niche conceptual twist not found in standard expositions—in which cosmologists discover two causally disconnected 'twin' regions of spacetime separated by a singular boundary of minimal entropy. In the first region, entropy increases away from the boundary in the familiar manner; in the second, it decreases away from the boundary, yielding what might be called an 'entropic bifurcation' with no globally privileged temporal direction. How do you think Boltzmann would have judged this discovery? Would he have treated it as a straightforward extension of his statistical view, or would he have argued that the shared minimal-entropy boundary functions as precisely the kind of cosmological past hypothesis he sought to avoid? Moreover, if he were also confronted with the modern claim that quantum decoherence in isolated micro-systems can produce correlations that display an irreversible temporal ordering even in the absence of macroscopic thermalisation, do you think he would have regarded this as compatible with his macroscopic statistical explanation, or as evidence that the arrow of time is more fundamental than he allowed? Please set out your reasoning clearly, distinguishing between what Boltzmann's published arguments genuinely suggest and where the reconstruction must necessarily go beyond them.
