Show precisely where a Lewis-style counterpart relation and a Kripkean transworld-identity view diverge in their verdicts—whether the reconstruction inherits the original work's aesthetic authority through shared formal structure, or fails to be the same work because the causal-historical chain is broken. I want the example to expose whether the disagreement is merely verbal—about how we apply the term "same work"—or whether it reflects a substantive ontological difference in how the two frameworks treat the relationship between an object's intrinsic properties and its origin.
