On Lewis's account, a convention is an arbitrary regularity that solves a coordination problem and persists through common knowledge—including knowledge that the regularity is arbitrary and that others conform. This appears to require that participants represent the problem and recognize alternatives. Yet many norms seem to emerge through evolutionary drift: agents reproduce behaviors with local adjustments, never representing the global pattern as arbitrary or explicitly coordinating on it. If a dialect gradually shifts to fixed SVO word order through generational transmission without speakers ever treating word order as a choice point, can this still be a Lewisian convention?
