I was arguing with my roommate about whether promises are actually morally binding or just a social trick we play on ourselves. She thinks it's all expectation-based — you promise, I expect, I plan around it, and breaking it harms me. I think there's something more like giving someone authority over your future choices, but I can't tell if that's just fancy language for the same thing. Anyway, I'm trying to figure out where the real gaps are in this debate, not the ones every intro ethics class covers.

There seem to be three messy clusters that don't get resolved together: one is whether promissory obligation holds when the social practice collapses — think post-catastrophe or digital spaces where there's no shared normative background. Another is whether the 'normative power' story actually explains bindingness or just renames it, especially when promises are made across deep moral disagreement. The third is whether extending promises to future people or non-human agents is conceptually coherent, or if we're just stretching an analogy until it breaks.

If I had three years and a small grant for pure conceptual work — no surveys, no empirical studies — which cluster should I actually tackle? Rank them by what's upstream: solving which one would change the answer to the others?
