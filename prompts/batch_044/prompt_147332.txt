In historiographical debates about the French Revolution—particularly those weighing class-struggle, ideological, and contingency-based explanations—people often invoke the idea that revolutions devour their own children, usually to explain the collapse of the Jacobin regime into the Terror and the Thermidorian Reaction. I am trying to use this idea precisely in an argument about whether 1789 was structurally destined to turn violent, and I realize I do not know what claim I am actually making. Can you separate the slogan into its distinct precise theses? For example: is it a claim about internal factional dynamics under conditions of radical uncertainty; about the institutional logic of revolutionary committees that must eliminate dissent to preserve legitimacy; about a psychological law by which revolutionary fervor necessarily burns out into paranoia; about a sociological pattern in which the social base of a revolution is destroyed by the very measures meant to defend it; or about a narrative pattern that historians impose retrospectively? For each reading, indicate whether it is a trivial platitude, a substantive empirical claim specific to 1789, a general law of revolutionary politics, or a false metaphor. Then diagnose which of these readings actually does explanatory work within the three historiographical frameworks—does it support a class-struggle account (the bourgeoisie sacrificing its radical allies once power was secured), an ideological account (the logic of republican virtue turning against its agents), or a contingency account (personal rivalries and military crisis making the Terror an avoidable accident)—and identify which reading is being smuggled in when someone uses the phrase to dismiss all revolutionary politics as inherently self-defeating. Finally, introduce and defend one conceptual distinction—perhaps between institutional auto-immunity and ideological overreach—that could clarify whether we are describing a mechanism internal to revolutionary states or merely a retrospective storytelling pattern, and tell me honestly whether that distinction resolves anything substantive or simply renames the problem.
