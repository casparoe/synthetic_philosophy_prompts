Me and my friend were going back and forth alot for like two hours about whether Lewis's convention stuff actually works for norms that evolve over time, and I think I cornered myself with this argument but I can't see the exit. (1) On Lewis's view a convention is a regularity R in population P solving a coordination problem, sustained by common knowledge and a preference for conformity conditional on others conforming. (2) Now take evolutionary norm dynamics: a norm like "don't steal" stabilizes because defectors get punished, but the specific content is path-dependent — small initial differences could have led to a norm allowing limited theft under conditions X. (3) So the "preference for conformity" in these cases is really just expectation of punishment, not an independent preference for matching others. (4) But Lewis allows that conventions can be arbitrary — driving on the right has no intrinsic merit, its just the regularity we settled on. (5) If evolutionary norms are path-dependent and arbitrary in content, then they fit the arbitrariness condition. (6) And if punishment is common knowledge and conditions behavior, then it plays the same structural role as preference for conformity in sustaining the regularity. (7) So evolutionary norms should count as full Lewisian conventions, just ones with teeth. (8) But then the normative force of "don't steal" collapses to "this is the regularity we happen to have plus you'll be hurt if you break it," which sounds like coercion, not convention. (9) Lewis wanted to distinguish conventions from coerced regularities, so either evolutionary norms aren't conventions (and Lewis misses a huge class of social norms) or they are conventions and Lewis can't distinguish moral norms from driving rules.

Their's probably an equivocation hiding between steps 3 and 6 about what "preference for conformity" means — maybe its not about psychology but about rational structure? Or maybe step 7 smugles in that punishment is part of the convention rather than external to it? I want you to take a definite stance: is the flaw in step 3, 6, or 8, and does fixing it save Lewis's account for evolutionary norms or kill it?
