I am a retired experimental physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, have turned seriously to philosophy of mind and cognitive science; I now host a podcast that examines conceptual problems at the boundary of science and philosophy. Next month my guest will be a philosopher who defends what she calls an "autopoietic dissolution" of the symbol grounding problem. Her core claim is that the problem only arises if one assumes cognition is essentially representational computation; once we understand living systems as maintaining organizational closure through regulatory coupling, symbols are not representations that require external anchoring but functional nodes embedded in homeostatic loops. She is particularly skilled at deflecting worries about abstract or non-biological symbols by insisting they are merely "derivative scaffolds" rather than base cases, and she answers concerns about biological chauvinism by suggesting that artificial systems could, in principle, be reorganized as autopoietic.

I would like you to draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate this framework. I want at least one question that presses the move from causal correlation to regulatory integration—specifically, whether embedding a symbol in a feedback loop genuinely explains its semantic content or merely relocates the mystery to the normative conditions that define loop success—and, for that, a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that normativity is internal to organizational closure rather than observer-relative. I also want a question directed at abstract symbolic domains, such as formal mathematics or counterfactual reasoning, that do not appear to serve immediate homeostatic ends, together with a follow-up anticipating her "derivative scaffold" deflection and asking whether "derivation" here functions as an explanation or only as a relabeling maneuver. Finally, please include a question that asks whether her position implicitly relies on a form of biological naturalism that Searle might accept, with a follow-up prepared for her response that her view is not naturalist in Searle's sense because it requires organizational closure rather than consciousness to ground original intentionality. The questions should be sharp enough for a colloquium audience, should anticipate the standard moves she makes, and should not include generic biographical prompts or requests for reading lists.
