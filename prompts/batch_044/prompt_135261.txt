I have been working through Kant's universalizability tests—specifically the contradiction-in-conception and contradiction-in-will formulations—under an interpretive frame that I want rigorously tested for exposure. My position is that the familiar counterexamples, from false promising to the refusal of mutual aid, miss their target because they rely on maxim formulations that are systematically underspecified: once a maxim is articulated with full generality, including the agent's precise end, the means adopted, the conditions of application, and the relevant situational variables, the logical contradiction either clearly obtains or clearly fails to obtain, and the charge of indeterminacy dissolves. The serious cost of this defense, however, is that the process of refinement seems to presuppose substantive judgments about which ends are rationally permissible—judgments that belong more naturally to the Formula of Humanity—so the universalizability test risks collapsing into redundancy rather than functioning as an independent criterion of moral duty. Before I commit to this reading, I want to know exactly what would have to be true for it to be wrong. Which textual details in the Groundwork, which structural results in deontic reconstruction, or which findings in moral psychology show that maxim refinement cannot rescue the test? Which rival interpretations—perhaps those that treat maxims as action-types rather than fully specified practical principles—have resources that this defense cannot accommodate? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward: for instance, evidence that defenders of universalizability themselves concede undermines the reading, versus objections they would dismiss as misunderstandings of how maxims function. I also want an honest assessment of whether this defense insulates the position into unfalsifiability—if every apparent counterexample can be answered by further refining the maxim, does the view become vacuous, and is that a genuine problem for a normative theory of this kind? Please separate in your reply what is settled among careful Kant scholars from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present a disputed interpretive claim as if it were the consensus. Present everything in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, writing as you would in a direct letter.
