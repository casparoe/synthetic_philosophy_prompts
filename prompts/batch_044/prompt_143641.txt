I hold a view about the special composition question that I have not seen defended in exactly this form, and I want to know how exposed it is. I think that a plurality of objects composes a genuine whole if and only if the parts stand in a relation of functional interdependence that is not reducible to pairwise interactions—meaning the causal and counterfactual behavior of each part depends on the states of the others in a way that cannot be reconstructed from summed binary relations—and only when that interdependence yields a unified causal profile that is distinguishable from the aggregate profile of the parts taken separately. On this view, tightly integrated artifacts (say, a functioning processor with interdependent gates) and certain ecological networks can be real composites, but scattered objects, arbitrary heaps, and even some biological colonies whose members interact only pairwise are not. I deny that life, consciousness, or biological organization is necessary for composition, and I reject both universalism and standard biological restrictivism.
