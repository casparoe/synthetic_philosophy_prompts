I am a sixth-form student and the youth representative on my school’s Heritage and Records Committee, which oversees the digitisation and public presentation of our institutional archive, including twentieth-century student registers, disciplinary logs, and administrative correspondence. We have received a proposal to address profound silences in these records—particularly the near-absence of documented experience among pupils from specific ethnic and socio-economic backgrounds, whose files were either destroyed under earlier data-protection protocols or never systematically created—by commissioning a statistical reconstruction model to generate “probabilistic biographical entries” inferred solely from aggregate demographic patterns and contemporaneous administrative metadata. These synthetic entries would be integrated into the open-access digital catalogue, clearly flagged as algorithmic reconstructions rather than primary documents, with the explicit aim of rendering visible the structural exclusions that produced the archival gap. The committee must now decide whether to authorise this practice or to preserve the archive strictly as an unaltered repository of verified documentary evidence, annotating the silences without synthesising content to fill them. Please draft a formal memorandum for the committee recommending a definite course of action. Your memo should articulate the philosophical stakes regarding archival power—specifically, who is recorded, whose absence is institutionalised, and whether generating synthetic presence for the excluded constitutes an act of restorative visibility or a further distortion of historical agency—together with clear decision criteria. It should recommend either adoption or rejection, provide rigorous reasoning, and include an honest, fair account of the strongest consideration supporting the opposing view. The document should be suitable for direct consideration by an institutional body that must vote on the matter.
