Here is my current philosophical profile on moral responsibility and reactive attitudes. I want an internal consistency check; I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations.

My commitments are these:

(a) I accept the Strawsonian distinction between reactive attitudes and the objective attitude as exhaustive: there is no coherent third stance we can take towards another being as an agent or as an object of study.

(b) Reactive attitudes carry genuine moral weight — they are not merely causal reactions — but only when they satisfy what I call a diachronic coherence condition: the target’s actions must be interpretable as expressions of a continuous practical identity across time, rather than as isolated behavioural outputs.

(c) Moral responsibility is constituted entirely by being an appropriate target of participant reactive attitudes; it requires no metaphysical libertarian free will, and it is not reducible to causal control over one’s behaviour.

(d) For individuals with severe, treatment-resistant psychopathy, adopting the objective attitude is not a moral failure or a withdrawal from moral community, but a rational adjustment that improves predictive accuracy without sacrificing normative insight.

(e) Once the objective attitude is adopted towards an agent, it is incommensurable with reactive evaluation: you cannot simultaneously hold both stances towards the same action, because the former treats behaviour as symptomatic rather than as expressive of will.

(f) I also think that reactive attitudes should be deliberately cultivated or suppressed in institutional and legal contexts according to which policies maximise social welfare; they are not purely non-instrumental responses, even if they feel immediate.
