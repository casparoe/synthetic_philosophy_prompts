I am a novelist developing a philosophical novel centered on a character who accepts Berkeley's master argument—that the mind cannot form abstract general ideas—yet must navigate a world of scientific laws, fictional worlds, and shared language. I want the conceptual framework to be genuinely current, not a museum piece. So I ask: among the live open questions surrounding Berkeley's idealism and the master argument, which three lines of work matter most today, and in what order should a serious researcher pursue them?

Specifically, consider (i) whether the master argument survives if abstract representation is reconceived as structural or schematic rather than Lockean; (ii) whether immaterialism entails that unperceived fictional spaces collapse, or whether they can be sustained by authorial intention without continuous mental presence; and (iii) whether Berkeley's critique of matter offers any independent purchase on current debates about the ontology of digital or virtual environments.

Rank these by upstream importance: which resolution would most reshape the others? By tractability for a philosopher working without empirical apparatus? And by relevance to how we understand narrative reality versus physical reality? I want a defended agenda—criteria, trade-offs, and what evidence or argument would change the ranking—not a bibliography or survey. The goal is to know where limited intellectual energy should go, not to list everything that remains unresolved.
