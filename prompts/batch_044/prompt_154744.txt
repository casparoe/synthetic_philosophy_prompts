hey, I'm drafting this novel and also got stuck on a philosophy argument I cooked up about fiction and greif. I think it proves writers are either irrational or have magic emotions, which can't be right. Here goes:

(1) When I write a death scene and actually tear up, I dont call anyone or plan a funeral, because I know I invented the character.
(2) So my emotional response doesn't require believing the character is real - narrative engagement is enough.
(3) But if belief isn't what anchors the feeling, there's no ontological wall keeping it inside the fiction. The emotion should spill into real-world behavior.
(4) So I should feel guilty killing characters, or avoid happy endings for survivors out of loyalty, just like real loss.
(5) I don't do any of that. I kill people in chapter three and feel totally fine writting brunch scenes in chapter four.
(6) Therefore my feelings in (1) must be "shadow-grief" - different in kind from real greif - or else I'm systematically irrational.
(7) But if they're shadow-grief, then fiction never moves us with real emotions, which contradicts the whole point of writting.
(8) And if writers are systematically irrational, that's a huge cost just to save the idea that fiction matters emotionally.
(9) So either belief IS required (making me irrational at my desk) or fiction is emotionally hollow. Both suck.

where's the slip? I really want to keep that writers can feel genuinely for made-up people without being crazy, and I dont want to invent a special "shadow" category. Which step should I reject, and what do I pay for rejecting it?
