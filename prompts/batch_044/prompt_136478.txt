im not sure if this has a name or if im just describing Molyneux with extra steps. There's this pattern I notice in sensory substitution research and in some robotics papers: a system builds up a rich spatial model through one modality alone -- like a blind person navigating via echolocation, or a robot mapping a room with only touch sensors. The model works fine but is encoded in a totally different 'geometry' than vision. Then vision gets added, and the question isnt whether they learn to associate sights with sounds or touches over time, but whether there's some immediate structural alignment that lets them 'see' the map they already had. Two concrete cases: the echolocation user who suddenly sees the corner they already knew was there, and the tactile robot that gets cameras and seems to align edges with no extra training. This feels different from classic Molyneux, since that problem is about recognizing specific shapes (cube vs sphere) with no prior spatial model in vision, and also different from ordinary multimodal learning where you train correlations from scratch. Is there an established concept for this specific thing -- cross-modal recognition of a pre-existing spatial structure when a new sensory channel is added? If not, how should I carve it out from Molyneux and from standard sensory integration, and what would you call it?
