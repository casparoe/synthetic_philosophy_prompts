I'm advising a small research group with roughly three years to work on the conceptual foundations of low-impact AI—specifically, whether "avoiding side effects" can be defined and bounded without first specifying a complete value function. The group can do philosophy, formal modeling, or conceptual analysis, but not large-scale empirical ML. Within this space, there are several competing open questions: whether impact should be measured counterfactually (comparing worlds with and without the agent) or via a state-space divergence metric that requires no reference world; whether a "local" impact bound can be formulated that applies only near an agent's task trajectory without requiring global value judgments about distant parts of the world; whether instrumental convergence implies that any agent capable of meaningful achievement will necessarily violate such local bounds; and whether analogies to environmental-impact assessment or to the precautionary principle in risk ethics actually help formalize the problem or merely import hidden value commitments that defeat the purpose.

Which of these lines of work—plus any critical ones I haven't listed that you think belong—matter most for the field over the next few years? I want a defended agenda rather than a survey: rank them by some explicit combination of tractability for conceptual work, how upstream they are of other questions, and how much progress would actually determine whether "low-impact alignment" is a coherent research program rather than just a slogan. For each, explain what result or clarification in the next two to three years would move it up or down your ranking. In particular, address whether the core obstacle is genuinely that we lack a value-independent metric of "side effect," or whether the deeper problem is that any metric formalizable without values will turn out to be either trivially satisfiable (and therefore useless for alignment) or unsatisfiable for any agent that does non-trivial work.
