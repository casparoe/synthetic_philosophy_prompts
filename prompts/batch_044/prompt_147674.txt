everyone in my bioethics circles compares an advance directive to a property deed — the 'real' person who wrote it owns the body, and the demented version is just a caretaker or squatter who has to follow the instructions left behind. people use this to argue that refusing treatment based on the directive isn't killing a person, it's just respecting property rights over a body that's no longer inhabited by the owner. audit this analogy: what's the owner, what's the property, what's the squatter, what's the deed? where does it break — especially if you think psycholgical continuity doesn't survive dementia but bodily continuity does? what does the analogy quietly import about identity vs. ownership that the target case might not have? and does the conclusion — that the directive binds us morally — actually survive once you see that bodies aren't owned like houses, or is the analogy doing all the work?
