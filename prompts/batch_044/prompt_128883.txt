Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers—Dr. Aris, who holds that collective intentionality requires synchronous co-presence and mutual awareness among participants, and Dr. Blythe, who defends what she terms "stretched intentionality," according to which collective intentions can be distributed across non-overlapping lifetimes and held together by structural role assignment rather than shared mental representation. Have them examine the case of a medieval cathedral constructed over three centuries by artisans who never met, each executing a specialized task according to a transmitted architectural plan without knowing the overall design or the identity of other contributors. The discussion should feature genuine back-and-forth: Aris should argue that without reciprocal belief and present-tense coordination, the artisans merely perform individually aligned actions with no collective agent; Blythe should counter that the role system itself constitutes a distributed we-intention that does not reduce to individual mental states, and that demanding mutual awareness confuses a contingent feature of small-scale cooperation with a necessary condition for group agency. Ensure neither character is a strawman, and ask them to address specifically whether the absence of a shared "we-feeling" or collective self-awareness undermines Blythe's position, and whether Aris's view can accommodate large-scale cooperative achievements without implausibly multiplying group minds. Write your response in American English, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
