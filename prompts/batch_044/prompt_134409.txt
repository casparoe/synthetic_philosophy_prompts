Question: Virtue ethics, especially in the Aristotelian tradition, holds that good people possess stable character traits—courage, temperance, practical wisdom (phronesis)—that guide their actions across different kinds of situations. The situationist critique draws on psychological studies of obedience, bystander behavior, and role-playing to argue that behavior is driven primarily by situational cues rather than by broad, cross-situational traits. One proposed compromise is that virtues are "local": they are genuinely stable dispositions, but only within specific domains, relationships, or contexts, and they do not aggregate into global character. Does adopting this local-trait view save virtue ethics from the situationist challenge? Or does it alter the theory so radically—particularly in its account of practical wisdom and moral unity—that what remains is no longer virtue ethics in any recognizable form? Reach and defend a clear conclusion.

Student answer:

Virtue ethics, particularly Aristotle's version, claims that a good person has global virtues—stable dispositions that cause them to act well in any situation that calls for virtue. The situationist critique uses experiments like Milgram's obedience study and the bystander-effect research to show that people's behavior changes dramatically depending on context, which proves that global traits do not exist. Because virtue ethics predicts consistency and psychology shows inconsistency, the theory is wrong. The local-trait response attempts to rescue it by saying that virtues are only local, such as being honest with friends but not with strangers, but this really turns virtue ethics into a kind of behaviorism in which situations control everything and there is no unified moral self. Aristotle actually acknowledged that circumstances affect action, so the situationists are attacking a straw man. Furthermore, practical wisdom does not require global traits; it only requires reading each particular situation correctly. Therefore, virtue ethics survives intact once we accept local virtues, and the situationist critique does not seriously damage it.
