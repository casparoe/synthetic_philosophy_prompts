By asymmetrical analogical debt, I mean that when a policymaker invokes a historical analogy, they borrow the moral and strategic weight of the original event's result without borrowing the structural conditions that made the original event what it was. In 1938 Munich, the specific conditions included a single revisionist power making a clear sequence of escalating territorial demands, democratic allies with a credible collective-security alternative that was actually available but rejected, and an information environment in which the aggressor's intentions were somewhat readable. In 2002-2003 Iraq, the conditions were structurally different: a regional power contained by sanctions and no-fly zones, no credible sequence of escalating territorial demands against neighbors, and an information environment defined by intelligence uncertainty rather than readable intentions. Yet the Munich analogy was used to justify preemptive military action rather than collective containment.
