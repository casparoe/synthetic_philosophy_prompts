hey, been digging into epiphenomenalism — the idea that consciousness is like steam from a train, doesn't actually push anything. there's a recurring argument i keep running into that feels like pulling the ladder out after you've climbed it, but only on one side.

people say: if my feeling of pain doesn't cause the belief 'i'm in pain', then that belief is just accidental, so i can't trust it. okay. but then they turn around and use that same belief — that i'm aware of pain — to set up the premise that awareness is causaly inert in the first place. so the judgment about consciousness gets used to build the theory that undermines the judgment. classic self-undermining.

but here's the specific thing i can't name. the unreliability attack always hits the 'inner' reports — 'this hurts', 'i see red' — while the physicalist explanation (neurons firing, etc.) gets treated as totally solid, even though both come from the same brain machinery with no causal link to the subjective side. it's not just the general paradox of phenominal judgment; it's an asymmetrical version where only the subjective half gets sabotaged and the physical half walks away clean. does this lopsided pattern have an established name? and if not, how do you draw the line between it and the broader problem without just calling it bias?
