I've been trying to understand the ethics angle of panpsychism — the worry that if sentience is fundamental then suffering might be everywhere. There's a specific argumentative move I keep seeing and I don't know if it has a name. People say basic physical entities have some kind of inner experience, but they insit it's completely neutral, no pleasure or pain. Then they explain human consciousness as what happens when lots of these entities are arranged smoothly together, and they explain suffering as what happens when that arrangment breaks down and the parts are out of sync. So well-being is the integrated version and suffering is the disintegrated version, but in both cases the parts themselves stay valence-free. It's not just the combination problem, because that asks how macro-mind arises from micro-mind; this is specifically about why suffering always seems to map onto disintegration while happiness maps onto integration, with nothing in the base level carrying either charge. Does this asymmetry have an established term? If not, can you coin one that distinguishes it from the combination problem and from regular emergentism about consciousness? I want to know exactly what the phenomenon covers and where its edges are — like, does it apply only to valence, or could you have disintegration that produces joy?
