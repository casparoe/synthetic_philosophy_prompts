I'm currently taking an upper-division seminar in population ethics, and I've been trying to stabilize my own reflective equilibrium on cases that pit severe, concentrated harm against astronomically large numbers of imperceptible harms. My verdicts are as follows. First, one person tortured for fifty years is far worse than 3^^3 people each suffering a dust speck in the eye for one second. Second, the same torture remains worse even if the number of dust-speck victims is raised to 10 raised to the power of 3^^3, though I confess this is the point where my confidence wavers. Third, one person paralyzed from the neck down for life is worse than every human being who ever lives—roughly one hundred billion—each experiencing a single itch so faint they never notice it. Fourth, one person enduring a constant, moderate migraine for one full year is worse than 10^30 people each enduring an identical migraine for exactly one femtosecond, too brief to register consciously. Fifth, one person being killed instantly is worse than 10^50 people each having their lifespan shortened by one millisecond with no awareness whatsoever. But here is the tension: one person subjected to a continuous, barely audible background hum for eighty years strikes me as clearly worse than a single other individual suffering the same hum for one second; yet when I imagine 10^40 people each experiencing that identical hum for exactly one Planck-time interval, distributed across all possible worlds, with zero lasting effect, I am genuinely unsure whether the aggregate outweighs the single lifetime case, and I feel pulled in both directions.

Is there a coherent principle—perhaps one that applies a non-linear weighting function to suffering based on its intensity-duration integral within a single, unified consciousness, or that introduces a phenomenal-salience threshold below which micro-harms fail to accumulate—that captures all five of my firmer verdicts without simply dismissing my uncertainty in the sixth? If no single principle can accommodate every judgment, I want to know which verdict I should abandon and why. Finally, apply whatever principle you settle on to a case I have not listed: a single person endures a persistent, low-grade anxiety that never escalates into panic but colors every waking hour for fifty years, versus 10^40 individuals each experiencing a single, sub-noticeable neural flicker of unease that leaves no memory or behavioral trace. Does the principle rank the individual lifetime case as worse, and does that result feel like a correction to my intuition or a vindication of it?
