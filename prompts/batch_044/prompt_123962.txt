I'm drafting the conceptual framework for a seminar paper on mental causation, and before I commit to the architecture I want to check whether my core positions are jointly sustainable. Here is where I stand: I endorse a non-reductive physicalism according to which mental properties are genuinely distinct from, and not identical to, the physical properties that realize them, and I take them to be multiply realizable across different physical substrates. At the same time, I accept the causal closure of physics—every physical event has a sufficient physical cause—so I do not dismiss the causal exclusion argument but treat it as a serious constraint. I also hold that mental causation is real and widespread, not epiphenomenal: my intention to raise my arm is genuinely part of the causal history of the arm's rising. I reject both eliminativism and type-identity reductionism as adequate responses, and I regard systematic overdetermination as metaphysically unacceptable in this domain, not merely improbable.

More recently, I've been considering whether mental properties might be reconceived as autopoietic control structures—functional profiles that maintain organizational closure within a self-regulating system, drawing on cybernetic models of living organization—such that their causal efficacy derives from participating in a self-maintaining loop rather than from competing with lower-level physical causes. If I add this fifth commitment, do the five hang together? Specifically, does the autopoietic framing genuinely resolve the tension between closure and mental causation, or does it smuggle in downward causation that violates the closure principle? If there is an unavoidable contradiction, please rank the revisions by philosophical cost: abandoning non-reductive physicalism, abandoning pervasive mental causation, accepting systematic overdetermination, or dropping the autopoietic framing. Also indicate whether any of the apparent tensions dissolve once the framing is applied correctly, so I can distinguish real contradictions from misunderstandings of my own position.
