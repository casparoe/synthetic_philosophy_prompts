I take a qualified Szaszian position: that psychiatric diagnoses of severe conditions such as schizophrenia or major depression are not, in the relevant sense, brain diseases analogous to diabetes or Alzheimer's, but rather normative designations for patterns of thought, affect, and behavior that violate social expectations, expressed in medical language that obscures the evaluative judgment involved; and that this distinction carries direct moral weight, since involuntary psychiatric intervention can then be justified only by openly defended social-protection or paternalist reasons, never by an unexamined appeal to medical necessity. I further hold that findings from psychopharmacology and neuroimaging — for instance, that antipsychotics alter dopamine signaling or that functional connectivity differs in affected groups — do not by themselves defeat this view, because showing biological correlates or pharmacological responsiveness is not identical to showing a pathophysiology that is causally sufficient for the condition and conceptually independent of normative framing. What, then, would genuinely count against this position? I want the arguments, observations, conceptual distinctions, or empirical developments that would be decisive rather than merely awkward, sorted by force. Would the identification of a biomarker that predicts symptom onset with near-perfect specificity and responds to targeted intervention, entirely prior to any social labeling, be fatal, or could a defender absorb it by distinguishing brain dysfunction from mental disease? What specific predictions does this normative-designation view make that rival biomedical or integrative biopsychosocial accounts do not make, and have any of those predictions failed? Have defenders of this position qualified it into unfalsifiability — perhaps by defining disease so narrowly that no psychiatric finding could ever satisfy it — and if so, does that insulation preserve its moral insight or render it empty? Please rank the potential defeaters, note which ones a committed defender of this view would themselves concede as damaging rather than dismissible, and give an honest assessment of whether the position risks nothing empirically or has merely been protected at the cost of explanatory poverty.
