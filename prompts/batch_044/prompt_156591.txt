I'm a regional commons governance officer who reviews ten to fifteen resource-management proposals each year for a highland watershed that operates as what I call an asymmetric common-pool public good (ACPPG): the alpine meadow is subtractable for local herders practicing rotational grazing, since grass cover depletes with consumption and regenerates on an annual cycle, yet it simultaneously produces non-rival, intergenerational benefits for a migratory bird population that nests there on a multi-year return cycle, where nesting success depends on grass height but the birds cannot be excluded and do not consume the stock on the same temporal scale. I have roughly forty-eight hours per proposal, must sign off with written justification, and work with a team that has no philosophical training; I need a reusable decision procedure—ordered questions, criteria for a sufficient answer, explicit stop points that return a "no" or hand the decision to a human values judgment—grounded in the logic of common-pool resource design principles and public-goods provision theory, but translated so a non-specialist can run it offline without consulting literature.

The procedure should handle proposals that mix technical management—grazing quotas, seasonal closures, fencing, predator control—with claims about justice to present herders and obligations to future bird populations. Specifically, it must distinguish exclusion mechanisms that are technically feasible for one dimension (issuing grazing permits) from those impossible for another (preventing bird access), account for temporal asymmetry in regeneration (annual grass versus multi-year bird cycles), and flag when a proposal solves a collective-action problem for one group while externalizing costs onto a non-participating group. Apply it to three proposals: (a) a rotational-grazing permit system combined with delayed nesting-zone closures; (b) installing permanent fencing to protect nesting grounds, which would physically exclude herders from parts of the meadow; and (c) a predator-culling program intended to boost bird numbers without restricting grazing. One of these should yield a recommendation my office will find politically unwelcome, and the procedure should still produce it clearly. Throughout, mark which steps are empirical checks and which are unavoidable judgment calls about values.
