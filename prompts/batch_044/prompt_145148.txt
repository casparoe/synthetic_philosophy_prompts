I am drafting a philosophical scene for a novel in which two interlocutors — call them the Malcolmian and the Dennettian — debate whether dreams are experiences at all, not in the abstract, but while observing a specific case: a sleeper awakened from deep slow-wave sleep with no detectable REM immediately writes, without revision, a coherent first-person account of being trapped in a burning building, an account that clearly integrates the sound of an actual fire alarm heard through the wall but transforms it into a narrative of rescue and escape. The Malcolmian should defend the view that there is no criterion of identity that allows us to treat a reported dream as an experience occurring during sleep, since the only evidence is the retrospective report, which may be a confabulation rather than a memory. The Dennettian should defend the view that dreaming is not an experience that happens in sleep but a narrative loaded upon awakening, perhaps analogous to a cassette being played into consciousness. Crucially, both must address the problem I am calling narrative residue: if an external stimulus is woven into the sleeper’s report with such specificity, does that integration happen during sleep, during the act of writing, or during the retrospective construction of the narrative? Each speaker should respond directly to the other’s most recent argument — if the Malcolmian claims the alarm-integration proves only that waking cognition is inventive, the Dennettian should explain whether that invention requires an experiential substrate to be transformed, or whether the cassette can load raw sensory traces without any accompanying phenomenology. Conversely, if the Dennettian argues that the writing itself is part of the narrative construction, the Malcolmian should say whether that collapses the distinction between experiencing and reporting, or whether it strengthens the claim that there is never an independent experience to be reported. The dialogue should be rigorous, balanced, and written in a polished, literary register appropriate to a novel; neither speaker should be a strawman. Please do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific editions; I want the scene to stand on conceptual precision alone.
