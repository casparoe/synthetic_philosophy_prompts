Here are my intuitions concerning the commensurability of pleasure and pain, particularly with regard to lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, and I should like to know whether a coherent principle unites them, which verdicts it obliges me to abandon, and what it implies for cases I have not considered.

First, I hold that a single episode of extreme, torture-level suffering lexically outweighs any conceivable quantity or quality of pleasure; no cosmic ledger of joy can render it acceptable. Second, I deny that distributing a large aggregate of minor sufferings—chronic, low-grade anxiety spread across vast populations over millennia—triggers the same lexical veto, even if the summed magnitude equals or exceeds that of one torture event. Third, regarding suffering that is severe and continuous yet remains just below the extreme boundary, I am inclined to judge a life containing it, balanced only against moderate pleasure, as not worth living; however, I cannot determine whether this reflects a concealed lexical boundary or merely a steep but continuous trade-off. Fourth, I believe that fragmenting an intense suffering into imperceptible micro-moments, so that no discrete instant crosses the threshold, eliminates its veto power entirely, despite the integral suffering remaining constant. Fifth, when comparing two possible worlds—one offering vastly greater total pleasure but containing a small number of individuals who suffer torture, and another offering only moderate pleasure but containing none—I unfailingly prefer the latter.

Can these verdicts be reconciled by a single principle—perhaps one that attends to the structural or phenomenological form of suffering rather than to its bare quantity? If reconciliation requires abandoning one or more verdicts, please say which and why. I am particularly concerned with the tension between my refusal to aggregate minor sufferings in the second case and my willingness to disregard an identical integral suffering when it is fragmented in the fourth. Finally, please apply your proposed principle to the case that I keep revisiting: a solitary being whose existence comprises pleasure of extraordinary intensity together with an unbroken, unfragmented suffering that hovers perpetually just beneath the torture threshold, never quite crossing it. Is such a life worth living on your account?

Please answer in British English.
