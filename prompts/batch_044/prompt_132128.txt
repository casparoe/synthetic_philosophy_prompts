Please keep the language concrete—refer to an actual visual detail like a synthetic image displayed under gallery lighting—rather than using abstract philosophical labels, and make the rhyme and repetition do the argumentative work. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who has taught aesthetics recognizes the circularity problem inherent in institutional accounts, but it should also stand alone for a reader who has never encountered the debate; do not include citations, footnotes, or references to specific texts. I'm writing for a general audience that follows science and culture, not for specialists, so the tone should be precise but not academic, and the poem should leave the tension open rather than resolving it with a punch line.
