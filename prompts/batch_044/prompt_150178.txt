I've been getting into this debate about whether the Anthropocene is even a real historical thing or just a branding exercise for geoligists, and it's become kind of personal cause my roommate is doing environmental history and keeps saying I'm reducing culture to ice cores. So here's what I need: the full desiderata for any theory that claims to define the start of a human-driven geological epoch — not just the Anthropocene, but the framework itself. What cases must it actually handle?

For one, it has to deal with the fact that the plutonium spike, the CO2 curve, and the plastic layer don't line up in the same year, and some regions show the signal decades later than others. It also can't collapse the difference between "humans started altering ecosystems" — which happened with Aboriginal fire management and Amazonian terra preta long before industrialization — and "the alteration became globally synchornous in the stratigraphic record." Those pre-industrial cases shouldn't be treated as noise, but they also shouldn't just push the start date back to 10,000 years ago without explanation. Give me the list of requirements, with why each matters, and flag which are non-negotiable versus camp-specific prefferences (like whether global synchrony is actually required, or whether local stratigraphic committees should have veto power over the date). Most importantly: is there a subset of these desiderata that's jointly unsatisfiable? Like, can you really have both strict global synchrony and meaningful local causal specificity? If so, that's the part I actually care about — not a scorecard of existing proposals, just the criteria and where they break.
