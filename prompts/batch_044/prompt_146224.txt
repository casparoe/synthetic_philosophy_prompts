I sit on the Joint Regulatory Committee for Agricultural Welfare Standards. We must circulate a formal written opinion before next month’s vote on Proposed Amendment 14-B, which would apply minimal welfare protocols—specifically, density caps and standardized humane-killing requirements—to black soldier fly larvae cultivated for industrial protein meal. The staff analysis introduces an Aggregate Moral Weight Index that multiplies estimated population size, intensity of potential suffering, and a probability coefficient for sentience. Applying this index, the staff conclude that if the per-individual probability of larval sentience exceeds 0.01 percent, the aggregate expected moral weight of these insects within our jurisdiction surpasses that of all farmed poultry combined. Proponents argue that our existing precautionary principle demands consistent application regardless of taxonomic distance, and that excluding insects solely because of a low individual probability systematically discounts vast populations in a way that undermines the coherence of our welfare calculus. Opponents counter that with such a low per-capita probability, diverting enforcement resources from vertebrate programs—where sentience is far more certain—would cause greater certain harm in exchange for highly speculative benefit, and that the index improperly treats probabilistic expectation as a direct moral claim rather than a decision-theoretic heuristic. The committee’s mandate requires a clear bottom-line recommendation—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—together with reasoning that treats the strongest consideration on the losing side with intellectual honesty, specifies a concrete implementation timeframe or sunset clause if the recommendation is affirmative, and engages the philosophical tension directly rather than merely invoking legal definitions of animal welfare jurisdiction. Please draft the opinion memo I will circulate, structured as an actionable institutional document with an explicit recommendation, a direct response to the scale argument as it applies to this concrete case, criteria by which the staff and affected parties could concretely respond, and conditions under which the committee should revisit the decision should new evidence about invertebrate consciousness emerge.
