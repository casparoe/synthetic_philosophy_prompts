I am preparing an exercise for my graduate seminar on contemporary political philosophy, and I wish to push students beyond the standard textbook opposition between liberal individualism and communitarian embeddedness. Please write a dialogue between two interlocutors of equal philosophical competence—one defending a liberal view that treats autonomy as ontologically prior to social attachment and requires procedural neutrality from institutions, the other advancing a communitarian position that regards the self as partly constituted by inherited narratives and unchosen obligations—focusing on the moral legitimacy of what I call an "ancestral obligation registry": a hypothetical mechanism that automatically assigns citizens collective maintenance duties for cultural practices established by prior generations, allowing exit only through a burdensome, multi-year formal disassociation process.

The dialogue must contain real back-and-forth rather than parallel speeches: each speaker should respond directly to the specific reasoning just offered by the other, and neither should be reduced to a strawman. Present your response as exactly five numbered exchanges, each exchange comprising brief contributions from both speakers together amounting to no more than two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
