Distinguish between what might be called “indexical conventions”—local coordination rules whose normative force derives from one’s contingent social position rather than from universal moral principle—and categorical moral obligations. Apply this distinction carefully to tipping practices, queueing etiquette, and jaywalking norms: do these generate genuine moral duties for those who find themselves within the relevant practice, or do they yield only prudential, aesthetic, or game-theoretic reasons for compliance? Crucially, assess whether a visitor who knowingly and deliberately flouts such a local convention commits a moral wrong that is distinct from mere practical folly, or whether outsiders stand in a special normative relation—perhaps one of permission rather than obligation—to these rules. Write an essay that takes a clear, defended position on whether everyday conventions carry authentic moral weight and on whether visitors are morally bound to follow them; weigh the main counterarguments, but reach and justify a specific verdict rather than ending on an agnostic note.
