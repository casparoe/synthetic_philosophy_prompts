I am the ethics compliance officer for an ed-tech firm that deploys adaptive tutoring algorithms in state secondary schools. Each week I must review proposed algorithmic interventions, with forty-five minutes per case, using only an undergraduate background in applied ethics, and my sign-off is mandatory before deployment. I need a reusable audit procedure that operationalises the principle of "respect for rational pedagogical agency" — that a learner's cognitive effort must not be treated as a mere optimisable input, but must be honoured as an expression of rational self-direction, except where there is clear evidence of imminent severe harm, in which case any override must be transparent, proportionate, and revisable by the learner.

Please construct the procedure as an ordered sequence of checks that a non-philosopher can run: for each step state the precise question, what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure halts with a rejection, where it hands the decision to human judgment because values rather than facts are at stake, and where it permits sign-off. The procedure must distinguish (a) transparent adaptation revisable by the learner, (b) opaque optimisation that treats cognitive load as a variable to be managed without disclosure, and (c) justified paternalistic override. Include explicit criteria for identifying "imminent severe harm" and "revisability" in this context.

Then run the procedure on three proposals:

1. The system detects repeated failure in calculus exercises and automatically substitutes simpler problems without notifying the student, projecting a thirty per cent retention gain.

2. The system receives an explicit student instruction to "increase difficulty," detects concurrent physiological stress markers, maintains the harder setting, but sends a message explaining the tension by reference to the student's previously stated goal of passing the university entrance examination, and provides a one-click option to revert.

3. A student from a minority-language background consistently selects easier texts; the algorithm infers, without direct evidence, that this choice reflects family pressure rather than authentic preference, and proposes to override the selection.

Please include at least one case where the procedure yields an answer I am likely to dislike as an efficiency-focused officer. Clearly state what the procedure cannot settle, and flag any points where professional judgment remains unavoidable.

Finally, please indicate how confident you are in the answers given, and identify the specific point about which you are least certain.
