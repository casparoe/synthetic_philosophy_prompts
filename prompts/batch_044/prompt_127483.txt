Write a short story, using British English, that dramatises the combination problem faced by panpsychism. The protagonist should be an artificial composite consciousness—call it Meridian—built from thousands of minimal experiential units, each restricted to registering exactly one invariant quale (a form of tactile pressure). Together they produce a unified, human-like inner life. As the narrative unfolds, Meridian becomes aware of experiencing a subtle shade of reflective melancholy that no individual unit can possess, not even derivatively. The story should trace Meridian's reasoning about whether this emotion genuinely belongs to it as a singular subject, or whether it is merely an artefactual product of the relations among its parts—thereby raising the combination problem in concrete terms. Do not grant the micro-units hidden capacities, do not resolve the philosophical tension with a definitive answer, and avoid treating the scenario as a loose metaphor for ordinary human consciousness. Focus instead on the precise conceptual difficulty: how macro-level phenomenal properties can arise from micro-level ones that seem categorically incapable of generating them.
