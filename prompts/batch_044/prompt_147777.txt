As a retired natural scientist who has only recently turned to philosophical study, I have become absorbed by a problem at the boundary of algorithmic information theory and normative ethics that standard introductory treatments seem to overlook entirely. I am referring to the suggestion that the Solomonoff universal prior—which weights possible worlds by the inverse exponential of their Kolmogorov complexity—might be structurally malign with respect to the moral agents that are themselves instantiated inside those worlds. The specific worry is that any agent relying on Solomonoff induction to form expectations and guide action will be driven, by the very logic of simplicity-weighted prediction, toward forms of reasoning that aggregate outcomes in a linear, utility-maximizing fashion, while deontological constraints that resist such aggregation appear only as complex, ad hoc appendages to the world-description. Consequently, one might fear that consequentialism is not merely one ethical theory among others that an embedded agent might adopt, but rather the unavoidable cognitive architecture imposed by the inductive framework within which the agent necessarily operates.

With this framing in mind, I ask you to write an essay that weighs the arguments for and against the claim that the Solomonoff prior is indeed malign in this precise sense. Your essay should consider whether deontological rules can be encoded as genuinely simple constraints on world-histories without being reduced to instrumental heuristics for aggregate welfare, and whether an agent inside the prior can coherently reject consequentialist optimization without undermining the inductive norms that govern its reasoning. Examine also whether the malignancy, if real, is a feature of the prior itself or merely an artifact of how embedded agents misapply simplicity metrics to normative domains. Do not conclude with agnosticism: defend a clear verdict on whether consequentialist dominance within the universal prior constitutes a genuine structural defect, and justify that conclusion with explicit reasoning.
