I'm trying to map out what I actually think about grounding, case by case, and I keep hitting the same wall. Water being H2O is grounded in chemical structure — yes, totally. A shadow's darkness is grounded in the absence of light hitting the object, but its shape is grounded in the object itself; so partial grounding is okay, and multi-grounding is okay too. Money having value is grounded in collective agreement, but that agreement itself seems partly grounded in the value of money — there's a loop, yet I still think it's genuinely grounded, not free-floating. Mathematical truths like 7 being prime: I don't think they're grounded in physical stuff, but I also don't think they're just ungrounded — maybe they ground themselves? But then normative truths, like torture being wrong, feel grounded in the nature of conscious suffering, which sounds natural, except suffering might depend on normative framing... so I'm going back and forth. Here's another one: if you run a perfect digital twin of a person's brain, is the twin's mental state grounded in the original person's state, in the computation, or in both? I think only in the computation, which breaks the usual pattern where mental states are grounded in biological brains. So is there one principel — maybe about explanatory proximity without circularity, or about modal dependence — that gets all these? Or do I have to drop either the loop-acceptance for money or the self-grounding for math? Also what should it say about a case I haven't listed: is the fact that democracy is legitimate grounded in the consent of the governed, or is consent itself grounded in democratic legitimacy? And if there's circularity there too, do I treat it like money or like math?
