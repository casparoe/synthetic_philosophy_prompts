I've been thinking that relying on LLMs for reasoning doesn't just save time — it actually erodes what we could call epistemic proprioception, the ability to feel out your own evidential pathways. Once you outsource the verifcation loop, the damage is structural and mostly irreversable, even if you later stop using the tool. What's actually going to count against this? I want the honest defeaters — not just 'people can learn to check outputs', but what observations, experiments, or conceptual distinctions would show the erosion isn't real or isn't irreversable? Rank them by how fatal they'd be, and say whether the view can be qualified into unfalsifiability without losing its point.
