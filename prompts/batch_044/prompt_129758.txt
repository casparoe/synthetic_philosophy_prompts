I am a PhD student in philosophy of history preparing a colloquium-style interview for a graduate seminar podcast series. The guest is an unnamed defender of what I will call redundancy pluralism in macrohistorical explanation: she maintains that major historical outcomes—such as the dissolution of the Western Roman Empire or the emergence of industrial capitalism—are not merely multi-causal but genuinely overdetermined, with multiple independent sets of conditions each sufficient for the result. She distinguishes parallel sufficient conditions, in which independent causal streams converge on an outcome, from conjunctive necessary conditions, in which interdependent factors must jointly obtain, and she advocates counterfactual redundancy testing—systematically varying combinations of absent conditions to check whether an outcome remains stable—rather than standard single-factor counterfactual analysis. She also claims that the historiographical habit of identifying a primary cause systematically obscures these redundant pathways.

I need eight to ten interview questions that press on the actual content of her position rather than allowing generic restatements. Specifically, please include:

1. At least one question on why the parallel-versus-conjunctive distinction does genuine explanatory work rather than functioning as a merely taxonomic exercise, paired with a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that the distinction alters which counterfactuals historians ought to run.

2. At least one question on how redundancy pluralism applies to microhistorical explanations—say, a particular minister’s decision to sign a treaty—where overdetermination seems implausible because the causal chain appears singular and agent-specific, with a follow-up anticipating her move to some form of scale-sensitive pluralism.

3. At least one question on the normative or methodological implication: if selecting a primary cause is misleading, does her view require that all redundant pathways receive equal narrative weight, and by what criteria should historians govern explanatory selection in practice? Include a follow-up for her probable answer that explanatory economy can be preserved without imposing a causal hierarchy.

4. At least one question that presses the interaction between redundancy pluralism and historical relativism—specifically, whether the presence of multiple sufficient conditions makes macrohistorical explanation more objective, because redundant pathways provide cross-checking constraints, or more perspectival, because different scholars can legitimately foreground different sufficient conditions.

5. One concluding question that asks what specific empirical finding or historiographical result could genuinely weaken her view, designed to make it difficult for her to deflect by saying the position is merely a research program.

Please frame all questions formally and appropriately for an academic podcast audience, with each follow-up explicitly keyed to the answer she is most likely to give.
