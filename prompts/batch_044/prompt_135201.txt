I have recently been appointed to an advisory committee tasked with framing regulatory guidelines for automated arbitration platforms that must adjudicate contracts containing genuinely contradictory obligations—specifically, cases in which two valid jurisdictional mandates impose opposite requirements on the same party. The philosophical implications have become personally urgent for me. Please write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors: one who defends dialetheism, maintaining that such contradictions can be literally true and must be acknowledged as such in legal interpretation; and one who holds a non-dialetheist paraconsistent position, arguing that reasoning should tolerate contradictions without ever affirming them as true. They should discuss whether accepting true contradictions is metaphysically or practically necessary for resolving these disputes, or whether a non-explosive logical framework alone is sufficient. The exchange must be a real back-and-forth: each speaker should directly address the other’s arguments about ontological commitment, interpretive methodology, and practical decision-making, rather than speaking past one another.
