Please write the opening remarks for a workshop attended by senior translators and field coordinators who draft emergency public-health directives in refugee camps. The audience is split: roughly half are applied linguists familiar with George Orwell's argument that decayed language—especially passive constructions and prefabricated phrases—both signals and enables political corruption by letting speakers avoid owning the harm embedded in policy; the other half are exhausted public-health practitioners with no philosophical training. The remarks should run about five minutes and must do real structural work with Orwell's idea: avoid passive voice when describing failures of aid distribution, reject euphemisms such as "collateral disruption" or "resource optimization," and explicitly name the agencies and states responsible for specific harms. At the same time, the tone must orient and sustain practitioners rather than lecture them, and it must not attribute false motives to anyone in the room. Because the audience includes both experts and non-experts, every philosophical reference should be immediately intelligible to the practitioners without being patronizing to the linguists. Begin the response with the remarks themselves, with no preface, commentary, or explanation of structure. Write in American English.
