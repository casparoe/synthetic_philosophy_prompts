Please defend the view that promises bind not primarily because they generate legitimate expectations or because breaking them causes harm, but because they enact a temporary jurisdictional transfer: in promising, the promisor places a specific domain of her practical reasoning under the promisee's normative authority. Breaking the promise is then structurally analogous to a legislature revoking its own enacted statute—except that the revocation is self-undermining, since the promisor remains both legislator and subject. Explain how this delegated-authority account handles promises unknown to the promisee, addresses the objection that it overstates personal autonomy, and distinguishes promise-breaking from mere change of mind in cases where no harm or disappointed expectation is present.
