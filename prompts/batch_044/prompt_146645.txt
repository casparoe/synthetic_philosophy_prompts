My claim: Darwin's inversion — selection producing design without a designer's comprehension — and Turing's inversion — an observer attributing comprehension to a system with none — aren't parallel analogues like people assume; they run in opposite directions of fit, and that confusion has occured in a lot of literature linking evolutionary design to machine intelligence. For each: how damaging, what do I concede upfront, my two-minute reply, and where that reply is weakest. Include the question I'm praying nobody asks — probably whether I'm smuggling teleology back into Darwin — and flag the weakest point in my own position that I should defend rather than hide. Keep it under 300 words, bullet format ok, no bibliographies or citation lists since I need this usable offline.
