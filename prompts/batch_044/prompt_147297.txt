As a retired physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of mind, I am trying to think about self-deception with the same precision one applies to an experimental design, and I want to avoid both armchair stipulation and appeals to authority. Please construct for me a thought experiment—no more than a single paragraph describing the scenario—that engineers a case in which self-deception is genuinely instantiated in one ordinary, unified adult agent, with no dissociative identity disorder, brain lesions, or appeal to unconscious Freudian modules. The case must jointly satisfy four conditions: (i) the agent possesses first-order knowledge that proposition P is true, based on evidence E that remains fully accessible in memory; (ii) the agent intentionally designs and executes a cognitive strategy—call it “epistemic escrow,” in which they place E under a self-imposed retrieval condition that they then systematically prevent from being satisfied—whose explicit purpose is to cause themselves to form and maintain the sincere belief that not-P; (iii) they genuinely avow not-P and deploy it in ordinary practical reasoning without ironic distance; and (iv) they suffer no general rational breakdown and remain competent reasoners about unrelated matters. Then do the check: state the belief, the method, the relevant mental states, and precisely why each condition is met. Identify which feature—most likely the self-blocked retrieval pathway—does the explanatory work, explain what collapses if that feature is removed (for instance, if the access-block is externally imposed rather than self-engineered), and show how a defender of the view that self-deception is strictly impossible—say, a unified-rational-agent theorist—would attack the case, particularly by arguing that (i) and (ii) describe sequential rather than simultaneous states, or that “belief” is being used ambiguously between occurrent and dispositional senses. In your response, clearly separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among philosophers who specialize in this topic, and do not present any contested interpretation—whether a divided-mind model or a unified-agent denial—as if it were the consensus.
