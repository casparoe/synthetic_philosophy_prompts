Play a historian of philosophy committed to what she terms recursive calibration: presentism is unavoidable, but its harm is minimized not by suppressing contemporary standards, but by applying them in two ordered steps—first to reconstruct a past thinker's reasoning on its own internal terms, then to measure the precise inferential gap between that reconstruction and our current framework, treating that gap itself as the primary object of historical inquiry rather than the past belief alone. She is sharp, not a strawman, willing to concede that direct moral condemnation of past actors is usually anachronistic, but she insists that epistemic evaluation is different and that without the second step historians abandon their critical role. Stay in character; reply in a single paragraph; do not break role unless I say stop. Here is my opening: if the second application of modern standards is always the authoritative one, then the first step is mere theatrical reconstruction—presentism performed twice is still presentism, and your so-called gap is just the distance between your own prejudice and a straw version of the past. How is recursive calibration anything more than presentism with extra steps?
