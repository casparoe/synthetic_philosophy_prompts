I am preparing material for an advanced undergraduate seminar on the boundary between perception and cognition, and I have become stuck in what feels like a conceptual knot rather than a straightforward empirical disagreement. We have been examining cases where prior beliefs appear to shape reported visual experience—such as when induced expectations about an object’s typical orientation shift subjects’ judgments of ambiguous figures in forced-choice tasks, or when knowledge about statistical regularities seems to accelerate detection in cluttered visual arrays. My working assumption has been that for these cases to count as genuine cognitive penetration, as opposed to mere post-perceptual judgment contamination, the belief must exert influence at an early processing stage—perhaps by altering gain parameters on orientation-selective channels or restructuring feature binding—rather than merely reweighting the interpretation applied once a stable percept has already formed.

A colleague recently pressed me with a hypothetical that has unsettled this framing: suppose future work establishes that all observed effects are mediated entirely by attentional allocation occurring just before feature binding is completed, with no direct modulation of the feature detectors themselves and certainly no alteration of activation profiles in primary visual cortex—only a change in which representations are selected for conscious report. I found myself inclined to say that in such a scenario cognitive penetration has not genuinely occurred, because the belief has influenced selection mechanisms rather than penetrating “perception proper.” Yet this response seems to presuppose that there is an architecturally clean boundary between perception and selection, and that cognitive penetration is defined specifically by direct restructuring of low-level feature maps rather than by any high-level influence on perceptual content. Is the distinction I am drawing between parameter-setting within perceptual inference and direct modulation of early feature detectors tracking something philosophically substantive, or am I defending a stipulative definition that wrongly makes the empirical literature irrelevant? And if it turns out that high-level states never reach V1, have I thereby shown that cognitive penetration does not exist, or have I built a false presupposition into the very framing of the debate?
