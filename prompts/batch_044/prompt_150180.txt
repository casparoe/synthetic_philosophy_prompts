I sit on my city's public-integrity oversight panel. The administration proposes accepting a $40 million infrastructure grant from a private consortium whose supply chain relies on documented forced-labor practices; the funds would build a trauma center in an underserved district, and no comparable public funding exists. The mayor argues that refusal leaves the district without care and does not help the laborers, while critics contend that acceptance makes the city structurally complicit in exploitation. Draft the panel's formal opinion recommending whether to endorse, reject, or conditionally endorse the deal, with a concrete course of action and timeline, and with an honest treatment of the strongest consideration favoring the opposing view. In your analysis, distinguish between proximate moral stain—borne directly by the officials who approve the deal—and distributive moral stain, in which the ethical cost is displaced onto vulnerable populations who never consented to the bargain.
