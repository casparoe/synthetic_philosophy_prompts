I am organizing a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who defends Quinean moral naturalism—the view that ethical sentences do not describe an independent moral reality but are artifacts of our conceptual scheme, continuous with empirical science yet lacking its observational anchoring and revisable in principle like other parts of the web of belief. This has become personally urgent: two months ago, while serving on a clinical ethics committee, I watched a predictive model convert a patient’s functional status into a probability distribution and then into a normative recommendation to withdraw care, and I could not locate the precise point where description ended and prescription began. I want eight to ten interview questions that do not let the defender restate familiar slogans. Each should press where the literature actually presses, and each must include a follow-up keyed to the reply he is most likely to give.

Please include at least one question on how Quine’s refusal to include moral properties in his ontology interacts with his own claim that scientific theory choice is guided by pragmatic virtues—simplicity, familiarity, predictive scope—that are not themselves empirically observed; if these virtues are indispensable yet non-observational, why are they ontologically innocent while moral values are not? Include at least one question on whether cross-cultural moral disagreement is best understood, on Quine’s model, as a failure of radical translation—implying no shared fact of the matter—or as a divergence in non-cognitive attitudes that share only surface grammar. And include at least one question about the institutional practice I just described—embedding normative clinical recommendations inside predictive, probabilistic frameworks—asking whether Quine’s framework exposes this as a category error, legitimizes it as the natural extension of empirical reasoning into value-laden domains, or reveals a hidden circularity in which pragmatic values must already be present to license the model’s design.

Keep your response under 600 words. Present the questions in numbered order, with the anticipated reply and the embedded follow-up clearly marked within each entry.
