I've been arguing with my coworker about whether death is actually bad for you, and I realized my own opinions are kind of scattered across podcasts and random reddit threads. I'm a software engineer with zero formal philosphy background, so I'm trying to write them down to see if they hang together before I embarass myself in the group chat. Here's where I land right now:

(a) The Epicurean line feels basically right: once you're dead there's no "you" left around to suffer anything, so death can't be a direct harm to the dead person in any experiential way.

(b) At the same time, I do think death is a real misfortune for the one who dies, mostly because it wipes out all the future good life they would've had — like cancelling a long-running project permanently with no backup.

(c) I also buy the symmetry idea: I don't feel bad that I wasn't existant in 1800, so I shouldn't feel bad about not being existant in 2100 either. The bare absence itself isn't the problem.

(d) Even with (c), I think it's totally rational to fear death and avoid it, because right now my relationships and side-projects make those future goods especially valuable to me, even if the later absence isn't painful.

(e) I'm a physicalist — no soul, just brain and body — but I think personal identity survives gradual replacement of biological neurons with synthetic ones, as long as psychological continuity stays intact. So in principle death is avoidable via slow uploading.

(f) But I also think that if my biological body dies and I'm reconstructed from a perfect backup file, that's not really me continuing; it's a copy running on new hardware. So biological death is still a harm because it kills the original process, even if a duplicate lives on.

Can you audit this set? Which pairs are genuinely in tension versus just looking weird on the surface, and how severe is each? What's the least costly revision if I have to drop one thing, and is there a single change that cleans up the most conflicts at once? I'd like to keep (d) and (e) if I can, but I don't want to cheat — I want to know what they actually commit me to and not just recieve a generic lecture.
