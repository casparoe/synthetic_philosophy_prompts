Here's an argument I cooked up for a study session and now it feels like it's eating everything. (1) Take any consistent formal system F. Gödel gives us a sentence G(F) thats true but unprovable in F. (2) A human mathematician can see that G(F) is true, since they can see F is consistent (or at least that if F is consistent then G(F) holds). (3) So if human reasoning were just F, they couldnt do that. (4) Therefore human minds arent identical to any consistent formal system -- thats the Lucas-Penrose move. (5) Now here's the twist: build an AI with base architecture A, and then bolt on a meta-layer M that checks consistency of A and outputs G(A). The combined system A+M can do what the human does for A. (6) But then Gödel applies to A+M too, giving G(A+M), which A+M cant prove. So we can bolt on M2, and so on. (7) The Lucas-Penrose advocate says: see, the human does this for THEIR own system, but no fixed formal system can do it for itself. Fair enough. (8) But now apply the same pattern not to individuals but to collective processes. Scientific communities over generations keep revising their own formal models of reasoning. Evolution produces organisms that adapt to descriptions of themselves. Even a market with reflexive pricing models updates it's rules based on predictions of those rules. Each one looks like an infinite hierarchy of self-correction, escaping any fixed formalization. (9) So the argument doesnt just show minds arent computers; it seems to show that ANY adaptive system that can represent its own rules isnt mechanistic. That proves way too much -- it makes mechanism useless as a category. (10) Wheres the exit? Is (2) wrong because humans dont actually see the Gödel sentence in a non-formalizable way, they just make a leap of faith? Or is the mistake in applying the individual argument to collectives? Or is there a hidden equivocation between being a formal system and being fully describable by one? I want to keep the intuition that theres something special about self-reference in minds, but I dont want to accidentally declare that evolutionary biology isnt mechanistic. Which step do I reject, and what does that cost me?
