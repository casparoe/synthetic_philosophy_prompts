At my school's philosophy circle, two members debated Nozick's position that we should apply utilitarian reasoning to animals but Kantian respect to persons. Maya argued that this creates a clean boundary: we maximize welfare for animals, so factory farming is wrong only if it produces more suffering than alternative systems, but we never treat people merely as means. Leo countered that once we consider animals with complex future-oriented projects—say, elephants with enduring social bonds—treating them as replaceable utility-bearers ignores the very individuality that makes Kantianism appealing for humans. Maya replied that individuality is a matter of degree, not kind, and that Nozick deliberately fixes the boundary at rational autonomy rather than social complexity. Leo then introduced a distinction he called project-based moral status: an animal need not possess full rational autonomy to have its own long-term projects violated, so even within Nozick's framework we should grant some side-constraints against killing or disrupting those projects, not merely minimize aggregate suffering. Maya said this smuggles Kantianism back into the animal realm and betrays Nozick's explicit division. They asked me to referee, and I honestly could not. Please explain who is right about what: does Nozick's framework allow side-constraints for animals with sustained projects, or does it restrict them to utilitarian calculation? Is Leo's project-based distinction a legitimate extension, or does it collapse the boundary Nozick intended? And who is more right overall?
