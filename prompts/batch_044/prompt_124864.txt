I'm writing an essay for an advanced seminar in philosophy of social science, and I've become stuck on what seems like a conceptual circularity at the heart of Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis. The standard interpretation treats the Protestant ethic as an independent cultural cause of modern capitalism, yet Weber himself emphasizes that values are historically embedded and shaped by the very material conditions they are said to explain. I want you to argue for the position that Weber's claim is best reconstructed not as an efficient-causal thesis—culture generating economic change ex nihilo—but as a modal-structural claim about elective affinity, in which the ascetic value-system operates as a constraint that determines which forms of economic rationality are live possibilities, rather than as an autonomous force producing capitalism.

In developing this argument, please address the objection that removing causal efficacy from values renders Weber's thesis empirically empty, reducing it to a tautology about conceptual compatibility between religious asceticism and rational bookkeeping. You should also integrate the idea of value inversion—cases in which sustained economic practices retroactively reshape the values initially presumed to motivate them—while showing how this does not collapse the reconstruction into pure economic determinism. Specifically, defend the view that values retain explanatory significance only when treated as mediating mechanisms that translate structural pressures into patterned action, not as independent variables on a par with material conditions. Your response should take seriously the broader philosophical question of whether culture can ever be a cause in social explanation, or whether axiological factors must always be understood as selecting among possibilities that structural conditions have already made available.
