Below are draft notes for an essay on Spinoza’s democratic theory. The central claim is that Spinoza’s defense of democracy in the Tractatus Theologico-Politicus and Tractatus Politicus is conventionally read as an argument for majority rule grounded in collective power, but if we take seriously his doctrine of common notions and the thesis that reason is identical in all people, democracy acquires an epistemic structure that standard interpretations overlook: democratic deliberation is not merely an aggregation of private judgments but the institutional form through which adequate ideas become collectively available. I extend this by proposing that Spinoza’s multitudo, when guided by reason, operates analogously to a distributed cognitive network in which no single mind holds sovereign access to adequate knowledge. Consequently, political authority ought to be dispersed not only horizontally among citizens but also vertically among local assemblies or councils, according to where rational understanding actually emerges in practice. This yields what I call “epistemic federalism”: a Spinozist democracy in which the right to command tracks the actual distribution of adequate ideas within the body politic, rather than a uniform one-person-one-vote scheme applied indiscriminately.

Please comment on these notes. Specifically: (1) Is the reading of common notions and the multitudo textually defensible, or does it illegitimately import modern concepts—such as distributed cognition or differentiated epistemic authority—that distort Spinoza’s metaphysics? (2) Does Spinoza’s actual political writing support the inference to vertical dispersion and differentiated local authority, or is this an unsupported extrapolation? (3) Where the interpretation is genuinely contested among specialists in Spinoza’s political thought, clearly separate what is settled—for instance, that democracy is for Spinoza the most natural form of government—from what remains speculative or minority; do not present a contested claim as if it were consensus. (4) Are there internal tensions I have missed, particularly between this proposed epistemic hierarchy and Spinoza’s commitment to the equality of natural right? (5) Setting aside accuracy, does this framing illuminate Spinoza’s position or render it more obscure?
