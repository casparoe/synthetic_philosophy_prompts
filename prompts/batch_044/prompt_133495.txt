Write an essay on the following controversy in mechanistic anomaly detection. A mechanistic detector identifies anomalies by comparing an observed process to a reference mechanism rather than to a statistical distribution. But this raises a foundational question: under what conditions should two processes count as instantiating the same mechanism? Consider in particular a case of synthetic mechanism transplantation: a biological signaling pathway is replaced by an engineered biochemical circuit that reproduces every observable input-output mapping and all counterfactual responses to single-node interventions, yet the intermediate states are implemented by synthetic molecules with no evolutionary homology and a radically different causal graph topology at the microscopic level. Some researchers argue that sameness of mechanism should be defined by preservation of interventionist causal structure at the level of abstraction where the detector operates; others hold that it requires shared implementation substrate or at least isomorphism of the full causal graph across scales. Weigh these positions, but also consider a third view: that mechanism identity is irreducibly context-relative, determined by the explanatory purpose for which the detector was constructed. Reach and defend a specific conclusion about which criterion should govern mechanistic anomaly detection in high-stakes domains such as medical diagnostics or autonomous system monitoring, and explain precisely how your chosen criterion handles the transplantation case.
