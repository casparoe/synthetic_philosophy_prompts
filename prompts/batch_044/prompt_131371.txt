I'm planning a single 75-minute session for a non-specialist philosophy reading group that knows Hume's bundle theory but has no background in Buddhist philosophy. The aim is to present anatta as a live view about personal identity rather than a historical curiosity. Don't lecture me on the doctrine; design the explanatory sequence. First, what dependency must be secured before anatta reads as a claim about identity and not merely as "no soul"? Second, choose one carrying example—explicitly not the chariot analogy—that demonstrates both the absence of an owning subject and the intact functioning of conventional personhood; specify what properties that example needs and must lack. Third, anticipate the misconceptions in the order they'll arise: that anatta collapses into Humean bundle theory, that it eliminates moral agency, and that it presupposes reincarnation without a traveler; for each, state the exact point in the sequence where it should be defused. Fourth, place the conventional/ultimate-truth distinction (sammuti-sacca and paramattha-sacca) in the arc and justify why it belongs there rather than at the opening. Fifth, propose one closing question that tests whether the group understands anatta as a denial of ownership rather than a denial of continuity. Finally, identify one genuinely advanced element—such as the Abhidhamma analysis of momentary citta and cetasika—that should be named only to be set aside, with a brief reason why including it would derail the session at this level.
