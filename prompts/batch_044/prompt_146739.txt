I'm working through whether risk-weighted expected utility (REU) can be undermined by a reductio my study group constructed. The framework evaluates acts by integrating r(u(x)) against subjective probabilities, where r is a continuous, strictly convex risk function that weights probabilities non-linearly. Here is our argument; my interlocutor thinks it is valid and forces wholesale rejection of REU, while I suspect a hidden slide or an implicit restriction, but I cannot pinpoint it.

(1) REU assigns each available act a value computed over the agent's subjective probability distribution over outcomes within the decision problem.

(2) For any concrete choice—accepting a job, traveling, investing—there is a physically nonzero but epistemically remote causal path leading to an outcome of arbitrarily large negative utility (say, an unrecoverable global collapse).

(3) Because the agent's total belief state assigns some nonzero credence ε to that remote path, REU applies r to ε, yielding a risk-weighted contribution of r(ε) multiplied by that arbitrarily negative utility.

(4) Given convexity near zero, r(ε) does not shrink proportionally with ε; hence for any fixed ε > 0 one can choose a negative utility magnitude M large enough so that |r(ε)·(–M)| exceeds the summed risk-weighted value of all other outcomes of the act.

(5) Therefore, for every available act, the REU value is dominated by this catastrophic tail and is arbitrarily close to negative infinity, unless there exists an alternative with literally zero credence in the cascade.

(6) Epistemic humility and physical indeterminacy prevent assigning literal zero credence to every remotely possible catastrophic cascade for every available option.

(7) Consequently REU assigns all options the same minimal value and cannot discriminate among them.

(8) A normative decision theory that is systematically incapable of recommending one act over another is vacuous, so REU fails as a general theory of rational choice.

I would prefer to preserve the formal core in (1) and the normative demand in (8), but I will accept whatever price is necessary. Which step fails? Explain why it looked unavoidable—particularly why pulling global tail risks into the decision calculus seemed required—and then spell out what rejecting it costs for REU's scope and ambitions.
