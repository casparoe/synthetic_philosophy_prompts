ok so i started this job where an algorithm assigns my shifts and now i'm weirdly into freedom stuff. here's an argument that republican liberty eats itself and i can't find the exit.

(1) Republican freedom is non-domination: you're free only if nobody has arbitrary power to interfere with your choices, even if they don't actually do it.
(2) Power is arbitary when the holder doesn't have to track your interests — there's no mechanism forcing them to consider what you care about.
(3) At my job, no human picks my hours; an algorithm updates and assigns them. There's no agent whose will i suffer, just a system. But it can still wreck my schedule without tracking my interests.
(4) So either domination needs an agent (and algorithms don't dominate), or domination is just being subject to arbitrary interference (and algorithms do dominate). I want to keep the second option.
(5) Now take democratic law. The legislature is a collective agent with elections and deliberation that are supposed to force it to track citizens' interests. So democratic law isn't arbitrary.
(6) But those tracking procedures are themselves set by earlier laws and enforced by bureacrats using algorithms. The mechanism that prevents arbitrariness is just another layer of potential arbitrary power.
(7) So if we accept (4), then democratic states dominate too, because the tracking mechanism can always be questioned. That makes republican freedom condemn every modern institution — useless as an ideal.
(8) The only escape is to say democratic procedures genuinely stop domination, which means algorithmic management might also stop domination if it tracks efficiency or worker welfare at the system level. But then republican freedom collapses into negative liberty plus good outcomes.

Which step do i reject? I really want to keep (1) and (4) — algorithms can dominate — but (7) feels like a slippery slope. Is the error in (6), or is republican liberty just doomed?
