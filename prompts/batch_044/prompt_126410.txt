I'm writing a paper for my upper-division seminar on cross-cultural moral psychology and metaethics, and I want to test a specific claim rather than rehearse standard positions. Here is the precise proposition: the documented divergence in folk moral judgment—where WEIRD populations tend to compress moral weight onto harm and individual fairness while many non-WEIRD populations distribute substantial weight across loyalty, authority, and purity—is best interpreted not as evidence that moral cognition tracks culture-specific norms rather than universal moral facts, but as evidence that human moral cognition tracks a single pluralistic moral reality whose properties are genuinely context-sensitive. On this view, strong convergence-based moral realism is false, because it wrongly demands uniform weighting across populations, but a weaker pluralistic-tracking form of moral objectivity survives, since different ecological and institutional contexts activate different genuine moral properties.

Please give me your own credence that this claim is correct, on a scale from 0 to 1, and decompose the reasoning into at least four sub-questions, each with a rough probability and one line of justification:

1. The empirical robustness of the divergence: that the WEIRD/non-WEIRD differences in domain weighting are real and not artifacts of translation, sampling bias, or confounded ecological variables.
2. The metaphysical requirement: that strong moral realism indeed requires substantial cross-cultural convergence in the weighting of moral domains, rather than merely convergence on thin core prohibitions.
3. The explanatory superiority: that pluralistic tracking of context-sensitive moral properties explains the divergence better than pure social construction, adaptive preference formation, or purely functional norm-variation.
4. The conceptual coherence: that this pluralistic-tracking view genuinely counts as moral objectivity and is not merely cultural relativism redescribed.

Show roughly how these terms combine into your final number. Then identify which term you are least confident in, and specify what empirical finding or philosophical argument would shift it most.

Finally, compare your position to two hypothetical interlocutors in the seminar: one holds that the divergence decisively undermines all forms of moral realism (credence about 0.85 that realism fails), and the other holds that the divergence is superficial and that convergence-based realism survives essentially intact (credence about 0.85 that realism survives). State your own final number, say which of those two people is closer to being right, and use your decomposition to identify which sub-question is actually doing the work in separating all three positions.
