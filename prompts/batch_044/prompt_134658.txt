I've been mapping out my commitments on rational irrationality and decision theory for a reading-group audit, and I want a consistency check before I present them. Here is where I've landed:

(1) In Parfit's hitchhiker, I hold that paying the driver is rational. I do not ground this in evidential correlation with the rescue decision; instead I appeal to a constitutive feature of rational agency—honoring commitments is part of what it is to be a cooperative decision-maker, and this gives a direct reason to pay even when the causal leverage is spent.

(2) In the counterfactual mugging, I hold that you should not pay once the coin has landed heads. The distinction from (1) is not temporal indexing but structural: in the mugging case there is no live decision architecture linking your present act to the past event, so paying lacks any rationalizing procedural link.

(3) I endorse causal decision theory (CDT) across the board. In Newcomb's problem I two-box, and if I evaluate the hitchhiker payment in isolation—looking only at the causal consequences of handing over the money—I see no reason to pay.

(4) I reject any sharp metaphysical distinction between ex ante rationality and ex post rationality that would allow an agent with the same beliefs and options to switch rational status between t and t+1 without an intervening informational or structural change.

(5) Now consider a modified hitchhiker: the driver rescues you only if a deterministic neural scan at the moment of pickup detects the honor-commitment trait mentioned in (1) with 95% reliability. Here I think paying is rational, but purely because of the evidential correlation between possessing the trait and having been rescued—not because cooperation has any constitutive value.

My questions: Is this package jointly tenable? Please rank the tensions by severity, identify which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and say what the least costly revision would be. In particular, trace whether (3) can survive alongside (1) and (5), whether (4) genuinely undermines the distinction I draw in (2), and whether (5) collapses the justification in (1) into an evidentialist account that conflicts with my CDT commitment. Deliver an audit, not a lecture—map exactly what each commitment commits me to and which one has to give if I want consistency.
