I serve on my university's institutional animal care committee. A neuroscience group proposes using decapod crustaceans in a learning protocol involving brief thermal stress, arguing that our vertebrate-only review mandate should not apply because sentience is gradated rather than binary. They propose a welfare-weighting framework in which organisms with integrated nociception receive proportionally reduced—but not qualitatively different—safeguards compared to rodents; they analogize extreme gradation to treating a thermostat's feedback loop as carrying negligible but non-zero moral weight. Opposing board members reply that scalar moral status should generate differentiated obligations—categorical prohibitions on specific harms for any valenced experience—rather than merely diluted versions of identical protocols. Draft the committee's decision memo recommending whether to approve the study with modified conditions, require full vertebrate-equivalent review, or reject it, with a concrete implementation timeline. The memo should explain whether gradated sentience implies proportional safeguards or categorically distinct ones, and it should engage the thermostat analogy seriously rather than dismissing it out of hand.
