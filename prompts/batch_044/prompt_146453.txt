I sit on my university’s Faculty Appeals and Professional Conduct Panel, and we must issue a written opinion by the end of this month. The case concerns a tenured professor in the laboratory sciences who was suspended after an accident during a classroom demonstration. The defense argues that she possessed a genuine dispositional competence for safe procedure, but that this competence was finked—genuinely destroyed upon triggering, not merely obstructed—by an acute, transient neurological event that occurred precisely when the demonstration began, leaving no intact underlying capacity. The administration counters that the neurological condition was an interfering factor that masked, rather than eliminated, the competence, and that treating the loss as a fink wrongly conflates a treatable or accommodatable impairment with a metaphysical disappearance, thereby undermining our institutional duty of care. Our panel must recommend one of three concrete courses: reinstatement with a monitored conditions protocol, permanent revocation of laboratory privileges, or a mediated settlement with an explicit oversight framework; the recommendation must include an implementation time frame and be directly actionable for the administration. Please draft the memo. It should apply the fink-versus-mask distinction seriously to this judgment of professional competence, present the strongest metaphysical and practical argument for the position you do not endorse, and avoid appealing to specific scholarly texts or citations beyond the conceptual framework already outlined here.
