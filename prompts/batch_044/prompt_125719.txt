I hold that Benjamin Franklin’s project of moral perfection, as set out in his autobiographical discussion of thirteen virtues, is best reconstructed not as an integrated scheme of character formation but as a system of independent moral capital accounts. On this reading, each virtue functions as a separate ledger, and the goal is not the cultivation of a unified moral personality but the simultaneous elimination of deficits across all thirteen accounts; the perfectly virtuous agent is therefore defined by zero negative balance in every ledger rather than by an exceptional mean or a harmonized disposition. The virtues operate as parallel behavioral constraints rather than as mutually reinforcing traits. What would count against this reconstruction? I want the objections, evidence, and conceptual tensions that should actually move a defender of the view, ranked from decisive to merely awkward. Specifically: do Franklin’s own texts contain indications that he treated the virtues as interdependent—say, that progress in temperance was expected to facilitate progress in silence? Does the historical context of eighteenth-century virtue ethics, particularly the influence of Stoic and Christian models of unified character, make the compartmentalized reading anachronistic? Are there conceptual difficulties in treating moral qualities as non-interacting balances—does the idea of an independent ledger for humility collapse once it interacts with the ledger for industry or order? And has Franklin’s later correspondence or life practice shown a shift toward a more holistic or pragmatic integration of these traits? I also want an honest assessment of whether the ledger interpretation has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if any apparent counterexample can be reinterpreted as a temporary adjustment to one account without affecting the others, does that insulation protect the view or diminish its explanatory value? Please distinguish what a committed defender of this reconstruction would concede as genuinely damaging from what they would dismiss as a misreading of Franklin’s practical method, and indicate whether you think the view survives its most serious exposure.
