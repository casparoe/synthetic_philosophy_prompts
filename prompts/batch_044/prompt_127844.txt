I am not a philosopher by training, but I have been thinking for some time about whether it makes sense to blame or praise people if every choice is the result of prior causes. I came across the concept of "reactive attitudes"—the emotional responses, such as resentment or gratitude, that seem tied to holding others responsible—and I have tried to apply it to my own experience. In doing so, I have noticed what seems to me an important but rarely discussed distinction: there is an immediate, visceral reaction, which I will call a "hot reactive attitude," and a slower, sustained stance of treating someone as accountable over time, which I will call a "cool reactive attitude." Some argue that determinism undermines only the hot layer, leaving the cool layer intact as a practical framework; others insist that the cool layer is merely an accumulation or institutionalization of hot layers and therefore collapses if determinism renders the hot ones irrational. Write an essay that examines whether moral responsibility can survive on the cool layer alone. Weigh carefully the arguments that determinism erodes both layers, against the arguments that the cool layer is conceptually and functionally independent. Reach and defend a clear conclusion: is this separation a genuine rescue of moral responsibility, or is it an artificial division that conceals the underlying problem?
