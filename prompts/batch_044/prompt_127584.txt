I sit on my university's Committee on Open Science Communication, and we must reach a decision by next Thursday's meeting on whether to approve a pilot mandate requiring all funded climate-modeling papers in our repository to include an ISOTYPE-style pictorial abstract—standardized, language-independent icons for variables and causal relations, plus a proposed “syntax of confidence” that uses graded line weights to represent statistical uncertainty—with no accompanying textual interpretation permitted in the abstract field. The proponents, drawing on Neurath’s program for a universal picture language, argue that this eliminates metaphysical ambiguity, renders empirical claims inter-subjectively inspectable without theoretical jargon, and achieves the neutrality between observational and theoretical frameworks that verbal language cannot. The critics reply that pictorial syntax cannot represent the modal and counterfactual structures essential to climate projections, that the line-weight grammar covertly encodes a specific statistical framework rather than remaining theory-neutral, and that suppressing textual interpretation violates our institutional mandate for epistemic transparency. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday: recommend approval, rejection, or conditional approval tied to a concrete, time-bound pilot with clear evaluation criteria; treat the proponents’ Neurath-inspired case seriously, including the claim that visual standardization solves inter-theoretical communication problems; and address honestly the strongest consideration on the other side—namely, that a picture language may be unable to distinguish observational content from theoretical commitment in precisely the way Neurath’s own anti-metaphysical program required—without merely restating the committee’s transparency rules as if they settled the philosophical point.
