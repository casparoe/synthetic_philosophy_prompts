Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a Searlean biological-naturalist and a Dennettian interpretivist on original versus derived intentionality, evaluated through the lens of a minimal synthetic system—call it a "two-bitser," a device with exactly two binary registers capable of four internal states, configured via simple transition rules to track two environmental variables (e.g., presence or absence of a chemical gradient and temperature above or below a fixed threshold). The statement should be formal and polished, contain no bibliography or reading list, and cite no specific page numbers or publication dates.

Structure the document as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: include shared commitments about what intentional attribution is supposed to explain, the predictive and explanatory success of the intentional stance in ordinary human cases, the formal architecture of the two-bitser, and the common ground that both sides reject—specifically, that intentionality is either a purely magical property or entirely eliminable in favor of bare physical description.

Second, a disagreements table with three columns—issue; formulation the Searlean accepts; formulation the Dennettian accepts. The issues should include: whether original intentionality is a specific causal-biological capacity of brains that artifacts can only borrow metaphorically; whether the original/derived distinction marks a real metaphysical boundary or a gradient of predictive utility; and whether the two-bitser's state-transitions can instantiate anything more than observer-projected "as-if" intentionality.

Third, identify at least three cruxes—sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party. These must include: (a) whether there exists a principled, non-arbitrary boundary between the two-bitser's four-state dynamics and the minimal intentional states of a simple biological organism (such as a bacterium tracking a nutrient gradient); (b) whether derived intentionality genuinely requires an original intentional source in the manner that a word requires an author, or whether distributed design and evolutionary selection can substitute for that source without rendering the attribution vacuous; (c) whether the predictive success of attributing beliefs and desires to the two-bitser in a restricted environment counts as evidence for real but stance-dependent intentionality, or merely as evidence of our own interpretive habits.

Fourth, for each crux, specify a concrete case, argument, or thought experiment that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about that case. For crux (a), both should evaluate a modified two-bitser whose transition rules were selected through an evolutionary algorithm rather than engineered; the Searlean should predict that evolutionary origin makes no metaphysical difference to the absence of original intentionality, while the Dennettian should predict that evolutionary selection creates the same kind of design-based reality that distinguishes genuine intentional systems from mere mechanisms. For crux (b), both should consider a distributed network of ten thousand two-bitsers collectively maintaining a stable tracking pattern across generations; the Searlean should predict that no single node and no collective acquires original intentionality because none possesses the requisite biological causation, while the Dennettian should predict that the collective can instantiate derived intentionality in a non-vacuous, functionally real manner if the pattern supports robust predictions. For crux (c), both should agree on the evidential weight of a blind prediction task in which human observers attribute goals to the two-bitser without knowing its internal rules; the Searlean should predict that high inter-observer agreement reflects shared projection rather than detected reality, while the Dennettian should predict that sustained predictive accuracy across novel environments distinguishes real pattern-tracking from mere projection.

End by stating the residual disagreements in wording each side would sign, and list the agreed-upon tests or scenarios that could in principle shift one or both positions. Do not render a verdict, do not summarize the literature beyond what is necessary for the collaboration's framing, and do not refer to any theories, texts, or figures that fall outside the common philosophical knowledge of these two positions.
