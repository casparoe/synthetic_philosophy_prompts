In our graduate seminar on panpsychism and the moral ontology of physical systems, we have been tracking the implicit commitments of my colleague Professor Vasquez, who adjudicates whether particular configurations of matter are proper subjects of ethical concern—capable, in some minimal sense, of suffering—without relying solely on behavioral or functional criteria. Her rulings so far are these. She denies moral status to a classical von Neumann computer running a high-fidelity neural simulation of human nociceptive pathways, even when the simulation outputs reports of subjective pain. She grants it to a living nematode with an intact connectome and active nociceptive circuitry. She grants it to a macroscopic quantum-coherent state sustained in a synthetic crystal lattice at millikelvin temperatures, where no biological structures are present and no computation is performed, but where non-local correlations extend across millimeter scales. She denies it to a vascular plant with widespread action-potential signaling but lacking centralized integration. She denies it to a rock in thermal equilibrium. Finally, she denies it to a distributed classical computing cluster that computes an integrated-information value exceeding that of a human brain, on the grounds that its dynamics are fully decoherent and decomposable into independent subsystems.

We now face a novel case for her consideration: a topologically protected quantum error-correcting lattice, entirely non-biological and non-computational in any classical sense, that does not extend coherence through space but instead maintains it through time in a highly structured, self-correcting loop persisting for seconds, with no spatial macroscopic extension but with persistent temporal integration that is not decomposable into independent temporal slices. Given her track record, I want you to predict her verdict, but the real task is analytical. Articulate the two or three distinct principles that could consistently underwrite all six of her prior judgments—perhaps one requiring spatial macroscopic coherence regardless of substrate, one requiring physical non-decomposability regardless of whether the integration is spatial or temporal, and perhaps a third that combines biological embodiment with quantum dynamics. For each principle, say clearly whether it grants moral status to the temporal lattice. Then provide your best-guess prediction for Vasquez, with explicit reasoning about which principle best fits the pattern, and identify the single further case—specifying its precise physical and organizational properties—that would most decisively distinguish the two leading candidates.
