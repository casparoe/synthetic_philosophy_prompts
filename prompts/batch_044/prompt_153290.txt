Write a dialogue between two philosophers—Mara and Elias—who disagree about whether institutional role can insulate a speaker's standing to blame when the institution they represent is structurally complicit in the same wrong. Mara holds that standing is undermined by structural hypocrisy: if the system one speaks for causes or perpetuates the harm being condemned, the speaker's moral address is compromised regardless of personal innocence, because blame expresses a normative relationship that requires the blamer not to be causally entangled in the wrong's architecture. Elias holds that standing tracks causal authority and remedial capacity rather than moral purity, and that separating institutional function from structural complicity is necessary for effective moral address; on his view, an agent can be personally innocent yet occupy a role that inherits institutional complicity, and that inheritance should shape the content of blame rather than cancel the speaker's entitlement to deliver it.

Center the exchange on a specific constructed case: a senior climate negotiator for a wealthy nation publicly condemns a developing country's coal subsidies, while knowing that her own government provides comparable state-backed export financing for fossil fuels through its development bank. Mara should press that the negotiator's voice is tainted by this structural parallel, and that granting her standing requires accepting a separation between personal and collective moral agency that collapses under pressure. Elias should reply by distinguishing the negotiator's direct contribution from her institutional mandate, and by arguing that denying her standing silences agents best positioned to alter the structural conditions. Both must respond to the other's actual points—Mara should address Elias's appeal to remedial capacity, not merely restate purity conditions; Elias should address Mara's concern about causal entanglement, not merely invoke role separation. Include at least one turn in which one speaker revises part of her view in light of the other, and end without declaring a victor.
