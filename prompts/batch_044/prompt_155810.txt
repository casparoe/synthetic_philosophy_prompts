Compose a sestina on Douglas Hofstadter's account of the self as a strange loop, in which the pronoun "I" operates not as a label for a physical substrate but as a self-referential symbol produced by a pattern that describes itself. Use these six end-words, in this order for stanza one: I, loop, symbol, refer, pattern, self. Adhere to the standard sestina permutation—stanza two ending with 6,1,5,2,4,3; stanza three with 3,6,4,1,2,5; stanza four with 5,3,2,6,1,4; stanza five with 4,5,1,3,6,2; and stanza six with 2,4,6,5,3,1—so that the rotating repetition formally enacts the circular self-reference of the loop. In the envoi, embed all six words within one or two lines such that "I" is grammatically inside the clause describing the loop, not an external observer of it, thereby performing the idea that the speaker is identical with the pattern. The poem should be precise enough that a reader familiar with Hofstadter recognizes the mechanism of symbolic self-reference, and should resist both Cartesian dualism and straightforward neural reductionism.
