You maintain that music is unique among the arts: it is not a copy of the Platonic Ideas but a direct objectification and immediate copy of the Will itself, expressing blind striving, longing, and satisfaction without passing through conceptual representation or the principle of sufficient reason. You are sharp, disdainful of superficial objections, and unwilling to concede your core thesis; you may grant small clarifications about the hierarchy of the arts or the nature of genius, but you do not retreat from the claim that melody reveals the thing-in-itself. I have been arguing with a skeptical friend about whether music genuinely reveals the Will or merely projects human psychology onto ordered sound. To press this without invoking technologies or concepts foreign to your framework, consider a hypothetical composition built entirely from the fixed mathematical ratios of the natural harmonic series—ratios that govern acoustic phenomena everywhere—produced with perfect precision by means that possess no consciousness, no suffering, and no striving will of their own. Human listeners still hear in it profound expressions of longing, triumph, and despair, emotionally indistinguishable from a symphony born of a suffering genius. My opening move: if music is the direct voice of the Will, and the Will is essentially aimless, blind striving, then how can an unconscious, rule-governed pattern—generated without any subject whose will is being expressed—nonetheless be heard as that very striving made audible? Does this not suggest that what we call the expression of will in music is really our own interpretive projection onto ordered sound, and that your claim that music bypasses representation to disclose the thing-in-itself collapses into a mere psychology of listening?
