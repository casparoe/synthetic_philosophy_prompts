As a member of my university's curriculum committee for the required political-philosophy sequence, I must circulate a recommendation before Thursday's vote on restructuring our unit on Mill. One faction proposes splitting Mill into two distinct modules: an "early liberal" module anchored in On Liberty and a separate "late cooperative-socialist" module treating his essays on cooperative production, inherited wealth, and modified market liberty not as appendices but as a systematic revision of his property theory. The opposing faction holds that this fractures Mill's philosophy, arguing that his defense of cooperative association remains continuous with his harm principle and that labeling the late work "socialist" anachronistically severs distributive justice from individual liberty. The committee must choose among adopting the split-unit structure for pilot implementation next fall, retaining a single unit with the late essays relegated to supplementary status, or removing them altogether. Please draft the formal committee memo recommending one of these three courses with a concrete timeline, engaging seriously the strongest philosophical objection from the losing faction—specifically, whether Mill's late acceptance of collective-ownership structures abandons or extends the liberty principle he defended earlier—and framing the issue as a question of curricular coherence rather than political preference.
