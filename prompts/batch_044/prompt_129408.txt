Write an essay on the controversy surrounding the ascription of historical agency to non-human environmental forces — specifically pathogenic microbes, long-duration climate shifts, and sudden volcanic eruptions — and on whether such ascription inevitably collapses into environmental determinism. Assess the claim that treating these entities as 'historical actors' corrects an anthropocentric bias in historiography and properly registers their causal weight, against the counter-claim that this framing obscures human social structures, political choices, and differential vulnerability, thereby reviving a discredited determinism. In developing your argument, analyse the distinction between mere causal efficacy and what might be called narrative or explanatory agency, and consider whether the three cases — microbial, climatic, and volcanic — can be accommodated within a single conceptual framework or whether they require separate treatments because of their differing temporal scales and modes of interaction with human societies. Reach and defend a specific conclusion: either that a carefully delimited form of non-human agency is defensible and historiographically valuable, or that the risks of determinism and the heterogeneity of these forces make the framework untenable, or some qualified intermediate position. The answer should be composed in American English.
