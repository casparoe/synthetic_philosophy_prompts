I run a philosophy workshop with about 9 grad students, 60 minutes. Design a classroom game that makes them feel the actual tension between conciliationism and steadfastness in peer disagreement — not just analyze it. The activity should use ambiguous perceptual judgments (like classifying barely-visible shapes or faint audio tones) where particpants first bet private confidence points, then see anonymized peer distributions, then choose whether to revise or hold firm. Add a twist: in one round, randomly assign half the group false "calibration expert" badges so they experience authority-flavored steadfastness, then revoke them for the final round so the same people now face real peer splits without that cushion. Use a token economy: points for being closer to the revealed ground truth, plus a small "conciliation bonus" if you moved toward the peer mean. Include clear rules, a timeline, materials needed, the payoff scheme, and a debrief that maps each round onto conciliationist vs steadfastist arguments — especially where the emotional pull comes from. Tell me what will predictably go wrong (e.g., people gaming the bonuses) and how to handle it. Also be frank about which feature of the philosophical debate — like the symmetry of peer status — the game can't reproduce on its own and you'll need to supply in discussion. No lecture up front; they should run into the problem themselves and only hear the names conciliationism/steadfastness at the end.
