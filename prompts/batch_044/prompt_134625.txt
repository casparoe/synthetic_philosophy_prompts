A colleague in my philosophy of biology seminar has constructed an argument that she now suspects proves too much, but she cannot locate the leak. She starts from the premise that calling DNA a "code" is not merely metaphorical only if the relationship between a codon and its corresponding amino acid is mediated by an interpretive mechanism that can misapply the mapping—there must be conditions under which the mechanism produces the "wrong" amino acid for that codon, analogous to a misreading. She then reasons as follows: (1) The ribosome-tRNA system is the interpreter of the genetic code; it physically matches codons to amino acids according to the mapping table. (2) For this to be interpretation rather than mere chemical causation, there must be a normative standard of correctness: the ribosome can malfunction and insert the wrong amino acid, producing a misinterpretation. (3) Therefore the ribosome represents the codon as specifying a particular amino acid, and this representation can be false. (4) But if the ribosome represents the codon, then representation is instantiated inside the cell by a physical mechanism governed by evolutionary function. (5) By the same reasoning, any causal mechanism with a normative standard of correct functioning represents its inputs: a thermostat represents temperature, a retinal cell represents light intensity, and so on. (6) So representational content is ubiquitous in biological mechanisms, not special to minds. (7) But then there is no principled line between the ribosome "representing" an amino acid and a human brain "representing" a belief; both are just mechanisms with teleofunctional norms. (8) Therefore either mental representation is not special—eliminating the mind-body problem by dissolving it into general biology—or the ribosome does not genuinely represent, which requires denying (2); yet denying (2) collapses DNA interpretation back into mere chemistry, undermining the claim that DNA is literally a code. She finds both horns unacceptable and suspects a subtle equivocation between "normative standard" in the biological-functional sense and "representational content" in the intentional sense. Which step should she reject, and what does each option cost? I would prefer to keep the idea that DNA carries genuine information and that mental representation is genuinely different from cellular mechanics, but I am willing to sacrifice either if the logic demands it. If this framing rests on a confused premise about biological representation, teleofunction, or the conditions for being a code, say so plainly rather than patching the argument.
