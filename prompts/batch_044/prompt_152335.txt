The five criteria are, first, the reflective endorsement criterion, which asks whether the agent would maintain the judgment after full deliberation and access to relevant information; second, the counterfactual framing criterion, which tests whether the judgment shifts when the decision is redescribed in morally loaded versus technically neutral language; third, the feature-tracking criterion, which examines whether the judgment tracks properties genuinely relevant to job performance or merely correlates with protected characteristics; fourth, the narrative integration criterion, which considers whether the judgment can be coherently embedded in the agent's broader practical identity without ad hoc exceptions; and fifth, the intersubjective justification criterion, which asks whether the agent can offer reasons that those adversely affected by the judgment could reasonably accept. Please assign each criterion to one of three tiers—high utility, moderate utility, or low utility—for resolving this specific case, and present your ranking and reasoning entirely in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, tables, or other structural formatting, as one might write in a formal letter or email. For each tier, provide a precise explanation of why the criterion succeeds or fails in this context, with particular attention to cases where two criteria conflict and to which conflict is more revealing about whether the judgment is preference or bias. I also ask that you state clearly what empirical finding or philosophical argument would lead you to reorder these tiers, and that you identify the specific step in your reasoning that is most likely to be wrong.
