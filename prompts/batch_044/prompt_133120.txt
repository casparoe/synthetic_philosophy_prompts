I have been trying to reconcile Popper's early claim that Darwinian natural selection is essentially tautological—fitness being defined circularly through survival—so that it functions as a metaphysical research programme rather than a falsifiable scientific theory, with his later evolutionary epistemology, in which knowledge advances via conjectures and refutations that seem structurally analogous to selection. My worry is that if evolutionary epistemology genuinely resolves the tautology problem by substituting problem-solving success for biological fitness, then Popper has not so much rehabilitated Darwinism as replaced it with a Lamarckian mechanism of directed idea-inheritance. Does this mean that his eventual concession that Darwinism is testable actually abandons his own demarcation criterion, because the only way to make selection non-tautological is to shift the domain away from biological evolution? I would appreciate an answer in British English that disentangles these claims, clarifying where the confusion lies.
