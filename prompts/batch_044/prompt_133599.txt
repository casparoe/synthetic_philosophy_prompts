My opening move: your nested design requires citizens to serve for extended periods across successive tiers, yet empirical observation of citizens' juries and mini-publics shows that retention collapses and self-selection bias — toward the highly educated, the retired, and the ideologically motivated — rises sharply as service lengthens. If you enforce retention through coercive penalties, you seem to sacrifice the voluntary legitimacy that distinguishes sortition from conscription; if you allow exit, the remaining pool becomes precisely the hidden aristocracy you claim elections create. How do you prevent this dilemma from forcing you either to reintroduce electoral filters for "willingness" or to accept a coercive state apparatus that undermines the democratic ethos of your proposal?
