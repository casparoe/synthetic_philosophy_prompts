I was arguing with my friend about Margalit and Rieff for this semenar I'm taking on ethics of memory, and she came up with an arguement that seems to show Margalits whole framework collapses into contradiction. I'm not sure where the slip is but it feels like it proves way too much. Here are her steps, numbered so it's easier to see:

(1) Margalit says societies have an unconditional, non-instrumental duty to remember grave wrongs done to vulnerable groups — not because remembering does anything useful now, but because the victims deserve acnowledgment.
(2) Rieffs point about theraputic culture is that modern societies only preserve obligations that have some present psychological or social payoff; anything purely symbolic gets treated as a burden to be "processed" and released.
(3) So for Margalit's duty to actually survive in a modern society, people have to find it psychologically sustainable — which means its cultural survival depends on present consequences (mental health, social cohesion, etc.).
(4) But if the duty's survival depends on present consequences, then it's not genuinely non-instrumental; it's actually justified by those consequences, even if Margalit denies it.
(5) Now, if it IS genuinely non-instrumental, then it shouldn't depend on present sustainability at all — but then Rieff predicts it will be abandoned, which means the society fails in its duty.
(6) So either the duty is instrumental (and Margalit's claim that it's unconditional is wrong), or it's non-instrumental and therefore structurally impossible for modern societies to maintain.
(7) Therefore there can't be a coherent unconditional duty to remember in modern conditions.

She thinks the problem is in (3) — that Rieff is being overstated — but I'm not convinced. Can you tell me which step is actually wrong? I want to keep (1) and (2) if possible, since those seem like accurate readings of both guys. Explain why the bad step looked okay, and then tell me what each option costs: what does Margalit have to give up if we reject (3), what does Rieff have to pay if we reject (4), and what happens if we bite the bullet on (6) and say the duty really is impossible?
