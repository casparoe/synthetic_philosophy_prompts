Question. Dr. K employs a two-track evaluation framework. Track-1 delivers first-order judgments via domain-specific heuristics; Track-2 delivers meta-cognitive reliability scores calibrated against long-run error logs. Dr. K receives Track-1 evidence that patient X has condition C, assigned confidence 0.85. Track-2 reports that Track-1's heuristic for C currently exhibits a false-positive rate of 40% due to a known contextual confounder, though Track-2 itself carries an 8% meta-error rate. Dr. K possesses no independent first-order access to X's condition outside Track-1. Is Dr. K rationally permitted to maintain belief in C at confidence 0.85? Must she suspend judgment entirely? In your answer, distinguish (i) the level-splitting diagnosis of epistemic akrasia, (ii) the total-evidence requirement associated with conciliatory views, and (iii) whether an asymmetry between downward and upward calibration might license a stabilized intermediate credence rather than either full retention or full suspension.

Student answer. Dr. K faces a classic case of potential epistemic akrasia. On the level-splitting view—associated with Feldman—she can rationally believe C via Track-1 while Track-2 reports that Track-1 is unreliable. This avoids contradiction because the beliefs operate at different levels: object-level versus meta-level. By contrast, the conciliatory position, defended by Christensen, holds that total evidence must be integrated. Once Track-2's assessment is included, Dr. K should revise her credence downward proportionally to Track-1's reported unreliability. Specifically, combining Track-1's 40% false-positive rate with Track-2's 8% meta-error rate yields an effective unreliability of roughly 32% (40% adjusted downward by 8%). This means her credence should fall from 0.85 to approximately 0.58 rather than remain at 0.85. The downward/upward calibration asymmetry—where downward adjustments are more permissive than upward ones—implies that when Track-2 downgrades Track-1, the adjustment should be applied without reservation. Thus maintaining 0.85 is akratic but not contradictory; it is a calibration failure rather than a logical contradiction. The stabilized intermediate view, which some conciliators allow, permits settling at 0.58 instead of suspending entirely, because full suspension would discard the first-order information that Track-1 still provides. Therefore, Dr. K should adopt the intermediate credence.
