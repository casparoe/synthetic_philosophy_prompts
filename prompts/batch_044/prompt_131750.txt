I am teaching an advanced seminar on suffering-focused ethics and have been working through the published verdicts of Dr. Elena Marchetti, whose writings on asymmetric negative consequentialism are familiar to the students. Over the past term we have catalogued her explicit rulings on five cases: she holds that the suffering of farmed animals ought to be abolished regardless of the aggregate pleasure lost to humans; that pharmacological suppression of bereavement grief ought to be resisted, on the grounds that such suffering is constitutive of love and moral recognition; that chronic degenerative pain should be relieved even when the sufferer claims it lends meaning to endurance; that engineering a congenital absence of pain-capacity into newborns should be rejected, because nociceptive experience is developmentally necessary for the emergence of intersubjective moral concern; and that post-surgical discomfort should not be fully anaesthetised if it functions as a protective feedback mechanism for healing behaviour, though intra-operative anaesthesia remains obligatory.

I would like you to predict her verdict on a new proposal now before her advisory panel: a reversible, adult-voluntary neuro-modulation protocol—tentatively termed “empathic dampening”—that selectively suppresses the affective-motivational component of empathic distress and anticipatory trauma without impairing cognition or sensory perception. The protocol is intended for humanitarian aid workers in active conflict zones; it would permit continued functioning under extreme conditions, yet preliminary studies suggest that sustained administration may attenuate the very motivational structures that drive altruistic risk-taking, even though subjects remain fully rational and consenting. Please tell me whether she is likely to endorse or reject this intervention, and, more importantly, lay out the two or three competing normative rules that are jointly consistent with her earlier five rulings but that diverge precisely on this case. For each rule, state its prediction for the empathic-dampening proposal. Then indicate which rule you take to be her actual implicit principle, with your reasons, and specify the single further hypothetical case—preferably one involving a non-human or artificial sentient system, or a cross-generational intervention—that would most sharply adjudicate between the rules you have identified. I should emphasise that I am not asking for bibliographic references, reading lists, or citations, but for a rigorous conceptual analysis that a student without web access could evaluate from first principles. Please present your answer in British English, maintaining a formal academic register throughout.
