ok so im trying to write up my views for a bioethics working group i just joined — my cousin got a synthetic heart valve last year and we got into a huge fight about whether it "really functions" or just mimics function — and i want a consistency check before i open my mouth. heres roughly where ive landed:

(1) for naturally evolved traits, real function is fixed by selection history, full stop: the heart pumps blood because thats what it was kept around for, not merely because it currently helps circulation. (2) but for engineered tissue, synthetic valves, or lab-grown organs, we should drop selection history entirely and ask only what causal role the part plays in the present system — Cummins-style, i guess. (3) malfunction is always failure of current contribution, not deviation from evolutionary design: if a heart stops pumping its malfunctioning, even if the mutation that broke it was technically adaptive in some weird ancestral environment. (4) i dont think (1) and (2) clash because natural kinds come with what i call "function inheritance" — they carry their selected role forward — but artifacts and bioengineered parts inherit nothing, so they need causal-role analysis instead. (5) i also think you cant read evolutionary history straight off present causal roles, so the fact that something currently contributes well doesnt tell you what it was selected for. (6) and heres the kicker: if we build a synthetic pump that circulates blood perfectly but was never selected, id say its functioning correctly, even though it has zero evolutionary function.

do these actually hang together? im especially worried about whether (3) clashes with (1) in divergence cases — say a trait was selected for X but now does Y well, so its not failing causally but its "inherited" function is still X. is "function inheritance" in (4) just a made-up patch, or does it do real work? and (5) seems to push me away from using causal roles for natural things, yet (2) embraces them for artifacts — is that split principled or am i just drawing the line where its convenient? please rank the tensions by severity, say for each what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs me, and tell me whether one single change — like weakening (1) or dropping inheritance talk — cleans up the most mess.
