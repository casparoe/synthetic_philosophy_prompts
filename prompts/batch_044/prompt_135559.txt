Construct and defend the thesis that institutional promises recorded in writing, when addressed to no specific present audience, still constitute genuine performative utterances—provided we accept a mechanism of diachronic performative binding, in which normative force is generated not by simultaneous uptake but by a document's accessibility to any future party meeting a specified recognition condition. Your argument should explain how this mechanism operates within Austin's framework of felicity conditions without collapsing the performative-constative distinction, and it should respond to the objection that absent co-present uptake the utterance is merely a constative report of the speaker's intent. You may treat diachronic performative binding as a theoretical construct you are introducing rather than an established term of art, but you should make its conditions precise enough that a critic could determine whether a given archival contract, testamentary clause, or public pledge satisfies them.
