I'm taking this metaphysics course and we're arguing about origin essentialism — like, could you actually have come from a different sperm and egg and still be the same person? My prof says some philosophers think your origin is essential to you, but I'm not totally convinced, especially when I think about adoption.

Anyway, this made me notice something weird in family law. When a fertility clinc accidentally implants the wrong embryo, courts usually try to "return" the kid to the genetic parents, as if the genetic link is the true family identity. But in adoption, the law treats that same genetic origin as totally irrelevant — the kid is just fully part of the new family, period. So what view of personal identity or origin does this double practice actually presuppose? Does it secretly commit to origin essentialism, that your genetic origin fixes who you really belong to? Or is it just protecting reproductive property rights, with no metaphysics involved? And can the same legal system consistently treat origin as destiny in mix-up cases but as nothing in adoption, or does that just show family law is philosophically underdetermined — working with several rival theories at once?
