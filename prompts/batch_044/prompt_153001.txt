Just as the same program can run on two servers without being split in half, so the argument goes, your pattern of thoughts, memories, and self-reference could be instantiated in another brain without diminishing the original, and both instances could genuinely claim to be you. This is often invoked in connection with Hofstadter's idea that the self is a strange loop—a pattern rather than a substance—that might be copied like a melody across different instruments.
