Consider the institutional pattern in which governments and scientific bodies devote substantial resources to projects designed to preserve human knowledge, genetic material, or safe conditions across millennia—nuclear waste repositories engineered with warning systems intended for civilizations thousands of years hence, global seed vaults, and climate stabilization targets framed around maintaining conditions for distant future generations—while justifying these expenditures in terms of civilizational continuity, intergenerational duty, or the preservation of value-bearing projects that outlive any present individual. At the same time, these same institutions treat the prevention of widespread present suffering—whether of wild animals, distant strangers, or nearby non-citizens—as falling outside their mandate unless that suffering threatens human survival or institutional functioning, and they regard human extinction as a categorically worse outcome than an equivalent number of present deaths distributed over time without threatening continuity.

What philosophical commitments about value, moral status, and the badness of extinction does this pattern presuppose? Must it depend on something like Scheffler's afterlife conjecture—that much of present life's value derives from its contribution to a collective human afterlife, a future in which our projects, values, and culture continue—so that extinction is bad not merely for the deaths it entails but for destroying the framework within which present lives gain meaning? Or can the disparity be explained without that commitment, say by tractability, reciprocity constraints, or a purely person-affecting intergenerational ethics? Is the practice consistent with treating individual welfare as the sole locus of value, or does it quietly presuppose that human continuity itself, or the existence of future valuers, carries intrinsic or non-instrumental weight? And if we rejected the idea that present lives depend for their value on belonging to an enduring human project—treating extinction as harmful only through the deaths and suffering it causes, with no additional afterlife loss—what exactly in these institutional priorities would have to change?
