I'm a doctoral candidate in political philosophy serving as an external advisor to a regional legislative drafting office. The office's staff attorneys—trained in statutory interpretation but not in conceptual analysis—must now annotate every technology-related bill with a liberty-impact assessment, yet they lack any shared framework for distinguishing negative liberty, positive liberty, and republican domination. Please design a reusable decision procedure, structured as ordered questions with explicit stopping rules and time limits suitable for a non-philosopher working under a thirty-minute deadline. The procedure must operationalize the difference between mere absence of interference, failure of self-mastery, and subjection to arbitrary power; it must also flag the exact points where normative judgment cannot be eliminated and must be signed off by a senior reviewer. Then apply the procedure to three proposed regulations: first, an ordinance directing predictive policing algorithms to allocate patrol resources across neighborhoods without individualized suspicion; second, a welfare eligibility rule conditioning benefits on completion of a state-mandated financial literacy curriculum; third, a housing ordinance allowing landlords to initiate eviction proceedings for "disruptive conduct" without judicial review. In at least one of these applications, demonstrate how the procedure yields a classification that runs counter to the attorney's likely political intuitions, and conclude by stating precisely what kinds of liberty conflicts the procedure is structurally unable to adjudicate.
