There's this long running stalemate in formal epistemology about imprecise probabilities — you know, the idea that instead of one sharp credence you hold a range or a whole set of priors. Precise Bayesians say it's just indecision in disguise; the IP crowd says sharp credences fake a precison we don't actually have. Both sides have been throwing the same formal examples at each other for decades — dilation, betting arguement, the works — and nobody moves. My working theory is that it's not a real math disagreement at all, but that one side is asking what a single rational agent should believe, while the other is asking what a group of agents with shared evidence should allow, and they just both call it rational credence. Is that the stall, or is there a deeper normative clash about whether rationality requires uniqueness? And if it's mostly framing, what would actually count as progress — a new aggregation theorem, or just splitting the questions apart? I also keep thinking people treat a credal set like it's one blurry mental state, when really it's more like a menu of possible precise minds. Does that reframing help, or is it just adding jargon?
