Student response:

Ramsey regards the question of life's meaning as a pseudo-problem generated by linguistic confusion rather than a substantive metaphysical issue. In Truth and Probability he develops a pragmatist theory of belief according to which a belief is a habit of action oriented toward practical success; applying this framework, he treats the demand for "the meaning of life" as a misplaced request for a factual substrate behind our evaluative commitments. This semantic diagnosis aligns with his logical atomism: just as atomic propositions do not include "meaning" as one of their constituent parts, human existence does not contain a hidden purpose as one of its factual properties. At the same time, Ramsey's ethical writings defend a form of objective value—he holds that some states of affairs are genuinely good or bad in themselves, independent of any particular attitude. This creates a serious tension. If objective values are real features of the world, then asking whether life as a whole has meaning should amount to asking whether those values apply globally across human existence, which is a legitimate metaphysical question rather than a category mistake. Consequently, Ramsey's pragmatist semantics appear to undermine the very objectivist ethics he elsewhere endorses, leaving his position on value and meaning internally unstable.
