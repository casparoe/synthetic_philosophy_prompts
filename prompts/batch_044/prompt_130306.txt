I work as a governance mediator for multi-generational family enterprises, running 90-minute sessions where family roles and corporate roles collide. Conflicts here are rarely contractual; they involve overlapping obligations—parent and child, elder and younger sibling, founder and successor, employer and kin—that Confucian role ethics treats as constitutive rather than derived from universal rules. But modern family firms create interstitial roles that sit between the classic five relationships: a founder’s spouse with no formal office but with family authority, or an adopted child who is also a senior executive. I need a reusable decision procedure I can run without philosophical training.

Design the procedure. It must include: a role-mapping step that identifies active classic relational roles and flags interstitial ones; a proximity-based priority rule modified by a rectification check—if one party has systematically corrupted the obligations of a role (treating a child solely as an employee, for example), obligations in that role are suspended and resolution shifts to the intact roles; a cross-domain conflict step for clashes between family obligation and fiduciary duty, with explicit stopping points where the answer is a value judgment the mediator must not impose; and clear markers of where judgment is unavoidable and where the procedure will misfire.

Then apply it to three cases: (1) a founder wants to appoint an unqualified child as COO over the objection of an elder sibling who is VP; (2) a senior non-family executive is pressured to conceal a family member’s financial error, with loyalty obligations invoked; and (3) one case where the procedure protects institutional integrity at the cost of family harmony—an answer the mediator will not want to deliver.
