As a novelist constructing a philosophical seminar scene for a work of literary fiction, I require a formal, polished decision tree that classifies live positions on the problem of verisimilitude—specifically, whether and how scientific theories can be said to approach truth—according to the logical architecture of commitments rather than by familiar labels. The tree should open with the question of whether scientific progress is measurable at all in terms of proximity to truth, then branch through concrete diagnostic nodes: whether false elements within a successful theory must be globally distributed or can instead be locally isolated without undermining the whole; whether structural correspondence between theory and world is necessary beyond predictive accuracy; whether verisimilitude is essentially a comparative relation between rival theories or can be assigned absolutely to a single framework; and how one interprets the formal obstacles raised against naive quantitative measures—whether as fatal, as surmountable through refined logical metrics, or as indicating that verisimilitude ought to be reconceived in epistemic rather than ontic terms. Each leaf should identify either a named position—such as Popper’s original comparative definition, the impossibility results associated with Miller and Tichý, Niiniluoto’s min-sum measure, similarity-based accounts, a structural-realist variant, or an epistemic-deflationary view—or be marked unnamed, with a brief diagnosis of whether the vacancy stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The branching questions must be phrased so that a respondent can answer them without prior knowledge of which interpretation they select; avoid asking whether one accepts a specific named measure, and instead present concrete comparative cases—for instance, whether a theory that contains one precisely false law but otherwise exhibits exceptional predictive reach should be judged closer to the truth than a rival that avoids falsehood entirely but offers weaker empirical coverage. Where two positions converge on all practical judgments—such as certain structural-realist and similarity-based approaches—explicitly flag the ambiguity rather than inventing a spurious split. The deliverable should be suitable for inclusion in a novel’s appendix as a handout, should require no bibliographic apparatus or citation, and should treat central figures not as encyclopedic commonplaces but as nodes in a live debate, with particular attention to combinations of answers that remain philosophically unoccupied and to the awkward commitments each occupied leaf must bear.
