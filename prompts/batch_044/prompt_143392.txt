For my graduate seminar on intertemporal ethics, design a decision tree that classifies normative positions on discounting future welfare—not economic machinery, but the philosophical justifications. The root node should ask whether present welfare carries intrinsic priority over identically sized future welfare. From there, branch on the basis of the justification a view offers: continuity of personal agency across time, epistemic uncertainty about distant outcomes, analogy to spatial distance, or pure temporal location bias. The tree must terminate in the following leaves: Sidgwick-style universalism with a zero pure rate; a rationalist pure-time-preference view that endorses exponential discounting as normatively valid; a descriptive hyperbolic-discounting position that denies normative authority; a “future selves as strangers” partiality account that analogizes intertemporal choice to interpersonal altruism; the intergenerational non-identity constraint that rejects comparative harm across generations; and a risk-premium-only view that discounts solely for outcome uncertainty, not for temporal location. At each internal node, phrase the branching question as a concrete claim a student could affirm or deny without knowing which interpretation it leads to. Then map the full logical space of answer combinations, noting any unoccupied cells—especially whether exponential pure preference can consistently coexist with rejection of the non-identity problem—and briefly diagnose whether each emptiness stems from incoherence, instability, or mere neglect. Finally, flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate, particularly the spatial-analogy view and the risk-premium-only view. Keep the instrument to about seven decision nodes, and frame every question formally enough for students who already grasp the distinction between instrumental and pure time preference.
