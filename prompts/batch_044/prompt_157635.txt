Write a villanelle addressed to the close of a philosophy-of-science seminar, in which the two refrains are, first, a precise formulation of the pessimistic meta-induction—that the historical record is a graveyard of theories once held with high confidence, so our present ones likely share that fate—and, second, the structural-realist reply that mathematical and relational structures survive theory change, making predictive success non-miraculous. The form should do the philosophical work: each of the five tercets should stage one round of the dialectic, with the rhyme scheme tracking either replacement (false / past / grave) or preservation (form / law / hold), and the closing quatrain should place the two refrains on adjacent lines without logical reconciliation, showing the stalemate between a base-rate argument and a no-miracles argument. Use strict iambic pentameter, avoid decorative lists of discarded theories, and instead embed the tension in sustained metaphors—architecture rebuilt on an unchanged foundation, or maps redrawn over invariant coordinates—so that a specialist recognizes the positions rendered correctly rather than merely named, and a non-specialist still finds a coherent poem. Compose it in the voice of someone who assigns non-trivial credence to both sides, and let the meter's regularity itself suggest the structural continuity that the realist claims, even as the refrains keep returning to deny it.
