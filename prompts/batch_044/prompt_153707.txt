I'm putting together a diagnostic taxonomy—essentially a decision tree—for a graduate seminar on material constitution, using the statue-and-clay scenario as the central probe. Rather than asking participants to self-identify with labels like "constitution theorist," "coincidence theorist," or "mereological essentialist," I want them sorted by their judgments on concrete cases: gradual particle-replacement of the clay, whether squashing the statue into a cube destroys it while the clay survives, whether the statue and clay can share every spatiotemporal part at every time, and whether being an artifact with a specific form is essential to the statue's persistence conditions. The tree should be built from the logical space of these answers first, with named positions—including two less commonly discussed variants, a hylomorphic constitution view that treats form and matter as ontologically inseparable yet allows distinct identity conditions, and a four-dimensionalist stage-coincidence view that treats both statue and clay as temporal stages of longer four-dimensional worms—located only after the branching combinations are mapped out.

Please construct an instrument with six to eight branching questions, each phrased without jargon so that someone unfamiliar with the literature can answer them directly. For each combination of answers, indicate which named view (or views) occupies that node, and explicitly flag any unoccupied combinations—such as a position that permits co-location, denies modal divergence between statue and clay, and yet rejects mereological essentialism for the clay—diagnosing briefly whether that gap seems incoherent, merely unstable, or simply unexplored. You should also indicate which pairs of positions the tree cannot cleanly separate (for instance, certain non-hylomorphic constitution views and coincidence views that yield identical judgments on all the test cases), and suggest what supplementary distinction—perhaps about the ontological status of properties or about cross-world identity—would be needed to split them.
