I was arguing with my friend last night about whether modern stoicism is actually stoic or just productivity culture with a greek veneer. For my philospohy course on ancient thought in modern life I'm sketching an essay and I want honest comments — not on writing style, but on whether the argument holds water or if I'm just strwmaning the self-help crowd.

The core claim is that Epictetus's dichotomy of control isn't about managing your reaction to things outside your control — that's the airport-book version. It's about recognizing that your prohairesis (the faculty of choice / moral character) is the only thing genuinely yours in a normative sense. Everything else — body, property, reputation, even beliefs you haven't actively endorsed — falls under not up to us. But modern revivalists like Holiday and Irvine seem to treat externals like career or health as a middle zone: not fully in our control, but still things we should systematically optimize with detachment. I think this middle zone collapses Epictetus's binary. If you treat health as an object of rational planning, you're extending prohairesis into domains he said don't belong to it. The modern version turns an ethical boundary into a management framework.

I'm not asking for citations or a reading list — I know I need to check the Enchiridion myself.
