Pain asymbolia demonstrates that a subject can undergo nociceptive stimulation and correctly identify its location and intensity without experiencing any negative affect. This supports the view that the badness of pain is not built into its sensory profile but is instead added by a separate affective-evaluative module. On the triadic framework we used in class, the sensory and cognitive-evaluative dimensions remain intact—the patient recognizes the stimulus as damage-related—while the affective-motivational dimension is selectively disrupted. Therefore, asymbolia shows that pain is not necessarily bad: without the evaluative uptake, the remaining sensory signal is merely informational. Against this, imperativist theories claim that pain is essentially an imperative command to withdraw or protect the body, not a representation of damage.
