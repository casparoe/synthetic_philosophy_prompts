Take the UK and France decision to keep funding Concorde in the early 70s long after it was obvious the plane wouldnt turn a profit, and analyse it through the sunk-cost lens — but dont just call it escalation of commitment. Ask whether the ministers were actually protecting institutional credibility or some technological option value, and whether that makes honoring sunk costs rational in this case, or if theyre just smuggling future benefits back into the books. Where does the theory explain what they faced and where does it stay silent? And does the episode show that honoring sunk costs is always irrational, or just that people are bad at separating whats already spent from whats still on the table?
