I serve as the literary representative on the Intermunicipal Commission for the Charter of Collective Memory, and I must circulate our formal opinion before next Thursday's vote. Our mandate is explicitly extra-legal: we are not defining citizenship for the state, but deciding who belongs to the demos that will authorize a shared narrative for the territory of Maréval—a region administratively dissolved three decades ago, whose legal boundaries disappeared but whose cultural boundary did not. Three groups claim deliberative membership in the drafting body. First, the present legal residents of the merged municipality, who hold current residence but no ancestral or narrative connection to Maréval. Second, dispersed descendants now living elsewhere who claim what they term "narrative citizenship"—the argument that Maréval's absence is the structuring absence of their collective identity, and therefore they are the primary subjects of any charter describing it. Third, a small network of "archival witnesses," unofficial custodians who preserved documents the state destroyed; they argue that custodianship of an erased past confers a unique form of membership, not as representatives of the living but as stewards of the dead. The philosophical stakes are the boundary problem: who belongs to the people that can legitimately speak for itself? Please draft the commission's opinion—actionable, with a concrete recommendation and an implementation timeline—specifying which of these groups receives voice and vote, or what tiered or weighted arrangement applies. Your reasoning must treat the strongest argument for the excluded group with genuine seriousness rather than dismissing it, and it must avoid resting the decision solely on legal residency or birthright, since the charter's authority is narrative rather than juridical. Write in the formal register of an institutional memo, but maintain the narrative precision of someone who understands that every boundary drawn around a people is also a decision about whose absence will be remembered.
