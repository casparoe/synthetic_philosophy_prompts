I retired from a biology lab about three years ago and have been slowly getting into philosphy since then — mostly by arguing with people at the coffee shop near campus. Lately I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about path dependence: whether history gets "locked in" or if the same outcomes would have arrived anyway. I'm not looking for a literature review or a reading list, just hoping to recieve some clarity on whether there's one principle behind my gut reactions.

Here are my verdicts on concrete cases. The QWERTY layout is clearly path dependent — if Sholes had arranged the keys differently in 1874 we'd be stuck with that layout instead, and there's no deep functional reason this particular mess won. Same with rail gauge being 4 feet 8.5 inches; it traces back to Roman chariots and English coal mines, and now the whole world's stuck with it.

But then take the genetic code: triplet codons specifying about 20 amino acids. I actually think that's NOT path dependent. Any replicating system that needs both storage and error correction is going to converge on something like triplets, probably with a similar alphabet size. The specific mapping of which codon means which amino acid might be arbitrary, but it's structure feels inevitable.

Here's the pair I can't reconcile. I think DNA being the primary storage molecule (rather than RNA) IS path dependent — once it got adopted in early life it was too hard to switch back, even though RNA can do both jobs. But I also think DNA's chemical stability makes it genuinely superior for long-term storage, so maybe life everywhere ends up with DNA. Those two thoughts pull in opposite directions. Then there's the 2006 IAU vote that reclassified Pluto: I feel like that was genuinely contingent — different people in the room in Prague and Pluto stays a planet. But my friend says the physical criterion (clearing the neighborhood) was always going to win eventually, so the vote was just the trigger, not the cause.

A few more quick ones. Writing direction in Western scripts — left-to-right — feels path dependent to me, could easily have been the other way. But calculus notation? I'm flipping constantly. Leibniz's dy/dx versus Newton's dots — is that locked in because of network effects in textbooks, or did Leibniz's notation genuinely win on merit? I can't tell.

So my verdicts are all over the place: QWERTY yes, rail gauge yes, genetic code structure no, DNA storage probably yes, Pluto vote yes, writing direction yes, calculus notation I don't know. Is there one principle that captures the pattern without just listing examples? Maybe something about network externalities versus functional optimization? If so, which of my verdicts does it force me to drop — probably the Pluto one? And what does it predict for something I haven't mentioned, like whether base-10 counting is path dependent (ten fingers) or whether we'd have converged on it anyway? I keep going back and forth on that too.
