I've been in this philosophy of mind seminar where we talk about creativity, and we got stuck on Hofstadter's knob-twiddling idea — the notion that being creative is mostly like turning dials on a theme, changing inversion or tempo or whatever, while the core concept stays intact. My study partners Maya and Leo started arguing about whether that can cover real artistic breakthroughs or if it's just describing remix culture with extra steps. Maya said there's a real difference between tweaking visible parameters and altering what she called shadow parameters — hidden dimensions that are conceptually seperate from the visible ones, and that decide whether a varient still belongs to the original theme at all. She thinks when a composer invents a genuinely new contrapuntal form, they're not turning an existing knob but exposing one that was always there but invisible, which changes the concept space itself rather than just adjusting within it.

Leo pushed back and said that's just twiddling a meta-knob at a higher level of abstraction; if you allow invisible dimensions, then every framework shift can be redescribed as parameter adjustment, and Hofstadter's model becomes unfalsifiable. Maya answered that unfalsifiability is exactly the problem — if the theory absorbs every counterexample by inventing a new knob, it stops explaining anything and becomes a tautology. Leo then said she's missing the point, because the model isn't meant to be a scientific law but a mechanistic sketch, and demanding a hard line between parameter and framework is like asking where blue ends and green begins. They asked me to adjudicate but I'm honestly not sure whose interpretation is closer to what Hofstadter actually meant, and also whether both of them are ignoring something important about how listeners recognize a theme across variations — like whether recognition depends on preserving some invariant relation rather than just family resemblance. Could you go through their moves one by one, rate each from clearly wrong to clearly right, and say what both are missing about theme-recognition?
