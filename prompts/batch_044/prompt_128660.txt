Design a paired extinction scenario that tests whether Scheffler's afterlife conjecture depends specifically on biological posterity or on the continuation of human projects regardless of substrate. In Scenario Alpha, humanity dies out but a durable, autonomous archive system preserves every human project—scientific, artistic, moral, and interpersonal—and actively extends them with perfect fidelity for millennia. In Scenario Beta, extinction is followed by total erasure: no records, no successors, no continuation. In both cases, specify that the extinction event itself involves the same number of deaths and the same mode of suffering, so that any difference in badness cannot be traced to the deaths. Keep both scenario descriptions together in under one paragraph, using only technologies or institutions that are conceptually possible without exotic physics. Then, for each scenario, state what the conjecture implies about the comparative badness of the extinction, and explain precisely which element—biological lineage, project continuity, or the expectation of future people—does the explanatory work in generating that verdict. Finally, describe how a critic of the conjecture could argue that Scenario Alpha removes the badness entirely, and identify the feature of the case that such a critic must attack to make that argument succeed.
