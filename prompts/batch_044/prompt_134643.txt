Hey — I'm a retired marine ecologist who got into philosphy a few years back and now I can't stop. Lately I've been chewing on Rousseau's worry that theater splits a community into passive spectators and vain performers, and I'm trying to see where my gut lands on different kinds of spectacle.

My verdicts: a harvest festival where the whole town dances and sings together, with no audience sitting seperate, feels healthy — not corrupting at all. A professional troupe putting on a comedy for seated ticket-holders feels corrupting in exactly the way Rousseau described. A live event where spectators can interrupt and take an actor's place to try a different ending — I lean that it's still corrupting, since the emotional frame is artificial even if bodies move, but I'm genuinely unsure. A livestreamed political debate with pundits framing every gesture — corrupting, though more like a slow drip than a single blow. A virtual reality experience where you live through a simulated crisis alone, with no human performers and no one watching you — weirdly, I think that's okay, closer to private moral imagination than to spectacle. A silent choreographed protest in the square where onlookers blur into participants — good, like the festival. But then there's the case I keep going back and forth on: a small town where every resident writes and performs a brief autobiographical piece for the neighbors, no professionals, everyone knows everyone personally. Is that theater? Is it corrupting?

So is there a principle that captures all of this? I can imagine two: one about whether there's a structural split between actor and spectator that encourages comparing yourself to fictional others and dodging direct civic engagement; another about whether the occurance of emotional catharsis replaces actual collective action. They both fit most of my calls, but they split hard on the interruptible live event and on the autobiographical town case. Can you lay out both precisely, say which clause motivates which verdict, and tell me which one I'd have to drop for simplicity? Also, what does the winning principle predict for a case I haven't listed — say, an AI-generated interactive narrative where the user co-creates the plot but there are zero human performers?
