Before assessing cyclical against linear macro-history, I want the full specification any adequate model of long-term civilisational change must satisfy. Set out the desiderata: the phenomena it must accommodate (for instance, cross-cultural institutional recurrence versus irreversible technological accumulation), the puzzles it must resolve (such as societies that enter prolonged “silent interregna” — extended periods lacking both cyclical repetition and linear progression — or that appear to skip predicted stages), the distinctions it must not collapse (morphological resemblance versus causal continuity; retrospective narrative fit versus genuine predictive constraint), and the logical or empirical bounds it must respect. For each requirement, give the motivating case or consideration; flag any subset you regard as jointly unsatisfiable; and mark which are non-negotiable versus camp-specific preferences. Conclude with a brief indication of where the cyclical accounts of Polybius and Ibn Khaldun, and the morphological schemes of Spengler and Toynbee, fail or succeed on the hard constraints — not an exhaustive evaluation, but enough to show whether the criteria are genuinely decisive.
