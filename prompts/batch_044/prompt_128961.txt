I am preparing a taxonomy for an advanced philosophy seminar on fatalism and rational agency, centred on the 'Death in Damascus' scenario: a figure is told he will die in Damascus, flees instead to Samarra, and finds that death had been waiting there all along. Rather than asking participants which named doctrine they prefer, I want a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions by the concrete commitments they make about modality, time, and practical reason.

The tree should open with the diagnostic question: does the story reveal a genuine logical contradiction in the notion of an unavoidable particular event? From this root, branch through (i) whether the inevitability at issue is logical, causal, or indexical; (ii) whether the underlying temporal model is linear, branching, or block-universe; (iii) whether the agent's flight is rational, pragmatically self-defeating, or retrocausally influenced by the very outcome it seeks to avoid; and (iv) whether statements about the future now bear determinate truth-values. Each leaf should be labelled with a named position — for example, logical fatalist, Oedipian causal determinist, open-future Aristotelian, indexical inevitabilist, retrocausal evasionist — or marked 'unnamed / unexplored' with one sentence describing its most awkward commitment. Any combination of answers that seems incoherent should be noted as such; any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate must be flagged explicitly.

Please ensure the taxonomy incorporates at least one distinction rarely found in introductory treatments: specifically, whether the 'appointment with death' operates as a rigid designator that picks out the same event across possible worlds, or as a descriptive role that different occurrences could satisfy. The audience is graduate students and informed non-specialists who will not have access to external sources, so do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and phrase every node as a question or claim that can be answered without prior knowledge of the position labels.

Write the response in American English. Separate clearly what is settled — the formal structure of the paradox and the logical relations among its premises — from what is genuinely contested among specialists, such as the correct interpretation of modal operators in the argument and whether branching-time semantics dissolve the contradiction. Do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus view.
