Having retired from a career in experimental physics and only recently begun to read seriously in philosophy, I find myself defending a modified Rankean position on historical knowledge—one that accepts interpretation as unavoidable yet insists on a core of evidential constraint analogous to calibrated laboratory measurement. I take Leopold von Ranke's aspiration to recover the past as it actually occurred not as a naive claim to unmediated access, but as a regulative ideal: historians, like scientists, correct for observer bias and instrument error, leaving a residuum of fact that resists arbitrary reconstruction. Against this, E.H. Carr's argument that the historian's present concerns inevitably select and shape the evidence, and Hayden White's contention that historiography is fundamentally a literary exercise in emplotment, strike me as conflating the framing of data with the fabrication of data. Still, I want to know precisely what would count against my view—not in general, but specifically. Which arguments or findings in the philosophy of science, particularly concerning theory-ladenness and the underdetermination of theory by data, would show that the scientific analogy collapses rather than merely needs refinement? Which historical cases—perhaps those involving retrospective coherence, where later knowledge alters the evidential weight of earlier records in ways that have no parallel in physical experiment—would be fatal, and which merely awkward? Would a defender of this position concede that competing narratives with identical factual bases but incompatible causal or moral conclusions undermine the claim to evidential constraint, or would they dismiss that as a problem of framing? I am also concerned that my view might be qualified into unfalsifiability: if every counter-example is absorbed as interpretation within constraint, does the position risk becoming insulated in a way that should trouble anyone who claims scientific kinship for it? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish what committed defenders of historical objectivity themselves grant as damaging from what they reject, and provide your answer in British English.
