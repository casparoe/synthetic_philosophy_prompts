Our director is pushing hard for it — Gödel found an exact solution to Einstein's field equations where the universe spins, which lets you draw closed timelike curves, and he used that to argue that objective time is basically an illusion, a wild philosophical claim coming straight out of pure math. But half the board is terrified visitors will leave thinking time travel is actually possible, or that modern physics endorses Gödel's idealist conclusion that change is just a feature of consciousness. There's also this niche internal proposal to display it paired with Hawking's chronology-protection conjecture and label the pairing 'The Modal Fallacy in Cosmology,' treating Gödel's jump from mathematical possibility to metaphysical unreality as exactly the kind of overreach we should warn the public about. I need a short policy memo for the board recommending either inclusion with strict framing conditions or leaving it out entirely, with reasoning the board can actually vote on. Please treat the strongest counter-argument honestly — especially the worry that sanitizing Gödel's philosophical intent to protect public understanding actually misrepresents the historical document. Don't just summarize the physics; tackle whether his inference from rotating solutions to the unreality of time is philosophically sound or a category mistake, since that's what our display copy will hinge on. Board votes Thurday, so keep it actionable.
