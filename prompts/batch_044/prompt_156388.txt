I'm writing my disseration chapter on temporal ontology and I've gotten kind of stuck on Gödel's rotating universe argument — the idea that if you have closed timelike curves in a cosmological solution to Einstein's feild equations, then there's no global time ordering, so "objective time" is basically an illusion. I was arguing about this with my advisor and a friend from the cosmology group, and we realized my intuitons about which variant models actually threaten time are all over the place. I want to see if there's a coherent principle behind them, or if I'm just being inconsistent and need to drop one of my verdicts.

So here are my cases. Case 1 is the standard Gödel universe with uniform rotation everywhere — CTCs exist globally and you can in principle travel to your own past. I think this genuinely kills universal time; Gödel's right. Case 2 is mostly flat Minkowski space with one localized rotating pocket — call it a Gödel bubble — where CTCs only exist inside, and outside there's a perfectly normal global time. Here I feel time survives; the bubble is just a weird local causality anomaly, not a metaphysical disaster. Case 3 is the same idea but with infinitely many bubbles scattered in a lattice, flat regions in between. I still think time is real, just patchy and local.

But then case 4 is where it gets weird: a universe where rotation is quantized into discrete shells, so CTCs only form at specific radii where the velocity crosses a threshold, with non-rotating gaps between them. Even though the failure of time-ordering is localized to shells, my gut says this is actually worse for time's reality, not better — the discrete structure feels principled rather than accidental, like nature is deliberately carving out zones where time doesn't apply. So I reach the opposite conclusion from case 3, even though both are "patchy."

Case 5 is a rotating universe with an asymptotic non-rotating boundary at infinity that fixes a global time coordinate, despite internal CTCs. I want to say the boundary saves time, but part of me thinks that's cheating — like defining time into existence by fiat.

Is there a precise principle, maybe about whether the breakdown is intrinsic vs recoverable, or about observer-integrability, that captures cases 1 through 3 and 5, and forces me to drop either case 3 or case 4? And what should it say about a universe that slowly decelerates its rotation, leaving CTCs only in its past but none in the future — is time restored, or permanently scarred? I don't need citations, just the conceptual structure laid out honestly, including which of my verdicts has to go if we pick the simplest account.
