I'm putting together a consistency check for a paper I'm writing on what I'm calling archival speaker meaning—the idea that Gricean M-intentions can be directed at future or unknown readers of a solitary text, without requiring live conversational interaction. Here are my commitments in this area and adjacent Gricean territory:

(a) I accept Grice's M-intention analysis of speaker meaning, and I hold that speaker meaning is conceptually prior to sentence meaning: conventional linguistic meaning derives from stabilized patterns of speaker meaning, not the other way around.

(b) I treat the maxims of the Cooperative Principle—Quality, Quantity, Relation, Manner—as normative ideals governing rational conversation, not merely as descriptive generalizations about how people talk.

(c) I side with the minimalist or semanticist camp in thinking that so-called conventional implicatures, such as the contrastive force carried by the word but or the sequential force carried by therefore, belong to what is said (truth-conditional content), leaving only conversational implicature as genuinely implicated.

(d) I take Grice's standard account of irony and metaphor literally: the speaker asserts the false or absurd content, thereby flouting the Quality or Manner maxim, and the hearer derives the intended meaning by recognizing that flouting; there is no separate expressive layer that replaces the literal saying.

(e) I apply the Gricean framework intact to solitary written philosophical texts—say, a treatise read centuries after the author's death—by treating the text as carrying the author's original M-intentions, directed at a represented or intended disciplinary audience, even when that audience is unknown or no longer exists.

(f) I maintain that a genuinely implicated proposition must be non-cancellable: if a speaker can append but I do not mean to imply X without contradiction or pragmatic anomaly, then X was never truly implicated; explicit cancellation reveals a miscalculation by the hearer, not a feature of the implicature itself.
