I am preparing a position paper for my school's philosophy symposium on the epistemology of mathematics, and I want to check whether my commitments are jointly tenable before I defend them publicly. Roughly, I hold six theses. First, mathematical platonism: numbers, sets, and structures exist as abstract objects lacking spatiotemporal location and causal powers. Second, a uniform causal constraint on knowledge: for any subject S to know that p, there must be an appropriate causal or informational connection between S and the fact that makes p true. Third, mathematical knowledge is acquired through pure reasoning and conceptual intuition, with no causal interaction between the mathematician and abstract entities. Fourth, evolutionary debunking arguments are sound for basic numerical cognition—our elementary arithmetic intuitions are evolutionary byproducts that need not track abstract mathematical truths. Fifth, despite fourth, mathematical knowledge is not globally undermined; at least some of our beliefs about numbers and structures constitute genuine knowledge. Sixth, the tension can be resolved by supplementing the causal constraint with a modal-structural reliabilist condition: a belief counts as knowledge when it is produced by a cognitive process that is structurally reliable across nearby possible worlds, even absent direct causal contact with the object of belief.

My concern is that these may not hang together as neatly as I hope. I suspect there is a genuine conflict between the second and third theses that reproduces what is sometimes called Benacerraf's problem, and another between the fourth and fifth if evolutionary debunking extends beyond basic arithmetic. At the same time, I wonder whether the tension between the third and fourth theses is only apparent—perhaps evolutionary origins bear only on primitive intuition, leaving higher-order reasoning untouched. I also worry that my sixth thesis undermines my second rather than saving it. Could you audit this package? Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each pair what must be revised and at what cost, distinguish real conflicts from ones that dissolve upon closer inspection, and advise which single revision would do the most to restore consistency while keeping the overall framework intact.
