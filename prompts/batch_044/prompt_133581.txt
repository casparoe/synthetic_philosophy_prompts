I keep encountering the slogan that "tolerance must have limits," usually traced to the idea that unlimited tolerance eventually destroys itself, and most recently it was used in a debate about deplatforming to justify suppressing a particular extremist movement. The speaker moved quickly from "intolerance is dangerous" to "therefore we ought to suppress it," and I want to know exactly which logical bridge was being assumed. Can you disambiguate this slogan into its distinct precise theses — logical contradictions within the concept of tolerance, strategic or game-theoretic arguments about the stability of cooperative norms, empirical predictions about pluralist dynamics, and normative claims about the boundaries of liberal rights? For each reading, say whether it is a tautology, a contingent claim requiring evidence, a normative principle requiring defense, or a category mistake. Crucially, distinguish versions that concern individual moral dispositions from those that concern institutional rules of a polity, since the inference from "tolerance is unstable" to "the state should suppress group X" seems to cross levels. Then lay them out as a menu: for each interpretation, state whether it actually licenses the suppression inference, which ones are being illicitly borrowed when people invoke the paradox to shut down argument, and which ones remain defensible but do not yield that conclusion.
