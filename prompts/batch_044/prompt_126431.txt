I am a novelist drafting a long-form narrative set in a post-human archipelago where four symbiotic species—each embodying one of Daniel Dennett’s cognitive architectures—maintain a shared, decaying archive. Before I can write their interactions with any conviction, I need to lead a ninety-minute masterclass for fellow fiction writers, none of whom have studied cognitive science, and I want a pedagogical plan rather than an exposition.

Plan the session around Dennett’s taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures, treating them not as evolutionary stages but as four incommensurable design spaces for consciousness. First, lay out the exact order of introduction and the dependency chain among them: which architecture must be secured before the next can be distinguished without conceptual collapse, and why presenting Gregorian language-capacity before Popperian internal modeling, for instance, would cause this particular audience to mistake representation for mere communication.

Second, designate the single carrying scenario that should thread through all four sections. I propose using the archive’s “mnemonic spore” colony confronting a novel, lethal fungal bloom—each species facing the identical threat. Specify precisely which properties this scenario must retain to make each cognitive architecture visible (for example, the absence of internal simulation in the Skinnerian variant, the absence of symbolic transmission in the Popperian variant) and which anthropomorphic properties it must deliberately lack (no emotional vocabulary, no visual perspective, no first-person narration) so that literary intuition does not smuggle Gregorian traits into the earlier layers.

Third, map the misconception terrain for this audience—professional writers accustomed to treating interior monologue as the default mode of access. Identify the predictable errors: that Darwinian hard-wiring equals “primitive instinct,” that Skinnerian trial-and-error is merely “behaviorism applied to human characters,” that Popperian simulation is reducible to “being clever,” and that Gregorian cognition is defined by vocabulary rather than by the cultural offloading of cognitive labor. For each misconception, indicate the precise point in the sequence where it should be intercepted and defused, and provide the exact redirecting question or counter-example to deploy there.

Fourth, propose one concrete check for understanding near the close: a brief comparative writing task—perhaps asking each participant to sketch a three-paragraph scene from the point of view of a Popperian creature responding to the bloom without access to Gregorian tools—that would reveal whether they have grasped the structural boundary rather than simply adopted the four labels.

Finally, state explicitly what must be left out and openly acknowledged as excluded at this level: the neurobiological substrates of each architecture, Dennett’s historical evolutionary narrative, any analogy to contemporary large language models, and all bibliographic references to primary texts. Confirm that the plan should stand entirely on its own, requiring no outside reading from the participants.
