For an upper-level philosophy seminar final (90 minutes, approximately 1500 words), write one essay question on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition. The question must be unanswerable by reciting the standard summary of Bradley's argument and Russell's reply via external relations; instead, it should require students to apply the regress to a specific, constructed case and evaluate whether Russell's solution genuinely secures propositional unity. To achieve this without relying on obscure secondary literature, inject the following scenario into the prompt: ask students to analyze the proposition expressed by 'A gives B to C' as involving a three-place relation with asymmetric roles (agent, recipient, object), and to show step-by-step where Bradley's regress threatens at the level of binding these roles together, where Russell's appeal to external relations is supposed to halt it, and whether the directionality of each role introduces a relocated version of the same problem—specifically, whether relating the relation to its terms in ordered fashion requires further relations that reproduce the original difficulty. The question should not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or named citations of recent authors; students should work with the conceptual machinery of Bradley and Russell alone. Provide a model answer of about 600 words that demonstrates the required moves, and a marking scheme (20 marks total) that distinguishes an A from a B answer by identifying five specific analytical moves the answer must contain, allocating marks to each, and noting for each the typical mistake that costs points. Present your response clearly labeled: (a) the exam question, (b) the model answer, (c) the marking scheme. Conclude with a line stating the total available marks in the form \answer{20}.
