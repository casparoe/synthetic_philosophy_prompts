I'm giving the opening remarks tomorrow for this workshop we're running at the museum — it's called After the Fall and the room is going to be wierd. We've got Late Bronze Age people, Maya archeologists, someone who works on Rapa Nui, and then these climate resilience folks who basically need collapse to be real so they can use it for policy warnings. I'm not an expert, I just run programs, but I have to say something that doesn't insult either side and doesn't sound like I'm delivering a settled opinion from on high.

What's been bugging me is whether collapse is even the right kind of word. We treat it like societies are objects that can fall over — but maybe they just stop being the kind of thing that has that failure mode. Once you've lost the palace bureaucracy, or the writing system, or the central authority, you're not in the same ontological category anymore. So maybe the Bronze Age systems didn't collapse; they transformed into something that can't collapse in that specific way. Same with the Maya lowlands — it's not that they failed, it's that they stopped being a centralized state, and centralized-state failure modes don't apply. Even Easter Island might be about a community changing its resource base and social form, not a society falling over like a tree.

But I don't want to sound like I'm denying the suffering, because people clearly died, cities emptied, knowledge was lost. The experience was catastrophic for individuals even if the "collapse" is a retrospective label we slap on to make sense of discontinuity. I want the talk to hold that tension — that collapse might be a category error, but the pain was real — and I want it to feel like I'm actually thinking out loud, not lecturing. The Bronze Age, Maya, and Easter Island should come up naturally, not like examples on a slide. And please don't put in citations or tell me to read specific scholars — I wouldn't know who to cite and it would sound fake coming from me. Maybe there's a moment where I admit I used to think collapse was obvious until I started wondering what kind of thing has to exist for failure to even be possible? Just write it like I'm talking, with maybe a typo or two since I'm rushing this.
