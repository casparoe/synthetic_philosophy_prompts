I've been trying to make sense of the self-sampling assumption versus the self-indication assumption, especially that idea about the presumptuous philosopher. From what I gather, self-sampling means I should think of myself as a random pick from some group of possible observers, so my being here isn't especially surprising. Self-indication, on the other hand, seems to say that the fact I'm here at all is a clue that there are tons of observers out there—which is why it's called presumptuous to assume the universe is small. But I'm stuck on what this actually means for how I should live. If self-indication really does make big, observer-filled worlds more likely from my point of view, then wouldn't I have a reason to support things that create more people in the future, like encouraging population growth, rather than things that might prevent people from existing, like working on artificial intelligence risks? Am I wrong to think that I could use this reasoning to decide between causes? Or is the error that I'm mixing up self-sampling and self-indication, or treating a rule about what to believe as a rule about what to do? I want to understand whether these anthropic ideas are just about updating beliefs, or if they really do tell us something practical about which global problems are worth focusing on.
