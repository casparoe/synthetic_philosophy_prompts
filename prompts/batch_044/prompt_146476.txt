Write a joke about the problem of material constitution, specifically the classic case of a statue and the clay lump that constitutes it (often referred to as "Lump"). The joke should turn on the actual philosophical tension: that the statue and the lump share all the same matter and occupy the same region, yet differ in modal and aesthetic properties—destroying the statue by melting it does not destroy the lump, which raises the puzzle of whether they are strictly identical or merely coincident objects. To keep it from being a generic museum or art-world quip, frame the punchline around a bureaucratic mishap—imagine a museum filing two separate insurance claims, one for the statue and one for the underlying clay, after a minor gallery accident, and an adjuster rejecting the second claim on the grounds that "it's the same object." The curator's reply should hinge precisely on the distinction between constitution and identity, not on broad stereotypes about philosophers, insurers, or art. The result should be funny mainly to someone who grasps why coincidence without identity is conceptually awkward.
