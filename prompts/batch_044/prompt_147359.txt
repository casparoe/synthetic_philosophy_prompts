The payoff structure is symmetric: (C,C) yields (3,3); (D,D) yields (1,1); and unilateral defection yields (5,0) to the defector. Because the agents are superrational—recognizing that isomorphic reasoning produces isomorphic outputs—they understand divergence is impossible, leaving only the symmetric outcomes to compare.

Second, the exam introduced a modified Luring Lottery developed for the course: each agent independently decides whether to purchase a single raffle entry at cost 2. If exactly one enters, that agent receives 10 (net 8); if both enter or both abstain, the payoff is 0 (with entrants bearing the -2 cost). After decisions are finalized, an asymmetric revelation protocol activates: a mediator privately informs each agent whether the other entered, but does not reveal her own entry status or outcome. At this stage, either agent may invoke a shared recall mechanism that nullifies all raffle outcomes, imposing an additional shared cost of 1 per agent.

The question required students to determine whether superrational reasoning leads to mutual entry, mutual abstention, or divergence in each case, and specifically to explain how the recall mechanism interacts with the informational asymmetry of the revelation protocol—not merely whether it is invoked.

Here is the student's answer, reproduced verbatim:

"In the Platonia dilemma, superrational agents recognize they must make identical choices. Comparing the symmetric outcomes, mutual cooperation yields 3 and mutual defection yields 1, so they converge on cooperation. However, if they were merely rational in the causal sense, each would defect regardless of the other, yielding 1. The Luring Lottery is different because entry costs 2. If both enter, each gets -2; if both abstain, each gets 0; divergence is impossible under superrationality. Therefore they abstain. The recall mechanism is redundant: even if the revelation protocol tells one agent that the other entered, invoking recall costs an additional 1, making the payoff -3 if they entered, or -1 if they didn't. Since there's no divergence in the first place, no one ever invokes it. Thus the mechanism doesn't change the equilibrium."
