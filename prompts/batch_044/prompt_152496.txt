I have been thinking on my own about how people decide which large-scale problems deserve the most attention and resources. Most discussions I have seen mention three criteria: the magnitude of the problem, how tractable it appears to be, and how neglected it currently is. I want to propose an additional criterion that I have not seen emphasized—call it reversibility, meaning how easily the consequences of misallocation can be undone if one's assessment turns out to be wrong.

Here are some rough notes applying this four-part framework to artificial-intelligence risk and climate change, written without any formal training in philosophy or economics:

- Magnitude: Both appear severe, though climate damage is already observable while extreme AI risk remains hypothetical.
- Tractability: Climate mitigation has established engineering and policy pathways; AI safety involves more speculative alignment research.
- Neglectedness: Climate receives enormous institutional funding; AI safety is comparatively small but growing rapidly.
- Reversibility: Over-investment in climate infrastructure still produces useful energy and adaptation assets; over-investment in AI safety might still yield beneficial capabilities research, but misdirecting talent away from climate could lock in irreversible ecological tipping points.

My questions are these. First, does reversibility genuinely add analytical value, or is it already implicitly captured within tractability and magnitude? Second, is it coherent to treat reversibility as an independent axis, or does it collapse into a judgment about temporal urgency? Third, am I being unfair to either case given my lack of technical expertise?

I would value comments on the logic of the framework, on whether my comparison holds up, and on any obvious objections I have missed.
