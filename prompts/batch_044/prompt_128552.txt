I'm writing a taxonomy for a workshop on Marx and automation, but I want it to sort people by there actual commitments, not by which book they cite. Build a decision tree with three branch points, each a concrete claim.

First claim: imagine a fully automated mine with zero human operators inside, but remote engineers maintain the AI and truck drivers still move the ore. Is the surplus value extracted there coming mainly from dead labor embodied in the robots, or from the living labor of the remote workers?

Second claim: Marx talks about knowledge becoming a direct force of production. Does that mean capital successfully absorbs collective intelligence into machinery, or does it show capital depending on social cooperation it cant fully privatize?

Third claim: if automation permanently removes more jobs than it creates in a sector, is that best read as Marx's cyclical reserve-army effect, or as a structural tendency toward absolute surplus population?

For each of the eight logical combos, place a position if one is well-known — orthodox productivist, autonomist, post-work fragment-style, eco-Marxist metabolic-rift — or mark it unnamed. For any empty combo, say whether its incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag if your tree cant actually seperate two leaves with these three questions alone. Keep it practical: I want to place a students view after they answer the claims, not after they name-drop there favorite theorist.
