Write a proposal for a one-semester term paper (approximately 6,000 words) on whether the wrong-kind-reasons distinction applies at the level of normative framework selection, not merely within an already-accepted framework. State the question precisely: can there be wrong-kind reasons for adopting consequentialism over deontology — or for retaining a particular conceptual taxonomy in ethics — in the same structural sense that there are wrong-kind reasons for belief or admiration? Explain why current treatments of wrong-kind reasons leave this open: they typically presuppose a fixed normative background and analyse first-order attitudes against it, without asking whether the distinction survives when the attitude in question is the choice of the background itself. Outline the method: examine whether the constitutive aims that generate wrong-kind reasons at the first-order level (truth for belief, the value of the object for admiration) have analogues for framework choice, testing this against three candidate domains (moral theory, epistemic policy, and aesthetic categorisation). Specify the possible outcomes — that the distinction either collapses into pure instrumental rationality at the meta-level, or that it survives with a modified account of theoretical virtue — and say what considerations would adjudicate between them. Conclude with an honest assessment of scope risks, especially the danger of sliding into first-order moral argument rather than meta-normative analysis, and confirm that the project is feasible within one semester without requiring empirical research or archival work. Write in British English.
