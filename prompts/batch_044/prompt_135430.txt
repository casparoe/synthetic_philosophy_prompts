Analyze the October 1917 Bolshevik seizure of power in Russia as a case study in historical overdetermination and multi-causal explanation. Do not merely list causes; instead, map the principal causal candidates—protracted war fatigue and economic disintegration, the Provisional Government’s failure to establish legitimate authority, the organizational readiness of the Bolshevik party, and the contingent timing of Lenin’s return and strategic decisions—onto the philosophical distinction between causal overdetermination (where multiple individually sufficient conditions operate concurrently) and mere multi-causality (where several jointly necessary conditions combine to produce an outcome). For each candidate, specify whether it functions best as an INUS condition, a contributing cause, or a redundant sufficient condition within the overall explanatory structure.

Then address the explanatory competition problem: if counterfactual removal of Lenin’s leadership still yields revolutionary upheaval through spontaneous soviet action, and if removal of the war context still yields crisis through agrarian collapse, does this demonstrate that the Revolution was genuinely overdetermined? Or does it reveal that “revolution in October 1917” is an under-described outcome that fragments into different events under counterfactual scrutiny—say, a localized Petrograd coup versus a broad-based social transformation? Evaluate whether the framework of overdetermination helps historians adjudicate between structuralist and intentionalist accounts, or whether it merely redescribes their disagreement without resolving it.

Finally, identify where the philosophical framework is silent. In particular, consider whether an overdetermined historical event carries different explanatory weight, predictive fragility, or normative significance than a causally fragile one, and whether recognizing redundancy in causation should lead historians to downgrade the importance of any single factor—including human agency—or instead to reconceive agency as operating within an overdetermined field rather than against it. Keep the analysis disciplined: say exactly who or what counts as the “player,” what the relevant options and outcomes are, and where the theory offers no guidance on how to rank or weight simultaneous sufficient causes.
