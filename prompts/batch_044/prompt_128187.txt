I hold that expected-value fanaticism—the refusal to reject choices with astronomically high payoffs at astronomically low probabilities—can be defended only by coupling it to a modal-realist metaphysics: if all possible outcomes are equally real branches weighted by measure, then maximizing expected value is not gambling but accounting for actual worlds. What would count against this combined position? Rank the defeaters by decisiveness: failures of modal realism or branching-worlds theories; decision-theoretic results showing that weighting by measure is incoherent or normatively irrelevant; cases where the metaphysics and the decision theory come apart; and whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability. I want an honest assessment of what defenders of this package would themselves concede as damaging, versus what they would dismiss, and whether the insulation is a virtue or a vice.
