I am a doctoral student in philosophy of religion and psychoanalytic theory, and I defend a reconstruction of Freud’s account of religion that departs sharply from the standard debunking reading. On my view, The Future of an Illusion and the social-psychological sections of Civilization and Its Discontents are not best interpreted as a genetic fallacy—religious belief undermined by its origin in infantile helplessness and the father-complex. Rather, Freud is advancing a structural-functional claim about collective psychology: religious ritual and doctrine operate as an irreducible mechanism for binding the death drive into socially tolerable forms of sublimation, such that any durable group formation must replicate the formal architecture of religious belief—an externalized, prohibitory authority that is subsequently internalized—regardless of whether the content is theistic, secular, or ideological. The label “illusion” therefore describes not epistemic deficiency but a specific mode of collective cathexis that rational consent alone cannot replace without releasing unbound aggression into the social field.

What would count decisively against this reconstruction? I want an honest map of its exposure, not merely the standard objections. Which arguments or textual moves in the 1927 and 1930 works, read without charitable supplementation, actually commit Freud to causal debunking rather than to this functionalist structuralism? Which empirical or theoretical developments—say, in the study of secular ritual, civil religion, or institutional mechanisms for aggression-binding—would demonstrate that the formal properties I attribute to religion are genuinely dispensable in group life? What distinctive predictions does this view generate about secularization trajectories or about the psychological structure of modern mass ideologies, and have observable patterns in either domain already contradicted them? I am especially concerned with whether the position risks insulation: if every successful secular binding mechanism can be redescribed as functionally religious, does that render the claim unfalsifiable, and is that a theoretical virtue or a fatal defect for a view of this kind? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguishing those that a committed defender of this functionalist Freud ought to concede as damaging from those they could consistently dismiss as misreadings. I weigh textual fidelity more heavily than theoretical elegance, so prioritize arguments that turn on what Freud actually committed himself to in these two texts.
