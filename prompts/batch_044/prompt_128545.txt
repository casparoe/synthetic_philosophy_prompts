Having spent three decades in molecular biology and only recently begun serious study in philosophy of science, I am presenting a seminar argument that interventionist theories of causation systematically misrepresent gene-regulatory networks: because downward feedback loops violate the modular isolation required for surgical interventions, treating transcription-factor dependencies as intervention-stable relations distorts the actual dynamics; a refined counterfactual framework that permits context-sensitive dependency without physical separation, I claim, captures these loops more faithfully. The audience is an interdisciplinary philosophy-of-science seminar of eight mechanist philosophers of biology strongly committed to interventionist explanation, three philosophers of physics who regard special-sciences causation as reducible or eliminable, and two biostatisticians convinced both approaches are merely equivalent parameterizations of the same structural-equation model. I need a Q&A preparation sheet: list approximately eight objections I will actually face, ordered from the most damaging to the least, each phrased in the vocabulary those specific interlocutors would use, together with a concise reply suitable for a two-minute exchange, an explicit note on where that reply is weakest, and a clear indication of what I should concede up front rather than defend. Please include the objection I am hoping nobody raises—specifically, the charge that without an interventionist invariance test my counterfactual relations collapse into mere correlation. The reply should be practical and should not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
