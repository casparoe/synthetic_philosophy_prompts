I am fluent in the vocabulary of machine learning—hypothesis classes, inductive bias, VC dimension, distribution shift, feature extractors, and prior distributions over model parameters—but I have no formal philosophical training.

Please restate Goodman’s problem of induction, specifically the grue/bleen paradox and the notion of projectible predicates, using only that machine-learning vocabulary. Treat “projectibility” as analogous to the inductive biases that constrain which hypotheses a learner considers from a given training set, and treat “entrenchment” as analogous to the prior probabilities or architectural constraints that favour certain feature representations over others. Then provide a rigorous residue report: identify which elements of Goodman’s argument translate cleanly into discussions of underdetermination, no-free-lunch theorems, and out-of-distribution generalisation; which elements can only be carried over with distortion (for instance, the linguistic and culturally transmitted nature of predicate entrenchment versus the engineered or data-driven origins of ML inductive biases); and which elements have no genuine counterpart in statistical learning theory at all—particularly any normative claim about which predicates we ought to project. In each case of residue, indicate whether the gap reflects a limitation of the ML framework (for example, its silence on normative epistemology) or instead reveals that the corresponding philosophical claim was less determinate than it appeared.
