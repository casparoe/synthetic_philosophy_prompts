I'm on the review board for my city's "unexpected spaces" grant -- we fund temporary instalations in laundromats, bus shelters, empty lots, that kind of thing. Our charter just says "art," and I have to deside if a proposal counts before it goes to the board chare for sign-of. I get maybe six a month, I have about fiften minutes per file, I'm not trained in aestetics, and the stakes are public money plus access to shared spaces.

I want a reusable decision procedure based on the idea that art is basically an intentional presentation that breaks the usual functional categorization of whatever it's made of -- so the thing is shown in a way that forces you to see it outside its normal use-category -- combined with the check that at least one recognized art-world practice (gallery, museum, critical writing, art school curriculum, etc.) has treated similar work as art. Not just the maker saying so.

Write me the procedure: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a hard no, where it stops with a yes, and exactly where it has to hand the decision to a person because it's values not facts. Be honest about where it will misfire or give weird answers.

Then run it on three proposals:
1. A stack of clean laundry baskets arranged in a laundromat with a small plaque reading "instalation: baskets."
2. A genuinely beautiful bus shelter designed by an architect who says she intended it as public art, but it's fully functional and looks basically like every other shelter except for color.
3. A performer who silently sorts trash in a park for three hours, with no audience, no photos, no record at all -- just the performer insisting later that it happened and it was art.

Make sure one of these gives me an answer I'll probably hate. And tell me clearly what this procedure flat-out can't settle, no matter how carefully I run it.
