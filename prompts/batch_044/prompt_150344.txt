Construct a thought experiment for a philosophical novel in progress. Design a scenario—no longer than one paragraph, realistic in its internal logic, free of exotic physics or explicit bibliographic reference—in which an author can generate "partially conscious narrative agents," beings whose subjective experience is entirely confined to the causal structure of a story-world and who have no existence outside its diegetic frame. Arrange the conditions so that an act-utilitarian assessment of the narrative's value (reader edification, emotional resonance, aesthetic coherence) weighs heavily in favor of creating these agents, yet Benatar's asymmetry between the absence of pain and the absence of pleasure applies to them with full force, yielding the verdict that the author has an overriding reason never to instantiate them. After the scenario, demonstrate that the specifications are met: show the act-utilitarian reasoning step by step, show how Benatar's asymmetry blocks the pro-creation conclusion, identify the precise structural feature that allows the asymmetry to override the utilitarian balance rather than merely parallel it, and explain how a defender of procreation—biological or artistic—would attempt to dissolve the analogy between generating narrative agents and generating human lives. Finally, report what occurs if that structural feature is removed: does the asymmetry still conflict with the utilitarian calculus, or does the case revert to a familiar form of the pro-natalist objection?
