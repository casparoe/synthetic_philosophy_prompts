I serve on my university's Committee for Interdisciplinary Program Approval. A proposal has arrived for a year-long seminar series, co-hosted by our physics department and our program in medieval Islamic philosophy, tentatively titled Custom and Causation. The proposal centers on Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism — specifically his argument that observed regularities, such as fire burning cotton, are not necessary causal connections inherent in created things but customary conjunctions sustained directly by divine will, which God could in principle interrupt though ordinarily chooses not to. The theology co-sponsor insists that this framework must be taught as a live epistemological option rather than a historical curiosity, because it rigorously separates predictive success, which requires only stable regularity, from metaphysical commitment to intrinsic causal powers in nature. The physics co-sponsor has submitted a formal dissent: she grants that Al-Ghazālī denies created things possess inherent efficacy, but she argues that presenting occasionalism as a live framework risks conflating methodological naturalism with metaphysical denial of causation, leaving students unable to articulate why scientific explanation seeks mechanisms rather than merely recording correlations, and she asks that the seminar be rejected unless it can demonstrate that students will learn to maintain that distinction rather than treating it as purely theological. Our committee must issue a written opinion recommending approval, rejection, or conditional approval with a concrete revision timeline or implementation deadline, and the opinion should treat the physics dissent seriously rather than dismissing it as secular prejudice. Please draft the opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should open with a clear bottom-line recommendation, then present the reasoning in continuous prose only — no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — as in a formal letter, and it should include an honest engagement with the strongest consideration on the losing side.
