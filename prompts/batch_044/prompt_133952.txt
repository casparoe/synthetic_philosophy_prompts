I'm on the research council's joint funding panel and we're deadlocked on renewing the Pure Math and Physics grant for another five years. The center does very abstract work — category-theoretic structures in quantum field theory, that kind of thing — with no near-term payoff. The director's defense leans on Wigner's point about the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics: formal structures keep turning out to describe nature far better than they should, so funding them blindly is rational even without a use-case. But half the panel thinks that's outdated in the age of deep learning. They say we now get predictions from black-box architectures that don't need explicit mathematical modeling, so the effectiveness has shifted to computation and funding pure math on Wigner-style hopes is just nostalgia. There's a split proposal on the table: half the grant to the math center, half to a new ML-for-science initiative.

We need a memo for Thursday's vote recommending full renewal, full redirect, or split with conditions, plus a concrete timeline for implementation if we go split. The memo should take the Wigner argument seriously — don't just call it old — but also deal with the bypass-by-computation objection honestly. I want the reasoning to show what we're sacrificing either way, not just cheer for one side. Can you draft it? Two pages max, clear bottom line up front, and keep the tone formal but readable. Also please address wether the split actually solves the philosophical tension or just papers over it.
