Analyze the 2012 Norwegian sentencing of Breivik—21 years with possible extention—through the Dennett vs Caruso debate on just deserts and punishment. Dennett defends a compatibilist view that the kind of free will worth wanting can ground responsibility without metaphysical libertarianism; Caruso rejects desert entirely and pushes a public-health quarantine model. So: map the court's reasoning and the international outrage onto both views. Does Dennett's framework license Norway's refusal to retribute, or does it actually demand suffering proportional to harm? Does Caruso's quarantine model explain the 21-year cap plus extention, or does it miss why the trial was staged as a moral reckoning for victims? Then flip it: does this case show Dennett's varieties of free will can do real work in penal policy, or prove Caruso right that once you drop the desert premise punishment collapses into something the public can't recognize?
