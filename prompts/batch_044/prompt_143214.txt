I'm working through a tension in comparative ethics for a seminar I'm preparing, and I'd like you to write an essay that takes a clear stand rather than remaining neutral. The standard comparison of Mencius and Xunzi frames human nature as either containing innate moral sprouts—the four beginnings—that wither without proper affective and communal cultivation, or as inherently driven toward self-interest and made good only through rigorous ritual, education, and artificial transformation. Rather than rehearse that abstract debate, apply it to what I will call "bureaucratic virtue fatigue": the observed pattern in which professionals in highly regulated fields—medicine, law, finance—complete extensive ethical training yet subsequently display not genuine moral commitment but a performative, cynical compliance that collapses as soon as oversight weakens.

Weigh the Mencian and Xunzian explanations of this pattern with precision. A Mencian account might argue that modern institutional environments are structurally hostile to the sprouts: they prioritize efficiency, competition, and hierarchical accountability over the conditions—empathy, shame, communal belonging, and reflective leisure—that allow innate moral tendencies to grow, so the fatigue represents cultivated goodness being starved. A Xunzian account, by contrast, might argue that this is exactly what happens when artificial transformation is half-completed: the rituals of compliance are adopted as instruments of self-advancement rather than internalized, because human nature was never genuinely altered, only temporarily constrained by external incentives. After weighing these, reach and defend a specific verdict: does bureaucratic virtue fatigue show that we have failed to cultivate innate goodness, or that innate self-interest is too resilient for institutional ritual alone? Your conclusion should address whether ethical reform should therefore prioritize environmental redesign that nourishes moral sprouts (the Mencian path), or stricter, more pervasive structural transformation that reshapes incentives entirely (the Xunzian path).

Please be explicit about the weakest point in your reasoning—what finding about moral psychology or institutional behavior would cause you to reverse your verdict? If your argument depends on the claim that people possess some innate moral tendency or lack thereof, identify the empirical or conceptual challenge that would most undermine that premise, and state clearly what it would take for you to concede that the opposing classical view offers the better framework.
