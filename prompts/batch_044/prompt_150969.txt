As a student representative on my school's Governance Ethics Subcommittee, I need a formal written opinion to circulate before Thursday's meeting of the Student Legislative Council. The case is Proposal 7-B, which would amend our charter to impose a "participation obligation" in all ranked-choice elections for student-government offices. Non-participation would not trigger disciplinary sanctions; instead, the student would forfeit the right to propose agenda items for one term. Supporters argue that voting is a civic duty analogous to jury service: because the council distributes tangible resources—club funding, shared facility access—abstention is not neutral dissent but a delegation of harm to others, especially since the ranked-choice format lets voters order preferences without endorsing anyone as worthy. Opponents, including two subcommittee members, contend that the ballot is primarily expressive: if a student genuinely judges every candidate unfit, forcing a complete ranking requires fabricating a false preference order, corrupting the moral integrity of the act. They add that the penalty—loss of proposal rights—silences dissent rather than fostering engagement. We must recommend either adoption or rejection, include a concrete implementation timeline or an alternative mechanism, and take the expressive-integrity objection seriously rather than dismissing it as apathy. Please also identify the strongest argument for the side you reject, specify what consideration or evidence would change your recommendation, and indicate where your reasoning is most vulnerable to failure.
