Aquinas draws a sharp line between a usurper and a legitimate ruler turned bad. In both cases he warns that overthrowing someone risks sedition and greater harm to the common good, so resistance is only okay when it wont make things worse.

Here efficacy mostly determines standing—because no legitimate authority ever existed, there is no one with proper jurisdiction to appeal to. Aquinas also seems to allow preemptive resistance in the usurper case—if his tyrannical intentions are clear, you do not need to wait for the worst—whereas for a legitimate ruler you must wait until the harm is actual and extreme.
