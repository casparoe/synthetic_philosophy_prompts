Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of calibrated epistemic modesty must satisfy—where modesty is understood not as blanket self-doubt but as the disciplined integration of first-personal reasoning (the inside view) with third-personal statistical evidence about reasoning reliability (the outside view). Specify the phenomena it must cover: cases in which domain-specific expert reasoning conflicts with peer-disagreement or base-rate statistics about experts in that domain; cases of deep framework disagreement where no shared statistical baseline exists; and cases in which an agent recognizes her own documented cognitive biases yet retains access to reasons she can evaluate only from the inside. For each phenomenon, state the corresponding requirement. Then list the distinctions the theory must respect—between object-level belief and meta-level confidence, between local expertise and global reasoning failure, and between genuine peer disagreement and mere noise—giving the motivating case behind each, and flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, especially any tension between demanding determinate belief-revision outputs and forbidding prescriptions that require the agent to adopt beliefs she cannot understand or assess from her first-personal standpoint. Finally, indicate which requirements are non-negotiable and which are negotiable if the purpose is rational self-governance rather than predictive accuracy. Conclude with a brief scorecard: how a pure inside-view rationalism, a pure outside-view statistical-deference model, and a two-layer view that assigns inside-view authority to step-level reasoning and outside-view authority to framework selection each fare against your list, noting for each which desiderata it meets and which it sacrifices.
