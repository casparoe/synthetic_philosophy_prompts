i hold that honoring sunk costs isn't always irrational — specifically when the cost was part of a diachronic commitment device that binds future preferences, not just in the usual behavioral-econ sense but as a credibility investment in your own preference stability. sticking with a losing project can then preserve the device's future value even if marginal payoff is negative. what would count against this? i want arguments and evidence sorted by decisiveness — empirical failures of commitment devices, formal results showing the binding collapses, or cases where honoring the cost clearly destroys rather than preserves credibility. also be honest: has this view been qualified so much that nothing could falsifiy it, and is that insulation a problem for a claim about rationality? include whether defenders of the standard fallacy view would concede anything here, and whether the position risks collapsing into "people just rationalize after the fact."
