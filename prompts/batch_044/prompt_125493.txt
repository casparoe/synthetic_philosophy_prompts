I sit on our university's AI governance board — we're the committee that approves pilot tech projects before they run in actual campus buildings. We've got a proposal in front of us for an autonomous facilities-management system that claims to solve the side-effect problem without baking in any human values. The reasearchers say they've defined impact purely structurally: the AI computes a 'natural trajectory' of the building's state-space over the next 48 hours assuming it never takes any action, then penalises any deviation from that trajectory, regardless of whether the deviation is good or bad. They call it a value-neutral impact measure and want to deploy it in teh library and student union, where it would control HVAC, lighting, and access routes in real time.

The philosophical problem, as I see it, is that picking the baseline already smuggles in choices about what counts as normal — the natural trajectory includes people moving around, doors opening, heat accumulating, but deciding which variables belong in the state-space and which don't is itself a normative call. One colleague argues this is just a technical framing issue and that we should approve with standard monitouring conditions. Another thinks we're being sold a sleight-of-hand: if you can't specify what's valuable without defining side effects, then value-neutrality collapses, and we shouldn't authorise deployment until they either admit the value-ladenness or show exactly how their baseline avoids it.

Please draft teh short opinion memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. Recommend a concrete course of action — approve, reject, or approve with conditions and a clear timeline — with reasoning the committee can vote on. Take seriously the claim that structural divergence is genuinely different from preference-based impact measures, but don't let it off the hook if it relies on hidden assumptions. Don't just restate legal policy; we need to know what we're committing the university to. I don't want bibliographies or reading lists, just the argument and the recommendation. Oh, and I was discussing this with a friend over coffee yesterday — she asked whether avoiding side effects is even coherent without knowing what state of the world we're trying to protect, which is basically the question I'm stuck on.
