The Rietdijk–Putnam argument that special-relativistic simultaneity undermines an objective present, and therefore supports a block-universe ontology, has been stalled for decades: presentists respond by positing a privileged but empirically undetectable foliation, or by reconstruing Lorentz symmetry as purely representational, while eternalists reply that such moves render the present metaphysically inert. Rather than adjudicating the positions, I want a diagnosis of the stalemate itself. Is this a substantive disagreement about whether spacetime geometry constrains ontology, or is it partly verbal—hinging on whether “exists now” must carry a unique hyperplane index—or are the sides answering different questions, with one camp asking about the structure of physical law and the other about the conditions for temporal experience? In particular, consider whether the hidden driver is not physics but a disagreement about explanatory standards: must a theory of time account for the phenomenology of passage, or is geometric invariance sufficient? Give me the diagnosis supported by what each side actually commits to, and say whether any formal result—say, a proof that presentist dynamics necessarily violate Lorentz covariance—or any conceptual distinction could actually move it, or whether the impasse reveals that “the present” was never a coordinate-independent physical quantity to begin with.
