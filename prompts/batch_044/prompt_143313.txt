For my philosophy seminar, I need to apply G. E. Moore's account of intrinsic goods. Moore treats both beauty and friendship as among the greatest intrinsic goods, and he argues that certain combinations form organic unities whose value exceeds the sum of the values of their parts. Consider two hypothetical minimal societies: Society X, in which every member participates in a shared, non-competitive appreciation of one beautiful form but has no personal bonds with anyone else; and Society Y, in which every member enjoys deep mutual friendship but shares no common aesthetic experience at all. Rank these societies according to how well they realize Moore's ideal of the good life, assigning each a score from 1 to 10 and placing them in tiers (S, A, B, C, or D). In one sentence for each, state whether the absence of friendship in Society X or the absence of beauty in Society Y does greater harm to the organic unity of the good, as Moore understands it.
