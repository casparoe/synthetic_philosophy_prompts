I coordinate a small hospice volunteer program where limited pain medication must be rationed among patients. I try to allocate doses to maximize comfort, yet I notice that my internal state during the decision feels morally significant: distributing with calm equanimity and without attachment to results seems ethically sound, while distributing the same doses out of anxiety, favoritism, or obsessive calculation feels corrupted, even though the patients' physical suffering is identical. I am unsure whether my duty is to perfect my allocation method, to purify my mind regardless of outcome, or to treat these as inseparable tasks. How should I understand the conflict between outcome and intention in this case?
