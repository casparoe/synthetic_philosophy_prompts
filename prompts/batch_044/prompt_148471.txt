I am preparing a brief ice-breaker for a philosophy of mind seminar I am running next term, and I would like a joke that introduces the concept of sphexishness without reducing it to a generic remark about insects being robotic. To be precise about the philosophical target: sphexishness refers to the rigid, stereotyped behavioural loop observed in the digger wasp Sphex ichneumoneus, which executes an invariant sequence—digging a burrow, dragging paralysed prey inside, inspecting the interior, then exiting to pull the prey in—and, if interrupted at any stage, begins the entire routine again from the first step rather than adapting to the altered circumstances. The philosophical point is not that the wasp is stupid, but that its actions display a mechanical determinism lacking the flexible, context-sensitive revision that we take to be characteristic of genuine agency and, on many accounts, of free will.

Could you write a concise joke that turns specifically on this compulsive repetition of the full loop upon interruption, contrasting it with the adaptive reasoning expected in a social or technological setting? The punchline should depend on the actual structure of the behaviour—perhaps the wasp is placed in a scenario where negotiation, tool-use, or problem-solving is required, and its response is not failure but an absurd, unbreakable restart of the burrow-checking sequence. It should be amusing to someone who understands why philosophers invoke this case in debates about determinism and freedom, and it ought to preserve the distinction between mechanical rigidity and mere lack of intelligence. Please keep it suitable for delivery aloud, and avoid framing the wasp as malicious or conscious—let the humour arise from the mismatch between the situation's demand for flexibility and the wasp's invariant routine.
