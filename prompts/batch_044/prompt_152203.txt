I am a software engineer without formal philosophy training, and I am trying to work out whether a cluster of views I have adopted about Adam Smith holds together. Here are my commitments: (1) Smithian sympathy, as described in The Theory of Moral Sentiments, is not mere emotional contagion but a constrained counterfactual simulation that computes the discrepancy between one's own affective state and a projected state of another; (2) the impartial spectator is therefore not an innate moral faculty but an emergent regularization mechanism that stabilizes when these simulations are iterated across observers and corrected over time; (3) this implies that all moral judgments are indexed to the computational limits of the simulator, so there is no genuinely detached standpoint available even in principle; (4) despite this, I want to hold that Smith is a moral realist in a weak sense: the convergence patterns of well-calibrated spectators track real normative properties rather than merely reflecting shared biases; (5) I further claim that the mechanism in The Wealth of Nations operates through the same discrepancy-computation structure, except that in market exchange the affective component is suppressed and only the informational residue—price signals—remains functional. My questions: Does commitment (3) undermine (4) by reducing convergence to intersubjective agreement? Does (5) conflict with Smith's well-known observation that exchange depends not on benevolence but on self-interest, since my account seems to require sympathy to operate even in markets? And is there a hidden tension between treating the spectator as emergent in (2) yet treating its deliverances as tracking reality in (4)? Please rank these tensions by severity, indicate for each whether it is real or only apparent, propose the least costly revision, and tell me which single change to my view-set would resolve the most conflicts.
