I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of science and cognitive theory, preparing a working paper on anthropic reasoning in cosmological and artificial-mind contexts. Following a sustained exchange with a colleague who defends the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) against my own commitments to the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA), we have agreed that our disagreement about Nick Bostrom's presumptuous-philosopher objection has become unproductive because we keep conflating three distinct issues: the formal definition of our respective principles, the proper calibration of priors over cosmological models, and the status of observer-counting in worlds containing synthetic minds. Rather than continuing to talk past each other, I want us to produce an adversarial-collaboration document — a joint statement that both of us could sign — that separates genuine disagreement from verbal confusion and records precise predictions in advance.

Please draft this document on behalf of the two parties. Party A is an SSA advocate who maintains that rational credence must be distributed over observer-moments within a reference class fixed by the reasoner's actual properties, and who takes the presumptuous-philosopher scenario to demonstrate that SIA implausibly rewards belief in vast universes irrespective of independent prior evidence. Party B is an SIA advocate who maintains that the evidential weight of finding oneself an observer is proportional to the total number of observers in the relevant kind, and who argues that the presumptuous-philosopher reductio fails because it applies an uncalibrated uniform prior over cosmological hypotheses rather than exposing an internal flaw in self-indication. Both parties should treat as central a framing we have developed in our private discussions: the elastic reference-class problem. Specifically, they must address a hypothetical scenario in which future synthetic cognitive architectures are instantiated with continuously adjustable parameters of subjective coherence, phenomenological density, and temporal integration, so that there is no sharp boundary between a full observer and a sub-observer fragment. Each side should state whether its principle requires drawing a hard boundary in this continuum, whether the boundary can be indexed to the reasoner's own architecture, and how each principle weights large populations of near-observer fragments relative to full observers.

The document should contain: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims about the formal structure of SSA and SIA, about the role of priors in anthropic reasoning, about the logical structure of the presumptuous-philosopher argument (paraphrased without quotation marks), and about the empirical or conceptual status of observer-counting; (2) a disagreements table with columns for the issue, Party A's formulation (worded so that Party A accepts it), Party B's formulation (worded so that Party B accepts it), and the designated crux; (3) a cruxes section identifying at least three sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party — I anticipate these will concern whether reference-class membership must be fixed independently of the theory being evaluated, whether the presumptuous-philosopher objection genuinely undermines SIA or merely reveals a misapplied prior, and how each principle handles the elastic reference-class scenario, including explicit predictions about what each side expects if humanity were to instantiate large populations of near-observer fragments; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case, argument, or empirical scenario both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Please write in a formal, polished tone suitable for a philosophical working paper, avoid attributing positions to named living scholars beyond Bostrom's well-known framing, do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not invent verbatim quotations.
