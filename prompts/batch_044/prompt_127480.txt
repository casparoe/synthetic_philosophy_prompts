I'm trying to understand these urban planning boards that drop public votes and instead use predictive well-being metrics — stress scores, commute models, satisfaction forecasts — to implement zoning changes automatically. Residents aren't asked for consent; they're told experts know what makes them happy better than they do. The justification sounds like the Grand Inquisitor idea: people claim to want freedom but really suffer under too much choice, so it's kinder to manage things for them. What view of freedom and happiness does that institutional design presuppose? Does it treat autonomy as purely instrumental to well-being, or does it deny there's any value in self-determination beyond satisfaction? And is it consistent with demcratic norms that treat self-rule as non-negotiable, or does it quietly replace autonomy with benevolent management? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly needs from what just fits well with it, and say what parts of democratic practice would have to change if we actually took that view seriously.
