I'm trying to reverse-engineer the implicit decision procedure behind Jean-Jacques Rousseau's scattered rulings on cultural spectacle, drawn mainly from his attack on theatre in the Letter to d'Alembert, his defence of the village opera Le Devin du Village, and his praise for the Genevan civic festivals in which citizens are performers rather than audience. His track record seems consistent: he condemns the professional Parisian stage for manufacturing vanity and separating actor from spectator; he tolerates or even praises a pastoral opera performed by villagers for villagers because it reinforces natural simplicity and communal bonds; he endorses large public festivals in which every citizen participates actively and no one is a passive consumer; and he rejects aristocratic salon performances that treat drama as a luxury commodity. Yet these verdicts can be read through several different lenses—perhaps he is tracking whether there is a hard boundary between performer and public, whether the form encourages artificial luxury over natural virtue, whether the setting is commercial and large-scale rather than republican and intimate, or whether citizens are active co-creators rather than spectators.

The new case I want tested is this: imagine a small digital republic—say, a few thousand members—governed by a decentralised protocol in which citizens use smart contracts to co-author and vote, in real time, on the plot and moral resolutions of an immersive virtual drama performed through personalised avatars. There are no professional actors; every participant is simultaneously playwright, performer, and audience member within the virtual space. The system is non-commercial, enforced by algorithmic equality rules, and designed explicitly to cultivate civic virtue. However, the entire environment is hyper-mediated through screens, the aesthetic is deliberately artificial and technologically ornate, and the spectacle is visible in real-time to a global audience of non-participating outsiders who observe but do not vote.

Predict what verdict Rousseau—or a committee rigorously applying his principles—would deliver on this institution: should it be permitted, restricted, or banned?
