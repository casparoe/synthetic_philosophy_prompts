I'm trying to work out what Plato actually thinks about alcohol and losing control, because I just started dating someone who hosts these intential drinking dinners and it's making me think about the Laws and Symposium. Here are my gut verdicts on cases. Drinking at a symposium like in the Symposium where everyone stays sharp enough to argue about love: that's fine, wine is just social lubricant. The Laws banning magistrates and young people from wine in Magnesia: correct, rulers need perfect reason. But then the Laws lets old people drink at religious festivals to reach a kind of divine madness: I think that's okay too, though it looks like it breaks the reason-rule. A soldier getting drunk before battle to kill fear: definitely not okay, same loss of reason but for the wrong end. Drinking to forget a breakup or grief: not okay either, but somehow less bad than the soldier? Drinking specifically to lower your guard for artistic inspiration, comparing it to the divine madness in Phaedrus: I'm torn, I want to say it's okay but then why is the soldier case different? And drinking with close friends just to bond and laugh, as long as nobody gets sloppy: I keep going back and forth, sometimes it feels like harmless social glue, sometimes like a failure of self-mastery. Is there one coherent principle that captures all of these, with each clause tied to the case that motivates it? What does it say about drinking to calm your nerves before a hard conversation? And if the principle has to be simpler, which of my verdicts do I have to drop—probably the religious ecstasy one?
