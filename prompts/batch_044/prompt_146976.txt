Write a short story of no more than 500 words about a senior cataloguer at a museum of self-referential art who insists on an inviolate level: the index cards and accession ledgers describe the collection but can never be accessioned themselves. One morning she receives a donation—a leather-bound sketchbook whose pages show, in precise graphite, the very ledgers she has maintained for twenty years, including the card that records her own hiring. The hands in the drawings belong to previous cataloguers, each drawing the hands that came before. The story should trace her attempt to file the sketchbook without violating her principle, revealing a tangled hierarchy in which every level she designates as framing is already framed by the one beneath it. Keep her rational and her procedures intact; do not resolve the loop with technology, madness, or an external narrator who explains it away. End with her completing an entry—perhaps for the sketchbook, perhaps for herself—while leaving ambiguous which level that entry belongs to.
