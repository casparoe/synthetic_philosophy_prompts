In discussions about whether large language models can genuinely perform speech acts—apologizing, promising, declaring, or authorizing—people constantly reach for an analogy with recorded sound: they say that an LLM producing “I sincerely apologize” or “I hereby open this session” is like playing a recording of a judge saying “I sentence you to ten years.” In both cases, the syntactic or acoustic form is perfect, but the authority that activates the institutional consequence is absent: the judge must be present in court with jurisdiction, just as an apologizer must be a responsible agent whose words can actually alter social relations. The analogy is used to argue that LLM outputs are necessarily hollow imitations—surface-level illocutionary acts that lack the “felicity conditions” (the right speaker, context, and uptake) that give them real force, so no genuine speech act occurs.
