I am preparing notes for a philosophy of art discussion group and wish to audit my own commitments regarding Schopenhauer's account of music before I share them. Roughly, I hold the following five positions. First, I accept Schopenhauer's distinction between music and the other arts: painting, sculpture, and poetry represent Platonic Ideas that mediate between the Will and the phenomenal world, whereas music bypasses Ideas and is an immediate objectification of the Will itself. Second, I take this ontological directness to mean that music is metaphysically prior to the representational arts, despite Schopenhauer's own tendency to treat all arts as parallel manifestations. Third, I endorse Schopenhauer's metaphysical pessimism in full: the Will is blind, insatiable striving, and the ultimate source of human suffering. Fourth, I believe that attentive listening to music can produce a form of cognitive liberation — not a mere temporary respite, but a structured awareness of the Will's nature that does not collapse into despair. Fifth, I deny that this liberating potential conflicts with the fourth point; rather, I think Schopenhauer underestimates how musical form — rhythm, harmonic progression, and melodic architecture — introduces an ordering principle that transforms the Will's raw expression into something that can be grasped without being overwhelmed by it.

Please examine these commitments for joint consistency. Which pairs pull against one another, which tensions are merely apparent, and what is the severity ranking? For each genuine conflict, specify the cheapest revision and its cost. I am particularly concerned whether my claim that music is liberating undermines my acceptance of Schopenhauer's pessimism, and whether my ranking of music's metaphysical priority is compatible with his actual hierarchy of the arts.
