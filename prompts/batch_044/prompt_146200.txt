I'm preparing a consistency audit of my own position on capital punishment for a philosophy seminar and want to check whether the commitments actually hang together. Here is the profile:

(a) I accept the core of Camus's argument against execution: that it involves the same calm deliberation and intentional killing of a defenseless party characteristic of premeditated murder, and that this structural analogy undermines the claim that execution expresses justice rather than institutionalized vengeance.

(b) I hold that retributive justice is a coherent and legitimate aim of criminal law, and that for acts representing an irreversible rupture with the conditions of shared civic life, the appropriate response is the permanent exclusion of the offender from the community of citizens.

(c) I maintain that any punishment that is not strictly necessary for immediate public safety, and that carries a non-negligible risk of being applied to an innocent person, must be rejected if it is also irreversible, because the moral cost of an irrevocable error outweighs any retrospective benefit.

(d) I believe that human dignity, in the sense central to Camus's account, requires that we never reduce a person to the single worst act they have committed; execution does precisely this by treating the offender's entire existence as cancellable by one deed.

(e) I also hold that life imprisonment without possibility of parole achieves both the protective purpose underlying (c) and the exclusionary purpose of (b), without incurring the irreversibility problem, and therefore that (a) and (d) do not in fact commit me to opposing all forms of permanent exclusion.

Please examine these for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision? In particular, assess whether my reliance on (e) to reconcile (b) with (a) and (d) is stable, or whether (e) actually violates (d) for the same reason that execution does. Rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what giving up the weaker claim would cost, and indicate whether there is a single revision that would clean up the most problems at once.
