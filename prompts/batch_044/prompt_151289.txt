I've been reflecting on the metaphysics of dispositions, particularly the distinction between a masked disposition and one eliminated by a fink—consider a fragile object encased in protective material that prevents breakage without altering its internal structure. If we apply this framework to moral psychology, imagine an agent whose neural and psychological substrate supports the virtue of honesty, yet a precisely calibrated trauma response suppresses truthful disclosure in every situation that would normally call for it. Does this agent genuinely possess honesty in a masked form, or does the interfering mechanism function as a fink that entirely nullifies the disposition? And if the latter is correct, doesn't virtue ethics collapse, since any cultivated character trait could be instantly nullified by an unforeseen masking condition?
