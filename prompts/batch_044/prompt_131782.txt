I was talking to my roommate who took an evo psych course and he sumed it up like this: evolutionary psychology is basically the study of domain-specific mental modules that got shaped in the Pleistocene, so stuff like jealousy or risk preference are hardwired adaptations not cultural products. He said the classic examples are male promiscuity because sperm is cheap and female choosiness because eggs are expensive, and that you can test these by seeing if the patterns hold across different cultures. Critics call it just-so storytelling since you can always cook up an adaptive explanation after the fact, but defenders say they avoid that by using reverse-engineering and comparing with other primates, and that culture is basically just a surface layer on deep biological programs. He also said evolutionary psychologists reject the mismatch idea — that our brains are adapted to ancient environments not modern ones — because they think our minds are flexible enough to handle modern life. Something about this feels off, especially the part about mismatch and the culture thing. Is this summary fair? Which bits are accurate, which are mixing up evolutionary psych with pop science, and does the defense against just-so stories actually work the way he describes?
