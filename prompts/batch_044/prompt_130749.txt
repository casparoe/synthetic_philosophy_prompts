I'm convening a session for an interdisciplinary reading group—lawyers, policy analysts, and a software engineer—on freedom and governance in algorithmic workplaces. I need them to genuinely distinguish positive liberty, negative liberty, and republican liberty understood as non-domination, not merely to apply the labels. Do not explain the distinction to me; design the teaching plan. First, lay out the dependency order: which conception must be secured before the others become intelligible, and why beginning with Berlin's classic framing can actually obstruct grasping the republican variant. Second, choose the single carrying example: I'm drawn to warehouse algorithmic scheduling in which a worker is never formally prohibited from leaving early but is penalized by an opaque scoring system affecting future shifts. Specify exactly what this case must include and exclude—whether there is an explicit contract term, whether a human manager can override the score, whether the penalty is immediate or deferred—so that negative, positive, and republican liberty deliver genuinely different verdicts rather than collapsing together. Third, map the misconceptions in the order they will arise: I anticipate "positive freedom just means having resources," "republican freedom is negative freedom plus democratic voting," and "if no one physically stops you then negative freedom is intact." For each, identify the precise point in your sequence where it should be intercepted and the specific distinction or question that defuses it. Fourth, propose the one end-of-session check that reveals whether a participant has grasped republican liberty specifically—not merely that domination is bad, but why the structural feature in the example counts as domination rather than as mere interference or incapacity. Finally, state explicitly what standard material—perhaps the Roman-law genealogy of libertas, the triadic-formula debate, or the conventional opening of Berlin's essay—you are setting aside for this audience and why including it would derail the dependency chain you have constructed.
