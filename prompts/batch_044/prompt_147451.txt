Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Aurelia and Basil, debating whether van Fraassen's Reflection Principle applies to an agent designed with periodic 'epistemic resets'—complete, scheduled erasures of non-inferential memory that leave only logical structure intact. Aurelia argues that if the agent can now predict its post-reset credence in a proposition, then rationality requires adopting that credence now, regardless of present evidence. Basil replies that reflection presupposes diachronic continuity of evidential access, which the reset destroys, and that treating a future self's beliefs as authoritative commits the present self to an indexical error—confusing 'I will believe p' with 'p is supported by my current reasons'. Ensure both characters are equally competent: neither should be a strawman, and each should respond directly to the specific claim the other just made. Structure the dialogue as exactly five exchanges, each comprising one or two sentences from each speaker, arranged without introduction or conclusion, and write it in British English.
