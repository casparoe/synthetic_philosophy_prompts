I am writing an essay for my value theory seminar on incommensurable values, and I would like your critical comments on the conceptual framework before I develop the full draft. The central move is to extend Ruth Chang’s analysis of personal hard choices into institutional decision-making, but I am introducing a distinction that I have not seen explicitly articulated: between threshold incommensurability, where values share an underlying metric but lack a precise exchange rate, and radical incommensurability, where applying any unified metric appears conceptually inappropriate rather than merely imprecise. I apply this to cases like a municipal government deciding whether to preserve a historic neighborhood or construct affordable housing on the same site. I argue that institutional practices such as cost-benefit analysis and weighted scoring implicitly presuppose that all competing values are at least threshold-commensurable, and that when decision-makers face what I call radical incommensurability, they do not genuinely resolve the conflict but instead conceal a hidden metric—usually economic efficiency—behind procedural neutrality. I am particularly concerned with three points. First, does the distinction between threshold and radical incommensurability actually do philosophical work, or does it collapse once we examine how institutional agents reason? Second, is it fair to claim that cost-benefit analysis presupposes commensurability, given that it often operates precisely by assigning shadow prices to goods that resist direct market valuation? Third, have I illegitimately moved from Chang’s first-person phenomenology of choice to third-person institutional deliberation without accounting for the difference between an individual’s practical identity and a collective’s authority to decide? Please comment on the coherence of the architecture, the plausibility of applying personal hard-choice theory to public policy, and whether there is an obvious objection from the literature on practical reason or democratic legitimacy that I am overlooking. I am not looking for a bibliography or literature summary, but for an assessment of whether the argument holds together conceptually.
