I'm writing a feature for the science desk about aquaculture welfare — specifically the gap between recognising that shrimp and crabs can suffer and actually knowing what that suffering looks like, since decapods don't have one brain but a distributed chain of ganglia. It's definately more philosophically messy than the fish-stunning debate, and I want to close the piece with something that captures the loop.

Can you write me a villanelle — yes, I know it's an old form, but the refrains circling back feel right for an argument that never quite lands. The two refrains should be: 'The ganglia fire, but no self receives it' and 'We weigh the harvest in tonnes, not in ones.' The first should carry the ontological problem — distributed nociception, no central subject, the headache of attributing suffering to an animal that may lack a unified experience — while the second should carry the scale problem: aquaculture produces billions, and welfare science is built for individuals. Use the six-line stanzas and the repeating A1/A2 pattern so that the form itself enacts the deadlock: each stanza tries to resolve whether there's a sufferer there, then gets pulled back to the industrial scale that makes individual welfare invisible. The turn should come in the sestet, where the naive view (we've recognised sentience, now we just need better stunning) meets the neurobiological ambiguity. The closing couplet should put the two refrains side by side without resolving them, since that's where the research actually sits.

Please keep it readable for someone who hasn't read the decapod neurobiology literature, but accurate enough that a welfare scientist wouldn't wince. Don't include citations or a reading list — this is for a magazine spread, not an academic paper. And since the 'no unified self' reading of crustacean ganglia is genuinely contested — some researchers think integration still happens across the chain — please include a brief note after the poem saying how confident you are in what's claimed, flagging the point you're least sure about, and noting where the poem takes liberties with a contested interpretation.
