I retired from biochemistry about four years ago and have been sitting in on philosophy courses ever since, mostly because I miss the feeling of not knowing what I'm talking about. Lately we've been on objectivity in history, and I think I've built a contradiction that I can't seperate. Ranke is always described as demanding that history show how things actually were — I took that literally, as if he wanted historians to act like court reporters, stripping away interpretation and just arranging primary documents in chronological order so the past could speak without mediation. Then I read Carr, who insists that facts are dead until a historian selects and animates them, and then Hayden White, who claims every historical account is structured by one of four master tropes — romance, tragedy, comedy, satire — that live in the historian's language, not in the archive.

So here's where I get stuck: if Ranke's scientific method requires zero interpretation, but White shows that narrative form is unavoidable and not derived from the sources, then Ranke's own histories must be either failed experiments in objectivity or just unconscious literature. So doesn't that mean Ranke was actually writing fiction without realizing it, and that the whole notion of scientific history is self-defeating? Or is the error somewhere in how I'm connecting White's tropes to Ranke's archival practice?
