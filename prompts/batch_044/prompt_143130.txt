I am a retired geologist who has recently begun working through philosophy of history and science. Before presenting at a reading group, I want a consistency check on the following profile. I hold that (a) volcanic aerosol injections, pathogen dynamics, and climate shifts are not mere background conditions but legitimate explanatory nodes in historical narratives; (b) calling them “historical actors” is defensible in a causal-role sense that does not require intentional agency, so treating the 536 CE dust veil or cholera outbreaks as actors involves no category error; (c) environmental determinism is false because institutional structures—property rights, grain-storage regimes, urban sanitation—always mediate between biological or climatic forcing and social outcomes; (d) nevertheless, once that mediation layer is fully specified, the result is deterministically fixed: identical aerosol loading combined with identical feudal institutions necessarily produces identical famine dynamics; and (e) the true peril of environmental determinism lies not in recognizing non-human causation but in treating institutional mediation as transparent rather than structurally transformative. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each indicate whether resolving it requires surrendering a metaphysical claim, an empirical claim, or merely a terminological choice.
