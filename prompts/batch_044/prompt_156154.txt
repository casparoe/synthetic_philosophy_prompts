Rank the following five proposed responses to Fitch’s paradox of knowability in order of philosophical plausibility, assigning 1 to the most plausible and 5 to the least plausible. Give a brief justification of one or two sentences for each.

The responses are:
A. Rejecting the knowability principle by distinguishing logical possibility from epistemic accessibility.
B. Accepting that some truths are genuinely unknowable but denying that this undermines the purpose of inquiry.
C. Blocking the proof by restricting distributivity over conjunctions within epistemic logic.
D. Adopting the ‘modal humility principle’: if a proposition entails its own unknown status, rational agents ought to withhold belief in it even when true, thereby dissolving the tension at the level of doxastic norm rather than formal logic.
E. Treating the result as a limit on ideal omniscience with no practical consequence for human or artificial reasoning norms.

Then, in roughly 150 words, identify the specific argument or counterexample that would cause you to reverse your first- and last-ranked choices.

Please write in British English and organise your answer in the following sections, in this order:

1. Rankings and brief justifications (around 250 words)
2. Defence of the top-ranked response (around 150 words)
3. Critique of the bottom-ranked response (around 100 words)
4. What would change my mind (around 150 words)
