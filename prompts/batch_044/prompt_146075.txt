I serve on the methodology board of an interdisciplinary consciousness-research unit, and we have seen a marked increase in grant applications and pre-registration protocols that claim to isolate, measure, or experimentally manipulate specific qualia—for instance, what applicants call the pure subjective redness of a presented patch, or the intrinsic what-it-is-likeness of a pain state. I would like a reusable decision procedure that my board can run on any such proposal, grounded in the argument developed by Chase and Sanborn that qualia are an incoherent posit: specifically, the claim that any attempt to fix upon a qualitative property through introspection inevitably collapses that property into some functional, relational, or cognitive-access feature, leaving no genuinely phenomenal residue. The deliverable should be an operational checklist—ordered stages, each with a precise question, a criterion for a sufficient answer, and a clear stop-point where the proposal fails to demonstrate a genuinely qualitative object of study—that a reviewer without specialist philosophical training could apply in roughly twenty minutes.

The procedure must include at least one stage where the distinction between a functional description and a phenomenal one genuinely cannot be resolved by further empirical data; that is, a point where the checklist must hand the decision back to a value judgement about whether to fund research that may be conceptually confused yet empirically fruitful. You should also specify explicitly what the procedure is not able to do—for example, whether it can adjudicate between eliminativism and a deflationary functionalist account, or whether it tacitly presupposes a particular theory of introspection. Please be honest about misfires: identify one kind of proposal that would wrongly fail, and one that would wrongly pass, given the limits of the procedure.

Apply the checklist to three cases. First, an fMRI study that claims to have identified a pure phenomenal-red neural signature that remains constant across variations in attentional load and cognitive task. Second, a behavioural study of chromatic synaesthesia that treats subjects’ reports of coloured sounds as direct evidence of non-functional qualitative properties; here the procedure should yield an answer the researchers are likely to reject. Third, apply it to a speculative protocol I am injecting—call it the phenomenal-residue audit—in which subjects are trained to report only those aspects of experience that survive a counterfactual inversion of all associated functional and cognitive-access properties, with the explicit aim of isolating a non-functional core. For each case, run the stages, state where the procedure stops with a negative verdict, where it hands over to judgement, and what the board should conclude about funding or approval. Write in formal British English.
