A colleague recently summarized a view she attributes jointly to Bernard Williams and Kwame Anthony Appiah about how harmful social norms disappear. She put it like this: Williams argued that moral beliefs are never genuinely responsive to reasons; they are just surface expressions of underlying collective honor codes, and an individual has no internal reason to change a norm unless his status group first feels shame. Appiah then showed that this explains dueling, foot-binding, and Atlantic slavery: in each case, abolition happened not because of moral arguments or shifting economic interests, but solely because elites experienced a sudden honor crisis that made the practice embarrassing. From this, she concludes that trying to end a modern harmful practice through rational debate is essentially futile; you must engineer status-based shame instead.
