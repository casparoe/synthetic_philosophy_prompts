I've been trying to formalise a tension that keeps surfacing in my thinking about meaning holism, and I keep returning to Quine's denial of the analytic-synthetic distinction. The argument below seems to show that if Quine is right, then the objective boundaries of formal systems dissolve into pure pragmatism, which strikes me as intolerable. Here are the steps:

(1) Quine holds that no statement is immune to revision; there is no sharp analytic-synthetic divide, only differences in how central a belief is to our web.
(2) If there is no principled boundary, then logical and mathematical truths are revisable in the same pragmatic way as empirical claims about physics.
(3) Gödel's second incompleteness theorem is a logical-mathematical result: a consistent formal system of sufficient strength cannot prove its own consistency.
(4) Therefore, on Quine's view, we could in principle revise our acceptance of Gödel's theorem if empirical anomalies accumulated and retaining it became too costly elsewhere in the web.
(5) But if Gödel's theorem is empirically disposable, then whether a given formal system is genuinely incomplete, or merely appears incomplete because we are working in a logic that suppresses self-certification, becomes a purely pragmatic choice rather than an objective feature of the system.
(6) Consequently, the distinction between intrinsic formal limitation and conventional logical framing collapses.
(7) This undermines computability theory and the foundations of proof, since "provability within S" loses any system-independent meaning.
(8) That result is absurd: formal limits are not conventions we can trade away for empirical convenience.
(9) So premise (1) must be rejected, or at least qualified by a hidden analytic boundary.

I feel the slide happens somewhere between (3) and (5), perhaps an equivocation between revising the logic in which the theorem is expressed and revising the theorem itself, but I cannot pinpoint it precisely. I would prefer to keep (1) and (8) if possible, since Quine's holistic picture is genuinely attractive and the objectivity of formal limits seems non-negotiable. Where exactly does this argument go wrong, which premise should I surrender, and what does surrendering it commit me to? Please give me a committed answer in British English: take a definite line on the faulty step, explain why it looked plausible, and price the alternatives rather than merely listing them.
