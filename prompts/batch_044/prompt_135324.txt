Please construct an argument that Hofstadter's knob-twiddling account of creativity — according to which novel concepts arise from continuous adjustments to parameters within an existing conceptual space — is inadequate for explaining radical conceptual change, and that such change is better characterized as a discontinuous restructuring of the space itself in which the prior dimensions of variation lose their semantic grip. Address specifically the counter-argument that any apparently abrupt shift can be embedded as a smooth trajectory in a sufficiently high-dimensional representation space, and show why this embedding fails to preserve the normative and phenomenological features of the transition: namely, that the agent cannot simultaneously operate both frameworks with shared evaluative criteria, and that the old "knobs" become literally uninterpretable rather than merely turned to extreme values. Then draw out the implication for contemporary analogies between human creative reasoning and gradient-based search in fixed latent architectures: if conceptual breakthroughs require incommensurable frame shifts rather than parameter tuning, does the knob-twiddling metaphor survive as anything more than a description of incremental refinement within an already-fixed paradigm?
