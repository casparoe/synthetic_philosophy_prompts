I'm preparing interview questions for a podcast episode with an unnamed philosopher of science who defends a position she calls modal-network realism. According to this view, scientific theories do not represent objects that stand in relations; instead, they map irreducibly modal networks of dependency—what must obtain if other conditions hold—where so-called individuals are only local consistency conditions on those networks, not nodes within them. She rejects both epistemic structural realism and the standard ontic version associated with Ladyman and Ross, and she denies that structures require a set-theoretic or categorical substrate. I want eight to ten interview questions that avoid having her simply restate the view. Please include: a question pressing the Benacerraf-style identification problem for modal networks—if there is no substrate, what distinguishes one network from an isomorphic copy?—together with a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that consistency conditions fix identity; a question asking whether her account can preserve a substantive ontology of spacetime or whether spacetime collapses into pure modal coherence; a question on empirical underdetermination, specifically whether observationally equivalent rival theories with different dependency structures leave her realism without the resources to break the tie; and a concluding question about what empirical or formal result could genuinely disconfirm modal-network realism, designed to make it harder for her to deflect with the claim that the challenge is merely a research program rather than an objection. The tone should be conversational yet philosophically precise, appropriate for an audience of philosophers of science.
