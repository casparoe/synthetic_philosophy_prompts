Hey can you make an argument for this position I find interesting but probably wrong? Even if you buy Putnam's Twin Earth argument for natural kind terms, applying semantic externalism straight to LLMs misses the point—not because they lack bodies (thats the usual objection people make), but because the social division of linguistic labor depends on being inside a community that causally interacts with the environment. An LLM trains on texts that encode that division, but its not part of the community, so it doesnt get full externalist meaning. At the same time its not pure internal syntax either, since its outputs are calibrated to that external community's usage patterns. So theres this weird middle status—deferral without membership—where the model inherits semantic structure without belonging to the social mechanism that grounds it. Argue that this is a genuinely distinct category, not just internalism or failed externalism.

Please separate whats settled from whats genuinely contested: lay out the standard Twin Earth and social-division points as the background, then flag the AI-extension parts as speculative. And dont present the debate about LLM intentionality or meaning as if theres a consensus among philosophers of mind and language.
