Write a proposal for a one-semester philosophy term paper of approximately 8,000 words on Aristotle’s account of exchange and money in Politics I.8–10 and Nicomachean Ethics V.5. The received reading treats Aristotle’s distinction between oikonomikē (household management) and chrematistikē (wealth-getting for its own sake) as anchored in a quantitative limit: natural acquisition aims at a sufficient stock of goods for the good life, whereas chrematistikē pursues an unlimited accumulation of coin. On this view, Aristotle’s hostility to tokos—interest-bearing lending—rests on a biological analogy: coin is barren metal, so demanding that it breed more coin is as unnatural as expecting a seedless stone to grow.

Your proposal should frame a different, precisely stated question: is the boundary between natural and unnatural exchange actually structural rather than quantitative—defined not by how much one holds, but by whether the exchange is mediated in both directions by chreia (need or use)—and, if so, does the defect in tokos lie not in the barrenness of metal but in a temporal decoupling, whereby the lender’s return is indexed to the passage of time rather than to the borrower’s present need? State the question so that it admits of a determinate textual answer.

Explain why the standard interpretation leaves this open: it reads Aristotle’s remarks about money arising by convention (nomisma) as merely explaining the origin of currency, without asking whether conventional status undermines the idea of a fixed natural limit on wealth; and it treats the reciprocity discussion in Ethics V.5 as a separate problem of distributive justice, rather than as a model for all exchange. Your approach should compare the Politics discussion of need-based exchange with the Ethics account of proportional reciprocity, injecting the conceptual distinction between quantitative caps and relational conditions, and it should deliberately avoid importing modern economic categories—such as liquidity, capital, or time-preference—that Aristotle does not possess.

Specify the two main possible conclusions—either that the structural reading collapses into the quantitative one because chreia itself sets a natural ceiling on holdings, or that the structural reading succeeds and requires reinterpreting the anti-usury argument as a critique of one-directional need-satisfaction—and identify which passages (for instance, the comparison of retail trade with household management, or the analysis of reciprocal exchange in justice) would decide between them.

Give an honest assessment of scope: the full comparison of Politics and Ethics, plus a defense of the structural reading against modern objections, risks exceeding one semester. Propose a deliberate narrowing—perhaps restricting the analysis to the Politics texts and treating the Ethics material only as a control—so that the project remains feasible. Note that you will supply all citations and references yourself; do not include a bibliography or literature review by citation in the proposal. Conclude with a brief statement of what failure would look like—namely, that the textual evidence for a structural condition is indistinguishable from the quantitative reading, leaving the reinterpretation unsupported.
