I am a retired physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, have taken up philosophy with the seriousness I once reserved for experimental design, and I am currently assisting with the final assessment for an advanced undergraduate seminar on Schopenhauer's metaphysics of art. Having spent decades evaluating analogical reasoning in scientific models, I find the usual exam questions unsatisfactory: they invite students merely to recite that music expresses the Will directly, to rank the arts in Schopenhauer's hierarchy, or to summarize his distinction between representation and immediate expression. I want instead an essay question that forces students to work through a genuine conceptual tension in Schopenhauer's account—one that cannot be resolved by labeling.

Specifically, Schopenhauer maintains that music is unique among the arts because it does not represent Ideas, those intermediate objectifications of the Will, but rather expresses the Will itself immediately, apparently bypassing the principle of sufficient reason altogether. At the same time, he constructs elaborate analogies between musical structures and specific grades of the Will's objectification: the bass corresponds to inorganic nature and gravitational attraction, harmony to the plant and animal kingdoms, and melody—particularly its striving toward resolution—to the conscious desire and satisfaction of human life. I want the exam to ask whether these analogies are compatible with music's alleged immediacy, or whether they implicitly reintroduce the very representational and causal structure that Schopenhauer claims music transcends. The question should require students to address the particular case of dissonance and its resolution, and to explain whether comparing melodic striving to the satisfaction of human will-fulfillment undermines Schopenhauer's broader epistemological claim that music offers direct, non-representational knowledge of the inner nature of the world.

Please provide the exam question itself, a model answer of approximately 650 to 750 words, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points totaling 25 marks. For each point, specify the conceptual move required for full marks, the characteristic mistake that should cost marks, and the precise distinction between an A-level and B-level execution. The scheme should also identify which interpretive issues—such as whether Schopenhauer's musical analogies are meant as literal metaphysical mappings or merely as pedagogical heuristics—are well-established in the scholarship and which remain genuinely contested, and should instruct that no contested interpretation be presented as the consensus view.
