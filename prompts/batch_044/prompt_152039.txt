I am a junior serving on the Student Governance and Expression Review Board at my high school, and I have been tasked with drafting our recommendation for Thursday's session on Case 2024-17. The case involves the school's debate society, which invited an external speaker whose remarks on local zoning policy were recorded and circulated; in response, a coalition of registered student organizations has petitioned our board to impose what they term an "institutional delisting"—a formal twelve-month prohibition on the event organizer holding any elected office in student groups, plus a mandatory public acknowledgment from the debate society that it breached its community-responsibility charter. Our board's authority is limited to registered organizations, and our charter distinguishes between "organic disassociation," in which individuals or groups freely withdraw cooperation, and "institutional delisting," in which this body removes access to shared resources and titles. The petitioners argue that the sanction is not punishment but protective association management; opponents argue that any formal deprivation of office by a governing body is punitive by nature and therefore requires procedural safeguards—specifically, notice, a hearing, and proportionality review—that were not provided.

Please draft the board's written opinion recommending a concrete course of action—approve the sanction, reject it, or approve with conditions and a clear time frame—together with reasoning that treats both positions seriously. In particular, the memo should address whether formalizing a social sanction through an institutional vote transforms it into punishment, and whether our charter's distinction between organic disassociation and institutional delisting holds up under scrutiny. I want the document to be actionable for board members who do not share my recent reading in moral philosophy, and it should honestly present the strongest consideration for whichever side loses, rather than dismissing it. I was discussing the boundary between private moral judgment and collective institutional action with classmates after school, and I want to make sure the recommendation does not conflate informal social disapproval with the specific kind of authority this board exercises.
