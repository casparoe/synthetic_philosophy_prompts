Argue that Adam Smith's opposition to British imperial administration—especially regarding the American colonies and the East India Company—should be reconstructed around the idea of 'sympathetic jurisdiction': the claim that legitimate rule depends on proximity close enough for the impartial spectator to operate between governors and governed, and that empire fails precisely because geographic and social distance attenuate sympathy and corrupt moral judgment on both sides. Rather than treating his anti-colonial remarks as mere applications of free-trade or anti-monopoly reasoning, show how this moral-psychological framework explains specific administrative failures he identifies. Be explicit about where your reasoning is weakest and what kind of textual, historical, or conceptual counter-evidence would lead you to reject this interpretation in favor of the standard economic reading.
