Write a Petrarchan sonnet (octave ABBA ABBA, sestet CDECDE, volta at line 9) on the question of what Descartes’s cogito actually establishes. In the octave, present the “thin” interpretation: that the argument secures only the occurrence of thinking, not the existence of a substantial “I” that thinks—use enclosed, self-reflecting rhyme sounds (for example, mind/find, doubt/about) to mirror the closed circle of immediate awareness in which no observer can stand in for the thinker. At the volta in line 9, stage the inferential leap to the “thick” claim: that there is a res cogitans, a persisting thinking substance. In the sestet, let the rhyme scheme and pronoun structure expose the gap—have the “I” of the conclusion fail to align with the bare indexical of the premise, perhaps through a near-rhyme or broken echo that refuses to close (for instance, being/seeing that almost match but leave the substance unanchored). The closing couplet should leave open whether the proof yields a self or only a momentary, non-transferable flicker of certainty that cannot be delegated to anyone else. The poem should be philosophically precise enough that someone familiar with the distinction between the occurrence of cogitatio and the substantiality of the res cogitans recognizes the argument being made, and structurally integrated enough that the rhyme, turn, and pronoun drift do the philosophical work rather than merely decorating it. Do not cite texts, scholars, or editions; render the idea entirely in the verse.
