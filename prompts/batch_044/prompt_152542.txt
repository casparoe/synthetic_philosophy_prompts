I'm the director of a clinical psychology residency and every quarter I have to write these character evaluations for the accrediting body — they want ratings on compassion, integrity, practical wisdom, that kind of thing. The problem is I'm actually convinced by both virtue ethics and the situationist critique, which puts me in a weird spot. If character is just stable dispositions then I should rate residents on whether they consistently act well, but if the situationists are right then behavior mostly tracks context not traits, so my ratings are basically astrology unless I control for pressure and setting. I need a reusable procedure I can run in about thirty minutes per resident that doesn't collapse into a gut-feeling form.

Can you turn virtue ethics — specifically the idea that virtues are dispositions to feel, reason, and act correctly across a range of situations, not just habits — into an operational checklist for me? It needs to specify what counts as evidence for a virtue versus just lucky behavior, how to weight situational pressure (high-stress crisis vs routine rotation), and where the procedure has to stop and admit it's a values judgment rather than a measurement so I don't recieve false precision. Include three worked cases: a resident who was rude to a patient during a double shift but usually kind; one who was exemplary in easy rotations but collapsed in crisis; and one ambiguous case where the procedure gives an answer I probably won't like. Also flag the points where I'm just dressing up judgment calls as steps, and say what the procedure absolutely cannot do — like predicting future behavior or replacing a real conversation. Don't give me bibliographies or citations; I already know the debate and can't search them. I just need something I can apply repeatedly without pretending psychology doesn't exist.
