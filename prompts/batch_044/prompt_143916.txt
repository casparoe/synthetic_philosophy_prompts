Having spent four decades calibrating instruments whose reliability depends on linear superposition, I have lately turned to decision theory and find myself arrested by Maurice Allais's demonstration that human preference systematically violates the independence axiom. I want a Petrarchan sonnet — octave with an ABBA ABBA rhyme scheme, sestet with CDECDE or CDCDCD, and a clear volta at line nine — that treats the paradox as a failed experimental control. In the octave, present the two classic choice pairs (the certain option versus the gamble, each with an identical common consequence appended) so that the rhyme scheme itself enacts the formal symmetry the axiom demands: shared outcomes should cancel like background terms in an equation. At the volta, shift from this formal elegance to the descriptive rupture: the appended consequence does not cancel psychologically, and the preference reverses. In the sestet, pursue the analogy to physical law: does rationality, like a conservation principle, require linearity in utility, or is the observer's framing an unavoidable coupling that makes the axiom a normative fiction rather than a descriptive law? End with the tension unresolved — between the axiomatic ideal and the stubborn non-linearity of actual choice. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of economics recognizes the structure, but it must not include citations, bibliographies, or requests for further reading. Write it in a voice that still thinks in terms of measurement error and control variables.
