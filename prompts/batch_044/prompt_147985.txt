Consider the following line of reasoning, which I have been trying to formalize but now suspect proves far more than its premises warrant.

(1) If determinism is true, then the complete state of the universe at any time t is fixed, with exact uniqueness, by its state at any prior time.

(2) In deterministic dynamical systems with positive Lyapunov exponents—systems exhibiting chaos—the separation between initially adjacent trajectories grows exponentially.

(3) Therefore, predicting any specific future state of a chaotic deterministic system requires specifying initial conditions with a precision that exceeds every physically realizable measurement bound.

(4) If a future state is such that no physically possible procedure could determine in advance whether it will occur, then prior to its occurrence there is no accessible fact of the matter about whether it will occur.

(5) But what is metaphysically fixed yet permanently epistemically inaccessible is, for all rational and scientific purposes, indistinguishable from what is not metaphysically fixed at all.

(6) Hence, in any deterministic world that contains widespread chaos, determinism is operationally equivalent to indeterminism.

(7) So either determinism is false wherever chaos is present, or the determinism/indeterminism distinction collapses into a pseudo-debate once dynamical instability is taken seriously.

I find (1) and (6) attractive and am reluctant to surrender either, yet I feel a slide has occurred—probably between (4) and (5)—that moves illicitly from epistemic limitation to ontological openness. Where exactly does the argument fail? Identify the weakest step, explain why it initially seemed defensible, and price the alternatives: what does it cost to reject (3), (4), or (5) respectively? In particular, do results about shadowing or structural stability show that approximate, rather than exact, predictability is sufficient to preserve determinism's empirical content, and if so, does that rescue (5) or undermine (6)? I want an honest assessment of whether the argument can be repaired without rendering determinism unfalsifiable, or whether its conclusion is genuinely unavoidable.
