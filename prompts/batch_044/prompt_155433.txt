Please describe the most important implications of the inside-view versus outside-view distinction for epistemic modesty in contexts where a reasoner must evaluate its own cognitive processes without access to an independent external audit—especially the reflexive calibration problem that arises when applying an outside-view base rate of reasoning error to one's present judgment requires deploying the very faculties whose reliability is being assessed. Focus on how this tension should shape one's attitude toward persistent disagreement with epistemic peers who share comparable credentials yet reach opposing conclusions on conceptual matters, and discuss whether genuine modesty demands a symmetrical downgrading of all first-order convictions or permits an asymmetrical retention of conviction grounded in first-person access to the transparency of one's own reasoning.
