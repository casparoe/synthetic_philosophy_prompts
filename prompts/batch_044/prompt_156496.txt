1. The Arendtian view that work (as opposed to labour) gets its meaning from leaving durable traces in a shared world, so if fabrication is fully automated there's literally no work left, just maintenance.
2. The Marxist line that automatation frees us for higher activity but only if we socialise the means; otherwise it just deepens alienation by separating us from the productive process.
3. The "ghost labour" claim—that meaning doesnt vanish but migrates to the overseer, even when human oversight is minimal and the algorithm does the heavy lifting invisibly.
4. The subjective-fulfilment view that work is meaningful iff the worker experiences it as valuable, which means automating away unpleasant tasks automatically raises the average meaningfulness of whats left.
5. The recognition view that meaningful work needs social acknowledgement of one's contribution, so automatation threatens meaning mainly when it hides whether a human or system did the task.
