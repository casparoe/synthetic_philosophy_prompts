Construct a thought experiment set in a realistic, small-scale cooperative—specifically, a group of salvage divers working in a decommissioned port—featuring two manual practices transmitted among them. First, design Trait A: a precisely copied but mechanically useless knot-tying ritual performed in reverse orientation before each dive, which is costly in time and offers no safety benefit, yet is imitated with near-perfect fidelity across generations of divers. Second, design Trait B: a modified knot that is faster to tie and slightly reduces rope wear, but breaks the traditional pattern; it is initially adopted only when a high-status elder uses it, then spreads via imitation of that elder despite being altered slightly by each learner. Present both traits in a single paragraph, with no appeal to writing, digital media, or formal institutions.

Then demonstrate that Dawkins-style replicator memetics predicts Trait A should spread—because it possesses high copying fidelity, longevity, and fecundity—while predicting that Trait B should fail or mutate out of recognizability, since it lacks replicator fidelity. Conversely, show that a guided-variation model of cultural evolution predicts the opposite: Trait A should be eliminated or never adopted because it carries negative adaptive value and is not supported by prestige, direct, or content biases, whereas Trait B should spread through prestige bias and direct adaptive benefit despite its low copying fidelity.

After walking through both theories’ reasoning for each trait, identify the single structural feature that drives the divergence—whether cultural transmission is governed primarily by replicator-level selection or by learner-level adaptive and social biases. Explain what happens if that feature is neutralized (for instance, if copying fidelity is perfect but learners possess no adaptive or social biases): do the two theories collapse back into the same prediction? Finally, show how a defender of memetics would attack the guided-variation verdict on Trait B, and how a defender of the guided-variation model would attack the memetics verdict on Trait A—without invoking exotic psychology, technology, or institutional structures.

Provide a committed analysis: take a definite position on whether the divergence you have engineered genuinely undermines one framework as an account of human culture, or whether it instead reveals a legitimate division of explanatory labor; defend that position with explicit reasons rather than leaving the comparison open.
