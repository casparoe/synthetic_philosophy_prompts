I am a third-year undergraduate in a philosophy of art seminar, and our recent discussion of autonomism versus moralism in aesthetic value left me wondering whether any of that theoretical separation survives contact with an actual institutional decision. To explore this, I have been asked to advise my university's public-art acquisition committee—which meets monthly, has no staff art historian, must sign off within about twenty minutes per proposal, and issues a one-page public justification for every acquisition—on formalizing a reusable decision procedure for proposals involving artists with documented serious moral failings. The works are destined for high-traffic campus spaces, and the university's 2019 mission revision requires that all public displays remain "compatible with ethical exemplarity," though that phrase is undefined. I want the principle translated into an ordered checklist or flowchart-in-words that a non-philosopher can actually run: the questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where the process stops with a clear refusal, and where it must hand the decision back to human judgment because the issue is irreducibly about values rather than facts. The procedure should also state explicitly what it cannot resolve—such as whether institutional endorsement is unavoidable or can be neutralized by framing—and identify the hard cases where it will misfire. Please apply it to three proposals: (1) an abstract painter recently convicted of financial fraud that exploited elderly collectors, with the work proposed for the main library atrium; (2) a sculptor with a documented history of domestic violence whose intimate figurative work is proposed for a quiet garden memorial; and (3) a bequest that requires displaying three apolitical landscape paintings together in a named wing, where the artist was a mid-twentieth-century collaborator with an authoritarian regime. In at least one case, the procedure should yield an answer I find unwelcome, and you should explain why. Throughout, please state how confident you are in each major claim and single out the point about which you are least sure. Please keep the entire response within 500 words, present the procedure as a numbered flow, and present the case applications in brief paragraphs.
