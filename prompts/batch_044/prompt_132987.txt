Construct a diagnostic taxonomy for a municipal ethics committee tasked with ruling on petitions to rename streets or remove statues honoring historical figures whose conduct or views are now widely condemned. The instrument should classify positions not by ideological label but by answers to concrete case-level questions, with explicit attention to the functional difference between a sculptural honorific object in a public square and a toponymic reference embedded in navigational infrastructure, postal addresses, and mapping systems.

Structure the tree with four successive splits. The root divides cases according to whether the marker operates primarily as an aesthetic honorific (a statue) or as practical reference infrastructure with implicit endorsement (a street name). The second split applies within each branch: whether the wrong associated with the figure is central to the commemorated achievement—meaning the legacy being celebrated is inseparable from the harm—or peripheral, meaning the harm is biographically documented but unrelated to the cited contribution. The third split asks whether retaining the marker in its original form, without physical alteration, produces a distinctive dignitary harm that contextual measures—such as added plaques, adjacent counter-commemorative installations, or mandatory educational framing—cannot adequately remedy. The fourth split distinguishes positions that weigh present-day symbolic exclusion of affected citizens more heavily from those that weigh future historical and navigational continuity more heavily.

The tree must yield between six and eight leaves. For each leaf, provide: (i) a descriptive compound label indicating marker type, centrality of wrong, remedy stance, and weight given to present versus future obligations (e.g., "Toponymic retention with contextualization for peripheral wrongs, weighing continuity"); (ii) a single sentence stating the position's most awkward or counter-intuitive commitment; (iii) a designation of whether the position is actively defended in current public debate, currently unnamed but coherent, or apparently incoherent with sibling branches. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely distinguish, especially where the sculptural/toponymic distinction collapses—such as if both are treated as pure reference lacking honorific content, or both as pure honor lacking navigational function.

In addition, identify one deliberately unoccupied combination of answers: specifically, a stance that demands immediate removal of street names for peripheral wrongs while insisting on unconditional retention of statues for central wrongs. Provide a brief diagnosis of why this combination is empty or unstable, referencing the asymmetry between navigational utility and explicit honorific intent.

Every node must be phrased as a concrete yes/no or either/or question that a committee member could answer about a specific petition without prior knowledge of philosophical taxonomy or the leaf it produces. Do not include questions that ask respondents to self-identify with named movements, and do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. The deliverable should be the tree itself, with branch logic clearly indicated, rather than a prose essay comparing positions.
