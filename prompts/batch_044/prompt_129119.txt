I'm preparing a philosophy seminar presentation on karma as a theory of moral desert, and before I present I want a coherence check on whether my commitments hang together. Here is my position:

(1) Karma should be understood as a structural feature of moral causation rather than divine punishment: actions generate desert-claims that tend, over time, to be satisfied by outcomes.
(2) Desert is strictly non-comparative—whether I deserve an outcome depends only on my own actions and their moral quality, not on how anyone else is treated.
(3) The best model is a "moral ledger": each action adds or subtracts a quantifiable balance that determines the valence of future welfare.
(4) At the same time, I think undeserved suffering creates a "moral debt in the universe"—a corrective claim that must eventually be repaid to the sufferer, even if they never performed any action to earn repayment.
(5) I reject reincarnation and strong personal continuity across lives; karma can operate across generations or social structures without requiring that the same conscious subject receives the payoff.
(6) Desert is backward-looking only: outcomes can be deserved solely in virtue of past actions, never future ones.
(7) I also allow "non-gradual cancellation": a single transformative moral act can wipe out an accumulated negative balance entirely, rather than reducing it proportionally.
