I serve on the curatorial advisory board of a national museum that is redesigning its permanent gallery on the later Roman Empire, currently provisionally titled 'The Fall of Rome?' I am completing doctoral research in late antique and early medieval history, and the board has asked me to draft a short written opinion for circulation before our vote in four weeks' time. The dispute is not merely presentational but curatorial-philosophical: one faction wishes to structure the exhibition around a discrete narrative of collapse, drawing together the classic Gibbonian account of institutional decay with more recent explanatory mechanisms — climate-driven agricultural contraction in the western provinces, large-scale migration and settlement pressures, and the progressive fragmentation of fiscal and military coherence — while the other faction advocates a 'transformation rather than fall' framing, stressing administrative continuity in the eastern Mediterranean, the regionalisation of economic networks, and the persistence of Roman legal, linguistic, and religious frameworks long after 476 CE.

Please recommend a concrete course of action: should the gallery retain its present title, adopt an alternative, and should the board commit to a provisional narrative framework with a specified review date (for instance, pending new archaeological synthesis)? Your opinion should engage seriously with the strongest empirical consideration on the losing side — whether that is evidence for abrupt demographic and urban discontinuity in parts of the West, or, conversely, the difficulty of identifying a single moment of systemic failure — rather than dismissing it. In addition, please address whether treating an empire as something that can 'fall' commits a philosophical category error, analogous to attributing biological death to a distributed institutional network, and whether this conceptual point ought to govern the exhibition's architecture regardless of which empirical mechanisms one weights most heavily. The opinion should be written as a formal board document with a clear actionable bottom line.
