Please construct and defend the claim that analogy-making is indeed the core of cognition, in the spirit of the thesis associated with Hofstadter and Sander, but only under a restrictive formal condition: the analogies that genuinely drive concept formation are those that preserve relational invariance across domains that share no surface features, explicitly excluding both loose metaphorical association and identity-based pattern matching. First, show why this restriction saves the thesis from the standard objection that formal deduction and direct perception operate without any analogical component. Second, address the deeper worry that the restriction collapses the thesis into tautology—specifically, that if pure logical inference is treated as a degenerate analogical mapping in which source and target domains are identical, then asserting that all cognition is analogical becomes no more informative than asserting that all cognition is cognition. Offer a principled way to block that collapse without abandoning the core thesis, and explain what this revised view implies about conceptual innovation in mathematics and science: namely, that genuine innovation consists in constructing high-invariance analogies across structurally distant domains rather than in accumulating abstract rules or extending surface similarities. Do not provide bibliographic references, reading lists, or citation lists; the argument should stand entirely on conceptual grounds.
