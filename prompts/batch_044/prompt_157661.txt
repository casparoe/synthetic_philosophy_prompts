so: write the checklist/procedure with ordered tests, pass/fail or judgment-call markers, hard stop conditions, and explicit "this is where you just decide" nodes. then apply it to three petitions: (1) a former intelligence officer who can map out unknown clandestine detention sites but personally ordered torture at two of them; (2) a low-level logistics clerk who was clearly coerced and offers only redundant paperwork; (3) a mid-rank officer whose testimony is factually precise about a massacre but frames every victim death as "unfortunate collateral damage from legitimate counter-insurgency," opening a hermeneutic gap survivors have to close themselves. include one case where the procedure spits out an answer you'll hate — probably the intelligence officer, where high epistemic value collides with direct perpetration but narrative non-substitutability doesnt apply since the harm is physical detention not cultural destruction, so the procedure might lean yes despite your gut.

fix the role (me, chair, 48-hour turn-around, sign personally), frequency (three a week), stakes (wrong yes = impunity; wrong no = lost historical record), constraints (no legal staff, no citation searches, must document reasoning in two sentences). make sure the procedure says what to do when info is missing, and be honest about which steps are just judgment calls in a nice coat.
