Take the role of a professional philosopher of mathematics who specializes in the metaphysics and epistemology of infinity, and think carefully about the structure of the disagreement before offering any verdict. Consider the long-running dispute between Cantorian actualists and strict finitists (in the tradition of Kronecker and modern constructivists) over whether actual infinities exist. The actualist side treats Hilbert's hotel and Cantor's power-set results as showing that completed infinite collections have coherent, if counterintuitive, cardinal properties, and takes the consistency and explanatory fruitfulness of set-theoretic mathematics—especially in continuous physics—as sufficient for ontological commitment. The finitist side grants the formal derivations but denies they apply to any real or possible totality, insisting that "infinite" can only mean potentially unbounded; they read Hilbert's hotel not as a metaphysical possibility but as a reductio of extending part-whole reasoning beyond the finite, and demand constructive accessibility or epistemic grasp as a precondition for existence claims.

This debate has been replayed for well over a century with the same repertoire of moves, and almost nobody changes sides. My working hypothesis is that the stalemate is not mainly about ontology but about what counts as an adequate justification for mathematical commitment: the actualist answers a question about what mathematics describes and explains, while the finitist answers a different question about what we are entitled to assert given our epistemic limits. They may therefore be running different questions—descriptive success versus constructive entitlement—under the single heading "do actual infinities exist?"

Please diagnose this disagreement concretely. Is it verbal, turning on different senses of "existence" or "actual"? Is it substantive but hidden, resting on an unstated standard of ontological commitment? Do bedrock intuitions about constructibility versus mathematical realism do all the work? Are there hidden formal or empirical premises—perhaps about whether physical space is genuinely continuous or merely dense—that neither side makes explicit? Or is the question itself ill-posed because "actual infinity" bundles together distinct claims about cardinality, completeness, and physical instantiation that should be separated?
