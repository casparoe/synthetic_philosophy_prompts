I've been arguing with my roommate about the Sleeping Beauty problem, and I think I've landed on something like this: the whole debate between halfers and thirders is misframed because it assumes self-locating beliefs should update like regular beliefs. I call my view centered-world indifferentism—basically, when Beauty wakes up, she shouldn't move from 1/2 at all, not because she's conditioning on being awake (the standard thirder story) or because there are two copies of her (the standard halfer story), but because centered propositions just don't carry evidential weight for an agent who can't tell them apart. The self-locating part is a red herring; the real issue is whether temporal location is the kind of thing that can be evidence, and I think it isn't. This lets me reject both the principal principle and the restricted versions people try to patch it with, without having to buy into any weird ontology about personal fission.

But I'm worried I'm just insulating myself from counterevidence. What would actually count against centered-world indifferentism? I want the arguments that should genuinely move me: maybe results from other areas about self-locating belief (like the doomsday argument or the duplicated prisoner problem), maybe formal results showing that ignoring centered information violates some coherence constraint, maybe intuitive cases where temporal location clearly does seem evidential. I also want to know whether defenders of the standard thirder or halfer positions would concede anything here—would they agree that if centered propositions lack evidential weight, their own machinery collapses? And is my view in danger of being qualified into unfalsifiability? If so, is that a problem for a view that's mainly about how belief-updating should work? Please sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and don't just list the standard objections—tell me which ones actually hit the core claim that self-location is evidentialy inert.
