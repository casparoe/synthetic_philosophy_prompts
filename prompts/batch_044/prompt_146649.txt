In metaphysics seminars and recent literature, a recurring stall has emerged over whether metaphysical grounding is a single, uniform relation that structures reality into hierarchical levels, or whether "grounding" is merely a family name for several distinct dependency relations—mereological constitution, set-formation, determinable-to-determinate relations—that share only a superficial resemblance. The monists repeatedly invoke the same stock illustrations—molecules composed of atoms, mental states realized by neural states—while pluralists counter that these examples quietly smuggle in different explanatory patterns and should not be unified. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not primarily about those examples at all, but about an unacknowledged divergence in what fundamentality itself requires. One side treats fundamentality as an ontological status: something is fundamental just in case it lacks parts, constituents, or any ontological dependents whatsoever. The other side treats it as an explanatory status: something is fundamental just in case nothing explains its existence or properties, even if it is composed of or correlated with other entities. These two conceptions come apart in a hypothetical scenario—mereological simples arranged by brute, non-causal correlation laws—where every entity is ontologically independent, yet some entities still seem to explain others. Is this the real fracture line? Is the debate substantive, with one conception genuinely capturing our pre-theoretical notion of fundamentality, or is it largely verbal, with each camp answering a different structural question under a single heading?

I want a diagnosis that engages what each side actually says about explanation and composition, rather than a survey of positions. Specifically: do the sides differ about hidden empirical or formal conditions—perhaps about whether grounding must be well-founded, transitive, or irreflexive—or about which cases should count as data for a theory of dependency? Is there a hidden formal question about dependency graphs or about the conditions under which explanation can occur without ontological reduction? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—a sharp conceptual distinction that dissolves the puzzle, a formal result showing the two conceptions are extensionally equivalent in all actual worlds, or an argument that the question is ill-posed because "fundamentality" fragments into incommensurable notions? Or should we expect permanent impasse because the disagreement ultimately traces back to bedrock intuitions about whether explanation requires ontological independence, intuitions that neither side can argue for from neutral ground? Give me an honest verdict on whether anything could move this debate, and why.
