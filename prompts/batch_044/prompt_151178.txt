I've been working through an argument about jumping out of the system—JOOTSing—in the setting of Hofstadter's MU-puzzle, and I think it collapses into something absurd, but I can't pinpoint the damage. Here are the steps:

(1) To see that MI can never become MU inside the formal rules, one must step outside and observe the invariant: the number of Is is always 1 modulo 3, something no rule touches.
(2) So recognizing any formal system's global boundary requires an external vantage point; inside, you only apply rules and never see their limit.
(3) Apply this reflexively: human reasoning—logic, mathematics, meta-cognition—operates within some framework H.
(4) Therefore, if anyone genuinely recognizes a deep limitation of H, they must have jumped out of H.
(5) But the instant they state or defend that recognition—using language, inference, or conceptual distinctions—they are still functioning within H, now directed at a meta-object rather than truly escaped.
(6) Hence no embodied thinker can consistently claim to have genuinely JOOTSed from their own cognitive system; at best they have climbed one internal rung.
(7) So either we never truly recognize our own cognitive limits, or JOOTSing is an incoherent metaphor for embodied reasoners, and the MU-puzzle teaches only about rule-application, not transcendence.

I'd prefer to preserve (1) and (4) if I can, but I'm open to costs. Where does this argument go wrong? Please reply in British English.
