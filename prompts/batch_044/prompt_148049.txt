my friend walked me into this argument about genocide labeling and i cant find the exit. (1) Lemkin's whole point was that genocide isnt just killing lots of people — its attacking a group because its that group. The Convention kept that with the intent-to-destroy clause. (2) So if a regime systematically wipes out a groups language, land, and economy, but claims its modernization and the deaths are just a side effect, then technically its not genocide, even if the group is destroyed. (3) But if we only call it genocide when the intent is clear, then some of the worst group-destructions in history dont count, which makes the word feel useless exactly when we need it. (4) So maybe we should split the moral category from the legal one: call it genocide morally whenever a collective is destroyed as such, wether or not the perpetrators had the specific mental state. (5) But then the category just tracks big group harms, not the specific wrong Lemkin wanted to isolate; it collapses into crimes against humanity or severe war crimes and loses its bite. (6) So either we keep the intent rule and accept it misses horrific cases, or we drop it and the term becomes redundant. My friend says this proves genocide has no stable moral core and is just a political label people fight over. I feel like something slipped between (3) and (4) — like im assuming that if a concept has limits its broken — or maybe between (4) and (5), where i jump from "broader" to "redundant" without argument. Which step should i reject, and what does giving it up cost? Id like to keep the idea that naming atrocities is politically loaded, but i dont want to concede the category is totally hollow.
