Write the joint statement that a strict evidentalist about belief and a pragmatic pluralist about belief-reasons could both sign after an adversarial colab on the wrong-kind-reasons problem. Numbered agreed-claims first: what they both accept about belief versus commitment or hope, about practical stakes, and about exactly where the problem gets its grip. Then a disagreements table with the split phrased in wording each side accepts—probably over whether belief is constitutively truth-aimed in a way that automatically screens out pragmatic considerations. Then the cruxes: one should be whether belief can stay evidence-responsive if pragmatic benefits partly sustain it; another is whether a medating attitude like practical commitment can absorb all the pluralist's work without corrupting belief itself. For each crux include a concrete case both agree is relevant—like believing you're a decent mentor partly because that belief makes you actually show up for students, and whether that belief can still be revised by counter-evidence. Each side should write down its prediction about the case in advance. No bibliography or citation list; this should stand on its own. Note if part of the dispute turns out to be verbal rather than substantive.
