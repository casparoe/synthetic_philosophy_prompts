As a retired physicist now working through philosophy of mind, I find the standard brain-computer debate too coarse-grained to be illuminating. I wish to focus on a specific intermediate claim: that the brain implements computation not through discrete symbol manipulation or digital state-transitions, but through continuous dynamical trajectories in a high-dimensional state space that are shaped constitutively—not merely constrained—by metabolic energy gradients. Let me call this the metabolic-analog computation thesis. What is your credence that this thesis is correct, and that it renders traditional computationalism true only in a deflationary sense (the brain computes in roughly the same way a weather system computes) but false in the strong sense (the brain executes a program over syntactically structured vehicles)? Decompose your reasoning: assign rough probabilities to (i) whether neural information processing is essentially analog and dynamical rather than digital and syntactic, (ii) whether metabolic energy landscapes play a constitutive rather than merely boundary role in defining those dynamics, and (iii) whether accepting (i) and (ii) dissolves rather than resolves the traditional identity question between brain and computer. Finally, indicate which of these three sub-questions would move your overall credence most, and what kind of empirical or conceptual finding would shift it.
