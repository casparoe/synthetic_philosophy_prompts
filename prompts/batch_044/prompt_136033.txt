Write an essay on a tension within ontic structural realism: can it consistently admit what we might call a "structural haecceity" — an entity's principle of individuation that consists solely in its unique, non-interchangeable position within a relational network, with no appeal to intrinsic, non-structural properties? First, present the case that structural haecceities rescue OSR from collapsing into pure relationalism, in which objects become featureless nodes lacking identity conditions altogether. Then present the opposing case: if individuation is genuinely and exhaustively structural, then symmetry transformations that permute nodes with identical relational profiles should leave the world unchanged, which seems to dissolve rather than preserve the particularity that a haecceity was introduced to secure. In weighing these positions, consider specifically whether accepting structural haecceities forces OSR to adopt a hidden constraint on which symmetries are physically or metaphysically permissible — and whether that constraint can be motivated without smuggling back in the very intrinsic properties OSR sought to eliminate. Reach and defend a definite conclusion: either that OSR must reject structural haecceities and accept a stronger eliminativism about individual objects, or that structural haecceities can be coherently retained only if OSR commits to a specific, defensible limitation on permissible permutations (state and justify that limitation). Your essay should not treat the dispute as merely verbal; it should turn on whether relational structure alone can ground particularity without circularity.
