I'm on the advisory board for the Meridian Science Museum's cosmology wing and we vote thursday on an interactive gallery proposal about Gödel's rotating universe. The designer wants more than physics: the display frames the 1949 metric's closed timelike curves as a live philosophical argument that objective time-passage is unreal, since in a uniformly rotating cosmos any observer could eventualy revisit their own past, which Gödel read as showing that physical "time" is only a local ordering relation with no global reality. Visitors walk through a loop corridor experiencing the same sequence twice, with a plaque saying duration collapses into spatial extension if vorticity never vanishes.

Some board members love this; others say it's misleading to present a cosmological model that data has basicly ruled out as if it were a current argument against time's reality. There's also a side fight about whether to include Gödel's private correspondance, where he apparently insisted the universe really is rotating, since that might make the metaphysical claim look empirically grounded.

Draft the memo I'll circulate. Recomend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a concrete revision timeline. Take seriously both sides: stripping out Gödel's philosophical conclusion risks making this just another physics display, but keeping it risks confusing obsolete cosmology with live metaphysics. Make it something both the designer and the skeptical physicists could actualy respond to.
