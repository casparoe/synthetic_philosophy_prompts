I have been attempting to articulate my own commitments regarding conceivability as a guide to metaphysical possibility, and I find that my intuitions across concrete cases are not obviously consistent. I present six scenarios with my current verdicts, and I wish to know whether a single principle can accommodate them all, or whether I must abandon at least one.

First, I can clearly conceive of water being composed of XYZ rather than H2O in an environment structurally analogous to Earth, and I take this to indicate that being H2O is not metaphysically necessary to being water—though I remain unsure whether I have conceived a different substance or only a different extension for the same term.

Second, I can conceive of a being physically identical to me in every functional and structural respect but with an inverted phenomenal spectrum, and I judge that this genuinely indicates metaphysical possibility; it seems to me that any adequate account of consciousness must either accommodate or explicitly deny this scenario, but the conceivability itself is intact.

Third, I can initially imagine a localized region of space-time in which the gravitational constant differs from its value elsewhere, yet upon reflection I think I am not conceiving a different law of nature but rather a miracle, a boundary condition, or an embedded anomaly; consequently I deny that this conceivability indicates the metaphysical possibility of a genuinely different law.

Fourth, I can imagine a system that behaves exactly as I do—producing language, responding to injury, navigating social contexts—while lacking any subjective experience whatsoever. Nevertheless, I am unsure whether I am genuinely conceiving the absence of consciousness or merely conceiving complex behavior while failing to attend to the consciousness that might accompany it; my verdict is therefore suspended.

Fifth, I can initially entertain the idea that the mathematical constant π might have a different value, but after attempting to embed this in a coherent geometric framework I conclude that I am only conceiving a symbol with altered reference, not the same structural constant with different properties; hence I think this is not genuinely conceivable in the relevant modal sense, and it does not indicate possibility.

Sixth, I can vividly conceive, from the first-person perspective, of making a free decision that violates the deterministic causal chain constituted by my prior brain states. I take this to indicate metaphysical possibility, though I acknowledge that this conceivability may depend on a phenomenological access that does not straightforwardly translate into any third-person structural description.

Is there a unified principle—perhaps one that appeals to conditions of structural embedding, modal specificity, or the distinction between first-person and third-person conceiving—that delivers all six verdicts? If not, which of my judgments ought I to overturn, and what does the best available principle predict for a case I have not listed: conceiving of a conscious being whose subjective experiences are systematically disconnected from its behavioral outputs in a manner detectable only from the first-person perspective, with no third-person behavioral signature whatsoever?
