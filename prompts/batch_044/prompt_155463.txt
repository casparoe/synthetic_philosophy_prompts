I have been working through a position I am inclined to defend in my dissertation, situated between the standard cranes-versus-skyhooks framing and debates about greedy reductionism. The view is this: explanatory autonomy for institutional and mental kinds does not require irreducible causal powers (skyhooks), but it also cannot survive greedy reductionism that decomposes every higher-level regularity into its base-level constituents. Instead, I propose a constraint-dependency thesis: an institutional kind—say, a monetary system or a social role—maintains explanatory autonomy when its sustaining conditions are recursively selected by prior instantiations of the kind itself, functioning not as efficient causes but as selection environments that constrain lower-level behavior. On this model, physicalism is preserved, since there are no non-physical entities; yet greedy reduction fails because the explanatory work done at the institutional level involves tracking which lower-level behaviors are permitted or reinforced, a task that cannot be replaced by a complete physical-level description without loss of the selective structure.

What would count against this position? I want an honest assessment of its exposure. Which empirical findings, conceptual arguments, or formal results in evolutionary theory, social ontology, or philosophy of mind would genuinely undermine it rather than merely complicate it? For instance, if institutional kinds turned out to be fully decomposable into individual belief-desire networks with no residual selective structure, that would seem damaging—but is that live, or has it already been addressed? Conversely, are there results showing that selection-environment explanations always collapse into standard causal-mechanistic ones at a finer grain? I also want to know whether the view has been insulated by qualifications: have I rendered it unfalsifiable by allowing any apparent reduction to be re-described as a hidden selection environment? If so, is that insulation a theoretical vice or an appropriate reflection of the domain?

Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. For each, indicate what a committed defender of the constraint-dependency thesis would concede as genuinely counting against them, and which objections they would dismiss as misunderstandings of the selection-environment model. Finally, are there rival frameworks—say, standard functionalist reduction or non-reductive physicalism with downward causation—into which my view risks collapsing, and if so, does that collapse count as a failure or as a clarification?
