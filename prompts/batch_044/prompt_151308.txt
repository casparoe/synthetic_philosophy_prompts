I've been chewing on this view I cobled together from Dennett and some predicitve-processing blogs—call it compressive illusionism if you want a name for it. The idea is that phenomenal consciousness isn't just mysterious, it's literally a user-illusion generated when a system's error-minimization dynamics get mislrepresented in an internal narrative layer. Specifically, the feeling of there being something it is like to be me is just the brain's compressed summary of high-dimensional interoceptive prediction errors being tagged with false metaphysical weight; there's no qualitative essence, just a convincing story the system tells itself to stabilize behavior and social coordination. I think this dissolves the hard problem rather than solving it, but I'm not sure if I'm just insulating the view from anything that could actually hurt it.

What would count against this? I don't want the usual "but I know I feel pain" handwaving. I'm looking for the defeaters that should move someone who already buys the predictive-processing framing—empirical results about neural correlates of reported experience, or about artificial systems with similar dynamics but no narrative layer, or formal arguments showing that if the illusion is purely compressive then some feature like the transparency of experience becomes unexplainable. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward. And be honest: has this kind of view been qualified into unfalsifiability, where every report of experience is just more illusion-data? Is that insulation a problem for a metaphysical claim like this, or is it actually appropriate?

Also, what would defenders of this specific framing concede counts against them—not just what critics shout, but the real pressure points they'd admit? Maybe something about boundary conditions where the illusion should break but apparently doesn't, or a result showing the compression mechanism can't account for the seeming immediacy of pain. I'd like an assessment of whether this position risks anything concrete or if it's been made too safe to be interesting.
