I run a philosophy workshop for about 12 grad students, 90 minutes. Design an in-class game that makes them feel Dennett's idea about the varieties of free will worth wanting — especially the gap between 'leeway' (actually being able to do otherwise) and 'elbow room' (having space to act on your own reasons). I want them to hit the wall that determinism might kill leeway but leave elbow room intact.

Set it up as a group simulation with roles: Navigator, Observer, Recorder. They guide a token through a grid of choices, but unkown to them the grid is deterministic — hidden rules funnel every path based on prior moves. Midway, reveal the rules. They keep playing with point payoffs (not cash): Navigators score for reaching goals they picked, but lose points for breaking hidden constraints. Even after seeing it's fixed, some should still defend certain moves as genuinely 'theirs'; others should check out.

Then debrief: map the discovery moment onto Dennett's distinction, ask what will predicatbly go wrong first run (probably people break character and laugh), and be honest about which part of the view the game can't reproduce — probably the evolutionary story about why elbow room is worth wanting — so I have to supply that verbally. No lecture up front, don't name Dennett until after, keep rules simple enough that I can run it without tech, and include a quick timeline for the 90 minutes.
