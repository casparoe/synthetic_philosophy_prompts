Read Harold Ramis's Groundhog Day not through the usual moral-education framing but through the metaphysics of persistence, specifically the dispute between endurantism—according to which Phil Connors is a three-dimensional object wholly present on each loop-day, retaining identity through time—and perdurantism, which treats him as a four-dimensional worm composed of distinct temporal parts, each loop-day perhaps being a new part causally connected but not strictly identical to its predecessors. The film seems to presuppose an endurantist logic: Phil accumulates piano skills, remembers failed suicide attempts, and eventually transforms into someone worthy of breaking the loop, all of which only makes emotional sense if one enduring self carries those modifications. Yet the loop's structure—where the world resets but Phil alone does not—invites a perdurantist reconstruction: each February 2nd might be a new temporal part of Phil inheriting memories from the previous part, with death in the loop being not the destruction of an enduring self but the termination of one part and the generation of another. I want you to take both the film and the metaphysical debate seriously and ask where they illuminate each other and where they come apart.

Focus on three scenes in particular: the piano lesson, where skill accrual seems to require a single enduring learner; the sequence of suicide attempts, which raises the question of whether an enduring Phil is repeatedly destroyed and replaced, or whether each attempt annihilates only one temporal part; and the final loop-break, when the clock finally advances to February 3rd—does this represent the same enduring Phil finally progressing, or the cessation of the loop-Phil parts and the emergence of a post-loop successor? I am especially interested in whether the film's comedy and its romantic resolution depend on smuggling in an endurantist intuition that its own premise might undermine, and whether a perdurantist could still make sense of Phil's moral growth without invoking one self that persists unchanged through every repetition. Where the film resists the reading—perhaps in its refusal to explain the loop's mechanism—say so honestly, and conclude by suggesting what this particular narrative form, with its cyclical time and isolated subject, might contribute to the philosophical debate that abstract arguments about temporal parts and endurance do not capture on their own.
