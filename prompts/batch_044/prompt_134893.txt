I have been working through my own position on consciousness and selfhood without formal academic training, and I would like a consistency check before I discuss it with others. Here is the package of views I have arrived at:

(1) I reject the Cartesian theatre: I do not believe there is a single location or central observer in the brain where experience is displayed for a conscious subject.
(2) I find the multiple drafts model persuasive: cognitive and sensory processing occurs in parallel streams, and there is no master copy or canonical version of any mental content.
(3) I maintain that phenomenal consciousness is real, but distributed across these parallel streams rather than localised in one region.
(4) I take the feeling of being a continuous inner witness to be a post-hoc narrative construction, not evidence of an actual observer watching the drafts.
(5) At the same time, when I sincerely report what I am experiencing now, I take myself to be accessing genuinely unified content, not arbitrarily selecting one draft from competing alternatives.
(6) I believe that coherent action requires a conflict-resolution mechanism that effectively functions like a theatre for decision-making, even though I deny that any such theatre exists for conscious experience itself.
(7) I think the work that people call binding is actually done by temporal alignment across drafts, not by spatial convergence in a single brain area.

Please audit these commitments for joint tenability. I want to know which pairs pull against each other genuinely and which tensions dissolve on closer inspection, ordered by severity. For each real tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what I would lose by making it, and treat the whole set systematically rather than singling out particular pairs.
