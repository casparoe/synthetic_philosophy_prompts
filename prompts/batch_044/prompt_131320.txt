okay so my study group cooked up this argument about Du Bois and now i think it proves too much but i cant see the exit.

(1) In "Criteria of Negro Art" Du Bois says all art is propaganda—he means every artwork serves some social or political purpose, and Black art in particular should be propaganda for racial uplift.

(2) If art is essentially propaganda, then what makes something good art just is how well it advances that purpose—call it the propaganda coefficient.

(3) So a technically sloppy but politically sharp poster that moves thousands to action is better art than a formally perfect poem about flowers that changes no ones politics.

(4) Du Bois himself praised Keats Ode on a Grecian Urn as beautiful, even though it has nothing to do with Black struggle or any obvious cause.

(5) If we keep (2), then Du Bois should have dismissed Keats as bad art, which he clearly didnt.

(6) So either Du Bois was wrong about Keats, or his claim that all art is propaganda collapses into the idea that art is only propaganda, which is absurd.

(7) Therefore we should reject (1) and say art can be non-propagandistic after all.

But i want to hang on to Du Bois's point—there is something right about art being embedded in social purpose. Which step do i actually have to drop, and what does it cost me? Is the mistake in (2) treating "is propaganda" as "value equals propaganda"? Or is there a hidden slide between (1) and (2) im missing? If i reject (2) do i lose the whole punch of Du Bois's view, or can i keep a weaker version that still bites?
