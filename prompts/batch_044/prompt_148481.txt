Analyse the 1857 United States Supreme Court decision in Dred Scott v. Sandford through the lens of Frederick Douglass's interpretive theory of the US Constitution—specifically his claim that the document is fundamentally anti-slavery when read according to its text and underlying universal principles, rather than according to the intentions, practices, or prejudices of its slaveholding framers.

Treat Douglass's hermeneutics as the theoretical framework and the ruling as the case under examination. First, map Chief Justice Taney's central moves—the denial of Black citizenship, the invalidation of the Missouri Compromise, and the reasoning that treated enslaved persons as property protected by the Constitution—onto the "intentionalist or slaveholder interpretation" that Douglass rejected. Second, examine whether Douglass's textual-principled reading can coherently accommodate or rebut these specific moves without collapsing into the opposing framework: for instance, does it have resources to address the citizenship clause and the Fifth Amendment due-process reasoning as Taney deployed them, or must it treat those passages as corrupted, inoperative, or misread? Third, discuss what the episode reveals about the theory itself—particularly whether a purely textual anti-slavery reading can constrain judicial interpretation when the court is ideologically captured by the opposing hermeneutic, and whether the subsequent Reconstruction amendments should be understood, on Douglass's view, as correcting an originally flawed text or as confirming a meaning already present. Finally, identify where the theory is silent or strained: are there features of the crisis (the Court's institutional authority, the political reaction, the path to civil war) that the hermeneutic framework explains well, and are there others—such as the problem of hermeneutic circularity, where historical practice and textual meaning each claim priority—that it fails to resolve?

Stick to the widely known features of the case; do not supply bibliographies, citation lists, or specific page references. The deliverable should run in both directions: the theory illuminating the decision, and the decision testing the limits of the theory.
