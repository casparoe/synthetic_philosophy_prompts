I am designing a single ninety-minute session for philosophy undergraduates who know Gödel's incompleteness results only by reputation and have just completed an introductory unit on modal logic. I want to teach them a reconstructed version of Gödel's rationalist position on personal survival after death—treating it not as religious consolation but as a logical consequence of combining his modal-ontological argument for a necessary being with a Leibnizian account of individual essences, according to which the properties that constitute a thinking subject are essential and therefore not vulnerable to the contingent physical event of bodily death.

Plan the explanatory architecture for me, not the explanation itself: the precise sequence in which the conceptual layers must be laid down and the dependency relations that make any other order fail; the one concrete analogy or scenario that should carry the entire session—something that makes intelligible why the destruction of a contingent substrate does not amount to the destruction of a formally complete essence, but that is not the familiar software-running-on-hardware image, which I want to avoid because it smuggles in functionalist assumptions; the predictable misconceptions—specifically, the slide into Cartesian substance dualism, the assumption that this is an empirically unfalsifiable hope rather than a metaphysical claim, and the erroneous recruitment of the incompleteness theorems as premises—and exactly where in the sequence each one should be intercepted; the check at the end that distinguishes students who have grasped the modal structure of the argument (that survival is a matter of logical consistency rather than spiritual persistence) from those who have only absorbed a vague reassurance; and what I should openly bracket as beyond this session—such as detailed exegesis of Gödel's unpublished philosophical manuscripts, neuroscience of consciousness, or comparative theology—and how to frame that bracketing so it reads as intellectual discipline rather than evasion.

The audience will be skeptical of metaphysics and prone to reduce any afterlife claim to psychology; keep the plan focused on the dependency structure and the misconception map.
