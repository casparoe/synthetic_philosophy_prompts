I am preparing a presentation for an independent study seminar in philosophy of mind—twelve students, one hour—on the relationship between access consciousness, phenomenal consciousness, and global workspace theory. Rather than a lecture comparing named theorists, I want a branching taxonomy that sorts positions by the answers to concrete diagnostic questions that a student could answer without knowing the labels in advance. The first branch should ask whether global broadcasting of information can occur with absolutely no phenomenal character—what I will call a "broadcast-only" architecture—versus whether broadcasting necessarily carries at least minimal phenomenal character. The second branch should ask whether phenomenal consciousness can exist without any global broadcasting—what I will call a "privately phenomenal" state—versus whether broadcasting is necessary for phenomenal consciousness. The third branch should ask whether, assuming both can exist, global workspace mechanisms are sufficient for phenomenal consciousness, necessary but not sufficient, or neither necessary nor sufficient. Each leaf should carry either a named position or "unnamed," with one sentence stating its core commitment and one sentence describing its most awkward implication—especially regarding hypothetical "super-blindsight" cases in which a subject integrates visual information globally yet reports no visual experience whatsoever. The tree should be built from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of author names; please note any unoccupied combinations and briefly diagnose whether each gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or merely being unexplored. Finally, please flag any pair of positions that this tree cannot genuinely separate, and explain exactly where the branching structure collapses them together.
