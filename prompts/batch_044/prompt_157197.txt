Consider the following exam item from an intermediate philosophy of language course, together with one student’s answer. I am grading the exam and need a precise, point-by-point assessment rather than a general impression.

Exam question: “Explain Frege’s puzzle about informative identity statements, using the Hesperus/Phosphorus example. Why does this case pose a problem for the view that the meaning of a proper name is exhausted by its reference? Then explain Frege’s own resolution in terms of Sinn and Bedeutung, being precise about what, exactly, is identical in a true identity statement.”

Student answer: “Frege noticed that if meaning equals reference, then ‘Hesperus is Hesperus’ and ‘Hesperus is Phosphorus’ should have the same cognitive value, since both names refer to Venus. But ancient astronomers learned something genuinely new when they discovered Hesperus is Phosphorus, so the meaning of a name cannot be just the object it picks out.

Frege solves this by distinguishing sense (Sinn) from reference (Bedeutung). Both names have Venus as their reference, but they differ in sense: ‘Hesperus’ presents Venus as the evening star, while ‘Phosphorus’ presents it as the morning star. So ‘Hesperus is Phosphorus’ connects two different senses to one and the same reference.

This shows that identity is fundamentally a relation between modes of presentation. The statement tells us that two senses we might have taken to pick out different objects in fact converge on the same object. Its cognitive value therefore depends on the descriptive content speakers associate with each name—‘the evening star’ versus ‘the morning star’—which constitutes the sense and, in ordinary cases, fixes the reference. What we learn is that our descriptive representations align, even though the celestial body itself was already self-identical.”
