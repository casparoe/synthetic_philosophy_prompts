Construct a thought experiment in the spirit of Dennett’s brain-transplant puzzle, modified so that ambiguity about the self’s location arises from functional equivalence rather than linguistic vagueness. Design a realistic near-future medical scenario—no teleportation, no perfect predictors, no exotic physics—presented in one paragraph: a patient’s biological brain is gradually replaced neuron-by-neuron with synthetic analogues in situ, while an identical biological brain is grown in a vat and linked to the original body via a real-time, bidirectional neural interface that shares conscious processing load. At the midpoint both substrates are equally causally integrated with the body and with each other, and the patient sincerely reports being “here” without being able to isolate a unique center. After the scenario, analyze it through three lenses: Dennett’s narrative-center-of-gravity view, a strict biological-continuity view, and an extended-mind view that treats the interface as part of the cognitive system. For each, state precisely where the self is located and why. Then identify the single structural feature that forces the three views apart, and explain what collapses back into a standard brain-transplant case if the bidirectional load-sharing link is removed. Deliver the response in two parts: first the scenario and analysis in prose; then a table with columns Theory, Location of the self, Key reasoning, and What breaks if the link is removed.
