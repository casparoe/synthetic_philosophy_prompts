Consider a research program of about three years aimed at the conceptual foundations of moral worth and right-reason acting, with one specific framing injected: distinguish between surface-level correct motivation, where an agent explicitly appeals to appropriate moral considerations, and deep structural alignment, where an agent’s decision procedure is stably coupled to correct moral outputs across counterfactual variations in non-moral beliefs regardless of conscious access. Which two or three open questions in this space deserve the most attention, and in what order? Defend the prioritization relative to goals of conceptual clarity and tractability for philosophical analysis rather than empirical study. For each question, specify why it is upstream of the others, what kind of argument or conceptual result over the next few years would shift its rank, and whether resolving it would genuinely change how we should think about moral luck and unconscious motivation, or whether the framing itself collapses upon analysis.
