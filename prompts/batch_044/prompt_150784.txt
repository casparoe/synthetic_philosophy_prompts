My neighborhood runs a tool-sharing cooperative funded by identical initial contributions. A professional carpenter member offers to repair tools at no charge, asking only to keep leftover materials; through dozens of voluntary exchanges everyone accepts, he gradually accumulates a substantial private stockpile of premium lumber and hardware. Other members now argue the outcome violates the cooperative's egalitarian purpose and propose a collective levy to redistribute his holdings, even though no individual exchange was coerced. I am trying to understand whether fairness is violated here, given that every step was freely agreed to but the overall pattern is starkly unequal. Does this case show that a just distribution can become unjust without anyone acting wrongly, or is there a deeper reason to prioritize the history of transactions over the resulting pattern? Respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
