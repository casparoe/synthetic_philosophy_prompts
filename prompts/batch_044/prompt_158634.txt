Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of moral learning from biography must satisfy, using Plutarch's Parallel Lives and the hagiographic tradition as central cases. For each requirement, state the phenomenon or distinction it protects—such as the difference between learning a virtue and learning a life-pattern, or between the comparative structure of paired lives and the singular focus of hagiography—and explain the motivating case behind it. Specify which results from psychology about emulation and disillusionment the theory must accommodate without contradiction. Identify which desiderata are non-negotiable—e.g., explaining why idealization and selective suppression of vice do not always corrupt moral education—and which reflect preferences of particular camps. Flag any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable, especially if one demands historical fidelity while another demands an unambiguous moral exemplar, and say which single requirement, if strictly enforced, eliminates the most existing accounts. Keep the emphasis on the criteria and their cases rather than on scoring named theorists.
