For my aesthetics seminar, construct a thought experiment in which causal-provenance essentialism and experiential structuralism about artistic value must deliver opposite verdicts on a single restored painting, but only because of a feature absent from standard forgery cases. Constraints: realistic setting (a museum conservation studio, not science fiction), one paragraph, and the verdicts must fall out of the theories rather than being stipulated. Engineer the case so that exactly one pigment layer has been replaced with a synthetic compound chemically indistinguishable from the original, applied posthumously by the artist's documented assistant using the artist's own brushes and documented technique, yet without the artist's authorization or awareness. After the scenario, walk through what each theory says and why, identify the feature doing the work—temporal-authorial discontinuity embedded within complete physical and procedural continuity—explain whether removing that feature collapses the divergence, and show how an essentialist could attack the case by arguing that authorized posthumous practice extends rather than breaks the relevant causal chain.
