I am writing in connection with a discussion that arose in my graduate seminar on Nietzsche’s philosophy of affirmation, where the motto “amor fati” — taken to mean that one ought to love one’s fate precisely as though every instant were destined to recur identically and without alteration — has become a point of contention. One of my students invoked it to claim that Nietzsche’s doctrine of eternal recurrence is not, at bottom, a cosmological speculation about the structure of time, but rather an ethical test: we are to evaluate the worth of our lives by whether we can genuinely will their infinite repetition. A second student objected that this renders the injunction either redundant or incoherent, for if the loop is genuinely deterministic, then my affective stance is itself part of the repeated sequence and therefore cannot be freely adopted; conversely, if my stance can alter the loop, then the repetition is not strictly identical and the cosmological claim collapses.

I should be grateful if you could disentangle the precise theses that might be compressed into this slogan, laying them out as a menu rather than selecting a single interpretation. In particular, please distinguish between: (i) a purely psychological recommendation to cultivate affirmation regardless of metaphysical commitments; (ii) a modal claim that all actual worlds are repeated in a cyclical or branching structure; (iii) an axiological principle that a life’s value is measured by the agent’s hypothetical willingness to relive it; and (iv) any further precise reading — perhaps involving indexicality across loops, a distinction between token and type repetition, or a non-causal sense of “willing” that escapes determinism — that might allow the slogan to do the work my first student requires without falling into the redundancy charge my second student raises. For each reading, indicate whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible position, or plainly false, and specify which of them, if any, genuinely supports using “amor fati” to dismiss the determinist objection without smuggling in an unverifiable cosmology.
