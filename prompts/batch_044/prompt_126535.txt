so heres my twist: imagine Chamberlain plays ball, but people dont pay him directly. they pay through a platform that takes a huge cut, controls access to the arena, and can freeze his earnings if he breaks some opaque rule. after the game he has a lot of credits, but the platform can revoke them. Nozick would probably say this is still just if the initial holdings were just and people chose to pay. but i want to argue that the mediation isnt just background -- its part of the transfer structure. if the intermediary has coercive power over the exchange, then saying 'people voluntarily paid' misses the fact that the terms of exchange are already shaped by an unjust framework. so the Wilt Chamberlain argument doesnt really defeat patterned principles; it just pushes the pattern into the infrastructure and pretends its neutral.

5. So the separation Nozick needs -- between free exchange and background conditions -- collapses.
6. Therefore the argument against patterned justice fails, or at least needs to be redone.
