Write a dialogue between a moral particularist and a generalist about whether moral reasons can have indexical granularity — that is, whether a reason applies only because of the specific framing history of the agent, not just the objective features of the case. The particularist should defend the idea that some moral responses depend on a kind of salience transfer that cant be captured by paramterized principles, since the transfer itself is shaped by prior commitments that arent part of the situation's public description. The generalist should push back that framing history can always be folded into the principle as a contextual variable, and ask whether the particularist is just describing a complex principle with hidden premises. Both should actually respond to each other: the particularist needs to say why folding history in still loses something about the reason's force, and the generalist needs to show that the particularist's salience transfer risks collapsing into arbitrary preference unless theres some constraint on which prior commitments count. Keep it casual and focused, dont ask for citations or reading lists.
