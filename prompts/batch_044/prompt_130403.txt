I'm giving a talk next tuesday at our dept's East-West ethics workshop. I'm defending this view I call nested autonomy: that both the Western liberal idea of the unencumbered self and the usual East Asian relational self miss the direction of fit. Agency isn't prior to community like Rawls thinks, or dissolved into it like standard communitarian readings suggest, but constituted through communal roles you actively renew and can revise from inside—think family obligations that are real but renegotiated, not just inherited. The audience is mostly comparative philosophers who know Rawls and MacIntyre cold, plus two anthropologsts who study modern Korean kinship structures, and one metaphysician who thinks social ontology is mostly bunk. They hate vague syntheses and will definately bring up Orientalism. I need a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get, ordered by damage, each phrased the way that specific audience member would ask it, with a quick reply and a note on where I'm soft. Include the one I'm praying nobody asks. No citation lists needed—just the strategic layout.
