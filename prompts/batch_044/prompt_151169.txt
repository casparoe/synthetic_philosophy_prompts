Karl Jaspers distinguished criminal, political, moral, and metaphysical guilt, maintaining that only political guilt applies collectively to a state or citizenry, while moral and metaphysical guilt remain strictly personal. His framework, however, does not clearly settle whether democratic regimes that inherit unjust institutional structures—such as constitutional orders founded on dispossession—carry a distinct form of structural political guilt across regime changes, or whether such inheritance reduces to present citizens' individual moral failures or to mere legal liability.
