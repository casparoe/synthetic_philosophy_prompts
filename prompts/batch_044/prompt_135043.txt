I am a doctoral student working on moral responsibility and the ethics of blame. I defend a restricted view of how hypocrisy undermines standing to blame: hypocrisy cancels standing only under direct normative parity, where the blamer is presently committing the identical wrong through the same mode of agency, but not under indirect structural complicity, where the blamer benefits from or fails to resist systemic conditions that also produce the blamed party's fault. On my account, extending the familiar intuition about vulnerable accusers to structural cases is a category error; shared vulnerability may even strengthen standing by making the blamer an epistemically situated witness rather than a detached accuser. What would genuinely count against this position? I want the defeaters sorted by decisiveness: which intuitions about specific cases—climate hypocrisy, tax avoidance, institutional discrimination—would compel me to concede that structural complicity also cancels standing; whether formal or analogical arguments show that direct and indirect complicity differ only in degree; and whether my distinction risks being qualified post hoc into unfalsifiability. Please also distinguish objections that defenders of an unrestricted view would themselves concede as damaging from those they would dismiss, and indicate whether insulation by qualification is a problem for a view of this kind. Lead with your bottom line in one sentence, then develop the exposure map. Hard limit: 350 words.
