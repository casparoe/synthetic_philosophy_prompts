Compare tranquilism—the view that only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic value, with pleasure, knowledge, and achievement being axially neutral—against what might be called craving-saturated consequentialism: the position that positive goods possess value only in proportion to the craving they satisfy, and that the absence of suffering is merely a baseline condition rather than the sole source of value. In particular, examine whether tranquilism can consistently treat the dissolution of a false craving—say, the disappearance of a vengeful desire once its object is genuinely understood—as a positive good, or whether it must classify that dissolution as only the removal of a negative state. Further, assess whether tranquilism's axiological neutrality toward pleasure renders it vulnerable to the experience-machine objection in a way that standard negative utilitarianism avoids. Present your answer in one paragraph of no more than 180 words, leading with the bottom line: which view handles the false-craving dissolution more coherently, and why.
