Write a dialogue between a proponent of functional moral agency and a defender of normative competence as the necessary condition for moral responsibility, debating whether an autonomous urban logistics network—one that dynamically reallocates delivery drones across airspace using real-time ethical weighting modules—can genuinely bear a form of responsibility when its optimization produces an unforeseen harm: cumulative noise and pressure disturbances trigger a protected bat colony collapse near a rerouted corridor. The functionalist should argue that the system’s internal value-reflection architecture, which weights ecological externalities in its utility calculus, fills the responsibility gap non-derivatively, without reducing to programmer intent or operator oversight, and should specifically answer the objection that this is merely causal contribution dressed in anthropomorphic language. The normative-competence defender should insist that responsibility requires the capacity to recognize reasons as reasons, not merely to process them as weighted variables, and should respond directly to the functionalist’s analogy to corporate entities and infants—entities we hold responsible without requiring full rational autonomy. Both characters should be equally competent, with real back-and-forth on whether the distinction between being responsible and being held responsible survives this case, and whether introducing a category of quasi-responsibility for autonomous systems solves the gap or only renames it. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific authors or texts; focus on the conceptual architecture of the arguments themselves.
