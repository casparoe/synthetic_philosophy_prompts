I am drafting the conceptual core of a graduate seminar on process philosophy and practical reason. Please explain the precise conceptual relation between Whitehead's pedagogical doctrine of 'rhythm' — the cyclical movement through romance, precision, and generalisation — and his metaphysical theory of 'actual occasions' and concrescence. Your answer should not merely summarise The Aims of Education, but should determine whether the three educational stages function as an analogical projection of metaphysical process onto learning, or whether they carry autonomous normative weight derived from the phenomenology of attention itself. You must also address the following internal tension: Whitehead condemns 'inert ideas' as dead knowledge that fails to be utilised, yet the categories of his metaphysical scheme — eternal objects, prehensions, subjective forms — are notoriously abstract and appear remote from the concrete rhythms he prescribes for education. Is this a genuine performative contradiction, or can one defend the view that these abstractions become 'animated' precisely through the pedagogical rhythm, so that the metaphysical system is itself the product of the educational process it describes? In constructing your response, avoid bibliographic lists and do not appeal to secondary commentary by name; instead, work directly with the conceptual architecture linking his metaphysics of becoming to his theory of learning.
