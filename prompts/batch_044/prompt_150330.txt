Draft the joint statement that could emerge from an adversarial collaboration between the Hierarchical Flexibilists and the Deep-Rigidity Theorists on sphexishness, mechanical rigidity, and free will. The Hierarchical Flexibilists maintain that human agency escapes sphex-like mechanical rigidity through multi-level, self-modifying architectures of action selection; the Deep-Rigidity Theorists maintain that such architectures are themselves deterministic, and that human flexibility collapses into a more complex, higher-order form of the same rigidity exhibited by the digger wasp Sphex ichneumoneus.

Structure the document as follows.

First, provide a numbered section of agreed claims. These should cover: the basic ethology of the Sphex burial-check loop and its invariant structure; the logical distinction between first-order behavioral rigidity and second-order capacity for rule-revision; the current comparative-neuroscience picture regarding action-selection hierarchies in insects versus mammals; and the shared recognition that resolving the free-will debate requires determining whether multi-level flexibility is genuinely open-ended or merely descriptively richer determinism.

Second, present a disagreements table in which each row states a precise disagreement phrased in wording that the respective camp would accept for its own view. Include at minimum: (i) whether second-order modifiability is ontologically distinct from complex determinism or only an epiphenomenal redescription of it; (ii) the status of “refractory sphex loops”—conditions in which an agent’s meta-cognitive flexibility mechanisms themselves become invariant under stress or perturbation—specifically whether their documented occurrence in human cognition undermines the claim to genuine flexibility or merely reveals its operational boundary conditions; and (iii) whether moral responsibility requires breaking sphexishness entirely, or only exhibiting a sufficiently complex, context-sensitive pattern of flexible response.

Third, identify three cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party. For each crux, specify a concrete case, argument, or empirical scenario that both sides agree is genuinely relevant, and record in advance what each side predicts the outcome will be. One crux must center on the refractory sphex loop: both sides should agree that if human subjects in high-cognitive-load, high-stakes experiments systematically revert to invariant behavioral scripts despite intact meta-cognitive awareness of alternatives, this bears directly on whether flexibility is structurally deep or only superficially layered; the Flexibilists should predict partial, recoverable reversion, while the Deep-Rigidity Theorists should predict revelation of an underlying invariant architecture.

Fourth, conclude with an assessment that separates substantive disagreement from verbal disagreement, particularly addressing whether “sphexishness” is applied with the same extension at first-order and second-order levels of analysis, and whether “flexibility” operates as a graded property or a binary threshold.

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Finally, include a brief meta-note stating what specific argument, empirical finding, or conceptual clarification would cause you to revise your framing of the agreements and disagreements, and identify the point in your reasoning—especially regarding the interpretation of second-order modifiability or the evidential weight of refractory loops—that is most likely to fail or be contested.
