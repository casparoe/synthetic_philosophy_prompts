Exam question: Endurantism holds that persisting objects are wholly present at each time they exist, whereas perdurantism holds that they persist by having distinct temporal parts at distinct times. Consider a symmetric fission case: organism Alpha divides into Beta and Gamma, each retaining half of Alpha's matter and continuous functional organization. An endurantist may respond that Alpha simply ceases to exist, with Beta and Gamma being genuinely new entities, thereby avoiding any contradiction in identity. A perdurantist, by contrast, must account for Alpha's four-dimensional 'worm' after division—whether it continues through Beta, through Gamma, through both (branching), or through neither.

Recently, some metaphysicians have proposed that perdurantism can avoid branching by treating pre-fission stages as related to exactly one post-fission branch via a primitive, non-mereological 'persistence relation,' the selection of which is determined not by intrinsic features of the branches but by an external indexical factor. Evaluate whether this proposal genuinely resolves the fission problem for perdurantism, or whether it merely relocates the difficulty. In particular, discuss whether endurantism's 'cessation' strategy avoids a structurally parallel problem regarding the selection of a successor, and whether the proposed primitive relation introduces an unexplained arbitrariness of its own.

Student answer: Endurantism maintains that an object exists fully at each moment, so in symmetric fission Alpha is present before division and absent afterward, while Beta and Gamma come into existence as new objects. This is coherent because identity is never violated—there is never a single object located in two places at once. Perdurantism, however, analyzes Alpha as a four-dimensional spacetime worm composed of temporal parts. After fission, both Beta and Gamma contain temporal parts that are spatiotemporally and causally continuous with Alpha's final pre-fission stage. This creates the branching problem: if both branches are parts of the same worm, then Alpha is identical to both Beta and Gamma, violating the transitivity of identity.

The proposed solution introduces a primitive persistence relation that connects Alpha's pre-fission stages to exactly one post-fission branch—say, Beta—through an external indexical factor rather than through intrinsic properties. Because the factor is external, it is not arbitrary in the way that selecting Beta based on, for example, being on the left side would be arbitrary. Therefore, the relation successfully blocks branching without requiring intrinsic differences between Beta and Gamma. This makes perdurantism more attractive than endurantism, because the endurantist's cessation response still fails to explain why Beta, rather than Gamma, should be treated as the 'true' successor if one wishes to preserve continuity, and it also cannot account for the fact that both successors inherit Alpha's properties. Consequently, the indexical perdurantist view resolves the fission problem cleanly and is philosophically preferable.
