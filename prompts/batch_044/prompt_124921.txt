I just came away from a long argument with a close friend about Parfit's hitchhiker case, and I want a diagnosis of why the disagreement never seems to resolve. The specific point of contention was whether the hitchhiker genuinely shows that rationality can require locally irrational behavior—refusing to open the box after the scan—or whether the case is structurally identical to the toxin puzzle with an unnecessary temporal asymmetry grafted onto it. My friend kept insisting that the alien's brain-scan introduces a branching problem: the agent who decides in advance and the agent who faces the box are not the same decision-maker in any robust sense, so the case is not about rational self-defeat but about which successor-entity should count as the agent for purposes of evaluation. I countered that this just relocates the problem into personal identity and that, once you fix a model of diachronic agency—whether linear, branching, or stage-theoretic—the rationality question becomes formal and answerable. But we kept talking past each other because rationality was sliding between global optimization over a life-plan, local expected-utility maximization, and compliance with a prior commitment mechanism. Looking at the literature, it seems this same triad of moves has been repeated for decades without convergence. So: is this stall substantive—hiding a disagreement about the ontology of future selves, about the formal scope of decision rules, or about which cases count as data—or is it largely verbal, with the agent and rational doing hidden disciplinary work? Is there a hidden formal distinction—say, between linear precommitment and branching commitment—that could dissolve it, or is the impasse a sign that the question is ill-posed because it presupposes a unitary agent where only a sequence exists? What, concretely, would count as progress: a formal model, a conceptual distinction about diachronic identity, or an admission that the two sides are answering different questions? Please ground the diagnosis in what proponents of each position actually say, not in a generic survey, and please separate what is settled—if anything is—from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic well; do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
