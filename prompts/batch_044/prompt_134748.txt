I am a postdoctoral researcher in affective neuroscience who has spent the past year modeling emotional regulation through interoceptive predictive processing—specifically hierarchical Gaussian filters in which affective valence is encoded as precision-weighted prediction errors across visceral and cortical levels, and in which policy selection is understood as the minimization of expected free energy over continuous state-space trajectories rather than over discrete symbolic actions. Recently, in discussions with philosophers of mind, I encountered Paul and Patricia Churchland’s eliminative materialism about beliefs and desires, and I want to determine whether that philosophical position is doing any work that my framework cannot already accomplish. Please restate the Churchlands’ core eliminativist thesis—that the ontology of folk psychology will be displaced by the taxonomy of a completed neuroscience—entirely in the vocabulary I use: hierarchical generative models, interoceptive inference, prior precision, prediction-error landscapes, and continuous dynamical state spaces that lack discrete propositional tokens. Be precise in the translation: render claims about the non-existence of propositional attitudes as claims about the absence of symbolic representations in my architecture; render claims about the theory-ladenness of mental-state observation as claims about how prior precision shapes the inference of latent states; and render the normative argument that belief-desire psychology is empirically inadequate as an argument about the explanatory superiority of continuous trajectory models over categorical mental-state ascriptions.

After the translation, provide a residue report. Which eliminativist moves survive intact—perhaps the denial that introspection reveals discrete mental representations? Which survive only with distortion—perhaps the claim that beliefs are theoretical entities analogous to phlogiston, given that predictive processing does employ high-level latent variables that function somewhat like hidden states? And which parts, if any, have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary—especially any appeal to the history of science, conceptual revolution, or the normative evaluation of false belief as distinct from merely suboptimal precision weighting? I want to know whether the residue indicates that my predictive-coding framework is missing something essential about mental content, or whether the philosophical debate is importing distinctions—such as that between ontological elimination and mere methodological redescription—that dissolve once everything is expressed in terms of precision-weighted interoceptive dynamics.
