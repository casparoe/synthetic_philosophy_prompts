Please present a rigorous argument that qualitative states—qualia—are not rendered conceptually incoherent by the possibility of "quining" them, using the familiar Chase-and-Sanborn intuition pump as your point of departure. First, reconstruct the scenario: two long-experienced tasters come to dislike the taste of a familiar coffee, yet report that they cannot locate any introspective difference between how the coffee tastes now and how they remember it tasting in the past, suggesting that the original quale has vanished without a trace and leaving no stable fact of the matter about its character. Then defend the coherence of qualia by advancing the following objection: any attempt to carry out this quining comparison requires a calibration standard, and that standard is either (i) a present qualitative state used as a benchmark, which makes the comparison circular, or (ii) a non-phenomenal representational or behavioral proxy, which substitutes representational content for qualitative character and therefore begs the question against the realist. Extend this to argue that the disappearance reported in the intuition pump is better explained as a failure of mnemonic reconstruction or conceptual framing rather than as an ontological dissolution of the quale itself, and that conflating epistemic inaccessibility with metaphysical incoherence is a category mistake. Finally, confront the strongest reply available to the quiner—that because qualia are radically private, no intersubjective or diachronic calibration is possible, leaving the realist with an unverifiable and therefore empty posit. In your answer, please state explicitly what argument or empirical finding would change your mind: identify the weakest link in the reasoning above and specify the consideration that, if established, would cause you to reject the defense of qualia's coherence.
