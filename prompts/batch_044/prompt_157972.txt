I came across a summary of Zhuangzi's butterfly dream—the well-known passage at the end of the second chapter of the Zhuangzi—that frames it as an early version of Cartesian dream skepticism. According to the summary, Zhuangzi wakes unsure whether he is a man who dreamed he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming it is Zhuangzi, and this demonstrates a perfect epistemic symmetry between waking and dreaming: since the two states are phenomenally indistinguishable, neither can serve as a privileged criterion for reality. The summary further claims that Zhuangzi's doctrine of wu hua, the transformation of things, should be interpreted not merely as a claim about impermanence but as a metaphysical leveling thesis—that all experiential states possess equal ontological status, so that appearance and reality collapse into one another. From this, the summary derives a normative conclusion: Zhuangzi advocates a form of epistemic quietism, holding that because we can never determine which state we inhabit, we ought to suspend all judgment about the external world and treat every perspective as equally valid. The summary even compares Zhuangzi to Berkeley, suggesting he is best read as an early phenomenalist who denies the mind-independent existence of material objects altogether.

This feels partly right to me—the phenomenological indistinguishability is certainly present—but also like it might be forcing Zhuangzi into modern skeptical categories he never intended. Is this summary fair? Specifically, does the butterfly passage actually target epistemic justification, or is its primary concern the fluidity of identity and the boundaries of the self? Is wu hua properly read as an ontological leveling of all states, or does it serve a different function in the text's broader argument about perspective? And does Zhuangzi's position really entail quietism and the denial of an external world, or does it point toward something closer to joyful acceptance of transformation? Please separate what is accurate from what is a subtle misreading or anachronistic import.
