I am preparing the framing lecture for a graduate seminar on metaphysics of science, and I want to get my conceptual bearings in order before I engage with the contemporary literature on dispositional properties. I keep returning to the classic problem cases—the electro-fink, where a disposition seems to vanish precisely when its triggering conditions are met, and the various masking scenarios in which a disposition is present but blocked from manifesting by an interfering factor—but I want to move past the standard dialectic and ask what an adequate theory actually owes us across the board. In particular, I have been working through an artificial but conceptually sharp case I will call System S: a synthetic catalytic structure that possesses a disposition to bind substrate A, yet this disposition is masked by a regulatory layer that activates only in the presence of substrate B. The twist is that B’s presence does not merely block the manifestation; it transforms the masking layer into an active inhibitor that structurally alters the catalytic base, so that when B is subsequently removed, the original disposition does not simply re-emerge but has been path-dependently modified. This looks like a recursive or nested mask, where masking is not a static blocker but a dynamic, history-sensitive transformation of the dispositional base itself, and I want any theory of dispositions to be able to say something coherent about structures like System S rather than treating all masking as a simple on-off overlay.

With that in mind, write me the desiderata: specify what any adequate theory of dispositions must be able to deliver. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or consideration behind it—whether drawn from fink-style disappearance, static masking, the recursive dynamics of System S, or broader metaphysical commitments such as avoiding circularity in analyses of manifestation conditions, preserving a real distinction between the disposition and its categorical base, or accommodating the intuition that dispositions ground genuine modal truths about what their bearers can do. Flag explicitly any pair of desiderata that you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable, and indicate which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which might be relaxed in exchange for theoretical elegance or ontological parsimony. Then, very briefly and without full expositions, score the three main contenders—the standard conditional analysis, a realist powers ontology of the kind associated with Mumford or Bird, and a dispositional essentialist view—against your list, noting for each where it succeeds, where it falters, and whether its failure is fatal or tolerable.
