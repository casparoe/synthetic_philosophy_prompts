Write the joint statement that a "ground-level" indirect normativist and a "transformative" indirect normativist could both sign after a long adversarial collaboration. By ground-level I mean: norms get their authority from what actual agents would endorse if they deliberated under improved but recognizably their own conditions (better info, less bias, but same basic values and cognitive limits). By transformative I mean: norms get authority only from what fully rationalized versions of agents would endorse, even if the reasoning is totally opaque to the actual people bound by the norms.

I want:
1. Numbered agreed claims -- about what indirect normativity is trying to do, why direct normativity is inadequate, what idealized deliberation is meant to filter out, and any shared commitments about the non-identity problem or about paternalism.
2. The disagreements table -- exact wording of each side's position that they'd both accept as fair to them, especially on whether transforming the agent in the idealization severs the justificatory link back to real people.
3. The cruxes -- I suspect one is whether justification has to be transparent or "available" to the actual agent to count as her reason, and another is how to treat cases where the idealized agent endorses a distributive rule that looks plainly unfair from the ground-level perspective.
4. For each crux, one concrete scenario both sides agree is a real test case (maybe something like a public-health mandate that saves lives but violates deeply held but arguably irrational commitments), with each camp's predicted verdict written out.
5. Say plainly which disagreements are substantive and which are just verbal confusion over "ideal agent" or "reason."

Don't hedge or list options neutrally -- take definite positions on what's genuinely at stake, and flag anyplace where one side is smuggling in direct normativity without admitting it. Keep it focused; no bibliographies or "see X for this." There's a typo or two in this prompt on purpose, don't fix them.
