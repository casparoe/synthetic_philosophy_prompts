I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently taken up philosophy seriously, and I have drafted the following argument for a seminar on Wittgenstein's On Certainty. It turns on what I call a calibration hinge—the unstated framework assumption in laboratory work that a reference instrument has not drifted since its last check. The argument seems to prove that hinge epistemology either forces an irrational dogmatism about the self or collapses entirely, yet each premise looks defensible in isolation. I would be grateful for a diagnosis.

(1) Wittgenstein holds that hinge propositions—such as that the earth has existed for many years, or that I have two hands—are not ordinary empirical beliefs but the fixed points around which doubt and justification turn; within a given language-game they are exempt from doubt.

(2) In scientific practice, every measurement protocol relies on a calibration hinge: the background assumption that the reference standard has not undergone an unobserved deviation.

(3) This calibration hinge is not itself tested within the experiment it enables; it belongs to the framework rather than to the body of claims being tested.

(4) By direct analogy, my belief that I am the same conscious subject who performed yesterday's calibration is not an empirical hypothesis about neural continuity, but a hinge that makes first-person data attribution and scientific responsibility possible.

(5) If a proposition is genuinely a hinge—exempt from doubt—then no localized empirical finding, such as a neuropsychological report of dissociative memory discontinuity, can rationally undermine it; to question it is not to correct an error within the framework but to abandon the framework itself.

(6) But if my observer-continuity hinge is thereby immune to all empirical evidence concerning my mental states, then a scientist with documented dissociation has no rational basis to revise her attribution of measurements, which violates the basic epistemic duty to respond to relevant evidence.

(7) Consequently, either observer-continuity is not a hinge at all—so Wittgenstein's category fails to capture first-person scientific practice—or hinges can in fact be rationally undermined by empirical findings, which means they are merely high-confidence empirical beliefs and the hinge/ordinary-belief distinction collapses.

(8) Therefore, hinge epistemology either sanctions an irrational dogmatism about the self in scientific work, or it offers no genuine distinction between framework conditions and ordinary empirical claims.

I am particularly reluctant to give up (1) and (4), since both seem essential to understanding how knowledge operates in both philosophy and laboratory science. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
