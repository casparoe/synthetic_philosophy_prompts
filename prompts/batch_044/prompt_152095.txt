I retired from running a biology lab a few years back and since then I've gotten weirdly into philosophy of belief, especially this self-locating stuff where you update just because you know it's Tuesday or you're copy number three. My friends and I tried to talk through the Sleeping Beauty problem over coffee but we kept just yelling 'halfer!' or 'thirder!' without knowing what we actually meant, so I want you to build a decision tree for us — not a quiz that asks 'which team are you on,' but one that sorts people by how they answer concrete cases.

Start with the usual setup: fair coin, wake Monday if heads, Monday and Tuesday if tails, amnestic drug so she can't tell the days apart. Then branch into some variations I cooked up: what if she learns it's Tuesday but still hasn't been told the coin result? What if on tails she gets woken every hour for a hundred hours with the same amnestic reset, but on heads she only wakes once? And what if she's told she's one of ten identical copies running in parallel, all with the same experience, and she knows this protocol? Each fork should ask whether her credance in heads shifts, whether the number of awakenings carries evidential weight, or whether being one of many copies changes anything. The leaves should name actual positions if they exist — halfers, thirders, maybe others — and for combinations nobody takes, note whether they're incoherent or just unexplored. Keep the branches logical rather than just listing philosphers, and flag any positions your tree can't seperate. We're not philosphers and can't search the web, so don't ask for citations or reading lists, and don't lean on obscure theories we wouldn't have heard of. About eight to ten decision points, plain language, and don't worry if there's a typo or two in my prompt — there probably is.
