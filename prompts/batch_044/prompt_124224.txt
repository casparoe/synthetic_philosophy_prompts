I've been in therapy for like three months now and my therapst keeps asking me to check in with my feelings, and honestly it's made me paranoid about whether I actually know what's going on in my head. So I built this argument and now I'm stuck.

(1) If I can mistake hunger for anger, then introspecion about emotional states is unreliable.
(2) There's no principled line between emotional states and cognitive ones -- they're both mental contents.
(3) So introspection is unreliable about beliefs, desires, and intentions too.
(4) If I'm unreliable about my beliefs, then I might be wrong about almost everything I take myself to think.
(5) But the one thing I seem to know for sure is that I'm conscious right now -- that's the classic Cartesian point.
(6) Yet consciousness is just another mental content I access through introspection.
(7) So if (3) is true, I shouldn't be sure I'm conscious either.
(8) But being unsure I'm conscious is absurd; it's the one thing I can't doubt.
(9) So (1) must be false and introspection about feelings is actually infallible.
(10) That can't be right either, because I literally misread my own irritation as sadness yesturday.

Where does this slip? I really want to keep (1) -- I've lived it -- and I'd rather keep (5) than (9), but I don't see how to have both without the contradiction. Is there a hidden scope shift between (3) and (4), or is the problem that consciousness isn't just another mental content in (6)?
