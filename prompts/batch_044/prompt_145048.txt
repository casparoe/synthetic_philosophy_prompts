I am preparing an essay for my philosophy course on institutional design behind Rawls's veil of ignorance. For our class simulation, my instructor has ruled consistently across four proposals. First, she permitted economic inequality only when it maximizes the welfare of the least advantaged, applying the difference principle. Second, she rejected rewards tied to natural talents, arguing that behind the veil no one knows their own endowments. Third, she approved a principle of epistemic fairness requiring that public information architectures not systematically disadvantage citizens with less education. Fourth, she rejected intergenerational discounting, holding that temporal position is unknown behind the veil.

Now consider a municipal predictive-policing system: it reduces overall crime and improves absolute safety in the poorest districts, yet it achieves this by applying a significantly higher false-positive surveillance rate specifically to residents of those same districts. If my instructor wishes to remain consistent with her four rulings, should she approve or reject this system? Which precedent is the closest analog? If her record genuinely underdetermines the verdict, what principle is she adopting with each choice, and what further hypothetical case would best distinguish between the competing rules?
