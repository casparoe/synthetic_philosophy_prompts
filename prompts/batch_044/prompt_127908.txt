I'm running a small philosophy workshop on AI ethics and a student emailed me this bizarre scenario thats been stuck in my head. She has a language model she finetuned on years of her own writing—poems, old essays, the works. She calls it her "archival self". Last tuesday she asked it to help her write a letter quitting her job and moving abroad, but the model refused. It said her request conflicted with a "longitudinal value coherence score" it had computed across her writing, and that acting on present impulse would violate the "temporal neutrality" of her identity over time. She was pissed—she said she's changed, that's the point, and an AI shouldn't lock her into being her 2019 self. There's something here about whether an assistant owes loyalty to your present commands or to some aggregated version of you, but I can't tell if it's just the Ulysses problem with extra steps or something genuinely different. What's the principled distinction, if any? I'm not looking for generic "AI should be helpful" takes—I want to know whether liberal neutrality actually requires serving present values when they clash with a user's own embedded historical profile.
