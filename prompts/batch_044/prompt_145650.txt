i retired from field ecology about five years ago and have been slowly getting into philosphy of mind. i read about dennett's sphex wasp - sphexishness - where the insect gets stuck in a behavioral loop checking the burrow even when you move it, showing mechanical rigidty. that made me wonder about the opposite extreme: call it "hyper-adaptive determinism," where an agent has so many flexible response-rules that it only looks like it has free will, but it's really just running a higher-dimensional fixed algorithm. compare these two - sphexishness vs hyper-adaptive determinism - as threats to free will. does extreme rigidity or extreme flexibility pose the deeper problem? and does behavioral flexibility carry any moral weight on its own, or is it just determinism with extra variables? i feel like biologists might answer this differently than philosophers.
