I'm building a taxonomy for a graduate philosophy seminar on the heterogeneity problem in hedonism—the challenge that the pleasure of tasting dark chocolate, the satisfaction of grasping a mathematical proof, and the affective relief of escaping danger seem to share no common phenomenal feature. Write a decision tree (not a flat list) that sorts responses to this problem. Start with the root question: is there a single phenomenal quality present in every case of pleasure? From there, branch by whether that quality is intrinsic or relational; whether heterogeneity is dissolved by treating pleasure as a determinable like color rather than a determinate like scarlet; whether non-qualitative accounts—pro-attitude, representational, or functional—avoid the problem altogether; and whether malicious or base pleasures count as genuinely pleasurable in the same sense as elevated ones. Each leaf should carry a position name (e.g., qualitative uniformitarianism, determinable hedonism, pure pro-attitude hedonism, representational-content view, or unnamed if the combination is unoccupied) and one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, especially positions that collapse once we allow family-resemblance relations among pleasures. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; construct the tree purely from the logical space of answers.
