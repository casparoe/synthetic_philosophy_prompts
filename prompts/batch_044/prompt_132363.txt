Please argue that Maimonides' take on animal sacrifice in the Guide isn't actually compatible with modern sentience-based animal ethics, even though people sometimes try to read it that way. His view is that sacrifice is a concession to human psychologcal weakness—people were too attached to idol-worship practices to drop them cold-turkey—so God channeled the impulse into monotheistic worship. The key point is that the animals' suffering isn't what's being weighed; it's entirely about human spiritual progress. That means you can't extract an intrinsic moral status for animals from his account without breaking the whole framwork. Address the objection that if God cares about human cruelty he must care about animal pain too. Start with your bottom line in the first sentence then reasoning, no preamble, and don't restate the question.
