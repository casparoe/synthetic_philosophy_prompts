I've been arguing with my roommate about whether quitting is always smart once you've already wasted time on something. I stayed in a bad job for almost a year after I realized it was wrong for me, and I kept telling myself it was because I'd already put in so much training — but now I'm wondering if that was just the sunk cost fallacy, or if there's actually something principled in not walking away from suffering you've already endured. So here are my beliefs, and I need someone to check if they actually fit together.

First, I think rational choices should only care about future costs and benefits — past investments are dead and shouldn't steer you. Second, I came up with this idea I call "trail loyalty": the feeling that leaving a path you've already walked partway is disrespectful to your past self, like the effort you already put in creates a kind of obligation to finish. Third, I think quitting too easily is a real character flaw, and sticking with hard things builds something good in you. Fourth, I also think emotions and "character" shouldn't have any place in practical reasoning at all — reason should just calculate outcomes, not honor feelings. Fifth, I believe in work or group projects there's a strategic value to finishing what you started, since people trust consistency, so sometimes continuing is smart even when the direct payoff is gone. Sixth, I think people overuse the "sunk cost" label and mix up actual commitments with just wasted money.

Do these hang together? I'm especially unsure whether "trail loyalty" is just a fancy excuse to violate the forward-looking rule, and whether saying perseverance is a virtue kills my claim that feelings don't matter. Also I wonder if the strategic-consistency point clashes with my first belief, or if that's only an apparant problem. I want an audit: rank the real tensions by how bad they are, say wich pairs only look bad, and tell me the cheapest single revision that fixes the most — but don't just assume I'll ditch the trail-loyalty thing, since I don't know yet wich commitment I'd actually drop.
