For my presentation in a graduate seminar on collective intentionality and social ontology, I want a consistency audit of my current package before I defend it. Here is where I have landed:

(i) I hold a reductionist account of shared intention in broadly Bratmanian terms: it is constituted by interlocking individual intentions, mutual responsiveness, and commitments to joint activity and mutual support, with no primitive or irreducible we-intention.

(ii) I take joint action to require shared intention in exactly this sense; parallel action with common knowledge of aligned ends does not suffice.

(iii) I think collective agents—corporations, committees, perhaps states—can bear genuinely non-derivative moral responsibility. The fault is genuinely at the collective level, not merely an aggregate of individual wrongs.

(iv) I treat the we-mode of intentionality as phenomenologically real but ontologically weightless: it describes how acting together feels, not a distinct metaphysical kind.

(v) I accept that institutional facts depend on collective acceptance, yet I think this acceptance is always decomposable into individual acceptance plus common belief about that acceptance, with no irreducible collective acceptance-state.

(vi) I maintain that every participant in joint action retains unilateral veto power: anyone can dissolve the activity by revising her own intentions, and exercising this power is never a betrayal of a collective-level obligation—only an individual change of mind.

(vii) I distinguish responsive compliance from coerced participation in what I call asymmetric joint actions: even when one agent directs and another complies, the activity counts as genuinely joint if the compliance is responsive to the director's intentions rather than forced; in such cases, however, primary moral responsibility lies with the directing agent rather than being distributed.

(viii) I deny that Gilbert-style plural subjecthood is a necessary condition for joint action; at best, it is a descriptive label for particularly stable patterns of interlocked intention.

Please audit whether these hang together. Specifically: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts only appear serious but dissolve on inspection, and for each real tension state the least costly revision and what it costs me. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally damaging. Trace precisely what my commitments commit me to, without lecturing on the literature or asking for reading lists. I have not decided which commitment I would drop if forced, so treat all as live.
