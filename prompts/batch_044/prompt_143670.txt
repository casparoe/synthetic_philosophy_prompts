I'm finalizing the conceptual framework for a workshop paper and need a consistency audit before I present. My commitments are: (1) luck egalitarianism is correct in holding that distributive justice requires neutralizing the effects of brute luck while preserving differences due to option luck; (2) the brute/option boundary should be fixed by a modal availability condition—an outcome is option luck only if the agent could have avoided it in a nearby possible world with the same character and information; (3) preferences formed through adaptive deprivation do not count as exercises of option luck, not because they fail the modal condition, but because they violate a responsiveness-to-reasons requirement on authentic choice; (4) the appropriate metric is capabilities, indexed not to actual preferences but to what the agent would value after critical reflection; and (5) equality has non-instrumental value specifically in eliminating arbitrary disadvantage, which is why the leveling-down objection misses the point. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are merely apparent, and what is the least costly revision? In particular, does (3) render (2) redundant for the central cases by substituting a normative criterion for the modal one? Does (4)'s reliance on critical reflection undermine (3), given that critical reflection itself may be adaptively shaped? And does (5)'s appeal to arbitrary disadvantage presuppose the very brute/option distinction that (2) and (3) are meant to ground, creating circularity? Rank the tensions by severity, specify which commitment should be revised first if consistency is required, and describe—not cite—the form of argument that would dissolve the deepest conflict.
