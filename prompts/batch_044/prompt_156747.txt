I'm preparing a consistency audit for a philosophy-of-mind reading group and want to check whether my profile hangs together before I present it. Here is where I stand:

(1) Constitutive panpsychism is correct: the macro-consciousness of a human subject is literally composed of, and identical to, the structured aggregate of micro-subjects associated with fundamental physical entities, bound together by a primitive phenomenal fusion relation.
(2) This fusion relation is non-causal, non-spatial, and metaphysically necessary whenever the relevant micro-physical arrangement is present; it is not an emergent law of nature but a direct compositional principle.
(3) I reject both cosmopsychism and emergent panpsychism on the grounds that macro-consciousness must be fully decomposed into micro-level subjects with no explanatory "free lunch" — there is no residue that the micro-level fails to account for.
(4) Phenomenal properties are essentially intrinsic and non-relational; nevertheless, the specific determinacy of a macro-subject's phenomenal character depends essentially on the fusion relation holding among its constituent parts.
(5) The macro-zombie scenario is conceivable — there is no conceptual entailment from macro-physical facts to macro-phenomenal facts — but metaphysically impossible, since the fusion relation is necessitated by the micro-physical base.
(6) No artificial system built from engineered, non-fundamental substrates (e.g., silicon chips) could ever be conscious, because consciousness requires the presence of fundamental physical entities with intrinsic phenomenal properties arranged in fusion, and such substrates lack the requisite micro-subjective base regardless of functional architecture.
(7) In split-brain cases, I take the fusion relation to be locally disrupted rather than globally suspended, yielding two distinct macro-subjects without altering the total population of micro-subjects.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to conflict? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each specify what commitment has to give and what that revision costs. In particular, examine whether my rejection of "free lunch" explanations in (3) conflicts with my acceptance of a primitive fusion relation in (2); whether my claim that phenomenal properties are non-relational in (4) is compatible with the claim that macro-phenomenal determinacy depends on the relational fusion structure; and whether my denial of artificial consciousness in (6) can survive my commitment to constitutive composition in (1) and (2) given that silicon chips are ultimately composed of fundamental particles. Do not signal which revision I should prefer; deliver an audit ordered by severity, with the least costly fix for each tension noted, and identify the single revision that would clean up the most problems at once.
