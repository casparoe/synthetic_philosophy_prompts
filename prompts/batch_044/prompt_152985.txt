For an advanced metaphysics seminar, design a decision tree that maps the logical space of ethical positions arising from panpsychism in fundamental physics. Take as the entry point the claim that elementary physical fields possess minimal phenomenal properties. Each node must be a concrete diagnostic question—phrased so a student can answer it without knowing the labels—such as: (i) does the combination problem yield macro-subjects? (ii) if so, is moral status restricted to them? (iii) if not, does near-zero valence aggregate linearly across micro-subjects? (iv) does sheer cardinality alone (e.g., a decoherent quantum configuration with 10^23 non-combined, minimally sentient units, none forming a macro-subject) generate an obligation to intervene? The leaves should carry a position name—use standard labels where they exist, otherwise mark them 'unnamed'—and each must include one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Include at least one logically possible combination that remains unoccupied, with a brief diagnosis of whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that your tree genuinely cannot separate. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or references to specific publications; build the taxonomy entirely from conceptual distinctions.
