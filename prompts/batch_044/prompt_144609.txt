The brochure should contain standard sections—"Our Philosophy," "The Treatments," "Guest Testimonials," and "Booking & Ethics Disclosure"—and should open with the facility's core value proposition in the very first sentence, followed immediately by the brochure text itself with no preface, title explanation, or commentary. The target of the satire is the logical extremity of negative utilitarianism and suffering-focused ethics: specifically, the claim that suffering carries radically greater moral weight than pleasure, that non-existence is not a deprivation but a successful absence of harm, and that the highest ethical achievement is the prevention of potential suffering rather than the promotion of flourishing. To prevent the piece from being a generic anti-natalist joke, embed the following niche conceptual distinctions into the copy as if they were standard spa service tiers: (1) the split between "classical negative utilitarianism," which allows suffering-reduction to trade off against pleasure at scale, and "suffering-focused ethics," which treats suffering reduction as lexically prior with no compensatory calculus; (2) the "reproductive asymmetry"—the idea that there is an asymmetry in moral status between creating a being who will suffer and creating one who will be happy—reframed as a choice between the retreat's "Genesis Wing" (closed to new bookings) and "The Null Suite"; and (3) the "pinprick objection" and its standard reply, presented as an in-house debate about whether the signature "Micro-Absence Therapy" is over-engineered for trivial harms. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to named scholars. Do not quote real texts; paraphrase any philosophical claims without quotation marks. Maintain sustained irony: a specialist should recognize that the author understands the actual distinctions being exaggerated, and a general reader should be able to tell what is being ironized without any winking.
