Write a parody of an international technical standard (in the deadpan style of an ISO-style or ASTM specification document) that establishes formal protocols for Comparative Atrocity Assessment (CAA). The document should be formatted as numbered clauses, definitions, normative references, and annexes, approximately 600 to 700 words, written in the voice of an imaginary standards body—the International Bureau for Moral Catastrophe Metrics (IBMCM)—and should target the real-world practice of ranking or quantifying historical evils through statistical measures such as adjusted mortality figures, demographic displacement ratios, and suffering-duration indices.

The satire must remain deadpan throughout: never break character to announce that the premise is absurd. Instead, exaggerate actual features of comparative moral discourse—for example, debates over whether intentionality should be weighted more heavily than scale, the problem of double-counting victims across overlapping regimes, and the administrative demand for inter-rater reliability when historians dispute casualty figures. Inject a fictional but conceptually coherent measurement framework: require the document to reference, in serious technical language, an invented Brennan-Chen Weighted Harm Coefficient (which applies a diminishing-returns factor to repeated suffering within a single population) and a No-Comparison Exemption Clause that permits certain events to be certified as Category Omega and thereby removed from comparative ranking on the grounds that comparison itself inflicts secondary harm. The bureaucratic form should quietly take substantive positions—for instance, defining moral obscenity not as an emotional reaction but as a measurable deviation from inter-subjective consensus on harm weights—without ever presenting them as opinions.

Include at least one Annex B: Worked Example that applies the standard to a comparison between two well-known historical catastrophes (use real events, such as the Holocaust and the Rwandan genocide, or the transatlantic slave trade and the Holodomor). The example should show step-by-step coefficient calculation, identify where the metric breaks down, and demonstrate how the Exemption Clause is invoked. Through pure procedural logic, the example should reveal why the attempt to rank the events collapses into contradiction or moral offense, yet the document must present this collapse as a successful calibration.

The critical force should come from the accuracy of the bureaucratic mimicry and from the internal consistency of the invented metrics, not from cartoonish exaggeration of the events themselves. A reader familiar with debates in comparative genocide studies, moral philosophy, or the historiography of mass violence should recognize the real arguments being satirized—especially the tension between quantitative comparison as an instrument of moral clarity and comparison as an obscenity—while a reader unfamiliar with the literature should still find the document readable as a dark absurdist artifact. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote from real texts; if you reference an actual publication or authority, paraphrase it in bureaucratic language without quotation marks. Keep the tone formal and polished.
