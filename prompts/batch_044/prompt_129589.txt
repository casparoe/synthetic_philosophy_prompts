I'm taking an advanced seminar on modal metaphysics, and we've been stuck for weeks on a question that seems simple but refuses to resolve: can one and the same individual exist in more than one possible world, or are other-worldly versions of a person merely counterparts—numerically distinct entities that resemble the original closely enough to ground our modal talk? The broadly Lewisian position treats individuals as world-bound entities; modal properties like being necessarily human are explained relationally, through patterns of resemblance among counterparts across worlds. The opposing view, often associated with a Kripkean approach to modality, insists that transworld identity is primitive: when we say I could have become a musician, we are talking about how this very individual might have turned out, not about a similar stand-in in a distant world. Both sides seem to have answers for the standard puzzles—essential origins, the logic of modal predication, even fission cases—yet the seminar hasn't moved an inch.

My working theory is that the disagreement is not primarily ontological but concerns explanatory priority and hidden verbal divergence: one camp wants to reduce modal talk to non-modal similarity relations, while the other treats modality as primitive and uses identity across worlds as a primitive tracking mechanism. But I want to test whether there's a deeper fracture. In particular, I've been thinking about what we might call modal overlap—scenarios in which two possible worlds share an initial spatiotemporal segment before diverging, so that an individual in the shared region appears to have two genuinely distinct futures within the same modal space. For the counterpart theorist, divergence naturally produces separate counterparts; for the transworld identity theorist, overlap threatens the uniqueness of identity, forcing either a denial of shared segments, a splitting of the original, or an abandonment of the one-one constraint on identity. Is this overlap problem the hidden engine driving the stall, or is it merely a technical curiosity that both sides can accommodate with minor adjustments?

Please diagnose the disagreement: is it verbal, with identity across worlds meaning different things to each side? Is it substantive, turning on whether modal properties are intrinsic or relational? Is it about bedrock intuitions—perhaps about whether personal continuity is more fundamental than world-boundedness—that neither side can argue for? Or is it a case of two research programs answering different questions—one semantic, one metaphysical—under a single heading? And given your diagnosis, tell me what, if anything, would actually count as progress. Would it take a formal result in the logic of counterpart relations, a conceptual distinction that dissolves the overlap problem, or a demonstration that one view systematically fails to preserve ordinary judgments about counterfactual identity?
