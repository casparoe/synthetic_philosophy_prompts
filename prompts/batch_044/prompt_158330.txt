At the pub last Tuesday my mates Maya and Leo got into it over Hofstadter's MU-puzzle and whether JOOTSing actually solves anything. I just sat there with my pint, couldn't call it either way.

Maya started: she reckons JOOTSing — jumping out of the system — is the only reason humans can solve the MU-puzzle at all. The formal rules of the MIU system never let you derive MU, but we spot the pattern by stepping outside and seeing the system's structure as an object. So consciousness is essentially this capacity to make our own reasoning rules into things we can inspect.

Leo said that's basically mystical hand-waving. If you're "outside" the system, you're either in a bigger system or you're not doing formal reasoning at all. He said Hofstadter just renamed the problem: instead of explaining how we transcend formal limits, he gave it a catchy acronym and called it solved. Leo thinks the real explanation is just that human brains use heuristics that aren't axiom-bound, not some magical leap.

Maya came back: it's not magical, it's recursive self-modelling. The brain represents its own representations, so it can treat the MIU rules as data rather than as binding constraints. She even suggested we could build this in — call it a JO-axiom — that lets a formal system reference its own derivation rules and suspend them for specific strings. If we did that, the system could derive MU without breaking consistency, which proves the jump is formalizable, not mystical.

Leo said that's exaclty his point: once you formalise the jump as a JO-axiom, you're not jumping out anymore, you're just enlarging the formal system. The "outside" view is now inside a bigger box. So JOOTSing either collapses into Gödelian embedding — there's always a larger system — or it appeals to something non-formal that can't be mechanised. He asked why, if we're so good at jumping out, people still get stuck on the MU-puzzle for ages. Maya said we get stuck when we confuse object-language rules with meta-language inspection — we forget to apply the jump. Leo replied that sounds like saying "people fail to be intelligent," which isn't an explanation.

They turned to me and asked who'd won. I genuinely don't know.
