You chair the Foundational Research Funding Panel of the National Institute for Digital Ontology. A consortium of philosophers of logic and computer scientists has submitted a proposal entitled ReProNet: Distributed Propositional Representation Without Primitive Unity. The project aims to construct a large-scale knowledge graph that explicitly abandons the idea of propositions as unified entities. Rather than encoding a claim such as Aristotle teaches Alexander as a single structured unit with an internal relation binding subject to predicate, ReProNet represents every piece of content as an open-ended chain of binary relational nodes — Aristotle-related-to-teaching-related-to-Alexander-related-to-Aristotle-related-to... — with no terminal glue that stops the chain and constitutes one determinately true or false unit. The applicants contend that Bradley's regress is not a fatal objection but a feature: in a sufficiently dense network, the global topology of relations supplies all the identity conditions a proposition needs, so that local unification becomes redundant. Opponents on the panel argue that without some analog of Bradley's internal relation or primitive propositional unity, the system cannot distinguish one specific claim from an infinitely extendible but determinately different one, and that distributing the regress across nodes merely obscures the failure to explain how discrete thoughts are possible at all. The Panel must decide whether to approve the grant outright, approve it subject to conditions (for example, a mandatory philosophical audit or a restricted pilot phase), or reject it. Draft the Panel's formal decision memo, addressed to the Institute's Executive Board. It should present a clear recommendation with a concise bottom line, set out the philosophical stakes in terms accessible to non-specialist board members, address the strongest consideration supporting the option you reject with genuine fairness, and maintain an even, institutional tone throughout. Write in British English, approximately 750 words, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or suggested further reading.
