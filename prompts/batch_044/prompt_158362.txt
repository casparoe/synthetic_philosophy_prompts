I'm constructing a pedagogical sorting instrument for an advanced philosophy-of-logic workshop, and I want a decision tree—not a named-label quiz—that maps what a respondent actually believes about the Liar paradox onto the major theoretical families. The tree should be built bottom-up from the logical space of commitments rather than from a checklist of theories. Start with concrete judgment tasks: present specific sentences (a direct self-referential liar, a strengthened liar that denies having any truth-value, a two-language pairing in which sentence A in language L₁ asserts that B in L₂ is false while B asserts that A is false, and a revision-theoretic sequence in which an initial assignment of falsehood to the liar is later revised). Each branching node should ask the respondent to choose among two or three specific verdicts—e.g., whether the sentence is meaningful but permanently ungrounded, whether the contradiction requires abandoning bivalence globally or only locally, or whether a meta-language can assign a stable value without spawning a revenge liar provided it lacks self-referential resources—so that someone can navigate the tree without knowing terms like dialetheism or hierarchical separation.

The tree must locate at least six leaves: a strict hierarchical separation view; a fixed-point or partial-grounding view; a paraconsistent view that accepts true contradictions; a deflationary or expressivist view that treats the liar as a pragmatic failure rather than a semantic one; a revision-theoretic view that allows iterative re-evaluation; and a contextualist or gap-theoretic view that denies a single context-independent evaluation. For each unoccupied combination—such as a view that combines strict hierarchical separation with global acceptance of true contradictions—provide a brief diagnosis of whether the emptiness stems from incoherence, conceptual instability, or mere historical neglect. Also flag any pairs of leaves that the tree's questions cannot cleanly separate, explaining which shared commitments cause the overlap.

In addition, include one niche diagnostic branch that tests commitment to what I will call inter-level preservation: the claim that a liar sentence evaluated at object-language level n can be determinately assigned a value at meta-level n+1 without generating a revenge liar, but only on the condition that the meta-language is structurally barred from containing its own self-referential apparatus. Use this branch to determine whether a respondent's hierarchical instincts are absolute or conditional. The audience is graduate students who understand basic logic but may not have memorized the literature; therefore, do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or named authorities, and do not frame questions as asking whether one agrees with a specific named philosopher. The final deliverable should present the branching logic in flowchart-style prose, followed by a scoring key that translates answer patterns into the six target positions, with explicit notes on ambiguous or hybrid patterns.
