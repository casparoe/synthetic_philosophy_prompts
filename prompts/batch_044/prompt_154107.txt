I've been arguing with my friends about those big history books—Harari's Sapiens, Graeber and Wengrow's The Dawn of Everything, that kind of thing—and wether they actually explain social stability or just tell neat stories. Specifically I'm stuck comparing Harari's claim that large societies hold together through shared fictional myths, like money or nations, which he calls intersubjective realities, with Graeber and Wengrow's argument that many early societies deliberately switched between hierarchical and egalitarian modes, including rituals where kings were mocked or symbolically overthrown, almost like hierarchy was performed part-time.

My concrete worry is about states that seem to run both scripts at once. Take ancient Mesopotamia or the Mississippian cultures: they had massive bureacratic infrastructure—irrigation, taxes, labor coordination—which sounds like it needs everyone to buy into a consistent story about authority. But they also had regular festivals where the ruler was humiliated, replaced, or parodied, which looks like the society was actively undermining the same hierarchy it depended on. Can Harari's myth-model make sense of that? If the shared fiction is what keeps people cooperating, wouldn't these ritual inversions weaken or contradict the fiction? Or does Graeber and Wengrow's seasonal-switch model do better—maybe people weren't buying a permanent myth at all, but treating authority like a seasonal role?

I'm also wondering if the two can be mashed together. Maybe the intersubjective fiction isn't 'the king is divine and eternal' but rather 'authority is temporary and reversible,' which lets people tolerate hierarchy because they know it has an off-switch. But then doesn't that undermine Harari's idea that shared fictions work best when they feel permanent and inevitable? I'd like you to compare these two frameworks head-on on this specific tension—stability through contradiction—not just summarize them. Which gives the better account? And if you think they can be reconciled, what would the hybrid theory predict about a modern analog, like a corporation that runs as a strict bureacracy for nine months and then holds a month-long carnival where all ranks are flipped? Would that be sustainable under either view?
