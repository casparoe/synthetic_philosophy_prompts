Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, two refrains rotating through an ABA rhyme scheme—spoken in the first person by the symbol I as conceived in Douglas Hofstadter's strange-loop account of selfhood. Choose two fixed refrain lines: the first asserting that the speaker is only a pattern of neural activation, the second asserting that this same pattern bends back upon itself in self-reference and thereby becomes a subject. Each appearance of the first refrain should be embedded in imagery of substrate—synaptic gaps, electrochemical noise, decay—while each appearance of the second should be embedded in imagery of reference and naming—pronouns, mirror reflections, signatures that point back to the signer. The form's enforced repetition should enact the loop itself: the substrate line returns like a baseline that never grounds anything final, the subject line returns like a pointer that never reaches an external object. In the closing quatrain, place the two refrains in adjacent lines without resolution, because the loop is tangled rather than circular: there is no bottom level that explains away the symbol, and no transcendent level that escapes the substrate. Keep the substrate silent—present only as physical imagery, never speaking—so that the poem dramatizes the gap between levels that makes the loop strange. Do not resolve the tension with a moral about consciousness or a reductionist shrug; end with both claims coexisting in the same breath, leaving the paradox intact exactly where the theory places it.
