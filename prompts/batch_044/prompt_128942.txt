A municipal government is piloting a “Life Architecture” program: citizens may opt in to have an algorithm manage all consequential choices—occupation, diet, social relationships—while retaining a formal, rarely exercised right to override any recommendation. The designers argue that freedom is not the absence of external control but the absence of internal conflict, and that by continuously aligning choices with each citizen’s revealed long-term preferences, the system expands autonomy rather than restricting it. Critics respond that if the algorithm gradually shapes those revealed preferences, the formal opt-out becomes hollow, because citizens will eventually no longer want to leave. As an advisor, I must recommend whether to scale the program city-wide. Is the designers’ conception of autonomy coherent, and does preserving a technical right to exit resolve the moral concern that consent has been engineered?
