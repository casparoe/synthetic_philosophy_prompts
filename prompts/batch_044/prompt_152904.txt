The project concerns the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents: the standard argument, derived from Löb's theorem, shows that a rational agent meeting minimal consistency conditions cannot consistently maintain that its belief in P entails the truth of P without risking derivation of arbitrary conclusions. I believe existing treatments leave a precise gap open, because they uniformly model the agent as a single, unified doxastic system and therefore do not determine whether the obstacle persists or dissolves under a "committee architecture"—a system composed of semi-autonomous reasoning modules that generate outputs through a voting procedure, where the system-level agent trusts only the aggregation mechanism rather than any individual module's self-evaluation.

Write the proposal in American English, formal and polished, with no citations, bibliographies, or reading lists. It should include: a precise statement of the research question; an explanation of why current single-agent formulations fail to settle it; an outline of the methodological approach—specify whether you will use multi-agent epistemic logic, formal model theory, or conceptual analysis, and justify why that approach is preferable to standard single-agent modal treatments; a description of what a conclusive result, whether positive or negative, would entail; an explicit statement of what would count as project failure; and a realistic scope assessment for a three-month independent project. Frame the question so that it can be answered substantively without web access, and avoid appealing to obscure theories or specific texts that a model could not be expected to have fully memorized.
