Here is an argument my study group constructed about Dennett’s real patterns and the three stances, and it now seems to prove that either physical causal closure fails or the intentional stance is ontologically redundant. (1) For Dennett, a pattern counts as real only if it supports predictions that are more compressed than a complete physical-level description. (2) From the physical stance, every event is determined by microphysical laws, so any reliable predictive pattern must in principle be derivable from those laws. (3) The design stance predicts by attributing functions; the intentional stance predicts by attributing beliefs and desires. (4) If a pattern is detectable only from a higher stance and not from the physical stance, then it is causally inert, because the physical laws alone fix behaviour. (5) Yet Dennett holds that real patterns are causally efficacious — they feature in explanations that genuinely work. (6) Therefore any real pattern revealed by the design or intentional stance must also be present, at least derivatively, in the physical description. (7) That means the higher stances never reveal patterns absent from physics; they merely provide compressed redescriptions. (8) If so, stance-relativity collapses into stance-independent reality seen at different granularities, and the intentional stance carries no independent explanatory weight. (9) But Dennett claims the intentional stance reveals patterns that are genuinely there and invisible from below. (10) So he must either abandon stance-relativity or deny causal closure. Where does this go wrong? I would rather preserve (1) and (5), and I am open to revising (4) or (7), but I want to know exactly what each revision costs.
