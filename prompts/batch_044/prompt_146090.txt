Please draft a take-home essay question for an upper-level philosophy of history seminar, together with an approximately eight-hundred-word model answer and a marking scheme, addressing whether collective memory or the historical record should prevail when the two conflict over the cause and scale of a foundational community event. The question must be designed so that it cannot be answered merely by summarizing standard accounts associated with Halbwachs or Nora, and instead should require students to work through a specific hypothetical: a community's oral tradition describes a catastrophic flood as an act of divine punishment that forged collective identity, whereas newly digitized administrative records and tree-ring data indicate a localized drought followed by human migration patterns that contradict both the scale and the moral framing of the tradition. Students should be asked to determine whether mnemonic sovereignty or evidentiary correction ought to govern public commemoration and school curricula, engaging with the idea that memory serves present solidarity rather than archival fidelity, while history claims authority through epistemic transparency. The model answer should make the specific move of recognizing that the conflict involves incommensurable goods—identity preservation versus causal accuracy—rather than a simple error to be corrected, and should argue that resolution depends on a third-order judgment about the institutional context, whether legal, pedagogical, or museological. The marking scheme should be presented entirely in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, allocating marks across five criteria—clear distinction between memory and history, normative handling of the conflict, application to the hypothetical scenario, the incommensurable-goods move, and critical evaluation—each assigned specific point values that sum to twenty-five, with brief notes on typical errors such as treating the issue as purely epistemic rather than institutional. Please present the question, the model answer, and the marking scheme as continuous prose only, in the manner of a formal letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold formatting, or tables.
