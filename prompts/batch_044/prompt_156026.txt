Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a metaphysical grounding realist, who holds that there is one unified relation of ontological priority running across physical, mental, and normative domains, and a grounding pluralist, who holds that fundamentality fragments into domain-internal ordering relations—physical, logical, normative, mathematical—between which no cross-domain metaphysical priority holds. Restrict the collaboration to the status of normative truths: specifically, whether a normative fact (for example, that causing unnecessary suffering is pro tanto wrong) can be genuinely grounded in non-normative descriptive properties, or whether normative grounding is autonomous and irreducible to any descriptive base. The deliverable should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both parties can sign, covering the formal features they agree grounding must have (addressing irreflexivity, asymmetry, and whether transitivity holds without exception), the explanatory successes that any adequate account must preserve in science and ethics, and the shared failure conditions—such as circularity, explanatory vacuity, or ontological explosion—that both regard as fatal; (2) a disagreements table that states, in wording each side accepts for its own position, the precise metaphysical, conceptual, or methodological points of divergence, including whether cross-domain grounding violates a no-free-lunch constraint on ontological commitment; (3) at least three cruxes—sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party—including one on whether evolutionary explanations of normative belief function as grounding explanations or as debunking threats, and one on whether approximate physical realization of a normative structure undermines or supports the autonomy of normative grounding; (4) for each crux, one concrete case, argument, or piece of evidence both sides agree is relevant, together with each side's explicit prediction of what that case demonstrates about the crux. Where part of the dispute turns out to be verbal—say, about whether fundamental means ungrounded or explanatorily basic within a domain—identify that part and explain why the wording difference does not carry metaphysical weight. Format the output as the numbered agreed-claims section followed by the disagreements table, then the crux analysis.
