Write an essay addressing the controversy over whether cultural values can function as genuine causes of large-scale institutional change, using Max Weber's thesis on the Protestant ethic and the emergence of modern capitalism as your central case study. Rather than summarizing Weber's historical narrative, focus on the philosophical problem of explaining with values: how can normative commitments—such as the ascetic conception of worldly vocation—figure in causal explanations of economic outcomes without being reducible to material conditions, technological change, or individual psychology? Weigh at least two competing philosophical interpretations of Weber's argument. On one side, consider the reading that treats religious values as altering the selection environment for economic practices—analogous to evolutionary selection rather than mechanical force—thereby exercising downward causation on institutional development. On the other side, consider the reading that treats Weber's account as an exercise in interpretive understanding (Verstehen), one that clarifies subjective motivation without competing with, or substituting for, materialist causal explanations. Reach and defend a specific conclusion about whether culture, construed in this way, can be a cause in its own right. In your answer, clearly separate what is relatively settled among scholars of Weber and social explanation—such as Weber's explicit rejection of both monocausal idealism and monocausal materialism—from what remains genuinely contested, including whether selection-environment models actually preserve an autonomous causal role for values or merely redescribe compatibility conditions. Do not present any contested interpretation—particularly the claim that Weber advanced a purely idealist or monocultural explanation—as though it were the established consensus.
