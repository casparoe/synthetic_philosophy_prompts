I am preparing a consistency check for an independent study on the philosophy of time, and I would like an audit of the following commitments. First, I hold a B-theoretic view: I deny any objective passage or moving present, and I regard past and future events as equally real. Second, I take the thermodynamic arrow to be the only fundamental temporal asymmetry—entropy increases in one direction and not the other, and no other physical fact distinguishes earlier from later. Third, I believe causal relations are grounded in this entropic gradient rather than being primitive: causes precede effects because cause-states have lower entropy than effect-states. Fourth, I think that in a hypothetical region where entropy systematically decreased, observers there should correctly reverse our temporal labels, treating what we call the future as their past, since the arrow is locally determined by entropy. Fifth, I also maintain that spacetime has an objective causal structure—light cones determine an absolute before and after—that is wholly independent of thermodynamic conditions. Sixth, I regard the psychological arrow, including memory and anticipation, as fully explained by local entropy increases in the brain, leaving no separate mental time-direction.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look incompatible, and for each real conflict what the cheapest revision would be and which commitment it costs. I want them ordered by severity rather than treated as equally bad. I am especially uncertain whether my B-theoretic denial of flow conflicts with accepting objective asymmetries, and whether grounding causation in entropy can survive my claim about an absolute spacetime structure. Please write your response as continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a formal letter.
