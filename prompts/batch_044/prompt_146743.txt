I am drafting the philosophical architecture for a novel about aging, consent, and the self across time, and before I commit my protagonist to a particular view I want a rigorous consistency audit on the commitments I intend her to hold jointly.

Here is the set:

(1) An advance directive written by a fully competent person binds that same person after severe dementia, not on the ground that the demented stage is a different person, but because personal identity is sustained by a first-personal narrative thread that the earlier self legitimately extends into the future.

(2) I reject branching or fission accounts of identity; I hold that there is one continuous subject, and I treat autonomy across time as a kind of contract that a single agent makes with herself.

(3) If, however, the later self—while still rational and not demented—develops values radically at odds with those in the directive (for instance, finding dignity in a state of dependency that the earlier self deemed intolerable), the directive should yield to present autonomy.

(4) I maintain that the authority of the earlier self is strong in medical and bodily-integrity decisions but weak to nonexistent in aesthetic, relational, or vocational choices, since the latter are precisely the domains in which a processual self legitimately revises its narrative.

(5) I also think that seeing persons as processes rather than static essences undermines any rigid, timeless contract, yet I still wish to defend the binding force described in (1).

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be? In particular, address whether (1) and (3) actually conflict once we distinguish incompetence from competent value transformation, and whether (2)'s contract model can survive (5). Order the real tensions by severity, specify for each what the least costly revision would be and what it costs, and tell me which single change would resolve the greatest number of conflicts without collapsing the overall view. I want discrimination rather than blanket condemnation: some of these commitments may sit together more comfortably than they first appear.
