For my advanced seminar on autonomy and political theology, I need a decision tree that classifies interpretations of Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor parable—not by asking which character a reader sympathizes with, but by sorting them through concrete normative commitments. The tree should open with whether human freedom retains intrinsic value when its exercise reliably produces suffering and error; then branch through whether cognitive and affective limitations make autonomy self-defeating for most people, whether legitimate authority can rest on manufactured rather than explicit consent if welfare is thereby secured, and whether the Inquisitor's diagnosis of human weakness is correct even if his remedy violates some further constraint. Each leaf should identify a named interpretive position (for instance, Ivan's rebellious affirmation of freedom, Alyosha's silent acquiescence in mystery, the Inquisitor's own paternalist calculus, or a contemporary liberal analog) or mark an unoccupied combination with a one-sentence diagnosis—whether incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. The instrument should include at least one pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate, and that pair should be flagged explicitly. Phrase every branching question so that a student can answer it without already knowing which interpretation it leads to. Do not include bibliographies, citation requests, or appeals to scholarship the student could not reconstruct from the parable's own logic.
