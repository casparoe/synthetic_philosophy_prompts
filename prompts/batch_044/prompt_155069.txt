I am a PhD student in moral and political philosophy co-facilitating a graduate workshop on intergenerational justice and rational choice. For about 14 philosophy and related-field graduate students with 80 minutes available, please design a runnable classroom simulation—complete with roles, rules, a minute-by-minute timeline, and a debrief—in American English. The goal is to make participants feel the specific conceptual pressure of pure time preference (discounting future utility solely because it is future, independent of risk, growth, or diminishing marginal returns) rather than treating it as an abstract parameter in a welfare function.

Please inject the following niche framing into the mechanics: the “present” and “future” should be two physically co-present subgroups in the room separated only by an epistemic barrier—they can observe one another but are contractually forbidden from negotiating intertemporal transfers. The group must collectively vote on a “pure rate” that mechanically discounts any payoff directed across the barrier, applied symmetrically in the rules but experienced asymmetrically because of assigned temporal position. The payoff structure should create a coordination failure that the participants discover through action before anyone names it.

Include: (1) the physical setup and role assignments; (2) the rules and incentives that generate the failure; (3) the timeline; (4) a debrief that maps specific moments in the simulation onto the distinction between pure discounting and other discounting reasons (risk, growth, opportunity cost); (5) an honest assessment of which feature of pure time preference the simulation cannot reproduce and must instead be supplied by the facilitator; and (6) a warning about what will predictably go wrong in running the activity and how to handle it. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations, and do not ask participants to quote or reference specific texts. The conceptual point should emerge from what happens in the room, not from explanation afterward.
