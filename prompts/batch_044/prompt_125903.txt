Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets closing in a quatrain, rhyme scheme A1 b A2 with two refrains alternating—spoken from the position of the absent-minded driver at the exact moment he reaches an exit he cannot identify. Let the first refrain be the action mapped to the first node: “Continue; this is not yet the turn.” Let the second refrain be the action mapped to the second node: “Exit now; this is the way home.” The form itself should carry the philosophical weight: because the driver lacks the identifying information that would distinguish the two nodes, the refrains must eventually be uttered in the same location, and the closing quatrain should force both lines together side by side, making visible the collapse of his ex ante plan into a single ex post choice. The poem should be accurate to the decision-theoretic structure—indistinguishable nodes, the failure of conditional planning without identification, and the tension between evidential and causal reasoning about which exit this might be—without citing any author or text. Compose the non-refrain lines in unrhymed iambic pentameter so that the strict repetition of the refrains does the conceptual labor while the language remains natural. Do not resolve the paradox; end with the two commands suspended in the same space, leaving the driver between them.
