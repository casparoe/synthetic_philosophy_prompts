hey so my study group cooked up this argument about Lewis-style modal realism and now we're stuck. (1) on this view every world is concrete and causally closed off, and "actual" works like an indeces like "here". (2) so take any decision i face — wether to take a job in berlin. there's a world where my counterpart takes it, and one where he doesn't, both fully real. (3) now if my counterparts share all my mental properties and are related to me by the counterpart reltion, then in the relevant psychological sense they're "me" distributed across worlds. (4) so rational self-interest shouldn't just care about this-world-me, but about the total welfare summed over all my counterparts everywhere. (5) but here's the kicker: since every option is taken somewhere, that summed total is constant no matter what i do locally. (6) therefore local choice makes no difference to my overall well-being, and practical reasoning collapses — there's never any reason to pick one option over another. (7) obviously that's wrong, we do have reasons. (8) so where's the crack? i really want to hang onto (1) and maybe (3), but i'm open. is there an equivocation between "counterpart" and "me" hiding in there, or is the mistake treating indexical actuality as if it were transparent across worlds? which step do you reject and what does giving it up cost?
