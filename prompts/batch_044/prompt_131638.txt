I am currently writing my dissertation in the philosophy of language, and I have become preoccupied with a problem that sits at the intersection of Donald Davidson's radical interpretation and the interpretive challenges posed by contemporary large language models. Davidson developed his account of radical interpretation to explain how an interpreter could understand a speaker's language and mental states from scratch, relying on the principle of charity and the holistic interconnection of belief, meaning, and rational agency. But Davidson never encountered transformer-based systems that generate syntactically fluent, context-sensitive utterances without possessing anything like a stable, unified point of view or diachronic conditions for belief retention.

Here is the conceptual puzzle. Suppose we treat an advanced LLM as an interpretive target: it produces outputs that are locally coherent and pragmatically appropriate within specific prompts, yet globally inconsistent across contexts, and it lacks the embodied, rational agency that Davidson took to be a precondition for genuine propositional attitude attribution. Would Davidson have argued that radical interpretation simply fails here, and that we should withhold all intentional ascriptions, treating the system as a sophisticated echo rather than a speaker? Or might he have revised his framework to allow for a distributed, non-agentive form of interpretation in which charity applies not to a unified mind but to transient, context-bound fragments of meaning?

Please construct an argument predicting Davidson's likely response. Take seriously his anti-reductionism about the mental, his insistence that belief and meaning are inseparable from rational normativity, and his skepticism about sharp boundaries between language and thought. At the same time, address whether the particular form of semantic instability these systems exhibit—what one might call fragmented intentionality—would force him to relax the holistic requirements of radical interpretation, or whether it would instead confirm his view that without a unified rational agent there is nothing to interpret. I am not asking for bibliographic references or a survey of secondary literature; rather, I want a philosophically rigorous reconstruction of how Davidson's core commitments would likely extend, or break, under the pressure of technological developments that emerged after his death.
