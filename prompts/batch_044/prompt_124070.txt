I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I am trying to formalise the logical space of a debate I have encountered about dietary ethics and wild-animal suffering. In particular, I want to compare beef, chicken, and crop-based diets not only in terms of farmed-animal welfare but also in terms of wild-animal deaths caused by agriculture—direct killing during planting and harvesting, pesticide exposure, and habitat displacement from land use.

Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy of the positions one might hold. Do not begin with named theorists; build the tree from the logical distinctions first, then locate the actual positions (named or unnamed) at the leaves.

Each internal node should be one concrete claim that splits the space, phrased so that a reader can answer it without knowing which philosophical label corresponds to which branch:

- Whether suffering experienced by wild invertebrates affected by crop production (for example, insects and small mammals killed during harvest) counts in the moral calculus, and whether it should be weighted differently from suffering experienced by farmed vertebrates.
- Whether 'harvest mortality'—direct deaths caused by planting, harvesting, and pesticide application—ought to be treated as morally equivalent to 'displacement mortality'—indirect deaths arising from habitat loss and long-term ecosystem change—or whether only one of these categories should be included.
- Whether the biological differences between cattle and chickens (lifespan, feed-conversion efficiency, and housing conditions) create a 'replacement-rate asymmetry' that reverses the intuitive ranking of beef versus chicken when the objective is to minimise total sentient suffering.
- Whether the empirical view that wild animals typically lead net-negative lives, owing to predation, starvation, and disease, undermines the argument that crop cultivation harms wild animals, on the grounds that reducing wild populations through agricultural land use might actually reduce aggregate suffering.

At each leaf, state the name of the position if it is widely recognised, or label it 'unnamed / unexplored', and provide one sentence describing its most counter-intuitive practical commitment (for example, that the view implies beef consumption is ethically preferable to a legume-based diet).

Please also flag any pair of leaves that your tree genuinely cannot separate, and briefly diagnose any empty branches—indicating whether they seem incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.

Write in formal British English, avoid any request for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and ensure that the taxonomy can be understood and evaluated without access to external sources.
