I'm currently taking a graduate seminar in the philosophy of mathematics, and I'm organizing a small interdisciplinary workshop titled "Actual Infinity: Hilbert's Hotel, Cantor's Diagonal, and the Finitist Challenge." The audience will be deliberately mixed: working mathematicians for whom Cantorian set theory is standard equipment, philosophers of mathematics divided over whether actual infinities exist, and one invited senior historian of science who has advanced the minority view that Hilbert's hotel should be read not as a cheerful endorsement of completed infinity but as a pedagogical reductio ad absurdum of treating an unending process as a finished totality. I have been asked to deliver the opening remarks, about five minutes long, to set the tone.

I want Hilbert's hotel to do real structural work in what I say, not just serve as an illustration. Specifically, I want to frame the workshop itself through the metaphor: each speaker is like a new guest, and the program is arranged so that accommodating an additional perspective never requires evicting one already present. At the same time, the remarks must respect the finitist constraint—shared by my invited historian and by intuitionist traditions—that an infinite collection is never given as a completed object, only as a potential procedure that can always be extended but never surveyed all at once. This means I cannot literally promise "infinite" discussion time, I should not treat the day's agenda as an actually infinite set of events, and I must avoid metaphors that smuggle in Cantorian platonism as if it were the neutral background of the room.

Please write the remarks. They should welcome mathematicians and skeptics alike without presupposing that Cantor's paradise is uncontroversial, they should explain why the day's disagreements are a feature rather than a failure of hospitality, and they should navigate the tension between open-ended accommodation and the finitist warning that "making room for everyone" becomes incoherent if we pretend the hotel has already been built in full. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I need this to stand on its own for an audience that includes people who do not share the same technical background.
