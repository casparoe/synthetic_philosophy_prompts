I've been trying to organize my thoughts on whether beauty is real or just personal, particularly through Hume's notion of a standard of taste. Here is my position: (1) Beauty is not an objective feature of things; it is a particular kind of pleasure or sentiment that occurs in a perceiver. (2) Nevertheless, Hume was essentially correct that there is a standard: people who compare widely, clear away prejudice, and possess delicate sensibility are better judges, and their convergence marks genuine beauty or deformity. (3) Therefore, to call something beautiful is not merely to report my own liking but to invoke what an ideal, qualified critic would feel. (4) Yet I also hold that deep cultural differences—such as the Japanese valuation of imperfection and asymmetry versus Western ideals of harmony—are not failures of judgment but equally legitimate aesthetic frameworks. (5) I further believe that if every genuinely qualified judge, across all cultural frameworks, happened to agree on a verdict, that agreement would confer a strong, almost objective status on the judgment, not merely widespread approval. (6) At the same time, I think that two equally qualified judges can sincerely disagree about a particular work—say, a minimalist sculpture—without either being mistaken, because there remains an irreducible private residue in taste. (7) Finally, I hold that an artificial system lacking consciousness could never genuinely experience beauty, though it might approximate expert predictions by processing patterns.

Please examine whether these commitments cohere. I am especially unsure about: whether my subjectivism in (1) conflicts with the normativity in (3); whether cultural pluralism in (4) undermines the universal convergence implied by (2) and (5); and whether allowing irreducible disagreement in (6) makes my condition for objectivity in (5) empty or contradictory. For each real tension, rank its severity, explain what must be revised, and identify the single change that would resolve the most tension at the least cost. Also point out if any conflict only looks serious but dissolves on closer inspection.
