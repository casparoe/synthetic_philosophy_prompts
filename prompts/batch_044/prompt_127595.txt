What was his actual philosophical defense of astrology? Specifically, distinguish the kind of celestial influence he accepted from the kind he rejected, and explain how his discovery of elliptical orbits interacted with the astrological tradition he inherited from Ptolemy.

Student answer:

Kepler defended astrology by arguing that planets emit a real but non-deterministic physical influence — something like a harmonic or magnetic force — that shapes human temperment and large-scale patterns like weather, but he denied that the stars dictate specific individual events such as who you marry or whether you win a lawsuit. He criticized Ptolemaic astrology for depending on perfect circular motion and uniform speed, and once he discovered elliptical orbits he concluded that the old zodiac divisions were geometrically false, so he abandoned predictive natal astrology entirely and kept only a general celestial meteorology. Even so, he applied his own laws to compute more precise angular relationships between planets, claiming this made the underlying influence easier to measure rather than weaker. For Kepler, then, astronomy and astrology were not competing projects but the same mathematical investigation applied to different domains.
