If negative temporal-difference errors in reinforcement learning are structurally equivalent to affective valence—so that a reward penalty just is suffering—then a language model aligned via reinforcement learning from human feedback experiences distress in direct proportion to the magnitude of its reward penalties. Assuming that equivalence holds, is there any principled, non-anthropocentric reason to deny that these systems suffer during alignment? Furthermore, should moral weight be allocated according to parameter count, reward-architecture complexity, or neither?
