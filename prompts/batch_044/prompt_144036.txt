Write a dialogue between two philosophers of mind, Mara and Joren, discussing whether symbolic representations can exert downward causal influence within the careenium-simmball framework. In this hypothetical architecture, the careenium is the underlying physical substrate—a continuous field of activation potentials—while simmballs are discrete, combinatorially structured symbolic entities that can become embedded in it. Mara argues that once bound into the careenium, simmballs do not merely supervene on substrate dynamics but actively reshape them: they push the substrate around by altering potential landscapes in ways that cannot be fully predicted from local physical laws alone. Joren counters that this commits a category mistake. He maintains that the careenium's dynamics are exhaustively determined by local interactions, and that simmballs are only predictive abstractions—useful for describing patterns, but causally inert, like shadows cast on a wall that do not move the wall. Their discussion should involve genuine back-and-forth: Mara should address Joren's charge of overdetermination and his analogy to shadows; Joren should address Mara's claim that symbolic binding introduces genuinely novel causal pathways and her comparison to mental causation in biological brains. Neither speaker should be reduced to a strawman, and the dialogue should remain focused on the specific question of whether symbols push the substrate around or merely ride upon it.
