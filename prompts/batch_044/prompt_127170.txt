I'm putting together a weird little unit for my grad seminer on mind and mechanism — my friend kept insisting that once you have syntax in a system, the symbols literally push the substrate around like little gears, and I think that's mostly metaphor but I'm not definetly sure where the analogy breaks. Can you rank these five takes on whether symbols actually push substrate, from the one you'd defend in print to the one you'd laugh out of the room monday morning? Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no intro and no conclusion, and skip bibliographies since half my students don't have library access right now.

The five are: (1) the careenium thesis, where symbols only gain causal force inside a turbulent feedback loop between representation and substrate, with neither layer dominating and the push happening in the turbulence itself; (2) classic Searlean biological naturalism, which says syntax is observer-relative so only brain chemistry pushes anything and semantics is just a post-hoc label we slap on; (3) simmball dynamics, where tiny symbolic modules bounce through neural tissue like billiard balls and literally transfer momentum at each collision; (4) standard computational functionalism, which holds that substrate doesn't care about symbols themselves, but the functional organization they realize pushes matter around via ordinary physical causation at every level; and (5) full eliminativism about symbolhood, claiming there are no symbols in the brain, only activation patterns, so asking whether symbols push substrate is like asking whether ghosts push walls.
