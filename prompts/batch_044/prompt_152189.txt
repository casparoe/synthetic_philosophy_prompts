In the scenario, which I shall call the Reflexive Abstention Game, there are exactly one hundred agents, each capable of perfect logical reasoning and fully aware that all others share this capacity. Each agent simultaneously chooses either to enter or to abstain. The payout rule is deliberately asymmetric: if precisely k agents enter, where k is prime, they share a prize that scales inversely with k; meanwhile, every agent who abstains pays a small fixed cost that is added directly to the prize pool. Thus abstention is not neutral — it actively subsidises entry. Each agent knows that every other agent is reasoning about this same structure, including the fact that abstaining enriches potential entrants.

Dr. Rahman should defend the view that superrational agents can still coordinate on a Pareto-optimal equilibrium — perhaps universal abstention, or a precisely calculated entry threshold — on the grounds that shared rationality operates as a logical correlation device that survives even when abstention carries a subsidy.
