You advise the bargaining committee of a manufacturing union. Every month you must review an employer proposal to introduce automated or algorithmic systems—robotic assembly lines, predictive scheduling algorithms, AI-driven quality control, or sensor-based maintenance platforms—and issue a signed, one-page recommendation: oppose, negotiate binding conditions, or accept with monitoring. The stakes are net job loss, work intensification, skill degradation, and the capture of workplace knowledge. You have forty-eight hours per review, no engineering training, and must justify the call to an executive board that includes non-specialists. You may not commission outside studies; you must decide from the proposal documents and worker interviews.

Write a reusable decision procedure grounded in Marx’s analysis of machinery, formal versus real subsumption, and the conversion of living labor and tacit knowledge into dead labor and proprietary capital. Do not summarize Marx in general; translate his framework into ordered questions a non-specialist can apply. The procedure must distinguish automation that replaces tasks within existing labor relations (formal subsumption) from automation that redesigns the labor process around the system’s logic, subjects remaining workers to its rhythm, and objectifies workers’ experiential knowledge as capital-controlled data or algorithms (real subsumption). It must also test whether any reduction in necessary labor time is translated into shorter hours and maintained wages, or instead into unemployment, speed-up, and expanded surplus extraction.

Prescribe your response in the following sections, in this order, with approximate lengths:

1. Role, Stakes, and Constraints (about 100 words): fix the decision environment, the frequency of reviews, the sign-off authority, and the information available.

2. The Procedure—Ordered Tests and Rules (about 450 words): present the sequence of questions the advisor must ask, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, and the rule that applies. Include a specific test that detects whether workers’ tacit diagnostic or organizational knowledge is being extracted, formalized, and locked into a proprietary system that then replaces their judgment. Inject one somewhat niche distinction drawn from Marx’s theory of technology—specifically, the difference between machinery that extends the worker’s physical capacity within their own rhythm and machinery that subordinates the worker to an externally determined tempo and logic—and show exactly how that distinction changes the recommendation between “negotiate conditions” and “oppose.”

3. Stop Points, Opposition Triggers, and Value Handoffs (about 200 words): state the conditions under which the procedure mandates opposition, mandates negotiation with specified non-negotiables, or stops and hands the decision to the board because the remaining disagreement is one of values (for example, whether preserving a specific craft identity outweighs economic security) rather than facts interpretable through the framework.

4. Three Worked Cases (about 350 words total, divided among them): apply the procedure to (a) a robotic welding line that eliminates half of an assembly team but leaves the remaining workers’ tasks and pace unchanged; (b) an AI scheduling and pacing algorithm that continuously adjusts shift targets based on real-time worker performance data; and (c) a predictive-maintenance platform that requires mechanics to enter failure patterns into a company-owned database that then automates diagnostics and reduces the mechanics’ role to sensor replacement. The third case should yield an answer the union will likely find unwelcome—conditional acceptance with strict monitoring conditions rather than opposition—and explain precisely why the procedure reaches that result despite the extraction of tacit knowledge.

5. Limits, Misfires, and Unsettled Cases (about 200 words): identify where the procedure will misfire, produce false positives or false negatives, or fail to settle the question because Marx’s framework underdetermines the empirical case—especially where the technical facts about data ownership or labor-time accounting are ambiguous.

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote Marx verbatim; paraphrase all theoretical claims in your own words. Ensure every step can be executed by someone without web access and without specialized training in Marxist scholarship beyond the concepts named above. Flag every point in the procedure where judgment cannot be eliminated and a human decision is still required.
