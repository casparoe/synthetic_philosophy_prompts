I've been thinking about people with pain asymbolia—those who report that something hurts yet insist it doesn't bother them, as though the sensation is present but the suffering has been detached. This raises a conceptual puzzle I can't quite resolve: is the badness of pain woven into the sensory experience itself, or is it produced by a separate evaluative or motivational reaction? If these can come apart, then someone might feel intense pain without anything actually being wrong with them, which seems strange. Please explain the core conceptual issue here—specifically, whether pain asymbolia supports dividing pain into a bare sensory-discriminative layer and an affective-motivational layer, and what that division implies for the claim that pain is intrinsically bad rather than bad only in virtue of our response to it. I want to understand exactly where the disagreement lies: is it about the nature of pain itself, or about what makes a state harmful to the person experiencing it?
