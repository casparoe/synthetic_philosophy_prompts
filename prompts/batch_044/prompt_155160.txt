My colleague advances a higher-order account of phenomenal consciousness and has delivered consistent verdicts across a range of cases: she judges that normal attentive visual experience is conscious; that blindsight involving accurate forced-choice discrimination without reported awareness is not conscious; that subliminal masked priming altering behavior without awareness is not conscious; that deep slow-wave sleep with intact sensory processing but no subsequent dream report or recall is not conscious; and that peripheral fringe awareness of a color without focal attention is conscious.

Consider now a new case. A subject is presented with a visual stimulus that activates first-order visual cortex and also a localized, module-specific meta-representational circuit in parietal cortex that tracks the stimulus presence, but a selective lesion prevents this signal from reaching the global workspace or becoming available for report or action guidance. The subject behaves and reports exactly as if unaware, yet the local meta-representation is accurate and occurrent.

Predict her verdict: is the subject phenomenally conscious of the stimulus? Identify the two principal higher-order interpretations consistent with her track record—distinguishing, for example, a global-broadcast actualist requirement from a local-actualist one, or either from a dispositional-access view—state what each predicts in this case, offer your best guess with reasoning, and name the further case (for instance, one involving intact global architecture but temporary suppression of all meta-representational formation) that would best separate the remaining competing rules.
