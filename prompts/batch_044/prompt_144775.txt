Please comment on the following essay outline and central claim.

The essay examines whether G. E. Moore’s account of friendship in Principia Ethica can accommodate what I call “pure contemplative friendship”—a relation in which two people derive intrinsic value solely from silently observing and appreciating each other’s mental processes, without conversation, practical cooperation, or emotional exchange. Moore lists friendship among the greatest goods, describing it as composed of the appreciation of beautiful mental qualities in another person, together with the pleasure taken in that appreciation and in shared interaction. The essay applies Moore’s principle of organic unity—according to which the value of a complex whole may differ from the sum of the values of its parts—to ask whether removing interaction and shared pleasure merely subtracts value or transforms the whole. Specifically, it asks whether isolating the appreciative component creates a more intense intrinsic good, analogous to viewing a single painting in an empty room rather than in a crowded gallery, or whether interaction is an essential part whose absence collapses friendship into mere distant aesthetic admiration. The essay also raises an asymmetry problem: if person A appreciates B’s mental qualities, and B appreciates A’s appreciation of B, does this generate a reflexive loop that conflicts with Moore’s assumption that intrinsic goods are non-derivative?
