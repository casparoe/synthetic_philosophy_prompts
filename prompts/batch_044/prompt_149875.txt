hey so my study group had this long arguement about whether the agricultural revolution was actually a mistake and i want an outside take, not my own opinion.

Maya argued it was basically a disaster: hunter-gatherers lived in the original affluent society with more leisure and flatter hierarchies. She said agriculture only happened because of climate stress at the Younger Dryas, and once surplus storage existed it created a class of store-keepers who controlled distribution. So the mistake wasn't farming itself but the property regime that came with it.

Leo said thats too neat. He proposed this metric he calls the autonomy-storage coefficient - basically caloric reserve months divided by labor hours, multiplied by the Gini coefficient of food access within the group. He claimed if you run that on ethnographic data, the coefficient actually improves for early agriculturalists compared to late foragers, meaning the transition was locally rational even if it eventually led to inequality. He acused Maya of romanticizing foraging and ignoring seasonal starvation risk.

Maya shot back that Leos coefficient is circular: it assumes storage is neutral, but in her view storage IS the mechanism of domination. She said you cant seperate the technology from the social form, so measuring them seperately misses the point.

Leo then said shes moving the goalposts: first she said agriculture was the mistake, now shes saying storage-plus-inequality was the mistake, and those are different claims.

whos right about which specific point? is Leos coefficient actually useful or just dressing up a value judgment? and is Maya wrong to treat storage and social form as inseperable? also, is there something both miss about the leisure-inequality tradeoff that doesnt depend on either framework?
