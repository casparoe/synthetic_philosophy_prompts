I am a graduate student in a course on the philosophy of law and collective violence, and I serve as the student representative on my university's Committee for Archival Designation and Ethical Naming. The committee must reach a decision by the end of this semester on whether our university's forthcoming digital archive should classify a documented mid-twentieth-century campaign of forced assimilation, linguistic suppression, and demographic dispersal directed against a specific minority population as genocide. International legal bodies have never formally adjudicated this case under the 1948 UN Convention, though several historians contend that it satisfies Raphael Lemkin's original, broader framework—which encompassed cultural, social, and economic destruction—rather than the Convention's narrower focus on physical and biological destruction. One faction of the committee maintains that an institutional archive must track only legally adjudicated categories in order to preserve epistemic authority and avoid conceptual inflation; the opposing faction argues that strict adherence to the Convention's scope risks a form of institutional forgetting about precisely the non-physical dimensions Lemkin sought to capture, and they propose adopting a tiered classification system that distinguishes legally recognized genocide from genocide under Lemkin's original criteria. I need a formal, concise opinion memo—no more than two pages—recommending a concrete course of action with a specific time frame, either approval, rejection, or conditional approval, that I can circulate before our final vote. The memo should treat the strongest consideration on the losing side with genuine seriousness: it must not merely restate the legal definition of genocide, but should address whether a university archive bears any distinctive obligation to preserve conceptual breadth, and it should honestly engage the risk that expanding institutional naming beyond legal verdicts undermines the archive's credibility. If the recommendation is conditional, please specify the conditions and milestones; if it is to reject the label, please specify what alternative framing the archive should adopt instead.
