While discussing with colleagues whether formal ethical frameworks suffer from an inherent limitation analogous to Gödel's results, I kept running into the same compressed argument: "Any consistent rule-based system containing arithmetic is incomplete, so an algorithmic moral code must miss some true moral judgments." I want to understand exactly where this analogy breaks down conceptually, rather than just being told that ethics is not arithmetic. In particular, Gödel's first theorem requires that the system represent all primitive recursive functions, that it be ω-consistent rather than merely consistent, and that syntactic self-reference be achieved through a specific arithmetic encoding of formulas. Could you explain, without reproducing the full technical proof, why each of these conditions is essential to the result, and what it means for the analogy if a normative system—say, a deontic logic with no arithmetic apparatus—fails to satisfy them?

Specifically, I am trying to separate two different failures that get conflated: (i) syntactic incompleteness in Gödel's precise sense, where the system generates a true arithmetical sentence that it cannot prove, and (ii) normative underdetermination, where the system's rules simply do not entail a particular ethical conclusion we take to be correct. If the formal ethical system lacks the representational capacity to encode its own syntax arithmetically, does Gödel's theorem apply to it at all, or does the argument collapse into a much weaker claim about axiomatic sparsity? I also want clarity on why ω-consistency is a stricter requirement than simple consistency, and why an advocate of "algorithmic morality is necessarily incomplete" might be smuggling in an assumption stronger than the one Gödel actually needs. Finally, please distinguish this first result sharply from the second incompleteness theorem—concerning a system's inability to prove its own consistency—and explain why confusing the two produces a distinct error when people say an AI "cannot verify the coherence of its own moral framework." I am not asking for a bibliography or historical survey; I want a conceptual map of which parts of the Gödelian machinery actually do work in this debate and which are being borrowed for rhetorical effect.
