As a retired experimental physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of social science, I am preparing to interview a philosopher of history for a small podcast series. My guest defends a specific, somewhat unfashionable position in the debate over why some nations are wealthy: she argues that the familiar tripartite framing—institutions versus geography versus culture—rests on a category error, namely the treatment of macro-historical development as if it were a replicable laboratory experiment with isolatable causal variables. Rather than pursuing ever-finer instrumental variables or larger cross-national datasets, she proposes that explanatory adequacy in this domain should be judged by what she terms structural narrative coherence, where a historical account is evaluated by the consistency and integrative power of its causal story across temporal and spatial scales, not by counterfactual dependence on any single factor. She insists that this is not a retreat into relativism, since coherence constraints remain objective, though they are not statistical or experimentally falsifiable in the standard sense.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that avoid biographical preliminaries and instead press directly on the substance of her view. I want at least one question addressing the formal analogy she draws between quantum entanglement and the co-determination of institutions with geographic conditions—specifically, how she prevents this analogy from smuggling physical determinism into historical explanation—and I want a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that the analogy is strictly structural rather than ontological. I also want a question targeting her claim that settler-mortality instruments, often invoked in the institutions literature, fail conceptually rather than merely empirically; include a follow-up that anticipates her probable defense that conceptual failure is itself a form of meta-empirical failure. Additionally, include a question about the normative stakes of abandoning counterfactual causal inference for explaining underdevelopment: do we lose the conceptual resources to assign institutional or collective responsibility, and how does her coherence framework recover or replace them? Provide a follow-up tailored to the case in which she appeals to distributed agency. The questions should be formal, precise, and accessible to an audience with some background in philosophy of science but no specialist training in economic history or cliometrics.
