You serve as the data-integrity officer for a multi-site study collecting first-person reports from non-communicative patients via semantic neural decoders—systems that map cortical activity to vocabulary strings, often yielding grammatically intact but phenomenologically ambiguous outputs (for example, the pain has a color or I am not present). The study adopts heterophenomenology: each decoded string is treated not as direct access to a private mental state, but as a text describing a heterophenomenological world, whose content counts as data regardless of whether it is literally true about consciousness. Your task is to design a reusable decision procedure that clinicians and technicians—without philosophical training—can apply to every string before it enters the dataset. Present the procedure as an ordered sequence of questions or tests. For each step, specify what counts as a sufficient answer, where the chain terminates in exclusion (the string is rejected as data), and where it must stop and escalate to a senior reviewer because the question involves interpretive values rather than operational fact. Explicitly flag cases in which the decoder's vocabulary is impoverished, metaphor-prone, or cross-modal, and state clearly what the procedure is not designed to settle. Then apply the procedure to two incoming reports: (1) a decoder outputs there is no suffering from a patient exhibiting sustained nociceptive reflexes and elevated autonomic markers; and (2) a decoder outputs the ceiling is singing in green from a patient in a minimally conscious state with no prior artistic or synesthetic history. For each case, walk through the steps, identify the point at which the procedure produces an answer that you, as the study's designer, would find unwelcome, and isolate the residual judgment call that remains after the procedure has done its work. Conclude by listing the two or three failure modes—scenarios in which correct application of the procedure will still yield false positives or false negatives—and explain why each failure is unavoidable given the commitments of the heterophenomenological method.
