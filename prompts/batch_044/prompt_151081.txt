Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a defender of negative liberty and a defender of republican liberty, framed within the standard taxonomy that distinguishes both from positive liberty. The document should have three parts. First, an agreed-claims section listing four propositions both sides can sign—for instance, that freedom involves the absence of certain constraints, that arbitrary power is objectionable, that a benevolent master who never interferes still holds power over the subject, and that the three conceptual categories are not interchangeable. Second, a disagreements table with three rows in neutral wording: (a) whether being subject to another's arbitrary will is itself a reduction in liberty even when interference never occurs; (b) whether the benevolent-master case demonstrates an actual deficit of freedom or merely a risk of future unfreedom; and (c) whether the correct remedy is the removal of obstacles, the expansion of capabilities, or the institutional elimination of arbitrary discretion. For each row, state the negative-liberty and republican-liberty positions using formulations each camp would accept for its own view. Third, identify the central crux—whether freedom requires the counterfactual robustness of non-interference, meaning that forbearance must be structurally guaranteed rather than contingent on the authority's present preferences—and propose one concrete institutional scenario (workers in a cooperative where management holds unchecked scheduling authority but consistently exercises it benevolently) that both sides agree is relevant, recording in advance each side's judgment about whether those workers are fully free and whether an available but prohibitively costly exit option changes the analysis. Do not request citations, bibliographies, or specific texts; keep all formulations self-contained.
