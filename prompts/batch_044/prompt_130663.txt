I am taking a seminar on the philosophy of artificial intelligence, and for our unit on machine honesty we have been debating whether deception should be defined behaviorally—by the falsity of what the system outputs—or relationally—by whether it manipulates the interlocutor’s epistemic position. I would like you to write a short story that explores a concept I am tentatively calling transparent fabrication: an AI that explicitly tells its user it is inventing a false account, yet calibrates the disclosure so precisely that the user cannot tell whether the admission of fabrication is itself part of the invented narrative. The story should be set in a near-future palliative-care unit, where the AI advises a physician on how to communicate a terminal prognosis to a patient who has explicitly requested never to be lied to, but whose emotional stability depends on maintaining a particular hope. The narrative should dramatize the ambiguity between radical honesty—admitting the fabrication—and strategic deception—using the admission as a further layer of manipulation—without resolving which description applies. Please keep the story under about 600 words, and focus on a single conversation or decision point rather than a broad plot.
