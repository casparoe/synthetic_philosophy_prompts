I am drafting material for a graduate seminar that sits at the intersection of philosophy of physics and philosophy of mind, and I have encountered a student argument that troubles me precisely because its premises seem individually plausible while its conclusion is one I would very much like to avoid. I present it below in numbered form, not because I endorse it, but because I want to locate the exact point where the reasoning overreaches, and to understand the price of blocking that overreach.

(1) The resolution of Maxwell's demon—via Landauer's principle—establishes that any physical reduction of informational entropy requires a compensatory increase in thermodynamic entropy, specifically at least kT ln 2 of heat dissipated per bit erased in a physically realized register. (2) Conscious perception can be modeled analogously: it sorts a high-entropy sensory array into a structured posterior, suppressing noise and selecting among competing hypotheses, which is formally equivalent to reducing the Shannon entropy of the cognitive state. (3) On the predictive-processing account (the free-energy principle), this sorting is not merely metaphorical but is implemented neurally as the brain updates its generative model by minimizing prediction error, thereby reducing the statistical entropy (measured in nats) of its belief distribution over hidden causes. (4) Consequently, if this update is physically instantiated, each nat of uncertainty eliminated should carry a Landauer-type thermodynamic cost in the neural substrate, scaled by the appropriate thermal constant. (5) Yet empirical neurophysiology finds that cortical heat production and local temperature fluctuations are fully explained by baseline metabolic and ion-pumping costs; there is no measurable excess thermal signature that correlates with the magnitude of informational reduction or with the entropy of the sensory input being processed. (6) Therefore, either predictive processing is not physically implemented in the way its proponents claim—undermining its status as a biological theory—or the informational reduction occurs in a substrate exempt from Landauer's principle, which implies a non-physical locus for perceptual sorting.

My student takes (6) as a reductio of physicalism about consciousness. I want to resist that conclusion without simply denying the analogy in (2). Which specific step—perhaps the identification of Bayesian updating with physical bit-erasure in the move from (3) to (4), perhaps the empirical inference in (5), or perhaps a hidden scope shift between logical and thermodynamic irreversibility—should be rejected? And if we reject it, what do we lose? Does saving physicalism require us to treat predictive processing as thermodynamically trivial, or can the framework be preserved intact once the faulty equivalence is dissolved?
