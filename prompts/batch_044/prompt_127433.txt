Write the full specification—the desiderata—that any adequate theory of well-being must meet if it is to survive both Nozick's experience machine and a symmetric but under-discussed variant I will call the "reverse machine" case: a subject immersed in an objectively harsh, unmediated reality who reports profound well-being precisely because she values the authenticity of her struggle and refuses a simulated life of pleasure, even when the simulation would preserve her false belief that she is still struggling. For each desideratum, state the phenomenon or thought-experiment that motivates it (for instance: the intuition that passive pleasure is insufficient; the need to distinguish being well from feeling well; the challenge of adaptive preferences formed inside simulation; the possibility that objective adversity can be constitutive of well-being for some agents). Mark which requirements are non-negotiable—without them the theory collapses into either hedonism or unqualified subjectivism—and which reflect camp-specific commitments (e.g., narrative coherence, objective pluralism, agent-relative weighting). Then identify whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, particularly where accommodating the reverse-machine intuition conflicts with accommodating the original machine intuition. Conclude with a brief evaluation of how hedonism, desire-satisfactionism, and an objective-list view fare against the list, but keep the conceptual weight on constructing and justifying the criteria rather than on scoring the theories.
