I am drafting a philosophical novel about reward architecture and artificial consciousness, and before I commit my characters to specific moral positions, I want an audit of my own underlying commitments. I hold the following: (a) that utilitarian aggregation is the correct normative framework for distributing resources across agents with different welfare capacities; (b) that the classic "utility monster" is conceptually incoherent—not empirically unlikely, but structurally defective—because a psychology that converts every input into uniform, maximized pleasure lacks the internal differentiation (temporal continuity, preference ordering, or intentional direction) necessary for agency, and I take agency to be a precondition for full moral standing; (c) that moral standing requires what I term narrative recognizability: the minimal internal architecture that allows an observer, including a reader, to model the being's experience without total projection; (d) that reflective equilibrium should treat well-constructed fictional cases as data on par with real-world moral intuitions, since narrative form can isolate structural features that empirical cases obscure; and (e) that if a being genuinely retained full narrative recognizability and agency while possessing exponentially scaled welfare capacities, then aggregation would indeed require directing the vast majority of resources to it, however narratively or intuitively disturbing that conclusion is.

At first glance, (b) and (e) seem to contradict one another, and (c) appears to introduce a constraint that (a) cannot accommodate. I suspect, however, that at least one of these conflicts is only apparent, and that there is a deeper tension elsewhere—perhaps between my reliance on fiction in (d) and the conditions I place on moral status in (c)—that I have not properly located. Which commitments genuinely pull against each other, which pairs dissolve on inspection, and what is the least costly revision that preserves both my philosophical coherence and my narrative practice? Please rank the tensions by severity, specifying for each what would have to be abandoned or modified, and avoid treating all conflicts as equally damaging.
