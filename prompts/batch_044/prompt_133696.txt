I serve on our municipal cultural funding board. A proposal seeks funding for a permanent public installation—the People's Index—that aggregates residents' real-time sentiment on local policy questions and renders the majority view as a luminous architectural display. The artists defend it as making democratic will transparent and accountable; critics charge that it treats the popular will as a pre-political statistical aggregate—the kind populist rhetoric presumes—while democratic theory holds that legitimate popular sovereignty requires deliberative formation rather than instantaneous polling. Our charter mandates that funded projects serve genuine public deliberation, though it offers no definition. Draft a formal decision memo recommending approval, rejection, or conditional approval, specifying a concrete course of action and timeline. The memo should take the artists' framing seriously—explain why visibility of sentiment might serve democratic accountability—while giving full weight to the strongest objection that aggregation is not sovereignty. State explicitly what argument or evidence would change your recommendation, and identify the point in your reasoning most vulnerable to failure.
