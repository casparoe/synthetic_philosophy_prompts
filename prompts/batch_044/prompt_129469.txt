i was arguing with a friend about Benatar's asymmetry — the antinatalist bit where absence of pleasure isnt bad for anyone but absence of pain is good for non-existent people — and i think i pushed it into a weird corner that proves way too much. here's the chain:

(1) for any potential being B, if B never comes to exist then B is not deprived of any pleasure (so not creating B does no harm), but B is also spared all suffering (so that's genuinely good).

(2) now take some action A that has a nonzero chance of eventually causing B to exist with net negative wellbeing. performing A risks producing harm, and since the pleasure B might experience doesnt count as a reason to bring B into being, there's no counterbalancing benefit.

(3) so we have a reason to avoid A, proportional to the risk-weighted expected suffering.

(4) but pretty much any long-term project — having kids, funding ai safety research, even planting trees that might eventually support sentient ecosystems — carries some tiny chance of eventually leading to suffering beings.

(5) therefore we should avoid almost all long-term projects, which is clearly absurd; Benatar never meant the asymmetry to imply you cant plant trees.

(6) so either (1) is false, or there's a hidden scope limit that stops the risk-propagation reasoning in (2)-(3).

i feel like the slip is somewhere between (2) and (3), maybe an equivocation about aggregating risks across possible worlds? but i cant pin it down. which step should i reject, and what does rejecting it cost? i'd rather keep (1) and (4) intact if i can, since they seem like direct applications, but i want to know what i'm paying if i have to drop one.
