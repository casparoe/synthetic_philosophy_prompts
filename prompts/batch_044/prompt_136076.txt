I'm participating in a philosophy of action seminar focused on the debate over internal versus external reasons. The discussion keeps collapsing whenever someone raises the possibility that an agent might have a genuine practical reason that does not depend on any prior desire or motivational state—say, a reason to avoid harming an innocent person even if she currently feels no inclination to care. At that point, another student invariably invokes the slogan that reason is, at bottom, nothing but the slave of the passions, treating it as an immediate refutation of any so-called external reason. They seem to take the slogan to show that if reason cannot move an agent without some pre-existing desire, then no reason can apply to her unless she is already motivated.

Please do not treat this as a request for a bibliography, reading list, or citations of specific texts; I need a conceptual disambiguation I can take to office hours. Lay out, as a menu rather than a single interpretation, the distinct precise theses that this slogan might actually be asserting. For each reading, specify its domain—whether it is a psychological claim about causal motivation, a normative claim about what counts as a reason for an agent, a conceptual claim about the nature of rationality, or a metaphysical claim about normative properties—and label it as either trivial, substantive and defensible, empirically false, or conceptually confused.

In particular, distinguish:
- The motivational internalist reading: reason can only cause action when coupled with a desire.
- The existence internalist reading: a consideration is a reason for an agent only if there is a sound deliberative route from that agent's existing motivational set to the action.
- The instrumentalist reading: reason can only determine means, never ends.
- Any other precise formulation you think is live in the literature or in seminar rhetoric.

For each, say whether it actually supports the conclusion that there are no external reasons, and whether it confuses an inability to motivate with a lack of normative standing.
