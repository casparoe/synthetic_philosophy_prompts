I have been modeling harm as a continuous field: every negative experience, from a dust speck in the eye to prolonged torture, occupies a point in the same multidimensional space, and there appears to be no topological rupture between these extremes. If harm-space is path-connected in this way, then the difference between torture and any number of specks seems purely quantitative—merely a matter of distance along the same axes rather than a difference in kind. Consequently, anyone who denies that enough specks can outweigh one torture seems committed to denying that this space is continuous, or to introducing an unexplained discontinuity. Is that right, or am I conflating geometric continuity with moral commensurability?
