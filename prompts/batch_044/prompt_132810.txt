I'm preparing my position paper for a graduate seminar in classical Indian epistemology, and I want a consistency check before I present. Here is where I have landed on Nyāya pramāṇa theory, and I am unsure whether the package holds together.

My commitments are these:

(1) I accept the standard Nyāya quartet—pratyakṣa, anumāna, upamāna, and śabda—as irreducible, independent pramāṇas; none can be analyzed away into the others.

(2) I think śabda is genuinely independent because it can yield knowledge of supersensible objects (such as the ātman or the binding force of dharma) for which no observed vyāpti is available, so it cannot be reconstructed as a hidden inference.

(3) At the same time, I hold that for śabda to operate we must identify the speaker as an āpta, and that identification is always achieved through prior successful applications of perception and inference tracking consistency across contexts.

(4) I maintain that perceptual cognition is svataḥ prāmāṇya—intrinsically valid—understood as a default entitlement: a cognition is valid unless and until another pramāṇa specifically challenges it.

(5) I deny that any pramāṇa requires internal self-monitoring or anuvyavasāya to be corrected; perceptual error (viparyaya) is explained entirely by misattribution of properties to an external object, not by any misrepresentation of internal content.

(6) I believe that upamāna produces knowledge of a similarity relation (sambandha) that is neither a particular object of perception nor a universal grasped through invariable concomitance, yet I also think that similarity is nothing over and above shared properties (sādhāraṇa dharma), which is precisely the kind of universal relation that anumāna tracks.

(7) I reject epistemic circularity across the board: no pramāṇa can be certified by an instance of itself, though one pramāṇa may defeat another without either having received prior external certification.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are in genuine tension, and which only look like they conflict? For each real tension, rank it by severity and say what the cheapest revision would cost me—would I have to abandon the irreducibility of one pramāṇa, weaken my account of intrinsic validity, or accept some form of circularity? Also tell me whether there is a single revision that would clean up the most tension at once, or whether the conflicts are localized and require separate fixes. I am not asking for a bibliography or a reading list; I want to know whether my own profile is stable.
