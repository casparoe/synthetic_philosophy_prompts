(1) Every historical record comes from someone with power and interests.
(2) So every archive is systematically skewed toward whoever could preserve or destroy docs.
(3) To correct that skew we'd need an unbiased sample of the past, but no such sample exists.
(4) Therefore every historical claim is just an interpretation of biased sources, with no real fact of the matter behind it's narrative.
(5) But if there's no fact of the matter, then saying 'history is written by the victors' can't be objectively true — it's just another power-laden claim.
(6) So the relativist view ends up undermining itself.
(7) The only escape is to admit some historical claims are objectively true despite bias, which feels like naive objectivism.
(8) So we're stuck between self-refutation and naive objectivism.

I feel like something slipped between (3) and (4), maybe an equivocation on 'unbiased access' versus 'no facts exist'? Or maybe (5) makes a scope error?
