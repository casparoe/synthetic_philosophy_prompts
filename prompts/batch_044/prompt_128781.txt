Below are the working notes for an essay on Strawson’s account of reactive attitudes and moral responsibility. I want to press a specific tension that I think is under-examined. Strawson’s familiar taxonomy—resentment, gratitude, indignation, and related participatory responses—seems to depend on what I am calling a singular addressability condition: the attitude is directed at a particular agent who is conceived as the direct source or origin of the action that prompts the response. From this, I derive an argument that the framework struggles to accommodate what I term distributed reactive attitudes, particularly distributed resentment: moral responses directed at institutional roles, collective procedures, or systemic patterns rather than at identifiable individuals. I consider whether such responses can be reconstructed within Strawson’s terms—say, as vicarious or analogical extensions of interpersonal resentment—or whether they are better classified as objective attitudes, which would imply that institutional moral life is structurally depersonalized in his scheme. I tentatively conclude that the reconstruction fails because distributed resentment retains the affective and participatory quality characteristic of reactive attitudes but lacks the singular target required by the addressability condition. Before I develop this further, I would like comments on four points. First, is the singular addressability condition actually present in Strawson’s framework, or am I importing a dyadic bias? Second, is distributed resentment conceptually coherent as a reactive attitude rather than as an objective assessment of harm? Third, does Strawson’s distinction between personal and vicarious reactive attitudes already resolve the problem I am raising? Fourth, if the argument holds, does it reveal a limitation in the framework or merely show that institutional morality belongs to a different domain altogether? I am not asking for a bibliography or for a survey of secondary literature; I want conceptual pressure on these specific claims.
