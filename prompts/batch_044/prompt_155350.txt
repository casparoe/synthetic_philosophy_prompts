I'm on this volunteer ethics board at my city's public health lab — not a scientist, just a citizen seat — and next wednesday we have to vote on a pilot study called "Gradient Zero." The researchers want to use targeted gene-therapy to suppress the neural pathways for chronic emotional distress in healthy volunteers, basically testing whether you can biologically enforce a "hedonic floor" where people dont feel bad ever. They frame it as step one of the hedonistic imperative, that idea about using biotech to abolish suffering altogether. Half the board thinks its a breakthrough, the other half says its messing with human nature and we should reject it flat. Can you draft the short memo I'd circulate before the vote? It should recommend one concrete course — approve with strict conditions, or reject — with reasons the researchers could actually respond to, and it needs to deal honestly with the strongest argument on the losing side, like whether pain is actually valuable for being human. Also please have the memo say how confident it is in its own claims and single out the one point it's least sure about. Sorry if I spelled hedonic wrong up there, and I know I ramble.
