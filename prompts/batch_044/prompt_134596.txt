I'd like a consistency audit on a cluster of commitments I've developed about language, meaning, and conceptual boundaries, largely within a Wittgensteinian framework but with one or two adjustments of my own. Here is the package:

(1) I take family resemblance to govern ordinary-language kind terms—game, language, number—such that no single necessary and sufficient condition is shared by all members, and attempts to impose one are category mistakes.

(2) I accept the private-language argument in its standard form: a speaker in complete isolation could not establish rules for correct use, because rule-following requires publicly checkable criteria.

(3) I hold that meaning is use, but I also maintain a strict distinction between grammatical propositions, which express internal relations of a language-game, and empirical propositions, which describe states of affairs within it.

(4) I treat form of life not as one more language-game but as the biologically and culturally invariant bedrock that makes agreement in judgments possible; where forms of life diverge radically, rational disagreement ends.

(5) I adopt what I call threshold institutionalism: once a practice achieves sufficient normative density—through explicit codification, professional gatekeeping, or formal axiomatization—the concepts internal to it cease to be family-resemblance governed and become defined by stipulative constitutive rules, with the transition being conventional rather than discovered.

(6) I think that recognizing an alien intelligence as engaging in language-games requires only that we can map family-resemblance links at the level of basic coordination practices—turn-taking, error-correction, rule-invocation—even if its underlying form of life is otherwise unrecognizable to us.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each tension, specify exactly which commitments pull against each other, what would have to give, and what the cheapest revision costs. Do not assume I am already inclined to drop any particular commitment; treat the set symmetrically. In particular, examine whether (4) and (6) can both stand given the invariance claim in (4), whether (1) and (5) conflict over the status of technical concepts like number, and whether (3) creates an unnoticed problem for (5) that actually dissolves once use is properly understood within institutional contexts. Be specific about the commitments involved rather than giving a general lecture on Wittgenstein.
