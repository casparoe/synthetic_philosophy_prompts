A podcast summarized the Hart-Devlin legal moralism debate like this: Devlin said law should back up shared morality because society is like a rope made of threads - pull out enough private immoral acts and the whole thing unravels. He claimed homosexuality is as dangerous to social cohesion as treason, and that we should use intense disgust and indignition as the test for what needs banning. Hart, in Law, Liberty, and Morality, called this majoritarian tyranny and said harm has to be physical or tangible - no bodily injury, no legal interferance. The podcast said Hart clearly wins since Devlin never proves private acts actually destroy society.

I'm skeptical but I can't spot the errors. Is this fair? Did Devlin literally equate homosexuality with treason, or is that a sloppy reading of his analogy? Was disgust meant as a rational standard or just as evidence of shared feeling? Does Hart really limit harm to physical harm, or is that a butchered version of Mill's principle he was actually defending? And is that rope metaphor even Devlin's? Separate what's right, what's a shortcut, what's wrong, and tell me whether the podcast's conclusion survives once you correct the missteps.
