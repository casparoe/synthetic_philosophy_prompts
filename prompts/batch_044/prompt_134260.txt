Please argue that Karl Popper’s criterion of falsifiability, when taken seriously as a demarcation between science and non-science, collapses once it is applied rigorously to the “protective belt” of auxiliary hypotheses that Imre Lakatos identified within mature scientific research programmes. Specifically, defend the position that any attempt to classify adjustments to this protective belt as either legitimate theoretical refinements or as ad hoc immunising stratagems necessarily imports normative commitments—such as explanatory coherence, predictive novelty, or progressive problem-shift—that Popper’s own anti-inductivist and anti-justificationist framework explicitly rejects. In doing so, address whether this failure is more damaging to the demarcation project than the well-known Duhem–Quine problem of underdetermination by auxiliary assumptions, and conclude by assessing what, if anything, remains of falsificationism if the boundary between science and pseudo-science cannot be maintained without smuggling in precisely the kind of methodological value judgements Popper sought to avoid. Please write your response in formal British English, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
