I was in my roommate's study session and two of the students got into a debate about Frankfurt cases that wouldn't quit. Alex was defending Frankfurt: "The cases prove you can be free without alternatives. You're free if you act on your own desires." Josh pushed back: "But the person's desires were engineered by an external agent. How is that freedom?"

Alex had a response ready: "But where do *any* of our desires come from? Genetics, upbringing, brain chemistry - we don't control the source of those, and we're still free. And look, the safety device in the case doesn't even activate! The person just chooses based on what they want."

Josh wasn't convinced: "That's not the same thing. Those sources are part of me, but an external agent engineering my desires is categorically different. Even if the device doesn't activate, the sheer fact that there's an apparatus there designed to guarantee the outcome—that has to matter for genuine freedom."

Alex said: "I think you're missing the whole point. Frankfurt isn't claiming this situation is ideal. He's just arguing that as a logical matter, freedom doesn't *require* alternatives. He's demonstrating that it's possible in principle."

"Maybe so," Josh replied, "but we're assuming the agent would independently want to do what they do. What if the controller has shaped their entire motivational structure, not just their preferences in this moment? Frankfurt hasn't shown that the desire is independent of the controller. He's just assumed it."

Who's right about what here?
