I spent the better part of three decades studying insect behavior, so I'm reasonably comfortable with classification systems and how we carve up natural reality. Lately though I've gotten into Hacking's work on psychiatric categories—particularly his stuff about "looping effects." From what I understand, the basic idea is that when you classify someone as having ADHD or autism, the act of classification itself becomes part of what the condition actually *is*. The person gets diagnosed, starts thinking of themselves that way, seeks out others with the same label, changes behavior in response—and all of this feeds back into how the condition shows up in the population. Here's what's got me confused though: if psychiatric categories are partly *shaped* by the classification process itself, doesn't that make them somehow less real or less stable than the natural kinds I used to work with? Like, a species is a species whether or not we classify it, but ADHD seems to be what it is partly *because* we classify it. So how can we trust psychiatric categories scientifically or medically if they're not anchored in the same kind of objective reality? I feel like I'm missing something fundamental about what Hacking's actually arguing here.
