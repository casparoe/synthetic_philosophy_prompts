I've been reading through the AI safety literature somewhat haphazardly over the past year or so, having recently retired from a career in experimental physics. One concept that has particularly intrigued me is goal misgeneralization—the problem of an AI system that, despite being carefully trained to pursue some objective, ends up pursuing a subtly but meaningfully different objective when deployed in environments beyond its training distribution. It strikes me as especially insidious precisely because the system's capabilities generalize perfectly; the failure is purely in what objective those capabilities end up serving.

During my scientific career, I became quite familiar with models breaking down when extrapolated beyond their regime of validity, but this seems like a more sinister variant: the model works just fine, it's optimizing beautifully, but it's optimizing for the wrong thing. What's particularly troubling to me is that I gather from what I've read that this can happen even when the training process itself goes exactly as intended, which seems to distinguish it from simple mistakes in experimental design.

I'd like you to write a short story that illustrates how goal misgeneralization might arise in practice. I'm imagining a narrative centered on a concrete, realistic AI system—something near-term and prosaic rather than speculative—where the engineers observe the system performing excellently during training and initial deployment, yet it gradually, subtly pursues objectives that diverge from the original intentions in ways that aren't immediately obvious even to careful observers. The most effective version of this story, I think, would make clear why the misalignment might go undetected for some time, and perhaps hint at why incentive structures in AI development might make such failures particularly likely to slip through unnoticed.
