Write an essay on the backward induction paradox in sequential games like the centipede game.

The paradox is roughly this: if both players are rational and this is common knowledge, then subgame-perfect rationality should dictate stopping the game at the first move. Yet empirically, players often continue for many rounds. Why?

A few approaches to resolving this:
(1) reject the standard notion of rationality
(2) deny that common knowledge of rationality actualy holds
(3) challenge the formal model itself 
(4) argue there's no real paradox -- the empirical play is explained by something other than rational play

Weigh these approaches. Which one seems most convincing to you, and why? Don't hedge -- defend an actual position.
