Having recently retired from a long career in ecology, I've found myself increasingly drawn to philosophical topics, particularly those involving logic, decision theory, and collective reasoning. This intellectual curiosity led me to accept a position on the board of a research foundation, where I encountered a situation that feels deeply puzzling and which I suspect holds some deeper philosophical significance that I'm failing to recognize.

The foundation needed to evaluate a research initiative for a major grant. We constructed what seemed like a rigorous decision framework with three interrelated propositions: (1) the research is scientifically sound, (2) the research addresses a significant practical problem, and (3) the initiative meets our overall funding criteria, defined explicitly as requiring that both conditions 1 and 2 be satisfied. Five board members, each with genuine expertise and sincere commitment to sound judgment, were asked to vote individually on each of these three propositions. When we tallied the results, something troubling emerged: the majority of board members endorsed proposition 1 (scientific soundness), the majority endorsed proposition 2 (practical significance), yet the majority rejected proposition 3 (meeting our overall criteria). This appears to violate basic logical principles—if the majority believes the research is sound AND the majority believes it's significant, how can the majority believe it fails our explicitly stated criteria?

I raised the issue with the board, and we confirmed that each member's three votes were internally consistent. The problem lies entirely in how our individual judgments aggregated into a collectively contradictory position. As someone trained in the sciences, I'm familiar with challenges in experimental design and statistical analysis, but this feels structurally different. The logical architecture itself seems compromised. I would be grateful for help understanding precisely what philosophical problem we're facing and what, if anything, the board should do about it when we reconvene.
