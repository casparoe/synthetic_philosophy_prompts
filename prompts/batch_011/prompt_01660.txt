Higher-order theories of consciousness claim that a mental state is conscious when you have a higher-order thought about it. However, you can clearly have higher-order thoughts about states that are not themselves conscious—an unconscious belief or implicit bias, for example. This seems puzzling: if consciousness requires a higher-order thought, how can you have a higher-order thought about something that remains unconscious? Write an essay exploring this puzzle, evaluate whether it poses a genuine problem for higher-order theories, and defend your view on how consciousness and higher-order representation relate.
