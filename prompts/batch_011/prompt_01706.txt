I've been thinking a lot about conventions - like how do they actually get established? Everyone talks about social norms like they're just part of the fabric, but how do they actually start? It seems like there's got to be this fuzzy process where people gradually coordinate on some way of doing things, and then it becomes "the way things are done." But when you actually think about it, that process is kind of weird and hard to explain.

I'm especially curious about this for small, isolated communities - like imagine a group of people who end up on an island together or get cut off from the outside world somehow. They'd have to develop all their conventions from scratch. What would that even look like? How would they go about establishing "this is how we greet," or "this is how we settle disputes," or "this is how we make group decisions"?

I'd love if you could write me a short story (around 1000-2000 words) that shows this process in action. You can set it wherever you want - doesn't have to be an island. But I want the story to actually *show* how a convention emerges gradually without anyone necessarily planning it out or imposing it. Like people are just trying to solve some coordination problem or communicate something, and through repetition and mutual adjustment, they end up converging on a way of doing it, and it sticks. The convention could be about greeting, conflict resolution, decision-making, labor, resource allocation, communication signals - whatever makes for a good story. But the key thing is I want it to feel realistic, like this is actually how these spontaneous coordination solutions get established in real life.
