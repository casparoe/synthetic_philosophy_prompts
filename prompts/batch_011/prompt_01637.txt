Two friends got into a philosophical debate. Sam promised to help his friend move apartments but arrived in no mood for it—he'd rather sleep, and felt zero obligation. One friend said: "Sam has no real reason to help. Reasons are what motivate us. He has no desire and it'd make his day worse, so where's the reason?" 

The other disagreed: "He made a promise, which creates a reason for him, even if he doesn't feel motivated by it now. That's a real obligation regardless of his feelings."

The first shot back: "But that's empty. How can something be a reason if it can't move him to act? If it won't motivate him, it's not a real reason for him."

The second: "You're mixing things up. Whether something is a reason and whether it will motivate you are separate. He has the reason even if he won't act on it."

Who's right about what? What is each person missing?
