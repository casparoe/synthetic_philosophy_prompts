Two friends were arguing about how we can know anything. One said you can't know something unless you have reasons for believing it, but then you need reasons for *those* reasons, and so on. He said at some point you gotta just accept some basic truths without justification, or else the chain never ends and you don't know anything. The other one said that's the whole problem -- you can't just decide to stop asking for reasons and call that knowledge. She suggested instead that maybe our beliefs all support each other in a web, so you don't need some special foundation to stand on. He said that sounds circular and unreliable -- if your whole belief system is wrong, the beliefs propping each other up doesn't make them true. She said well, by his logic one of three things has to be true: either there's some unjustified starting point (which seems unfounded), or the regress is infinite (which seems impossible), or beliefs can loop back on themselves. He then said he was actually fine with the infinite regress -- that doesn't bother him. She pushed back: if you're always required to produce new reasons you don't have, how do you ever actually *know* anything?

Where is each of them actually right? Is there something both of them are missing?
