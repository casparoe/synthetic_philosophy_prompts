I've been reading about the symbol grounding problem, which I understand to be about how our internal symbols can come to represent or refer to things in the external world. I'm skeptical that this constitutes a genuine philosophical difficulty. In my work as a software engineer, I encounter this issue regularly, and it seems straightforward to solve. When I encode information in code, I ground my symbols through explicit mappings to worldly referents: the symbol 'distance' gets its meaning through a defined connection to measured spatial quantities; 'RED' is grounded through its mapping to specific wavelengths or pixel values; 'user_id' connects to identifiers in a database. These causal and functional relationships seem to establish what each symbol is about.

The philosophical literature often frames symbol grounding as if it's a profound puzzle or perhaps even insoluble, but I'm genuinely confused about what remains mysterious once the mappings are made explicit. If the problem is asking how symbols acquire meaning, doesn't specifying these connections fully answer the question? What aspect of the problem am I not seeing?
