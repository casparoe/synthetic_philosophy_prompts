Write a dialogue between someone defending the handicap principle / costly signaling as an explanation for luxury consumption, and someone skeptical of this framing. The skeptic's main argument: luxury goods get faked all the time, and they're not actually that costly relative to the purchaser's wealth anyway. So it seems weird to invoke honest costly signaling as the explanation -- maybe it's just that having taste/money lets you buy nice things, and the correlation is easy enough to detect without needing the signal to literally be super expensive?

The handicap principle defender should actually grapple with the fakery and relative-cheapness points rather than just reasserting their view.
