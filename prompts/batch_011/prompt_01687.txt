For my philosophy of language course, I need to write a dialogue between a defender of Grice's distinction between what is said and what is implicated, and a critic who argues that Grice's account is fundamentally too narrow. The defender should explain why Grice correctly captures the saying-implicating distinction by grounding "saying" in the conventional meaning of the words uttered. The critic should contend that this approach fails to account for cases where what speakers say depends essentially on context—contexts where conventional meaning underdetermines what is actually said—and that this limitation undermines Grice's entire framework. The two should engage with each other's core arguments substantively, and where it would be illuminating, they should discuss a concrete example to ground their disagreement.
