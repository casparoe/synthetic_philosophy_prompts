I've been going back and forth on whether I want to have kids, and over the past few months I've been talking with friends about it a lot. Everyone's position is basically the same: "Sure, life has hard parts, but it's also full of joy and meaning and deep connection. You can't imagine how special it would be. You'd be missing out on one of the most meaningful experiences a person can have." I get that argument and I'm not trying to dismiss it. But here's what keeps nagging at me: there's something that feels off about how we're supposed to weigh these things against each other, and I can't quite articulate what.

So like, if I decide not to have kids, you could say they're missing out on all that happiness and meaning. But they wouldn't exist to actually experience that loss, right? Like, there's no one there to feel sad about not being born. But if I do have them, even if their life ends up being pretty good overall, they're going to suffer—pain, illness, anxiety, loss, whatever. That suffering would be *real* and they'd actually experience it. It seems really weird to just put those two scenarios on a scale and weigh them like they're commensurate. The suffering of an actual person feels like it carries way more weight than the forfeited happiness of someone who never existed. But is that actually a good way to think about it, or am I just rationalizing a pessimistic intuition? I'm genuinely confused about how to properly reason through this.
