Heard an argument at lunch about Wittgenstein's take on religious belief. One person said: 'Wittgenstein's whole idea is that religion isn't making empirical claims—it's a form of life, its own kind of practice.' Someone else said: 'So basically you're saying religious belief isn't actually about reality?' First person: 'No, you're presupposing that only empirical claims are about reality. That's the confusion. Religious practice has its own internal logic.' Skeptic: 'OK, but then different religions can't actually contradict each other. They're just different forms of life. Doesn't that make all of them equally valid?' First person: 'Within their own framework, yeah. But why should they all operate under the same framework?' Skeptic: 'Because actual believers think there's one truth about God or the afterlife, not just different valid frameworks. If Wittgenstein's right, believers are confused about what they're doing.'

Who's getting Wittgenstein's actual position right at each point? What's he trying to do that both of these people might be missing?
