G.E.M. Anscombe objected vehemently to Oxford's decision to honor President Truman, arguing that his ordering of atomic bombings on Hiroshima and Nagasaki amounted to murder. Yet accounts of her position contain an apparent paradox: she herself conceded that the bombing likely shortened the war and saved lives on balance. If the utilitarian calculation favored Truman's action, how can Anscombe simultaneously maintain that it was murder? What principle allows her to reject the very consequentialist framework that would seem to vindicate the bombing, whilst refusing to embrace wholesale pacifism?
