I was talking with two colleagues about why Twitter became so dominant for real-time news (this was before the whole Musk thing took over). One of them said it was basically because Jack Dorsey and the early team made a series of brilliant individual decisions -- the 140 character limit forced interesting constraints, they nailed the follower/retweet model, good execution and all that. He said if different people had been running things or made different product choices, we'd probably be using something totally different today.

The other person pushed back hard. She said Twitter's dominance was almost inevitable given the technological landscape and what people needed at that moment. She said the platform succeeded because market conditions were right, and while Dorsey & co. executed well, if they hadn't existed someone else would have built something similar. She even claimed specific design choices only mattered at the margins.

He then said "Wait, so you're saying individuals don't matter? That's obviously wrong -- specific human decisions literally shape history." But she clarified: "No, I'm saying that *which* individuals and *which* decisions matter depend on the structural environment. Individual action still shapes things, but it's heavily constrained by what's possible at that moment."

He shot back: "But that's unfalsifiable -- whenever something happens, you just say it was structurally inevitable. How would you ever know if an individual decision actually changed the outcome?"

She said: "By looking at whether the same outcome happens across different contexts when structural conditions are similar, regardless of who the specific individuals are."

He seemed unconvinced but honestly I couldn't tell who was actually winning the argument. Can you walk through who's right about what here, and what either of them might be missing?
