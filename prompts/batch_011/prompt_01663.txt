I'm reading through Galileo's Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina for my dissertation (history of science), and I keep getting tripped up on one passage. He's making this argument that we shouldn't subordinate physical conclusions to biblical passages, and part of his justification is this doctrine about "accommodation"—the idea that the Bible uses language adapted to common understanding.

Here's the bit that's confusing me. He writes: "These propositions uttered by the Holy Ghost were set down in that manner by the sacred scribes in order to accommodate them to the capacities of the common people, who are rude and unlearned. For the sake of those who deserve to be separated from the herd, it is necessary that wise expositors should produce the true senses of such passages, together with the special reasons for which they were set down in these words."

So my question is: how exactly is this doctrine supposed to support his case that astronomical observations about heliocentrism shouldn't be condemned? Like, he's trying to say the Bible isn't actually making claims about physical matters when rightly interpreted, but when he talks about "wise expositors" finding the "true senses," isn't he kind of opening the door to the interpretation problem he's supposedly trying to block? If theologians can reinterpret Scripture to match whatever discoveries come along, how is that actually constraining the relationship between Scripture and nature at all? Or am I misreading where he's going with this?
