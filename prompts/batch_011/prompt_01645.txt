Two people in my philosophy of AI seminar got into it about wether neural networks actually have representations. One person (Alex) argued that when we analyze a neural net, we find meaningful structure that encodes information about the world - like when you find neurons that respond to specific concepts. That's a representation in the most important sense. The other person (Jordan) said that's just us projecting meaning onto statistical patterns. The network doesn't "represent" anything any more than a correlation in weather data "represents" future rainfall - we interpret it that way, but the network itself is just doing matrix multiplications.

Alex pushed back: "But we can use those patterns to predict and manipulate the world, which is what representations are supposed to do." Jordan said: "Yeah, but that just shows the patterns are useful. A thermostat regulates temperature without having any representation of temperature. Usefulness doesn't make something a genuine representation." Alex then said: "But a thermostat is way simpler and doesn't have internal structure encoding specifics about the world. Neural nets are different in degree and arguably in kind." Jordan: "Not really - both are just input-output systems. The diference is just complexity. Saying a neural net has representations because it's complicated is just anthropomorphizing."

They went in circles after that. Who's actually right here, and what are they each getting right or wrong about what makes something a representation?
