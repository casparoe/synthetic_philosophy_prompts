So I was reading about epistemology the other day (yeah, weird hobby for a software engineer, but bear with me) and I came across this debate that's apparently been going on for centuries called the regress problem. It's got me genuinely confused.

The basic issue is: you believe something because you have evidence for it, right? But then you need evidence that your evidence is good. And evidence for *that*. And then evidence for that evidence... and it feels like you're stuck in an infinite loop. Philosophers have come up with three main ways to escape this: foundationalism (some beliefs are just self-evident and don't need further justification), coherentism (beliefs are justified by how well they fit together with your other beliefs), and infinitism (it's fine if the justifications literally just go on forever).

I was arguing about this with a friend and we couldn't figure it out lol. Could you write a dialogue between a foundationalist and a coherentist where they're actually trying to convince each other their view is right? They should both sound competent and they should actually respond to each other's real points, not just strawman each other.
