I recently received an inheritance that lets me work part-time indefinitely. As a software engineer, I've realized my skills could contribute to high-impact work on global poverty and disease prevention. But if I instead took a lucrative position at a major tech company, I could donate most of my salary to organizations with proven track records—likely affecting hundreds of lives over time. The cost would be that this path consumes nearly all my discretionary time, leaving little for personal relationships or rest.

A friend recently challenged me on the choice: if my career decision will predictably affect hundreds of lives, isn't it simply obligatory—not merely admirable—to pursue maximum impact? My inheritance complicates this; it means I have the luxury of opting out. But I'm troubled by the implication that my moral obligations shift based on economic luck. Something about their reasoning feels wrong, but I can't articulate what.
