Moore noted that while these words are not nonsensical on their own and the situation they describe could actually occur in reality, there is nevertheless something absurd about asserting them. He also observed that this absurdity disappears entirely in the past tense (when we say "I went to the pictures, but I did not believe that I did") and when we shift to other people (when we say "He went to the pictures, but he does not believe that he did").
