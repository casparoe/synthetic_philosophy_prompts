My friend and I were debating Kant this evening, and they made an argument I'm still turning over in my mind. They said that since Kant grounds morality in the categorical imperative—in the principle that we should only act on maxims we could will as universal laws—it follows that we cannot rationally will cruelty to animals as a universal law. Therefore, they concluded, animals must have direct moral status. I know Kant is famous for restricting the moral community to rational agents, but their logic seemed compelling. If the categorical imperative forbids actions that produce suffering, doesn't that suffering have to count morally in its own right, regardless of which creature experiences it?
