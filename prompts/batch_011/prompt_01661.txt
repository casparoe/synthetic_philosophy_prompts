I recently learned that 'jade' actually refers to two entirely different minerals—jadeite and nephrite—that merely look similar. What puzzles me is this: ancient artisans clearly used a single, consistent concept for their material and possessed genuine expertise with it. Yet they were mistaken about its nature. This makes me wonder about how words attach to things in the world. Did those artisans successfully refer to anything determinate, or did their concept fail because it cuts across two distinct natural kinds? It seems wrong to say they simply got their material wrong, but it also seems wrong to say 'jade' successfully refers to both minerals as one. How should I think about cases like this?
