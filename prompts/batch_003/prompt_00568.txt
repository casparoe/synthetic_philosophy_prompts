Working on my third novel and my writing group keeps flagging something I don't think is actually a flaw. My main character spends the whole book slowly falling out of love with her husband - theres no scene where she 'realizes' it, it just happens, and by the time she notices she can't say when it started or even if its fully done happening yet. The group says this is a cop-out, that she (and the reader) need one clean moment where she just knows. But honestly, when I think about my own life, I've never once caught the exact moment my feelings changed about something - I only notice after the fact, and even then part of me doubts whether I can trust the noticing, like I might know something shifted without really knowing that I know it. So is my group right that this is just lazy writing, or is there something real behind the idea that nobody actually gets that kind of clean, certain access to their own mind, even about something as big as falling out of love?
