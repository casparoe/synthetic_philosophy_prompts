I'm about a third of the way into a novel where a minor philosopher/theologian character gets absolutely torn apart by a satirical scandal-sheet, and its loosly based on what actually happened to Kierkegaard with The Corsair -- the cartoons of him with one trouser leg shorter than the other and a hunched back, kids yelling "Søren!" and "Either/Or!" at him in the street until he gave up his habit of walking around Copenhagen chatting with people. Doing research for the book I fell down a rabbit hole into "Two Ages" (the "Present Age" section especially), which he wrote right on the heels of all that, and now I can't stop chewing on his theory of "the public."

Here's what I keep going back and forth on with my writing group: is his whole apparatus -- the press as an anonymous, irresponsible non-entity, "leveling" grinding everyone down into gray sameness, the "phantom public" that isn't a people or a generation or a congregation but a kind of abstract void, the idea that a crowd can do cruel things no single member of it would ever own up to -- actually a serious, freestanding piece of social philosophy? Or is it basically a very smart, very wounded man rationalizing his own public humiliation into a whole metaphysics, one that's also pretty contemptuous of ordinary people and democracy generally? I've read takes arguing he basically predicted Twitter pile-ons and cancel culture a century and a half early, and takes calling the whole thing aristocratic sour grapes in philosophical dress. Both sides seem to have real ammunition and it's driving me a little nuts.

Could you write me an essay that actually wrestles with this instead of splitting the difference -- lay out the strongest case that the theory holds up as independent philosophy (prescience and all), the strongest case that it's a rationalized grudge shot through with elitism, maybe even whether it matters at all where an idea comes from if the argument works on its own terms -- and then land on an actual verdict? I don't want a "well, both sides have a point" ending; I want something definite enough that I could believably put it in a character's mouth.
