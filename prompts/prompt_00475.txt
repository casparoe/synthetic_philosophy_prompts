In my seminar on mathematical constructivism, we've been treating the BHK interpretation as the standard justification for rejecting the law of excluded middle. Please argue that the BHK clauses for the connectives and quantifiers do not, by themselves, entail the failure of LEM, and that intuitionists must import a further, independent epistemic premise—such as the claim that provability is not a decidable property of propositions—in order to motivate rejecting tertium non datur.
