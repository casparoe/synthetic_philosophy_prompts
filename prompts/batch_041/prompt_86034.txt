A friend in my decision theory group has an argument about acausal trade that I have not been able to break, and I suspect it proves too much.

(1) Two agents trade acausally when each one's choice is a function of the other's — the way a single decision procedure, instantiated twice, yields the same output twice.
(2) Nothing in that story requires either instantiation to cause the other, or to be near it in space or time; the link is logical or computational rather than causal.
(3) So consider every possible agent whose decision is linked to mine in that sense: my duplicates, but also any agent that predicts me by reasoning about what an agent with my decision procedure would do, and any agent whose behavior my procedure happens to predict.
(4) Since my procedure is what it is, there is a fact about how each such agent behaves conditional on what I decide.
(5) With some of those partners the gains from trade are enormous, and with the largest they are unbounded.
(6) So the value-maximizing policy is to cooperate with all of them, not merely with the ones I can identify.
(7) But that is not a policy anyone can hold: many of the partners demand exclusive commitments that contradict one another, and some are hostile to cooperation with their rivals.
(8) Therefore there is no such maximizing policy, and something upstream of it is broken.

His fallback is to restrict trade to agents whose decision procedures are identical to mine, but identity looks like a threshold on a continuum — exact copies, near-copies, agents that share only my final choice rule — and he says any cutoff is arbitrary.

I would like to keep (1) and (2); I take the acausal case seriously, at least as seriously as the Newcomb reasoning that made one-boxing seem right to me. What I want from you is where the argument actually fails, why the failing step looked fine, and what each available repair costs — in particular, which repairs leave ordinary acausal trade standing and which quietly give it up. If (7) is doing real work here, say so and say why the incoherence of the joint policy does not by itself refute the pairwise trades; if the flaw is earlier, tell me which step it is and why I should have caught it.
