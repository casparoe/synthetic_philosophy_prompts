I'm on the board of our local historical society, and we're rededicating a room named after a guy who donated the land for the library in 1912. It turns out he was also a leader in the campaign to keep Chinese kids out of the public schools here. We're writing a new plaque. One board member says we should just describe what he did and not impose our values—he was a product of his time. Another says that's a cop-out, because plenty of people in his time thought it was wrong, and anyway we're the ones writing the plaque now. A third person, who used to teach history, said something that stuck with me: there's no neutral way to say he was a product of his time, because deciding what his time thought is already us picking and choosing from the past. She said the real question isnt whether we judge him but which of our own standards we're smuggling in when we say we're not judging. I have to draft the plaque and a short note explaining our approach. How do I handle this? I dont want to either whitewash him or pretend we're not judging him.
