I am drafting the specification for a decision rule to be implemented in a resource-bounded agent — finite precision, finite running time, no oracle — that must choose among actions whose outcomes include arbitrarily small probabilities of arbitrarily large, and in some cases unbounded, value. Before I evaluate any particular proposal, I want the requirements list: what any adequate theory of rational choice under uncertainty, in this setting, has to deliver.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. I want the concrete decision problem the theory must get right; the distinction it must not collapse (for instance, between a tiny chance of an enormous finite payoff and a nonzero chance of an infinite one, or between a value that is merely large and a value that is lexicographically prior to all others); and the structural result it must be consistent with (stochastic dominance, time consistency across a sequence of choices, immunity to a sure loss or to a diachronic money pump, aggregability across affected parties). Include at least one case you construct yourself: an action that looks attractive only because of a probability so small that no bounded agent could reliably distinguish it from zero, paired with a modest but certain alternative.

The proposals I am comparing all take expected-value maximization as the baseline, and I want the requirements to settle whether that baseline forces what its critics call fanaticism — the demand to accept any nonzero chance at a sufficiently large payoff, however small the chance and however modest the sure alternative — or whether some requirement blocks it. So do the part I care most about: identify which subsets of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, and argue the conflict with the case that forces it. I expect at least one genuine impossibility here, not merely a hard tradeoff, and I want to know which single requirement, if kept, excludes the largest number of otherwise attractive theories. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable once the agent is bounded and which are merely preferences of a particular camp.

Do not survey the literature, name authors, or supply references; I want the criteria and their motivating cases, not a scorecard of who said what. Write in American English.
