ok so me and my friend got put in charge of one of the friday history club sessions and i'm kinda stuck. it's 60 minutes, about 18 of us, ages 16-17, and honestly a decent chunk of them are only there because their mates are. we just did versailles and the war guilt clause and everyone nodded along like it was obvious someone was guilty, which bugged me, because i read there's a real fight among historians about whether germany was mainly to blame (a guy called fischer i think started it and it turned into a huge row) vs the idea that everyone kind of slid into it. and the more i think about it the actual fight seems to be about who or what you're even allowed to blame. like, a whole nation? the handful of people who signed the telegrams? or the system itself, the alliances and the plans that were drawn up years before and couldn't be stopped?

so i want a game where they hit that wall themselves instead of me lecturing them about it. can you design the whole thing — roles, rules, materials, timeline, and the debrief?

heres the rough shape i had, feel free to rip it up: split us into 5 or 6 groups, each one is a country in 1914 (germany, austria-hungary, serbia, russia, france, britain). you give each group a stack of evidence cards — stuff like the blank cheque, the schlieffen plan, the naval buildup, the promises each country had made to the others, the mobilization timetables that once started were basically impossible to stop. we go three rounds; after each round every group has to write a blame chart on the board that adds up to 100%, split among the countries. and here's the rule i want: nobody is allowed to put a single percent on no one, or on circumstances, or on the system — every point has to land on a nation. then after the last round you flip over a couple of structure cards, the ones that only make sense if the culprit is the system and not any one country, and ask them what the hell they do with those under the rules they've been playing by.

what i want them to walk out seeing is that the same pile of facts can support germany was mainly to blame or everybody was to blame depending on whether you're blaming people, states, or structures, and that the rules of the game quietly forced one answer on them. so the debrief needs to connect the moment they couldn't place the structure cards to the actual philosophical problem of whether a nation can be responsible at all, and to the two historian camps.

also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time (i bet someone refuses to blame anyone, and the adds-up-to-100 rule starts a fight, and we run out of time) and how to handle it, and be honest about what the game can't reproduce and that i'll have to just tell them.
