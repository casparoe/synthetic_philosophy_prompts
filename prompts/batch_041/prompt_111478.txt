I keep going back and forth on national character and I've ended up somewhere I can just about defend: it's a coherent notion as long as you read it as a statistical claim — a claim about how traits and attitudes are distributed across a population — and there's real, measurable, reasonably stable variation between countries that isn't just an artefact of who's asking. Given that, generalising about peoples is acceptable provided you keep it at the level of distributions, flag the spread, and don't use it to size up an individual. That feels like the sane middle to me.

So what would count against it? I want the defeaters, sorted by how much they actually threaten. I'm especially after the ones that go at the statistical reading itself rather than at the rude uses people make of it. For instance: the worry that the same questionnaire doesn't mean the same thing in different places, because people answer a question like 'how tidy are you?' against a local yardstick, so a country where everyone is like that comes out average; or that a genuine difference in means tells you next to nothing when the spread inside a country swamps the gap between countries; or that 'national' is just the wrong unit and the whole thing collapses into a claim about institutions, or about class and cohort, at which point it isn't character at all. There's also the stability point — how much can a countries profile shift in a generation before the notion stops doing any work? And does the track record of earlier attempts to profile whole societies count against the project or only against the way it was done?

Then the other half, which I care about more. Suppose all of that came out fine — would that make it ok to generalise? Tell me what would have to be true for a generalisation to be permissable in practice, what a defender of my view has to concede versus what they can wave away, and whether truth is even the relevant question. The thing that would move me most is a case where the statistical claim is true and the generalisation is still the wrong thing to make; if you've got that, it kills the second half outright. And if it turns out I've quietly qualified the view into something nothing could touch, say so.

Don't give me examples of what particular nations are supposedly like — I want the methodology and the concepts, not a list of stereotypes. And skip the reading list, I'm not going to chase citations.
