In a reading group on the cosmological argument, a member closed down debate by intoning that "out of nothing, nothing comes" — evidently to show that the universe must have a cause — and none of us could say precisely which claim that one-liner had just made, or whether it was even consistent with his own affirmation of creation ex nihilo. Give me the menu of distinct theses that sentence could be expressing, ordered from the trivial to the radical and sensitive to how "nothing" and "comes" are being read (absence of any thing versus a bare vacuum or field; creation of substances versus coming-to-be of events); for each, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible principle, or false, and whether it is the reading the argument actually needs — for the kalam premise that whatever begins to exist has a cause, and separately for the contingency version, if those need different readings. Give exactly five readings as a numbered list, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
