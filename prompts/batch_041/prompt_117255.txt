I'm a postdoc applying for a six-month pilot grant in digital ethics and I need a two-page proposal. My half-formed idea: work on AI paternalism keeps asking when an assistant may refuse or override a user's request, and treats a refusal as a discrete event that a principle licenses or forbids (self-harm, third-party harm, irreversibility, and so on). I think that framing misses what is distinctive about the case. An assistant is not a bystander who occasionally blocks you; it is a participant in the process that produces the preferences it later evaluates. It answers some questions and not others, offers menus instead of blank prompts, asks clarifying questions that quietly redefine the goal, and declines in ways that teach the user to stop asking. A refusal can therefore be self-justifying: the assistant refuses, the user adapts, and the adapted user no longer registers a complaint, which then reads as consent. I suspect the same worry attaches to ordinary helpfulness, so the hard question is whether doing good and dominating can be told apart here at all. Turn this into a proposal: state the question sharply enough to have a definite answer; say why the standard defenses of paternalism and the standard anti-paternalist objections do not already settle it; set out what I would actually do over six months and in what order, including how I would test the hypothesis that the fix must be procedural (a duty to keep the request re-raisable) rather than substantive; describe what a result would look like as distinct from a restatement of the worry; say what would count as the project failing; and give a candid account of scope. I will supply the citations myself, so do not spend words on them.
