I am a high school student preparing for a debate tournament, and I have been reading about moral trade: the proposal that two people who disagree about values can nonetheless both come out ahead by exchanging actions. The standard illustration is a vegetarian who cares chiefly about animals agreeing to give a percentage of her income to an anti-poverty charity, in return for a donor who cares chiefly about poverty giving up meat — each party then regards the resulting world as better than the status quo.

In the explanations I have come across, this is almost always put by analogy with international trade. Two countries gain by specialising according to their comparative advantage even when one is more productive at everything, and moral trade is supposed to work the same way: the disagreement between the parties plays the role of differing opportunity costs, the exchange is a Pareto improvement, and so it is presented as uncontroversially good.

I would like that analogy audited rather than repeated. Please make the correspondence explicit — what plays the role of the two countries, the goods, the production possibilities, the opportunity costs, the exchange rate, and the market that discovers the price — and then say where the mapping breaks. I am especially interested in the disanalogies that bear on the conclusion the analogy is being used to support, for instance: that the actions traded usually fall on third parties who are not at the table and cannot consent; that in economics a preference can be traded away without any loss of integrity, whereas a moral conviction presents itself as a claim about what is true and binding, so a person willing to trade it may seem not to have held it as a conviction at all; and that economic exchange rates are discovered by markets with many participants, while moral exchange rates are set bilaterally by bargaining power. Distinguish the disanalogies that genuinely undermine the "uncontroversially good" conclusion from those that are merely picturesque, and say whether that conclusion survives intact, survives in weakened form, or fails. If it fails, propose a better analogy for what moral trade is and for what it can and cannot achieve.

Please keep the whole response to a maximum of 800 words, and lead with your verdict.
