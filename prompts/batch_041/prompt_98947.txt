I'm putting together materials for a working group on advance directives, and two of our regulars have been circling each other for months. He thinks a directive signed while competent should bind the later self even when that later self is now demented, cheerful, and saying she wants to live — his line is that the earlier person had a stake in the shape of her whole life and gets to set the terms. She thinks only the preferences of the self that is actually living the life have authority, and that we're otherwise treating the earlier person as the owner of the later one. They keep writing separate seminar papers at each other. I want the joint statement instead.

Draft the document they could both sign. What do they actually agree on — about what a person is across time, about whether a competent refusal and a demented "no" are the same kind of thing, about what even makes a preference authoritative? Then the disagreements, each half worded so the person who holds it would sign their own wording. Then the cruxes, and for each crux a case or piece of evidence both of them would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance — and say which cruxes are probably just verbal.

One thing I want them forced to deal with: two directive designs. A "fade" clause, where the directive stops binding unless the person reaffirms it at set intervals, and a "lock" clause, where the earlier self makes it un-overridable. Does the fade clause move anyone, and if it doesn't, why not — is it because the later reaffirmation is itself suspect, or because the earlier self already priced that in? Note that a demented patient's stated preferences can flip from visit to visit, which seems to matter to one of these views more than the other.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. At the end, add a short note on which of the agreed claims is the most fragile — what argument would blow it up — and where your own framing of the whole dispute is most likely to be wrong, ie what would have to be true for you to have set it up badly.
