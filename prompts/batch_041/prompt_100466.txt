I write an ideas column and I'm assembling a fiction feature on hard choices — stories that take the philosophy of value conflict seriously instead of using it as mood. I'd like you to write one for it.

Write a short story, roughly 1,200–1,800 words, built around a single decision that the character cannot settle by appeal to any common measure. The catch is that I don't want the familiar shape in which the tension comes from picking between a smaller and a larger harm, so that the arithmetic is monstrous but undeniable. I want the harder and stranger case: two options that are genuinely comparable — it is not a category mistake to weigh them, the way it would be to ask whether a sonnet is heavier than a promise — and yet neither is better than the other, and they are not simply equal either. If you want a label, call it near-parity: a case where reason can say "these two belong in the same league" but cannot say "take this one." The character has to actually choose. No paralysis, no third door, no outside force deciding on their behalf. The story should be honest about what the choice costs and about what it fails to settle.

A few constraints, to keep it from drifting into the well-worn version of this material:

- Don't let a death, an emergency, or a late revelation collapse the symmetry before the decision is made.
- Don't resolve the ending by having the character uncover a hidden fact that makes one option plainly better; if the resolution needs such a fact, you've written a different story.
- The goods in conflict should be specific and recognizable — loyalty against honesty, a vocation against a person, a promise to the dead against a duty to the living — rather than abstractions like Good against Evil.
- Keep the narrator out of the way. Let the difficulty be visible in the scene, not announced in commentary.

You don't need to name or cite any philosopher or text; I care about the dramatization, not the scholarship. Please write in American English.

After the story, give me a short structured summary for the magazine's metadata, returned as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "title": the story's title, as a string.
- "word_count": your approximate word count, as a number.
- "the_choice": one or two sentences describing the decision and the two options.
- "values_in_tension": an array of short strings naming the specific goods pulling in each direction.
- "why_neither_option_wins": two or three sentences on why the comparison bottoms out without a verdict, rather than one option simply outweighing the other.
- "what_the_choice_settles": one or two sentences on what the ending resolves and what it leaves open.
- "concept_illustrated": one sentence naming the philosophical idea the story dramatizes.
