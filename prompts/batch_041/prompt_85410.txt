I am setting the final examination for an upper-level undergraduate metaphysics seminar. The exam is closed-book, and the last unit of the course is the problem of saying when some things compose a further thing, usually called the special composition question. By the end of term my students can recite the standard menu of answers — nihilism, universalism, and the assorted moderate proposals — without having any grasp of why the menu has only two stable entries. I want one essay question that makes them work that out rather than reproduce it, together with the answer standard I will mark against.

Please build the question around the following candidate answer, which I have written for this exam and which should appear in the question in exactly the form given here:

(CU) Some things compose a further thing just in case an intervention on some of those things — a change in them that is not produced by the others — would make a difference to the properties of the others.

Students should be told only that CU is a moderate answer in the spirit of proposals that treat composition as a matter of causal or dynamical integration, and that the question is whether it survives. Nothing in the question should signal its fate.

The question should have three parts: first, an account of why the pressure that makes moderate answers hard to sustain applies to any moderate answer and not merely to the well-known ones; second, an assessment of CU that identifies which horn of that pressure it lands on, if any, and what defects it has independently of that pressure; third, a verdict on what a defensible answer would have to look like, including whether the question itself should be rejected. It should be answerable in sixty minutes.

Deliverables: the question as it will appear on the paper; a model answer of roughly 700 words; and a marking scheme consisting of five must-hit points worth 25 marks in total, each with its mark value, a one-line statement of what a top answer must contain, and a one-line note on how candidates typically get that point wrong; plus a short band descriptor distinguishing an A script from a B script. The scheme should be written so that a candidate who names the standard positions and their usual slogans cannot reach the middle band on that basis alone. No reading list or citations should be required of the students, and the model answer should not lean on any.
