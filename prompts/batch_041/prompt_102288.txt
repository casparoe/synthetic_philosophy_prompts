Last night I spent four hours arguing with a friend about Kavka's toxin puzzle, and we came to realize that we disagree less about the puzzle's answer than about what would count as an answer at all. I would like your credence in one precisely stated claim, together with the decomposition that produces it.

The setup: a billionaire will pay you a very large sum if, by midnight tonight, you form a genuine intention to drink a vial of toxin tomorrow. The toxin will make you violently ill for a day and leave no lasting harm. He pays on the intention alone; whether you actually drink is irrelevant to the payment, and he will never make the offer again. You know all of this now, and you also know that once the money is in your account you will have no reason whatsoever to drink, since at that point the illness buys you nothing.

The claim I want a number on, call it C, is that a fully rational and fully informed agent can satisfy this offer: sincerely intend at midnight, collect the payment, and then decline to drink, with no stage of the sequence constituting a failure of rationality and no self-deception anywhere in it. I mean possible in the sense that there exists a coherent, non-self-deceiving description of such an agent. If you think the question as stated is ill-posed, give your credence in the best precisification you can state, and tell me what you had to change in order to make it precise.

My friend put himself at roughly 0.15; I put myself at roughly 0.6, and neither of us could say what we were actually disagreeing about. Give me your own credence, but more importantly, decompose it. I want a number and a one-line justification for at least the following:

1. Whether intending is something an agent does at will or something that happens to her given her evidence and dispositions, and if the latter, whether indirect self-management, such as habituation, pre-commitment, self-hypnosis, or deliberate cultivation of character, can carry her to the midnight intention.

2. Whether an intention can be genuine at midnight if the agent knows with certainty that tomorrow's payoff structure will give her decisive reason to abandon it, that is, whether intending now and rationally abandoning the intention later is a coherent combination, or whether an intention one is certain to abandon was never more than a wish.

3. Whether the puzzle is better read as a reductio of any decision theory that evaluates each act by its expected utility at the time of acting, rather than as a description of a possible psychology, and correspondingly, how much of your credence in C survives if you accept such a theory.

4. Whether the agent's history and character matter, specifically whether someone with a long track record of doing repellent things for money can sincerely intend where a fastidious agent cannot, and if so, whether that establishes C or merely relocates the puzzle into the distribution of psychologies.

5. Whether the answer changes when the offer is renewable, say the billionaire repeats it nightly for a year, paying each time you form a fresh sincere intention, so that an agent with a reliable intention-forming capacity could accumulate an absurd fortune without ever drinking. Does that absurdity refute C, or only show that such a capacity is rare?

6. Anything else you think belongs, including what genuine intention would have to mean for a payment conditioned on it to be intelligible at all.

Then combine the terms, give the resulting number, and tell me which term your number is most sensitive to and what argument or evidence would move it most. Finally, if your decomposition allows you to tell, say whether my friend and I most likely differ on 1 or on 3.
