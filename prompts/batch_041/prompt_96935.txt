I'm in a metaphysics reading group that's been circling personal identity and de re modality for weeks now, and the "could you have come from a different sperm and egg" question has started to drive me a bit up the wall, mostly because everyone argues about it using the same two or three cases and I've come to suspect the real disagreement is about what we're demanding of an answer, not about the cases. So before we read anything else I want the specification written down first: what does any adequate theory of what is essential to a person (or to an organism generally -- I don't think the theory should have to draw that line itself, but say so if you think it does) actually have to deliver? Give me the full list of requirements -- the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the results it has to stay consistent with, the questions it should be allowed to leave open -- with the case or consideration behind each requirement, and then mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are really just commitments of one camp (origin essentialists, haecceitists, the people who think the whole question is confused, four-dimensionalists, whoever).

Then the part I care most about: say which subset of these requirements can't be jointly satisfied, if any. I suspect there's at least one genuine clash, and if so I want the list itself to be the result rather than a shopping list. Some cases I want the list to cover that don't usually come up in the group: a person who is a tetragametic chimera, i.e. two embryos that fused into one body with two distinct cell lines, so that no single genome is even necessary for being that person and there may be no single zygote you can point to as where they came from; and pushing the other way, a child whose genome was edited after fertilization so that it now matches what a different egg and sperm would have produced -- if your theory says that child's origin is the edited genome, or the donor's gametes, or neither, say which and what it costs. Also, what should the theory say about someone who began as one cell of a very early split embryo where the other half was absorbed -- is there a fact of the matter about which cell they came from, or is that a question the theory has to be permitted to leave unanswered? And I want to know whether "couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg" should be derived from something more basic or taken as a primitive, and what follows either way -- especially for the fact that this is supposed to be necessary but only knowable after the fact, which I've never heard explained in a way that didn't feel like a magic trick. Also does the theory owe us any account of why the intuition is so strong here but so weak for, say, which lump of bronze a statue came from, or is that just a different topic? Finish by running two or three of the going views against your list in a line each if you have room, but the list is what I actually want. No citations please, I'm not going to check them and half of them would be wrong anyway.
