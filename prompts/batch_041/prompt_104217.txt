I spent thirty years as a geologist and only lately got into moral philosophy, so I'm missing a lot of the vocabulary. Here's a pattern I keep noticing and I don't have a word for it. Two people disagree quite deeply about what matters. Say one is a committed vegan and the other is devoted to keeping a family farm alive. They can still strike a bargain: the farmer spends a weekend helping build a wildlife refuge that the vegan cares about more than she cares about a weekend of her own time, and the vegan spends a weekend doing some chore that keeps the farm going, a chore she thinks matters less than the refuge does. Neither has changed their mind, each still thinks the other is weighting something wrongly, but by each of their own lights they both come out ahead. It isn't just ordinary trade, because what's being swapped isn't goods, it's effort spent on things the other person happens to value. And it isn't compromise, because nobody gives up a belief.

Does this have a name? I assume it's been noticed before, and what I want is less the label than the boundaries: what's the closest existing idea (log-rolling? comparative advantage?) and exactly where does the analogy break? Two things I can't sort out on my own. First, does it still count if the thing you're doing for the other person is something you think is actually wrong, and not just lower priority? Second, does it matter if the two people share the same values but disagree about the facts -- is that the same phenomenon or a seperate one? If there's no established name, coin one and tell me what falls inside it and what sits just outside.
