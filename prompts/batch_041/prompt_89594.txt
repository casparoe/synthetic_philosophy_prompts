I am assembling a multi-investigator grant proposal on the metaphysics of laws of nature, with room for roughly five subprojects staffed by early-career philosophers over three years. The review panel will include physicists and historians of science who suspect that the Humean/anti-Humean dispute is a clash of intuitions with no way to make progress, so the proposal has to argue, not assume, that there is fundable work here.

Which open questions should the five subprojects be built around, and in what order should I rank them? I care more about the reasoning than about the list. For each question: why it is upstream of the others (which other questions in the area would be settled or reframed if this one were); how tractable it is for people trained in metaphysics and philosophy of science rather than in physics or mathematics; how crowded it already is, so I can tell whether the proposal would add anything; and what would count as real progress within three years.

I am told the live fault lines are no longer just "Humean supervenience, yes or no." Framings I have heard include: whether the special sciences' ceteris paribus generalizations can qualify as laws under a best-system account at all; whether laws are better identified with symmetry principles than with either regularities or necessitation relations; whether the idea that laws "govern" the world is coherent or is a metaphor doing illicit work; whether grounding talk illuminates the debate or only relabels it; whether the dispute survives the possibility that fundamental physics has no laws in the traditional sense; and whether "law" names a single thing or instead a cluster of roles — explanation, counterfactual support, inductive projection, the organizing of special-science generalizations — that come apart in different cases, in which case the Humean/anti-Humean dispute may be several disagreements that only look like one. Treat these as candidate framings, not a fixed menu: add ones I have missed, say which are genuinely distinct and which are the same question in different dress, and drop the dead ends.

Close by telling me which single question, if it were settled, would most change the rest of the agenda; what result or argument over the next few years would move a question up or down my ranking; and which currently popular question I should decline to fund even so.
