I live in a housing co-op of about twenty households, and we have begun drafting a constitution for how the group decides things. Writing down what I actually believe has left me with the uncomfortable feeling that not all of it can be true at once. Here is where I have landed.

(1) If every member prefers one option to another, the group should choose the option everyone prefers; it seems absurd to choose what everyone agrees is worse.
(2) Each member has a few matters that are nobody else's business — what they read, who they spend the night with, what they do with their own body — and on those matters the group must not overrule the member, however many others want otherwise.
(3) Preferences about how other people conduct their own private lives — wanting a housemate to stop reading a certain book, wanting a neighbour to dress differently — should carry no weight at all.
(4) The group should decide purely by asking how well each option satisfies members' preferences; judging whether those preferences are worthwhile is not the group's business.
(5) Whatever comes up, the group should be able to say which of two options it prefers, and its answers should never run in a circle.
(6) To make (2) and (3) work, I would settle the list of private matters once, by unanimous agreement, and thereafter keep those matters off the agenda, with everything else decided by counting preferences.
(7) And I am sure that if a member asked the group to forbid him from doing something in his own private sphere, I would refuse: that sphere is not something a person can hand over, even willingly.

I suspect some of these cannot survive together, and I would rather find out now than in the middle of a fight. Which items pull against each other, which only appear to, and if one has to go, which would cost me least? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity and say, for each, exactly what would have to give.
