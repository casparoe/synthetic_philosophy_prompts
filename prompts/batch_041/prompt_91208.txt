I do this maybe five times a day, and a bad call can cost us a lot of money. My boss keeps saying that if I know tomorrow-me, after sleeping on the same news, is gonna say 70%, then I should just say 70% now, otherwise I'm being irrational. I looked it up and this is basically van Fraassen's Reflection principle, and I definately want to actually use it instead of just quoting it at him. Can you write me a decision procedure I can run on a whiteboard in ten minutes for when I should defer to my future self's credence and when I shouldn't? I need the questions in order, what counts as a good answer to each, where the procedure just says no, don't defer, and where it hands the decision to me because it's a judgment call. It has to handle the case where future-me has new evidence, the case where future-me only exists if some other thing happens (like I only get to update if the deal doesn't blow up first), the case where future-me has just thought about it longer but also forgot the base rate, and the case where future-me is less tired and just better at reasoning. Then run it on two cases: (1) I'm at 60% now, but I know that after the quarterly report drops tomorrow I'll be at 85%, and (2) I'm at 40% now, and I know that if I'm still employed next month I'll be at 70%, but the only way I'm still employed is if the project succeeds. One of those should give me an answer I won't like. Also tell me where the procedure can't help and I just have to pick, and don't give me a bibliography or reading list — just the procedure.
