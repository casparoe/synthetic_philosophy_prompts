Write the joint statement that a committed teleosemanticist and a committed causal-informational theorist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the naturalisation of mental content.

By the teleosemanticist, understand someone who holds that a state represents whatever it has the proper function of indicating, where that function is conferred by a history of selection — whether natural selection, learning, or deliberate design — and who therefore holds that a state's content depends on such a history and not on its current correlations alone. By the causal-informational theorist, understand someone who holds that a state represents what it reliably carries information about under normal conditions, with content fixed by the state's present causal profile and with no essential role for any history of selection or design. Assume both are arguing their corners in good faith and are willing to have their positions made exact.

I want the document they could both put their names to: first, a numbered section of agreed claims — about what any theory of content must explain, about the phenomena both accept (misrepresentation, the disjunction problem, indeterminacy, the possibility of states that covary with nothing), and about which of the usual disputes are real; then a disagreements table, each row giving the precise point at issue and the formulation of each side's position in wording that side would accept; then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — and, for each crux, a case or argument both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Use at least the following two stipulated cases as agreed test material, and add others only if both sides would accept them. First: a signalling circuit that was selected in one species to indicate predators and to drive flight is copied by an experimenter, with no further selection, into a second species living in an ecology where the same internal state now covaries with a harmless stimulus and drives a different behaviour — what, if anything, does the state represent in the second species? Second: an engineer builds, from scratch and with no selection history, a device whose internal state reliably covaries with ambient temperature and drives a cooling response — does the state represent temperature, and if so in virtue of what?

For each side, state what it would count as evidence or argument against its own position, and say whether there is anything both sides agree would actually move the debate. If you judge that part of the dispute is merely verbal — for instance about whether learning or design counts as the relevant kind of history, or about whether the producer's correlation or the consumer's use does the fixing — say which part and why. Write the statement in American English.
