I'm drafting a novel and I want to try a scene out as a standalone short story first, so this is me workshopping it out loud.

The premise: a woman who knows every logical consequence of everything she believes. Not the obvious ones — all of them. If she believes A, and A entails B entails C entails D, she believes D too, instantly, no matter how long the chain. I know that's the usual idealization logicians slap onto knowledge and that no real person could be like that, but I want to take it seriously as a character trait and just live inside it.

What I keep circling: if you already know everything that follows from what you know, can you ever learn anything? Learning feels like ruling out possibilities you hadn't ruled out. She ruled them all out the second she believed the premises. So she never gets that click of something falling into place, and nobody can ever surprise her with an argument, because she's known the conclusion since before they opened their mouth. She can't be wrong about logic, but she can still be wrong about the world, and that's worse — her errors fan out into infinity in every direction.

The loophole I want to use, and this is the part I need the story to earn: she only gets the consequences she can represent. If a conclusion needs a concept she doesn't have, it's invisible to her. It follows from her beliefs but she doesn't believe it. So her omniscience has a hole in it, shaped exactly like her vocabulary.

Two things I want out of it: a single day that makes this feel like a genuine affliction or a genuine gift (your call which), and somewhere in there, make me feel the gap between believing something and merely being unable to believe otherwise — if that gap is even real for her. Maybe a moment where someone tries to tell her something new and she finishes their sentence, except with the actual truth, which is worse.

Under 1500 words. Don't let it turn into a lecture about logic, keep the concept inside the scenes. No citations needed, just write it.
