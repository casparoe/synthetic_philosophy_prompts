I was recently appointed to the science advisory panel that reviews the models our state environmental agency uses for rulemaking, and the first case in front of us is proving harder than I expected. The agency wants to set a new cap on nitrogen fertilizer using a regional watershed model that treats all farms as a single representative grower, assumes runoff is proportional to that year's application with no carryover from earlier years, and ignores tile drainage; the panel agrees all three assumptions are false. The agency points out that the model has tracked a decade of river nitrate measurements to within a few percent, and my colleague's position is that assumptions are only scaffolding — a model that predicts well has earned the right to be used, whatever its inner picture. The actual purpose, though, is to answer a counterfactual: how much would nitrate fall under each of three candidate cap levels. Please draft the panel's written recommendation to the agency director — a clear bottom line on whether the model may be used to set the cap and, if so, under what conditions and by what date, with the reasoning, and with a fair statement of the strongest case for my colleague's position rather than a brush-off.
