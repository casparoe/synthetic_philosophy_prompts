In Douglas Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment, a large enclosure contains a vast number of tiny magnetic marbles — simms — that careen around, collide, and cohere into transient clumps. Certain recurring clump-types, which Hofstadter calls simmballs, behave in ways that track regularities in the world outside the enclosure: the walls change their bounciness in response to external events, the simms respond, and the simmballs then interact with one another in ways that look, from a distance, like symbol manipulation. Hofstadter asks which level is doing the pushing — the simms or the simmballs. Your task is to argue for the claim that the simmballs push the simms around: that the higher level is genuinely causally efficacious, and that this is not merely a claim about which description we happen to find illuminating or convenient.

Write a single sustained argument with a clear thesis, not a survey of positions or a catalogue of considerations. You may refer to views by their content; I will supply citations myself, so do not bother with them.

Several constraints, which I would like the argument to respect:

First, fix what "push" is to mean. Say exactly what it would take for a higher-level regularity to push a lower-level event around, and defend that reading rather than one that makes the question trivial. The hard case is that the simm-level dynamics appears to fix everything: given the positions, velocities, and magnetic states of the simms, there seems to be nothing left over for the simmballs to contribute. Explain what, on your reading, the simmball level contributes nonetheless.

Second, meet the objection that the simmball level is at best a redescription — causally sufficient from below, explanatorily useful from above, and causally idle in between. If some part of that objection is correct, concede that part precisely and show that your conclusion survives it; do not simply assert that causation is level-relative and leave it there.

Third, before applying your criterion to the careenium, apply it to at least two uncontroversial cases — a traffic jam causing a delay, a rise in temperature causing a phase change, a bank run causing a collapse, or the like. Your verdict on the careenium must be consistent with your verdicts there. If your criterion vindicates the simmball level, it must not also vindicate every arbitrary gerrymandered description; if it makes the simmball level idle, say whether it also makes the traffic jam idle, and whether you accept that consequence.

Fourth, consider a criterion I am drawn to and would like you either to adopt and defend or to replace with a better one: a higher-level regularity pushes a lower-level event around when the downstream effect depends on the higher-level type rather than on its particular realizer — that is, when substituting a different micro-configuration that realizes the same simmball-type leaves the downstream simmball-behavior intact, while substituting a different simmball-type does not. Does the careenium satisfy this? Does your everyday macro-case satisfy it?

Fifth, address the fact that simmballs are defined by their role and have fuzzy, interest-relative boundaries: whether a given clump of simms counts as one simmball or two, or as a simmball at all, is not written into the simm-level facts. Does this threaten the causal status of simmballs, and if not, why not — and is the same true of the macro-objects you appealed to earlier?

Finally, close by stating the strongest objection to your own position, the one that would make you abandon it, and say what would have to be true for it to succeed.
