I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have been reading about hedonism — the claim that a person's wellbeing consists in their pleasure and the absence of pain. The objection I keep running into is that pleasures are too heterogeneous for any single thing to unify them, and every proposed repair I have looked at seems to buy its unity by quietly ignoring some class of cases. Before I read any more of them, I want the requirements document: what must a theory of pleasure deliver in order to count as an adequate solution to the heterogeneity problem? Please do not give me a survey of the literature or a list of authors; I want the specification itself.

State each requirement as a demand on the theory, and give, for each, the case or consideration that puts it on the list. I expect the list to cover at least the following, though you should add anything I have missed and drop anything that is not genuinely a requirement:

- Cases the theory must classify correctly, with the right verdict: relief pleasures, such as the pleasure of an itch finally scratched or nausea finally subsiding; anticipatory pleasures, which have no present pleasant stimulus; acquired tastes, where the same stimulus is unpleasant at first and pleasant later; pleasures taken in things that are otherwise painful or in another's suffering; pleasures with no sensory character at all, such as the satisfaction of finishing a hard problem; and states of absorbed activity in which it is unclear that any pleasure is felt.
- Distinctions it must not collapse: between a pleasure and the thing that causes it; between the intensity of a pleasure and its duration; between pleasure and mere wanting or approval; between saying what pleasure is and merely listing what produces it.
- Constraints it must be consistent with: that liking and wanting can come apart, so a person may pursue something they do not enjoy and enjoy something they do not pursue; that a person can be mistaken about what they find pleasant; and that pleasure is supposed to be something we have reason to seek.

Then mark which of these are non-negotiable and which are preferences of one camp rather than real requirements, and say plainly if any item on the list is not a requirement but an assumption smuggled in by one side of the debate. I most want to know whether any pair of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, and if so which pair — my suspicion is that the demand for a single shared feature and the demand to cover the non-sensory cases pull in opposite directions. Tell me which single requirement, if retained, rules out the most otherwise plausible theories.

Finally, in a short paragraph each and without scoring them, say how the main families of answer fare against the list: the view that pleasure is a distinctive sensation or feeling tone; the view that it is a favourable attitude taken toward an experience; the view that it is a matter of desire or motivation; and the view that pleasure is not one thing at all.

For calibration, the purpose I have in mind is this: I want the theory precise enough that it could be turned into a procedure comparing two experiences' contributions to a person's wellbeing — a long mild pleasure against a brief sharp one — without the comparison resting on introspection I do not have. If some requirement is defensible only relative to a different purpose, say so.
