ok so this is for a reading group that's been stuck for like a year, and I want to draft the document we should have produced instead of arguing. We split into two camps and I'll name them the way we actually do: the culture-as-cause side (the Weberians) and the materialist side. 

The Weberian position: the Protestant ethic thesis taken seriously — values can be a genuine cause, the predestination anxiety thing feeding into worldly asceticism and this-worldly discipline, and out of that the spirit of capitalism; plus the general claim that you can explain a big historical outcome by a shift in what people believed and valued. Elective affinity, ideal types, all of it.

The materialist position: that whole story is told after the fact. The real causes are economic conditions, technology, class, geography, state structure; the religious stuff is either downstream (people rationalize whatever position they're already in with the beliefs lying around) or a proxy for something else (literacy, print, guild structure, sitting on a trade route); and even when the correlation is real, "values did it" isn't an explanation until you can name the mechanism and rule out the rivals.

What I want is the joint statement these two camps could both sign. Specifically:

- a numbered list of claims both sides accept — about what counts as a cause, about what we even mean by culture, about the historical record, about what the thesis is and isn't claiming, about what would falsify what;
- the actual disagreements, each one written in wording the side in question would accept as fair to its own view;
- the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions where settling one would move at least one side;
- for each crux, a case or piece of evidence both sides agree would bear on it, plus what each side predicts about it, recorded in advance.

Stuff I want addressed somewhere in there: wether the thesis is even a causal claim or something weaker (interpretive adequacy, adequate causation, elective affinity) and whether that's a real difference or just two people using "cause" differently; the direction of causation between the ethic and the economy — did the beliefs cause the behavior, or did the behavior come first and the beliefs get retrofitted to it; the aggregation problem (a region's religion correlating with its economy tells you nothing about any individual's motives, so what would?); and whether "values caused it" is doing any work that "people did what their institutions rewarded" doesn't already do.

Also throw one newish thing into the pot so we're not just re-running the original fight. Someone in the group keeps insisting the live version of this isn't about religion at all but about psychology — that a society's kinship and marriage norms reshape its psychology over centuries and that's what eventually shows up as economic takeoff. Either that vindicates the culture-as-cause side or it's the same mistake in new clothes, depending who you ask. Take a position on whether the analogy helps and where it breaks.

And: is any part of this fight merely verbal? If so, say which part and why, and what both sides should just stop saying.

Format: numbered agreed-claims section first, then a table of disagreements with columns for the claim as each side words it, the crux, the case or evidence both accept, and each side's prediction.
