Hey, I'm deep in a section on the Cartesian theater vs the multiple drafts model and I need a fresh thought experiment. Not Libet, not color phi, not blindsight, not the usual Orwellian/Stalinesque pair — my comittee has seen those a hundred times and so has everyone else. I want something new that actually pulls the two views apart on the one question they genuinly disagree about: is there a fact of the matter about when a content became conscious, independent of when you probe the system for it.

What the case has to satisfy:

- one paragraph, an ordinary person doing an ordinary thing (making tea, driving to the shop, typing an email) — no lab, no electrodes, no split brains
- stipulate the behavioral and functional story completely, so both views accept every fact about processing, report, and behavior — the split has to fall out of the interpretation, not out of extra data I've smuggled in
- don't describe any moment of awareness or any point where it all comes together in the setup; that's the thing under test
- the multiple drafts verdict can't just be that there's no fact of the matter and moving on — the view should still say something definite about what happened, just not a definite when
- ideally the case survives being probed twice, with the two probes disagreeing, in a way the Cartesian view has to call one of them wrong and the multiple drafts view doesn't

Then, for the case you build: say what the Cartesian theater is forced to say, what multiple drafts says, and which single feature of the case is doing all the work. Run the control for me — if I hold the whole processing story fixed and only move the probe earlier or later, what changes and what stays fixed on each view? And then the hostile part: how would a Cartesian theater person redescribe my case so the disagreement evaporates, and why is that redescription ad hoc or not?

Context, since it fixes what counts as cheating: I'm trying to argue that the two views don't disagree about the data at all, only about whether an extra fact is needed on top of it. So a case that stipulates the verdicts instead of generating them from the views is useless to me — it has to be a case where you can watch the disagreement come out of the commitments. It's for a seminar paper, doesn't need to be publishable, but it does need to actually work.
