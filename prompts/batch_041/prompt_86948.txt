I have a set of verdicts about killing and letting die, and I want to know what principle, or small set of principles, they amount to. Switching off a ventilator at the request of a competent patient who cannot breathe without it: permissible, and I would not call it killing. Giving that same patient a lethal injection at their equally competent and equally settled request: impermissible, and plainly killing. Declining to put such a patient on a ventilator in the first place, knowing they will die without it: permissible. Switching off an automatic defibrillator so that it will not fire when the patient arrests, at the patient's request: impermissible, and I do call it killing, even though the machine stands to the heart much as the ventilator stands to the lungs. Refusing a stranger's request for my kidney: permissible, and not killing. Watching a child drown in a shallow bathtub while I read a book: nearly as bad as holding the child under. Throwing a life ring to a drowning stranger and then drawing it back when I recognise him: killing. Standing on the shore and never throwing it at all: letting die, though it may be just as bad. Disconnecting a feeding tube at the patient's request, knowing they will die of dehydration: permissible. Never having connected it: permissible, and I feel no pull whatever to call it killing.

Is there an account of the killing/letting-die line, and of the permissibility verdicts, that gets all of this, or nearly all? State each clause of it precisely and name the case that forces the clause; say what it predicts for cases I have not listed, such as a nurse who must keep squeezing a hand bellows to keep an anaesthetised patient breathing and stops, and a case where the agent who now switches the ventilator off is the one who first switched it on against the patient's wishes; and tell me honestly which of my verdicts it cannot save. If the only account that saves every verdict needs a clause that does no work anywhere else, say so, and say which single verdict you would give up in exchange for the simpler principle. You need not cite any literature; I want the principle itself.
