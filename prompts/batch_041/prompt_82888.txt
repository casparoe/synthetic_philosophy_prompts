I'm in the middle of a phil of language module and the conditionals week left me with something I cant shake. Standard line: the ones with a past-tense if-clause and a 'would' are counterfactuals, true or false depending on the nearest worlds where the if-part holds. Fine. But I keep running into that exact shape where no world-hopping seems to be going on, and I dont know if there's a name for what the past tense is doing instead. Case: I asked my mate to lend me fifty quid and he says 'if I had a spare fifty I'd give it you.' I heard a no. He did have a spare fifty, and he didn't lend it. On the textbook story I cant see how his sentence comes out true -- if he does have a spare fifty, the nearest world where he has one is just this one, and he handed over nothing -- and yet what the sentence actually did was tell me he was skint, which was false. Is there an established name for that extra bit, the falseness of the if-part that the 'would' seems to carry? And is it the same thing as the past tense in 'if I were you I'd take the job' or 'would you mind if I sat here', where it looks more like politeness or distance than unreality? If there's no name for it, coin one, but either way I want the distinctions: is the if-part presupposed false, implied false, or is that just what this past tense is for, and where do those three cases come apart?
