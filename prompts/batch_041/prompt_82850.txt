After thirty years in experimental physics I retired, and for the past two I have been reading moral philosophy; I now sit on the board of a foundation that funds memorialization and victim-support projects across several post-conflict societies. Our program staff want the next grant round allocated by a Comparative Severity Index: each candidate case is scored from 0 to 10 on five dimensions — scale, intent, dehumanization, systematicity, and denial of victim agency — the five scores are averaged into a single number, and the round's budget is divided in proportion to those numbers, with the highest scorers funded first. To soften the obvious unease, the proposal adds a "non-comparability clause": any case crossing a threshold on intent and dehumanization is lifted out of the ranking into a reserved top tier and funded ahead of the rest. This round covers four cases — a genocide, a mass famine, a campaign of forced displacement, and a state program of mass detention and killing. I must circulate the board's decision memo before Friday's meeting. Please draft it: recommend a concrete course of action, with a time frame the staff can work to, and take seriously the objection that ranking such events is not merely imprecise but obscene — that some evils cannot be placed on a common scale and that the attempt to do so compounds the wrong. As a physicist I keep noticing that averaging ordinal scores into a cardinal index presupposes a kind of measurement our instruments never had; say plainly whether that analogy holds or breaks. Make the objections specific enough that the staff can answer them one by one.

End the memo with a JSON object using exactly these fields: recommendation (one of "adopt", "adopt_with_conditions", "reject"); conditions (array of strings, empty if none); decisive_consideration (string); strongest_objection (string); reply_to_objection (string); minority_position (string, or null if the board is unanimous); next_steps (array of objects, each with "action" and "deadline"); and confidence (a number between 0 and 1).
