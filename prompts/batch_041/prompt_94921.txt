My friend and I argued for like two hours last night about whether species are natural kinds and neither of us budged an inch. What bugs me is that we agreed on all the biology going in: no essences, gene flow and selection hold some populations together and not others, the boundaries go fuzzy in the bacterial cases and in ring species. He still says species are obviously real kinds. His line is that a kind is just any grouping you can run a reliable induction on — if "this drug works on this group" keeps panning out, the group is a kind, end of story — and my line is that by that standard "the stuff on my desk right now" is a kind too. I have a hunch (just a hunch) that we aren't actually disagreeing about the biology, or even about which groups exist, but about something like: does calling something a kind require the grouping to be independent of us, or only stable enough to bet on? So diagnose it for me. Is this a real philosophical disagreement, a verbal one about the word "kind", or two different questions sharing one name — the metaphysician's "is there a mind-independent grouping here" versus the working biologist's "does this classification earn its keep"? And be honest about whether anything could actually move it: a distinction, a case, or admitting the question is malformed. Write it in American English, my friend keeps correcting my spelling and its getting old.
