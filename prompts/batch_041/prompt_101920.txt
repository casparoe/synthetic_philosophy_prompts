Ok so this is a weird question from someone who's never taken a philosophy class in his life. I got made a team lead at work about four months ago, so now I'm the one doing performance reviews and breaking up fights between people on my team, and it has turned out to be way harder than I expected. Here's the thing that keeps happening and I can't find a word for it.

Take one specific fact about a situation — the person did it on purpose, or what they did caused someone pain, or they told the truth even when it cost them. My gut says these things are obviously good or obviously bad. On purpose = worse. Caused pain = bad. Told the truth = good. Fine, easy. But then I keep hitting cases where the exact same fact is doing the opposite work, and not because something else is outweighing it. One person on my team comes to me and admits a mistake a coworker made, and I think, that took guts. Another person comes to me and admits a mistake a coworker made, and the very same truth-telling is what makes me trust them less, because of what it says about them that they couldn't keep their mouth shut. A guy on my team is intense and blows up when he thinks somebody's being picked on, and the intensity is exactly why I'm glad he's there; the same intensity in a different week is why I'm writing him up. When my mom had her hip replaced the pain was the whole point of it, the thing that was good about the situation. When my kid got hit in the face at school the pain was the entire reason it was bad.

What's bugging me is that this isn't "context matters" in the boring sense. I get that you have to weigh stuff against other stuff. What I mean is the same single feature, all by itself, seems to flip from counting for to counting against depending on what else is going on, and I can't state any rule in advance for which way it'll point. I also notice it happening outside of moral stuff. "Lots of people believe it" is a reason to think something's true when those people are all in a position to know, and a reason to think it's false when they've all just been copying each other. Same feature, opposite direction.

So does this have a name? I feel like people have definately argued about this for centuries and I just don't know what to google. I don't want a reading list, I want to understand the shape of the thing. If there is a name, tell me what it actually claims and, more importantly, what it's not. Is it the same as saying morality is relative? Same as saying rules have exceptions? Same as saying some reasons are stronger than others? Because none of those feel like what I'm describing — I already knew all three of those and they didn't cover it. Is there a name for the opposite view too, the one that says if a feature counts for something in one case then it has to count for it in every case? And does what I'm describing apply to reasons in general, reasons to believe stuff as well as reasons to do stuff, or is it specifically a claim about ethics? If there's no established word for it, coin one, define it, and tell me about the cases on the edge where I wouldn't be able to say either way.
