Ok, longtime lurker on LessWrong/SSC/the EA forum, first time posting something like this, so sorry in advance if this is a known thing I just never learned the name of.

There's a shape of problem I keep running into in alignment discussions and I've never seen it named. Best way I can put it: a thing everyone agrees is one big hard problem gets split into two halves, each half is obviously easy *given* the other half, and the hard part just... moves back and forth between them depending on who's arguing. Nobody ever has to defend the claim that the hard part lives in their half, because they can always say "well, that bit's trivial once you've solved the other one."

Concretely, the version I see most: someone says the alignment problem is really about figuring out what we want, since human values are fragile and complicated and we don't know them. Somebody else says no, specifying is the easy part, you can spec it as "do what we'd endorse on reflection", one sentence, done — the hard part is checking whether a given output actually satisfies that. Then a third person says checking is easy; if you already knew what you wanted you could just look at the thing and say yes or no. And now we're back at the start, same people making the same moves in the opposite order. Same pattern with corrigibility (the machine has to already be the sort of thing that defers to corrections, or the correction mechanism doesn't bind) and with interpretability (to trust the probe you need ground truth about the internals, which is what the probe was for).

What bugs me is that this isn't just "hard problems are hard", and its not ordinary circular reasoning either, because every individual step anyone takes is actually valid. It's that there's no load-bearing piece you can point to. With a normal hard problem you can at least say "if I had X, the rest follows"; here X is cheap given the other X, so the crux keeps trading places and the difficulty never adds up from the parts. I dont think it's just the problem of the criterion / diallelus, because there the circularity is the thing under dispute rather than a side effect of how people carve up a research program.

So: does this have a name, or is it something people keep independently reinventing? And more than the name I want the carving — what exactly the phenomenon is, how it differs from plain circularity, from wicked problems, and from just "the problem is conjunctive", where its edges are (is there a version of it that isn't about research programs at all?), and what it would even look like to escape it.

Can you do it in three parts: "Nearest neighbours" (~250 words) — the closest established ideas, and exactly where each stops fitting; "The thing itself" (~400 words) — your definition, with a boundary case or two, at least one from outside AI; "Name" (~100 words) — the term if there is one, otherwise a coinage plus why it earns its keep.
