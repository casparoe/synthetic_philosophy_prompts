I am an undergraduate currently taking a module on formal epistemology, and one of our assignments is to write a sustained piece of satire on a topic from the syllabus. I should like you to draft it for me, working to the following brief.

Write a mock customer-support FAQ page for a company that markets a personal belief-revision assistant — a device or app that promises to update its owner's credences automatically whenever the owner acquires evidence that is not entirely certain. The product's whole selling point is that it handles uncertain evidence 'properly', on the model of Jeffrey conditionalization. The humour must come entirely from the epistemology being correct, and the FAQ should, without ever winking at the reader, quietly take positions on the genuine difficulties. In particular it should address, as blandly and cheerfully as a real support page would: first, what exactly the device does when the observation fed to it is merely probable rather than certain, and how that differs from simply waiting until the owner is certain and then updating on the evidence in the ordinary way; second, whether the order in which several uncertain observations are entered can alter the resulting credences, and what the company's official position is if it can; third, the assumption that the owner's conditional probabilities of other propositions, given the evidence, are held fixed throughout the update, and what happens to customers who violate it; and fourth, at least one dissatisfied customer whose complaint is a genuine, well-aimed objection to the whole procedure, answered by a support agent who does not quite concede the point. Give the company a plausible name, and let the piece be legible to someone who has never studied the topic while still being fair to someone who has. About 650 words, formatted as numbered questions and answers, in British English.
