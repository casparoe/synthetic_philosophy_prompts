I am currently taking a graduate seminar in bargaining theory, and I would like to work out my instructor's implicit commitments before our next class. All term she has been distributing two-person bargaining problems and, for each one, announcing the single payoff pair she regards as the correct outcome. Every problem has had the same shape: a convex set of feasible utility pairs that is closed under downward moves in both coordinates, together with a disagreement point giving each player's utility in the event of no agreement. Utility is measured in arbitrary units, and she has repeatedly insisted that her answer must not depend on the units chosen.

Her record so far:

1. Disagreement point (0,0); feasible set the triangle with vertices (0,0), (2,0), (0,2). Her verdict: (1,1).
2. Disagreement point (0,0); feasible set the triangle with vertices (0,0), (2,0), (0,1). Her verdict: (1, 1/2).
3. Disagreement point (0,0); feasible set the triangle with vertices (0,0), (3,0), (0,1). Her verdict: (3/2, 1/2).
4. Disagreement point (0,0); feasible set {(u1, u2) : u1 ≥ 0, u2 ≥ 0, u1²/4 + u2² ≤ 1}. Her verdict: (√2, √2/2), which she wrote out as (1.414, 0.707).
5. Disagreement point (1,1); feasible set the triangle with vertices (1,1), (3,1), (1,2). Her verdict: (2, 3/2).

Next week's problem, which she has already circulated: disagreement point (0,0); feasible set {(u1, u2) : u1 ≥ 0, u2 ≥ 0, u1 ≤ 1, 2u1 + u2 ≤ 3}.

The four solution concepts we have formally covered in the course are the Nash solution, the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution, the egalitarian solution, and the utilitarian solution.

What I would like from you is:

(a) a prediction of the verdict she will announce next week, with the calculation behind it;

(b) the most economical description of the rule her record actually displays, stated precisely enough that I can check it against each entry;

(c) an audit of the four concepts against the record: which of them fit exactly, which are excluded and by which entries, and whether any rule that is not on the course list fits her record just as well;

(d) for every rule that survives (c), what that rule predicts for next week's problem, and which prediction you would bet on, with your reasons; and

(e) one further bargaining problem, ideally of a different shape from next week's, that I could put to her in order to separate the surviving rules, together with what each of them would answer on it.
