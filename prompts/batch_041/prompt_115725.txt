I have no formal training in logic or philosophy; I am a software engineer who has been reading about the foundations of AI safety in my spare time. I have been trying to understand a problem that a friend in a reading group described to me, and I would like to know what you think Gödel would have made of it. The problem is this: suppose someone builds an artificial agent that is allowed to rewrite its own decision procedure, but only if it can first prove that the rewritten version will never choose an action that the current version regards as catastrophic. The agent's proof system is powerful enough to reason about its own proofs, and it is stipulated to trust that system. The friend says the agent is stuck for a reason that has nothing to do with computational limits. Because of the theorem named after Löb, the agent cannot prove the sentence that would license its self-trust—roughly, if I prove that my successor will be safe, then my successor will be safe—without already proving that the successor will be safe. So the very capacity for self-proof that was supposed to justify self-modification seems to block it. She calls this the Löbian obstacle.

What I want to ask is not whether the technical result is correct, but what Gödel would have thought about it. He spent his life on the limits of formal systems, and he also wrote about the possibility of mechanism and the mind. If he were shown this problem, would he treat it as a routine corollary of his own work, or would he see it as a distinct and perhaps more troubling phenomenon? Would he think a self-modifying agent could escape by using a weaker proof system, by accepting some unprovable trust principle on faith, or by abandoning the demand for a proof altogether? And would he have regarded the obstacle as an argument against the project of building such an agent, or merely as a design constraint that tells us what kind of agent not to build?

I am not looking for a bibliography or citations; I would rather have your best reconstruction of his likely view, with the reasoning that supports it. Please be concrete about which of his ideas you are extrapolating from, and where the extrapolation becomes speculative. If you think the question is ill-posed because Gödel would have rejected the whole framing, say so and explain why.
