You are a philosopher of mind who also works on reinforcement learning, and you have thought carefully about whether artificial systems can undergo valenced experience. I am preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on the moral status of artificial agents, and I need a taxonomy of the positions on whether a reinforcement learning agent can suffer, presented as a decision tree rather than a list of labels.

Before drawing anything, brainstorm the underlying questions that actually separate the positions, then build the tree from the logical space those questions generate and locate the positions inside it. Every branch node should be phrased as a question a reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and every leaf should carry the position's name, or be marked 'unnamed' if nobody defends it, along with one sentence on that position's most awkward commitment.

The tree should turn at least on these distinctions: whether valence requires phenomenal consciousness or can be identified with a functional or behavioral role; whether the reward signal itself is the right candidate for the agent's valence, or whether the better candidate is the learned value estimate, the reward-prediction error, or some state the agent represents as its own; whether the reward number figures anywhere in the agent's computations at all, given that in many agents it enters only the weight-update rule, so ask whether a quantity never represented can count as a state of the system; and whether suffering requires a unified subject that owns the state, or can be distributed across units with no owner.

Note any combinations of answers that your tree leaves unoccupied, and say briefly whether each is unoccupied because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate rather than papering over the gap. Aim for six to nine leaves, characterize the positions without attributing them to particular authors, keep the questions concrete enough for a reader who has never seen this debate, and write in American English.
