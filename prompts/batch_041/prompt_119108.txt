Before we assess any particular answer to the boundary problem, I want the specification on the table. Set out the desiderata that any adequate theory of who belongs to the demos must meet, with the case or consideration behind each. Cover at least the following. The cases it must get right: the resident non-citizen who is governed by nearly every law but may not vote; the expatriate who may vote but is not governed; the outsider gravely affected by a polity's decisions, such as those displaced by its dam or breathing its emissions; children and others excluded from the franchise on grounds the theory must justify rather than assume; future people; and the secession referendum, where the choice of electorate may determine the outcome. The distinctions it must not collapse: being subject to a decision versus being affected by it; membership versus the franchise; the demos as author of a decision versus the demos as its object. The constraints it must respect: that every actual boundary is drawn by an act that already presupposes a demos; that a demos persists through the complete replacement of its members; that demoi are nested and overlapping, so the theory cannot assume a single body; and that most existing states would fail several plausible criteria, so it must say what follows. Add the requirement that the theory be determinately applicable by a body that must actually decide and give reasons, rather than returning that the answer depends on the demos.

For each requirement, say why it is genuine rather than the prejudice of one camp. Then identify the pairs you judge cannot be jointly satisfied — I expect the all-affected and the all-subjected principles to founder here — and say which single requirement eliminates the most candidates. Close with a brief verdict on how territorial, all-affected, all-subjected and stakes-based accounts fare against the list; I do not need the survey in detail.
