I have just come back from a long argument with a close friend about whether an armed intervention could ever be justified to stop a massacre, and he kept ending his turns with the one-liner that there is no such thing as a just war. I suspect we were not disagreeing about the same claim, and I want a map before I speak to him again. Lay out the distinct precise theses that line could be expressing, from the nearly trivial to the radical: readings about the concept of war, about the intentions war necessarily involves, about the moral equality of combatants, about whether just war theory constrains or licenses violence, about absolute versus contingent pacifism, and about whether the slogan is really a strategic claim about nonviolence rather than a moral one. For each, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false; what it commits you to; and what it would rule out. Then tell me which reading best fits someone using it to close down a conversation about intervention, and which reading I should have pressed him on.
