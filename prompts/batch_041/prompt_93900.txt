My friend Dana and I have this long-running thing where I feed her little scenarios and she tells me whether the person did the thing on purpose. The shape is always the same: someone does something for one reason, knows some other effect is coming along for the ride, and genuinely doesn't care about that other effect either way. She's never once waffled on me. The record so far:

- A CEO switches to a cheaper process to cut costs. He knows it'll dump sludge in the river. He could care less about the river. Dana says: he polluted the river on purpose.
- Same switch, except this time the new process will clean up the river as a byproduct. He doesn't care. Dana says: he did not clean up the river on purpose.
- A variant where the byproduct is neither good nor bad — the widgets come out a slightly different shade of grey. He foresees it, doesn't care. Dana says: not on purpose.

Now two new ones.

(a) A doctor treats an infection with the standard first-line drug. The protocol calls for exactly this drug, it's what any competent doctor would give, and nobody thinks she's done anything wrong. She knows the drug causes permanent hearing loss in a small fraction of patients. She doesn't care about the hearing, she just wants the infection gone. Did she cause the hearing loss on purpose?

(b) A factory owner buys a new machine purely to cut costs. As a side effect, the machine needs less tending, so the night shift ends up taking an extra twenty minutes of break each night. That's nice for them, but it's against the company's break-time rules. He doesn't care about the breaks one way or the other. Did he give the night shift the extra break on purpose?

I'm definately less interested in the yes/no than in the rule Dana is actually running. Lay out the candidate rules that fit everything she's said so far, tell me what each one predicts for (a) and (b), say which you'd bet on and why, and tell me which case I should put to her next to actually settle it — and what the result would show.
