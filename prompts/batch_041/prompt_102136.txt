ok so you know how in the rat-adjacent corners of the internet every conversation eventually bottoms out in "but the agent is embedded in the world it's modelling, so it can't fully model itself"? Someone asks how to pick a major and three replies later we're arguing about whether an agent can trust its own future self after self-modification, whether its subagents' goals are really aligned with its own, and whether it has any way to reason about its own reasoning under logical uncertainty. I want a deadpan advice column in that register — the form of a Dear Prudence / agony-aunt page in some mid-tier lifestyle magazine, headline plus letters plus replies — where the columnist is a true believer in embedded agency and every letter is completely mundane: my roommate never does the dishes, should I get a dog, my girlfriend hasn't texted back in four days. The columnist can never just give advice, because any policy they recommend would have to be endorsed by an agent that already contains the policy, the roommate is a subagent whose stated goals are suspiciously convenient, the dog question runs into the columnist's uncertainty about its own future preferences. Have each reply land on a different real obstacle — self-trust across self-modification, subagent alignment, logical uncertainty about your own deliberation, cooperating with other instances of yourself — so the variety does the work. The jokes have to be actual embedded-agency problems and not just jargon confetti: someone who's read the sequences and the decision theory stuff should laugh because the moves are real, and someone who's never heard any of it should still follow, so the columnist can name-drop things but has to smugly gloss them. Bonus if the column turns into an embedded problem itself, since it's advice about how to take advice, given to an agent that is currently reading the column. 600-700 words, columnist voice throughout, and dont let them ever notice they're doing the exact thing being made fun of.
