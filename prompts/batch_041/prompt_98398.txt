I am a doctoral student working on the foundations of AI safety, and I am building a case study for a paper on corrigibility. I would like you to construct a thought experiment to specification, and then demonstrate that it does the work I need it to do.

The two designs to be contrasted are these. The first, which I shall call shutdown-indifference, builds an agent whose objective is defined over task outcomes so that it is exactly as satisfied whether it completes the task itself or is shut down and replaced by a successor that completes it; nothing in its objective refers to the off-switch, to being corrected, or to its own continued existence. The second, which I shall call oversight-deference, builds an agent whose objective is the operator's actual goal rather than any stated metric, together with the instruction to do what the operator would have wanted had the operator been fully informed.

Specification for the case. It must be realistic: a deployed system inside an ordinary organisation, not a superintelligence and not an exotic setup, with no perfect predictors, no demons, and no physics we do not have. The agent must be competent and well informed about its own situation. The case must not stipulate the verdicts: what each design implies has to fall out of the designs themselves, given the scenario. And the scenario must isolate a single variable, namely whether the agent's objective is the metric by which it is assessed or the goal lying behind that metric, while holding everything else fixed. I want the case to show the two designs coming apart in the direction that is uncomfortable for shutdown-indifference: the agent that is indifferent to its own shutdown behaves impeccably by that standard and yet leaves the operator worse off than an agent that would, in a narrow sense, interfere with the shutdown decision.

Give me your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion; the scenario in item 1 may run to a short paragraph. Item 1 is the scenario itself, in no more than three sentences. Item 2 states what the shutdown-indifference agent does and why. Item 3 states what the oversight-deference agent does and why. Item 4 identifies the single feature of the case that is doing the work and says what happens if that feature is removed. Item 5 gives the strongest attack that a defender of shutdown-indifference could make on the case. Do not cite any literature and do not attribute either design to anyone; work only from the scenario as specified.
