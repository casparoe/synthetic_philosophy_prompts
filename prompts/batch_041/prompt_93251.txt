I sit on our county Board of Health as its public member — the one seat that isn't a physician or a sanitarian — and next week I have to vote on a licensing application from a body-art studio that wants to offer tongue splitting, eyeball tattooing, and scarification to adults who sign a detailed consent form and wait a week. The studio is clean, the operator is certified, and nobody proposes doing any of this to anyone who hasn't asked for it. Three of my colleagues want to deny the license. Two say the practices are grotesque and degrading to the human body, that the county shouldn't put its stamp on mutilation, and that a storefront like this in a strip mall would coarsen the neighborhood and draw in teenagers. A third adds that a community is held together by a shared sense of what is decent, and that once we stop enforcing that sense we lose something we can't get back. Our own rules let the panel deny a license for practices contrary to public morals, which is why this landed on my desk rather than the inspector's.

Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote: a clear recommendation (grant, deny, or grant with conditions) with reasons, and an honest account of the strongest case against my recommendation. I especially want you to keep separate three things my colleagues are running together — that these practices are disgusting or degrading, that they harm the people who request them, and that they harm bystanders or the community at large — and to say what weight, if any, disgust at a practice should carry when a public body decides whether to permit it, given that we're withholding permission in advance rather than punishing anyone after the fact.
