In a launch-readiness meeting, a colleague ended a discussion about whether to delay a training run for an additional safety evaluation with one sentence: if we don't build it, someone else will. I have since heard the same sentence used to justify shipping, to defeat a proposed internal safety standard, and, in a different register, to argue that no engineer at the lab bears any responsibility for what the system eventually becomes. I suspect these uses trade on quite different claims, some nearly vacuous and some substantive empirical or game-theoretic theses. I would like them separated.

Please lay out, as a menu rather than a recommendation, the distinct precise claims the sentence could be making, ordered from the trivial to the radical. For each reading, state what would have to be true of the world for it to hold, classify it as a tautology, a plausible empirical prediction, a contestable theoretical claim, or simply false, and say what it does and does not imply about what this lab should do. I am especially interested in the readings that concern the structure of competition rather than the inevitability of the technology: the claim that the frontier arrives at roughly the same time either way; the claim that it arrives earlier and less safely if the cautious actor steps back; the claim that who builds it matters more than when; and the claim that the sentence is not a prediction at all but a self-fulfilling expectation or a bargaining threat.

To make this concrete, assume three possible shapes for the alignment tax in my setting: a safety procedure costing a fixed amount of compute per run, independent of model scale; a procedure whose cost grows with scale, so that the tax is proportional to capability rather than a fixed levy; and a procedure that costs little at the current frontier but removes a large risk at the next one. Say how the force of the sentence changes across these three cases, and whether any reading of it is strong enough to carry the weight my colleague placed on it.

Finally, tell me which reading people almost always have in mind when they use the sentence to close a discussion like mine, and whether that reading is among the ones that survive scrutiny. If the sentence is being asked to do work that none of its readings can support, I would like to know precisely where the gap lies.
