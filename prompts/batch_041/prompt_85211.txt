Longtime reader of the rationalist blogs — I'm in a Discord of about forty people who mostly wandered in through the Sequences, and we have some version of the same argument every few months in different clothes. Last night's was whether a mind could be non-computational. Someone brought up Penrose, someone else said "fine but what if physics lets you do hypercomputation," and it got shut down in one line: "computable just means computable by a Turing machine. That's the definition. There's nothing to argue about." Same move shows up whenever anyone worries that the brain might do something our laptops can't, and I've also seen the related line that the Church-Turing thesis is really a definition dressed up as a thesis.

I'm pretty sure that's not the end of it, but I can't say exactly where the trick is, and I'd rather not be the guy who objects without being able to state the objection. So lay it out for me as a menu. What are the distinct precise things that one-liner could be claiming? I want the whole range, from the trivially-true reading where it's just a stipulation about how we'll use a word, through the reading where it's a substantive claim about what a human being can do by rote with paper and pencil, through the reading where it's a claim about what any physical system in this universe can do, through whatever it is that's being said when the fact that several totally independent formalisms — Turing machines, lambda calculus, recursive functions — all turned out to pick out the same class of functions gets treated as evidence. For each reading: is it analytic, empirical, a theorem, or a category error? Is it plausible? What would it even take to falsify it, and if the answer is "nothing," is that a strength or a bug?

Then tell me which of those readings actually licenses the shutdown move he made — which one makes "could a mind be non-computational?" a non-question — and which ones leave it as a live question that he's just refusing to engage with. And say which reading I should probably just concede, because I suspect at least one of them is definately right and I don't want to be defending something indefensible in front of people who'll notice.
