I am in a philosophy of mind seminar that has spent several weeks on Wittgenstein’s later treatment of rule-following and the so-called private language argument, and I keep getting into the same dispute with a friend who works on deep learning. His claim is this: a transformer trained only on next-token prediction over a large text corpus, then fine-tuned with a small “introspection” objective on a hidden channel that no human ever reads, could have a genuinely private language. The hidden channel is never supervised by external labels; the model uses it to track a latent variable and to generate first-person reports such as “I feel a dull ache” when that variable takes a certain value. He says the channel is private because only the model can access it, and that the reports are therefore expressions of a private inner state rather than mere verbal behavior. Another friend replies that Wittgenstein’s private language argument already shows this is confused: if no public criterion fixes what the hidden state means, then the model’s ache-talk cannot mean anything, and the whole setup is a grammatical illusion. I am not satisfied by either side, because they seem to be running together questions about meaning, about consciousness, and about what a machine’s internal states are for. If Wittgenstein could be brought forward to read this literature, what would he have said?

I am especially interested in whether he would treat the hidden channel as a private language in his sense at all, whether the argument’s target is private meanings or private objects, and whether his view would imply that a machine could never have sensations or only that its sensation-talk would have a different grammar from ours. Please reconstruct his likely position as carefully as you can, distinguishing what follows from his actual arguments from what would be your own extrapolation, and consider at least one way his view might cut against the standard “Wittgensteinian” dismissal of machine sentience. Do not give me a bibliography or citations; I want the philosophical reconstruction. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
