Im a software engineer — no philosophy degree, so be gentle — trying to put together a short research proposal (like two pages) for a part-time fellowship. The fellowship is for people coming from industry who want to do a bit of philosophical work on their own field, and my field is ML infrastructure. I've got a half-baked idea and I want you to turn it into an actual proposal.

Here's the idea. Where I work we ship models that output a number between 0 and 1 and everybody calls it "the probability." But that number is doing at least three different jobs at three different points. During training it's meant to be a frequency-ish thing you can calibrate against held-out data. When it feeds a decision it gets treated like a subjective degree of belief that you're supposed to update as evidence rolls in. And when someone audits the system later — or asks "was the model right to be 0.8 confident that one time?" — it gets read as a propensity of the situation to produce the outcome. Same number, three readings, and I've watched our engineers quietly slide between them without ever noticing they've switched. My hunch is that this is the exact thing the old frequentism vs subjectivism vs propensity fight is about, just never labeled as such in our world.

And my second hunch, which is the part I actually want to test: the way out isn't to pick the one true interpretation, it's to make the interpretation relative to the role the number plays — so "what does this probability mean" has no answer until you say which stage of the pipeline you're in and what the number is going to be used for downstream. I want to find out whether that survives contact with the nasty cases (the reference class problem, one-off events, the times when a well-calibrated number and a genuine credence come apart) or whether it just quietly collapses back into subjectivism with extra steps.

So write the proposal. I need:

(a) the question stated so it actually has a determinate answer, not just "interpretations are complicated";
(b) why the three usual positions don't already settle it — and I don't want a literature review with citations, just the arguments and where they leave the gap open, I'll add the refs myself later;
(c) the method, concretely, month by month over the fellowship, and why this approach rather than the obvious one of just arguing for whichever interpretation I already like;
(d) what a real result would look like, and what would count as the project failing or dissolving into definitions;
(e) an honest scope check — is this a two-page-proposal-sized thing or am I kidding myself?

One constraint: please don't lean on specific published papers, named theorems or technical terms I probably haven't read, because I can't look them up while I'm writing this and I'd just be bluffing. Plain language, and if you need to invent a distinction to make the idea work, go ahead and invent it as long as you flag it as yours.
