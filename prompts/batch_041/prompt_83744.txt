Hey — so I just moved onto a team that works on AI systems checking each other's work: you get two models to argue about some question, and then a third model, a weaker one, or a person with not much time, has to decide who's right. I came into this from a totally different area so I'm missing alot of the vocabulary. Two weeks of reading transcripts and there's a pattern I keep seeing that I dont have a word for, and it's bugging me.

The model that's actually right has to explain itself, so every step it hands the judge is a step the judge can go and check — and some of those steps look shaky even when they're fine (small sample, old source, a step that only works in the simple case). So the judge pokes at one, gets confused, and now the true answer is in doubt. The model that's making things up gets to *choose* what it says. So it says things that are technically specific but practically impossible to check in the time available — 'sure, but that effect disappears once you control for X' — and the judge can't knock it down, so it just stands. The honest side pays a tax on every sentence, because its sentences can be checked. The lying side pays nothing, because it only ever says the uncheckable. Give the judge more budget and the liar just buries the claim one level deeper. And the honest side can't make itself cheaper to check, because it doesn't get to pick which facts are true.

Is there a name for this? I know the Gish gallop thing, where you fire off a hundred weak objections and the other side can't keep up, but that seems to be about volume, and I think what I'm seeing happens with a single claim. And I know the saying about how it takes ten times the effort to refute nonsense as to produce it, but that's about effort — this feels like it's about who gets to pick. Does the picking version have a name, or does it need one? If it needs one I'd want the edges, not just a label: is it still the same thing when the honest side gets punished for being specific rather than for being wrong? What's the closest thing that already has a name, and what exactly is different about this?
