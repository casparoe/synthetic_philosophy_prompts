So my flatmate and I have a long-running bit where we argue about Daniel Dennett's "counterfeit people" essay — the one where he calls AI-generated fake people, quoting him, "the most dangerous artifacts in human history," and proposes to outlaw both making them and "passing along" ones somebody else made, with extremely severe penalties, a mandatory watermark in the spirit of the EURion constellation on banknotes, and the big AI companies held liable for whatever their products (or their products' products) get up to. I keep saying you can't legislate your way out of the intentional stance and he keeps saying civilization is at stake, and honestly I'd like to see what a world where he actually won looks like, on paper, before I concede anything.

So: write me a deadpan FAQ — the flat, cheerfully unbothered register of a real government agency's help page, numbered Q&A, about 800 words — for the office that has to run the thing after the law passes. The target is the proposal itself (the marking regime, the criminalization, the liability chain), not the man, so no snide remarks about the philosopher — if someone who fully agrees with him reads it they should think "fair, that's a real problem," not "that's a strawman." And it has to land for someone who's never heard of the essay, so the stakes should be legible from the page alone.

Invent the institutional furniture and stay consistent with it: call the statute the Authentic Interlocutor Act, put it under a fictional Office of Interlocutor Integrity, and have them issue a P-1 Personhood Provenance Stamp, verified by an Interlocutor Verification Layer that device makers are required to bake into phones and laptops. Give it a Counterfeit Person Appeals Tribunal, and keep the "passing along" offence as scary as he wants it. The comedy should come out of the actual joints of the proposal rather than silly invented flaws — what counts as a counterfeit person in the first place; who certifies the certifier, and who watermarks the watermark; whether forwarding your mum's text message is now a felony; what happens to a real human who fails the check (provisional-human status, I'd guess); whether the intentional stance means we're sitting ducks no matter what; and the banknote analogy being quietly load-bearing the whole way down, because there's alot riding on that watermark. Deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader, and a closing section on what to do when your own verification fails. Also my flatmate says hi.
