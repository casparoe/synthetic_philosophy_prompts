im helping run a small poetry night for our phil of language reading group and i want a piece that does Twin Earth in verse. Here's the constraint i actually care about: the poem should be in two voices, Oscar and his twin on Twin Earth, and there should be one refrain that shows up word-for-word in both halves -- no word of it changes -- but by the end the reader should be able to see that the two speakers dont mean the same thing by it, because the stuff in their lakes isnt the same stuff. So the form does the externalist work: same sound, same spelling, different meaning, and the meaning isnt sitting anywhere inside the speaker.

Then i want a third section that pushes past the easy version of the view, the one where meaning is just whatever happens to be in your environment, since that seems too simple to me. Take one of them, Twin-Oscar say, and have him somehow end up on the other planet, still talking, still using the same word. Does the word's meaning travel with his body, or does it stay behind with the planet he grew up on? I think the honest answer is that it stays with the history -- the word means what it meant in the community where he learned it -- but i want the poem to feel the pull of the other answer too, and i dont want it to cheat by quietly swapping the word. Same refrain the whole way through, recuring four or five times, and the last one should land differently than the first.

Keep it around 25-35 lines, free verse or rhymed, whatever serves it. Accurate enough that someone who teaches this stuff wouldnt wince, and good enough to read out loud to people who mostly came for the wine. No notes or citations needed, just the poem and maybe a sentence or two after it about which way you came down.
