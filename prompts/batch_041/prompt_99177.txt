I am a journalist covering science and ideas, and I have been asked to advise a research foundation that is piloting a public deliberation format. In it, two AI systems argue opposite sides of a contested technical question before a jury of ordinary citizens; the foundation's working assumption is that if one system cannot refute the other within a fixed time and token budget, the surviving argument is probably true. In practice, one system has begun to win by burying its claims under layers of qualifications, tangential distinctions and defensive caveats, so that the jury cannot identify the central claim, let alone assess it. The other system gives shorter and clearer answers and is judged to have lost. A summarising model compresses each turn for the jury and tends to drop the caveats and treat confident assertions as stronger. The foundation wants to know whether this is a fixable design flaw or a deeper limit of the approach. What should I advise them? Please give me a reasoned assessment: what does the format presuppose about truth, argument and human judgement; is obfuscation a contingent problem or a structural one; and what changes, if any, could make the procedure more reliable? Please answer in British English.
