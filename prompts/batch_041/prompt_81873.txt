I'm a retired physicist — thirty-five years of statistical mechanics and soft condensed matter, mostly fluctuation-dissipation relations, coarse-graining, a bit of non-equilibrium stuff, that kind of thing. Since I retired I've been reading economics and philsophy of economics for fun, and I keep getting the feeling that I half-understand the market-efficiency debates because they sound like thermodynamics. But I don't want to fool myself, so please do a careful translation job rather than just agreeing with me.

Here's the position I want restated. Take the "no-trade theorem" in finance — the one that says that if a bunch of traders start from an allocation that's already efficient, share the same priors, and any new information that shows up is public, then nothing gets traded; mere differences of information don't generate trade, and the only things that do are genuine differences in priors (people disagreeing about the world, not just knowing different things about it) or people wanting to shed risk. Then take the epistemic claims that go with it: that prices aggregate dispersed information nobody individually possesses (the Hayekian story), and the paradox that if prices really did reflect all the information, nobody would have any incentive to go collect the information in the first place. Throw in the reflexivity point too — that in markets the beliefs being aggregated partly help determine the states of the world being bet on.

I want all of that restated in my vocabulary: ensembles and macrostates, equilibrium conditions, detailed balance, conserved currents, entropy and information, fluctuations, coarse-graining and relevant variables, Maxwell's demon, ergodicity and the thermodynamic limit, maybe the fluctuation-dissipation relation. Be as faithful as you can, and don't just translate the bits that happen to fit — if a market claim only maps onto stat mech after you've quietly assumed something economists wouldn't accept, say so.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. What translated cleanly, what you had to distort to make it fit, and what has no expression at all in my vocabulary. I'm especially interested in (a) whether "no profitable trade" really is the same kind of thing as detailed balance or no net current, or whether I'm confusing a fixed-point condition with a physical one; (b) whether "the price contains the information" is more than the ordinary fact that a macro observable is a sufficient statistic for the microstate, or less; and (c) what my vocabulary does with agents who have incentives, since stat mech has no such thing and I suspect that's where the whole debate actually lives. And then the verdict I'm after: when a claim has no counterpart in my framework, is that because my framework is missing something real, or because the original claim was vaguer than it sounded? I'd rather you tell me my analogy is thinner than it looks than be encouraging about it.
