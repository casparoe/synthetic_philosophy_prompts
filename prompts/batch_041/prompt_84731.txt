A friend of mine is doing a PhD on Fodor and keeps making a move I cant tell whether its fair or not. Fodors whole thing is that thought runs on a language of its own, Mentalese, and my friend says: fine, but look at what the analogy to an actual language is doing there. Its not decoration, hes says its the argument. Once you lay the two side by side, he claims, the correspondence is basically: a compositional syntax, a stock of primitive symbols, a semantics thats compositional and truth-apt — and everything else drops out. No speakers, no conventions, no phonology, no immersion-learning, nothing anyone could ever quote or mispronounce. So on his view "language" ends up meaning nothing more than "structured," the whole label is a metaphor doing no work, and the real load is carried by the systematicity argument, which stands or falls on its own whether or not you call the symbols linguistic. And he thinks theres a better analogy sitting right there: the genetic code. DNA is a code nobody speaks — no community, no conventions, no speakers — but its combinatorial, it maps systematically onto products, and calling it a code isnt a scandal. So if the genetic code gets to be a code without a community, why cant Mentalese get to be a language without one?

What I want from you: lay the correspondence out properly, what in a natural language maps onto what in Mentalese and what has no counterpart at all (a table is fine if thats easier). Then where the mapping actually breaks, and which of the breaks matter for the specific job Fodor is using the analogy to do versus which are just noise. Then adjudicate the genetic-code swap — is it better, worse, or does it just relocate the problem. And finally say whether the thing the language analogy is being used to buy (thought is computation over structured symbols, and thats the only serious story about productivity and systematicity) survives the audit, or whether my friend is right that the analogy was carrying the conclusion the whole time.

One thing though: keep the settled and the contested apart for me. That systematicity and productivity are real features of thought isnt really in dispute; whether Mentalese is the right or the only explanation of them, whether "language" in the LOT is literal or a façon de parler, and how much of the argumentative weight the analogy is actually bearing — thats all live and people who know the area disagree. Dont hand me the textbook version dressed up as the consensus.
