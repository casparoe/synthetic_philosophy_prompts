I chair the content-ethics panel at a mid-size game studio, and we've got a fight on our hands over one scene in the game we're about to ship. In it the player has to put down their companion — a dog-ish NPC who's been with them for fifteen hours or so — to open the final area. There's no alternative route. Our playtest lead ran about sixty sessions, and alot of testers cried, and a handful said afterwards that they felt like they'd actually done something wrong, that they'd killed her. One tester with a history of losing a pet asked us to cut it.

Our creative director says it's fiction, nobody's hurt, and the whole point is to make the loss land — a story where nothing costs you anything is a worse story, and if we sand off every sharp edge we're just making a screensaver. Our player-research lead wants to turn this into a rule going forward: if a tester says they themselves did something wrong, that counts as a harm we have to mitigate; if they only say they're sad, it doesn't. I don't think that line holds up, but I can't yet say why.

I need to get a decision memo to the panel before Monday. Recommend one concrete call — ship as is, ship with an opt-out or an alternative path, or cut the scene — and give reasons we can actually argue about rather than just gesturing at "art" or "safety." Take the creative director's position seriously: if we're going to overrule him I want the strongest version of his case stated in the memo and an honest answer to it, not a straw man.
