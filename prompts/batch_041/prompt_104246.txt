Fourth-year PhD in cog sci, and I've got a problem that started out as straight procrastination and has turned into something I can't stop chewing on. Figured asking you was a better use of an afternoon than asking my roommates again.

I hadn't written a word of my dissertation in about six months. Two months ago I set up a deal with a friend: every Friday I owe him 1,000 words, and if they don't arrive, $50 goes to a political cause I find genuinely loathsome. Its working. I'm writing. But now I don't understand what was wrong with me before, and the two explanations I've been given don't fit together.

My roommate is in behavioral econ. His view is that nothing was wrong with me. I just want present things more than future things, and the size of that gap isn't constant -- it shrinks as the deadline gets closer, so all the deal does is move the payoffs around. He says a will that's weak isn't a thing that happens; it's a story we tell about an agent that isn't really there. At 9pm the scrolling was worth more to me than the dissertation, at 9am it wasn't, and neither of those guys is wrong about anything.

My advisor, who's a philosopher by training, says that's just redescribing the behavior and calling it an explanation. What's interesting, he says, is that I knew while I was doing it that I was doing the worse thing. Not that I wanted the scrolling more -- I didn't, I was miserable the whole time -- but that I did it anyway, knowing I'd rather not. And he thinks the contract is me refusing to look at that, like an alcoholic hiding the bottles.

They can't both be right, and from the inside my roommate's story is just what it feels like. Except that when I'm at my desk at 9pm with the browser open, it doesn't feel like I've been outbid. It feels like nobody's home.

Two things I can't get past. First: is this even a disagreement about me, or just two vocabularies for the same facts? What could I actually catch myself doing that would settle it one way or the other?

Second, the contract. I don't feel like someone writing a dissertation, I feel like someone being made to. If I finish this way, is the thing mine in the same way it would have been otherwise? My roommate says the pages are the pages. And I've basically put a gun to the head of a guy who won't exist until April -- he didn't agree to any of this, and he might want something completely different by then. So should I keep the contract, and does the answer depend on which of those two stories is true?
