So my buddy Marcus got done dirty at work and now he won't stop talking to me about it, ha. Short version: this guy Dave on his team took credit for basically the whole project, Marcus complained to their manager, and the manager kind of shrugged and said "that's just Dave." Marcus is stewing and keeps flip-flopping between three plans for what to do about it.

Plan one: at the next team meeting he stands up and calls Dave out in front of everyone and makes him admit it — he says he wants Dave to have to look everybody in the eye. Plan two: he says nothing, keeps his head down, does great work, and trusts that over a year or two people will figure out who actually does the work. Plan three: he writes a long post (not on the company slack, on linkedin or whatever) telling the story with names taken out — about being overlooked, doing the work, getting no credit — and sort of... living in that, I guess? Letting people see him as the one who got wronged. He's definately leaning toward this one lately and it's making me a little uneasy but I can't articulate why.

I've been thinking about why these three feel so different to me even though they're all just responses to being treated badly. I've started calling them the fist, the résumé, and the wound, cause that's kind of what each one is betting on — that people will fear you, or respect you, or feel for you. And I have this nagging feeling they're not just three tactics, they're three different answers to the question of what you're actually owed when someone does you wrong — like whether being wronged gives you a claim on other people's fear, or their respect, or their sympathy. Is one of these actually the right way to be, or morally better than the others? Or is "better" just whatever gets Marcus what he wants? And here's the thing that's really bugging me — I can imagine each one working, so does that mean the answer is just strategic, or is there something underneath that makes one of them the grown-up choice?
