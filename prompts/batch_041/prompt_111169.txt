I have been turning over the old raven paradox — the claim that all ravens are black is confirmed by a black raven, and, if you read the claim as equivalent to everything is either a non-raven or black, also by a white shoe — and my gut refuses to treat those two pieces of evidence alike. What I want is the principle, if there is one, behind the following verdicts of mine. I am giving them as they actually strike me, not as I think they ought to strike me.

1. I draw an object at random from the world and it turns out to be a black raven. This confirms all ravens are black strongly.
2. I draw an object at random from the world and it turns out to be a white shoe. This confirms it, but so faintly that I would call it nothing at all. So I already part ways with the view that every positive instance counts alike.
3. I walk to my shoe rack, pull out a shoe, and see that it is white. This confirms all ravens are black not at all; I was going to get a non-raven whatever I pulled.
4. I walk to a rookery, pull out a bird, and see that it is black and a raven. This confirms a lot. Cases 3 and 4 are both deliberate samplings, and yet one seems to confirm and the other does not.
5. There are ten objects in a sealed box and I know at least one of them is a raven. I pull one out and it is a white shoe. This confirms every raven in the box is black noticeably, because the box's contents are fixed and few, so ruling out an object as a possible counterexample is real progress.
6. Same as 5, except the box holds a trillion objects and I still know at least one is a raven. Pulling a white shoe confirms, but very little. In a box of a hundred trillion, essentially nothing.
7. Consider two hypotheses together: all ravens are black, and all ravens are black or white. A black raven confirms both, and I feel it confirms the first at least as much as the second. A white raven destroys the first and confirms the second, but confirms the second only weakly.
8. I sample at random from the non-black things and check each one for being a raven. I keep getting shoes. Compare this with case 2, where I sample at random from everything and happen to get shoes. The first procedure seems to me to confirm less, even though the objects I end up inspecting are the same sort of object.
9. A witness tells me she saw a raven and it was black. I take this as nearly as confirming as case 1. A witness tells me she saw a black thing and it was a raven. I take this as confirming considerably less.

I keep coming back to a rough principle of my own: evidence confirms a hypothesis to the degree that it raises the hypothesis's probability, that is, to the degree that the evidence is more probable if the hypothesis is true than if it is false. That seems to get cases 1 and 2, since a black raven is far more likely under all ravens are black than under its denial, while a white shoe is about equally likely either way. But it seems to get cases 3, 8, and 9 only if I smuggle in something about how the evidence was gathered, and it leaves cases 5 and 6 a mystery: in both I inspect one object and find it white and not a raven, so the evidence seems to be the same kind of event, and yet my verdicts differ sharply with the size of the box.
