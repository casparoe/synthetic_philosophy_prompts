I run the analytics shop for a mid-size hospital network, and our quality committee keeps having the same fight: someone shows the board that Hospital A has a lower mortality rate than Hospital B overall, someone else points out that inside every single severity band B is actually better than A, and then everyone shrugs and we publish whichever version the CEO likes. That's not a method. I need an actual procedure my analysts can run before we publish any two-group comparison like this — the questions to ask in order, what counts as an answer to each, when to report the pooled number, when to report the stratified breakdown, and when to say the comparison shouldn't be published at all. My analysts are solid on regression and p-values but basically innocent about causal inference, so it has to survive that. Run it on three cases: (1) the two hospitals above, where severity is plausibly the thing driving the reversal; (2) two surgical teams where the reversal only appears once you stratify by whether the patient was transferred in from another facility, which I have a bad feeling is not a real confounder; and (3) a comparison where the strata disagree but I think the pooled number is the honest one — say, where the thing we'd be splitting on is something the treatment itself causes. And tell me straight where the procedure can't save us and a human just has to make a judgment call, because I'd rather know that up front than have the flowchart pretend to settle it.
