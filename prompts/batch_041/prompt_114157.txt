I chair the ethics panel of a Mars settlement outfit -- we're the people the board asks before it signs anything, not the ones flying. The board wants to sign the founding charter next month and I keep getting stuck on what everyone here calls the nurture clause: every child born on the base owes the settlement twenty years of assigned work before they get a claim on their own quarters, their rations, and a vote in the assembley. The official reasoning is that the collective paid to gestate, feed and school them, there's no way back to Earth, and the base dies if a whole cohort turns 19 and decides it'd rather do something else. The wrinkle nobody planned for is that the clause is transferable -- you can buy out your own years by paying someone else to serve them, and two of the original crew have already done exactly that with hired substitutes. Now the first Mars-born cohort has turned 18, four of them have petitioned to have the clause struck as void, and one has stopped showing up to her shift, so the board wants the panel's written opinion this month.

Draft it: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and give me a concrete thing to do about the girl who's stopped working and about the petition. Give the strongest version of the case that the clause is legitimate before you knock it down, and don't rest anything on Earth law or on 'consent' by itself -- there's no court with jurisdiction and everyone here knows you can't consent to the terms of your own birth. No sources needed, I just need something the board can actually vote on.
