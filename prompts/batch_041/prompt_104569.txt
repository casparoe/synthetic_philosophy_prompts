Can you explain the philosophical point behind sphexishness? The story goes: a digger wasp drags a paralyzed cricket to her burrow, leaves it at the entrance, goes inside to inspect, and if you nudge the cricket a couple inches away while she's in there, she'll haul it back, drop it at the entrance, and inspect all over again — and supposedly you can keep her looping like that more or less forever. Hofstadter coined the term off the wasp, and Dennett leaned on it when talking about free will. But I don't want the insect trivia, I want the actual conceptual issue: what is supposed to separate this kind of rigid looping from genuine flexibility, and is it a difference in kind or just in complexity/degree? Does getting out of the loop require the system to be able to represent its own routine at some higher level and notice it's stuck? And here's a wrinkle I half-remember — I think later work showed the wasp does eventually adapt after enough repetitions, and that the repeated inspection might even be doing something adaptive. If that's right, does it defuse the worry, or just push it up a level, so the real question becomes whether we're sphexish too, just with a much longer and harder-to-notice loop?
