Since I retired from the lab I've been sitting in on a little philosophy reading group, which has been humbling in the way I probably needed, and last week we spent an hour and a half on a single letter. Two of the members, Nadia and Gerry, went at each other, and I've been chewing it over ever since, because I honestly couldn't tell who had the better of it. Thirty years in a wet lab spoils you — when two people disagree about what a word means you can usually settle it with an instrument. I'm definately no use here.

Background. My neighbor's daughter just finished art school and her final project was a typeface — a plain geometric sans, everything built out of circles and straight sticks. Unremarkable except for one thing. Her lowercase a is the ordinary one-storey shape, a bowl with a stem running down the right side, but flipped left to right: bowl on the right, stem on the left. Set a word in it and the a looks like it's been through a mirror. When she showed us, a couple of us read the word without noticing, a couple of us tripped on it, and Gerry said flatly that the letter wasn't an a at all, it was just a backwards a. Nadia said of course it's an a. Off they went.

Roughly how it went:

Nadia: an a is a bowl with a stem on it. Hers has both. You've flipped it, that's all. A shape doesnt stop being itself because you look at it from the other side.

Gerry: letters aren't shapes, they're signs, and signs don't survive being flipped. b and d are mirror images of each other and nobody thinks they're the same letter. Kids write letters backwards constantly when they're learning and we correct them — we don't say 'ah, that's a mirror b, still a b.' If a flipped a is an a, then a flipped b is a b, and then b and d are the same letter and reading falls apart.

Nadia: but b and d being two different letters is a fact about the alphabet, not about the shapes — the shapes are literally mirror images of each other, so if identity lived in the shape, they'd be one letter. That's my whole point: it's the system that does the work, not some essence hiding inside the outline. And look at the a's you actually read. The one in this sentence is a two-storey thing with a little roof over a bowl. The a on an old Bauhaus poster is a circle with a stick. Those two have almost nothing in common and nobody hesitates over either. So if there's a rule for what counts as an a, nobody can state it. It's a family, the family has fuzzy edges, and her mirrored a is inside the fuzzy edge, not outside it.

Gerry: fuzzy around what, though? Around how it looks to a reader, which isn't something we get to vote on. If the system were all that mattered, a font whose a was a triangle would be fine, and a font whose a was identical to its o would be fine, and it isn't. Reading is fast and automatic and it works on fonts nobody's ever seen before, which means our eyes are doing something, not just consulting a convention. And here's the thing — if she'd mirrored the whole alphabet, the whole page backwards, like the word on the front of an ambulance, I'd say fine, that's a mirror-image font and the a in it is an a. She mirrored one letter. That's not a style, that's a broken a.

Nadia: if mirroring the whole font still leaves you with an a, then a-ness depends on the company the letter keeps. You just argued my side for me.

At that point I mentioned something I half-remember reading about years ago — some project, Letter Spirit I think it was called — where the goal was to get a computer to design a whole alphabet in one consistent style, and the hard part apparently wasn't making any single letter look good, it was getting all twenty-six to look like they came from the same hand. That set them off again about whether a-ness lives in the letter or in the letter's relation to the rest of the font, and then we ran out of time and went home.

So: can you take it move by move and tell me who's right about which piece — not just who wins overall? And if you can, give me your verdict on the actual question: is her mirrored a an a, or is Gerry right that it's just a backwards a? And what are the two of them both missing?
