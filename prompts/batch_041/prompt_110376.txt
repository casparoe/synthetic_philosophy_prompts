ok so I'm a backend engineer, never taken a philosophy class in my life, but I read alot of this rationalist/EA stuff online and I've ended up with a pile of views I'm no longer sure are even compatible with each other. can you do a consistancy pass on them? I don't want a lecture on what I should believe, I want to know which of these are actually fighting each other and which just look like they are, ordered by how bad it is, plus the cheapest way out of each one and what that fix would cost me. and at the end, if I have to drop one, which one is pulling the least weight in the rest of the pile?

here's the pile:

1. I'm a total utilitarian about ethics, and more specifically I think the right action is the one with the highest expected total welfare. everyone's welfare counts the same.

2. I don't think time or distance matters by itself. I have no pure time preference — a life ten thousand years from now counts as much as one today, and a stranger on the other side of the galaxy counts as much as my neighbour.

3. I take the mugger thought experiment at face value: a guy tells me he's got a button that will put a billion billion people through agony unless I hand over my wallet, and I figure there's maybe a 1-in-10^25 chance he's telling the truth. I think I should hand it over. That just seems like what expected value means.

4. At the same time I think fanaticism is a real bug, not a feature. I don't want my life or my donations or my career dictated by 1-in-10^40 chances of astronomically good outcomes. Something has clearly gone wrong once the probabilities get that small.

5. My fix, which I invented myself so it's probably bad: a stakes ceiling. No single scenario is allowed to count for more than 10^18 life-years of welfare when I'm comparing options. Below the ceiling, compare expected values as normal. Above it, everything ties.

6. In my actual life I'm pretty risk-averse — I have insurance, I don't buy lottery tickets, I'd never bet my rent on a coin flip. My friends tell me this contradicts (1).

7. I think the universe is probably spatially infinite, so there's probably already infinitely much good and bad in it. I don't see how my view survives that, but I also don't think it should change anything I actually do, so I mostly try not to think about it.

where's the real trouble here? and is there one single change that would clean up more than one of these at once?
