I'm trying to get my position on the socialist calculation debate straight before a seminar, and I keep flip-flopping, so what I want is a consistency check rather than a summary of the debate. Here's where I've landed:

1. The Austrian objection at its best is epistemic: the knowledge you'd need in order to plan is dispersed, largely tacit, and tied to particular times and places, and market prices are a device for aggregating it that no central body can replicate.
2. The Austrian objection at its best is really about discovery: markets are a procedure for finding out what's worth producing, and that knowledge doesn't exist until rivalrous competition with real losses generates it, so no central body could have it even in principle.
3. Mises's original challenge was about calculation rather than incentives — no prices for capital goods means no rational way of comparing alternative uses — and the incentive critique is a seperate, later layer that people now tack on.
4. Lange's competitive solution was a serious and internally coherent answer to the static allocation question: a board adjusting prices by trial and error, managers following cost and pricing rules, consumer and labour markets left intact.
5. The decisive failure of that model is the agency one: managers have no residual claim, and the board has no profit-and-loss test to tell it whether its trial and error is converging on anything.
6. The old computational objection — too many equations for any planning board to solve — was always a red herring, and it's definately dead now that machines can do the sums.
7. Soviet-type economies failed mainly because of the knowledge problem, not because of incentives.
8. So I think the idea that you could just let a sufficiently good algorithm run the allocation deserves to be taken seriously; the classic arguments don't refute it, they only show it would need the right incentive structure.

What I want to know is which of these actually hang together and which are quietly pulling against each other. I suspect some of them only look like they clash. Rank the genuine tensions by how bad they are, say for each what would have to give and what that would cost me, and then tell me the single revision that would tidy up the most. Don't tell me who won the debate and don't send me off to read anything — I've done the reading, I just want to know whether my own package is stable.
