So my friend Priya has gotten really into this Daniel Dennett thing about the three stances — physical, design, intentional — and now she wont shut up about it. The way I get it: sometimes the only way to predict a thing is to treat it like it has beliefs and desires, and the real question is when that's a fact about the thing vs. just a handy way of talking.

Anyway she's been handing down verdicts on everything and I'm trying to reverse-engineer her rule. So far:

- A chess engine she plays online: real. "It genuinely is trying to win. There's a fact of the matter about what it wants."
- Her dog: real, obviously.
- The thermostat in her house: no way. "It doesn't want the room at 70 degrees, the dial is just a switch. Nothing in there is about anything."
- Her mom's plants leaning toward the window: no. "That's design stance stuff, theres no wanting in it."
- The hurricane everyone said was "aiming" at Florida: no.
- The vending machine at school that "eats" your dollar: no.
- And she says being alive or being conscious isn't even the point, because the chess engine counts and it's neither.

She hasn't ruled on this one yet and I want to know what she'll say. When a hawk dives into a flock of starlings, the whole flock turns like it's one animal, and you can actually call the flock's next move by assuming it's trying to get away from the hawk. But no single bird is running that plan, and nothing anywhere in the flock is representing the hawk or the flock.

So what's her verdict, and whats the rule she's actually running? I bet there's more than one rule that fits everything up there — I want the candidates, what each one says about the starlings, which one you'd bet on and why, and what else I should ask her about so that her answer would tell the two rules apart for good. And if there's a rule that fits all her calls that I've completely missed, say so.
