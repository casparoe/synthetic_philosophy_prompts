My gran died a few weeks ago and its turned into a mess over one line in the will — she left "the tea service" to my sister, and now my brother is arguing that a tea service isn't a thing you can leave to anybody, it's just a bunch of cups and saucers and a pot, so really each cup got left to whoever, and he should get some. Which is a lawyer question, fine. But it sent me down a rabbit hole and I ended up listening to a podcast episode about something called the special composition question, and before I say any of this out loud at the next family dinner I want to know whether the guy knew what he was talking about.

What he said was roughly this. The special composition question asks when a bunch of things add up to one thing. There are three main answers. The nihilist says never — there are only elementary particles, and tables and chairs and people are convenient fictions. The universalist says always — any bunch of things you like composes a further thing, so there is a genuine object made out of my left shoe and the Eiffel Tower. And van Inwagen says sometimes, and the sometimes is when the parts are caught up in a life: a chair is just some wood arranged chairwise, but a cat is a real object because all its parts are pulling together to keep it alive. So the upshot is that the only real objects are living things. Which is basically the Ship of Theseus puzzle again — same planks, new planks, is it the same ship — and in the end it's a verbal dispute anyway, since "object" is just a word and you can carve things up however you like, so it doesn't really matter how you answer.

That last bit is doing alot of work for me, because if it's all just verbal then my brother is right that it's nothing but cups, and if it isn't then maybe gran could leave a collection as a collection. So: how much of what he said is right? Go through it claim by claim — what would someone who actually works on this accept, what would they wince at, what's just wrong — and tell me whether "it's all verbal so who cares" is something the field settled or just one camp's line, and whether the Ship of Theseus thing is really the same question or a different one that sounds like it. And if van Inwagen's view doesn't actually say what the guy said it says, tell me what it does say, and why the difference matters for whether a tea service can be one thing.
