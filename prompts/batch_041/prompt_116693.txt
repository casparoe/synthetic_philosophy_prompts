A friend and I argued late into the night about the cluster of problems usually gathered under the heading embedded agency: self-reference and reflection, logical uncertainty, decision theory for agents that are part of the world they are deciding about, ontology identification, and the rest. Her claim, which I reject, is that this is two problem sets wearing one name — problems that arise because an agent must represent itself, and problems that arise because an agent is causally inside the world it acts on — and that the first set is an artifact of a representational choice that will eventually dissolve, while the second is where the real work lies. I think the split misdescribes why the field is stuck, but I could not say why as clearly as I wanted to.

Suppose the cluster is worth treating as a single agenda. I have roughly two years of full-time attention to give it — one person, conceptual and theoretical work only, no engineering — and I need to decide what to work on. Which open questions should I take up, and in what order? I care more about the reasoning than about the list: which questions are upstream of which, which are tractable for someone doing philosophy rather than ML research, and what argument or result over the next year would move a question up or down. If the premise that these form one agenda is confused, or if two years of one person's attention is simply the wrong unit for the question, say so plainly rather than ranking from my menu.
