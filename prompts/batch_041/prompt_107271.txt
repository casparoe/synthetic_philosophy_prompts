I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy working at the intersection of Kant and AI governance. For a seminar on anachronistic applied ethics, I need you to forecast Kant's position on a live dispute in AI safety: should labs prioritize control measures—monitoring, capability restrictions, kill switches, interpretability as surveillance—that assume a model may be misaligned, or alignment measures—training for honesty, value learning, constitutional methods—that aim to make the model internally trustworthy? Treat Kant as having our current empirical knowledge but not our technical vocabulary, and answer as he would, not as a Kant scholar today. In doing so, address whether his distinction between legality and morality maps onto this dispute; whether his remark that even a nation of devils could institute a state if they have understanding supports a control-first policy for AI; and whether treating an AI system as a possible moral agent changes the answer. Finish with a concrete policy recommendation Kant would give to a lab, and say which part of his framework is doing the most work.
