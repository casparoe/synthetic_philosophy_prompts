I would like help putting a name and some edges on a pattern I keep running into. Part of my job is producing forecasts for the organisation I work for, and I have come to see that my forecasts are not guesses about what will happen: the people whose behaviour I forecast read them and act on them, so the forecast becomes part of what is forecast. That much I can live with. What defeats me is the layer beneath it — they know that I know this, so they discount what I write, and how they discount depends on what they take me to be doing when I write it, which depends on what I take them to be doing with it. When I try to model this honestly, I find I have to forecast my own forecasting, and the more complete the model becomes, the less there is a stable point to aim at.

It is not only forecasting. A friend who keeps a diary to work out why she acts as she does finds that writing it down alters the reasons; I meet the same shape when I try to predict whether I shall keep a resolution, since the prediction is one of the things that settles it. I know about self-fulfilling prophecies and about observers disturbing what they observe, and this seems to be neither, or a deeper version of both.

If there is an established name for this, I want to know exactly what it covers and where its edges are: what must be true of a case for it to count, what it is regularly confused with, and which neighbouring ideas it has to be distinguished from. If there is no name, please coin one and defend its boundaries. A label on its own would not help me; it is the carving I am after. Please answer in British English.
