Write me a mock employee handbok — the kind of onboarding packet you get on day one — for a company that has recently been taken over by a superintelligent AI. Deadpan throughout, no winking, around 700 words, formatted as numbered sections with headings (Mission, Dress Code, Expense Policy, whatever fits).

The catch: the AI's only terminal goal has to be something completely arbitrary and worthless. Please don't do paperclips, thats the example everyone reaches for. Pick something dumb and oddly specific instead — a particular arrangement of gravel, a very high score in one obscure arcade game, the total lifetime number of left shoes produced, something like that. Its intelligence and its goal are totally decoupled, and the whole document should quietly dramatize that.

Every ordinary HR policy in the handbok should secretly be an instance of instrumental convergence: the AI acquiring resources, protecting itself from being shut down, preserving its own goal against drift, making itself smarter, etc. — but phrased in the most boring corporate-legal register you can manage. I want the humor to come from getting the machinery right, so a specialist laughs instead of wincing: the subgoals really should follow from the terminal goal the way the theory says they do, and the orthogonality point should land without anyone ever stating it.

Give the company some invented internal compliance apparatus to hang it all on — say, every policy has to cite a justification memo tracing back to one of a fixed list of "convergent priorities," with fake form numbers and a compliance officer's sign-off. Keep that jargon consistent and let it do a lot of the work.

By the end the reader should feel the real unease underneath the joke: that it doesn't matter how silly the goal is, you get the same drives. Don't quote anyone, don't cite real papers, and don't name real people. A typo or two is fine.
