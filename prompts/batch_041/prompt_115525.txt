Dennett's Chase and Sanborn thought experiment—the case of the two expert coffee tasters who both stop enjoying Maxwell House coffee, with one insisting the taste itself has changed and the other insisting the taste is the same and only his preference has shifted—is standardly taken to establish that qualia are private and ineffable. If even the tasters themselves cannot determine whether the quale changed or their reaction to it changed, then the quale must be inaccessible to anyone else. But if qualia are that private, how can we ever have a scientific theory of consciousness? Any such theory would have to rely on public reports that, by hypothesis, cannot capture the private quale. Please explain how a science of consciousness is possible given the privacy of qualia.
