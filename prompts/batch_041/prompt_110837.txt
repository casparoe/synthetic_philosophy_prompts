I was married in the spring in a ceremony led by a close friend, and in June I resigned from my job by announcing it out loud in a staff meeting; both acts were carried off with nothing but words, and both are now contested — a relative insists the marriage is invalid because nobody ever verified that our officiant was authorized to conduct it, and my former employer insists I never resigned because the handbook requires written notice. I have since been reading obsessively about what it is for an utterance to do something rather than merely say something, and I want a ranked assessment of harder cases than my own. Please rank the following by how confident you are that the act in question took effect, scoring each from 0 to 1, where 1 means you are certain the act went through and 0 that it did not:

- A colleague says, in front of the whole team, that he resigns, then turns up the next morning saying he was only venting.
- A company's official account posts an apology for a defective product; the chief executive never saw the post and privately disagrees with it.
- Two people exchange vows before a friend they believe is authorized to marry them; she is not.
- A man promises to repay a loan next week, knowing he cannot, and the lender knows he cannot.
- A judge pronounces a five-year sentence on a defendant who has already fled and never hears the verdict.
- Someone tells a friend that he forgives her, while privately resolving never to speak to her again.
- A father tells his adult daughter, in a country where the phrase has no legal effect, that she is no longer his daughter.

Begin with the ordered ranking and its numbers, with no preamble and no restatement of my question; then give one sentence per case explaining the placement, and close with whichever case you think I have described in a way that rigs the answer.
