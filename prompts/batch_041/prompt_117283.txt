I host a small philosophy podcast, and I recently went through a lengthy asylum process in a country that was not my own. That experience has made me want to revisit Michael Dummett's late arguments about immigration with a new urgency. Next week's guest is a philosopher who has been defending a broadly Dummettian position: there is a standing presumption in favor of admitting immigrants; the only legitimate ground for restricting immigration is the receiving country's capacity to absorb newcomers, and even that assessment must be conducted without racial or cultural bias; refugees have a stronger claim than economic migrants; and much opposition to immigration is rooted in racism. I would like you to draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having the guest restate this position. Please include at least one question each on the following: (1) how the presumption in favor of admission is supposed to work and where the burden of proof lies; (2) how a capacity limit can be specified and applied without smuggling in the very racial or cultural biases Dummett condemned; (3) how the guest distinguishes refugees from economic migrants, and whether that distinction can bear the moral weight Dummett places on it; (4) whether the position ultimately collapses into open borders, and if not, what principled stopping point it offers; and (5) whether Dummett's broader anti-realist philosophy, which denies that truth can outrun our capacity to recognize it, has any bearing on the objectivity of moral claims about borders. For each question, please provide a follow-up that is keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give, so that the interview can press the view rather than merely display it. The questions should be specific enough to avoid generic prompts about justice or sovereignty, and they should not ask for reading lists, citations, or bibliographies.
