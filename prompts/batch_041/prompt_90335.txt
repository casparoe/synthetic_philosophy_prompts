I retired from the chemistry dept six years ago and, with the time I got back, I've gotten mildly obsessed with philosophy — reading on the train, argueing with my neighbor who teaches it. Anyway, here's my problem. My old lab had a running joke: we kept a tally of my confident predictions, and I've kept adding to it since I retired. The upshot is that my guesses about measurable stuff are usually right, but my guesses about how people will behave are terrible — when I announce that some group "will never go for that," I'm right about half the time, and the surer I am the worse I do. Now I've been roped onto the board of our little land trust, and we have to decide whether to sell a parcel to fund the stewardship work. My read of the members is that they'll never approve a sale; my read of the numbers is that not selling is nuts. A philosopher friend says the tally settles it: if my people-judgments are coin flips, I should stop pretending I have information and just go with the average, or abstain. My old lab mate says that's rubbish, the tally is just a fact about the past while I have actual reasons here about these particular members. They can't both be right and I can't get them to fit. One more wrinkle: alot of that tally is from the years when I was drinking and sleeping four hours a night, so I'm not even sure the old me is evidence about the current me. What should I do about the vote — and how should I be thinking about this, because I keep concluding I shouldn't trust my conclusion and it's making me dizzy.
