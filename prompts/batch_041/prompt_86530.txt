I hold what I will call the constitutive version of the social-constructionist thesis about race, gender, and money: these kinds are not merely causally shaped by social forces but are brought into and sustained in existence by collective attitudes and practices, such that absent the relevant practice there would be no fact of the matter about who or what falls under them. The causal version — social forces influence how the categories get applied — strikes me as too weak to be interesting, since it is true of virtually every category we use. I want to know what would count against the constitutive version. I am not asking for the standard biological-realist or natural-kind objections, which I take to be answering a different question; I want the cases and arguments that should actually move someone who already accepts the framing.

In particular: what should I make of kinds whose extension seems fixed by facts that obtain whether or not anyone accepts them, yet which exist as kinds only because a practice singles them out — a regulatory category of people exposed to a pollutant, say, or a legal status like adulthood or citizenship? If such cases show that social kinds can have a discovery structure (membership is an objective matter, learnable by anyone, and the practice's own self-image can be systematically wrong about what it tracks), does that defeat the constitutive view, or can it be redescribed as just another social kind? What of money, whose paradigm status as a social kind is usually taken for granted — are there cases where money's causal powers outrun anything a collective attitude could plausibly underwrite? And what of the looping phenomenon, where a classification feeds back into the classified so that members come to fit it — does the constitutive view predict this, or merely accommodate it after the fact?

Rank what you give me from decisive to merely awkward. Tell me what a committed defender would concede counts against the view and what they would dismiss, rather than only what critics say. Then assess honestly whether the thesis, as I have stated it, is insulated from refutation — whether any category turns out to be socially constructed if you look hard enough — and whether that is a defect or the correct level of description. Close by saying what would change your mind about your verdict and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
