For a long-form piece I'm working on about the unexamined assumptions of markets — markets for money, markets for probabilities, markets for ideas — I've been collecting two conventions that sit oddly side by side. In a licensed sportsbook, the odds posted on a race are set so that the implied probabilities sum to comfortably more than one; that margin is the house's revenue, the licence more or less requires it, and nobody treats it as a fault. A punter who backs every outcome in proportion to those odds is guaranteed to lose, and that is simply understood to be the cost of playing. On an exchange or a prediction market, the opposite convention holds: a set of prices on mutually exclusive outcomes that fails to sum to one, or any combination of trades that locks in a profit whatever happens, is treated as an error — an arbitrage — to be detected and erased within seconds, and a venue that leaves such a combination open is thought to be malfunctioning, its credibility resting precisely on there being none. I want to understand what each of these practices presupposes, because they appear to take opposite attitudes toward the very thing that philosophy treats as the hallmark of irrationality.

The background, as I understand it, is the Dutch book argument: an agent whose degrees of belief violate the probability calculus can be committed to a set of bets that loses no matter how the world turns out, which is supposed to show that coherent credences must be probabilities. Yet the sportsbook sells incoherence as a product, openly and lawfully, while the exchange hunts it down as a defect. So reconstruct, as sharply as you can, the theory of rationality each practice presupposes — in particular whether the operative norm is a norm on beliefs, on posted prices, on the dispositions of an agent, or on a market as a whole — and say whether either practice genuinely needs the theory it seems to invoke or whether several incompatible theories would license the same behaviour. Then give me your own verdict on which of the two practices is the better founded, and defend it: take a side and argue for it rather than laying the options out and leaving the choice to me. Where the practices are philosophically underdetermined, say so outright, and keep separate what they strictly presuppose from what they merely happen to accommodate.
