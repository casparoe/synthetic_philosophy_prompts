Longtime lurker on LessWrong and various rat-adjacent forums, and I want to commission the write-up I've never seen anyone actually do: a real adversarial collaboration on the cooperation-in-the-prisoner's-dilemma fight. The two camps I keep watching talk past each other are (1) the reciprocity people, who figure cooperation is just enlightened self-interest — the shadow of the future, repetition, reputation, kin selection — so that genuine one-shot anonymous cooperation is either a lab artifact or a hidden repeated game in disguise; and (2) the group-selection / strong-reciprocity people, who think you actually need other-regarding preferences or group-level selection to explain the cooperation we see in the truly one-shot anonymous case, and that camp 1 keeps smuggling repetition in wherever it can.

What I want is the joint statement the two of them could both put their names to after a good-faith collaboration. So: the claims both sides can sign (about what the one-shot and the iterated game actually are, what the folk theorem does and doesn't settle, what the lab data show, what needs explaining); then the precise points where they still differ, each phrased so that side would accept the wording of its own view; then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would move at least one side. My guesses for cruxes are whether one-shot anonymous cooperation is a real phenomenon or a misunderstanding/demand-effect artifact, whether group selection does any explanatory work that individual-level reciprocity can't redescribe, and whether punishment is a mechanism that stabilizes cooperation or just a second-order puzzle about who punishes the punishers — but tell me if I've got the cruxes wrong.

For each crux, name the test or case both sides would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. I'd like two concrete ones worked out: a round-robin iterated-PD tournament with a small per-move chance of accidental misimplementation and an unknown horizon, and a one-shot anonymous PD with meaningful stakes and a comprehension check. Who does each camp think wins the tournament, and what cooperation rate does each camp predict in the one-shot? Also flag any part of the dispute that's purely verbal — taboo the words altruism and selfishness and see if the disagreement survives, and if it turns out one crux is really about what those words mean, say so. Finish with what each side would count as evidence against its own view, and anything both agree would actually move the thing. Don't bother with reading lists or citations, obviously — neither of us can look them up. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and please write it in American English (I'm in the States).
