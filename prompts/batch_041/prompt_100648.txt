So two people in my reading group got into it over whether the "features" you find in neural nets with sparse autoencoders and linear probes are actually in the network or just something the researcher projects onto it, and I couldn't referee it — I've been lurking on LessWrong and the alignment forums for years and I still can't tell who's right, so I'm outsourcing the call.

Roughly how it went. (1) Sam says a feature that fires on the Golden Gate Bridge and predictably steers the output when you add it to the activations is a real representation: it's causally load-bearing, it recurs across random seeds, it composes with other features. (2) Alex says that's a map/territory confusion — there are infinitely many bases you could decompose the activations into and the autoencoder just picks one, and worse, a probe is a supervised classifier that can *learn* the concept from your labels rather than read it off the net, so decodability shows almost nothing. Sam: but not every decomposition steers the model or survives a new seed, so the ones that do are the real ones. (3) Alex: causal efficacy is cheap — a thermostat's bimetallic strip "represents" temperature under that test and so does a rock propping a door open, so your test isn't testing representation. Sam: that line proves too much, because by that standard human beliefs are just neurons that happen to cause behavior, so either nothing represents anything or the net's features do. (4) Then Alex floats a test I hadn't heard before: take the feature and see whether the net will use it in a recombination it was never trained on — does the Golden Gate feature plug into an inference the training distribution never rewarded? If it only works in the contexts it was trained on, Alex says it's a disposition, not a representation. Sam says that bar is impossibly high and humans mostly fail it too.

Go through it move by move and tell me who's right about what. Don't give me "both have a point" — rate each of the four moves, flag where each of them is smuggling in an assumption, and then say whether Alex's recombination test is actually a good test or just relabels the disagreement.
