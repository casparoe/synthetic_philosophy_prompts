Her line, as I've got it: combatants on both sides of a war have the same permission to kill, but not the same liability to be killed, and those two things come apart in a way the usual debate doesnt track. More specifically she says (i) an unjust combatant who reasonably believes his war is just is permitted to kill, because the permission rides on his evidence rather than on the facts; (ii) that same combatant is still liable to be killed by the just side, because liability to defensive killing tracks the threat you pose, not your culpability; and (iii) liability to punishment after the war tracks culpability again, so most ordinary combatants on the unjust side are killable but not punishable. She also has this move where the equality is a sliding scale — strongest for conscripts who are themselves victims of the regime, thinning out to nothing for volunteers in a war of aggression they could easily have recognized as such.
