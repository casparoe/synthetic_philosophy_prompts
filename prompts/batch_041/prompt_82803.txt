I write about behavioral science and ideas for a general-readership site, mostly long features about language and mind, and I'm trying to get out of the rut of writing the same piece every couple of years about whether your language shapes how you think. So I want to do a quiz instead: readers work through a bunch of concrete cases and get sorted into the view they actually hold, rather than the one they'd say they hold if you asked them straight out.

Can you build it for me? Ten questions, each one a concrete case with three answer options, and no question that just asks whether language shapes thought. Every question has to be something a reader can answer without knowing the labels, so the quiz sorts them by what they say about cases. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions.

Here's what I want the buckets to be. Describe them in plain language, don't just name-drop a school of thought and move on:

- the hard determinist: language sets the outer limit of what can be thought at all
- the lab-influence view: language shifts how people do on non-verbal tasks, colour chips, spatial reasoning, counting, but it doesn't fence anything off
- the only-in-the-moment view: the effect lives in the second or two where you assemble a sentence, and not in what you perceived or remember afterwards
- the reporting view: language changes what people bother to say and what they can reconstruct for retelling, and nothing else
- the universalist: concepts are more or less the same everywhere, language is a coat of paint
- the artifact view: the interesting effects come from the tools we bolt onto language, writing, number notation, lists, code, rather than from the mother tongue itself
- the deflationist: most reported effects are task artifacts that would dissolve under better controls

And then three I've been chewing on that I don't think I've seen spelled out this way. I want these as buckets too, even if the quiz can only half seperate them:

- the cost view: language doesn't change what you can think, it changes what's cheap. It hands you ready-made chunks, so it shifts what you bother to work through. The effect is on effort, not on possibility.
- the coordination view: the real action isn't inside one head at all. Shared labels let a group nail a category down, and individuals then inherit it as a social fact, the way legal or medical categories get inherited. So relativity is a claim about how concepts get distributed through a population, not about brains.
- the inner-speech view: whether language bends your thought depends on whether you happen to be someone who talks to themselves a lot, so the honest version of the hypothesis is a claim about a spread of people, not about a language.

For the cases, use things like: languages that give you absolute directions instead of left and right; languages that force you to mark where your information came from; languages with no exact words for numbers past two or three; the verb-grammar difference in how English and Spanish package a motion; noun gender and how people describe a bridge; two words for blue. The cases should pull apart the things the buckets actually disagree about, which is perception versus memory versus what you can get out in words, the moment of speaking versus the hours after, grammar versus vocabulary versus habits of use, and whether the difference is in the person or in the task.

A few things I need from the quiz itself:

- some questions about tasks you can't just do in words, pointing, matching, remembering a shape, doing it while repeating something out loud
- at least one about an adult learning a second language, and one about a kid still learning the first
- options that are genuinely tempting, so nobody can see which answer wins
- a short note at the end on which buckets the quiz can't really tell apart, and which answer combinations nobody seems to occupy, and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unappealing, or just unexplored
- one line per bucket on its most awkward commitment, so the results page doesn't read like a horoscope

No reading list or citations please. I don't need names and dates, just the positions stated cleanly enough that I can write the intro copy myself.
