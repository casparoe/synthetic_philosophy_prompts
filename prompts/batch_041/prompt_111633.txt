I've been reading about animal minds and AI on and off, and I keep running into a four-rung ladder that goes roughly like this: Darwinian creatures, whose behaviour is basically wired in and sorted out by natural selection; Skinnerian creatures, who learn by trial and error from what actually happens to them; Popperian creatures, who can run the trial inside their own heads and let the bad idea die instead of them; and Gregorian creatures, who borrow thinking tools -- words, numbers, maps, other people's tricks -- from the culture around them. I want to turn that ladder into a decision tree I can actually use to sort cases, instead of a list of labels I nod along to.

Please take the role of a philosopher of mind who's good at drawing boundaries between things people normally lump together, and go slowly, because I think the ladder is doing more than one job at once. Start with a short brainstorm: what are the questions that really carve up this space, before you attach any names to anything?

Then build the tree. Every branch node should be a question I could in principle answer by watching the creature for a while, not one that only makes sense if I already know which box it's meant to land in. I want these cases placed, as leaves or as honest failures to place: a slime mold routing around an obstacle, a songbird, a rat in a Skinner box, a crow bending a piece of wire into a hook, a kid doing long division with a pencil and paper, a chess engine, and a chatbot.

The thing I actually care about: does it matter whether the imagining-before-acting happens inside the skull or gets offloadded onto paper, a tool, or another person? If someone can only simulate by writing it down first, are they Popperian, Gregorian, or a rung of their own? Flag any two cases your tree can't seperate, and say if any of the four rungs turns out to be empty or to be doing double duty. Keep the whole thing to about a page.
