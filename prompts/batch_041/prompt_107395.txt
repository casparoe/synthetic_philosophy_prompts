I am assembling materials for a graduate seminar on demarcation, and I need a worked case that isolates a specific point rather than rehearsing the textbook contrast between astrology and astronomy. Please construct a thought experiment with the following specifications. An amateur researcher advances a revisionist claim about some ordinary, well-documented episode in the historical record — not a lost continent, not ancient astronauts, not a wholesale rewriting of the chronology, and nothing so famous that it already arrives with a label attached. The claim must be straightforwardly falsifiable in Popper's sense and must already have survived at least one genuinely risky test, so that a naive falsificationist would be obliged to classify the researcher's work as scientific rather than pseudoscientific. The case should nonetheless be one in which the reasonable verdict is that this is pseudohistory. Keep it realistic: an independent scholar with genuine archival competence, a comprehensible motive, and a claim that a professional historian would have to take seriously enough to test. Give the scenario in a single paragraph.

Then do the analysis, and please write in American English. State the verdict that each of the following delivers, and why: (a) Popper's falsifiability criterion; (b) the contrast between progressive and degenerating problem-shifts; and (c) a criterion that locates pseudoscience in a distinctive failure of epistemic practice — for instance, the systematic use of immunizing stratagems that shield a central claim from refutation while leaving it formally falsifiable. Identify precisely which feature of the case is doing the work of converting a testable claim into pseudohistory, and say candidly whether that feature earns its keep on epistemic grounds or merely smuggles a social judgment in under an epistemic label. Finally, tell me what happens if the feature is removed — does the case collapse back into ordinary contested history? — and give the strongest line of reply available to the amateur researcher, who will insist that the criterion doing the work misclassifies her. The purpose of the exercise is to determine whether falsifiability can be sufficient for demarcation once a claim's surrounding practice is taken into account, so a case that simply stipulates the pseudoscientific verdict, rather than generating it from the criteria, will not do.
