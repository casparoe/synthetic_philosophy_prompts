I am a novelist — four books, two of them historical — and I am stuck on the fifth. The book is set in a fictional coastal town over roughly forty years, and its subject is a structure rather than a villain: five households drawing on a single depleting stock of fish, each of them acting sensibly at every turn, none able to bind the others, all of them ending up worse off, and none able to leave. I have been calling this a multipolar trap, or Moloch, though I have no economics and no game theory. My grasp of it is that it is a situation and not an event; that nobody wills it; that every party's local choice is defensible; and that it is stable rather than catastrophic, which is what makes it unbearable.

What I want is a translation, not an explanation. Take that structure and restate it as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually think in — I have taught workshops for a decade, so the following is not decoration but the machinery I make decisions with: scene and summary; want against obstacle; the turn; rising action; the difference between a chronicle and a plot, one thing after another versus one thing because of another; dramatic irony; the moment of recognition and the reversal that follows it; the lie a character believes; point of view, and what a given viewpoint can and cannot see; and the distinction between a villain and an opposing force.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the structure arrive intact, which arrive only after I have distorted them, and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all? I have tried this twice, and both drafts collapsed — the first into a villain, the second into a storm.

Please treat the residue as the point of the exercise rather than an afterthought. Where my vocabulary fails, say plainly whether the failure is mine — the craft has no way to render no one did it, or nothing changed, or there was no first move — or whether it is evidence that the talk of traps is quietly doing something craft would never permit, such as personifying an equilibrium and assigning it intentions. I suspect I can only write this book by smuggling in an event, a decision, or an agent that the concept denies. Tell me whether that is a cost I should accept, and if it is, what I owe the reader on the page. Then reverse the exercise once: is there anything my vocabulary insists on — a choice, a moment, a change — that the concept has to deny, and does that denial cost the concept something, or does it only show that the concept was never a story to begin with?
