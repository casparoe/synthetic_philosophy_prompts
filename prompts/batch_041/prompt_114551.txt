Right, I want a thought experiment built to order. Tightness matters way more to me than realism here.

The target: take the old demon who knows the exact present state of some system -- every position and momentum, or whatever the state variables happen to be -- plus the exact laws. I want a system where that demon can in principle grind out the entire future exactly, but cannot reconstruct the past, not even in principle. The present pins down the future; the present does not pin down the past. Not because he's ignorant of something, not because of noise or measurement error, and not because we've stashed the information in some part of the world he isn't looking at -- it has to fall out of the dynamics themselves.

Extra specs, because the easy version of this is boring:
- The same case should also be chaotic in the ordinary sense: the future is fixed but unpredictable in practice, since tiny errors blow up. I want the fixed future, the unpredictable future and the unfixed past all sitting in one scenario.
- No smuggling. Dont hide a bit of the state from the demon, and dont win by stipulation -- i.e. dont just declare that the past doesn't exist, or assume time-asymmetric laws and then not defend that move.
- Don't name a famous equation and stop. Give me the actual scenario: what the system is, what counts as its state, what the demon knows, what he computes, and where the retrodiction failure comes from mechanically.
- The many-to-one-ness has to belong to the dynamics, not to our description of it. So the answer cant be the boring one where the fine-grained dynamics are reversible and he merely can't tell which coarse-grained box it came from. If it turns out no real system can do this and you need an idealization, say so, and say exactly which physical assumption you had to drop to get there.

Then, after the scenario:
1. Step through what the demon knows and doesnt know at each stage, and point at the precise step where going backwards fails.
2. Say which feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens to each of the three facts -- fixed future, unpredictable future, unfixed past -- if you remove that feature. E.g. swap in an invertible version of the same dynamics: what survives?
3. There's a slogan that determinism means the past is as fixed as the future. Someone who believes it will say my case is either not deterministic or is using a broken notion of a state. Give me the strongest version of that complaint, then tell me whether it lands. Relatedly, notice theres two definitions of determinism floating around -- one that only constrains the future given the present, one that constrains every time given any time. Say which one my case satisfies, which it violates, and why that's not a cheap trick.
