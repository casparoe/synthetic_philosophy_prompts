For a seminar paper on Fitch's paradox of knowability, I want the evaluation criteria fixed before I judge any of the proposed responses. Here is the paradox as I understand it. Grant the knowability principle: every truth is knowable. Take any truth p that happens not to be known. The proposition that p and no one knows that p is then itself a truth, so by the principle it must be knowable. But knowing a conjunction requires knowing each conjunct, and knowing that no one knows that p entails that p is not known, which contradicts the other conjunct. So no proposition of that form can be known, hence none can be true, hence no truth is unknown, hence every truth is known. The knowability principle thus collapses into the claim that everything true is known.

What I need from you is the specification: the desiderata that any adequate response to this paradox has to meet. For each desideratum, state the consideration that motivates it and the kind of failure it is meant to catch. In particular, I want you to adjudicate whether each of the following is a genuine requirement or merely the prejudice of one camp:

- that the response actually block the derivation rather than merely register discomfort with its conclusion;
- that it preserve the purpose for which the knowability principle was introduced — giving an anti-realist account of truth along the lines of verifiability — instead of draining the principle of content;
- that any restriction placed on the principle be independently motivated rather than gerrymandered to exclude exactly the propositions that cause trouble;
- that the response be explicit about which logical principles carry the derivation — distribution of knowledge over conjunction, the factivity of knowledge, the modal logic governing possibility, classical versus intuitionistic negation — and that any alteration of those principles be justified rather than merely convenient.

Then tell me which of these desiderata can be jointly satisfied and which cannot. I suspect that the requirement to block the derivation and the requirement to preserve the principle's anti-realist content pull against each other; if they do, say precisely where the conflict bites and which side a defender of knowability should concede. Also say which single desideratum does the most work in ruling out candidate responses.

Finally, test the resulting list against two repairs I have seen sketched: first, restricting knowability to propositions whose being known would not be self-contradictory, so that the proposition that p and no one knows that p falls outside the principle's scope; second, denying that knowledge distributes over conjunction. For each repair, say which desiderata it satisfies, which it violates, and whether the violation is fatal.
