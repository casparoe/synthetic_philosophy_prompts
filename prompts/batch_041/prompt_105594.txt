Me and two friends have been going in circles for like three weeks now on the boundary problem — basically, when a polity makes a binding collective decision, who actually gets to count as part of the demos, i.e. whose interests or consent have to be taken into account for the decision to have any legitimacy. The two options everyone reaches for are the all-subjected principle (everyone the state's coercion actually lands on) and the all-affected principle (everyone the decision actually affects), and we keep finding the same two failures: all-affected seems to balloon until the demos is the entire planet and every future generation, while all-subjected either leaves out people who are clearly shaped by the decision but never get coerced by it, or, if you stretch "subjected" enough to catch them, it just becomes all-affected again with extra steps. So over beers we tried to cook up our own criterion to stress-test: the demos should be all and only those people for whom, if the decision went against them, they'd have neither an acceptable exit nor a real voice in revising it later — we've been calling it the "no-exit-no-voice" test. The idea being it's supposed to capture why tourists and random foreigners don't belong (they can leave, or they were never in the revision game) while still catching people like long-term residents without citizenship or folks downstream of a dam decision who can't move and can't vote on it.

Here's what I actually want from you. Take the proposition to be: the no-exit-no-voice test states the correct necessary-and-sufficient condition for membership in a demos (setting aside how you'd operationalize "acceptable exit" and "real voice" — assume for the sake of the question that they can be made precise). I'm sitting at about 0.3 on that. Give me your own number, but I care way more about the decomposition: how much rides on whether all-subjected is the true ground of inclusion in the first place, how much on whether affected interests deserve to count even when the affected party has both exit and voice, how much on whether the exit/voice clause is doing independent work or is just a dressed-up restatement of "subjected," how much on whether there's even a single correct criterion here versus a pluralist or context-dependent story where "demos" means different things for different decisions, and any other terms you think belong. A rough probability and one line of justification for each, then how you combine them into your final number. And then tell me which of those sub-questions my 0.3 is most sensitive to, and what argument or piece of evidence would actually move me most — like is there some kind of case that would break the no-exit-no-voice test cleanly, or is the real fight upstream at whether a demos has a determinate boundary at all?
