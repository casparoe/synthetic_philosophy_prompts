Sorting-quiz time. I run a small reading group and I'm sick of people anouncing which camp they're in before they've said anything about the cases, so I want a decision tree for the nonidentity problem that sorts them by their answers instead of their labels.

Build it from the logical space up: questions first, then the cells, then the names. 6-8 branch nodes, each one a question someone can answer without knowing which position it leads to, and 6-10 leaves, each named by the claim it makes rather than by whoever defends it -- put "unnamed" where the cell is empty and say whether it's empty because the view is incoherent, because it's unattractive, or just because nobody's bothered. One clause per leaf on the commitment that leaf has the hardest time explaining.

Axes I want, roughly in the order I'd ask them:

1. Does calling a choice wrong require a particular person who is worse off than they otherwise would have been? And if so, what does "otherwise" mean -- that same person never existing, or a different person existing in their place? I want the same-number and different-number cases to come apart here.
2. Is harm essentially comparative, or is putting someone in a bad state enough?
3. Can existence itself be a harm? Can it be a benefit? The asymmetry position should drop out as a leaf, not get smuggled in as a premise.
4. Can someone be wronged, or a right violated, with no one who ends up worse off?
5. Do the person's own later attitudes about having been born count as evidence about their interests, or as a seperate question entirely?

Cases to run everyone through, in this order: (a) a couple who delay conceiving for a year and end up with a different, mildly worse-off child -- no one harmed, or someone? (b) a prenatal intervention that cures a condition but changes who the kid turns out to be, so the untreated kid never exists; (c) a woman who says flatly that she'd rather not have been born, and means it, though her life is worth living by any external measure; (d) a breeding programme for flat-faced dogs that produces dogs who suffer but whose lives are still worth living.

Two house rules: flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate on these cases instead of pretending a branch does it, and don't name a single philosopher or paper -- I'm reading this offline and I don't want a bibliography, just the positions stated as claims.

Cap it at 800 words, tree first, and if you've got leftover thoughts put them in one short paragraph at the end.
