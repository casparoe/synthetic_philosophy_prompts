I'm the secretary of a small darts league — nine teams, a printed rulebook that's been added to since 1974, and an AGM every October in the back room of the King's Head. We've got ourselves in a right tangle about what counts as a proper rule change and I'd like someone to tell me what I actually think, because I keep contradicting myself.

Here's where I come out on the ones we've argued about:

1. In 2011 the AGM used the amendment rule (Rule 30, two-thirds of those present) to change Rule 30 itself, so that a simple majority would do in future. I think that was fine. If two-thirds of us want to make it easier to change things, who's to stop us?

2. In 2013, on the back of that, a simple majority added Rule 31: 'Rule 31 may not be amended or repealed.' I think that one's worthless. You can't tie the hands of people who haven't joined yet, and any AGM can vote it away. But then I also think the 2015 rule — subs can only go up if the rise is passed at two AGMs running — is perfectly good, and I'd hold anyone to it. Both of those are just trying to stop a later AGM doing something. Why is one real and the other not?

3. In 2016 the comittee used the bit in Rule 2 about 'the spirit of the game' to throw out an eligibility rule the AGM had passed by a big majority. I think that was bang out of order. The committee's not there to overrule the AGM. Though I'll admit I can't say what's actually wrong with it, seeing as the committee was only doing what Rule 2 says it can.

4. In 2017 the AGM passed a rule about forfeits on a show of hands when Rule 30 said postal ballot. So it wasn't passed properly. I say it isn't a rule and we'd have to do it again. Procedure's procedure.

5. But then the no-smoking rule in the pavilion. Never passed at any AGM as far as anyone can find, not in the printed book, enforced since about 1998 by whoever's running the night. I'd call that a rule of the league the same as any other. Same with the handshake at the end of a match, which isn't written down anywhere either.

4 and 5 seem to pull opposite ways and I can't make them sit down together.

The one I really can't call: at last year's AGM someone pointed out that a simple majority of those present — usually about eleven people out of two hundred members — could vote to abolish the AGM altogether and hand the lot to the committee. Nothing in the book stops it. Half of me says that's what the rulebook says, so it's a valid change, and then we've no AGM, which is a mad thing to arrive at by following the rules. The other half says a vote can't be allowed to kill off the very thing that makes votes count.

So: is there one principle about when a rule change sticks that gets all of 1 to 5, and that tells me what to do about the last one? What I've been working with is something like 'a change is good if the rulebook in force at the time let you make it that way, and where the rulebook is silent, whatever we've all been doing counts as the rule' — but I can see that's two principles wearing one coat and I don't know which to drop. If there's no single principle, give me the smallest set, say which of my verdicts I have to give up, and be straight with me about it. I'm not after the names of philosophers, I want the actual principle.

Two more I haven't had to decide yet, if it helps to test it on them: can a rule that was passed perfectly properly be killed off by the committee simply never enforcing it for ten years? And can the league bind itself now never to merge with the county association, or can a later AGM always undo that?
