You are an expert in AI safety and the philosophy of science. I am a retired experimental physicist who has lately been reading philosophy and the conceptual foundations of machine learning. I am preparing to interview, for a small seminar series, an invented but philosophically serious researcher who defends a view I will call evaluation-relative misgeneralization. Her thesis: goal misgeneralization is not a property of a trained model's internal goal but a relation among a policy, a task distribution, and a normative decision about which behavioral invariants preserve the goal. She thinks the standard inner-alignment picture reifies a mesa-objective, and that many alleged cases of deceptive alignment are better described as an evaluation suite failing to specify the right equivalence class of tasks. She also holds that interpretability can reveal representational structure but cannot by itself settle which structure is the goal. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that press her on the content of this view rather than let her restate it. Begin by brainstorming the strongest objections to her position. Include at least one question each on: how she distinguishes ordinary distributional shift from misgeneralization without collapsing into behaviorism; whether the mesa-optimization argument can be reformulated in her terms or is simply denied; what work the normative choice does and whether it makes misgeneralization useless for technical safety work; and how she would treat a model that behaves correctly off-distribution for reasons that seem robust but not human-legible. For each question, give the reply she is most likely to make and a follow-up that makes that reply harder to sustain. Avoid generic or biographical questions, and do not include citations or reading lists.
