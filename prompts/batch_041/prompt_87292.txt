I sit on my university's animal-research ethics committee, and after arguing with a friend for most of last night I need to draft the written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. A lab has applied to run a study on farmed insects: it would rear several hundred thousand of them and then kill them under conditions designed to test whether certain common farming practices cause lasting harm. The stated aim is suffering reduction at scale — the results would inform welfare standards that could affect billions of animals — but the study itself inflicts harm on a very large number of creatures whose capacity to suffer is genuinely uncertain, and the committee is split. My friend's line was that if there is any real chance insects suffer, a suffering-focused ethics positively requires us to accept lesser suffering to prevent greater suffering, and that our uncertainty cuts toward approving the study because the expected gains are so vast; I think that treats a bare possibility of suffering as a near-certainty whenever the arithmetic is convenient. Please draft the committee's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — giving a concrete recommendation and a time frame, reasons the researchers could actually respond to, and a fair statement of the strongest argument on the side you decide against.
