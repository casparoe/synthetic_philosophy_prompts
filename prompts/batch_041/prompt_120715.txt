Please describe the most important implications of Karl Popper's shifting appraisal of Darwinism for how we should assess adaptationist explanations in evolutionary biology. Popper once treated the theory of natural selection as a metaphysical research programme rather than a testable scientific theory, on the grounds that its core principle — that the organisms which survive and reproduce are the ones best fitted to survive and reproduce — comes close to a tautology and therefore cannot be falsified; he later partially retracted this, holding that natural selection can be testable when it is formulated in terms of specific mechanisms of heredity and variation and used as a framework for testable theories. The question I want you to address is whether that later concession actually rescues adaptationist hypotheses, which often seem to be confirmable no matter what trait is observed, or whether the tautology problem merely relocates to the auxiliary assumptions about inheritance, development, and environmental stability. Consider in particular whether construing fitness as a probabilistic propensity rather than as actual reproductive success changes the situation, and what that would imply for the evidential standards we should apply to a particular adaptive story about, say, a behavioural trait in a social animal. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
