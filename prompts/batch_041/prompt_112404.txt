I'm a senior software engineer — mostly distributed systems, backend services, and observability tooling — with no formal training in philosophy, and I've gotten into a long-running argument with a close friend who did a PhD in philosophy of mind. His position, as best I can reconstruct it, is a version of illusionism: that consciousness is not an additional fact about what a system does over and above its information processing, but rather a kind of user illusion — an interface a system renders to itself, where the felt qualities we believe we introspect are closer to the contents of that rendering than to properties of the underlying machinery. Some days this strikes me as obviously correct, other days as a confusion dressed up in good metaphors, and I've started to suspect that the reason I can't settle it is that I keep evaluating it in a vocabulary that isn't mine. So I'd like you to do the translation work explicitly, and to be as honest about its failures as about its successes. State the position in its strongest form rather than the cartoon version — I'm not interested in knocking down a straw man, I want to know whether the real thing survives the trip.

Please restate the main claims of this position, claim by claim, in the vocabulary I actually use: interfaces versus authoritative state, the kernel/userspace boundary, syscalls, rendered views versus the source of truth, telemetry and self-reporting, caching, eventual consistency, leaky abstractions, and the general gap between what a system exposes about itself through an API and what is actually going on inside it. For each claim, say how faithful the translation is, what had to be bent or fudged to make it fit, and what simply has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Then give me the residue report I actually care about: for anything that didn't survive, tell me whether the failure is a defect of my engineering vocabulary — something it structurally cannot express — or a sign that the philosophical claim was less clear than it looked once you try to say precisely what it amounts to. The one thing I'd ask you not to do is smuggle my framework's assumptions in and call it a translation; in particular, don't quietly assume that anything a system reports about itself is epiphenomenal or causally inert just because a dashboard isn't the source of truth. I want the disagreement preserved, not dissolved.

Please give me the answer as a JSON object with these fields: `translations`, an array in which each element has `claim` (the original claim stated plainly, without jargon), `engineering_rendering` (the claim restated in my vocabulary), `fidelity` (one of `clean`, `distorted`, or `no_counterpart`), `what_was_fudged` (a string, or null when nothing was), and `residue` (what did not carry over, or null); `residue_summary`, a string describing the overall pattern in what failed to translate; `residue_diagnosis`, an array in which each element has `residue_item`, `verdict` (one of `defect_of_engineering_vocabulary`, `original_claim_was_unclear`, `both`, or `neither`), and `reasoning`; and finally `bottom_line`, a short string giving your verdict on whether my suspicion that this is just a rendering-versus-state distinction in disguise is right, or whether the translation is missing something real.
