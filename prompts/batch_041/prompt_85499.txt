I share an office with Priya, who's writing a thesis on something in the metaphysics of modality, and for the last few months I've been keeping a tally of where she comes down on the usual rigid-designation cases, mostly because she's the only person I know who'll give a straight answer instead of saying it depends. So: 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' — true, necessary, and she's adamant we know it only a posteriori. 'Water is H₂O' and 'gold is the element with atomic number 79' — same verdict, necessary a posteriori, no hedging at all. 'Cicero is Tully' — necessary. But 'this table is made of wood', said while pointing at the actual wobbly table in our office — contingent, because that very table could have been made of ice; whereas 'this table came from that particular block of wood' she says is necessary, it couldn't have come from a different block. 'The standard metre bar is one metre long' — a priori, she says, but contingent. And 'pain is the firing of C-fibres' she says is just false, on the grounds that if it were true it would have to be necessary, and she can perfectly well conceive of the pain without any C-fibres firing.

The reason I'm asking is that we've hit a new case and she won't commit. It's 'red is the surface reflectance profile R', where R is the particular spectral curve a colour scientist measured off a sample swatch we were passing round — and yes, there's a whole side argument about red light, red LEDs and afterimages, none of which reflect anything, which she keeps waving away. I'd like your prediction rather than mine. What will she say: necessary a posteriori, contingent a posteriori, or not a genuine identity at all? More than that, I want the rule she's actually running. I can see at least three that fit her tally so far — one that says any true identity between rigid designators is necessary; one that says necessity tracks whether some hidden structure is doing the explaining of the appearances, and lapses when the term's reference is fixed by how the thing looks or feels; and one where she's quietly a two-dimensionalist for whom 'necessary' only ever means necessary in the secondary intension. Say which fits her best, what each of them predicts about the red case, and name the further case that would actually seperate them, because I'd rather settle this than keep taking notes.
