I'm a science and ideas journalist, and I keep tripping over the same thing, so I want an argument, not a reading list — please no bibliographies, I can't use them and neither can you.

Here's the practice I'm staring at. Every institution I walk into cuts European history into the same lumps — Middle Ages, Renaissance, Scientific Revolution, Enlightenment — and the cutting is weirdly physical. The library's call numbers change right around 1500. The job ad wants a medievalist if you stop at 1500 and an early modernist if you start there. The survey course does the whole thing in twelve weeks. Nobody decided this last Tuesday; it's just how the furniture sits.

I don't want you to tell me which periodization is correct. I want to know what the practice presupposes.

Does treating the Renaissance as a thing you can date and locate presuppose that periods are real joints in history rather than filing tools? Can a hard-nosed instrumentalist use them anyway without contradicting herself, and if so, is anything actually lost? What would a version of the practice that took the anti-realist view seriously even look like — would the shelf marks go away, or just change?

The words themselves look loaded. Middle, dark, rebirth — that seems to smuggle in a story about a fall and a recovery, and "Renaissance" seems to presuppose that what changed was high culture and ideas, not plague, climate, or trade routes. Which of those commitments does the shelving actually need, and which is it just carrying along by habit?

And what about the export? Medieval Japan, medieval India. Does using the label there presuppose that the European sequence is the default timeline for everybody, or is that just a harmless borrowed adjective? Then take the alternatives seriously: some people want to stretch the Middle Ages out to 1800, others want to junk the Renaissance entirely. When you try that, whats actually breaking — the labels, or the causal stories the labels were quietly holding up?

Last thing: tell me where this practice is underdetermined. Where would several incompatible theories of how history works all license exactly the same shelving, so that the furniture can't tell us which one we've committed to? And don't just say periods are constructed, I know that part, thats the easy bit.
