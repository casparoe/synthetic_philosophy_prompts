I want to put a specific position about dispositions on the table and have you tell me what would count against it, ranked from what should genuinely move me to what is merely awkward.

The position is this. Every true disposition ascription — every claim to the effect that a thing is disposed to M when C — is made true by an occurrent, intrinsic property of that thing together with the laws of nature. Call that property the base. The view carries three commitments. First, wherever there is a disposition there is a base: no bare dispositions. Second, base plus laws is sufficient for the manifestation, so a thing that has the base cannot fail to M when C obtains. Third, the disposition is nothing over and above the base and the laws — grounded in them, not identical to them, and not an extra ingredient in the world. Dispositions are thus real but derivative, and their reality is inherited from the occurrent.

I want the objections kept apart rather than bundled, because I suspect they bear very differently on the three commitments:

- Cases where the disposition is uncontroversially present but base plus laws plainly does not deliver the manifestation: the finkish cases, in which the arrival of the trigger destroys the base at that very moment, and the masking cases, in which the base and the trigger are both intact but some further condition blocks the outcome.
- Cases where the manifestation does occur, and a base of the relevant kind is present, but intuitively there is no disposition at all — the manifestation comes by another route. These threaten sufficiency in the other direction.
- Cases where a disposition seems to obtain with no occurrent intrinsic base: fundamental propensities whose whole nature is to produce a certain outcome, dispositions that appear to depend on facts about other things rather than on the bearer's intrinsic constitution, and dispositions of collectives or abstracta.
- The suspicion that the view is either trivially true or false. If nothing constrains which occurrent properties may serve as bases, the three commitments look cheap and unlosable; if something does constrain them, the view becomes exposed to empirical and conceptual risk. Is the needed constraint actually available, or does the view oscillate between vacuity and refutation?
- Whether the position collapses into a rival — a regularity account in Humean dress, or a view on which the base is itself dispositional, so that the occurrent foundation quietly disappears.

Tell me which of these a defender would concede as counting against the view and which they would simply dismiss, which are live rather than merely logically possible, and what the position predicts that its rivals do not. If it turns out to be well insulated by tacit qualifications, say so plainly and say whether that is a defect in a view of this kind. For my part, I would be moved by a case in which the disposition is clearly present and its base clearly absent, and unmoved by far-fetched science fiction, so tell me whether the good objections all end up resting on cases of that sort.
