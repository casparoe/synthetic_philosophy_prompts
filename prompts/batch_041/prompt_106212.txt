ok so this is a strange request but I need a funny one out of you.

A while back I fell down a rabbit hole on this idea I'm pretty sure is called tranquilism, and the whole thing goes like this: the only thing that's actually good, all by itself, is the absence of craving and suffering. Not pleasure, not happiness, not getting what you want. Pleasure is only good because it switches off a wanting for a little while, and getting what you want is only good because it stops you wanting it. So the good state is the flat calm one, where nothing is pulling at you and nothing hurts. If you're not craving and not hurting then theres nothing bad in you at all, and thats the whole theory. It's weirdly beautiful, I get why people fall for it.

Anyway I tried to live like that for about four months and it broke my brain in a very specific way, and now I want to laugh at it instead.

What I want: write me a FAQ page, the customer help page for a made up clinic that sells a treatment which removes your cravings. The kind of thing a company would put on their own site. Numbered questions with answers underneath. About 700 words. Dead flat, no jokes that announce themselves, no winking at the reader, the clinic believes every word it prints. Make up the company and the founder, don't drag real people into it.

The jokes have to come out of the actual logic of the view, not out of me being silly, so here are the bits that genuinly bug me and that I want the page to have to deal with somehow:

- if wanting to be craving-free is itself a craving, how do you even sign up? do you have to be tricked into the treatment? can you consent to it?
- once the craving to be treated is gone, wouldn't the patient just get up and walk out halfway through? and if the treatment also removes the urge to tell other people about it, how does the clinic advertise? how does the founder want the business to do well, if wanting is the disease
- with no cravings left, what makes anyone do anything, and does the theory even care? is someone in a deep sleep, or in a coma, or a rock sitting on a beach, already living the ideal life? because if the rock is fine then so is being dead, and the theory owes us an answer on that one, the dead don't crave anything either
- can you compare two people's calm? if mine is an 8 and yours is a 3, does that mean anything, or is that just a fake number the clinic made up for the brochure
- is there a kind of suffering that no amount of calm can pay back, like some things are just off the scale and can't be traded for anything no matter how much good you pile on the other side? I think the theory needs something like that and I want the page to trip over it
- grief. somebody comes in because they can't stop aching over a person they lost. is loving someone a craving. what does the clinic tell that person

And it has to stay readable for someone who's never thought about any of this for one second. Also don't be mean about it, I actually like the idea, I just want to see the seams.
