For a presentation to my school's math club, which is spending a term on fair division, I want to explain the conceptual difference between the Nash bargaining solution and the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution rather than just reciting their axioms. Take two bargainers with disagreement point (0, 0), each of whom can receive at most 10, and a feasible frontier given by y = 10 − x²/10 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 10; there the Nash solution is roughly (5.77, 6.67), while the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution — the point at which each bargainer receives the same fraction of the largest payoff available to them — is roughly (6.18, 6.18). Please explain what each solution takes to be the essential fact about a bargain: why the Kalai–Smorodinsky point is settled by the disagreement point and the two maximum payoffs alone, so that it is blind to the shape of the frontier in between, whereas the Nash point is fixed by the local trade-off the frontier presents at the agreement; and whether the disagreement between the two is better understood as one about fairness or as one about what rational bargainers would agree to. I would also like to know why the two solutions coincide when the frontier is the straight line x + y = 10, and what that shows about when the distinction between them actually matters.
