I am writing a chapter that defends a specific verdict — that an individual who buys factory-farmed meat is morally responsible for a share of the resulting animal suffering even though her individual purchase makes no difference to how much suffering occurs — and before I commit to any one account I want the specification settled. Write the desiderata: what must any adequate theory of individual responsibility for collective harms deliver?

The family of cases I need it to handle: a lifetime of carbon emissions; a single vote in a large election; one purchase among millions; a threshold harm in which my contribution is neither necessary nor sufficient; and the contrasting case in which my contribution is necessary (the deciding vote, the last person needed to lift the car). For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that motivates it, and a verdict on whether it is a genuine desideratum or an artifact of one camp — consequentialist, Kantian, virtue-theoretic, or the metaphysics of causation. Include, as constraints the theory cannot simply contradict, the facts that aggregate contributions do cause the harm, that thresholds are frequently vague and unknown, and that agents act under uncertainty about both.

I have three convictions I want tested against the list rather than smuggled into it. First, that an act can be attributable to an agent as hers even when it is not a difference-maker, and that causal responsibility and attributability can come apart. Second, that some acts may be wrong in virtue of what they constitute or express as part of a practice, independently of their marginal causal contribution. Third, that backward-looking blame and forward-looking reasons for action are distinct questions, so that a theory should not settle one by quietly settling the other.

Tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are negotiable, and flag any pair that looks jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect real tension between the demand that the theory be action-guiding and scale sensibly, on the one hand, and the demand that it respect the difference-making intuition, on the other; and again between a share-of-the-harm conception of responsibility and the indeterminacy of any actual share. If a single requirement does the most to exclude the popular accounts, say which and why. A table for the requirements and their motivating cases is welcome; skip the scorecard against named theories, since I will run that myself.
