I am a philosopher of science preparing a case study for a graduate seminar on decision-making under uncertainty. I want you to analyze the decision to launch the space shuttle Challenger in 1986 through the lens of imprecise probabilities, and I want the analysis to run in both directions: the theory should illuminate the episode, and the episode should test the theory. I am not interested in a rehearsal of the familiar organizational narrative—pressure, schedule, routinized risk—except where it bears on the formal question. Treat the pre-launch evidence about the solid rocket booster O-rings as failing to determine a unique probability of catastrophic failure. Instead, let it determine a set of credal states: a set of probability distributions over the relevant outcomes, reflecting sparse data, an unprecedented low-temperature regime, and contested engineering models. Simplify the decision to a terminal choice between launching and scrubbing, with outcomes including mission success, vehicle loss without crew loss, and vehicle loss with crew loss; you may add outcomes if the analysis requires them, but say why.

Use the following imprecise-probability apparatus explicitly. Distinguish risk (known chances) from ambiguity (a set of admissible chances). Then apply, in turn: maximin (choose the act with the best worst-case expected value); maximax (choose the act with the best best-case expected value); Hurwicz with an optimism coefficient you state and justify; E-admissibility (an act is admissible if it maximizes expected value for at least one prior in the set); and a precautionary rule that permits an act only if its lower expected value exceeds a threshold you specify and justify. You may also consider whether the case exhibits dilation—whether conditioning on the cold-temperature evidence widens rather than narrows the set of admissible posteriors; if you do not know the term, treat it as the question whether that evidence sharpens or blurs the risk estimate.

Then do three things. First, reconstruct the problem: what are the acts, states, outcomes, and the set of priors? What evidence constrains the set, and what leaves it unconstrained? Second, report what each decision rule recommends, and say whether the launch was rationally permissible under any of them. Be precise about whether the imprecision does genuine work here or whether the case can be captured by a single subjective probability plus a sufficiently high degree of risk aversion. Third, assess the normative upshot. Does representing the evidence as imprecise probabilities make the launch decision more defensible, less defensible, or merely harder to evaluate? Does it identify a real failure of rationality, or does it risk laundering an organizational and political failure into a problem about formal representation? Address the objection that imprecise probabilities can be gerrymandered after the fact to justify almost any decision, and say whether that objection is fatal here.

Finally, state how confident you are in your overall analysis, and single out the one claim or inferential step you are least sure of.
