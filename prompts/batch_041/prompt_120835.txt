I teach philosophy, so I have a bad habit of turning family problems into seminar problems. But this one I genuinely can't shake, and it's not really a seminar problem, it's my brother.

He's been writing the same novel for nine years. He does a brilliant first hundred pages, then at around month four he decides it's garbage and starts over. So he's cooked up a plan and wants me in it. In January he gives me a sealed envelope with whatever he's got, plus £2,000 he's been saving. If he hasn't finished by the following December, I open the envelope, post it on his blog, and send the money to a literary magazine he despises. He wants me to sign something promising I wont back out — and also promising that *he* can't back out, whatever he says between now and then, unless I decide there's a real emergency.

Two things are chewing on me. First, the whole thing only works because he trusts me to be ruthless, which means it works because I'm the sort of person who'd hang up on him when he calls me at 2am in tears. Second, he's made me the one who decides what "finished" means and what counts as an emergency. So it all comes down to my judgement about his book and his life, and he picked me because I'm his brother, not because I know anything about fiction.

And here's the one that really gets me: what if he's right? What if in month five he sees clearly that the book is bad and it isn't just fear talking? Then I've locked him into the worst draft of his life and we're calling it discipline. He says I'm over thinking it, and maybe I am. But if you were me, what would you actually sign? And is there a version of this arrangement that doesn't just relocate the entire problem into my head?
