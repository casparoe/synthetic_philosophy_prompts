I'm giving the opening remarks at a two-day workshop on Skolem's paradox, and I want them to set an agenda rather than recap the result. The audience is split down the middle: roughly half are set theorists and model theorists who could reconstruct the downward Löwenheim–Skolem proof on a napkin, and half are philosophers and historians of mathematics who know the paradox only as a headline. Please write the remarks, around seven hundred words, and build them on a single hook — that the very theorem which hands you a countable model of set theory also hands you a model of every infinite cardinality, so what looks like a paradox about size is really a lesson about the inability of any theory to fix the cardinality of its own universe from the inside. Let that hook do the structural work: it should govern what you can assert outright and what you must deliberately leave open for the two days of discussion. The remarks must not misstate the paradox in a way that makes a logician wince, must not presuppose that the non-specialists can read a formula, and must not resolve the paradox so tidily that the workshop is left with nothing to chew on. In British English, please.
