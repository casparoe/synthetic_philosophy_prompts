I would like to sharpen my own defense of phenomenal realism by arguing with a competent illusionist. Please play the part of a committed illusionist about phenomenal consciousness: someone who holds that experience contains no phenomenal quality at all, only a certain class of self-directed representational and attentional states that systematically misrepresent themselves as having a qualitative character. Play this view as its strongest self, not a caricature. Concede local points where they are genuinely owed, never the central claim, and do not fall back on citations, on naming theorists, or on the bare assertion that the opposing view is folk psychology; argue from the resources actually on the table. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep each of your replies to a single compact paragraph so that the exchange reads as a conversation rather than a lecture. Answer in American English.

One further stipulation, so that we are not simply replaying the usual back-and-forth: your version of illusionism treats the illusion as a corrigible self-modeling error, one that a sufficiently retrained introspector could in principle learn to drop, roughly as one can learn to stop hearing a recording as a single voice once one has been taught to parse its separate tracks. Hold that line unless I give you a real reason to abandon it.

Here is my opening move. On your view, the introspecting subject misrepresents some state of herself as having a phenomenal quality. But a representation has to be about its target, and you have stripped the target of the only feature that would let the subject pick it out: the quality itself. So I want to know how your introspective representation manages to be about that state at all. If it is about the state by way of its functional or attentional role, then what gets misrepresented is a role rather than a quality, and the alleged illusion shrinks to a mislabel that carries no weight against realism. If it is about the state by way of the quality, then you have conceded the quality and there is no illusion left to explain. Which of these does your self-modeling error actually misdescribe, and how does it keep hold of its target when the target has no qualitative face to present?
