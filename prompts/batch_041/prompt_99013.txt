The other is a structuralist: Versailles, the Depression, a constitution that let the president rule by decree, a conservative elite that would rather hand power to a radical right-winger than share it with the left — all that made some hard-right authoritarian regime close to inevitable, and Hitler was just the bus that happened to arrive on schedule. They keep talking past each other and I'm sick of it.

Write the joint statement they could both actually sign. I want:

- A numbered section of agreed claims, and make these non-trivial — not just "history is complicated" and not just restatements of the question. - Then a disagreements table. Each row: the question, each side's position written so that side would accept the wording as its own (no strawmen, no loaded terms), and what each side thinks the other is really committed to.
- Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would actually move at least one of them off their position. I suspect at least these, but add whatever you find: (a) whether the counterfactual should be a closest-world "what if he'd died in the early twenties" or a distribution over many nearby worlds; (b) whether his specific ideological project was idiosyncratic or just the local dialect of a broader European fascism; (c) whether the personalist structure of the party is evidence for the individualist or itself needs a structural explanation; (d) whether any of this is even testable.

Push them to use comparison cases rather than pure intuition — other interwar authoritarian regimes, movements that carried on or collapsed after their leader died or got sidelined, cases where the "structural" conditions were all present and nothing happened.
