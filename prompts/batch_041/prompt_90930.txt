At a technology-policy reading group last month, someone ended a discussion of transformative AI with the line "We've been here before." The reference was to the printing press and, when pressed, the Industrial Revolution and nuclear weapons; the implication was that each of those looked world-ending to contemporaries, that we adapted, and that the current alarm is therefore a familiar genre rather than a live problem. The phrase did a lot of work in that room and I couldn't say exactly what work. Please give me a menu of the precise claims "We've been here before" could be making in that context, as a numbered list of exactly five items, each item a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion. For each reading, state the thesis as sharply as you can, then say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible empirical or historical claim, or something that is false or question-begging as stated. Somewhere among the five, distinguish a claim about the past record from a claim about what the present case shares with the past cases, and distinguish both from the claim that our having adapted before shows we can adapt again. Be explicit about which readings, if any, actually license the dismissal that was performed in the room, and about which past case the reading would have to lean on to do so. Treat the slogans of the individual analogies -- the printing press changed everything, the bomb did not end the world, the Industrial Revolution was ultimately a net good -- as separate candidate readings only if they are doing distinct work, and do not let any item collapse into the others.
