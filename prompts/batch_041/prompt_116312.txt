I'm a freelance journalist — science and ideas, mostly long features, the occasional audio thing. Next month I'm doing a long interview with a researcher who's spent years arguing about coherence arguments for expected utility maximization, i.e. the whole move where someone says: if your preferences run in a circle (A over B, B over C, C over A), then I can sell you a series of trades that leaves you worse off whichever way things go, so your preferences are irrational, so you'd better have a utility function. Her line, roughly, is that the argument is sound but much narrower than people take it to be. What it really shows, she says, is that a single agent whose preferences stay put across time and across the way you ask the question — and who can't commit to a plan in advance — is pumpable. Most of the cases people actually care about, humans with incomplete preferences and language models whose choices flip when you reword the prompt, sit outside that, because there's no one stable agent there to pump. She also thinks the heavy lifting is done by the axioms the pumps lean on (the sure-thing/independence thing, continuity) rather than by the pumps themselves, and that when people slide from this to "so our AI should be a utility maximizer" they've quietly changed the subject from a theorem about what can be done to you into a design spec.

Can you draft eight to ten interview questions that actually dig in — no "so what is a utility function" softballs. I want at least one question each on: whether incompleteness and intransitivity really come apart for her purposes, or whether incompleteness is just intransitivity with extra steps; what's left of the argument if the agent can bind itself in advance, or simply doesn't care about being exploited later on; and whether the framing-dependent preferences you see in LLMs are the same phenomenon as incomplete preferences in people or a seperate thing entirely. For every question, give me a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. She's very good at deflecting with "that's a scope objection, not a refutation", so I'd like the follow-ups to make that move harder to use as we go. And work in one question about whether any of this is meant to be normative at all or is just a fact about what a certain kind of agent can be made to do, plus one about what she'd tell an alignment person who insists a utility function is the only way to make an agents behaviour predictable.
