I'm a third-year undergraduate in a seminar on rational choice and collective action, and our final unit was on what the assigned reading called the unilateralist's curse. The mechanism, as I have reconstructed it: a number of agents are each independently in a position to do a thing on their own; the thing is one-off, in the sense that one actor suffices for its effect, and the second and third actors add nothing and can undo nothing; and each agent's estimate of whether the thing is worth doing is honest but noisy. Each agent is right that, conditional on their own estimate clearing their threshold, acting is justified, and because each must decide on the strength of their own estimate, with no way of making their decision contingent on the others', the redundancy does not enter the deliberation. So the group's outcome is decided by the maximum of many independent draws rather than by anyone's judgment, and the probability that someone acts climbs with the number of agents even when the true value of the action is negative. The remedy our instructor floated — a principle of conformity, agents deferring to an aggregated verdict rather than to their own — strikes me as true and inert, and I don't want it in the poem.

For the creative component of my final assignment I want a sestina. Strict form: six stanzas of six lines and a three-line envoi, the standard end-word permutation, and in the envoi three of the six end-words at the ends of lines and three folded into the middles. Let the six stanzas be six agents, each in the first person, distinguishable only by what they happen to notice rather than by biography, and let each of them reach for the same six end-words. I would suggest signal, estimate, threshold, harm, unlikely, someone — or six words of your own that are equally concrete, chosen and then kept fixed. The fixed end-words are the point: six people solving the same problem in six different orders and arriving at the same place. Make the sixth stanza the moment after, still in the same six words. Their arithmetic should be ordinary and their characters sympathetic; the failure should be structural rather than characterological, and it should not depend on the setting, so keep the setting concrete but small, and please keep it well away from the techno-thriller register. Don't end on the remedy or on any policy; end where the seminar ended, with the thing done and no one having chosen it.

Please don't cite or gesture at any literature — I don't want you reaching for sources you can't check, and the poem doesn't need them. After the poem, add a short note, a few sentences, saying how confident you are that the poem renders the mechanism correctly, naming the single point you are least sure of, and flagging any liberties you took with the idea for the form's sake.
