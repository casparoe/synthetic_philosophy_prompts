ok this has been bugging me for weeks and I can't get a straight answer out of anyone. "Never again" keeps turning up in my life meaning completely different things. It's on a wall at the memorial in Berlin. My brother-in-law said it over dinner to argue we should of intervened somewhere (Syria I think, could of been Bosnia, it was late). A talk on the Armenian genocide ended with it, and then a guy in the audience shouted it about the Confederacy, which I still don't understand. And last month it was on a sign at a climate march next to a picture of a melting earth. Two words, and everyone acts like they've said something.

Can you just lay out the different precise claims "never again" could be making? I want the whole menu, not a defense of one reading. My guess at the items, add or split as you see fit:

- the plain factual reading: this won't happen again. Which is just false (Cambodia, Rwanda, Bosnia) and nobody seems embarassed by that, so what's going on there?
- the vow reading: it's a promise, not a prediction. Fine, but a promise by who, to who, and to do what exactly? Is it even the same slogan as "never forget", or is forgetting the thing it's really forbidding?
- the prevention reading, the one that gets used to justify sending troops: never again means we have to stop the next one. What does that presuppose about how these things happen and about our ability to see them coming?
- the "never again to us" vs "never again to anyone" split — a sovereign-power reading vs a universal one — and I have a feeling these two actually fight each other in practice
- the legal reading: the slogan written into statutes, memory laws, prosecuting denial
- and an inverted one, where the same words get claimed by people who say they're the ones in danger now (the covid demos in Germany with the yellow stars, that whole mess)

For each: is it trivial, is it a real claim that could be true or false, or is it not a claim at all? And which reading is actually doing the work in the four places I care about — Germany, Japan, Turkey, the American South? My rough sense is Germany turned it into rearmament and backing Israel, Japan into a clause renouncing war, Turkey into a law against insulting Turkishness, and the South into the opposite of the slogan entirely (the South will rise again). If that's roughly right, then the four aren't four versions of one memory culture at all, and the comparison everyone makes is confused. Tell me which reading each place is running on, whether it's actually available to them, and what each reading commits you to that people who repeat the line probably haven't thought about.
