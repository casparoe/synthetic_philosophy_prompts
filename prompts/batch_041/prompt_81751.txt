I have just spent most of a long dinner arguing with a friend about the Nordic model, and I would like a second opinion on whether my side of that argument is actually coherent, because I came away with the suspicion that I had been defending a package whose parts do not all fit. His line was the familiar one — Denmark, Sweden and Norway are small, unusually homogeneous, and one of them happens to be sitting on a large pile of oil, so the tax-and-transfer architecture everyone admires is a product of circumstances rather than a recipe anyone can follow — and my instinct was to resist it, but I want to know whether I can resist it without contradicting myself. Here is roughly what I believe. (1) High tax levels and high social trust are causally connected, and I take the arrow to run mainly from trust to institutions: a population that already expects compliance and reciprocity will tolerate a heavy tax burden without mass defection, whereas a population that expects evasion will read the same rates as an invitation to join in. (2) The Nordic outcomes — low inequality, high intergenerational mobility, low poverty — are produced by the institutional architecture (universal transfers, coordinated wage bargaining, publicly funded childcare, active labour market policy) rather than by culture or homogeneity, which is exactly why I resist the small-village story. (3) That architecture is exportable in principle: a country that adopted the same set would get broadly similar results, allowing for differences in size and timing. (4) I do not think culture is a deep constraint on institutions at all — institutions shape culture over a generation or two, so at the level that matters the causation runs from structure to culture. And (5) what actually sustains the whole arrangement politically is a shared sense of national membership: people pay because they think of the recipients as fellow members of a scheme they are in together, and I do not believe solidarity of that kind is simply a byproduct of the tax code.

I am not asking you to adjudicate the empirical literature or to tell me which of these is true, and I am not looking for a reading list. I want to know whether the five commitments can be held simultaneously, and I suspect at least one pair cannot. Go through the set and tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other and how severe each conflict is, which pairs only look like they conflict and why the appearance dissolves, and for each real tension what the cheapest revision would be and what that revision would cost me — what I would have to stop being able to say. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and give me a rewritten version of the set that is internally consistent. Order the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
