I'm in a small reading group on the ethics of influence — we started off with nudge-type stuff and now we're properly in the weeds — and two of us have been going at each other for about a year now. Let me call them the Mechanism camp and the Autonomy camp. The Mechanism person says the thing that makes influence manipulation is the route it takes: manipulation works by bypassing or short-circuiting someone's rational capacities, leaning on biases, emotional pressure, frames they'd disavow if they saw them, whereas persuasion gives reasons and lets the person actually weigh them. The Autonomy person says the route is a red herring; what matters is whether the influence respects the other person's standing as someone who gets to run their own life — whether they could in principle own the outcome as theirs, whether it honours the norms of the relationship, that sort of thing. On her view a lot of so-called pure reasoning is manipulative (cherry-picked but true premises, an arguement you know lands because of their insecurity) and some non-rational routes are perfectly fine (a mate hugging you into not driving home drunk). They keep talking past each other and I want to force the issue. So: please draft the joint statement the two of them could both actually sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want (1) a numbered list of the claims both camps accept — about what even counts as influence in the first place, the common ground on our cases, and anything where their disagreement turns out to be merely verbal; then (2) a disagreements table, and then (3) a short machine-readable index of the cruxes as a JSON object.

For the table please use exactly these columns: Crux, Neutral question, Mechanism camp's position in wording they'd accept, Autonomy camp's position in wording they'd accept, Case or argument both sides agree bears on the crux, Mechanism camp's prediction, Autonomy camp's prediction, Moves which side (mechanism / autonomy / both / neither). For the JSON, one entry per crux with fields: crux_id, question, genuine (true or false), decisive_case, moves (one of the four values above). A few things to seed it, since these are the cases we've been fighting over and I don't want a generic essay on manipulation. Case one: a disclosed persuasion profile — a company tells you up front exactly which levers it will pull on you (scarcity, social proof, your fear of missing out) and you opt in anyway; is disclosed manipulation still manipulation, and if the Mechanism camp says no, doesn't that show the mechanism isn't really doing the work? Case two: a tutor who, to get a student to accept a correct conclusion, mirrors the student's own pet phrases and flatters them slightly — no falsehoods, but the acceptance is engineered. Case three, the one that started all this: a phone assistant that, with full transparency, waits until your stated resolve is at its weakest — late, tired, just after a row — to ask whether you really still want to keep the commitment you made, using only true information, at a moment chosen to maximise compliance. The Autonomy person says it's fine because it's transparent and it's still you deciding; the Mechanism person says the timing is the whole trick and that's the manipulation. Also, we invented a test we've been calling the sunlight test — would the influence keep its grip if the target understood, at the very moment of influence, precisely what was being done to them and why? — and I'd like you to say whether that test survives scrutiny, because I suspect it quietly collapses into one camp's position rather than being neutral between them. And please say plainly which bits of their quarrel are just about words, and which bits are the real seperate cruxes, because I can't always tell.
