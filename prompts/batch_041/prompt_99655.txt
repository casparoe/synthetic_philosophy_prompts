A few of us have started a small reading group on psychiatry and its critics — we've been working through Thomas Szasz's argument that mental illness is a myth — and the thing that keeps tripping us up is that we end up talking past each other because we're actually answering different questions without noticing. So I want to build a proper taxonomy of the positions, as a tree where every branch is labeled with the question that seperates it from its sibling, and every leaf is a named position (or 'unnamed' if it looks like nobody actually holds it). Audience is just our reading group, so keep it self-contained: no reading list, no citations, and please don't assume we've memorized which theorist said what — describe each position by what it claims, and use a name only where the name is doing real work.

Two fault lines I want baked into the structure, because I think they're the real ones. First, keep the metaphysical question (does calling something a mental illness assert a bodily disease, or is the term a metaphor or a normative label?) seperate from the political question (is it ever okay to treat or confine someone against their will on psychiatric grounds?). Alot of people answer one of these and think they've answered the other, and the tree should force them apart — so I don't want leaves that just say 'Szaszian' or 'anti-psychiatry'; I want a combination of answers to land you somewhere specific. Second, don't let the disease question collapse into yes/no: include the option that 'mental disorder' is a practical or administrative kind (more like a legal category than a natural kind or a bare value judgment), and the option that the concept is fine in a reformist sense while its current categories are junk.

Then build the diagnostic questions out of concrete cases, each phrased so someone can answer it without already knowing which position it points to. Cases I'd like in there: grief that's lasted far longer and harder than anyone expected; a person who hears voices and is frightened by them; someone who drinks heavily but isn't distressed and functions fine; someone who's a danger to others and refuses help; a patient who asks to be admitted when the doctors think they're fine. For each case the answer options should be the genuinely different things the positions say — roughly, 'this is a disease of the brain', 'this is a problem in living, not a disease', 'this is a disease only in the sense that we've collectively decided to treat it as one', 'the label is doing social or political work here rather than describing anything'. Aim for about seven to nine leaves. At the end, flag any pairs of positions the tree can't actually seperate, and for the combinations nobody occupies, say whether you think that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
