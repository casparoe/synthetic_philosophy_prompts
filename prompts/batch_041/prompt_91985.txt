I'm a PhD student in computer science working on the statistical foundations of algorithmic fairness, and I keep encountering a pattern in how high-stakes scoring systems are actually built and audited that I want to think through philosophically rather than statistically.

The practice is this. A vendor builds a risk-scoring tool for a consequential decision — screening job applicants, or flagging defendants for a diversion program. The development pipeline does four things: it drops the protected attribute from the feature set, on the reasoning that a model that never sees race cannot discriminate by race; it optimizes predictive accuracy and then certifies that the score is calibrated within each group, so that a score of 0.3 denotes the same probability of the outcome whichever group you belong to; the buyer's compliance office separately requires evidence, at the chosen operating threshold, that the selection rate is equal across groups; and the model card reports group-wise false-positive and false-negative rates. I know the impossibility results well enough to know that these last three guarantees cannot hold together except in special cases (equal base rates, or near-perfect prediction). I am not asking how to repair the pipeline. I am asking what it presupposes.

Reconstruct the implicit theory of fairness under which this pipeline and its audit make sense: what "bias" is taken to be a property of — the data, the model, the decision policy, or the world — and whether fairness is treated as a measurable quantity or as a normative relation; what deleting the protected attribute is supposed to accomplish, and what view of causation and of proxy variables that assumes; and what it assumes about the commensurability of the several group-wise metrics it reports side by side. Then distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, identify which rival accounts of fairness it quietly rules out and which it leaves open, so that the same pipeline is licensed by several incompatible theories, and say whether its parts presuppose consistent things. Finally, state what would have to change in the pipeline and in the audit if the presupposition that fairness is a property of the model were replaced by the view that it is a property of the decision policy together with a normative account of which inequalities are unjust. No citations needed; I'll handle references myself.

After the prose analysis (roughly 1,000 words), give me a JSON object with exactly these fields: `practice_components` (array of strings, the parts of the practice as you have analyzed them); `presuppositions` (array of objects, each with `presupposition`, `status` taking one of "strict presupposition", "fits but not required", "inconsistent with another part of the practice", `supported_by` naming the component that carries it, and `rivals_excluded`, an array of strings); `internal_consistency` (object with `verdict` and `tensions`, an array of strings naming the incompatible pairs); `underdetermination` (array of objects, each with `question` and `theories_that_license_the_practice`, an array of strings); and `counterfactual` (array of objects, each with `presupposition` and `what_changes`).
