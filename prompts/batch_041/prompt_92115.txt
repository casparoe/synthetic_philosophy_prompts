My roommate and I keep arguing about AI safety and he's got this analogy he's definately attached to. He says a low-impact AI is basically a good houseguest. A good guest doesn't need to know what the homeowner actually cares about -- she just puts everything back where she found it, doesn't rearrange the furniture, doesn't hammer a nail in the wall. He says that's the whole appeal of "avoid side effects" as a safety rule: it's a value-free constraint you can bolt onto whatever goal the AI has, and the houseguest shows it's doable. When I push back he gets more formal and says impact is just how much you shrink the set of things that could still happen afterward -- if you leave open all the doors that were open, you've had zero impact, regardless of what the owner happens to value.

I think he's getting something for free but I can't put my finger on what. Can you do a proper audit? Give me the correspondence table -- house, guest, furniture, owner, "where you found it" -- then the places the mapping breaks. I'm especially curious about three things: whether "put it back where you found it" secretly needs a theory of which changes count as the same world; whether the agent just standing there counts as impact (and impact relative to what baseline anyway, since the guest's mere presence changes alot); and whether the shrink-the-reachable-set thing is really value-free or whether it's smuggling in a baseline and a similarity metric. Then tell me whether the conclusion survives -- that low impact can be specified without a full theory of value -- or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole time. And if there's a better analogy for what he actually wants, say what it is.
