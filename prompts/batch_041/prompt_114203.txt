Two camps in just war theory have argued for decades over the moral equality of combatants: the traditionalist camp, which holds that combatants on both sides of a war have the same permissions and the same liabilities under jus in bello regardless of whether their cause is just, and the revisionist camp, which holds that this symmetry is false — that what one may do, and what one is liable to, depends on whether one is fighting for a just cause. I want the document the two camps could actually co-sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, not a transcript of the argument.

Write the joint statement in this order. First, a numbered section of claims both camps accept: about the structure of the problem, the relevant concepts (permission, liability, culpability, excuse, and the difference between a combatant's being blameworthy and his being liable to be killed), what is uncontroversially at stake, and the terms of the central case below. Second, the precise statements of the remaining disagreement, each phrased in wording that the side it belongs to would accept as its own. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp. Fourth, for each crux, a case, argument, or consideration that both camps agree bears on it, together with each camp's prediction about how it would come out. Finally, say which parts of the dispute, if any, are merely verbal — disagreements about words rather than substance — and which are genuine.

Build the document around this case, which is meant to isolate the disagreement rather than to be decided by it. Two neighboring states go to war over a disputed border. Through a chain of reasonable inferences from different but equally credible evidence, each side's government and public conclude that the other is the aggressor; each is sincerely and non-culpably mistaken about the other. The ordinary combatants on both sides are conscripts, equally unable to learn the truth, fighting with the same weapons under the same in bello rules. Assume, for the purposes of the exercise, that exactly one side is in fact fighting for a just cause, but that no one on either side could reasonably be expected to know which.

Use this case to force the two camps apart where they really differ. In particular, address: whether the symmetry in this case is a symmetry of permissions, of liabilities, or merely of blame; whether an unjust combatant who is fully excused is nonetheless liable to be killed, and by what; whether the just combatant who kills him does something that requires a justification he can give; whether duress and non-culpable ignorance defeat responsibility outright or only excuse it; and whether the in bello constraints of proportionality and discrimination apply identically to both sides. Also address whether the two camps can agree that some version of the traditionalist thesis is true as a matter of the laws of armed conflict even where it is false as a matter of morality, and whether that division of labor resolves the dispute or merely relocates it. Where the traditionalist can accept the revisionist's description of the underlying metaphysics and still hold the practical conclusion, and vice versa, say so explicitly.

Write the agreed-claims section as numbered claims and the remaining disagreement as a table with columns for the crux, the traditionalist formulation, the revisionist formulation, the shared evidence or case, and each side's prediction. Do not cite literature or supply a reading list; the statement should stand on the arguments themselves. Write in American English.
