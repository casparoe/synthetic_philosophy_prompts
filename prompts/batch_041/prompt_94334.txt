I am preparing a short written statement of my views on the long-term future for a workshop, and before I circulate it I would like a consistency audit of the package below. Please take each item at face value rather than as a gesture in a direction.

(1) The far future dominates. Because the number of lives that could exist across cosmic time is astronomically large, and their value correspondingly enormous, we should be willing to spend almost any present resource for a very small increase in the probability that the far future goes well, and to accept very small probabilities of securing it. Nothing available in the present is comparable.

(2) There is a substantial probability — no less than one in three, I would say — that we are living in a computer simulation run by a more advanced civilization. I accept the familiar simulation argument, on which at least one of its three disjuncts must hold and on which the alternatives to our being simulated are not antecedently likely.

(3) If we are simulated, then what has ultimate value is what happens in the world of the simulators, not what happens inside the simulation, and our actions inside the simulation may have little or no effect on that world. The simulators may suspend or terminate the simulation at will, and it is their decisions, not ours, that fix the real far future.

(4) The possibility that we are simulated should make no difference to the expected value of work to safeguard the far future. I should reason and act exactly as though that possibility did not exist.

(5) I am not a fanatic in the pejorative sense. I do not think we should sacrifice substantial present-day goods for arbitrarily small probabilities of arbitrarily large goods, and I think ordinary morality rightly constrains what may be done for the sake of the future.

(6) The value of a possible world is the total welfare of all its inhabitants. I see no relevant difference between a mind realized in silicon and one realized in neurons, so I count the welfare of simulated inhabitants at full weight.

(7) If the simulators were preparing to switch us off, I would regard that as a loss of the same order of magnitude as the loss of the real far future.

Please audit this rather than lecture me about it. Tell me which items are in genuine tension and which only appear to be; for each genuine tension, how severe it is, what would have to give, and what that concession would cost. Do not assume I already know where the fault lines run, and do not assume that any particular item is the one I would abandon.

Begin with the single most serious tension, stated in one sentence. Then give the audit, ordered by severity. Maximum 750 words.
