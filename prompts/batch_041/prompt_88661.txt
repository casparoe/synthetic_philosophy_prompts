Ok so I need a Q&A prep sheet for a talk I'm giving in three weeks, and I want it to be honest with me rather than reassuring.

Setup: it's my local rationality meetup's monthly talk night, except this month we've merged with the philosophy-of-mind reading group from the university, so the room is roughly half Sequences-and-blogs regulars (a few ML people, a couple of old-school reductionists who think "emergence" is a word people use alot when they don't have a mechanism) and half grad students who are used to arguing in a much more technical register. I'm the speaker, so what I want is the questions they'll actually ask, in the words they'd actually use, not a defense of my position.

Topic is the careenium: Hofstadter's thing with the frictionless table full of tiny magnetic marbles careening around and clumping into bigger structures, which he uses to ask who pushes who around in there -- the marbles, or the symbols the clumps implement. The version I keep seeing passed around the blogs takes the reductionist line: the symbol-level story is a convenient way of describing the marble-level story and nothing more, the marbles do all the pushing. My thesis is the opposite. I want to say that when the careenium outputs "cat", the answer to "what made it do that" is genuinely "the cat-symbol", and that the marble-level description is not a more fundamental competitor -- the symbol-level causal claim is a real causal claim, its not a summary we find convenient. I am not claiming there's an extra force or a ghost in the table, and I'll say that up front so I don't burn the whole Q&A on it.

I've also got a move I'm not sure about and I want you to tell me if it's any good before I put it in the talk: suppose the same symbol-level story could be implemented on a totally different substrate -- not marbles at all, but a continuous fluid with no discrete parts, tuned so the same clumps and the same dances happen. If the symbol-level causal claims come out identical in both versions, does that support my line or does it just prove that the symbol talk is substrate-neutral description, i.e. exactly the reductionist's point? Be blunt, I can take it.

Deliverable: the eight or so objections I'll actually get, ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they'd do, phrased the way each half of the room would phrase them (they'll sometimes be the same objection in two vocabularies, and I want both), a reply I can give in two minutes each, and a note on where the reply is weakest or where I'm bluffing. Include the one question I'm hoping nobody asks.

And please, this is the thing that drives me up the wall with answers to this kind of question: tell me what's actually settled among people who work on causation and levels of description versus what's a live fight, and don't present a contested position as if it's consensus. If my framing of the careenium is idiosyncratic, or if I'm attributing to "the usual reading" a view that isn't actually usual, say so. No citations or reading lists, I can't look anything up from the podium anyway and I'd rather you not guess at references.
