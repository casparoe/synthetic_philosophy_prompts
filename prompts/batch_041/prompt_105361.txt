My father died in March, and as executor I have spent the past two months trying to work out what my siblings and I will owe in federal estate tax on his estate, which is large but consists almost entirely of shares in the company he founded and never sold. My accountant's explanation of the mechanics has left me more confused than I was before. The estate, he tells me, will be valued as of the date of death and taxed on that value, and we will take the shares with a basis equal to that same date-of-death value. As I understand it, this means that the appreciation my father accumulated over forty years is taxed once at the estate level and then taxed a second time, as capital gains, on the same dollars when we sell — which is exactly the double taxation that opponents of inheritance taxes invoke. I had assumed that a tax of this kind was defensible, if at all, as a way of keeping dynastic fortunes from hardening into permanent advantage, but if the same money is taxed twice, then it seems to me the tax simply burdens a family without doing anything for equal opportunity. I would be grateful if you would tell me whether the mechanics are as I have described them, and whether, if they are, the objection to the tax is as strong as it sounds.
