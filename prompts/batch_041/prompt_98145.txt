Ok so I'm putting together a session on the combination problem for panpsychism and I want your actual take, not a survey of who said what. Here's what keeps bugging me: everyone says "the combination problem" like it's one problem, but I think it's at least three, and the proposed fixes are good at different ones. So what I want from you is a teir list.

The candidate fixes I want ranked (my labels — rename them if mine are bad):

1. Phenomenal bonding: treat the relation that fuses micro-experiences into a macro-subject as a primitive, not something that admits further explanation.
2. Cosmopsychism: start from one cosmic subject and explain individual minds by decomposition/fragmentation rather than by building up.
3. Panqualityism / subjectless qualities: deny that the base level has subjects at all; what's fundamental is qualities, and subjects are a later construction.
4. Russellian monism with intrinsic natures: the intrinsic properties posited to underlie physics are experiential, and combining them is just ordinary physical composition.
5. Brute emergence: macro-experience emerges from micro-experience and that's the end of the story.
6. Illusionism about phenomenal unity: the apparent unity is a representational artifact, so there's nothing to combine in the first place.
7. No-owner micro-experience: experiences at the base level that belong to no subject, with subjects appearing only at the macro level.
8. Structural/gestalt combination: the macro-experience is a genuinely new quality, not a sum of the micro ones, but lawfully produced by them.
9. Deflationism: deny that combination is special to consciousness — treat it like any other case of a macro-property arising from micro-properties, no extra mystery.

Now rank them three times, because I don't think one ordering does the job:

(a) by how well each handles the subject-summing worry — why would many subjects yield exactly one subject, rather than none or many?
(b) by how well each handles the quality/grain worry — why is the macro-experience a smooth single quality when the micro-experiences are heterogeneous?
(c) overall — which one you'd actually bet on as the most promising research program.

For each fix, a sentence or two: where it lands in each ranking and why. Then be explicit about which of these moves are pretty much settled to be dead ends versus which are genuinely live and contested — I don't want you telling me something is the consensus when it's actually one camp shouting loudest. Flag any fix that only looks good because it's quietly changed the question (e.g. by making combination a priori intelligable, or by denying there's anything to explain). Also, where the micro-level is assumed to contain subjects at all, say so, since a couple of these moves only make sense if it doesn't.

End with a single line that starts 'Verdict:' and then just the label of the one you'd bet on (like 'Verdict: 2' or 'Verdict: phenomenal bonding').
