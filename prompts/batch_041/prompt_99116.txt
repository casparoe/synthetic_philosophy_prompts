Im putting together a 'which theory of confirmation do you actually believe?' decision tree for a small weird-epistemology reading group, and I want it built off Hempel's raven thing -- you know, 'all ravens are black' vs 'all non-black things are non-ravens' and the green apple. But I dont want the usual encyclopedia version where it's just Hempel vs 'Bayesians say it's a tiny bit of evidence' vs Popper and done. Build it from the logical space first, then drop the positions into it.

The starting commitment is just: 'a green apple does / does not tell me anything about whether all ravens are black.' From there every branch node should be a question you can answer without already knowing which camp it puts you in, and each one should split the space on a real disagreement, not on a label. The distinctions I care about splitting on: whether confirmation is an instance-counting relation or a relevance/evidence relation; whether the amount of confirmation from a green apple is zero, tiny-but-real, or actually substantial once you get the background right; whether 'all ravens are black' is even the right form of the hypothesis (should it be read as a claim about ravens specifically, or about every object in the universe?); and what role sampling and selection play -- does it matter whether you went looking at apples versus just happening to notice one?

Cases I want in there, because these are the ones my group keeps fighting about: the plain green apple you notice; a white shoe (also a non-black non-raven, but an artifact -- does the kind of thing matter?); a black shoe as a control, since that's a non-raven that isnt an instance of either the hypothesis or its contrapositive and every view should call it irrelevant -- it'll catch answers that overconfirm; a green apple you deliberately check *because* it's a non-raven; a case where you lived among ravens and never once saw a non-black one (absence of a counterexample where you'd have seen it); a generalization with a stipulated small domain, like 'all the ravens in this aviary are black,' where you can just count them; and a case where the observation is itself manufactured, like catching a raven and painting it black before you look at it. Maybe one with a finite versus an open-ended population too.

Leaves should be the positions -- instance-confirmation (Hempel-style), the 'it's real but vanishingly small' line, the 'zero, because it says nothing about the raven-producing process' line, the severe-testing/falsification line, the 'the hypothesis should be restricted to ravens, so non-ravens are simply irrelevant' line, and the 'the whole puzzle comes from reading a normal-conditions claim as a strict universal' line. Name them, or mark 'unnamed' if nobody really holds it. I'd rather the tree come out around 8-10 nodes with 7 or 8 leaves.

And if any two leaves end up being sorted by the same answers, tell me instead of pretending the tree separates them.
