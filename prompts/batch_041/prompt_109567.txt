ok here's something I keep flip-flopping on and I want an actual number, not a hedge. Imagine the universe is enormous -- maybe infinite -- and there are lots of other agents out there running decision procedures more or less like mine. Now imagine I'm deciding whether to pay some real personal cost to help agents I will never meet and can never causally affect: they're so far away that nothing I do can reach them, ever, not even at light speed. The boring causal reasoning says obviously don't bother, zero effect on them. But the evidential reasoning says my choice is evidence about what other agents who share my decision procedure will choose, and if we're correlated like that then my choosing to help is evidence they'll help too, and that correlation might make the cooperation worth it even with no causal link at all.

Give me your credence that, in a universe like that, it's rationally required (or at least clearly the thing to do) to pay a real cost to help those unreachable agents on purely evidential grounds. My gut says like 0.3 but I don't trust it. And I'm definately not looking for 'it depends on your decision theory' -- I want a number plus the decomposition: how much rides on the universe actually being large enough and containing enough agents correlated with me, how much on whether to think about it evidentially (my choice as evidence about others' choices) versus functionally (it's about what my decision algorithm outputs, wherever it happens to be instantiated), how much on whether the correlation actually buys you anything once you account for all the other agents out there who might be doing something else entirely, and how much on whether 'rationally required' even has a determinate answer here. Rough probability for each, how they combine, then tell me which sub-question the final number is most sensitive to and what would actually move it.
