My friend and I have been arguing for years about what it actually means to follow a rule, and I want to try to pin down what she's really committed to. She's not academic and neither am I, but she thinks about this alot and she's consistant about it. Here's where she's landed on the cases we've made up over the years.

Someone who has spent his whole life adding numbers, and has never once seen a sum with a number bigger than 50 in it, gets asked what 68 + 57 is and says 5. She says he means plus and he's made a mistake. I pointed out that every sum he has ever done in his life is also consistent with some other rule that gives 5 there, and she says that doesn't matter, he means plus.

A parrot that says the word red whenever it sees something red is not following a rule, according to her. Nor is the vine on her fence that always winds the same way round the pole.

Her grandad and his brother made up their own scoring system for a card game when they were kids. Nobody else on earth has ever used it and they kept it up for fifty years. She says they were following a rule, their own one, not just doing whatever came into their heads.

Her cousin plays our board game by a version of the rules he picked up when he was eight and never changed. We've all stopped correcting him. She says he isn't making a mistake exactly, but she won't say he's following a rule either — he can't say why he does it that way, arguing with him doesn't move him, he just does it that way.

A guy we used to work with did the right thing only when the boss was around, and something completely different when he wasn't. Not following the rule, she says. He's following the boss.

Now the new one. Her brother-in-law works at a company where a chatbot decides refund requests. Nobody wrote out its rules; it was trained on a year of the company's own past decisions, and now it just decides on its own and it's the only thing doing that job. When someone pushes back it explains why it decided the way it did, and sometimes it changes its mind and sometimes it holds firm. The case I want to ask her about is a request that looks nothing like anything in that year — say a guy who wants to return one half of a set, thirty one days after buying it, because his house burnt down. The bot answers.

What is she going to say about the bot — that it's following the rule, that it's only doing something that happens to match the rule, or that there's no fact of the matter about what rule it's following? And for that weird request that's unlike anything it's seen, is there a right answer for it or not? I want to know what rule she's actually running, what other rules fit everything she's said just as well, what each of those predicts about the bot, and which case would actually tell them apart. Tell me which way you'd bet and what would change your mind — where is your reasoning most likely to be wrong.
