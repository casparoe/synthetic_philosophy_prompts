I spent about thirty years in a biology department before I retired, and since then I've been trying to teach myself philosophy, mostly by reading and by arguing with anyone who'll listen. The argument I keep having is with an old colleague of mine, Diane, who has very firm opinions about what is and isn't alive, and I've started to suspect her opinions don't all come from one single rule. Let me just lay out her rulings and see if you can tell me what she's actually committed to.

Diane says: an oak, a horse, a mule (the sterile kind), a brewer's yeast, a mushroom — all alive, obviously. A flu virus, a bacteriophage, a prion — not alive, "just borrowed machinery." A mitochondrion on its own in a dish — not alive. A strand of RNA in a test tube that copies itself — not alive, she says, even though it's plainly making more of itself. Fire, a growing salt crystal — not alive. And the giant viruses, the ones with genomes bigger than some bacteria, make her uncomfortable, but she still files them under not-alive.

Now a grad student in my old department has built something and we're arguing about whether Diane would call it alive. It's a little lipid vesicle, a bubble, and inside it a set of reactions keeps itself going: it pulls in simple food, builds its own parts, the bubble grows and splits in two, and the daughters keep right on doing it. There's no DNA, no RNA, nothing you'd call a gene, and no inheritance of any kind — it's a self-maintaining chemical cycle that keeps a boundary going and copies that boundary. Diane hasn't seen it yet.

So what is she going to say, and more to the point, what rule has she been running all along? My hunch is that more than one rule fits everything she's said so far, and that they seperate on this thing. If you had to guess, which one does she actually believe, and why that one over the others? And what's the case that would finally force her to show her hand? She keeps telling me this is what a Europa or Mars mission would be up against, and I'd like to know what a person who designs those instruments is really committed to, whether they know it or not.
