Ok so I did 35 years of controlled experiments — NMR, mostly — and now I'm retired and have been reading philosophy, and these "Frankfurt cases" have got me hooked. You know the shape of them: somebody's wired up so that if he were about to decide otherwise, a neurosurgeon (or a demon, or whoever) would step in and make him do X; but as it happens the guy decides X on his own and the machinery never fires. Then everyone argues about whether he's responsible.

Write me a parody product listing for a device that does this — an Amazon-style page for a consumer gadget that promises to guarantee your future decisions while never actually doing anything. Include a product name and tagline, a spec sheet, a few bullet points, a "customers also bought" line, a customer Q&A, and three or four reviews. Give it a base model that only ever monitors, plus a deluxe tier with a "flicker of freedom" add-on that inserts a genuinely available but completely idle alternative into your deliberation, and a premium subscription whose fine print quietly takes a side on whether any of this matters for blame.

The jokes have to come from getting the arguements right, not from generic nerd humor — the "the machine never fired, so it's irrelevant" objection; the worry about whether an idle device can even be said to have done something; the difference between could-have-done-otherwise and would-have-done-otherwise; the suspicion that the whole setup begs the question by describing the agent's deliberation from the inside; the defender of the principle who insists the case sneaks in a flicker anyway. It should be funny to someone who's read the literature and still make sense to someone who hasn't. 600-700 words, deadpan throughout.
