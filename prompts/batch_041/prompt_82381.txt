I'm a science journalist working on a long feature about how medicine decides what counts as a disease, and I keep hitting a wall I can't get a straight answer on. Everyone I call gives me the naturalist line — a disease is a statistically subnormal functional capacity relative to some reference class of the species — and then when I press on which reference class, the whole thing seems to dissolve. Here's the thing that started it. I spent a few weeks in an old coal town where basically every man past fifty has the same chronic cough and the same reduced lung capacity, clearly from decades of dust; nobody there thinks of themselves as sick, it's just what a body does in that town, but by any species-wide yardstick they're all diseased. Flip it around: I'm told (maybe wrongly) that average lung function among kids in places that cleaned up their air has drifted up over the decades, which would mean an identical fifty-year-old with identical lungs is less healthy now than he'd have been in 1965. Nothing about him changed. So what I want is a careful explanation of the actual conceptual issue here, not a survey of the literature: what exactly is a naturalist committing to when they name a reference class, is there any principled way to fix it (age and sex everyone grants, but environment, cohort, occupation, ancestry?), and does the fact that the class keeps getting redrawn — by clean-air law, by a guideline committee nudging a blood-pressure cutoff down a notch and turning millions of people into patients overnight, by a new drug making some previously untreatable state a treatable one — push you toward the normativist side, or can a naturalist absorb it? And please be straight about whether the normativist gets out any cleaner: if disease is whatever we decide is bad enough to warrant treatment, what do they say about a town that doesn't experience itself as ill while the exposure is definately doing real damage?

Write it as a proper explainer for a smart general reader, 800 words or so, gloss any jargon, and if I've mislabeled what the naturalist is actually committed to, say so at the top instead of playing along. Then close with a short structured summary I can paste into my notes: a JSON object with keys positions, hard_cases, and open_question — positions as an array of the two or three live positions you've distinguished, each with name and core_claim (one sentence); hard_cases as an array of the cases we discussed, each with case, naturalist_verdict, normativist_verdict, and what_it_pressures (which part of which view it stresses); and open_question as the single question doing the most work, the one I should put to my next source. Keep the JSON valid and dont put anything else after it.
