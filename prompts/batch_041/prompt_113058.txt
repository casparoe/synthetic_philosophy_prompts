I'm a backend engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and over a long weekend at a rented cabin I ended up as the reluctant referee in an argument I could not follow. It started during a nature documentary: a shorebird was shown dragging one wing as if it were broken, drawing a fox away from its nest, and the narrator said the bird was luring the predator away from its chicks. One friend, a field biologist, said the narrator's language was harmless shorthand but strictly false, since the bird has no plan, no representation of its chicks' danger, and no intention whatever; the only true description, he said, is that birds whose nervous systems produced this display left more surviving offspring, and the display persists for that reason. My other friend, who did a philosophy degree before moving into data science, said he was conflating the question of whose reason it is with the question of whether there is a reason there at all: the display is organized around an end — it works only by producing a specific effect, and it is present because it produced that effect — and that, she said, is a perfectly good reason, one that simply floats free of any reasoner. She called it a reason with nobody home. He answered that "floats free" was doing no work, since on that standard we could equally say a river wants to reach the sea or a virus tries to spread, and the vocabulary would have stopped distinguishing explanation from decoration. She replied that the difference is selection: the river's meander is not there because it produced the seaward result, whereas the display is there because it saved chicks, and that difference is what a reason is, minus the ghost. He came back with two points — that this only restates the natural history you already had to know, so the teleological vocabulary adds nothing you did not put in, and that citing a later effect as what produced a trait reverses the order of cause and effect, since the survival came after the display rather than before it. She said the claim was never that survival reached back in time; it concerns the statistical structure of the ancestral population, and the description earns its keep because it is not a summary but a generator — if the display really is organized around saving the chicks, that tells you what the bird will do when the fox is nearer the nest, or when the chicks have already fled, which the bare selection history does not hand you. They went around this several times, and the rest of us gave up.

Please adjudicate their exchange move by move, rating each of the following from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justifying each rating: (1) his claim that the narrator's description is strictly false because the bird has no intention; (2) her reply that he has confused whose reason it is with whether a reason is present; (3) his objection that the notion of a reason without a reasoner licenses saying that a river wants to reach the sea or that a virus tries to spread, and so stops distinguishing explanation from decoration; (4) her answer that the difference is selection — the meander is not there because it produced the seaward result, whereas the display is there because it saved chicks; (5) his rejoinder that this merely restates the natural history you already had to know, and that citing a later effect as what produced a trait reverses cause and effect; and (6) her response that the description is a generator rather than a summary, since it yields predictions about what the bird will do when circumstances change. Then tell me which of them has the better overall position and why, and — since I suspect this is a live dispute rather than a settled one — what both of them are leaving out, including any distinction that would let one of them win a point the other never addressed. I would also like to know which parts of this are matters of fact, which are matters of definition, and which are matters of taste, because from where I sat all three were talking past one another. For what it is worth, in my own work we say things like "the scheduler wants to batch these writes" and "the cache expects hot keys," and I have never taken that as more than shorthand; I mention it only because it may be why I could not follow the disagreement. Please write in American English and define any term of art you need, since I do not have the vocabulary.
