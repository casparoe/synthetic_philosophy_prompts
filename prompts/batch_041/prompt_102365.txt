In a seminar on the Historikerstreit, one participant defends a line I associate with that debate: that the Nazi extermination of the Jews is best understood as a reaction to the Bolshevik terror that came first — the later deed answering an earlier one — and concludes from this that the Holocaust is therefore comparable to other twentieth-century mass crimes and so not singular in kind. I want an audit of that analogy. Set out the correspondence explicitly: what plays the role of the original and the copy, the cause and the reaction, the agents and their perceptions, the deeds and their victims. Then say where the mapping breaks, and separate the disanalogies that actually bear on the use he makes of it from those that don't. Name what the picture quietly imports — about how explanation bears on responsibility, and about what is supposed to follow from what — and keep the genetic question (was this deed a reaction to an earlier one?) apart from the typological one (is this deed of a kind with others?), saying which, if either, the analogy can carry. Work from the analogy as given; no citations or reading lists. End with a single line beginning "Verdict:" stating in one sentence whether the analogy can bear the conclusion he draws from it.
