Write a short story built around a message that must be read at three levels. Assume the view that any message carries a frame message (the part that announces that it is a message, and that you are the kind of thing meant to decode it), an outer message (the instructions for how to decode it, which are almost never written down and are instead supplied by the receiver's prior knowledge), and an inner message (the content proper). The story should put pressure on the idea that the meaning is located in the message itself.

A few constraints. Somewhere in the story, two readers must extract different inner messages from the very same marks because they bring different outer messages to them. Elsewhere, someone must receive something they cannot decode at all and yet learn something that matters simply from the fact that it was addressed to them, with the frame doing the work the content cannot. Let the regress show itself once: whatever tells you how to read the outer message has to come from somewhere that is not itself a message.

Do not resolve the story by declaring the meaning to be whatever the reader decides; the point I am after is that meaning lives in the fit among marks, code, and decoder, and that this fit can fail in interesting ways, so that a reader can get the inner message exactly right and still be badly wrong about what happened. Give the readers a real, workable code rather than a mystical intuition, and pick a setting other than the obvious one of a signal from space or a cryptic dying message — a recipe card, a lease, a musical score, a set of exam instructions, a tattoo, a road sign. Roughly 1,200 words.
