Thirty years in a physiology lab taught me that "the same mechanism" is a phrase we use constantly and define never; now that I have retired into philosophy of science, I would like to see someone make a story out of that. Write a short story set in a lab that screens samples for anomalies by a mechanistic test rather than a black-box one: a sample is flagged not because its output deviates, but because the causal pathway producing that output is not the pathway that normally produces it. The plot should turn on a case the test cannot adjudicate, in which two instances are the same mechanism under one defensible individuation and different mechanisms under another, whether because the parts match while the organization differs or because the input-output profile matches while the parts differ. Let the disagreement carry weight, a diagnosis, a career, a decision to shut the program down, and let the story dramatize the choice among individuation criteria rather than settling it by fiat. I care less about the technology than about what each character's answer to "is it the same mechanism?" commits them to.
