I'm on the board of a small meditation group — we call ourselves a Buddhist center but honestly half of us are just stressed-out parents who like the sitting. We're a registered nonprofit and our best program teaches meditation and basic coping skills to people in recovery from addiction, for free. It genuinely works and it's the thing I'm proudest of. Problem is we're broke, we've been running it on fumes and volunteer hours for two years.

A local casino company just offered us $40k a year for three years to keep it going. They want thier logo on our flyers and a non-voting seat on our board. Two of our members say take it, full stop: more people get helped, money is fungible, and refusing is basically letting people relapse out of pride. Our teacher (a Theravada guy, ordanied, the real deal) says absolutely not — the money comes out of other people's addiction, and taking it makes us part of the harm, karma and all. I see both sides and I keep flip-flopping, which is why we haven't voted in three weeks.

Please write the memo I'll hand out at Thursday's meeting so we can finally decide. I want a clear recommendation — take it, don't, or take it with conditions — with the reasoning spelled out, and I want you to be genuinely fair to the strongest point on whichever side you come down against. This is going to people who will argue, so it needs to hold up.

A few things I want you to actually wrestle with instead of waving at:

- Our founding charter says we don't accept money that comes at the cost of another's suffering. Is that the whole answer, or does it need interpreting? Because part of me thinks the harm is happening regardless of whether we take the check — the gambling is going on tonight either way. So what would it even mean for the money to be "ours"?

- Our teacher says Buddhism is about the state of mind you act from, not the results — that what counts is the intention, and ours would be good. But another member insists the Buddha cared a lot about outcomes and that "just have good intentions" is a cop-out. I genuinely can't tell which one of them is describing our own tradition correctly, and I'd like you to sort that out, because it seems like it decides the whole thing.

- The flip side of the teacher's view: if refusing is about keeping our own hands clean, is that even a Buddhist idea, or is it something we imported from somewhere else? Is refusing really the compassionate choice, or is it us protecting our own conscience while the people we could have helped go without? That one bothers me.

- The teacher also mentioned we could "dedicate the merit" of the donation, whatever that means. Does that actually change anything about whether we should take it, or is it a fig leaf?

And whatever you recommend, tell us a time frame and who has to do what — do we need to answer the casino by a certain date, do we attach conditions, who drafts the reply.

At the very end, on its own line, put the bottom line in this exact form so I can read it out at the vote: Verdict: ACCEPT, Verdict: DECLINE, or Verdict: ACCEPT WITH CONDITIONS — just those words.
