For two years I have coordinated a neighborhood child-care exchange: families earn credits by sitting for one another's children and spend them when they need a sitter, with a single shared sheet recording every balance. Since the spring, spending has all but stopped. Every family I ask says it wants the exchange to survive and would use it if it believed the others would, yet each is hoarding its credits against a future emergency, and a few have told me privately that they no longer trust the sheet to be honored. Nobody sits, so nobody earns, and the exchange is dying of disuse rather than of overuse. My neighbor argues that the only decent move is for me to keep sitting without claiming credits until confidence returns; my partner says that is a sucker's play and that I should spend down my balance now, while it still buys anything. I hold the largest balance in the group, which I know colors my judgment, and I have been tempted to unilaterally credit every family with a few extra hours to get things moving again. What is actually broken here, what should I do, and what am I likely to get wrong if I follow my first instinct?
