I've got an argument I half-built for myself and now can't get out of, and I need someone to tell me which premise to give up. Context: I'm basically a deontologist about killing. I don't think I get to kill one innocent to save five, ever. But I've never been able to say why in a way that doesn't sound like I'm just intoning the words "side constraint" and hoping nobody asks a follow-up.

The argument:

(1) A rule that's supposed to protect people from some harm is doing its job to the extent that it actually protects them from that harm. If a rule lets more of that harm through than the alternative does, the rule is worse at its job than the alternative.
(2) Take the constraint "don't kill the innocent, even to prevent other killings." Someone else is about to kill five innocents, and the only way to stop him is for me to kill one innocent. The constraint says I may not kill the one, so five die. The alternative — kill when killing minimizes killing — gets one dead instead of five.
(3) So the constraint lets more innocents die than the alternative does.
(4) So the constraint is worse at protecting innocents than the alternative.
(5) So the constraint fails at the very thing constraints are for.
(6) So either rights aren't for the sake of the people who hold them, which is nuts, or I should drop the constraint and just minimize killings.
(7) But if I minimize killings then I'm carving up healthy patients for their organs whenever the arithmetic says so, which is exactly the thing I became a deontologist to rule out.
(8) So the constraint can't be justified by what it's for, and giving it up costs me the thing it was for. The whole position is unstable.

This seems to proove too much — the same shape of argument would take out any constraint at all, and probably any agent-relative reason, including the ones I'd defend with my last breath. I don't want the first disjunct of (6), and I don't want (8). I'd give up almost anything else, and (2) seems to me to be just... true.

But every time I go looking for the standard reply I get the phrase "agent-relative" and then nothing, and I honestly can't tell if that's a diagnosis or just a label that makes me stop asking. So: where does this actually go wrong, why did that step look fine when I wrote it, and what do I owe if I patch it there rather than somewhere else? And if the patch is "the constraint isn't in the business of minimizing anything," tell me what it is in the business of, because "it's not a policy, it's a constraint" has never felt like an answer to me. I've got alot riding on this one.
