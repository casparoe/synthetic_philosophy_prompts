Ok, I'm supposed to write a position paper for a seminar and I want a stress test first, because I have a feeling I'm quietly helping myself to something and I can't see where. Here's where I've landed on panpsychism:

(1) Experience goes all the way down — it isn't built out of anything non-experiential, it's just there at the bottom.
(2) But there are no tiny subjects down there. Micro-level experience is real, it just isn't had by anyone. The lights are on but nobody's home, and at that level there's no home to speak of.
(3) So I think the combination problem is real, but not in the usual summing-up-subjects form. If there were never any micro-subjects, the question isn't how they add up, it's how a subject shows up at all.
(4) My answer: a subject just is a bundle of experiences that represents itself as one thing. Subjecthood is a matter of a self-model, and it comes in degrees.
(5) I'm a physicalist and I don't want spooky causal powers. I think the experiential is the intrinsic nature of the physical rather than a second thing running alongside it.
(6) Still, I think there's a fact of the matter wether a given system is a subject, and it isn't up to us or to our interests. Thermostat: maybe a flicker, but not a subject. Dog: subject. Corporation: no.

Where am I cheating? Rank the tensions by how bad they are, tell me which ones are real and which only look like it, and for the real ones tell me what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me.
