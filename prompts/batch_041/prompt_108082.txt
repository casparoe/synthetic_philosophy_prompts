My reading group is doing Newcomb's problem this week -- the setup with the predictor who's basically never wrong, the thousand in the clear box, and the million in the opaque one exactly when she expected you to take only that one. Would he take one box or two, and would the causal-versus-evidential split the whole debate turns on even be available to him -- once causation is just constant conjunction plus a habit of the mind, is there any Humean reason to prefer the act that brings about the good outcome over the act that merely predicts it? Work from his account of causation and his rules for judging causes and effects, flag where his system pulls the other way, and tell me whether he'd think the puzzle is even well posed. Lead with your verdict, then the reasoning, then the countercurrents. 350 words max.
