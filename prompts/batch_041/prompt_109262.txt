I am assembling a specification for a theory of rational betting under repeated multiplicative risk, and I want the criteria before any verdict on the contenders. Set out what such a theory must deliver in order to count as adequate: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must preserve, and the results it must not contradict, with the case or consideration behind each requirement and the failure mode it rules out. State each requirement as a test the theory must pass. Then separate those that fix the subject matter -- without them it is no longer a theory of the same thing -- from those that merely constrain outputs; mark each non-negotiable or camp-specific; and identify which are load-bearing, in the sense that dropping them changes which strategies come out admissible.

The list must cover at least the following, none of which I regard as settled:

- the relation between maximizing the growth rate of wealth and maximizing expected wealth, and whether the theory is required to deliver one, the other, or an explanation of why they coincide;
- what the theory says when the relevant probabilities are estimated rather than known, and whether robustness to that estimation is a requirement of the theory or an add-on to it;
- whether the criterion is a special case of expected utility maximization or a rival to it, and if the former, whether the choice of the logarithmic case is thereby arbitrary;
- ruin and near-ruin: whether never risking total loss follows from the theory or must be imposed from outside it;
- the one-shot and finite-horizon cases, where the long-run framing does not obviously apply.

I also want the specification to rule on three cases I keep returning to, since I suspect they are where the requirements begin to conflict:

(a) an agent whose holdings are not fungible and do not simply add across periods -- hours of attention, standing within a community -- so that gains and losses differ in kind and not only in size;
(b) an agent who cannot choose the stake and can only accept or decline a bet that someone else has sized;
(c) an agent whose alternative to betting is not a fixed reserve but an asset that decays, so that the outside option carries a drift of its own.

If some pair of your requirements cannot be satisfied together, name the pair, explain why, and say which you would surrender; that is the part I most want. I do not need citations or a reading list -- argue from the cases. End with a final line of the form \answer{...} whose braces contain, in order, yes or no on whether the full set is jointly satisfiable, and a short name for the single requirement that does the most to exclude candidate theories.
