Our data ethics review board has a new protocol before it. Its precedents are these: it approved a study using aggregated, anonymized ride-hailing data to map transit gaps; it rejected a study that scraped public tweets to infer users' political affiliations; it approved with conditions a study using de-identified electronic health records to train a sepsis model, provided clinicians reviewed any individual-level output; it rejected a study that used a public DNA ancestry database to identify adoptees' biological parents without their consent; and it approved a study using aggregated smart-meter data at the neighborhood level to study energy poverty. The new proposal uses high-resolution smart-meter data from a utility, under customers' broad signup consent to energy-related research, to infer which households have someone home during working hours, whether they are likely unemployed, and whether they use a home medical device. The data are pseudonymized, not shared, and no decisions are made about individuals; the aim is to study energy poverty and target assistance. Predict our decision if we are to be consistent with our precedents, identify the closest precedent, state the competing rules our record could be running and what each predicts for this case, give your best guess with reasons, and name a further case that would best distinguish those rules; if the precedents genuinely underdetermine the outcome, say what principle we would be adopting either way.
