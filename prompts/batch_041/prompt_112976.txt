A friend of mine who reads a great deal of popular complexity writing has settled on an analogy for historical contingency: history, he says, is not like the weather but like a sandpile. Grains are dropped one at a time; most land harmlessly, but sooner or later one grain triggers an avalanche that reshapes the whole slope, and the next large avalanche may be set off by a trivial grain or a substantial one indifferently, because the pile has driven itself into a critical state in which avalanches of every size are possible. From this he draws three conclusions: that the for-want-of-a-nail saying is literally true but misleading, since the nail is not a special kind of cause, only the grain that happened to fall at the critical moment; that singling out one decision or accident as the cause of a large historical outcome is therefore arbitrary; and that structural and accidental explanations of the same event are not rivals but two descriptions of a single process. He also insists that the sandpile differs in kind from the butterfly-effect weather analogy, and that this difference is what makes his case.

Please audit the analogy. Say what corresponds to what, where the mapping holds and where it breaks, which features of the sandpile he is quietly importing into history that history does not have, which disanalogies are fatal to his conclusions and which are irrelevant to them, whether he is right that the sandpile differs in kind from the weather analogy in a way that matters here, and what analogy would serve him better if the sandpile does not.

Return a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "mapping": array of objects, each with "sandpile_feature" (string), "historical_counterpart" (string), and "assessment" (one of "holds", "partly holds", "breaks").
- "decisive_disanalogies": array of objects, each with "disanalogy" (string) and "why_it_matters" (string, naming which of his three conclusions it damages).
- "harmless_disanalogies": array of objects, each with "disanalogy" (string) and "why_it_does_not_matter" (string).
- "smuggled_assumptions": array of strings, each stating an assumption the sandpile picture carries into history without warrant.
- "weather_versus_sandpile": object with "difference_in_kind_is_real" (boolean) and "consequence_for_his_argument" (string).
- "conclusions": array of three objects, one per conclusion above, each with "claim" (string, restated in your own words), "verdict" (one of "survives", "survives_weakened", "fails"), and "reason" (string).
- "better_analogy": object with "analogy" (string) and "what_it_gets_right" (string).
