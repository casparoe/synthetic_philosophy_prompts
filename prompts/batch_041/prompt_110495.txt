When two options are incommensurable, reason simply runs out: there is no fact of the matter about which is better, so the choice comes down to what you plump for. This is why hard choices hurt, and why we spend the rest of our lives rationalizing them. The lesson is that practical reason is not one faculty but a bundle of incompatible standards, so any theory that promises a single answer — utilitarianism above all — has to be false. Incommensurability is also what makes hard choices good for us: precisely because nothing tells us what to do, we get to define ourselves in choosing, and a life with no such choices would be a life without agency."
