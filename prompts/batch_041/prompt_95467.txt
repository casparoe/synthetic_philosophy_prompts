A friend in my phil of mind reading group (Dev) has been pushing this arguement he calls the consumer's revenge, and I can't decide if it wrecks teleosemantics or wrecks itself. We're working with the consumer-based version of teleosemantics, the one where a state's content isn't fixed by what the producer was selected to indicate but by what the downstream consumer mechanism needs to be true for the whole setup to do its proper job. Ok.

(1) A state has representational content when it has the proper function of being tokened in some condition, and proper functions are fixed by selection history or by design -- not by anyone's intentions or interpretations.
(2) In consumer semantics, the content of a state is whatever the consumer needs to be the case for the consumer to perform its own proper function.
(3) Take a single internal signal that feeds two downstream mechanisms with different proper functions. Say a visual edge signal: one gadget uses it to decide whether to grab, another uses it to decide whether to swerve. Grabbing needs there to be an edge within reach; swerving needs there to be an edge ahead.
(4) By (1) and (2), the signal has content relative to the grab gadget and content relative to the swerve gadget.
(5) Those two contents come apart -- something can be within reach and not ahead, or ahead and not within reach.
(6) So one and the same state represents two different things, and can be correct by one and incorrect by the other at the same time.
(7) A theory of content that can't say which one the state really represents can't do the job content was introduced to do, namely let us say when the system got the world wrong.
(8) If instead we pick the real consumer, then we're the ones choosing, and the choice is made by us, not by the biology.
(9) And once content is fixed by history plus whatever consumer you like, the bimetallic strip in a thermostat, the repressor in a bacterial operon, and an immune receptor all come out representing too.
(10) So either consumer semantics makes content indeterminate, or it makes content arbitrary, or it hands content to everything with a function.
(11) Any of those means teleosemantics proves too much and isn't a theory of content at all.
(12) Therefore we should give up consumer semantics and go back to something causal-informational.

I'd rather keep (1) and (2) than accept (11), because I dont have a replacement and I don't actually believe a thermostat represents. But I can't find where it goes wrong. Which step do I drop, why did it look fine, and what does dropping it cost me? And if the fix is that the consumer isn't up to us to choose, tell me what does choose it, and whether that answer survives the same move run again one level up -- i.e. whether the whole thing just starts over again at the level of the system as a whole.
