I teach a 400-level seminar on rationality, commitment, and strategic interaction, and I am writing the final exam. The unit on escalation of commitment ends with the dollar auction: a $1 bill is auctioned, the highest bidder pays what they bid and receives the dollar, and the second-highest bidder also pays what they bid but receives nothing. My students can all recite that this produces escalation and that sunk costs are not supposed to matter, but most of them cannot say precisely why the escalation is or is not rational, and they treat the sunk-cost fallacy as a complete explanation. I want one essay question that forces them to go past the label. To keep it from being a memorized recitation, I want the question built around a variant I have been using in class: the auctioneer is a charity, and every dollar paid by either bidder is donated to a cause that both bidders independently value at some rate α, where 0 < α < 1. So a bidder who pays x receives a warm-glow benefit of αx in addition to whatever else they get. The question should ask whether this charitable feature eliminates, mitigates, or exacerbates escalation; it should require the student to set up the payoffs of the second-highest bidder at an arbitrary stage, compare stopping with raising the bid, and derive the condition under which continuing is rational. It should also ask what the result implies about the popular claim that dollar-auction escalation is just the sunk-cost fallacy, and it should ask what changes if α is private information rather than common knowledge. The question should be answerable in about 75 minutes.

Please write the exam question itself, a model answer of about 700 to 800 words, and a marking scheme worth 20 points. The marking scheme should name the specific moves a top answer must make -- for example, the correct comparison of payoffs, the role of the gap between the two bids, the effect of α on the escalation threshold, and the point about private information -- and should say which moves distinguish an A answer from a B answer. It should also list the most common errors that should cost points, such as treating the warm glow as a rebate that reduces the bidder's own payment rather than as a benefit tied to total donations, or assuming that any altruistic motive must reduce escalation. Please write all of it in American English.
