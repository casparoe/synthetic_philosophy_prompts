I'm in a small reading group that has now spent the better part of a term on the unity of the proposition, and we have stalled badly. We can all state the regress the same way: if a proposition is a complex of constituents, then something has to hold those constituents together; but if the holding-together is itself another constituent — a relation, say — then it too must be held together with the others, and so on without end. Where we split is over what this shows. One camp treats the regress as a genuine, unresolved explanatory demand and keeps producing unifiers: an unsaturated constituent that completes itself, a relation that is tokened rather than instantiated, a whole that is somehow prior to its parts. A second camp treats the demand as illegitimate from the outset — the regress only gets going if you assume that the unity of a proposition must be a further fact over and above the constituents simply being arranged as they are, and once that assumption is dropped there is nothing left to explain. A third member insists the whole dispute is verbal, a disagreement about what we are using the word 'proposition' to mean. We can recite each other's moves by heart, and nobody has shifted position in months.

My own hypothesis — which I would like you to test rather than assume — is that this is not really a disagreement about unity at all, but about what counts as an explanation, and specifically about whether an explanation of unity is allowed to help itself to the very unity it is meant to explain. I want a diagnosis, not a survey: is this a verbal dispute, a substantive disagreement about explanation, or two groups answering different questions under one heading — one asking what a proposition is, the other asking how a sentence manages to say anything at all? Engage the positions I've described rather than the literature in general. Then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a concession one side could make — or whether we should expect this to stay stuck, and why. To keep it concrete, one of us keeps pressing an analogy I can't evaluate: a token event, such as this particular rope's being knotted, is not a further thing over and above the rope and the knotting, and this is supposed to show that the regress is harmless. Does that analogy help, or does it merely relocate the problem? Lead with your diagnosis in a sentence or two, then give me no more than 400 words in total.
