Nearly every explanation of Ellsberg starts by saying the second urn has an unknown proportion of red balls. But it isn't unknown, its unstated. The experimenter fixed it and chose not to tell you. I think that's doing far more work than anyone admits, and that a real chunk of what gets filed under ambiguity aversion is really aversion to having your payoff hinge on the unstated choice of an agent, not aversion to missing information as such.
