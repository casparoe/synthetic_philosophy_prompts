There's a view I've been kicking around: composition is a matter of control, not stuff. Some things compose a further thing just when they're bound into a single feedback loop — parts continuously reading and rewriting each other's states so the whole can regulate itself and correct errors. A heap of sand doesn't qualify, a bacterium does, a corporation maybe does, you and your phone probly don't even while you're doomscrolling. Write an essay on whether that's a defensible answer to the special composition question or whether it just relabels life or function and inherits all the same arbitrariness. Weigh nihilism, universalism, and restricted views, but spend most of the essay on whether a control-loop criterion can handle the usual nightmares: the problem of the many (why is a cloud one thing rather than a swarm of overlapping cloud-candidates?), vague boundaries, and the suspicion that any restriction is just a line drawn for convenience. Reach a conclusion and defend it — don't end on it's complicated.
