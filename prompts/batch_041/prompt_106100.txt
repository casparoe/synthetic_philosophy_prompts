A friend of mine is a card-carrying functionalist and he cornered me with this last week and now neither of us can find the exit. (1) What makes a state a mental state is just its causal role — what tends to bring it about, what it tends to bring about, how it hooks up with the other states. (2) So any system whose internal states stand in the right causal relations to each other and to its inputs and outputs is in those mental states. (3) Now imagine a book. For every question anyone could ever ask me, the book has a page giving the answer I'd give, and that same page also says which page to turn to for whatever I'd say next, so you can keep the conversation going as long as you want. (4) So for every input the book has exactly my dispositions to respond. (5) But those dispositions are all there is to my mental states — that's what (1) says. (6) So the book has my mental states, toothache included, and it's conscious. (7) Nothing in the argument needed paper, either. A nation of people with walkie-talkies passing notes would do the same job, and so would the cracks in my ceiling if you read them off as a table. (8) So consciousness is wherever there's a table somebody can read, which is basically everywhere, which is nuts. I really dont want to give up (1), I'm not ready to say a computer could never think, but (8) is a reductio and I can't spot the bad step. Which one is it, why did it look innocent, and what does it cost me to drop it?
