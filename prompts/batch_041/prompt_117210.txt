I am a Year 12 student in the middle of an extended project on personal identity and mind uploading, and I have been trying to think through the gradual replacement thought experiment without simply repeating the usual talking points. The standard version, as I understand it, replaces the neurons of a brain one at a time with silicon components that reproduce the same input-output profile. What interests me is that people seem to slide between two different things when they ask whether the person survives: whether each step is locally undetectable from the inside, and whether the whole process preserves a single continuous causal chain of information-bearing states. I have six replacement schedules I would like you to rate from 0 to 10 for how much personal identity you think survives, and then to rank from most to least identity-preserving. The first is one neuron replaced per day over forty years, with the person asleep for each replacement. The second is one neuron replaced per second, with the person awake and reporting no change. The third is half the brain replaced at once during a coma, then the other half a year later. The fourth is replacement by a distant duplicate that receives a continuous stream of the original's neural states and sends back motor commands, so that the original body is gradually puppeteered by the duplicate. The fifth is replacement of the whole brain in a single step by a functionally identical silicon brain, with the original destroyed. The sixth is one I have invented: the replacement proceeds in reverse order, starting with the neurons least connected to the rest of the network and ending with the most hub-like ones, but each new component introduces a small random delay that is compensated for elsewhere in the network, so that the overall input-output profile is preserved at every stage while the local timing of signals is altered.

Please explain in a sentence or two why each lands where it does, and say which single consideration does most of the work in your ranking and why. I am not asking for references, a reading list, or citations, because I cannot search online from the school computers and would rather have your own reasoning. Please write in British English, in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write an email or a letter.
