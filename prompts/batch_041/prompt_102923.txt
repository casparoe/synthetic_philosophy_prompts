I want the specification a theory of inductive simplicity -- whatever plays the role of Occam's razor in a theory of inference -- would have to satisfy before I trust any particular formalization of it. Set out the desiderata, each with the consideration that motivates it: the cases where simplicity is supposed to guide inference and that the theory must get right; the invariances it must respect, including whether it can be invariant under re-describing the same hypothesis when a description-length measure is fixed only up to a constant that depends on the chosen language; how it must connect to Bayesian conditioning and to the choice of prior; what it must say about hypothesis spaces that are infinite or continuously parameterized, given that complexity is standardly defined for finite strings; whether it must yield a method a bounded agent could actually run, when the optimal method is uncomputable; and whether it may be a purely pragmatic rule or must claim to track truth. Flag any pair of desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied -- the tension between preferring simpler hypotheses and results showing that no inductive method dominates unless it builds in a bias is the one I care most about -- and say which single requirement excludes the most otherwise attractive accounts. Skip the survey of proposals; the requirements are the deliverable.
