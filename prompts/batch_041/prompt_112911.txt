Two months ago I agreed to sit on the allocation committee of a small foundation that funds pain relief, and I have spent most of the time since discovering how little of my value theory survives contact with a budget line. A family member's illness this year has kept the question from being merely academic. Before the next meeting I want an audit of my own position, because I no longer trust my sense of where the fault lines are.

Here is the package.

1. Pleasure and pain lie on a single scale of value. There is a fact of the matter about how much good a given pleasure is worth and how much bad a given pain is, and comparative questions have determinate answers that turn on the amounts involved and nothing else.

2. I am a negative utilitarian with a lexical threshold. Some severity of suffering is such that no quantity of pleasure, however vast, and no number of beneficiaries, however large, can outweigh even a small amount of suffering at or above that severity. Below the threshold, trade-offs are not merely permitted but required.

3. The threshold is discovered, not stipulated. It marks a real difference in the character of the experience rather than a line drawn for administrative convenience.

4. My committee should therefore fund interventions against extreme suffering ahead of anything that buys large aggregate gains in mild well-being, and I should not be moved by ratios that trade across the threshold.

5. Above the threshold I am an ordinary consequentialist: I add up what is good and bad for whoever is affected, and I maximize the sum.

6. The absence of pleasure is not itself a harm. We are not required to create happy people, and a smaller world with less suffering need not be worse than a larger, happier one.

Tell me which of these genuinely pull against one another and which pairs only appear to. Order the genuine conflicts by severity, and for each say what would have to give, what the cheapest repair would be, and what that repair costs me elsewhere in the package. For the pairs that dissolve, say briefly what the dissolution turns on. End with the single revision that would clean up the most for the least, and be explicit about which commitment I would be surrendering to make it. I want the audit itself, not a literature review: no citations, no reading list, no more than 700 words, in American English.
