I am co-authoring a paper on social construction, and our draft contains a running table of kinds my co-author has already classified. On the constructed side: money; race; deafness; and the category of a child prodigy. On the not-constructed side: biological sex; the chemical element gold; and the category "mammal." She treats the table as fixed, and when I have pressed her for the general criterion behind it she says only that the entries speak for themselves. I do not need the names of the positions she might be running, or any sources for them — just the criteria, stated sharply enough that they can come apart.

The case we now have to classify is myopia, for the section we are drafting on medical kinds, and I would like your prediction of her verdict before I put the question to her. Two features of the case seem to be doing the work. First, the category is not conferred on anyone: myopia is measured, and the line between myopic and not is a threshold on a continuous quantity, so no one's recognition makes a person myopic. Second, the members' condition is plausibly produced by social arrangements: prevalence has risen steeply in a number of populations within two or three generations, far too fast for genetic change, and the leading explanations appeal to how children now spend their days — years of schooling, sustained close work, time spent indoors — rather than to any change in human biology.

Please do four things. First, set out the general criteria that fit her recorded verdicts; there should be more than one, and each should be stated precisely enough that the others can disagree with it. Second, give the verdict each criterion implies for myopia. Third, give your best guess at her actual verdict, with your reasons for preferring the criterion you settle on. Fourth, name the further case that would most efficiently separate the criteria that agree about myopia, and say what each possible verdict on it would show. If her recorded verdicts genuinely leave the answer open, say so rather than manufacturing a resolution.

State how confident you are in each claim you make, and identify the single point you are least sure of.
