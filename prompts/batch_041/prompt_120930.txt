I am a high school student writing a paper for my philosophy of mind class, and I would like help with a difficulty I have found in Searle's distinction between original and derived intentionality. As I understand it, a machine's states have only derived intentionality: they mean what they mean because we lend them our own meanings, the way a sentence means something because a speaker means something by it. Consider, though, my grandmother's answering machine, which has been sitting in her hallway for a year with nobody calling it and whose designers are long dead. Nobody is lending it any meaning at this moment, so by this account its recorded greeting means nothing at all, just as a book means nothing while it sits unread on a shelf. If that is right, then derived intentionality is not really a kind of intentionality but only a manner of speaking, original intentionality is the only kind there is, and Dennett's reply that the difference is one of degree rather than kind would be beside the point. Would a machine that builds another machine, which then outlives every person involved, fare any differently? I would be grateful if you could show me where my reasoning breaks down.
