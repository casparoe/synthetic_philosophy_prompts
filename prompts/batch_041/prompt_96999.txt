I teach a graduate seminar in normative ethics whose final unit is on what is sometimes called moral trade, and I need an essay prompt for the take-home final that will make my students argue rather than report. The idea we work with is simple. Two people hold different moral views, and each is close to indifferent to what the other cares most about. One is consumed by animal suffering and regards global poverty as a shame but not her business; the other is consumed by global poverty and regards animal suffering as a shame but not his business. Each can do something cheap that the other prizes: the first can write a cheque to an anti-poverty charity she would never otherwise support, the second can give up meat, which costs him little and means nothing to him either way. If both perform, each has done more good by her own lights than she could have managed alone, and neither has changed her mind about anything. My students split down the middle. Half see the trade as a plain improvement and think that refusing it is either irrational or a failure to take one's own values seriously; the other half think something is corrupt in the picture itself — that a commitment you would surrender for the right price was never a commitment, and that the picture quietly models moral conviction on the model of a taste.

The case I actually want the essay to turn on is one my tidy example hides. Suppose the animal person's half of the trade is not a cheque but a performance: she is asked to argue in public, and in print, that eating meat is morally unobjectionable — a claim she believes is false, and believes it is harmful to spread — in exchange for a very large donation to animal welfare that will spare a great many animals. She would be doing, by her own lights, something wrong in order to bring about a great deal of what she takes to be right. And suppose a further worry is live: that if trades of this kind are legitimate, moral commitments become bargaining chips, so that people have reason to adopt commitments strategically, and the practice may corrode the sincerity on which moral testimony and mutual trust depend — in which case even a trade that is impeccable taken one at a time might be wrong because of the practice it sustains.

Please write the essay. Weigh the strongest version of each side, then commit: I do not want a survey that ends by observing that the answer depends on your metaethics. Specifically, I want the essay to say whether the clean trade is permissible or even obligatory, and what that verdict rests on — in particular, whether it requires treating moral commitments as preferences whose satisfaction can be compared across persons, or whether it needs only that each party prefers the outcome by her own lights; whether the performance case differs in kind from the clean case or only in degree, and why; and whether the practice-level objection, if sound, tells against individual trades or only against the institutions that would grow up around them. Argue from the case as I have set it out rather than reviewing the literature; no bibliography is wanted, and I would rather see one position defended against its best objection than a balanced inventory of everything that has been said. About 1500 words.
