In a cognitive science reading group I attend, we keep returning to a slogan that is usually stated as competence without comprehension, and to the pair of inversions that are supposed to underwrite it: Darwin, who showed that the appearance of design in nature requires no designer, and Turing, who showed that a machine can reproduce the outputs of a reasoning mind by mechanically following rules. I think I grasp the slogan well enough, but a friend has been pressing a particular analogy on us, and I suspect it is doing far more argumentative work than it can bear. He says the ant colony is the cleanest picture of what both inversions amount to. No individual ant has the faintest grasp of what the colony is up to — it does not know why it lays down a pheromone trail, or where the foraging frontier lies — yet the colony as a whole finds near-optimal routes, allocates foragers, and appears to solve problems that no ant could even state. From this he concludes that comprehension was never needed for competence anywhere, that the ant colony, a trained neural network, and a market are all instances of a single kind of thing, and that all our talk of understanding is merely a description we project onto systems of mindless parts.

What I want from you is an audit of the analogy rather than of the slogan. Lay out the correspondence explicitly — what stands in for the ant, for the pheromone, for the queen (if anything corresponds), for the environment, and for whatever we are calling the colony's mind — and then tell me where the mapping breaks. In particular, I want to know whether the colony's competence is representational at all, or whether solving is merely something an observer attributes; whether there is any place where a genuine grasp could be hiding, as in a stigmergic update rule that carries more information than any ant possesses; and whether the conclusion that there is no understanding anywhere actually follows from the analogy, or whether the analogy quietly assumes that any system's competence can be decomposed into comprehension-free parts. If the ant colony turns out to be a poor fit for Turing's half of the pair in particular, tell me what analogy would fit better, and why.
