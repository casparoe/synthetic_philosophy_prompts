I sit on the board of a small foundation that funds an after-school mentoring program for kids in a mid-sized city. We're meeting next week to decide whether to commision an independent evaluation of the program. The evaluator would randomize some sites to continue the program and some to a waitlist, then compare graduation rates. The wrinkle is that our program staff are true believers, and they've told us that if we run the evaluation they'll read it as a signal that we're planning to shut the program down. Morale will crater, and the program will likely get worse during the evaluation period, which could make the results look bad even if the program is actually effective. Half the board says we should never be afraid of information; one member keeps invoking something called Good's theorem, that a Bayesian decision maker can't be made worse off by getting more information. The other half says this is different because the information is about our own decision, or because gathering it changes the thing being measured.

One board member, who used to teach decision theory, says the theorem only applies if the information doesn't change the set of available actions or the utility function, and that our staff's morale is just a cost of the information, not a counterexample. Another says the theorem assumes the decision maker is Bayesian and that the information isn't about the decision maker's own act, and that we're in exactly the exception. I don't know enough to adjudicate. I need to circulate a written opinion before Thursday: recommend whether we commission the evaluation, with a concrete timeline and any conditions, and give the reasoning. Please actually engage with the theorem -- what assumptions does it need, which ones fail here, and what would a defender of the theorem say to rescue it. If you think we should do it, say how to structure the evaluation so the morale effect doesn't swamp the result; if not, say what we should do instead. I want a clear recommendation, not a survey of options, and an honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side. Keep it to something I can circulate, maybe 800-1200 words.
