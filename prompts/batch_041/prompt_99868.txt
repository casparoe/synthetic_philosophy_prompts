Some friends and i have been arguing for weeks about Schopenhauer on music, and i want to close the reading group with a poem. What i keep getting stuck on: he says music is the one art that skips the Ideas entirely and copies the will itself, melody being the highest grade of the will's objectification while harmony and its deferred resolutions are the will's endless not-quite-satisfied striving. But copy is a representing word, and the will is supposed to be exactly what can't be represented — so the music chapter looks like it breaks his own rule, and maybe music ends up doing the metaphysics better than the philosophy does. Can you write me a pantoum — 4 quatrains, standard interlocking repeats, closing stanza folding back so the first line comes last — where the recurring lines are the two readings, music copies the will / music just is the will speaking, so the form itself is the will coming back around and never landing, and the last stanza forces the two readings up against each other so you feel them collide instead of resolve. Dont let it cadence off at the end; loop it. Someone whos actually read the chapter should recognize it and someone who hasnt should still get a real poem. Start with the poem itself, no preamble.
