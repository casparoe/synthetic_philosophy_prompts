I am a software engineer by trade and have no formal training in philosophy, though for the past several weeks I have been sitting in on a small reading group on the philosophy of biology that a friend runs. I have been asked to open our final session, and I have volunteered to bring a poem. I would like you to write it.

The session is on the idea the group has been calling free-floating rationales. The thought is that there can be a perfectly good answer to the question of why a creature does what it does — doing that keeps it alive, or gets it fed, or gets its offspring out — even though nothing anywhere holds that answer. Not the creature, not its genes, not anything inside it. The answer is a fact about the world and about the history that shaped the creature; it can be correct; and it can license us to say the creature is now getting it wrong, without there being any mind in the picture to be right or wrong. The version I keep turning over is the one where the rationale has gone stale: the world the behavior was tuned to no longer exists, the behavior continues anyway, and the answer to why it does that is still true in the old sense and no longer true in any other. I am interested in this partly because of my own work — I have shipped heuristics calibrated on traffic that no longer resembles our traffic, and they still run, and nothing inside them corresponds any longer to the reason they were written.

Please write a poem of roughly fifteen to twenty-one lines in terza rima. The form is the point. In terza rima each tercet's middle rhyme becomes the next tercet's outer rhyme, so the pattern is handed down the chain and no single stanza holds it — which is the shape of the thing I am after, the reason living in the lineage rather than in the animal. If you must choose between strict iambic pentameter and a clean interlock, keep the interlock. Close the chain as terza rima closes it, with a final line that settles the rhyme the last tercet leaves hanging.

A few things I care about:

- Invent your own creature. Please avoid the examples everyone reaches for — the beaver's dam, the cuckoo's egg, the moth around the porch light — and give me something I have not seen before.
- Keep any inner life off the page. No mental vocabulary at all: nothing knows, wants, tries, intends, expects, means, remembers, or learns. In particular, no as if — that is the escape hatch, and I want the poem to have to show the mismatch between what the behavior is for and what it now accomplishes rather than hedge around it.
- Hold two things apart that are easy to run together: the mechanism that fires, which should be dumb and local, and the reason the behavior persists, which is a fact about a world that has changed. Both should be present, and neither should collapse into the other or into a mind.
- No philosophical vocabulary and no explanation. Do not use the words evolution, adaptation, or instinct, do not use the group's term for the idea, and do not include a line that tells the reader what the poem is about. Someone who knows the idea should recognize it and not wince; someone who does not should simply have a poem.
