When the spacing and timing are right, subjects do not report two stationary dots; they report a single dot traveling from the first position to the second, and, crucially, they report it changing from the first color to the second somewhere in the middle of that flight. The wrinkle is that the color the dot appears to have at the midpoint can only be settled once the second dot has already been shown — yet subjects report the color change as occurring "on the way," as though the journey were experienced with its ending already folded into it. Philosophers describe the two candidate stories about what is going on with a pair of labels I find useful. A Stalinesque revision is one in which the perceptual system delays and edits the experience before it is ever presented, so the subject never has an unedited experience at all — like a show trial whose verdict was fixed in advance and whose proceedings were staged only for show. My instinct is that this is essentially the difference between buffering with delayed emission and rewriting a log after the fact, and that my world's vocabulary should be able to say everything worth saying here.
