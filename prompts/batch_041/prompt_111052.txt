I'm a first-year in a decision-science-ish lab and I keep getting hit with 'good enough is good enough'. Most recently my advisor used it to wave off a question I asked about our model: we fit choice data with a stopping rule where the agent searches until it finds an option above some threshold, and the threshold drifts up after a win and down after a loss. I asked whether the model is meant to be saying people can't do better, or that they shouldn't, or just that it's a decent predictive shortcut. He said 'it's satisficing, good enough is good enough, move on'. Then a referee said the whole thing is unfalsifiable, because any choice is satisficing relative to some threshold, and my advisor said the same sentence again, which I don't think can be right both times, but I can't articualte why.

So: give me a menu of the seperate precise claims that 'good enough is good enough' or 'people satisfice, they don't maximize' could be making, sorted from the trivial to the radical, and for each say whether it's a platitude, a real empirical claim, a normative claim about rationality, a normative claim about morality, or something that sounds like a claim but isn't one. In particular I want to know about:

- the reading where it's a psychological claim (people in fact stop searching), and whether that's supposed to be surprising at all
- the reading where it's about the formal setup: once the option set has to be generated by search and not everything is comparable, 'the best' isn't even defined, so maximizing isn't an option you could take even in principle
- the reading where it's a stopping rule *inside* a maximizing framework — satisficing as what you optimally do when search costs something — and how that differs from the previous one
- the reading where it's a norm of rationality: stopping short of the best is rational, or at least not irrational
- the reading where it's about the heuristics people say beat optimizing models in some environments
- the reading where it's about what you're morally allowed to do — someone in my ethics reading group claims there's a whole view on which you may deliberately do less good than you could, and I want to know if that's the same word doing the same job or a homonym
- and the annoying reading where it's vacuously true, since any action is 'good enough' for some threshold, which I think is what the referee was getting at

Then tell me which readings would actually support what my advisor was doing with it — both times — and which ones make the slogan do no work at all. If two of the readings are secretly in tension with each other (the one that says maximizing is impossible and the one that says satisficing is the sensible way to pursue the best seem like they might be), say so explicitly, because I think that's the thing I keep tripping over. Also tell me which reading makes the drifting threshold in our model do real work and which reading it's just decoration on. List format please, I want to bring it to our meeting.
