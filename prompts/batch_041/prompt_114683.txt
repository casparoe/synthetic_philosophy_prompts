I run a small reading group on comparative ethics and we keep getting stuck on whether karma is really a theory of moral desert or just a fancy way of saying that actions have consequences. Part of the problem is that we're using desert in at least three different ways and not noticing: (a) the suffering is deserved and its being deserved is itself part of why it's good that it happens; (b) the suffering is just the natural upshot of what you did, and the fact that it fits isn't a further good on top; (c) nobody deserves anything, because the one who acted and the one who suffers aren't the same person in any strict sense. I want to stop re-litigating this every week and make a one-page handout that sorts the positions instead. So: please build me a taxonomy of positions on the question "is karma a theory of moral desert?", written as a decision tree. Every internal node should be labeled with the question that splits the views beneath it, phrased so a reader can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to, and every leaf should carry the name of the position (or say unnamed if nobody actually defends it) plus one sentence on that view's most awkward commitment. Build it from the logical space first -- the questions and the combinations of answers they allow -- and only then drop the historical positions into the cells, noting which cells come out empty and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just never got developed. Somewhere around ten to twelve leaves, and assume the reader is a smart undergrad with no background in South Asian or Buddhist philosophy, so keep the jargon to a minimum and explain any term you can't avoid.

The branch questions I care about most: (1) does the karmic order need someone running it (a judge, a lawgiver, a cosmic administrator) or is it a law with nobody behind it? (2) is the link between how you acted and what happens to you desert-constituting, or merely causal -- a regularity that happens to track moral quality the way weather tracks pressure, so that no one is owed anything? (3) does karma need a persisting self that owns the deed and later collects, or can it run with no owner at all? (4) is the mechanism exceptionless, or can it be interrupted -- by insight, by grace, by someone handing their surplus merit over to you? (5) is the unit the individual, or the family/group/nation? (6) does it need rebirth at all, or can it be cashed out this-worldly as a story about how actions build character and character builds later actions? Use those as your spine but add whatever else the tree needs.

For the diagnostic cases, use concrete ones rather than abstract labels wherever you can: a child born with a painful lifelong condition; a person who does real harm and then genuinely reforms; a family or a whole people said to be working off a shared debt; someone who suffers terribly despite apparently living well; and a case where merit earned by one person is ritually handed to another. Two more things. Where two positions in your tree give the same answer to every question you pose, flag them as ones the tree can't separate rather than pretending your questions distinguish them. And if you find yourself wanting a position that nobody historically held -- something like karma as a lawless causal stream with no ledger and no debtor -- go ahead and include it, just mark it as invented.
