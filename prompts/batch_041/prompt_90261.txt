Two camps have been talking past each other about Maxwell's demon for as long as the demon has existed, and I want the joint statement they should have written together, rather than the exchange of rebuttals they did write.

The first camp — call them the information-thermodynamicists — hold that the demon is exorcised by the physics of information: a correlation with the gas can be spent as fuel, the demon's record is a physical resource, and the Second Law survives only because handling that record is thermodynamically costly, paradigmatically because erasing a bit dissipates at least kT ln 2. The second camp — the statistical-mechanical sceptics — hold that the demon never threatened the Second Law in the first place: the correct object is the closed system of gas plus demon plus apparatus, and once the accounting is done there, by ordinary statistical mechanics and without any privileged notion of information, nothing needs exorcising; the demon's supposed debt is a bookkeeping artefact of coarse-graining, and information does no thermodynamic work of its own.

Draft the document both could sign. It should contain: a numbered section of claims both camps accept — about what the Second Law does and does not assert, about the status of the demon's memory, about the Szilard engine, about fluctuation theorems, and about what the demon story is even supposed to show; then a table of the residual disagreements, with each camp's position written in the wording that camp itself would accept; then the cruxes, defined as the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp, each accompanied by the case, argument or experiment both camps agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance.

Do not let this degenerate into a recitation of the textbook demon. Work through a family of variants I care about, and let the cruxes emerge from them:

(i) a demon with a finite register of N bits that it must overwrite, so that erasure is forced;
(ii) an accumulating demon whose memory grows without bound and is never erased during the run, but which is finally permitted to thermalise;
(iii) a demon that measures reversibly, records the outcome and never reads it back, so that the record is a correlation that is stored but never used;
(iv) a variant of (iii) in which the record is handed to a second agent who spends it as fuel elsewhere.

For each variant, state where each camp books the entropy, whether the Second Law holds, and — the point of the exercise — whether the two camps actually make different predictions or merely give different descriptions of the same physics.

Two further cruxes I want treated explicitly: whether Landauer's principle is an independent law, a derived consequence of statistical mechanics, or a constraint built into what we are willing to call a memory; and whether the demon shows the Second Law to be merely statistical, or whether that is a separate question which the demon does nothing to settle.

Finally, tell me which parts of the dispute you judge to be verbal, and name the precise formulations that make the verbal character of those parts visible. Do not adjudicate who is right; produce the text both camps could put their names to.
