I've been reading alot about those experiments where someone decides to wiggle a finger and it turns out the brain signal shows up a few hundred milliseconds before they say they felt the urge to move, and the urge itself comes a bit before the finger actually goes. I'm not in academia, just a person who got interested and read a bunch of stuff, and what strikes me is that nobody seems to have moved on this in forty years. The scientists say it proves the decision is made before you're conscious of it, so free will is an illusion; the philosophers say it proves nothing of the sort, and then they argue about what "free" is even supposed to mean. Everyone knows the results, everyone has heard everyone else's replies, and the two sides just keep restating themselves at each other.

So put on both hats for me — philosopher of mind and cognitive neuroscientist — think this through carefully, and tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is. Here's my hypothesis, and I'd rather you test it than just agree with it: maybe it isn't one disagreement but three. One is empirical and genuinely open, because I read that some researchers now think that brain signal is just random noise in the motor areas that happens to cross a threshold, in which case it isn't an early decision at all, just a coincidence of timing. One is conceptual: whether "free" requires that the conscious self start the action, or only that the action flow from your own reasons and character the normal way. If it's the second, the experiment may be beside the point. And one might be that the two sides aren't asking the same question — the lab people are asking about the initiation of a pointless finger movement nobody had a reason to make, while the philosophers are asking about moral responsibility for things you did for reasons. Also, Libet himself apparently thought there was still room for a conscious veto, which makes me suspect even he didn't read his own results the way the headlines do.

Given all that, tell me: substantive dispute, verbal one about the word "free", or two questions wearing one name? Where does the impasse really come from — facts, standards of evidence, or starting intuitions neither side can argue for? And what would actually count as progress — a better experiment, a conceptual distinction, or dropping the question? I dont want a survey of the literature, I want your diagnosis with reasons. And please no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables, just write it out as continuous prose like an email to a curious friend.
