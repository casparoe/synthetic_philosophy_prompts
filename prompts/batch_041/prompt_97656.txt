I've got no philosophy training, just a lot of opinions and a long commute, and I keep going in circles on this. Please act like a political philosopher who's spent years on religion-and-state questions, and before you commit to anything, brainstorm the cases on your own and write out a couple of candidate rules side by side. Then tell me what principle actually fits my gut verdicts, if any does.

A state school says its teachers can't wear a headscarf or a cross or a kippah while they teach. Fine by me. The teacher is speaking for the state.
The same school says the students can't wear a headscarf. Not fine. She's not the state, she's fourteen and trying to get through maths.
A public hospital tells its nurses to take theirs off. Fine, same reason as the teacher.
The same hospital tells a patient to take hers off. Not fine, not even close.
A town votes to ban minarets on mosques. Not fine. I can't get past the feeling that it's a rule aimed at one group even though it's written to apply to everyone's buildings.
But a law that says you have to show your face in a bank, a courthouse, on a bus — fine. And I realize that sits badly next to the student one.
A school that serves no meat at all: fine, everybody eats the same lunch. A school that serves pork and shrugs when a Muslim or Jewish kid says there's nothing they can eat: not fine.
Now the one I keep flipping on. A town passes a rule that everyone's hands have to be bare and visible inside public buildings — they had a pickpocketing problem and the cameras can't tell whose hands are whose. There's a small religious group near me whose practice is to keep their hands gloved at all times, indoors, even while eating. Half of me says the rule is fine, it's about hands and cameras, not about religion. The other half says I've just built a minaret ban and stapled a security excuse to it.

My own attempt at the rule: the state can be as strict as it likes about its own voice and its own employees, but it should bend for ordinary people going about their lives, unless it has a reason that has nothing to do with religion — knowing who's in the building, not poisoning anyone. Does that survive my list? If it doesn't, is there a better one that saves everything, and what does the simpler version cost me — which of my verdicts do I have to give up? State the rule precisely, say which case is doing the work in each part of it, and then tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed, like a school that bans headscarves only during gym class, or a private employer instead of the state.
