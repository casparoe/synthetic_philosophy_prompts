There's a pattern I keep noticing and I've never found a word for it. Someone — often me — ends up holding two claims that cant both be true. Each one, on its own, seems more obvious than any argument against it, so dropping one feels like the bigger error. Then comes the second move, the one I can't name. Instead of drawing anything out of the pair, they wall it off: they reason quite happily everywhere else, they just won't touch the place the two claims meet, and if you push them they'll say you can get anything at all out of a contradiction, so there's no point poking at it. I've seen the same thing in maths — a paradox turns up and nobody abandons the theory, they just stop using the two steps together and carry on.

What gets me is that this doesn't feel like the same thing as believing both claims, and it doesnt feel like the opposite either. Refusing to conclude anything sounds like a limit on what follows from the contradiction, but the bit about getting anything out of it sounds like there's no limit at all. So are they quietly dropping one claim, keeping both but gagging the logic, or something else entirely?

Is there an established term for the walling-off? I'm after the joints more than the label: what exactly is being accepted or rejected, how many separate things are bundled up here, and where the line falls between a contradiction you can carry on with and one that really does wreck everything downstream. If nothing fits, tell me the nearest ideas and where each one breaks.

And be straight about which parts of this are settled and which are genuinely disputed among people who work on it — don't hand me one camp's line as if it were the consensus. British English please.
