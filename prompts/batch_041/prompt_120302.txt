Last night I lost an argument about frontier AI release that I am fairly sure I should have won, and I would like you to tell me where it went wrong. My friend's position is the familiar one: publishing the weights of a frontier model is handing out unlabelled weapons, because the capability travels and the licence does not. My position — the one I defended badly — is that the controversy is narrower than it looks once you notice what open-sourcing actually consists in. Open-sourcing a model means publishing the thing that determines its behaviour, which is to say its source: the weights, the training and inference code, the configuration, everything a competent outsider would need in order to reproduce and examine it. And publishing that is precisely what makes a system verifiable. Anyone — a university lab, a regulator, a hobbyist in another jurisdiction — can take the artefact apart, run it, probe it, and check whether the safety claims made about it are true; that is why we have always been willing to trust open source with our infrastructure. So I take the real disagreement to be about timing rather than direction: some months of internal review, then release, the way a project discloses a bug once the patch is ready. My friend kept insisting that open weights are not the same thing as open source, which I regard as a distinction without a difference — the weights are the model, and the code is scaffolding. He also said something I have not been able to shake: that publishing the design of a lock and mailing out copies of a key that opens every lock in the building are not the same act, and that the second is closer to what a release is. I told him this was exactly backwards, since a published lock design is what lets strangers find the flaw.

So: is the disagreement really only about timing, and are open weights really just open source under another name? I would rather have my reasoning audited than my conclusion endorsed. Tell me which premise is doing illegitimate work, and where my picture has to be repaired rather than merely qualified — whether the trouble is that access is not the same as auditability, or that the thing being released is not the thing that would need to be examined, or something else entirely. I would also like you to be explicit about your own confidence: mark the steps where the argument is decisive and the steps where you are extending beyond anything established. And tell me plainly what would move you to my side — what would have to be true about a released model, and about the people examining it, for openness to deliver the verification I am claiming it delivers. If the honest answer is that my question is malformed, say so and show me the malformation rather than humouring the framing.
