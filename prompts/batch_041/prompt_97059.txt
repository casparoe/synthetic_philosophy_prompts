I'm a novelist, and I've spent the better part of a year trying to write a character who is genuinely free — not merely unpredictable — and I keep running up against a pattern I have no word for, which I suspect is not only a novelist's problem. It began with a nature programme about a solitary wasp that provisions a burrow with prey, and, before dragging the prey inside, goes down first to inspect the burrow. While the wasp was below, the presenter nudged the prey a few inches away; the wasp emerged, dragged it back, and inspected again. He repeated this, I think, some forty times, and each time the wasp ran the whole sequence from the beginning; it never simply carried the prey in. What struck me was not the insect's stupidity but the shape of it: the animal had a repertoire, and the repertoire had no exit. So I began collecting human versions. A colleague who, whatever the objection, replies that she has considered it and stands by her view — the same sentence, the same cadence, whether the objection is trivial or devastating. A committee I used to sit on that had a rule for everything, including a rule for suspending the rules, so that even its departures from procedure were procedural; you could never point to the moment it had been flexible, only to a higher rule that had licensed the flexibility. And, most troubling to me as a writer, the suspicion that my supposedly free character is merely a longer routine — that when she changes her mind it is because a different script has come up, and the script for changing her mind is as fixed as the first.

So: is there a name for this, or a literature? I don't want a bare label. I want to know what exactly the thing is and where its edges lie. In particular: (i) how one tells it apart from behaviour that is merely predictable — a sensible person would also re-inspect the burrow, and predictability alone cannot be the mark; (ii) whether the literature distinguishes the first-order case, where an agent is plainly stuck in a routine, from the second-order worry, where the agent varies its behaviour endlessly but always in the same way of varying, so that apparent flexibility is only a nested rigidity — and whether anyone has argued that the second-order case is inescapable, that there is no getting outside it from within; and (iii) what, if anything, this has to do with free will, since the whole reason I care is that I wanted to write a character who has some. If there is no established term, coin one, define it, and tell me which of the nearest existing concepts it must be distinguished from, and why none of them quite does the job. I have a hazy sense that a philosopher of mind once used a wasp as the emblem of something in this vicinity, but I may be conflating it with something else entirely.
