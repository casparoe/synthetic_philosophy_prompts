So here's an episode I want you to work through using Popper's own views on Darwinism. Popper at one point called Darwinism, or at least the natural-selection part of it, not a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research program, and creationists ran with that line for years. Later he walked it back, saying he'd been misunderstood and that Darwinism does make testable claims, or at least that the whole thing is more testable than he'd made it sound. Then in the 2005 Dover intelligent design case it all came back: the court had to decide whether ID counted as science, and one line of argument against it was that it makes no testable predictions, while the defense kept saying evolution has been called unfalsifiable too, so why the double standard. I know the broad strokes; what I want is the case analysis. Who are the players and what are their actual commitments? Does the creationist use of Popper hold up by Popper's own lights, or is it a misuse of a remark about one piece of a larger theory? How does Popper's distinction between the hard core of a research program and its protective belt apply here -- is natural selection itself testable, or is the testability located in the conjunction of selection plus genetics plus the fossil record plus biogeography, and if so does that make the creationist complaint trivially true of every scientific theory? Then turn it around: what does this whole episode show about falsifiability as a demarcation criterion in the historical sciences? Does it confirm Popper's view, embarrass it, or reveal that he was answering a question the Dover court didn't actually need to answer? Be honest about where the Popperian framework is just silent on what was really at stake in the courtroom, and don't force it to speak.
