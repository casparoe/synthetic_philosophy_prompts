Treat the following distinction as the organizing frame for your answer, and accept it for the sake of argument even if you would draw the line differently. Call an introspective report thin when it asserts only that a mental state of some type is occurring right now — that one is presently anxious, or that one presently has a visual experience as of a red expanse. Call it thick when it asserts more than the bare occurrence: the state's content, its object, or its causal role — that one is anxious about the deadline, or that one's visual experience is of a uniformly red surface rather than a surface with unnoticed variation.

Argue for the following thesis: the standing dispute over whether introspection is reliable is largely an artifact of treating thin and thick reports as exercises of a single capacity, and once the two are separated, the defensible verdict is that thin reports are nearly immune to the standard skeptical arguments while thick reports are unreliable in a way that no amount of care or training repairs. Your argument should do the following.

First, identify what makes thick reports epistemically fragile without invoking deception, self-interest, or social pressure; the mechanism you name must be one that would operate even in a solitary, honest, and maximally attentive introspector.

Second, explain why that same mechanism leaves thin reports untouched — or, if you conclude that it does not, say so plainly and revise the thesis rather than quietly weakening it.

Third, work through a concrete case in which a person sincerely reports the reason for a choice they have just made, and use it to show where the thin/thick line falls in practice and why the report lands on the side you claim.

Fourth, state what sort of observation would count against your verdict, and whether it is the sort of thing one could in principle obtain.

Do not lean on the authority of named researchers or on the prestige of a research program; argue from the structure of the case. Where you appeal to a phenomenon, describe it well enough that it stands on its own.
