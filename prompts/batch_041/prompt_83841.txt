Germany shut down its last nuclear reactors in 2023, on the ground that the risk of a catastrophic accident, however small, could not be justified, and did so while still burning lignite; France has run a reactor fleet supplying the large majority of its electricity for decades, treats its spent fuel as an engineering problem with a deep geological solution, and defends the fleet with risk estimates. Yet German radiation protection is governed by an international framework whose central operational rule is not precaution but optimization: dose limits, plus the requirement that exposures be reduced as far as reasonably achievable, which is to say traded against cost. And energy debates in both countries routinely rank technologies by deaths per unit of electricity generated, a metric that treats a death from coal particulate as commensurable with one from a radiation release and aggregates both across populations and generations.

What philosophical commitments does each practice presuppose? Can a state coherently refuse a low-probability catastrophe from one source while accepting a large, certain, chronic mortality from another, and is the difference a matter of value (some harms being categorically different, not merely weighted more heavily) or of knowledge (the catastrophic probabilities not being credibly estimable, so that expected-value reasoning does not apply)? What does the deaths-per-unit metric presuppose, and which rival views of value does it quietly rule out? And since both countries must dispose of waste hazardous for longer than any state has existed, yet discount future costs in ordinary policy analysis and design their repositories to remain retrievable, do those practices presuppose inconsistent obligations to distant future people, or can they be reconciled? Reconstruct the implicit theory behind each practice, distinguish what it strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, and say where it is underdetermined -- and what would have to change in the practice if the presupposed view were replaced by its leading rival.
