Treat Maxwell's demon as a template for any physical agent that converts information into work: finite memory, a finite rate at which it can measure, and an obligation to erase what it stores. On that picture the resource that matters is not how much the demon knows but how much of what it knows bears on the degrees of freedom that actually do work; a demon with perfect knowledge of the gas's total energy and none of its microstate extracts nothing. An adequate account therefore owes us bounds stated in terms of task-relevant correlations rather than raw mutual information.

I am assembling a research agenda for one theorist with roughly three years, strong in statistical mechanics and information theory, and no laboratory. Which open questions in the physics of information should come first, and why? Weigh at least these, and add whatever I have missed: whether the erasure cost is a law of nature or a convention about what counts as erasure; whether a fully self-contained demon, whose measuring apparatus and memory are inside the system being accounted for, can retain any advantage at all; whether quantum coherence or entanglement is a genuine thermodynamic resource or an artifact of where the accounting line is drawn; and what replaces the second law once the demon's measurement channel has finite rate and its memory finite capacity.

Defend the ordering rather than merely listing: which questions are upstream of which, which are disputes about definitions dressed up as physics, which are tractable with current tools rather than waiting on experiment or on a settled theory of measurement, and what evidence or argument would move a question up or down the list.
