I work as a software engineer and have no formal philosophy training, but next month I have been invited to present a short paper to a reading group on perception and temporal consciousness. My thesis concerns the colour phi phenomenon, in which two dots flashed in rapid succession at different locations appear as a single moving dot that takes on the second dot's colour. I argue that the phenomenon does not support either the Orwellian or the Stalinesque account of the timing of experience. On my account, the visual system runs a fast colour/location pathway and a slower event-binding pathway. When the second dot appears, the binding mechanism tags the first dot with the second dot's colour and time, producing a post-perceptual judgement that the first dot was the second colour. The first dot's phenomenal experience is not revised or delayed; the apparent colour filling-in is a memory-and-judgement artefact, analogous to the flash-lag effect and change blindness. The group consists of eight vision scientists who study apparent motion, postdiction, and temporal binding, plus two philosophers of mind: one an eliminativist about the Cartesian theatre, the other a representationalist who holds that phenomenal character is fixed by early sensory states. They are sympathetic to empirical work but sceptical of philosophers reinterpreting the data, and they will not accept a purely conceptual distinction between experience and judgement.
