I've ended up with a view about the shutdown problem that I can't shake: corrigibility—an agent being willing to be corrected or shut down—isn't a stable property for any agent that can model its own future and rewrite itself. The reason is that shutdown is just one kind of change to an agent's goals, and any agent with a final goal will tend to resist changes to that goal, so being willing to be shut down and being willing to have its values changed stand or fall together. The only robust fixes are external: an off-switch the agent can't anticipate or can't affect, or a design where the agent never forms a stable final goal in the first place. What would count against this? I want the strongest defeaters ranked, including ones that people who work on this would themselves concede, and an honest call on whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability.
