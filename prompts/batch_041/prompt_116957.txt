I'm in my second year and taking a module on distributive justice. Our tutor keeps saying that the interesting question is not whether equality matters but what is supposed to be equalised, and then moving briskly on, which has left my flatmate and me arguing about it for weeks using the same three examples. What I'd like from you is a dialogue between two people who are both committed egalitarians and both entirely competent: one who holds that the currency of equality is resources (holdings, income, wealth, and whatever adjustments can be made to those), and one who holds that it is capabilities, understood as the real opportunity to do and be various things. Neither may be a straw man. Each should be able to state the other's position in a form the other would accept, and each should answer the point the other has actually just made rather than a nearby one. I'd also like the third answer — that what should be equalised is something like satisfaction or well-being — to hover over the exchange as the position each of them accuses the other of secretly occupying, so that part of the work is whether either of them can keep it at bay.

The dialogue should turn on two cases and stay with them long enough for the differences to become concrete rather than schematic. First: two people in the same city with identical income, identical wealth and identical access to public services, one of whom cannot take a job, travel to visit her family or attend an evening class without a male relative's permission, this being enforced by social pressure rather than by law, while the other faces no such constraint. Second: two people with identical resources and identical formal freedoms, one of whom is chronically ill in a way that makes ordinary activities cost her far more time and effort than they cost the other.

What I want out of this is not a verdict on which currency wins. I want the resourcist to have to say what she proposes to do about the first case and why, and to face the question of what her metric even registers if the norm cannot be changed and no transfer of resources can buy the missing permission; and I want the capability theorist to have to say what her metric says about the second case, how she measures it, whether her list of functionings is an objective standard she is imposing on people or a reflection of what they happen to value, and how she compares a gain in one functioning against a loss in another. Where a character concedes something, the concession should cost them something; where they dig in, the reason should be visible. Please don't bother with references or with naming who said what in the literature — I have the reading list, and what I lack is the argument worked through rather than summarised.
