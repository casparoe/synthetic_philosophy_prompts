The volunteer sea-rescue club I've been part of for eleven years — I do the radio, I've never actually pulled anyone out of the water — has its fiftieth anniversary dinner next month, and I've somehow ended up giving the after-dinner speech. The problem is the timing: the treasurer has put a motion on the agenda for the same evening. Wind up the club, sell the boat, and hand the whole endowment (call it £400k) to a global health charity that, on the published numbers, saves a life for something like £2,000. His argument is the drowning-child one — Singer's pond, the kid, the shoes you ruin getting in — and I don't think he's wrong about the logic. I speak immediately before the vote.

So I need a speech that does all of this at once, or as much of it as one speech can:

- never says the boat is a better use of the money than the charity is, because I don't believe that and half the room knows I don't;
- never says the last fifty years were a hobby, because that's false too, and it's a rotten thing to say at somebody's fiftieth;
- actually engages the drowning-child argument instead of deflecting it with "it's different when it's your own harbour," which I think is a cop-out and would be embarrassed to say out loud;
- keeps two questions seperate that I'm afraid the room will mash together — "is this the best possible use of £400k?" and "should there be people who are on call for their neighbours, as a standing fact about the town?" — and shows they're different questions without pretending the second one automatically answers the first;
- and leaves the volunteers — including two men in the room who went into the harbour in 1994 and came back with a girl who is now somebody's mother — with something true. Not a consolation prize. Something they can actually believe.

The room is about sixty volunteers and their families (a plumber, a lot of retired people, several nurses), the mayor, two of the donors who paid for the boat, and one member who teaches moral philosophy at the university and who will be checking whether I've answered the treasurer or just made everyone feel warm. Five to seven minutes, meant to be said out loud, no jargon, no footnotes. Write it.
