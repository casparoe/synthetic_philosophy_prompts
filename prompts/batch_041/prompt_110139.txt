After a long career in the field sciences — decades spent collecting, measuring, and sorting organisms, in the course of which the question of whether two populations were "the same species" was a practical matter and never a philosophical one — I have retired into the philosophy of biology, and I have arrived at a position I would like you to assess.

The position is a compound one, formed over several years of argument with a former colleague, and I state it here as a single proposition so that it can be true or false. First, species taxa are real entities in nature: each is a particular — a segment of a lineage — rather than a class or a set defined by shared properties. Second, the category species is nevertheless not a natural kind, because there is no single, theory-independent property shared by all and only species. Third, there is nonetheless a single correct inventory of species-level lineages in nature, awaiting discovery. Fourth, the many competing species concepts are therefore not rival theories of what species are, but alternative and context-relative criteria for delimiting the very same lineages, so that pluralism at the level of concepts is entirely compatible with monism at the level of entities.

I would like your credence that this entire conjunction is correct — a number, please, not a survey of positions — together with the decomposition that produces it. Break the question into the sub-questions it actually rests on, assign a rough probability to each, say how they combine, and mark the one your final number is most sensitive to. I take the natural sub-questions to include at least the following, though you should add any I have missed: whether species taxa are particulars rather than classes; whether the boundaries of lineages are determinate in nature or partly a matter of convention; whether the category admits of any unifying property at all; whether species concepts can function as criteria rather than as definitions; and whether the compound position is stable or collapses under pressure into either full realism or full conventionalism. On that last point I am particularly interested in whether the third and fourth clauses can be held together with the second, or whether the position is incoherent as stated and would need to be repaired.

For calibration: I put the whole conjunction at about 0.3, while my colleague — the more robust realist of the two of us — puts it near 0.8. Beyond your own number and its decomposition, tell me where that disagreement most likely lives: which sub-question, if we resolved it one way or the other, would move our two numbers together. Finally, say what would count as resolution — what empirical finding or conceptual development, if it arrived, would settle the matter rather than merely shift the weight of opinion.
