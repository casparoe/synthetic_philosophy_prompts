I'm a second-year PhD student working on reference and modality, and I'm trying to write down my own position for a workshop where someone else will be assigned to attack it. I'd like you to do a dry run first. Here is the package I actually hold. First, semantic externalism for natural kind terms: the reference of a word like "water" is fixed by facts about the environment rather than by anything inside the speaker's head, so that a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me raised on a Twin Earth, where the clear drinkable stuff has a different underlying composition, would mean something else by the word, and no amount of introspection on my part could settle what my own term refers to. Second, a two-dimensional framework of the sort Chalmers develops: every sentence has a primary intension, a function from centered worlds to truth values that is supposed to capture its a priori epistemic content, and a secondary intension, which is its ordinary truth-condition; the primary intension is something my Twin and I share, while the secondary intensions come apart. Third, I believe there are contingent a priori truths of the familiar kind, sentences we can know by reflection alone even though they are not necessary — indexical ones like the sentence "I am here now," and the Kripke-style cases where reference is fixed by description, such as the sentence that says the actual metre stick is one metre long. Fourth, I accept the necessary a posteriori: theoretical identities like the claim that water is H2O are, if true, necessary, yet knowable only by empirical investigation. Fifth, I am a modal rationalist: I think ideal conceivability entails metaphysical possibility, and I take this to be a substantive thesis rather than a deflationary one. Sixth, and this is the one I am least sure how to state, I think a priori reflection is a reliable guide to the deep structure of reality — to what kinds of things there fundamentally are and what they are like — and not merely to our concepts or our language.
