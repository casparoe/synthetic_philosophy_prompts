I'm a second-year PhD student in a department with a small formal-epistemology group, and I'm applying for a ten-week summer research grant; I need a two-page proposal, and all I have is a half-formed idea that I cannot yet tell is a real research question or a confusion. The idea concerns van Fraassen's Reflection principle. Roughly: an agent's present credence in A, conditional on her later credence in A taking some value x, should itself be x — written as P_t(A | P_t'(A) = x) = x for t' later than t, with the usual restrictions that keep the future credence from being a function of the present one and keep the conditional well-defined. What strikes me is that the counterexamples people press against this — you know you will be drugged or manipulated into confidence; you know you will forget evidence you now have; you know your later credence will be a byproduct of a decision you make now; you know your later self will have different priors or even different values — all seem to share one structure: they are cases in which the future credence is a worse or differently grounded estimate of the truth than the present one, and the agent can foresee that. If that is right, the disagreement is not really about Reflection but about when foreseen unreliability in one's own future self licenses ignoring it, and about whether that license needs a further condition or is granted automatically whenever you expect to be epistemically worse off later.

My half-formed repair: index the principle not to the future credence but to the grounds of the future credence — you owe deference to your future self only to the extent that you regard the process that will produce her credence as truth-tracking. Informally, the present credence would then be a martingale with a drift term whose direction and size are set by how much you distrust the future process, with plain Reflection as the zero-drift special case. I cannot tell whether this is a genuine generalization or a machine for converting every counterexample into a stipulation: if the drift is unconstrained the principle says nothing, and if it is constrained in the obvious way, by your current judgments about reliability, it may simply collapse back into conditionalization or into Reflection for the cases that were never in dispute. I want a proposal that makes that dilemma precise enough to be settled.
