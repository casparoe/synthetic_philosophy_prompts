I am a graduate student in philosophy working on a paper about the conditions of valid consent, and I have been arguing with a friend who is a lawyer. We keep talking past each other about a case in which a person has full competence, is not deceived about the non-moral facts, and is not coerced, but is mistaken about the moral status of the act to which they are agreeing. For example, they believe that undergoing a particular medical procedure is morally permissible when it is in fact morally obligatory, or they believe that a sexual act is morally permissible when it is in fact morally wrong. By moral understanding, I mean a correct grasp of whether the act is morally permissible, obligatory, or wrong, and of the moral reasons for and against it; by factual understanding, I mean a correct grasp of the non-moral features of the act, its consequences, and the alternatives. The question is not whether it would be wrong for anyone to rely on such consent, nor whether the person is blameworthy for their ignorance, but whether the consent itself is valid—whether it succeeds in altering the rights and permissions that govern the act.

Let P be the proposition that valid consent requires moral understanding, and not merely factual understanding. My credence that P is true is 0.35; my friend's is 0.7. Please give me your own credence, but more importantly decompose the question. I want rough probabilities for the sub-questions you think P depends on, with a line of justification for each, and an account of how they combine into your final number. At a minimum, I would like you to consider: whether consent is best analysed as a waiver of rights, a transfer of permission, or a communication of a mental state; whether the validity of consent is fixed by the consenting party's subjective understanding or by an objective standard of what they ought to have understood; whether moral ignorance is a species of incompetence, a species of non-culpable false belief, or a species of deception; whether the moral status of an act is part of the material facts that must be understood for consent to be valid; whether requiring moral understanding would make valid consent impossible or excessively demanding in morally contested domains; and whether the answer varies across different kinds of act, such as medical procedures, sexual relations, research participation, and contracts.

Then tell me which sub-question your final credence is most sensitive to, which term you are least confident in, and what evidence or argument would move you most. Finally, given my 0.35 and my friend's 0.7, tell me where our disagreement most likely lives—whether we are disagreeing about the analysis of consent, about the epistemology of moral ignorance, about the moral status of the acts in question, or about something else entirely. Whilst I do not need citations or reading lists, I want your reasoning in your own words. Please answer in British English.
