Ok so I keep running into this way of explaining the language of thought hypothesis, and I want to stress-test it. The pitch goes: mentalese is the machine code of the brain. Thought runs on this inner code, and the words we actually speak are just the high-level interface — the GUI sitting on top of the operating system. A friend of mine leans on this hard. He says it proves thinking can't be happening in English or any natural language, because the inner code has to be up and running before you can learn a natural language at all, the same way the CPU has to be executing instructions before any program you wrote can run. He also says mentalese is like the instruction set you're born with, while natural languages are just apps you download later.

I get the rough shape of it, but how far does the analogy actually hold? Lay out the correspondence explicitly for me — what plays the role of the machine code, the compiler, the hardware, the GUI, the programmer — and then tell me where the mapping breaks. I'm especially curious whether the analogy can really carry the strong conclusion that thought is not in natural language, or whether it only supports something weaker, like the claim that thought is systematic and compositional (if you can think one thought, you can think its structural variants) and that natural language just piggybacks on that. And are there features of machine code that just dont transfer — that it's arbitrary, that it's meaningless to the hardware, that it could be swapped out for a completely different instruction set, that you can't introspect it? If those don't transfer, does the analogy quietly smuggle in a little programmer or a homunculus that the theory is supposed to do without?

And if the analogy can't carry the strong conclusion, what's a better picture of the thought-language relationship — or is there just no good single picture and we should drop the metaphor?

One request: please be careful to separate what's actually settled among people who work on this from what's genuinely contested, and dont hand me a contested reading as if it were the consensus. American English, please.
