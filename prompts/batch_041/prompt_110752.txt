I'm in a theory of knowledge class and we've just spent two weeks on bootstrapping and easy knowledge, and I've come out of it with a pile of gut reactions that I suspect can't all be right, so I'd like you to tell me what principle, if any, they add up to.

Gas gauge first. My car's gauge has been right for as long as I've had the car; I glance at it, it says a quarter tank, I know I need gas. That's completely ordinary knowledge and I feel no circularity in it at all. But when I picture actually checking the gauge's reliability — read it, see the tank is where it says, conclude 'so the gauge was accurate this time,' do that at a bunch of fuel levels, then announce 'my gauge is reliable' — I think that's rubbish, I haven't learned anything about the gauge that way. Vision seems the same shape. I look at the wall and I know it's red, and honestly I also think it's fine for me to conclude from that that my color vision is working, right now, on this wall. But if I look at fifty walls, note each time that the wall looks red and the wall is red, and conclude my color vision is reliable in general, that strikes me as circular and I don't think I get to know it. Those two can't both be right, I think, since the only difference is one wall versus fifty and 'right now' versus 'in general.' There's a cousin case I feel worse about: I know the table is red by looking, but I don't think looking at it has taught me anything about whether the lighting is normal rather than red-tinted, even though 'the table is red' seems to entail that a white table isn't being made to look red.

Memory: I remember eating eggs, so I know I had eggs, fine, and I think I also get to know my memory is working at the moment, but I don't think I can bootstrap my way to 'my memory is reliable' by remembering things and checking my memories against other memories. Testimony: my flatmate has told me a hundred things and I've checked most of them against stuff I could see for myself, so I do think I know he's reliable, and that doesn't feel circular at all, since he wasn't the one doing the checking. But some guy who's only ever told me one thing, that the station is that way, and it is — I know where the station is now, but I don't think I've learned he's reliable.

My tentative principle, tell me if it survives: your allowed to take the deliverance of a faculty at face value, since that's just what using it is, but you can't use a faculty to certify that same faculty, because then the thing under scrutiny is doing all the work. The problem is that this seems to kill the single-wall case, which I definately want to keep, and I can't tell in that case whether my color vision is being used or being certified. It also makes me nervous about 'I have a hand, so I'm not a brain in a vat,' which I do think is cheating, though I can't say what makes it cheating when the gas gauge is fine.

What I want back: the principle, or two or three competing candidates, stated precisely enough that I could actually test them; the case that motivates each clause; what each predicts about cases I haven't listed; and an honest note about which of my verdicts it can't accommodate, plus whether a clause could save my own principle and what that costs. Please write it as continuous prose — no headings, bullets, bold, or tables, just argue it out the way you'd write an email, I read this stuff better as an argument than as a list.
