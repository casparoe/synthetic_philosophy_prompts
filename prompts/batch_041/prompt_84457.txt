Me and my friend were argueing until 2am about this and I need a third party. He claims he can prove we're all logically omniscient and I couldnt find the hole. Here's his argument, tidied up:

(1) Knowing a claim just means having ruled out every possibility where it's false -- if your evidence leaves no room for it to be wrong, you know it.
(2) Take a tautology: either there's a prime bigger than the biggest one I can write down, or there isn't. There's no possible situation where that comes out false.
(3) So there's nothing to rule out. Nothing, vacuously, has been ruled out by any evidence whatsoever -- which means anyone who understands the sentence knows it.
(4) Now say I know p, and p can't be true unless q is. Every situation where q fails is a situation where p fails, and I've already ruled those out. So I know q.
(5) Repeat that, and everything that follows from anything I know, I know -- plus everything that follows from that.
(6) So I know every logical truth and every consequence of everything I know. Which means I know the rules of chess, and the rules of chess settle whether White wins with best play, so I already know who wins -- I just haven't sat down and noticed. Same deal with every theorem anybody will ever prove, including the ones that eat a whole career. Math can't teach me anything I didn't already know, and neither can logic.

Then he pushed further and said this shows a rational person has to be certain of every theorem of arithmetic, so being unsure whether some unproven conjecture is even provable is straight up irrational.

Obviously something's off. But where? I'd rather not throw out (1), because I don't know what knowledge would even be then, and I really don't want to throw out (4) -- a proof is just a chain of (4)s, so if closure breaks I don't see how proving anything ever tells me something new, which is the whole point of doing it. Tell me which step to give up, why it looked fine, and what it actually costs to give it up -- and if you think there's a seperate flaw hiding in the vacuous move in (3), say which one is doing the real work.
