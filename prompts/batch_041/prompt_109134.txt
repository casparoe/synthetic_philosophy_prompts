My friend thinks the argument is more or less airtight and that anyone who wants to keep mental causation has to give up a premise and pay the price. Here's my list of escape routes, in the words I've been using them. 1) Identity theory: the mental property just is the physical property, so there's no competition, one cause wearing two labels. 2) Supervenience non-reduction: mental properties are distinct from and supervene on the physical, but the physical realizer is still sufficient, so the mental is a rider. 3) Emergence: the higher-level property is genuinely new and carries causal powers the base doesn't have, and we just take that as brute. 4) Overdetermination: the belief and the neural event each cause the hand to move, each sufficient on its own. 5) Epiphenominalism: the mental is real but causally dead. 6) Downward causation: the mental state reaches down and alters its own physical base. 7) The difference-making / different-question route: causation is about what makes a difference rather than about pushing, so the belief counts as a cause even with a complete neural story, because the two are answers at different grains. 8) Something I made up and have been calling the shunting view: the neural realizer only has the powers it has because of what it realizes, so the mental adds no push but also isn't a dangler — more like the rule that makes a move legal, it doesn't move the piece, but the piece doesn't move that way without it.
