What I'm going to say, roughly: you flash a red dot on the left, then a blue dot on the right, quick, like a fifth of a second apart. You don't see two dots. You see one dot that travels across and turns from red to blue somewhere in the middle. But it couldn't of turned blue in the middle — the blue dot wasn't on screen yet. So the standard two stories are: (a) my brain let me have the experience of a colour change and then went back and rewrote the timestamp, or (b) my brain fixed the whole thing up before it ever got to me, so I never actually experienced a change happening at the middle, it only feels that way afterwards. I call them the re-edit and the pre-edit.

My actual claim, which is the bit I want you to attack: both of those are wrong, and so is the idea that the brain is sneaking around lying to us. I think the colour change is real, I really do see it, but it never happens *at* a moment. It's like a smudge on a windscreen — you can point at the smudge, but there's no line where the smudge starts. Asking "when did I see it turn blue" is like asking which step of a staircase a ramp is on. And I think you can tell I'm right because the moment people report moves around depending on how you ask them: press someone for a number and they'll hand you a number, but that number is a decision, not a readout.
