I am a retired experimental physicist who has spent the past few years reading philosophy and decision theory, and I have run aground on the Kelly criterion.

The claim I keep encountering is that the Kelly rule for sizing bets — which, as I understand it, directs one to maximise the expected value of the logarithm of one's wealth — is not merely a defensible convention but the uniquely rational policy, so that anyone who bets more or less than Kelly is simply mistaken. This puzzles me. Expected-utility theory is ordinarily presented as licensing any utility function, or at least any concave one, and the logarithm is only one concave function among many; if I prefer to maximise the expected square root of my wealth, or the expected logarithm less a penalty for variance, precisely what is my error? When I press the point I am told that Kelly maximises the long-run rate of growth of wealth, and that this is a theorem rather than a preference. Yet the only result I have been able to reconstruct for myself is that Kelly maximises the expected logarithm, which is where I began; and although I see that in a multiplicative process the logarithm converts growth into a sum, an algebraic identity is not an argument. I am also told that the criterion is geometric, because it maximises the geometric mean of final wealth, but the geometric mean of a random variable is by definition the exponential of the expected logarithm, so this too appears to be a restatement rather than an explanation. Having spent a career distinguishing the average over an ensemble of systems from the average over time in a single system, I suspect that the phrase long-run growth is doing something equivocal here, and that the theorem concerns a time average whose relation to the expected logarithm rests on assumptions I have never seen set out.

Is the Kelly criterion anything more than expected-utility maximisation with one particular, and frankly arbitrary, concave utility function, dressed up in the language of growth?

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question. Please write in British English.
