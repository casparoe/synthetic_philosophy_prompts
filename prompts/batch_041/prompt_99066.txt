Compare the unilateralist's curse — the way a bunch of agents who each figure some action is probably fine will, just by acting independantly, end up with someone actually doing it — with the "problem of many hands", where a bad result comes from lots of people each adding a small, individually harmless contribution and no single one being decisive. Are these really the same phenomenon seen from opposite ends, or do they come apart? I'm most interested in where they'd prescribe different fixes, say in a case where one actor's move is by itself enough to cause the harm versus one where the harm only shows up once many small moves stack up. And tell me which of the two is harder to pin responsibility on, and why. Throw in a quick thought about what changes if the agents can watch each other before acting — does the first case start to look like the second?
