I recently began volunteering with a local habitat-restoration trust, and the reading I have done since — on the welfare of wild animals, and on the arithmetic of lives lived versus lives never begun — has unsettled something I had long taken for granted: that destroying a habitat is straightforwardly bad for the animals in it. Next month I am to lead a ninety-minute session on this question for the trust's volunteers, non-specialists who are committed conservationists and who assume both that nature is a balanced whole and that fewer animals must mean less suffering. I want a plan for the explanation, not the explanation itself. Please reply with exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, and no introduction or conclusion: first, the order in which the ideas must be introduced and what depends on what; second, the single example that should carry the session and the properties it must have and lack; third, the misconceptions the group will bring or form, in the order they will surface, and the point in the sequence at which each must be headed off; fourth, the question I should ask at the end to tell whether they have understood the question rather than merely been made uncomfortable; and fifth, what I should deliberately leave out at this level and say plainly that I am leaving out.
