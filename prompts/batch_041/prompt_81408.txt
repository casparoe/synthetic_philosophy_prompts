I am trying to build a procedure that sorts any action into one of three boxes — forbidden, required, or beyond the call — and the third box keeps collapsing into one of the other two. Before I commit to a theory of supererogation, write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of it has to deliver. Give the full list — the phenomena it must fit, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it may not erase, and the results it must remain consistent with — with the case or consideration behind each requirement. Keep at least these in view: an act that is genuinely optional yet morally better than the act one was required to perform; the blamelessness of simply not doing it; an act that costs the agent nothing and still counts as going beyond; two agents who can each contribute only a fragment of a good that neither is required to complete; and the drift by which what was once beyond duty becomes duty as it grows easier. Then say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the prejudices of one camp, and flag any pair you believe cannot be jointly satisfied. That last part is what I most want; skip the scorecard of existing views.
