Please construct a thought experiment to the following specifications, then demonstrate that your case meets each one.

Background. I want to pry apart two demands that a theory of content might place on a representational state in a symbol-using system. Call the first referential grounding: the state's tokening is reliably and counterfactually tied to some property or individual in the system's environment, so that it is produced when the relevant condition obtains and withheld when it does not, and would track that condition across a range of nearby circumstances. Call the second conceptual grounding: the state occupies a position in a wider network of states whose transitions mirror the inferential and practical relations among the worldly conditions those states purport to be about, so that the system can deploy the state in reasoning, prediction, and action in ways that respect how the target conditions actually hang together. The question I care about is whether referential grounding by itself is enough for content, and the case is meant to isolate that question: it should show referential grounding present while conceptual grounding is absent, with nothing else varying.

Specifications. Build one realistic scenario. No science fiction, no stipulated phenomenology or consciousness, no appeal to a hidden meaning faculty, and no reliance on the system being a mere lookup table or a fixed input-output map. The agent may be a machine, an animal, or a person, but you may not simply announce that it lacks understanding; the absence of conceptual grounding must fall out of facts you state about how the state is produced and how it is used. Keep the scenario to a single paragraph. It must be the kind of thing one could encounter in a laboratory, a workplace, or a home rather than in a philosopher's stipulation.

Then deliver the following.

1. Identify the state, the target property or individual, and the mechanism that secures referential grounding, with enough detail that I can see the counterfactual dependence is real rather than asserted.
2. Explain precisely which feature of the system's architecture or environment blocks conceptual grounding, and why that feature blocks it rather than merely being correlated with its absence.
3. Name the single feature of the case that is doing the work, and specify the minimal change that would flip the verdict, so that I can see the case is engineered rather than gerrymandered.
4. Give the strongest reply available to a defender of a use-based or inferential-role account of content. Such a defender will either argue that the state is after all conceptually grounded, or that referential grounding was never genuinely achieved. Say which line is more promising here and why.
5. State whether the case can be reversed, so that a state is conceptually but not referentially grounded, and if it cannot be, say what about the two demands makes the reversal impossible.

I am not asking for a survey of the literature, and the case should stand on its own terms: a reader who knows no theories of content should be able to evaluate it, and the verdicts should be generated by the mechanisms you describe rather than by stipulation.
