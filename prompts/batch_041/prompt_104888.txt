I keep having alot of different gut reactions to whether something is a code or just carries information, and I want a principle that fits. The standard genetic code: definately a code -- the mapping from codons to amino acids is arbitrary and could have been otherwise, and the cell uses it to build proteins. A hormone binding a receptor: not a code, just a signal, even though it carries information about blood sugar. A methyl mark on DNA that silences a gene: signal, not code. But a prion converting another protein to its own shape: I want to say that's code-like, because the shape is copied and determines the next one's shape, even though there's no arbitrary table. And a computer program: code. A book: not code, even though it's arbitrary symbols that cause effects. A ribosome: not code, it's a machine. I also think RNA editing that changes the message after transcription is still code, but the fact that a protein's folded shape is caused by its sequence doesn't make the sequence a code for the shape. Is there a single principle here? A mate suggested it's about whether there's a re-mappable convention plus a decoder, but that seems to make the prion case not code, which I don't like. What principle gets most of these, what does it say about a case where a neuron's firing pattern is copied by a downstream neuron, and which verdict would I have to give up? No need for references, just the principle and the cases.
