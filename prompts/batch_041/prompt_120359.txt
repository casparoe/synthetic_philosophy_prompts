I retired a few years back after thirty-odd years as an industrial chemist, and I've been spending the retirement reading philosophy, mostly ethics, which I never took a single course in. I keep circling back to Anscombe and Truman. As I understand it she basically called him a murderer over the bombings, and made a stink at the time about him getting an honorary degree, which I admire even though I'm not sure I agree with her.

Heres what's bugging me. In my old job we talked about products and byproducts constantly, and which is which is a fact about the reaction, not about what I was hoping for. If a byproduct comes off at 40 percent I have to deal with it exactly as if I'd been aiming at it. So the whole intention-versus-foresight thing in ethics looks to me like it might be doing something completely different from what I'd naturally assume, and I want to see the difference pried apart.

So build me a thought experiment to order. I want two agents who do the exact same physical thing, with the exact same knowledge and the same goals, but where the causal structure of the world differs: in one, a person's death is the lever that produces the good outcome, and in the other it's an effect that comes along with the good outcome and could in principle have been avoided but isn't. Same death, same number of lives saved, same everything either agent knows. Constraints: realistic (a hospital, a factory, a lab -- no trolleys, no bombers, no runaway anything, and nobody is a cartoon villain), one paragraph, and dont tell me who intends what, build it so that the description is forced by the mechanism rather than stipulated. The person who dies shouldnt be a bad guy or a volunteer, just somebody in the wrong place. And spare me the terror bomber versus tactical bomber pair and the organ donor, I've seen those.

Then do the work. What does Anscombe-style absolutism about intentionally killing the innocent say about each of the two agents, what does plain act-consequentialism say, and what does a standard double-effect theorist say. I especially want to know whether the double-effect verdicts come out the same or different for the two agents, because depending on whether you read "intends" off the world or off the agent's plan I suspect you get different answers, and I want to know which of those two readings Anscombe's position actually needs.

Finish with three things: which single feature of the case is doing all the work; what happens to the whole thing if I remove that feature, say by making the two agents' plans differ instead of the worlds mechanism; and how a committed defender of double effect would attack the case rather than accept the verdict.
