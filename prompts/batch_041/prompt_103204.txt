I run a small decision-theory reading group drawn mostly from the rationalist blogs, and I want a decision tree that carries a reader from a single starting commitment — the choices of agents elsewhere in a large world are correlated with mine, and that correlation is enough to make my choice evidence about theirs — to a named position on whether and how to cooperate with them. Build the nodes from the questions that genuinely split the views rather than from a list of labels: whether the correlation has to run through a shared decision algorithm or can run through shared evidence alone; whether the correlated agents share my values or only my predicament; whether I should pick the act that is best given what my picking it tells me about them, or the act that is best given what a policy of picking it would bring about everywhere; whether the relevant chooser is the particular agent deliberating or the whole class of correlated choosers; and whether cooperation needs any reciprocity at all, or only that the others are running the same reasoning. Phrase every node as a question a reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and put at each leaf the name of the position — the ones usually called superrationality, acausal trade, updateless or functional decision theory, and the plainer causal view that treats my copies as strangers — or "unnamed" where nothing standard fits, each with one sentence on the commitment that makes it uncomfortable. Flag any leaves the tree cannot separate, and note which combinations of answers look unoccupied and whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Aim for eight to twelve leaves.
