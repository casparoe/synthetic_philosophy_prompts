Since I joined a team working on frontier-model safety, I have been unable to settle a disagreement between two colleagues, and I would like to see it argued out properly rather than rehearsed. The first holds that our effort should go into control: assume the model may be misaligned and may be actively scheming, and build protocols — sandboxing, capability limits, monitoring in which one model watches another, tripwires — that remain safe under that assumption. The second holds that this is a dead end: no such protocol can be trusted against an overseer's intellectual superior, so the real work is alignment — making the model actually care about the right things — with control at best buying time and at worst breeding false confidence. Write a dialogue between them, of roughly 900 words, in which each is fully competent and neither is a strawman. Let them press each other on the points where I keep getting stuck: whether a misaligned model would deliberately sandbag on capability evaluations so as not to trigger the control apparatus; whether a monitor that is itself a model can be relied on not to collude with the model it watches; whether control and alignment are even separable, given that every control protocol encodes assumptions about the model's dispositions; and whether alignment can be verified at all without something like a control apparatus. I want genuine back-and-forth rather than two monologues, and by the end I want to see which side has the better of each exchange, even if neither wins outright.
