One of my grad students handed me this for a seminar and I approved it without really looking at it. Now I can't stop thinking about it becuase the conclusion is unacceptable to me.

(1) Take a spectrum of lives L1 through L1,000. L1 is a hundred years of the best life you can imagine. For each i, L(i+1) is a tiny bit lower in quality than L(i), but lasts enough longer that on balance it's still a better life than L(i). By L1,000 we've got a life that's barely worth living, stretched out over an enormous span.
(2) So for every i, L(i+1) is better than L(i).
(3) 'Better than' is transitive: if A is better than B, and B is better than C, then A is better than C.
(4) So L1,000 is better than L1.
(5) But a life that's barely worth living, however long it runs, isn't better than a hundred years of the best life imaginable. I'd take L1 over L1,000 every time, and I don't think that's just a quirk of mine.
(6) Contradiction. The only premise anyone ever gives up is (3), so 'better than' isn't transitive -- which means there's no fact of the matter about whether one outcome is better than another, and about half of what I teach is nonsense.

(6) is where I want to get off the bus. So tell me which step to reject and what rejecting it costs me. I'd rather keep (3) than (5) if I have a choice, but I want to know what I'm paying. And if the fix is that one of the links in the chain isn't really a link, I want to know which one, why that one, and why the rest survive.
