I'm a control engineer — day job is stochastic control, Kalman filters, cost-to-go, sensor fusion, that whole world. My vocabularly is basically that world and not much else. I keep bumping into a philosophy result and I want it translated into my language, warts and all.

The result: I. J. Good, 1967, "On the Principle of Total Evidence" (not asking you to look it up, just working from the content). If you're a Bayesian expected-utility maximizer and someone hands you free information before you have to act, you should always take it — the expected value of information can't be negative. You take the info, condition on it, then pick your best action, and in expectation you can't do worse than acting blind. The philosophy around it is mostly the caveats: what happens when the info isn't free, when your preferences depend on what you believe (you'd rather not learn you're sick), when the agent isn't a clean expected-utility maximizer, when the info is about your own future choice, that kind of thing.

What I want you to do: restate that whole package — theorem plus caveats — in the vocabulary I actually use. State, observation channel, estimator, policy/controller, cost-to-go, value function, certainty equivalence, separation principle, and the fact that in stochastic control a control action both steers the state and tells you something about it. As faithfully as you can, not as a loose metaphor.

Then the part I actually care about, the residue report. Which pieces land cleanly in my vocabulary? Which ones I have to bend my machinery to hold at all? And which ones have no expression in my world? In particular: is "information is never bad" for me a substantive theorem, or is it just already baked into how I set up an optimal-control problem? And if it's baked in, does that mean the philosophers are arguing about less than it looks, or does my framework just not have the words for whatever they're really disagreeing over? Keep the translation honest — don't quietly smuggle in my cost-functional worldview and call it a translation. The stuff that doesn't survive is the interesting part.
