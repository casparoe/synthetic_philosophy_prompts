I teach an upper-level seminar in philosophy of mind and language, and I am drafting the final examination. I need a single essay question on Twin Earth and semantic externalism that cannot be passed by reciting that meaning is not in the head, nor by reproducing the standard H2O/XYZ intuition in either direction. The question should compel students to separate three things that the standard example leaves fused: the environment at the moment a term is used, the environment at the moment the term was introduced into the community, and the chain of deference along which the term travels from the speakers who use it to whatever it is that fixes its reference.

To give the question a crux that students cannot have memorized, build it on the following case, and state the case in full within the question itself. Twin Earth is as Putnam described it, with one addition: the twin community's seas were composed of XYZ for most of their history and were converted to H2O by a natural process a few generations ago, no one having noticed the change. The twins' word for the watery stuff was introduced while the substance surrounding them was XYZ, and every use of it today is of a substance that is H2O. Ask the students to determine what that word refers to on a twin speaker's lips today, and then to say what their answer shows about whether externalism is best read as an environmental thesis, a causal-historical thesis, or a social-deference thesis, and whether the case exposes a genuine indeterminacy in the theory or merely a distinction the theory is obliged to draw.

Please give me: first, the essay question itself, worded so that a prepared student could answer it in ninety minutes; second, a model answer of roughly 700 words that takes a definite line rather than surveying the options; third, a marking scheme worth 25 marks, built from five must-hit points with the weight of each, and for each point a one-line note on the error that typically costs a student that point.

Constraints: the question and the model answer must not require citations, reading lists, or references to particular publications; philosophers should be named only where a student in such a seminar would already be expected to know the name. Everything the question demands must be answerable from the standard seminar material on externalism together with the case as stated.
