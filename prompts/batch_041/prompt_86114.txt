I'm moderating a Q&A for a student paper series, and next weeks guest defends a view she calls the rising critical level: the repugnant conclusion is avoided, she says, because the welfare level a life has to beat to be worth adding isnt fixed (the usual version just sets it once and leaves it) but climbs as the total population grows. Draft me eight or nine questions that actually get into that, rather than making her restate why the repugnant conclusion is repugnant. I definitely want one on whether the view buys its escape only by landing you somewhere equally unwelcome in the other direction -- a huge population of lives that are clearly worth living getting ranked below a tiny population of wonderful ones, so the view tells us to stop creating happy people. One on whether a life can be good for the person living it while counting as bad overall just because enough other people happen to exist, and what that does to the idea that a life's value is a fact about the life. And one on the shape of the rising threshold: what the view implies if it climbs fast, what if it climbs slowly, and whether any version is actually stable. Press her too on whether it needs an impersonal betterness relation or whether a person-affecting version is even possible. Give a follow-up for each question, keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. And in the questions themselves be careful to separate what's actually settled in population axiology from what's genuinely contested -- don't hand me a contested line as the consensus.
