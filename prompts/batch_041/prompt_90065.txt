OK so I'm a retired ecologist -- 35 years counting things, mostly cicadas and the occasional salamander -- and since I hung up the clipbord I've been reading a lot of philosophy, mostly the metaphysics stuff, and I've gotten a bit obessed with the fight over what a law of nature actually is. The Humean side says a law is just the best summary of what happens, full stop, nothing extra holding the pattern together; the other side says no, the law does something, it governs, the regularities hold because of it. I keep thinking about my 13-year cicadas, how the pattern comes around so tight it feels like it *must* be a rule, and then I remember it might just be... the pattern.

I want a poem about this. Here's what I want the form to do the heavy lifting: give me a pantoum -- the one where the 2nd and 4th lines of each stanza come back as the 1st and 3rd of the next, and the last stanza loops back into the first. I want the poem readable two ways at once: one where the returning lines are just lines that happen to return (the pattern is all there is) and one where it feels like the form is *making* them return (something is holding them in place). Please don't resolve it. I don't want a poem that tells me which side wins -- I want one where the looping itself is the whole argument, so that a Humean and an anti-Humean could both read it and each feel it was written for them. Four or five stanzas is plenty.

Keep the language plain, I'm not a poetry guy, but I'd like it to be right -- if a philosopher read it I don't want them to wince.
