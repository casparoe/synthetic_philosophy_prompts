I spent most of last night arguing with a close friend about a decision we had to make together, both of us reasonably informed and neither of us willing to update, and I came away convinced that the way people wave around the Aumann agreement theorem does far more work than the theorem can actually support. My half-formed thought is that the result is really a claim about priors rather than about disagreement: it tells you that once the priors are common and the updating is public, disagreement cannot survive, so any live disagreement must be hiding entirely in the priors — and the genuinely interesting question, whether two people should have the same priors or whether a persistent difference in priors is a rational failing rather than just a fact about where each of them started, is left completely untouched. I also suspect the result quietly needs common knowledge in the strong sense rather than a few levels of mutual knowledge, and that the gap between those two is exactly where real, stubborn disagreement lives.
