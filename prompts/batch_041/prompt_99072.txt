Over the past year I have been drawn into arguments about transgender rights — in a family group chat, at work, and in a reading group — and I keep noticing the same pattern: the exchange runs down the same grooves, no one's position shifts, and each side leaves convinced the other is arguing in bad faith or begging the question. I would like to stop litigating particular cases and instead understand what kind of disagreement this actually is. Consider three disputes. On sports: one side holds that eligibility should track sex, because that is what makes competition fair; the other holds that it should track gender identity, because exclusion is the real harm. On single-sex spaces such as changing rooms and shelters: one side holds that these must be organized by sex to protect privacy and safety; the other holds that they should admit people by gender identity. On youth medicine: one side wants puberty blockers and hormones available to minors who seek them; the other wants them restricted pending stronger evidence. My hypothesis — which I would like tested rather than granted — is that much of the deadlock is not a dispute about facts, or even about values, but about which concept our shared words should express: each side uses 'woman', 'man', or 'safe' to mean something different, then hears the other's sentences as false when they are really proposals about how the word ought to be used. I also suspect both sides treat the empirical questions — do blockers help or harm, do trans women have an athletic advantage — as decisive, when settling them would leave the central disagreement untouched. So for each of the three disputes, diagnose what is actually at issue: a factual question, a clash of values, a contest over the meaning or proper use of a term, or several of these stacked. Say where a hidden empirical question is doing the real work and where it is not, and tell me what would genuinely count as progress — a shared definition, a distinction, a piece of evidence — or whether this is the kind of disagreement we should not expect to resolve at all.
