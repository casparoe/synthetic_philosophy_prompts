My half-formed idea is about the white horse dialogue and the School of Names more broadly. Here's the thing I keep circling. Everyone treats the claim that a white horse is not a horse as either a dumb sophism or a cute point that horse and white horse name different kinds of thing (ask for a horse and a white one will do; ask for a white horse and a brown one wont). But I suspect the whole family of School of Names puzzles, the white horse one plus the hard-and-white stuff and the pointing-at-things ones, only look paradoxical because we assume a common noun just picks out a fixed class of objects and that the word doing the is-work is unambiguous. My hunch is these texts are really poking at what happens to nouns, classifiers, and the copula when you dont assume that, and that a lot of the puzzles come out as nearly trivially true, or as puns trading on different senses of is, once you stop importing our assumptions.
