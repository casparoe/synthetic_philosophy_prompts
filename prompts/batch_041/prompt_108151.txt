I am giving a one-hour guest session on the Meno problem to about twenty internal-medicine residents during the ethics block of their second year. None of them has read any Plato, and all of them think about epistemic quality in the vocabulary of their own field: sensitivity, specificity, calibration, base rates. Do not explain the problem to me. Plan how I should explain it.

Give me the order in which the pieces have to arrive, with the dependency made explicit at each step: what has to be in place before the question can even be asked, and why it cannot be asked earlier. Name the single case that will carry the session and say what it must have and what it must lack. The road to Larissa and the statues of Daedalus are spent, and any case built on a lucky guess will let them off too easily. I have been considering a resident who gets the diagnosis right because the previous patient had the same thing; tell me whether that case can bear the weight, and if it cannot, what could.

They will arrive with misconceptions, at least these: that knowledge is true belief with a receipt attached, so that the question is only about certainty; and that knowledge is worth more because it is more likely to be true. Tell me which of these is the real obstacle, where in the sequence each has to be headed off, and what further misconception I should expect that I have not named.

The session has to reach, and not settle, the objection that if truth is what we value, anything added to a true belief either already counts toward the truth or adds nothing; keep separate throughout the question whether knowledge is worth more than true belief and the question whether it is worth something else. Give the two or three strongest replies in a form the residents can state back to me. Close with one question I can put to the room that will show whether they have understood the problem or merely been made uncomfortable by it. Finally, tell me what to leave out at this level and say so out loud in the session.

Write the plan in American English.
