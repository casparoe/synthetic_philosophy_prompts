Ok so I'm a science journalist, I write long features mostly about psychiatry and the weirder corners of philosophy of science, and somehow I've been asked to give a plenary at a big psychiatry conference next month — it's a panel on the future of diagnosis, god help me. I want to argue something thats been bugging me for about a year now. The whole field is racing to make diagnosis dimensional, turning everything into spectra and continuums and "you're a little bit of everything" instead of yes/no categories, and everyone acts like this is obviously more humane and more scientific. My hunch is it actually makes Hacking's looping effect worse, not better. His thing was that when you stamp a category on people they start living up to it and the category itself drifts to match. Fine. But I think a spectrum pulls the same trick more sneakily, because a continuum quietly invites you to locate yourself somewhere on it and then become a person who lives at that spot — and it's harder to resist than a label, because a label at least has a border you can argue with, whereas a gradient just tells you that wherever you land is the real you. I've been calling it the gradient trap, for now.

What I need from you is a Q&A prep sheet. Picture the room: mostly clinicians and clinical researchers, DSM-fluent, most of them think Hacking is a sociologist who's never sat with an actual patient, and they get twitchy when philosophy people tell them their categories aren't real. Then there's two philosophers of psychiatry who know the interactive-kinds stuff inside out and are going to want to know whether my gradient trap is actually a new animal or just looping in a new hat. And there's always one person from the patient / neurodiversity side who is going to hear the whole thing as "so now you're saying we're faking it, but in a spectrum way."

So: give me the eight or so objections I'll really get, ranked by how much damage they do, each one phrased the way that particular person would actually say it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. And include the one question I'm praying nobody asks.
