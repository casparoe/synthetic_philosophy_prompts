I run a small reading group on agency and machines -- mostly grad students in philosophy and law, plus two engineers who keep us honest -- and we've spent three sessions circling the so-called responsibility gap for autonomous systems without getting anywhere, because the discussion keeps collapsing into label-sorting: someone announces they're a "tracing" person or a "distributivist," someone else says the gap is obviously just a liability-insurance problem, and then everyone talks past everyone. I'd like to replace that with a real diagnostic instrument. Please build me a decision tree, or an equivalent taxonomy, that takes a reader from a few plain-English commitments about a concrete case to a named position. Every internal node should be a single question with two or three answer options; every leaf should be a position -- named if it has actual defenders, "unnamed" if not -- carrying one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Build the tree from the logical space first (the questions, then the answer-combinations they permit, then the positions that occupy those combinations), not from a list of famous names, and note which combinations are empty and whether they're empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate rather than papering over it.

The distinctions that matter for our purposes are these. First, whether the alleged gap is a gap in attribution -- nobody satisfies the conditions for being responsible at all -- or a gap in allocation -- somebody is responsible but our institutions can't figure out whom to burden. I've been calling these the gap in the ledger and the gap in the conversation, and I'd like the tree to keep them apart, since I suspect half our disagreements are one person answering the first question and the other answering the second. Second, whether responsibility must terminate in a human individual, or may terminate in a collective, an institution, or a designed process (a rule, an audit trail, an insurance pool). Third, whether the recommended remedy is ex ante -- constrain design, require some form of meaningful human control, restrict or ban deployment -- or ex post -- liability rules, no-fault compensation, after-the-fact attribution to whoever profited. Fourth, whether the gap is a reason to slow or halt deployment, or merely a reason to revise our accounting while deploying anyway. For the concrete cases, please anchor the nodes in: an autonomous surgical robot that selects a plan the supervising surgeon would not have chosen; a lethal autonomous weapon; a high-frequency trading algorithm whose interaction with others triggers a flash crash; and a content-moderation classifier. The tree should let a reader who has never heard the term "responsibility gap" answer every node without knowing which position it leads to, so phrase the nodes as questions about those cases rather than as doctrinal slogans, and keep it to roughly eight to twelve nodes with no more than seven leaves.
