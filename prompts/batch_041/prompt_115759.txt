Here it is. To become a US citizen you have to stand in a room and swear an oath. You renounce all prior allegiance to any other country. You swear to support and defend the Constitution against all enemies. You swear you'll bear arms for the country when the law requires it. And the oath does have an escape hatch for conscience -- but only for religious conscience. If it's your religion that stops you from bearing arms, you can strike that clause and promise to do some form of civilian national service instead. If your objection is philosophical, or political, or just your own personal moral code, it doesn't count and you're stuck with the full oath.

Now compare what's demanded of someone who was just born here. Nothing. Ever. You never swear anything to keep your citizenship, you can legally burn the flag, you can spend your whole life argueing that the country should be dissolved, you can move to Berlin and never come back and give up the citizenship entirely, and nobody thinks the state has any complaint against you. Meanwhile the state is plainly in the business of growing patriotism: monuments, national holidays, museums, July 4th fireworks, history curricula, veterans' days, flag poles on every lawn in town.

So: loyalty is a condition of entry but not a condition of membership. It's publicly cultivated but never legally demanded. And where it is demanded, the only conscience the state recognizes is a religious one.
