I'm a high school student and I'm in debate club, and I keep getting into fights with my cousin about corruption. I want to know what principle, if any, actually fits my gut verdicts. Here are the cases as I see them.

My uncle in our home country gives a envelope of cash to a clerk to get a permit for his shop. Everyone does it, the clerk doesn't hide it, and without it the permit takes six months. I think that's corruption. My other uncle pays a private doctor extra to see my grandma after hours instead of waiting in the public hospital. I think that's fine, even though it's money for faster medical care. A company gives a hamper of wine and chocolate to a procurement officer at Christmas. The officer doesn't have to give them the contract, and the company doesn't say anything about it. I think that's corruption, but milder. A village chief's daughter gets a scholarship that everyone knows is because she's the chief's daughter, and the chief later helps the scholarship board with a land dispute. I think that's corruption. A professor writes a recommendation for her own student who she genuinely thinks is the best. I think that's fine. A congressman takes a big campaign donation from a company, and later votes for a bill the company likes. I think that's corruption, even if the vote is actually good policy. A nurse takes money from a family to move their relative up the queue, but she puts the money into the ward's fund for poor patients. I think that's still corruption. A foreign NGO comes in and says our country is corrupt because officials take gifts, but the NGO hires the director's nephew as a consultant. I think that's hypocritical, but I'm not sure it's the same kind of corruption.

My cousin says the village chief stuff is just clientelism and that Westerners call it corruption because they don't get how families work. I definately don't buy that completely, but I can't say exactly why.

The case I keep going back and forth on is my cousin's wedding. My family gives a lot of money to my cousin's new in-laws, and in return the in-laws' family will support my uncle's business with their connections. Nobody calls it a bribe, it's just how marriage works here, and everyone knows. I don't think it's corruption, but I can't say why it's different from the envelope to the clerk.

I also have a half-baked principle: I think something is corruption when it's hidden and against the rules, and not corruption when it's open and everyone expects it. But that makes the envelope case not corruption, which I don't accept, and it makes the congressman case maybe not corruption if he discloses the donation, which also feels wrong. So that principle is probably dead.

Is there a principle that gets most of my verdicts, or a small set of candidate principles if no single one works? I want it stated precisely, with the case that makes each part of it necessary. Tell me which of my verdicts it has to overturn, and what it predicts for a case I didn't list, like a government that publishes a fee for speeding up a passport and gives the money to a public fund. Also, is the Western standards thing doing any real work here, or am I just assuming my own culture's rules are the default? I'm not asking for citations or reading lists, just the principle and the cases.
