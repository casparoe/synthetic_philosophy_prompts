Right, I'm finally writing up the hedonism paper I've been threatening to write for years, and before I send it out I want someone to tell me whether the package actually hangs together. Here's where I've landed. I'd like an audit, not a summary — which bits pull against which, which only look like they do, how bad each real conflict is, and what the cheapest repair would be.

(1) Welfare hedonism: the only thing that's good or bad for someone in itself is how their experience feels — pleasure and pain in a broad sense. No objective list, no desire-satisfaction rider, no separate good of "meaning".

(2) Commensurability: every pleasure and every pain has a position on one scale of felt goodness/badness, so any two experiences can in principle be compared and some rate of exchange between them stated. When people tell me two experiences are "incommensurable" I hear that as vagueness or ignorence on their part, not as a structural fact about value.

(3) Additivity: welfare aggregates by adding up, with the lexcial rule applied to whole distributions rather than person by person.

(4) The floor: there's some level of suffering — I call it the floor — such that no amount of pleasure, however vast, and no number of happy lives, however large, can compensate for even a little suffering below it. Above the floor, trade-offs are ordinary and gradual; below it, the trade-off is simply off the table. Note this is not global negative utilitarianism: I think pleasure is genuinely good, it just can't buy off agony.

(5) A verdict I'm attached to: I'd refuse a deal that gave a billion people lives of mild contentment at the price of one person spending a day below the floor. But I'd take a deal with a one-in-a-billion chance of that same day, if it secured the billion contented lives with certainty. I'm not going to let a fantastically unlikely disaster veto an enormous certain good.

(6) The floor isn't a stipulation I made because it sounded rhetorically good. It's supposed to be discovered — it falls out of the structure of experience, and a theory that can't find it is failing to describe the phenomenology.

I've had a nagging feeling that two of these are doing incompatible work, but every time I try to pin it down I decide the problem is somewhere else. So: rank the genuine tensions by how much they cost me, say which pairs are only apparently in tension and why they dissolve, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what I'd lose by making it.
