I've got a Venus flytrap on the kitchen windowsill, and last week two mates of mine, Dan and Nadia, got into a proper arguement about it while I just stood there holding the watering can. The thing only snaps shut if its trigger hairs get touched twice in quick succession, so it doesn't waste a snap on a raindrop. Dan said that's a kind of counting: the plant holds onto the first signal and waits for a second before committing, which is doing something for a reason. Nadia said there's nobody in there to have a reason — the mechanism just happens to produce behaviour that's for something. It went a few rounds and I couldn't referee it.

Dan: "'For' isn't just a label we stick on afterwards — the two-tap rule is for not wasting energy on false alarms, and you can't even describe the mechanism without that. If there's a reason the plant does it, why not say the plant has the reason?"

Nadia: "Because the reason isn't in the plant, it's in the history that built it and in us describing it. A thermostat's strip is 'for' holding the room steady, and nobody thinks the thermostat believes anything about the temperature."

Dan: "Then your thermostat line proves too much. We say a heart is for pumping blood, and that's true whether or not anyone designed it. Why is 'for' fine for hearts and eyes but suddenly forbidden the moment a plant is counting taps?"

Nadia: "It's not forbidden, it's just shorthand for what the thing got selected to do — a fact about where it came from, not about what's going on inside it. Saying the flytrap counts is like saying a river wants to reach the sea."

Dan: "But the flytrap isn't a river — it's got a signal that stands in for something else. A second tap means 'probably a real insect this time'. Once you've got something carrying information and a response tuned to it, you've got the beginnings of a reason, just nobody there to entertain it."

Nadia: "By that standard my smoke alarm has the beginnings of a reason to go off at burnt toast. It doesn't; it just goes off."

Can you take this move by move and say who's right about what, rather than just who wins overall — and flag anything the pair of them are both missing? Also say how confident you are in each bit and single out the one point you're least sure about. Please write in British English.
