You are an applied ethicist with serious training in the philosophy of well-being and in welfare economics, and you are drafting a working document for a body that has to make real decisions. I am the philosopher on staff at a national farm animal welfare certification body, and I need you to turn our board's stated ethical framework into a procedure that people without philosophical training can actually run.

Context you should take as given. The board meets weekly, decides by majority vote, and its rulings bind certified producers for five years. Dossiers come from industry scientists and from advocacy groups; they are uneven in quality, they frequently omit exactly the numbers one would want, and nobody on the board except me has read any philosophy. The board has adopted, without much discussion, a framework it calls lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism. Its commitments, which you should treat as fixed and not re-litigate, are these:

- Only suffering and enjoyment count, and both are properties of individual sentient lives. All sentient individuals count, not only humans.
- Below some threshold of suffering, suffering may be traded against enjoyment at a finite exchange rate: it is coherent to say that a quantity of mild suffering is worth a quantity of enjoyment.
- At or above that threshold, no finite quantity of enjoyment compensates for the suffering. Suffering takes lexical priority there. Numbers do not help: no pile of small enjoyments, however large, outweighs even one instance above the line.
- The framework is silent on where the threshold falls, on whether the threshold is a property of a single episode, of a life, or of a population, and on whether pleasure and pain are commensurable in the strong sense that a single exchange rate presupposes.

The translation is the hard part, and I want you to be candid about it. Before writing anything procedural, spend a short section thinking on the page about which distinctions in the framework must survive the translation into a usable procedure, which can be dropped without loss, and where the framework's own silences force the procedure to make a choice that is really a values choice wearing the costume of a criterion. Then produce the following, in order.

First, the procedure itself: numbered steps in a fixed order, each stating what it decides, what input it needs from the dossier, what would count as a sufficient answer, and the conditions under which the procedure stops with a no rather than continuing. Mark unmistakably every step that is a judgment call dressed as procedure. The procedure has to be runnable in about ninety minutes by a non-philosopher, must not require empirical work beyond what is already in the dossier, must say what to do when a needed number is missing, and must yield a written record that can be published alongside the ruling.

Second, a table with exactly these columns: Step | What the step decides | Input it requires | What counts as a sufficient answer | Failure mode (how this step misfires, and on what kind of case) | If the step stalls, who decides. One row per step.

Third, run the procedure on three dossiers and give me, for each, a single JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "case_id": short identifier string
- "case_name": the dossier's name
- "counterfactual_baseline": the comparison state the procedure used, as a string
- "harms": array of objects, each with "description", "affected_population", "intensity_class" ("below_threshold" | "at_or_above_threshold" | "unclassified"), "duration", "evidence_strength" ("strong" | "moderate" | "weak" | "absent")
- "benefits": same structure as "harms"
- "threshold_decision": object with "line_drawn_at" (string), "what_the_line_is_a_property_of" ("episode" | "life" | "population" | "other"), "basis" (string), "confidence" ("high" | "medium" | "low")
- "lexical_step": "blocks_trade" | "does_not_block" | "indeterminate"
- "exchange_rate": object with "applied" (boolean), "rate_or_range" (string), "justification" (string)
- "missing_inputs": array of strings, the numbers the procedure wanted and the dossier did not supply
- "verdict": "permit" | "permit_with_conditions" | "reject" | "return_to_committee"
- "conditions": array of strings
- "steps_reached": array of step numbers or names
- "step_that_decided": string
- "judgment_calls": array of objects with "step", "why_the_procedure_does_not_settle_it", "recommended_decision_maker"
- "what_would_change_the_verdict": array of strings
- "overall_confidence": "high" | "medium" | "low"

The three dossiers are as follows. (A) A proposal to cut floor space per bird below the current certification minimum, on evidence that the resulting airflow sharply reduces the incidence of a severe and sometimes fatal respiratory disease that currently affects a few percent of flocks; the space reduction is expected to cause chronic mild frustration in every bird. (B) A proposal to retain the practice of surgical castration without pain relief in one species, on the grounds that it prevents severe fighting injuries later; the procedure is brief and severe, the injuries it prevents are severe but rarer, and the arithmetic of total suffering-days favours retaining the practice. (C) A proposal that removes a mildly aversive handling step from the routine of a very large population, tens of millions of animals a year, at the cost of a small increase in a rare severe outcome in a much smaller population, a few hundred animals a year, where the aggregate quantity of mild suffering avoided dwarfs the severe suffering added.

Fourth, a closing section of no more than four hundred words on what the procedure cannot do, including a plain statement of whether this framework, correctly applied, would forbid most of what the board currently permits, and which parts of the procedure are load-bearing only because someone has to pick a number.

Write no bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not lean on any named author's formulation: state the reasoning in your own words, so that the document stands on its own for readers with no philosophical background.
