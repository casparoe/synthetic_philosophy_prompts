Here's the position I've ended up holding: interpersonal utility comparisons are objective. There's a fact of the matter about whether my back pain is worse than yours, and it's the same kind of fact as whether my back pain is worse than my back pain was yesterday — not a convention, not a stipulation we adopt so the aggregation math will go through, and not something true only relative to a chosen scale. That's what makes claims about who is worse off in ethics and policy real claims rather than decisions about where to draw a line.
