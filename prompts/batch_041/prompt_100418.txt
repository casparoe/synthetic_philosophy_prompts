Bit of a long one, sorry. I've fell down a hole reading about honour cultures, dignity cultures and victimhood cultures, and I keep arguing with people about whether any of the three is actually morally better or whether that's the wrong question entirely. I want to turn it into a quiz for my book club (clever people, no philosophy degrees) that sorts someone by what they actually believe, not by which label they'd put on themselves.

So what I want is a decision tree or taxonomy of the positions on 'is any of these cultures morally superior?', where every branching point is a question or a concrete case someone could answer without already knowing which position it leads to. Concrete cases please, the kind of thing you'd actually argue about: someone insults you in front of your friends and you can either let it go or make them pay; a colleague says something racially loaded and you can report it, shrug it off, or call it out publicly; a kid films a fight instead of stepping in; a public figure gets shamed into apologising and then un-apologising. Not 'do you think honour matters?'.

Build it from the logic first, then place the views in it. The questions I think actually do the splitting: is standing something you can deserve, or only something other people grant you? Is dignity culture's refusal to retaliate a strength or just a way of letting wrongs go unmarked? Is victimhood culture a genuinely third thing, or dignity culture with the demand for recognition turned up until it becomes a status you compete for? Does a culture have to be a package deal, or can you bolt honour's demand for respect onto dignity's universalism? Then the leaves. I want at least these in there: plain honour culture, dignity or conscience culture, victimhood culture, and that 'face' culture thing I keep seeing mentioned alongside them, where the currency isn't standing or rights but not being made to lose face. Plus whatever hybrid views and whatever 'they're all just different equilibria and ranking them is a category error' view you think are actually held.

Tell me which combinations of answers nobody seems to occupy, and whether that's because the view is incoherent, just unattractive, or simply unexplored. And flag any two leaves your tree can't really pull apart — I'd rather have that admitted than fudged. Keep it to five or six branching questions and six to nine leaves, and dont assume I know any technical vocabulary. British English please, and finish with one line starting 'Bottom line:' with a single sentence on which position, if any, comes out looking best.
