I'm doing a session in my philosophy of mind seminar on levels of description and I want to use The Matrix, mainly because it's the one film my students have all actually seen, and because the two-level structure is sitting right there in the premise instead of being something you have to argue for first. What I want to get at is a thought experiment I've been useing for years and can no longer remember where I got it — the careenium. Picture a big hollow sphere packed with little bouncing balls (they get called simmballs, for reasons that will become clear in a second). The collisions settle into stable, repeating patterns, and those patterns are supposed to be the symbols, i.e. the meaningful states of the thing. The catch is that the ball-level story is already complete: every bounce is fixed by the bounces before it, so the pattern level starts to look like nothing but a redescription of what the balls were doing anyway. But the careenium is supposed to be a mind, and when we explain its behaviour — why it does this rather than that — we cite the symbols and not the balls. So: do the symbols push the substrate around, or is the substrate pushing itself and the symbols are just how all that pushing looks from one floor up? A colleague in cog sci keeps telling me the answer is obviously that the symbols are the real causes and that the film proves it, and I'm not so sure.

So read the film through that. The simulation is the symbol level; the plugged-in bodies and the machines that farm them are the substrate. Where does the film actually stage the question — the way your avatar's abilities track your self-image, the spoon, the prophecy of the One and the fact that the whole resistance is organised around a story it didn't write — and where does it just presuppose an answer? It seems to me the film needs the symbols to be real, because the machines' control is exercised through the content of the simulation and not through the chemistry of the pods; but it also keeps reassuring us that there's a real world underneath doing all the actual work, and then in the sequels it goes murky (the One turns out to be a designed anomaly, and Neo can do things outside the Matrix), which either vindicates the symbols-push side or just shows that every level has another level under it. I'd like an honest map of that, and then the other direction: what does the film give me that the thought experiment doesn't? My hunch is that it's something about power — that in the film, whether the symbolic level is causally real is not a neutral metaphysical question at all, it's the very thing the system is engineered around, and that's a dimension the careenium leaves out. Tell me if that's too neat.

Don't hand me the Plato's cave / Baudrillard reading, they've heard it, and don't worry about whether the film's physics hangs together, I only care about the levels. I'm not after citations, just the reading — and where the film resists the lens, say so rather than forcing it.
