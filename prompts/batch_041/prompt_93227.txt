My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about the utility monster, and I would rather settle the matter with a joke than with another round. I want the joke to turn on the version of the case we actually disagreed about, since the familiar one is too well-worn to be funny. Imagine a being whose advantage lies not in size or appetite but in the shape of its welfare: give it twice the food and its welfare rises fourfold, give it three times and it rises ninefold. It is not cruel; it merely converts any resource into more welfare than the rest of us can. My position was that this is what turns the case into a reductio of total utilitarianism — the total climbs fastest when everything goes to the one being that absorbs it best — while my friend's was that the monster is simply an enthusiastic diner and the real monster is the arithmetic. Write a joke that lands on that disagreement, with a punchline that depends on how the argument actually works (that the utilitarian is serving the total rather than the monster, and that the rest of us enter only as inputs to the monster's welfare), not on the word "monster" or on generic jokes about utilitarians being cold. It should be funny to someone who knows the case well enough to see what the punchline is doing. Keep it to a setup and a punchline, and write it in American English.
