I'm writing a long piece on the fortunes of the idea that a culture's values can be cited as a cause of its economic life, and I keep running into the same non-argument. Take the familiar thesis that ascetic Protestantism helped produce the capitalist work ethic. One camp treats it as an ordinary causal hypothesis to be tested against the record — did the beliefs come first, is the mechanism specified, do the cases where the beliefs were absent show the predicted absence? — and reports that the record doesn't bear it out. A second camp treats it as an interpretation of a meaning-structure and says the demand for difference-making evidence is a category mistake, because the claim on offer was never that kind of claim. A third holds that interests, institutions and selection do the real explanatory work and that the ideas are at most a legitimating gloss. The dispute has now run for over a century, and every round ends the same way, with each side convinced the other has changed the subject; I've stopped believing the disagreement is about the historical facts.

Please act as a philosopher of the social sciences and diagnose the impasse rather than adjudicate the thesis. My own hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the sides keep sliding among three levels — individual motives, aggregate outcomes, and the meaning-structure of an institution — so each side believes the other is answering a different question, and each is right. Consider that possibility seriously, and consider the alternatives: that the parties disagree about what a cause has to be, a difference-maker versus a constitutive condition; that they disagree about whether the content of the beliefs matters or only their social function; or that the question of whether a culture can be a cause is simply ill-posed as posed. Start by brainstorming the candidate diagnoses before committing to one, then argue for the diagnosis you settle on using what each side actually claims, and flag anything you'd need to check before you'd be confident. End by telling me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a counterfactual or formal test, a change of question — or why nothing should be expected to move it.
