I have recently joined a clinical AI ethics committee, and we are trying to write a policy for how a triage nurse should update her beliefs when a diagnostic tool returns a full probability distribution over a partition of patient states rather than a single label. The tool is not a black box in the relevant sense: we know its training distribution and its confusion matrix, and we can therefore compute, for any output distribution q, the likelihood of seeing q in each state. The disagreement on the committee is between two updating policies. The first is the standard rule for uncertain evidence associated with Richard Jeffrey: if the tool's output fixes new probabilities q(E_i) over a partition {E_i}, the nurse's new probability for any proposition A is the q-weighted average of her old conditional probabilities P(A | E_i). The second policy treats the tool's emitting exactly q as itself an event D_q and tells the nurse to ordinary-conditionalize on D_q, using her prior over how the tool generates outputs. Call this policy output-conditionalization. I want a thought experiment that isolates the difference between these two policies and shows why they can come apart even when neither the prior nor the tool is unreasonable.

Please construct a case with the following specifications. First, it must be realistic enough to describe to a hospital committee: a clinic, a call center, a weather desk, or the like; no demons, no infinite partitions, no exotic decision theory. Second, it must be short enough to fit in one paragraph. Third, the two policies must agree on the posterior probabilities of the partition cells themselves—that is, after the update, both assign the same probability to each E_i—but they must disagree about the posterior probability of a downstream proposition H that cuts across the partition. Fourth, the disagreement must not be generated by making the tool obviously miscalibrated or by giving the nurse a bizarre prior; it should fall out of the structure of uncertain evidence and the fact that output-conditionalization can use information about how q was generated that Jeffrey conditionalization discards. Fifth, the case should make clear whether the disagreement disappears if the tool's output is replaced by a certain observation of one of the E_i. After the scenario, do the formal check: state the partition, the prior, the output distribution q, the likelihoods, and compute the posterior for H under each policy. Then identify the single feature of the case that does the work, say what happens if that feature is removed, and explain how a defender of the policy that comes out worse would attack the case. Finally, tell me which policy the case supports, if either, for the committee's purposes, and why. I am not asking for a literature review or citations; work from the definitions above.
