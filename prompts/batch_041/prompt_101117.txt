So this is a slightly odd thing to want a number on, but I've been having a long-running argument with the three people I share a house with, and it's somehow turned into an argument about what a convention even is. Background: we have an unwritten rule that whoever cooks dinner doesn't do the washing up — the others clear up. It just sort of happened, nobody agreed to it, and it could just as easily have gone the other way (cook cleans, everyone else cooks) and it'd work about as well. But now it's fraying: two of us have started doing the pans anyway when it was a big meal, so it's not like everyone conforms anymore, and the newest housemate genuinely didn't know about it for her first month and nobody corrected her. My one housemate says that means it was never really a rule at all; I say a convention can survive people free-riding a bit around the edges.

What I want is your credence that this arrangement is a convention in Lewis's strict sense — not just "a social norm", the actual technical thing — with the reasoning broken out. How much weight do you put on each of: (a) whether there's real universal conformity right now, given the pans situation; (b) whether everyone actually *expects* everyone else to do it, rather than merely assuming they probably will; (c) whether the cook-cleans arrangement is a live alternative that people would switch to if the option were put to them, versus something nobody has ever really considered; (d) whether people go along out of interest — they'd prefer to wash up *given* that others are cooking — versus out of the fear of looking like a freeloader; and (e) the common-knowledge requirement, which is where I suspect the whole thing dies, since "everyone knows that everyone knows that everyone knows..." is a much stronger claim than anything that's actually true around a shared kitchen. Give me a number for each term, say how you'd combine them into the overall credence, then give the overall credence. Then tell me which single term is doing the most work — if I could go find out the truth about just one of them, which would move your number most, and in which direction. And if your verdict is "not strictly a convention, but close in these particular respects", say what the nearest thing is that it *is*, and what observation about the house would most cleanly settle whether it was ever one.
