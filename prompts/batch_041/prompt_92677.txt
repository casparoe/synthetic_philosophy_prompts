I have been having panic attacks for some months now, and in trying to explain them to a therapist and to a friend who works on affect, I have realised that I do not know what I think an emotion is; the three of us appear to be answering different questions. I would like to build a "which theory of emotion do you actually hold?" quiz for the students in a course I am teaching, so that they can discover their commitments from what they say about cases rather than from the labels they happen to like. Please write eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options: a panic attack in which the bodily surge plainly precedes any thought about what is threatening; a patient with a spinal cord injury whose grief is blunted but still unmistakably grief; a person afraid of a snake she knows to be harmless; a driver who swerves before registering the truck and only reports fear afterwards; someone who names a feeling for which her language has a word and mine does not; an infant who startles at a loud noise; grief at the death of a fictional character; a mood of dread with no object; and someone who says she is furious while reporting no felt anger at all. Phrase every option so that it can be answered without knowing which theory it leads to, and let no question simply ask whether emotions are judgments, perceptions, or feelings. Then give a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the main positions — the bodily/feeling theory, cognitive appraisal, the judgment or attitudinal view, the perceptual view, the basic-emotion view, psychological constructionism, and one deflationary option denying that emotion names a single kind — naming each resulting profile by its content, and where two positions would answer identically across the whole quiz, flag them as positions the instrument cannot separate rather than papering over the gap.
