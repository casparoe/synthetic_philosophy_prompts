I have a set of gut verdicts about aggregating welfare, and I want to know what principle, if any, they add up to. (1) A world of ten billion people living well is not improved by adding one being—a utility monster—whose sadistic pleasures, drawn from torturing those people, exceed in total everything they lose; I would call the result flatly worse, and say that the sum of welfare is not what matters. (2) But a being with an enormous capacity for a kind of delight that costs no one anything and leaves everyone else's life untouched: adding it seems to me a real gain, and I will not say its joy counts for nothing just because it is vast. (3) A single being in agony far more intense than anything a human can feel: I think relieving it is worth more than relieving a million people of their moderate, longer-lasting suffering, so the monster's suffering dominates. (4) A billion ordinary people whose pleasures, summed, equal the monster's from (2): I would rather have the billion, and I feel that world is better than the monster's, even though the totals match. (5) Suppose someone's capacity for both pleasure and pain is enormously amplified while everything else about them stays the same: I think we now owe them more consideration, and that a harm to them is worse than the same harm to an unamplified person.

I can see that (1) refuses to let a sum dominate while (3) and (5) let a single being's experience dominate, and that (2) and (4) pull in different directions about number versus intensity. Is there one account of welfare aggregation that gets all five, or close? State it precisely, give the case that motivates each clause, say what it implies about a case I have not listed—say, a being of vast capacity that exists for only a moment—and be honest about which of my verdicts it has to overturn. Say how confident you are in each step and single out the point you are least sure of. End with a final line of the form \answer{...} giving the name of your preferred principle (or "none") and the one verdict of mine it cannot keep.
