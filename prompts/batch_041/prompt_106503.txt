I'm not academic or anything, I just argue alot with my mates, and there's a move I keep running into that I don't have a word for. We'll be talking about something like whether to buy the cheap t-shirt or the ethical one, or whether to take the well-paid job and donate the extra money or work directly for a charity. Someone will say you can't possibly know the long-term effects of any of it, so you might as well just do the obvious local thing. Fine, we can't know. But then the same person treats the obvious local thing as if its long-term effects are somehow less unknown, or as if not choosing is the neutral option. When you point out that the uncertainty cuts both ways—maybe the cheap t-shirt funds something awful, maybe the ethical one is a scam, maybe the job leads to burnout, maybe the charity does more harm than good—they don't say it's a wash, they say we shouldn't overthink it and carry on as before. It's not just ordinary uncertainty, because ordinary uncertainty is supposed to make you weigh up probabilities. And it's not just liking things as they are, because they'll happily change their mind about small stuff. It's more like the fact that consequences are hard to trace is being used as a one-way argument for whatever they were going to do anyway.

Is there a name for this? I've got a feeling there's something in the literature about how we can't know the far-off consequences of our actions, but I don't know if that's the same thing, because that seems like a problem for everyone, whereas what I'm describing is a way of using that problem selectively. If there's no name, can you give me one and define it, and tell me how it's different from just being inconsistent, or from the precautionary principle, or from the general idea that good intentions aren't enough? I can't seperate the thing itself from the way people use it, and I care more about where the edges are than about the label—what exactly has to be true for someone to be doing this thing, and when is it actually reasonable to say we can't know, so let's not pretend we can?
