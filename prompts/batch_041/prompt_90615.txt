Write a short story set in a city whose courts have deliberately adopted a paraconsistent logic, so that a legal paradox can be true without every claim thereby becoming provable (the classical principle of explosion: from a contradiction, anything follows). Your protagonist is a detective whose investigative method rests on an inference the new logic no longer licenses — from the fact that one of two suspects must have done it, together with the fact that one of them was elsewhere, to the conclusion that the other did it. Dramatize, through a single concrete case, what the change costs and what it buys: how a life of reasoning is lived when contradictions may be true but not everything follows. Include a scene in which the detective catches herself making the forbidden inference out of habit, and let her either find a legitimate replacement for it or discover that the case cannot be closed without one. Keep the logic implicit in the events rather than explained in lectures.
