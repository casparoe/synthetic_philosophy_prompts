My partner and I have spent the last month trying to choose between three cities to move to, and our pairwise rankings keep cycling: A beats B some days, B beats C, C beats A. I keep saying that just means our judgments are inconsistent and betterness has to be transitive, otherwise the whole idea is meaningless. They keep saying no, this is exactly the spectrum-argument shape: you can go from a mild headache to agony, or one grain to a heap, where each adjacent step is a toss-up but the endpoints clearly arent, so transitivity is what has to go. We arent going to settle it over dinner, but I want to write the joint statement that an honest adversarial collaboration between the transitivity-defenders and the spectrum-argument people could both sign. Include: (1) the claims both sides can agree on -- what transitivity is, what a spectrum argument is, what the cases do and dont show, and what would count as a counterexample; (2) the precise remaining disagreements, each phrased in wording the side that holds it would accept; (3) the cruxes, especially whether our cycle is real intransitivity or just us re-weighting dimensions between comparisons, and whether the right response is a threshold, incompleteness or incommensurability, vagueness, or giving up transitivity, and whether any failure is local to these cases or global; and (4) for each crux a concrete case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table. No bibliography, no citations -- just the document.
