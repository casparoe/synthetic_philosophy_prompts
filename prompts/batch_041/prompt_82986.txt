I retired some years ago from experimental physics, and since then I have been reading philosophy, mostly the foundations of decision theory. There is a style of argument I keep meeting, and I have no name for it.

It runs like this. One lays down three or four conditions on a person's preferences: that they be complete, that they be transitive, that a preference not reverse when the same outcome is presented differently, that no outcome be so good or so bad that it dominates every gamble against it. These read to me as conditions on a representation rather than on conduct -- they say what must be true of a person's preferences if a certain mathematical description is to fit them. From them it is proved that the preferences must be describable by a utility function whose expectation the person maximizes. So far, so ordinary; I spent a career among theorems of that shape. What I have no name for is the move that follows. The person who fails one of the conditions is described not as someone the representation fails to fit, but as incoherent, inconsistent, irrational -- as having made a mistake. And two of the conditions look to me as though they presuppose what the theorem is meant to establish: the one about preferences among gambles presumes that the person already weighs outcomes by their probabilities, and the continuity condition rules out anyone whose priorities are lexicographic or who attaches infinite value to something, which seems less a demand of consistency than a substantive taste written into the axioms.

Is there an established name for this move specifically -- the conversion of conditions on a representational scheme into norms binding on the agent, so that failure to be representable is reported as a failure of rationality? I have met "coherence" and "Dutch book" as labels, and I have met the general unease about representation theorems, but neither seems to pick out the move itself. I want the carving, not the label: which named things come nearest, how the thing I am describing differs from each, where its edges are (does the same move appear when the axioms govern belief rather than preference?), and, if nothing fits, a coined term with a definition and clear boundary cases. I am not after a reading list; I want the distinction drawn.

Please end with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing the name you settle on, or the words "no established term" followed by your own coinage.
