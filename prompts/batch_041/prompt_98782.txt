I'm a novelist, partway through a comic novel set in a small town where the local paper's consumer-advice columnist — an unshakably cheerful man, the kind who rates everything five stars — has lately taken to reviewing not restaurants but the whole of creation. I want one of those columns as a standalone set piece, and I'd rather you write it than me, becuase every time I try it slides into cheap isn't-the-world-awful irony and I lose the thing I actually find funny, which is a man sincerely, untroubledly defending the claim that this is the best of all possible worlds. So this needs to be satire from inside the position: he believes it, he's never winking, and he's a little smug about it.

Write about 600–700 words in his voice — a five-star consumer report on the world, rating up top, pros and cons, the works — and make every defence he mounts one the actual view can actually mount. He should insist the world is best not because it holds the most good things but because it's the most that this world could be: you can't have every perfection at once, some of them crowd each other out, so the cons have to be weighed against what the alternatives would cost, not against some blank ideal that was never on the menu. Let him sort the kinds of evil like a man separating his recycling, and let him be genuinely baffled that anyone expects the best world to be a world without suffering, as if best meant most comfortable. Give him a running feud with an imagined one-star reviewer — a friend who keeps insisting a better world was obviously possible — whom he can't stop quoting back and correcting, and who is the only person he calls by name. The humour has to come from the reasoning being right, so that someone who's read a little of this stuff laughs rather than winces, while a reader who's never heard of any of it still follows the joke. He's also a bit too fond of the word sufficient, if that helps you find his voice. No real quotations from anyone, and don't name any scholars or works — it's an in-universe column, it shouldn't read like it's citing anything.
