ok so I have to write a short philosophical profile for my metaphysics reading group and I want a sanity check first, because I suspect my views on grounding don't all fit together and I'd rather find out now than in front of everyone. here's what I actually think:

1. Grounding is real and objective. There's a mind-independent structure of dependence in the world, the 'in virtue of' relation, and it's one topic-neutral relation rather than a different one for parts/wholes, mind/physical, moral/natural, etc. It's not causation.
2. Grounding is necessitating: if A fully grounds B, then necessarily, if A obtains then B obtains.
3. Grounding is a strict order — irreflexive, asymmetric, transitive. Nothing grounds itself and there are no grounding circles.
4. There's a fundamental level. The grounding order is well-founded, so every non-fundamental fact traces back to facts that nothing grounds. Foundationalism, basically.
5. To say A grounds B is to give a metaphysical explanation of B — it's the non-causal 'because'. So explanatory considerations are a decent guide to what grounds what.
6. Explanation is interest-relative: what counts as explaining a thing, and how far down you have to go, depends on the questioner's interests and the contrast class they have in mind.
7. Grounding facts aren't themselves grounded. The fact that A grounds B just obtains; it isn't true in virtue of anything. Grounding is primitive.
