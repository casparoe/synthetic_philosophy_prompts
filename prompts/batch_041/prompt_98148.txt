The proof is basically Arrow's theorem in disguise, and it shows the whole idea of individual rights is self-undermining -- the only way out is to drop the rights, since obviously we can't drop the principle that if everyone prefers something then it should happen. He illustrates it with a prude and a lewd reader arguing over who gets to read a racy novel, and the upshot is that a genuinely liberal state is impossible.' Some of that feels off to me but I don't know the result well enough to pin down where. Can you go through it: what would someone who actually works on social choice accept, what's a misleading simplification, and what's just wrong?
