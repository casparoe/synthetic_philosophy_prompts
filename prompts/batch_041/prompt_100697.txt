My writing mentor runs a kind of salon every second Friday — eight or nine of us, a lot of wine, and she insists the whole point is that everyone drinks enough to stop performing. Her line is that you only find out what a person is really like once they're three glasses in, and that learning to stay decent while you're drunk is the actual training ground for staying decent the rest of the time. She also has this whole riff about how the Spartans got it backwards when they banned the stuff outright. I've been going for a year now, I still can't decide wether she's onto something or has just found a high-minded excuse for a piss-up, and I'd like to know before I keep dragging my students along to it. Is there a serious case for the drinking party as a place where character gets tested and built? And if there is, what would have to be true of hers for it to actually be that, rather than a party with a story attached to it?
