I'm putting together a handout for a small reading group on prudential value, and I want one worked case in it that does a very specific job, so bear with the spec.

Take tranquilism to be the view that the only thing that makes a life go better or worse for the person living it is craving and suffering, their presence or absence. Craving = the conative pull, the wanting/hankering/needing, the leaning-toward; suffering = an aversive state that is actually felt. On this view craving is bad in itself even when it gets satisfied and even when it isn't unpleasant, and it's absence is good in itself even when the person is in pain. Pleasure, achievement, relationships, getting what you want: none of it matters except as it bares on craving and suffering. The case is supposed to earn its place in the handout by showing that this isn't hedonism with different words, i.e. that the craving/suffering distinction is doing real work.

So: build me a case where the tranquilist has to rank two lives, and the ranking comes out the way a straight hedonist would find perverse. Specifically, the tranquilist ranks higher the life that contains more felt pain and less pleasure. Constraints: ordinary contemporary setting, two people or two stretches of one life, no monasteries, no meditation retreats, no sci-fi (nothing wired into anyone's head), no trickery with false beliefs or ignorance, whoever is doing the ranking knows both lives completely, and the whole scenario in one paragraph. Everything else about the two lives should be as equal as you can make it, so craving and suffering are the only things moving. And the ranking has to fall out of the view, not be announced by you. If you can't get it to fall out, say that instead of fudging it.

Then, in order: (1) what each view says and why, tranquilism, hedonism, and a desire-satisfaction view, being explicit about which facts about the two lives each view is actually reading off; (2) which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens to the verdicts if I take that feature away; (3) does the case survive the obvious counter, that craving just is a kind of suffering, so tranquilism collapses into hedonism with a funny vocabulary, and if it doesn't survive as written, fix it or tell me no ordinary case can do it; (4) how a hedonist who thinks the tranquilist has the ranking exactly backwards would go after the case.
