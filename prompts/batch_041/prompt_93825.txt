I was talking with some friends in a reading group — we're doing a weird mix of legal theory, self-reference, and a guy who writes compilers — and I keep bumping into a structure I don't have a good word for, and it's come up alot. Picture a set of rules arranged in levels. There's a level that says how the other levels are supposed to be made, and lower levels that do the actual governing. Normally you'd think the top level is fixed, because it's what gives the lower levels their authority, so it can't be touched by them. But in the cases that bug me, the top level is itself produced or revisable by the lower levels. A constitution that can only be amended by following its own amendment clause, where the clause itself can be amended the same way. A court that has the final say on what the constitution means, while its final-say status is something the constitution grants and the court interprets. A compiler that compiles itself, and whose spec says the compiler is the definition of the language, so spec and compiler each seem to ground the other. A game where the referee's rulings are final, but the players appoint the referee and can vote to change the rules. Escher's Drawing Hands is the picture I keep thinking of, but I've also heard a couple of terms for things like this and I can't tell if they're the same thing or a bigger umbrella.

Is there an established name for this exact setup? If there is, what is it, and how does it differ from the nearby stuff — ordinary circular reasoning, a metalanguage/object-language regress, bootstrapping, a fixed point, a self-amending rule, a basic norm, a constitutive rule? If there isn't one, can you coin a term and define it, and tell me where the edges are: when is the loop vicious and when is it benign or even necessary? I'm especially interested in the top-level angle: in these cases there's supposed to be a level that settles what counts as a valid move at the other levels, and yet the loop runs back through it. So is that top level being violated, or is that just what a top level is in this kind of hierarchy? Does it matter whether it's logically untouchable versus just treated as untouchable in practice, so everyone acts like it's fixed even though it isn't? I don't just want a label, I want the carving — what the thing is, what it isn't, and which of those nearby concepts fits best.
