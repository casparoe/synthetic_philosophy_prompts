I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I am applying for a six-month interdisciplinary fellowship that funds practitioners to spend part of their working time on a research project. I need a short research proposal, and I have a half-formed idea that I would like you to turn into one.

For the past two years I have worked on a code-review tool that flags 'technical debt'. Within that small world the phrase does at least three different jobs: it names a deliberate shortcut taken with an intention to repay it later, it names any code that is expensive to change, and it is used as a mild accusation against a colleague. I have a folder of cases in which two experienced engineers call the same change 'technical debt' while meaning incompatible things, and each is plainly right by their own lights. My half-formed idea is that this is not merely sloppiness to be corrected. The term may be doing useful work precisely because its meaning is fluid: it lets a team gather a family of worries under one heading, and pinning it to a single definition might destroy what makes it useful. But I cannot tell whether that is a real insight or a convenient excuse, and I do not know how to tell productive slippage from mere equivocation.

Please write the proposal. State the research question so that it has a determinate answer. I suspect three things are being run together and I want them separated: drift (a term's meaning changes for everyone over time), spread (different subgroups hold different meanings at the same time), and stretch (a single speaker extends a term to a new case in the moment). Say whether each of these can be assessed as productive or pathological, and on what basis, without assuming that there is a canonical definition to appeal to. Explain why the obvious position — that a term carrying several meanings is simply ambiguous and ought to be disambiguated — leaves the question open. Describe what I would actually do over six months, given that I cannot lean on formal philosophical training and will be working part-time alongside my job. Say what a result would look like, which considerations would decide between the possible answers, and what would count as the project failing. Be honest about what could make this too large for six months of part-time work.

Lead with a one-sentence statement of the research question. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I will add references myself. Keep the whole proposal to a maximum of 700 words.
