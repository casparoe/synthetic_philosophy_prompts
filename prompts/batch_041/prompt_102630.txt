I'm in this reading group on probability and evidence and we keep going around in cirles on the reference class problem. Every meeting someone trots out a different example — the John Smith life-expectancy thing where the same man is 40, a 40-year-old American male, a 40-year-old male with his medical history, and each gives a different number; Sleeping Beauty; the doomsday argument; the actuary who just reaches for a table; whether "the chance of rain tomorrow in my town" even has a reference class — and we always end up trading intuitions about which class is the real one without ever agreeing on what we're even asking of a theory. So before we read one more paper I want the spec first.

Write me the desiderata: what would a theory of reference-class selection actually have to deliver to count as adequate. I mean the cases it has to get right, with the case behind each requirement (the ones above, plus maybe the risk-score / predictive-policing case where a model spits out "high risk" and the entire question is which population you're implicitly comparing the person to); the distinctions it isn't allowed to collapse — between the class that's metaphysically privileged and the one that's epistemically relevant, between a class being salient and its being correct, between objective chance and my credence, between a class fixed once and a class that shifts as evidence arrives; and the background results it has to stay consistent with (the probability axioms, conditionalization / total evidence, exchangeability, the fact that frequencies only ever reach us already grouped). For each item, say why it's a genuine requirement and not just a prejudice of one camp.

Then the part I actually want: which of these desiderata can't be jointly satisfied. I have a strong suspicion that "there's a determinate, unique right class" fights with "the choice can't be arbitrary, it has to be invariant under how we describe the event," and maybe with total evidence as well — and if that's right, then the list itself is the result, not a preamble to one. Tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out candidate theories, and keep any scorecard of frequentist vs. Bayesian-exchangeable vs. propensity views to a couple of sentences, since the criteria are the deliverable here, not the grading. Frame the whole thing for someone who has to actually output a number and defend it — a working modeler or actuary — not just for a metaphysician.
