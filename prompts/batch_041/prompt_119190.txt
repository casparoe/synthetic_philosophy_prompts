Since retiring from physics I have been working through the metaphysics of individuality, and I would like a decision tree that sorts the positions one can take on Black's two identical spheres — a universe containing nothing but two qualitatively indistinguishable iron spheres. I do not want the tree organised around the names of the views; each branch should be a question about the case that a reader can answer without knowing where it leads, and the names should appear only at the leaves. Let the first branch ask whether such a description is possible at all, and the later branches separate the ways numerical distinctness might be grounded: in a monadic property, in a relation the spheres bear to one another, or in nothing qualitative at all, which is where a bare thisness or primitive haecceity enters. Rather than stating the test cases in the abstract, build the nodes around three of them: the original pair; a variant in which the two spheres are exchanged by a symmetry that leaves every qualitative fact untouched, so that one can ask whether the same world with the spheres swapped is a second possibility or the same possibility redescribed; and a variant drawn from physics, two indistinguishable particles, where the corresponding question is whether their exchange is a physical process or a mere relabelling. Include leaves for the substratum or bare-particular view, the bundle view, haecceitism and its denial, the view that the spheres are discerned by the irreflexive relation each bears to the other but not to itself, and the view that there is no fact of the matter; flag any pair your tree cannot separate and give one sentence on the most awkward commitment of each leaf. Finally, mark any combination of answers that no one holds, and say whether that is because the position is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored.
