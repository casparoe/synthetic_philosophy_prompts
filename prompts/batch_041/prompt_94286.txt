I'm a second-year PhD student in sociology (leaning STS, don't hold it against me) and I've gotten myself into an argument with my cohort that I can't climb out of. The setup is that everyone at the table happily agrees race, gender, and money are all "socially constructed." Great, I'm on board, I'm not one of those people. But here's the thing that's eating at me. Money seems to be constructed in the strongest possible sense — it isn't backed by anything, a central bank can just conjure more of it, whole countries redenominate and swap out their currency and everyone adjusts within a generation or so. So for money, "socially constructed" seems to cash out as something like: we made up the rules, and we can un-make them. But then race is supposed to be socially constructed in the same sense, and it's just... not like that. You can't pass a law declaring race over and have it stick; the categories keep reasserting themselves, and the whole thing is stickier, more material, more painful than money ever is. So what actually explains the gap? Is "socially constructed" secretly doing two totally different jobs in these two cases while we use one phrase for both?

There's a second layer that's confusing me more. Someone in my theory seminar said money is an "institutional fact" — it's real only because we collectively recognize it as money — and then someone else shot back that race is different because the category shapes the people who fall under it, and those people then reshape the category, in some kind of feedback loop. But I honestly don't see how that loop is anything other than another rule we could in principle change, so it doesn't feel like it gets me out of the puzzle; it just relabels it. And I'll admit the darker thought I keep having: if race were really just socially constructed the way money is, wouldn't we have managed to dismantle it by now — and if we can't dismantle it, doesn't that suggest it's tracking something real after all? I'm pretty sure that last step is wrong, but I can't articulate exactly where it goes off the rails, and I suspect the money example is doing some illegitimate work in my head. Can you untangle the whole thing for me — what's actually different between these kinds of social construction, whether malleability is even something you can read off of "constructed" in the first place, and where my inference from "can't change it" to "must be real" breaks? I don't need a reading list, I just want the actual distinctions laid out.
