I am assembling a position on the foundations of normativity and would like a consistency audit before I commit it to writing. My commitments are as follows.

1. Normative realism. There are facts about what an agent has reason to do and about what it would be right to do, and these facts are not constituted by anyone's actual attitudes, conventions, or practices. The correct answer to "what ought I to do" does not vary with what people happen to believe or approve.

2. Idealization as the method. The way to specify those facts is by reference to an idealized deliberator: a counterpart of the agent who is fully informed of the non-normative facts, vividly aware of them, free of logical and imaginative error, and fully coherent in their cares. An act is right, or a consideration is a reason, just in case that idealized counterpart would endorse acting on it.

3. Procedural specification of the ideal. The idealized deliberator must be characterized by their reasoning process and epistemic position, not by their substantive moral conclusions. I do not want to build the answers in. If I have to pick out the ideal agent by the verdicts they reach, the account is either circular or trivial.

4. Internalism about reasons for actual agents. A consideration is a reason for me only if it is suitably connected to my existing motivational set — something I already care about, or something reachable by sound deliberation from what I care about. Reasons for me cannot come wholly from outside my concerns.

5. The is/ought gap. No set of purely non-normative premises entails a normative conclusion.

6. Substantive verdict. The account should vindicate robust demands that do not hinge on my contingent cares — in particular, that there are genuine reasons to aid distant strangers, and that these do not evaporate merely because I happen not to be moved by them.

I want an audit rather than a survey. Tell me which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and order the genuine tensions by severity. For each, state precisely what has to give and what the cheapest repair would cost me elsewhere in the set. Then identify the single revision that would resolve the most, and say what it would force me to abandon. I suspect at least one of the apparent conflicts is not real, so please discriminate rather than flagging everything, and be explicit about which commitment you think is doing the quiet work that makes the others look compatible when they are not.
