So I've been reading around the platonism vs nominalism fight in philosophy of math and it feels completly stuck. Both sides accept the same theorems, agree on every causal fact about the world, and neither has budged on whether numbers exist in like fifty years. The indispensability people keep saying we should believe in whatever our best theories quantify over; the nominalists keep running paraphrase programs that, as one critic put it, only re-describe the commitments instead of actually removing them; and it just cycles. My hunch is the stall isn't really about whether numbers exist but about two things tangled together: whether 'exist' for numbers is the same thin word as for tables or a heavier metaphysical one, and whether math is even supposed to explain anything in the physical world. So is this partly a verbal fight over senses of 'exist', partly a real disagreement about what counts as an explanation, or is the old Benacerraf dilemma still doing all the work with nobody actually answering it? Give me a diagnosis with reasons rather than a survey of the literature, and be honest about what would count as progress here -- a new argument, a formal result, or just admitting the question is ill-posed.
