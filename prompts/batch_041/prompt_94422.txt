Hey, I'm putting together the first meeting of a reading group on Wittgenstein's On Certainty and I want to kick it off with a dialogue script instead of a paper, so people have something to push against. Can you write it?

Two characters, both sharp, neither one a strawman. The first is a Popperian critical rationalist: thinks there's no such thing as a privileged source or a foundation, that the whole demand for one is a mistake, and that knowledge grows by guessing and then trying hard to knock the guess down. For her, anything that's been parked beyond criticism is just a dogma nobody's gotten around to questioning yet. The second is a Wittgensteinian about certainty: thinks that under any practice there's a layer of things we simply don't doubt, not because we've justified them but because doubting them would undercut the whole business of giving reasons, so the demand for a justification down there isn't a gap that needs filling, it's a confusion. And these aren't even beliefs the way ordinary beliefs are, they're more like the way we go on.

The fight I want is over whether a commitment that's beyond criticism is a scandal or a structural necessity, and whether the Popperian demand to criticize everything is even coherent, since it looks like it's asking us to criticize the very thing we'd need in order to criticize anything.

Keep them circling one concrete case so it doesn't float off into abstraction: a mutual friend who's gotten into skeptical philosophy and now says he might have popped into existence five minutes ago with all his memories intact. The Popperian wants to say it's unfalsifiable and so not even a real claim; the Wittgensteinian wants to say the doubt can't get off the ground, because it strips away everything that makes the words mean what they mean. Let them work out how much daylight there is between those two dismissals, because I suspect there's alot.

A few things I want out of it:

- Real back and forth. Each one should answer the actual last thing the other said, not the thing they wish the other had said.
- Give the Popperian the sharp dilemma: either hinges can be criticized (then they're not hinges) or they can't (then you've immunized them, which is the dogmatism you claim to hate). Make the Wittgensteinian answer that without just repeating form of life.
- Give the Wittgensteinian a counter-punch: the rule that everything must be criticizable is itself held without criticism, so the Popperian has a hinge too. The Popperian needs a real answer here, not a dodge.
- Let them find one genuine point of agreement (I think they both hate the idea of a self-justifying foundation) and then split again about what follows from it.
- At least one moment where somebody has to actually concede something, and it should sting a little.
- No quoting anyone, no citations, no name-dropping a parade of other philosophers. Just the two of them and the arguement.
- Don't resolve it. End on the live disagreement so we can pick it up in the room.

Aim for something you could read aloud in about ten minutes.
