I sit on the advisory board of a small foundation that has agreed to fund a programme on Maxwell's demon and the physics of information: roughly three postdocs for three years, or the equivalent spread over a longer period, jointly hosted by a physics department and a philosophy department. We are not funding instrumentation. We are funding conceptual and theoretical work, and we will judge it in five years by whether it changed what the field takes to be at issue. I need a defended agenda rather than a survey; assume I know the standard literature and do not need a reading list.

Please rank the open questions that most deserve that effort and defend the ordering. For each, say why it is upstream of other work in the area rather than a leaf; whether it is tractable for a philosopher, for a physicist, or only for the two working together; how neglected it currently is; and what result, argument, or calculation over the next three years should move it up or down the list. Name the two or three candidates you would drop as not worth a marginal person-year, and say which single question you would fund first if you could fund only one.

Treat at least the following as candidates, and add others I have not listed: where the demon's advantage is actually paid for — acquisition of information, erasure, or the finiteness of memory — and whether these are rival answers or three names for one thing; whether the paradox depends on where one draws the boundary between the system and the demon's memory; whether the demon's possibility is really a question about coarse-graining and initial conditions, so that the literature is a disguised debate about the arrow of time; what changes in the quantum case, where measurement back-action and coherence alter the cost of acquiring information; and whether the demon is a useful model of a bounded agent that must pay for the information it uses.

One colleague wants to reorganise the whole area around a distinction she draws between two ledgers: a thermodynamic ledger, in which the demon's knowledge is a physical resource with a price, and an epistemic ledger, in which the demon's knowledge is a representational state that costs nothing until it is used. On her view the central question is not where the entropy goes but what it is for a physical record to count as used, and she thinks the paradox recurs wherever we smuggle in a notion of using information that has no thermodynamic counterpart. Tell me whether that reframing should reorganise the list or is one line item within it, and what it would take to show that it is a genuine advance rather than a relabelling.
