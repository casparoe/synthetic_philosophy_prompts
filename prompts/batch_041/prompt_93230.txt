I have been defending a package I call slot-theory and want a consistency audit before I commit it to a paper. The commitments are these. (1) Propositions are structured: the proposition that a loves b has a, b, and loving as constituents, and its structure mirrors the sentence that expresses it. (2) A relation is a constituent of a proposition in its own right, not merely a syncategorematic feature of the whole; when I say that a loves b, loving is one of the things the proposition is about. (3) What distinguishes a genuine proposition from a mere list of its constituents is the unity of the proposition, and this unity is not a further relation added to the constituents. (4) Bradley's regress is a sound argument against any account that posits such a further unifying relation: once you add a relation to bind a and b, you need another relation to bind that relation to them, and so on. (5) Unity is supplied instead by the relation's own unsaturated character: loving has argument places, and when those places are filled by a and b, the result is a proposition rather than a heap. Filling is not a relation; it is what it is for a relation to have a slot occupied. (6) The proposition that a loves b is the very same proposition as the proposition that b is loved by a; they differ only in how they are expressed, not in their constituents or their arrangement.

I am aware that these are meant to fit together, but I want to know whether they actually do. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each genuine tension? For each genuine one, say what has to give and what the cheapest revision would cost. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and say which single revision would clean up the most.
