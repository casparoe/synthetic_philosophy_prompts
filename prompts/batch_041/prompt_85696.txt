Two researchers have spent years arguing about what the colour phi phenomenon shows. In the standard demonstration, a blue dot is flashed at one position and, a fraction of a second later, a red dot at a nearby position; at the right spacing and timing, subjects report a single dot travelling between the two positions and changing colour abruptly about halfway along the path — that is, at a point in the sequence earlier than the flash of the red dot that supposedly caused the change. The first researcher holds that the colour change is built into experience itself, at the input stage: the visual system buffers and re-edits the sequence, so the subject is conscious only of the tidied-up version and there is no moment at which she experiences the unrevised sequence. The second holds that her experience is exactly what its timing suggests — blue dot, then red dot, with no colour change in between — and that what gets rewritten is the memory trace, laid down afterwards and adjusted for coherence. (The two options are often called Stalinesque and Orwellian revisions.)

Write a dialogue in which they try to settle the matter. Make both competent: each should have a real answer to the other's strongest move, and neither should be a strawman. Have each of them propose one experiment or manipulation they claim would discriminate between the two hypotheses, and then have the other explain why it fails to discriminate, since both hypotheses can accommodate almost any report and differ only over where in the stream the editing occurs. Let them consider, midway through, a third possibility: that there is no fact of the matter about when the colour change was experienced and the question is malformed. One of them should concede something specific to the other before the end. Neither may cite literature; they argue from the phenomenon itself and from what each hypothesis predicts.

Format: "Dialogue" (about 1000 words), then "Concessions" (about 100 words) listing what each granted the other, then "The crux" (about 150 words) naming the question the exchange leaves open and saying whether any experiment could settle it.
