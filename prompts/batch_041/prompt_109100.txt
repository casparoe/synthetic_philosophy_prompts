ok so I never took a philosophy class but I've been going down a rabbit hole on this stuff and I want a sanity check before I go argue about it online. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1) I take the no free lunch thing seriously — there's no learning method that beats random guessing across every possible problem. Any learner that's actually good at something is good because of assumptions baked into it, not because it's generally smart all by itself.
2) I think science really does zero in on how the world is. Not that it's guaranteed, but it's not dumb luck either — the predictions keep landing.
3) I think Bayesian updating is basically the right story of how you should change your mind when evidence comes in.
4) I think preferring the simpler hypothesis is a real rule, not just an aesthetic taste — if two theories fit the data equally well, the simpler one is more likely to be true.
5) I think that old problem of induction — that you can't prove the future will resemble the past without already assuming it — actually has a way out: the world is compressible, there's a short underlying description of how it works, and that's why simple methods can latch onto it and predict.

Do these hang together? I've got a nagging feeling that a couple of them are quietly fighting each other, but I honestly can't tell which ones.
