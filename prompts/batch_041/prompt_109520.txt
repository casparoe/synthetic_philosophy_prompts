Okay, so I'm a novelist, and through a series of small mistakes I've ended up on the steering committee of an international writers' festival we put on every other year. Mostly I approve budgets and nod along. But now there's an actual dispute and I have to write something up before the next meeting, and I'd rather not wing it in front of people who will remember.

Here's the situation. Our city has the usual set of local customs, and two of our visiting fellows from this year's cohort ran afoul of them. One, who's from a country where tipping isn't done and leaving money is faintly rude, ate at the restaurant we always send fellows to, paid the bill exactly, and left nothing. The server there is a friend of one of our board members and complained, and to be fair the servers at that place really do live off tips. The other fellow jaywalked in front of a local writer who was crossing with her kid, and also cut the coffee queue at the venue twice by claiming he was holding a spot for someone. A staff member has now filed a formal complaint asking us to discipline both of them, or at least to put a clause in the fellowship agreement saying visiting writers must follow local customs.

The board meets in three weeks and I've been asked to circulate a short written opinion beforehand. So what I want from you is that memo. Recommend a concrete course of action, not a vibe: what goes in the fellowship agreement (if anything), what we say to the two fellows, what we say to the staff member who complained, and by when. Give the strongest version of the argument on the side you end up rejecting, because half the board will be on that side and I don't want to get shredded.

Two things I'd like you to actually work through rather than skip. First, I don't think all these customs are the same animal, and I want the memo to sort them. Some conventions seem to be pure coordination, where the content barely matters as long as everyone does the same thing. Some seem to distribute burdens, like tipping, which is arguably just passing a restaurant's payroll onto customers. Some seem to be about showing respect or acknowledging who was there first, like the queue. Does the kind of convention change whether a visitor owes compliance? Say so explicitly, with the case behind each claim.

Second, and this is the part that's keeping me up: the tipping case is ugly because both ways out look bad. If the fellow complies, he props up an arrangement a lot of people think is unjust and quietly confirms that servers should depend on the charity of strangers. If he doesn't, a specific underpaid person eats the cost, and he also free-rides on everyone else who does pay. I want the memo to say which horn we take and why, not to wave it away with a line about being guests of the city. Everyone on this board already knows how to say when in Rome, so spare me that.

You can bring in the philosophy if it helps — the stuff about conventions as solutions to coordination problems, the older line about customs arising from mutual interest rather than agreement, the moral/conventional distinction, all of that — but keep it in service of the actual decision. No reading list, no citations, the board won't read them. Plain prose, a clear bottom line, and a honest paragraph for the losing side. Thanks.
