I'm a backend engineer at a big health system, no philosophy training at all, and I keep bumping into something I can't sort out. One of my services takes lab values and vitals, runs them through a risk model, and when the score crosses a line we attach a condition to the patient's chart. That label is what gets them into the care program, and honestly it's also what lets the clinic bill for any of it — no diagnosis code, no reimbursement, no program.

Two things about it bug me. First, alot of the people we flag feel completely fine. As far as anyone can tell nothing is wrong with them right now; what we're really saying is that they have a higher chance of something bad in five or ten years. Second, the line is just a config value. It's moved twice since I joined, following guideline updates, and every time it moves a few thousand people gain or lose a disease overnight without a single thing changing inside their bodies. The kidney one is stranger still: until a few years ago the same lab number went through a different multiplier depending on the patient's race, so two people with identical results could land in different stages. Taking the race part out was the right call I think, but it also meant a bunch of people's stage changed instantly. Their kidneys didn't change at all.

So what is this whole setup assuming a disease is? That there's a fact inside the body we're detecting and just getting better at measuring, or that a disease is whatever we've decided is worth treating — a line somebody draws, and then we act on it? Do the different parts of the system assume the same thing, or is the billing logic quietly on one side while the doctors and the patient-facing copy are on the other? Does the thing actually need to pick one, or could the exact same code run under both stories? And if you took the other view seriously, what would really have to change in what I build — the thresholds, the labels, what we show patients? Also, can "more likely to get a disease" be a disease itself, or is that just a mix-up on our end?
