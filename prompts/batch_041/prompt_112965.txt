Ok so something's been bugging me for a while and I can't get a straight answer out of anyone. I edit a small student paper, and we have a style rule I've never been able to justify: we never spell out the n-word, ever, not even inside a direct quote from a police report or a court transcript — we write 'the n-word' and move on. But we spelled out the f-slur in full last month in a piece about a hate crime and nobody blinked, and I've seen the r-word printed in quotes in national papers. When a song lyric has the n-word in it we just don't quote the line. Meanwhile my roommate's copy of Huckleberry Finn has it all over every page, and it seems to me that Black writers usually spell the word out when they're writing about it while white writers usually don't. TV bleeps it but the closed captions print it. So the same word is unprintable in the news, printable in a novel, and printable or not depending on who's typing.

What does this whole practice assume about what a slur even is? If the badness lives in what the word says — some contemptuous or false claim about a group — then writing 'the n-word' should be exactly as bad as writing the word, since everybody knows what you mean. If the badness lives in the word's shape or sound, then any word with that history should be equally unprintable, and the euphemisms should be safe, which they definately arent (look at how colored, Oriental and retard all went). If it lives in who's saying it, in what tone, to whom, then our newsroom rule is just etiquette with no theory behind it. And then there's queer, which was the slur and is now the word in the job ads.

So which of these does the practice actually presuppose, and which does it merely happen to fit? Does it need a view about slurs at all, or would several incompatible theories license the same rules? And if I swapped in the view that slurs are ordinary words with nasty connotations, so that quoting one is always fine, what in the practice would have to go — the ban on spelling it out, the quoting, or something else I'm not seeing?
