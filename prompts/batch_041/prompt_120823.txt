I am in the third year of a cognitive science degree and am currently taking a seminar on perception and cognition. My computational background is considerably stronger than my philosophical one: I am comfortable with hierarchical Bayesian models, generative and discriminative models, priors and likelihoods, amortised inference, feed-forward versus recurrent architectures, and signal detection theory's separation of sensitivity from response criterion. I have also internalised the general modeller's reflex that any layer in a network can receive top-down input, and I suspect this reflex is doing more work in my thinking than I realise.

I would like you to translate the dispute over the cognitive penetration of perception into that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then tell me what does not survive the translation.

The dispute as I understand it has four strands. The first holds that perceptual processing is encapsulated: early vision is informationally sealed off from beliefs, desires, expectations and other central states, so that what you know or want cannot alter what you see, though it can alter what you say, how quickly you respond, or where you look. The second holds that cognition does penetrate perception, so that a perceiver's concepts, expectations, moods or knowledge genuinely shape the content or the phenomenal character of their experience, and not merely their judgement about it. The third, which I find the most interesting, argues that most empirical demonstrations of penetration fail as demonstrations, because the effects can be explained by response bias, task demands, or by the fact that the measurement is itself contaminated by the very effect it is meant to detect. The fourth, predictive processing, appears to make top-down influence so ubiquitous that the original dispute threatens to dissolve rather than be settled.

Here is what I want, in this order.

First, a systematic translation of each of the four positions into the vocabulary above: priors and likelihoods, generative models, top-down versus bottom-up pathways, readout and decoding, sensitivity versus criterion, architecture versus training, identifiability and confounding. I want this honest at the level of claims rather than a loose analogy. Where a philosophical claim is about information flow, tell me what the corresponding quantity is in my vocabulary. Where a claim is about phenomenology, tell me what quantity, if any, is supposed to correspond to it, and do not manufacture one if there is none.

Second, a residue report. For each position, distinguish three cases: what carries over cleanly; what carries over only if I accept a distortion, and what exactly is distorted; and what has no counterpart at all. Some of the residue will be obvious — nothing in a generative model is like a felt quality — so I am more interested in the less obvious cases. Does the idea of a perceptual state being answerable to the world rather than to the model's own priors have any expression in my vocabulary? Is the personal/subpersonal distinction statable? Does the distinction between conceptual and non-conceptual content mean anything once every representation is a vector? Is the thought that an experience can itself be rational or irrational, and not merely the belief formed on its basis, formulable at all? And does the contaminated-measurement argument have a counterpart in terms of identifiability or confounding, or is it doing something my statistical vocabulary cannot capture?

Third, a judgement. Where the translation fails, say whether the failure is a defect of my vocabulary — a respect in which computational modelling of perception is impoverished as a description of perceiving — or evidence that the original dispute is less substantive than it appears and the residue is obscurity rather than content. Do not settle this by assuming the target framework's own commitments and presenting the result as a neutral translation; in particular, do not offer predictive processing as though it were a neutral rendering of the debate. If you think the dispute only looks deep because of distinctions a modeller would simply refuse to draw, say so plainly; if you think the modeller is missing something, say what it is.

Around 1500 words. A short mapping table for the first part is welcome if it makes the correspondences clearer, but the residue report is the part I care about, and an honest report of failure is worth more to me than a smooth translation.
