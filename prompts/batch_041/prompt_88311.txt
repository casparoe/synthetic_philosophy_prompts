I'm in a reading group on consequentialism and I got volenteered to make the handout for our session on the demandingness objection, and I want it to be a decision tree rather than a list of -isms, because last time we just put names on the board and spent the whole hour arguing about what the labels meant instead of about whether a theory can really ask that much of us. Here's my angle, tell me if it's a bad one. I think people lump several distinct complaints under demandingness depending on who's supposed to bear the cost of complying: the agent's welfare (she'd be worse off, maybe miserable), her projects and relationships (her life stops being hers to live), her motivational capacities (no human could sustain this, so it can't be a requirement in the first place), or her epistemic position (she can't tell which act is the maximizing one, so the theory gives her nothing usable to act on). My hunch is that which of those you find decisive, plus a couple of downstream questions, sorts you into a position more reliably than asking whether you're a consequentialist. I'd also like the tree to be built from the logical space rather than from the roster of names, so please work out the questions and the answer combinations first and only then say who lives where.

So build the tree. The root should be that cost-of-compliance question. Every later node has to be something a reader can answer without already knowing where it leads, so a concrete case or a yes/no claim rather than do you accept the agent-centered prerogative. A few nodes I'd like in there somewhere if they fit: whether the objection is supposed to show the theory is false or merely that it can't be anyone's decision procedure (criterion versus guide); whether a theory only a saint could live up to is refuted by that fact or is instead a compliment to the theory and an indictment of us; whether the fix is to revise the theory or to revise our intuitions about the cases; and whether the demands get capped by what we could get everyone to do together rather than what you can do alone. I want 9 to 12 leaves, each labeled with the position that lives there, or unnamed if the combination is empty, plus one line on the most awkward thing that position has to say about a concrete case. What I want most is the empty boxes: combinations your tree allows that apparently nobody occupies, with a guess at whether that's because the view is incoherent, because it's unattractive, or just because nobody has bothered. If two positions can't be seperated by any of your nodes, say so instead of fudging it, I'd rather have a tree that admits a gap than one that pretends to cut where it can't.
