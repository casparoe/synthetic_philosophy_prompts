I teach an upper-level seminar on consciousness and cognition and I am setting the final examination. I want a single essay question that cannot be answered by reciting the global workspace versus phenomenal consciousness literature, so I have invented a case for the students to work on. Please write the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme naming the specific moves that separate an A from a B.

The case I want them to analyse is this. A patient recovering from a disorder of consciousness is trained on a bedside task. On some trials she correctly uses a briefly presented word to guide a two-step action, fetching the named object from one of three drawers. On debrief she insists she had no experience of the word at all, not a faint one and not a fleeting one; she describes it only as something happening in the machinery and says she found herself doing the right thing. On other trials she reports a vivid, richly detailed experiential state, a colour or a face, that she cannot use in any way: it does not guide the task, she cannot recall its content a moment later, and it never enters her reasoning. Assume both patterns are robust and replicable.

The question should force them to say what access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness are each supposed to be, whether this pair of dissociations is even coherent, what global workspace theory must say about each half of it, and where the line falls between a genuine theoretical disagreement and a merely verbal one. It should also make them confront the evidential problem that reports are themselves access phenomena. In the marking scheme, include the specific missteps that should cost marks, in particular the tendency to redescribe the first pattern as the patient merely being wrong about her own experience, and the tendency to treat the second pattern's report as self-certifying evidence of phenomenal character without access. Set the total at 20 marks with an indicative breakdown, and add a one-line note on how candidates typically mishandle each part.
