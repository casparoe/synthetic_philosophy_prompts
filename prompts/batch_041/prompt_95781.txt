I have been reading about Hobbes's account of why geometry counts as the model of certain knowledge, and I keep noticing a pattern in it that I do not have a word for. I would like to know whether there is an established concept for it, and if there is not, I would like a term coined and defined carefully enough that I can tell what falls inside it and what does not.

The familiar part first, so you can see what I am not asking about. Hobbes treats geometry as the one science whose demonstrations we can genuinely know, because we make its objects ourselves: a line is drawn, a figure constructed, so the geometer knows the thing from the cause he himself produces. In natural philosophy, by contrast, we do not make the bodies we study, so we can at best reason from observed effects back to possible causes, and our conclusions there are correspondingly weaker. That much is a recognizable position in the philosophy of mathematics, and I am not asking for its name.

What interests me is a second job that the same move seems to perform. When Hobbes's own geometrical claims are challenged -- his attempted squaring of the circle, the long pamphlet exchange with John Wallis that ran from the 1650s into the 1670s -- the response is not to grant that the construction was faulty and the result wrong. It is to redescribe the disagreement as verbal. The critics are said to be importing terms that have no constructive basis, algebraic symbols or infinite quantities whose meanings have not been fixed by any operation we perform, and so the objection is not a counterexample to geometry's certainty but evidence that the objector is doing something other than geometry. He even reads the opposition as partly theological and institutional rather than mathematical, which makes the dispute look like a contest of authority rather than a test of a proof. The result is that the certainty of the field, grounded in our making its objects, is never exposed to the objections raised within the field, because those objections are reclassified as failures of language or as foreign intrusions.

The pattern I want a name for is therefore this: a discipline's authority is secured by the fact that we produce its objects, and that very security then converts internal dissent into a defect in the dissenter's terminology or methods. The constructive premise is not just an epistemological thesis about how knowledge there is possible; it functions as a device that makes the discipline's certainty survive every apparent counterexample. The stronger the claim that we know the domain because we make it, the less any disagreement inside the domain can count against it.

The nearest concepts I can think of all seem to miss something. Maker's knowledge, or the thought that we know what we make, is a positive thesis about the possibility of knowledge and says nothing by itself about how to treat dissent. Mathematical constructivism is a view about what mathematical objects and proofs are, not a strategy for absorbing criticism. Popper's immunizing stratagems and conventionalist twists are close, but they are typically ad hoc and retrospective -- a rescue mounted after a prediction fails -- whereas here the immunizing is built into the original grounding, before any particular objection arrives. A diagnosis of merely verbal dispute can be correct or incorrect in a given case and need not be tied to a constructive epistemology at all. And it is not simply the genetic fallacy, because the appeal to how the objects are made is being used as a criterion of what counts as a legitimate move in the field, not as a substitute for assessing a claim on its merits.

What I want from you is the carving, not a bare label. Is there an established term for this configuration, or for the family it belongs to? If there is one, how does it differ from each of the near misses above, and which of them is closest? If there is not, name it and define it, and tell me what the definition implies at the edges: does the pattern require a constructive or maker's-knowledge premise, or can any strong grounding of a discipline's certainty do the same work? Does it require that the dissent be reclassified as verbal, or is that only one of its forms? Is a single thinker's practice enough to make it a concept, or does it need to be a recurring move across fields? I am less interested in whether Hobbes was right about geometry than in whether the shape of the move he makes has a recognized name and a precise boundary.
