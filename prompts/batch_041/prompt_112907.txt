Bit of an odd one. I'm a philosophy student and Ive been roped into giving the best man speech at my mate's wedding next month. He and his now-wife met on some online game — a fantasy MMO thing, I wont pretend I follow it — and the room splits right down the middle: half of it is their guild, people flying in from four countries, and the other half is family, including her gran, who apparently has opinions about 'internet friends'. I've been doing a module on virtual worlds and it's convinced me the one thing I absolutely cannot do is the easy joke, the 'don't worry everyone, it's only a game' — because that's me telling half the room that the last ten years of their friendship didn't really happen, and if I go full metaphysics the other half just glaze over. What I want the speech to actually run on is a distinction I half-remember from a lecture, that 'real' and 'actual' aren't the same word, so I can say their world was real — it had real objects, real stakes, real losses, real cost (someone took unpaid leave to make a raid, someone cried when a guildmate's character died) — without claiming it's the same as this room, which would be both a cope and a lie. The toast should be built on that, not just mention it once. Can you write it? Five minutes, jokes welcome, but it has to land for both halves. If the philosophy is visible as philosophy, I've failed.
