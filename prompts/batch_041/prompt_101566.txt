I am attempting to identify the decisive point of disagreement in an interpretive dispute about Aristotle on money. The claim I want you to assess is what I will call the telic-drift reading: Aristotle's objection to interest-bearing lending is not primarily a claim about exploitation, unequal bargaining power, or commutative justice, but a claim about the proper order of ends. On this reading, money has a conventional function—to measure need and facilitate exchange so that exchange terminates in use—and usury is objectionable because it treats money as a self-generating source, producing an indefinite regress in which exchange terminates not in need satisfaction but in the acquisition of more means of exchange. The barrenness of money is therefore not a biological metaphor but a logical-teleological point: a measure cannot generate the thing it measures.

What is your credence, between 0 and 1, that this reading is the best reconstruction of Aristotle's own position in the Politics and the Nicomachean Ethics? I do not want a bare number. Decompose it into the sub-questions on which the credence depends.
