A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about truth, and I came away holding a more deflationary position than I expected. I think truth is not a substantive property that explains why our beliefs succeed or why inquiry converges; the truth predicate is primarily a device for endorsing assertions and for expressing generalizations we could not otherwise state, and the whole content of calling a proposition true is given by the equivalence between asserting that it is true and asserting it. I am not a correspondence theorist, and I am not a coherence theorist; I take truth to be thin, and I suspect that much of the apparent depth in theories of truth comes from confusing the word with the property. What I want from you is not a survey of the literature but a map of this position's exposure. What would have to be true for it to be false? Which arguments, intuitions, formal results, or developments should move me, and which are merely awkward? I am especially interested in whether the truth predicate is indispensable for stating the correctness conditions of logical inference, whether a deflationary truth predicate can do the expressive work it is supposed to do in mixed inferences and blind ascriptions, whether the liar paradox and the related family of paradoxes show that the equivalence schema cannot be had in full generality, and whether truth's normative role in inquiry, the thought that we ought to believe what is true and that truth is the aim of assertion, is something a deflationist can explain or only redescribe. Suppose someone shows that adding a truth predicate to a base theory yields new theorems about the original vocabulary, or that truth is needed to state the validity of arguments whose premises and conclusions mix object-language vocabulary and truth-talk. Is that a decisive objection, a friendly extension, or a change of subject? I also want to know what my view predicts that a correspondence or coherence view does not, and whether any of those predictions have been tested or could be. And I want to know whether the view has been insulated by qualifications: if deflationists retreat to a theory of the truth predicate rather than of truth itself, or to a minimalist account that gives up on any explanatory role, does the position still deserve the name, or has it collapsed into quietism or expressivism? Tell me what a committed deflationist would themselves concede as counting against the view, not only what an inflationist critic would say. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest if you think the position is well insulated and whether that is a problem. The thing I find most persuasive against my view is the sense that truth has a normative and explanatory role in inquiry that the equivalence schema seems to leave untouched, but I cannot tell whether that is a genuine defeater or just a rival idiom. Answer in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; write it the way you would write a long email.
