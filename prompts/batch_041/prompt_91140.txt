I'm a novelist — third book, and this one's got me stuck in a way the first two didn't. It's about a French-Algerian schoolteacher in Lyon and her English sister-in-law, a community organiser in Birmingham, and the whole thing turns on an argument they keep having about a scarf (a veil, a hijab — the family never agrees on the word) and whether the school should make room for it. The problem is I don't want to write the novel where one of them is the enlightened one and the other's a bigot; I want the reader to feel the pull of both, and right now they just talk past each other, which is useless to me because I need them to actually clash on something real.

So here's what I'd love: write the document the two of them could never quite write — a joint statement, as if a French republican universalist and an Anglo-American multiculturalist sat down in good faith and tried to produce a text they could both put their names to. Start with the shared claims; I suspect they agree more than either would admit, something about the state having to justify itself to every citizen and nobody being asked to check their deepest commitments at the door, and maybe that what really frightens them both is domination rather than difference itself. Then the precise disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept as fair — I'm fairly sure one crux is whether neutrality means the state stays silent about identities or treats them evenhandedly, and another is whether recognition is a precondition of equality or a threat to it. For each crux, give me the case or argument both sides would actually agree bears on it — the scarf in the classroom, the kosher or halal meal, the census box, whatever cuts — and what each of them predicts about it, written down in advance. And tell me where the fight is really just about words; I've got a hunch that 'secularism' and 'laïcité' don't translate into each other cleanly and alot of the heat is just that, but you tell me. Format it as a numbered set of agreed claims, then a table of the disagreements, then the cruxes. This is scaffolding for me, definately not going in the book, so don't soften it to keep the peace.
