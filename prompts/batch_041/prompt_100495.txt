I'm putting together a fiction-writing assignment built around Hofstadter's Person Paper — the one where he takes the sexist habits baked into everyday English and re-runs them in terms of race, so they suddenly read as obviously absurd. The swap is the whole trick, and everyone's already seen the race-for-sex version, so I want my students to invent a fresh one. Write me a short story, 700–1000 words, set in a world whose grammar and manners treat some category that has nothing to do with sex or race as the unmarked default — handedness, blood type, which side your family was on in some half-forgotten war, whatever, but pick one, commit to it, and keep its internal logic consistent so it doesnt collapse into an allegory with the labels filed off. Tell it in the voice of someone who sincerely thinks the default is just clean, natural common sense — a style-guide editor, a schoolteacher, a radio host — and let them be reasonable about it rather than a cartoon. Let the default surface at the level of actual words, the way the Person Paper does: a pronoun, an honorific, a stock phrase that treats one kind of person as the case and the other as the deviation. The story should never announce its own point — no character turns to the reader and explains the moral — but by the last line a careful reader should be able to reconstruct the argument that the "neutral" term was never neutral, and that you only ever catch it from the outside.
