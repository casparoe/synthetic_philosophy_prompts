I retired from a chemistry lab a few years ago and have spent the time since reading philosphy, mostly arguements over whether machines and tools can be "political" on their own or only the people who use them. I keep having gut reactions to examples and I want to know if there's a principle behind them or if I'm just being inconsistent.

A bench in our train station has armrests every two feet so nobody can lie down on it. Political, no question -- somebody decided who gets to rest there.

The freezer in my old lab opened only with a keycard. The cleaning crew had no cards, so they couldn't reach the samples and had to wait for one of us. I'd say that one's political too.

But my neighbor's gate opens only with his key. Not political, it's just his gate.

The reimbursement forms at my old university had boxes sized for dollars and nothing else. I think that's political -- it quietly assumes everyone is paid in dollars.

A screw head that only one company's driver fits, so you can't open your own machine -- I want to say political, but it also just seems like a business move, not a claim about who counts.

And the one I keep going back and forth on: a vaccine that has to stay frozen, so it never reaches the hot, poor places. Nobody set out to skip them. Is that politics, or just physics?

Can you tell me what principle, if any, covers these? Be honest about which of my verdicts it forces me to give up, say what it predicts for a case I haven't listed, and tell me what would change your mind -- which argument or example would push you to a different principle, and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
