Write a dialogue between two alignment people who both know the field well but disagree about whether "safety" should be cashed out as a worst-case guarantee or an average-case / expected-value property. A is the guarantee person: average-case targets are unserious, because with an unbounded or heavy-tailed loss the expectation gets dominated by outcomes that are astronomically unlikely and astronomically bad, so the only thing that matters is the worst case — and the alignment-relevant worst case is the one an actual optimizer, searching as hard as it can, will find, not the worst case over some hand-picked input set. B is the expected-value person: worst-case bounds are either vacuous or covertly average-case, since you can always enlarge the policy class or the input space until the bound is trivial, and "the worst case a real optimizer reaches" just smuggles back in a distribution over how hard the optimizer searches. Have them really go at each other. B should press A on where the measure over "possible inputs" actually comes from, and on why a guarantee about a limit case tells you anything about the system you deploy — especially since the deployment distribution is itself produced by the same selection process that made the model, so it isn't a fixed thing you can average over. A should press B on whether expected value is even well-defined once the utility scale is unbounded, and on whether "typical" means anything other than the mean of whatever distribution you happened to assume. Let them find at least one point where they agree the disagreement is substantive rather than verbal. Don't cite papers or hand me a reading list, I just want the argument.
