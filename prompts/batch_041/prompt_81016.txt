I'm the rapporteur for a seven-person research-funding panel at my institute and we've been deadlocked for months over what started as a procedural anoyance. Our terms of reference say we fund a project only if three things hold — the science is sound, the team can realistically deliver on the timetable, and the budget is proportionate — and we're meant to vote on the three seperately, then apply the rule. Last cycle produced the strangest profile: six of us affirmed the science, six affirmed the team, five affirmed the budget, and four of us voted against funding, so the project was declined. Every individual vote was perfectly consistent with the rule — each of the four dissenters rejected exactly one criterion and drew the obvious conclusion from it. So our written decision, which has to state the grounds in the panel's voice, currently reads: sound science, capable team, proportionate budget, declined. An appeal is pending, and the appeals panel only accepts a justification built out of claims a majority of us actually endorsed. And since we're also supposed to recommend a voting procedure for next cycle, and we're split on that in the same way, I can't even tell whether the meta-question escapes the problem or just reproduces it one level up.

Two of my colleagues have agreed to stop shouting and do this properly as an adversarial collaboration, and I'm the one circulating the result. Call the first camp the coherence camp: she holds that a decision we can't pair with a single consistent set of reasons that the panel itself endorses is defective and not merely awkward, since we are answerable to the appellant in one voice, and that the dilemma is therefore a real cost of collective decision-making to be managed — by voting on the criteria, by restricting what goes on the agenda, or by giving someone authority to author the panel's view. Call the second camp the deflationist: he holds that there is no panel mind, hence no panel belief-set that could be consistent or inconsistent, that a vote is a procedure that outputs a decision rather than a set of attitudes, and that demanding consistency of the outputs imports a constraint that only applies to a single reasoner — so the right response is to stop asking the panel to have a view at all, since a decision can be justified to each member by that member's own reasons even when there is no one reason-set we all hold. I'd like you to draft the joint statement those two could both sign: the claims they agree on, in a numbered list, and please make them substantive rather than throat-clearing; then the precise points where they still part ways, each worded so that its own side would accept the wording as fair rather than as a strawman; then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them — with, for each crux, the argument, case or piece of evidence both would agree bears on it, and each side's prediction about it recorded in advance. I suspect one crux is whether the whole paradox depends on voters voting sincerely on the criteria while their real commitment is to the outcome, so that the profile above may be an artifact of asking people the wrong question, and another is whether "we decided" can be true in the absence of "we believe"; if some part of their fight is really about words, flag it separately and say why it's verbal rather than substantive.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a table of disagreements with these columns: Crux | Coherence camp's wording | Deflationist's wording | Evidence or argument both accept as bearing on it | Coherence camp's prediction | Deflationist's prediction | Substantive or verbal. After the table, give me the same content as a JSON object with fields "agreed_claims" (list of strings), "cruxes" (list of objects with keys crux, coherence_wording, deflationist_wording, shared_evidence, coherence_prediction, deflationist_prediction, kind), "verbal_disputes" (list of strings), and "residual_disagreement" (one string stating, in a sentence both would sign, what they still disagree about).
