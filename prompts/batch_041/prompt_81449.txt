I am a PhD student working at the intersection of philosophy of mind and cognitive science, and I have come to hold a position I want to stress-test: that qualia, understood in the standard way as intrinsic, private, ineffable, and directly apprehensible features of experience, are an incoherent posit. The claim is not merely that qualia are false or explanatory idle; it is that the notion cannot be rendered consistent with the rest of what we know about perception, introspection, and the metaphysics of properties. What would count against this position? Put crudely: what would have to be true for the incoherence claim to be false?

I do not want a survey of the qualia literature, citations, or reading lists. I want a map of the position's exposure. I am less interested in the well-rehearsed zombie and inverted-spectrum arguments than in defeaters that engage the incoherence charge on its own terms. Which observations, arguments, intuitions, formal results, or theoretical developments should move me? Which of these would a committed defender of the incoherence charge concede as genuine defeaters, and which would they dismiss as merely changing the subject? I would like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an explanation of what would be decisive and what would be only a reason to qualify the view.

I am especially interested in the following pressure points.

First, the incoherence charge often proceeds by taking the four marks jointly and arguing that no single entity can have all of them. But a defender of qualia can drop one or more marks and retain a minimal phenomenal character, or can distinguish the determinable from its determinates, or can treat qualia as role-occupants in a quality space rather than as intrinsic atoms. Does the survival of any such minimal or structuralist notion count against the incoherence claim, or only against a particular over-strong characterization? What would a proponent of incoherence have to say to show that the minimal notion is either not qualia at all or collapses into something else?

Second, consider formal or empirical developments that might give the posit independent work to do. Suppose a quality-space model with a metric structure explains similarity judgments, discrimination thresholds, and the structure of introspection better than its rivals; suppose psychophysics delivers stable, fine-grained introspective reports that are not reducible to functional or representational facts; or suppose a theory of perceptual constancy or sensory substitution needs to posit a qualitative character to explain why certain transitions are experienced as changes in appearance rather than changes in belief. Would any of these count against the incoherence claim, or would they only show that some coherent surrogate for qualia is theoretically useful? What would be the strongest version of this challenge?

Third, I am worried about a self-undermining structure in the incoherence charge. If the charge relies on introspective evidence to show that the four marks cannot be jointly satisfied, does it not thereby rely on the very first-person access it denies is theoretically respectable? And if it instead relies on a functionalist or representationalist account of experience, does it not beg the question against the qualia theorist? What would count as a non-question-begging demonstration of incoherence?

Fourth, I would like to know whether the position risks unfalsifiability. If every attempt to specify qualia is either dismissed as an over-strong characterization or reclassified as a coherent surrogate, then what would it take for the incoherence claim itself to be false? Is there a version of qualia that would satisfy the incoherence charge's own standards, and if not, is that a problem for the charge or a virtue of it? I would also like an honest assessment of whether the position has been insulated by definitional moves, and whether that insulation is acceptable for a claim of incoherence rather than mere falsity.

Finally, tell me what the position predicts that its rivals do not. If qualia are an incoherent posit, what should we expect to find when we look at the psychology and neuroscience of introspection, at the failures of introspection, at the meta-problem of why we seem to have qualia, and at the persistence of the intuition that experience has a qualitative character? Which of those predictions are borne out, and which would count against the position if they were not?
