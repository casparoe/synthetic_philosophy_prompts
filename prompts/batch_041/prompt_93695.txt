I'm the TA for a fourth-year seminar on animals, nature and ethics, and the last three weeks have been on whether wild animals are living good lives or bad ones and whether habitat destruction is therefore good or bad for them. I have to write one of the final exam questions and I'm stuck, because every mock answer I've seen this term is a slogan -- either wild life is a bloodbath so clearing the forest is a mercy, or habitat loss is self-evidently a catastrophe -- and nothing in between. I want a question that makes them actually do the reasoning instead of picking a team. The case we've been hammering all term is this: one hectare of lowland tropical forest versus the same hectare after conversion to rough pasture. The forest holds roughly 1000 small, short-lived, high-fecundity animals -- call them Type A, they breed fast and almost all of them die young -- plus about 10 individuals of a much larger, longer-lived species, Type B. The pasture holds about 200 Type A but 40 Type B, since the big grazers do better on open ground. For the sake of the argument we've been scoring lives on a scale where 0 marks a life neither worth living nor worth not living, and we've been using -0.5 for a Type A life and +8 for a Type B life, though I keep reminding them those are placeholders we're using to expose the structure of the argument, not measurements. What I want from the exam question is this: make them state the argument that converting the hectare is good for the animals, make them work out what the placeholder numbers actually imply, make them work out how good a Type A life would have to be before the verdict flips the other way, and then make them commit on which premise they'd bet against and what would have to be discovered for the question to be settled. I also want them to say out loud what the whole calculation is taking for granted -- in particular about whether bringing an animal into existence can count as a harm or a benefit to it.

So please write the exam question itself (60 minutes, essay, closed book, no calculator beyond arithmetic), a model answer of around 650 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 that names the specific moves an A answer makes that a B answer won't, with five must-hit points, the marks each is worth, and a one-line note for each on how students typically get it wrong. Two things to avoid: I don't want the question to be answerable by naming camps or reciting anybody's position, and I don't want the model answer to stop at "well, it depends whether their lives are worth living" -- the arithmetic has to actually be done and the threshold named, and the sensitivity of the conclusion to which species you're unsure about is the bit I most want to see them get. Also take animal sentience as given, I'm not marking a debate about that again, and nothing in the question or model answer should need a citation or a reading, they're writing straight off the case.

One last thing, since I'll be marking off this model answer and scheme rather than just admiring it: have the response tell me how confident it is in the model answer and the mark allocation, and single out the one step it's least sure about -- I'd rather know where it's guessing than get a show of authority I can't check.
