I've been building a dumb little rule-based simulator of a house — objects, rooms, rules like 'if the agent is holding the cup and sets it down, the cup ends up on the table' — and it works out what's true after each action. Fun for about a week, and then it turned into four seperate nightmares, and I honestly can't tell if they're one nightmare or four. First one: every rule has to end with a giant list of everything that didn't change. Write the cup rule and the simulator concludes that the cup is now on the table and also that the lights went off, the door swung open and the cat has ceased to exist, because nothing in my rule said otherwise. So I keep appending 'the lamp is where it was, the door is still shut, the cat is still a cat' — for every rule, for every object, forever — and every time I add a new object I have to go back and edit every rule to mention that this object too survives being ignored. Second one: I write 'if the agent turns the handle, the door opens', and then it's 'unless it's locked', then 'unless the handle's broken', then 'unless something's wedged against it', then 'unless the agent is a ghost'. The exception list never closes, and the more honest I am about it the less the rule predicts. Third one: side effects. Turning the tap on fills the sink, the sink filling wets the floor, the wet floor makes the cat slip, and now I'm writing rules for consequences I never thought about when I wrote the tap rule, and if I write them in the wrong order the system gets muddled about which change caused which.

Fourth thing, which may or may not belong with the other three: when I sit down to write the knowledge base at all, I have to decide which facts about the world are worth writing down in the first place. The colour of the cup matters for matching it to the saucer and not at all for pouring into it — I just know that, nobody taught me — and when I try to get the system to work out for itself which facts about a situation are the ones that matter for the action it's considering, it can't, and I can't explain to it how I do it either.

So: same problem wearing four hats, or four problems? I suspect at least one of them is famous and the others are its awkward cousins, but I don't know which is which, and what I want out of an answer is the carving rather than a label: for each one, what exactly the problem is, what it isn't, which of the four are really the same thing and which differ in kind rather than just in difficulty. And the bit I'd most like answered — does the fourth one have a name of its own, and does anyone think it's solvable at all, or is it just a fact of life that you can't get around? For context, I did an economics degree and I've never read any AI stuff, so I don't know the vocabulary and I probably won't recognise the terms you use.
