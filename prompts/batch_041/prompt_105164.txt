Right, here's something I keep chewing over and can't get straight. I've ended up with a pile of gut verdicts about what a probability statement actually *is*, and they don't obviously fit any one story. Here's the pile:

- The 1/2 on a fair coin is a fact about the coin and the toss, not about my head. If every mind vanished it'd still be 1/2.
- '70% chance of rain tomorrow' isn't a fact about the world in the same way. It's the forecaster's degree of belief, and if two forecasters disagree I don't think one of them has to be simply wrong.
- But 'this radium atom has a 50% chance of decaying in the next 1600 years' does feel like a fact, even though it's one unrepeatable atom that either decays or doesn't. So single-case doesn't automatically mean subjective.
- 'The chance Caesar crossed the Rubicon' — I want to say that's 0 or 1, and my 0.9 is just my ignorance.
- Now the one that wrecks me. A bent coin whose bias was fixed at manufacture and which I've never flipped. I want to say the chance it lands heads next toss is objective — there's a fact about the coin — but I also want to say the only thing I can *mean* by it is my credence, since I've got nothing else to go on. Those two wants feel incompatible.
- And a case I made up to test myself: a process that runs exactly once, is never observed, and leaves no trace, but which our best physics says is genuinely indeterministic. Is there a fact about its chance, or is 'chance' just empty here? I lean towards there being a fact, which surprises me.
- Also: 'the probability that a randomly picked villager is left-handed' — pick by household and you get one number, pick by person and you get another. Both seem defensible, which can't be right.

Is there one account — frequentist, propensity, subjectivist, or some hybrid — that gets me all of these, or close? I suspect the only thing that saves every verdict is a messy two-storey story (objective chance for some cases, credence for others, plus a rule for when the second tracks the first), and that a cleaner single-storey view would force me to drop one of the bullets. If so, tell me which bullet has to go and why. I'd also like to know what the surviving view says about a case I haven't put down: a deterministic universe where the coin's landing was fixed at the Big Bang and nobody could ever have known it — is calling that '1/2' just a lie, or is it still fine?

And please be straight with me rather than polite: if the whole way I've posed this is confused — say, if I'm treating 'chance' and 'credence' as rival answers to one question when they're really answers to two different ones — just say so instead of humouring me and building a principle on top of the mess.
