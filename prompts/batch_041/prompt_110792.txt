Call it the accountability test. Two strangers swerving to avoid each other on the pavement: no such warrant. A rowing crew, a couple cooking dinner, two people carrying a sofa up a stairwell: warrant. And it cuts across the shared-intention cases rather than just tracking them — some of them pass it and some don't.

What I want the proposal to do is make this precise enough to be attacked. State the question so it has a determinate answer — something like: does the accountability test sort the contested cases correctly, and is the warrant it picks out grounded in prior shared intention or is it the more basic phenomenon?
