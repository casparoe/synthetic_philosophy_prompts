I spent thirty-five years in a physics department and retired a few years back, and since then I've been reading philosphy on my own, mostly the arguments about God. Last week in a reading group a retired philosopher kept saying "whatever begins to exist has a cause" as if it were a law of nature, and honestly it carried his whole argument for him: the universe began to exist, so the universe has a cause, and the cause is God. I've spent my life around causes and something about that sentence felt slippery but I couldn't put my finger on it.

What I'd like from you is a menu of the different precise things that sentence could actually be claiming, with a verdict on each — true, false, or just a definition dressed up as a claim. Then tell me which of the readings is the one that actually does the work of getting him from "the universe began to exist" to "the universe has a cause", because I suspect one reading is carrying everything and the rest is camouflage.

Things I keep bumping into and can't untangle: what does "begins to exist" mean, and does it mean the same thing for the universe as for a chair or an idea? What kind of cause is meant — the pushing kind, the necessary-condition kind, the explains-why kind, something else? Does the cause have to be a thing, or can it be a state, a law, a fact? And does the principle survive what we know from quantum mechanics, where events seem to happen without any cause, or does it survive only as some weaker statistical or in-practice claim? Also, is the principle supposed to apply inside the universe as a physical regularity, or is it supposed to be a piece of metaphysics that applies to the whole thing from outside? Those seem like very different animals to me and he slides between them.

Keep it practical — I want a list I can bring to the next meeting, with the reading I should have pushed back on marked out. No need to send me off to read anybody, just tell me what the options are and which one I was actually arguing with.
