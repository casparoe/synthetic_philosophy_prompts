I've been thinking alot about what David Hume would make of a modern monetary arrangement, and I'd like your best reconstruction rather than a summary of his actual essays. Here's the setup. A central bank issues a digital currency that pays no interest but loses 2% of its value every month unless you spend it; it also pays a negative rate on bank reserves. Separately, a private consortium issues a stablecoin pegged to a basket of commodities and short-term government bonds. The stablecoin's supply is managed by a smart contract that expands when the basket's market value rises and charges a small fee when the peg drifts. Most cross-border trade is settled in this stablecoin. A government with a persistent trade deficit imposes a tariff on imports and uses the revenue to buy the stablecoin, hoping to prop up its exchange rate. What would Hume say about each part? In particular: (1) Is the stablecoin money in his sense, or is it more like a bill of exchange or a commodity? (2) Does the tariff fix the trade deficit, or does it just change the route by which his adjustment process works? (3) Are the expiring currency and negative rates inflationary, deflationary, or neither, and what would he say about the short-run vs long-run distinction? (4) Would he predict that the expiring currency drives out the stablecoin, or the other way around, and why? (5) If the stablecoin's supply grows with the basket's value, does that make it closer to commodity money or credit money on his view? Im not looking for citations or a reading list, just a careful extrapolation from his arguments. And please dont just say he'd oppose it because he was a classical economist; be specific about which of his claims apply and which dont.
