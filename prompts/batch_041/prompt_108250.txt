So I'm in this members' club -- we own our own building, we have a four-page constitution, monthly meetings -- and I've been going around in circles on something for weeks, and I want to know if there's a name for it, because I keep needing the distinction and I don't have it.

The setup: the first line of the constitution says any rule can be changed by a two-thirds vote of the members present at a meeting. In April we voted, two-thirds exactly, to change that line so that changing the constitution now takes ninety percent. Nobody thought twice about it. Last month we tried to change it back, got 84%, failed. Then somebody pointed out that the April vote might not have been valid at all -- because if the ninety percent rule is in force now, and it's the rule about how rules get changed, then what rule was in force when we made it? We've had three meetings about this and produced two contradictory sets of minutes, and everyones just proceeding as if the one that suits them is correct.

Then it got worse, and this is the part I actually care about. Three more proposals came in:

1. Any vote taken in violation of the rules is retroactively valid if a later meeting ratifies it.
2. Redefine "member" so that two people who resigned in 2019 were never members -- which would make several old votes pass that previously failed.
3. Change the amendment clause so that amending it requires unanimity of everyone who has ever been a member. One of them is dead.

My gut says all four of these are the same trick and that our whole constitution is basically a bluff. A friend says no, the first one is just ordinary rule-making and only 2 and 3 are the dodgy ones. I can't tell if thats a real distinction or one he's making up.

So: does this have a name, or an established treatment? I don't want to be told "it's a paradox" or "it's self-reference," obviously it is, I want to know which of these four moves are actually the same move and which aren't, where the line sits between changing a rule and changing what counts as having followed it, whether a rule can reach backwards and re-describe the past as compliant, and whether "you can't change this" is ever anything other than a bluff or a trap. If nothing established covers it, coin something and tell me what it leaves out -- a bare label won't help me.
