For a political theory seminar I'm taking on hierarchy and self-reference, I've been asked to test a concept from formal systems against a political episode, and I want help thinking it through rather than a summary of the history.

The concept is Hofstadter's, from Gödel, Escher, Bach. A hierarchy is tangled when you cannot move up or down its levels without eventually arriving back where you started, so that what controls and what is controlled turn out to be the same thing; Escher's Drawing Hands is the picture of it, two hands each drawing the other. Hofstadter's claim is that such a picture is determinate only because of an inviolate level: a level that nothing inside the picture draws, the artist standing outside the frame, which fixes what the tangled parts are doing. My question is whether that idea survives the move from formal systems into politics, and I want to test it on Watergate.

Take the special prosecutor. He is appointed from inside the executive branch, his subpoena power issues from the branch he is investigating, and the president under investigation can have him removed, which is what happened in October 1973, when Cox was fired for insisting on the tapes and the attorney general and his deputy resigned rather than carry out the order. The president's counterclaim, executive privilege, was a doctrine whose scope only the courts could settle. That is the tangle, and I don't need it retold.

What I want is the levels made explicit. Assign each actor, the prosecutor, the White House, the attorney general, the courts, the grand jury, the House Judiciary Committee, the press, the electorate, to a level, and say precisely what makes this a tangle rather than an ordinary quarrel between branches. Then take each candidate for the inviolate level and test it against what actually happened: the unanimous Supreme Court order to surrender the tapes, the officials who resigned rather than obey, the pardon that ended the matter without a verdict. Which of these stood outside the picture, and which only looked outside it from where the actors were standing? If every candidate turns out to be drawn by a hand inside the frame, if the meaning of the Constitution is settled by the parties it is meant to constrain and the Court's judgments are executed by the executive it judges, then say so, and tell me what follows for the theory: does it need a different account of how tangles are resolved, or does the political case expose a distinction the formal-systems version never had to make? Where the theory is simply silent, I would rather you said that than pressed it into service. And one last thing: is the pardon a move made from the inviolate level, or evidence that the level was never the law at all, but the discretion of whoever holds the force?
