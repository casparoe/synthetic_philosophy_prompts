I am setting the take-home final for an upper-level seminar on consciousness, and I need one essay question that cannot be answered by reciting the difference between access and phenomenal consciousness. The question must turn on a distinction I will stipulate in the stem, which my students have not encountered: that a content can be globally available in two grades. Call them report-grade availability, where the content can be explicitly queried, held in working memory, and verbally reported, and silent-grade availability, where the content is never reportable and never enters working memory as such, yet systematically constrains inference, decision, and the guidance of action across many downstream systems, showing up only in its effects. The scenario I plan to give them is a patient whose visual system affords some content, say the affective gist of a scene, silent-grade availability only, contrasted with a matched patient in whom the same content is processed only locally and modulates nothing downstream. Please write the essay question, a model answer of roughly 700 words, and a marking scheme built from five must-hit points worth 20 marks in total, each with a one-line note on how candidates typically miss it. The question should force students to say whether a theory that identifies consciousness with global broadcast is a theory of access or of phenomenal consciousness, whether it can be both without collapsing the distinction, and what the silent-grade case is supposed to show either way; it should separate students who understand what access has to mean from those who have quietly equated access with reportability. Write everything in American English.
