So I'm taking this formal epistemology seminar — 9am on Mondays, which is its own kind of epistemic hazard — and the first half of the term was all scoring rules and the accuracy-dominance argument: the idea that your credences have to be probabilities because any non-probabilistic credence function is accuracy-dominated, i.e. there's always some probability function that's closer to the truth no matter how the world turns out. That part I'm sold on, it's a beautiful argument. Then last week the prof introduced imprecise credences, where instead of a single probability function your doxastic state is a whole set of them — you're 'mushy' about the exact number rather than committed to one. And the room basically split in half and we've been going in circles ever since. The precision people say an imprecise state can't be a genuine belief state because it can't guide decision: if all you've got is a set of functions, there's no fact of the matter about what you should do, you get indeterminate choices, and the imprecise agent is an easy mark for a sequence of bets that leaves them worse off no matter what. The imprecise people say a single sharp function is a fiction that pretends you're certain about probabilities you've got no business being certain about, and they insist you can run the same accuracy machinery on sets of functions and it doesn't just spit out 'be sharp' — so the precision people are smuggling in an assumption and calling it a result. Every point gets a ready reply, nobody's moved an inch, and honestly I'm starting to wonder if they're even disagreeing.

My question isn't really who's right, it's what kind of disagreement this is. Is it a substantive disagreement about the nature of credence, or is part of it verbal — the two sides using 'credence' or 'belief' for different things, one a psychological state and one a normative ideal? Or are they answering different questions under one heading, one side about what a rational agent's representational state can be and the other about how to choose when you're indeterminate? My own hypothesis, and feel free to shoot it down, is that the decision-theoretic objection and the accuracy-based objection aren't even aimed at the same target, so they can both be right and it only looks like a contradiction because both sides insist on calling the object 'credence.' If that's wrong I want to know where the real crux actually is. And whatever your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress here — a formal result, a distinction, a change of question — or whether this is just a case where the question is under-specified and I should stop waiting for a single verdict. Please seperate what people who actually work on this agree on from what's genuinely contested, and don't hand me a contested view as if it were the consensus — I want to know where the live fault lines are.
