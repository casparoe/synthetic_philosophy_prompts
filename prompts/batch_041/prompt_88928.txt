ive been reading too much pascals mugging / fanaticism stuff lately and i want a poem about expected value fanaticism -- the claim that a small enough probability of a big enough payoff dominates everything, so no finite amount of ordinary evidence ever really settles it, and that all the usual fixes (discount tiny probabilities, cap the utility scale, refuse to put numbers on it) cost you something. i want the form to do the work rather than the content: the stakes should escalate by orders of magnitude stanza by stanza and the form should escalate to match (longer lines, wilder rhymes, numbers that stop fitting on the page), and the poem should be unable to end, because any ending you could write is dominated by a longer one -- so the last lines have to turn that failure into the argument instead of just stopping. voice of someone genuinely convinced, not a satirist. one stanza should be the counter-mugging, the reverse wager that cancels the first, and the poem should show why that symmetry doesnt get you out. someone who knows the literature should recognise the moves and not wince; someone who doesnt should still just think its a good poem. 20-30 lines, rhymed if you can pull it off.
