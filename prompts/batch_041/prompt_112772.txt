I'm a second-year student in a philosophy of emotion course, and something that happened over winter break has been nagging at me in a way that feels embarrassingly disproportionate to the event. I spent most of the break playing through a long narrative video game — the kind where your choices accumulate, characters remember what you did, and the story bends around your decisions. About forty hours in, a companion I had grown genuinely fond of was killed, and she was killed as a direct result of a call I had made several chapters earlier, a call I had believed at the time was the right one. I put the controller down and cried, and for two days afterward I carried a guilt that I would describe, without qualification, as real — the same texture of feeling I have had after actually hurting someone I love. When I mentioned this to my roommate, he laughed and said it was absurd to feel guilty about a person who does not exist, that I had been moved to tears by lines of code, and that whatever I was feeling was at best a kind of make-believe I had chosen to indulge.

I don't think he is simply wrong, but I cannot articulate why he is not right, and the account I would ordinarily give of my own emotions does not seem to fit here. When I am sad, it is normally because I believe that something has happened; in this case I know perfectly well that nothing happened — no one died, the character never existed, and the whole sequence was authored by a team of writers I will never meet. Yet the guilt does not feel like a game or a performance; it feels like the genuine article, and it is specifically guilt rather than a vague melancholy, which seems to matter. What I would like from you is not reassurance that my feelings are valid but a careful sorting-out of what is actually going on. Take the emotion at face value: what would have to be true for it to count as a real emotion, and what does that account owe me as an explanation of why it is guilt-flavored and why it attached to this particular character? Then take my roommate's side: if the feeling is not a genuine emotion but something else — pretense, a game, a misfiring — what exactly is it, and does that story explain why it has the staying power and the felt authority of the real thing? Finally, tell me whether the fact that I made the choice that killed her, rather than merely watching it happen, changes which explanation I should accept; I have a suspicion that it does, but I cannot say why, and I would rather be told which account actually survives scrutiny than be told that it is fine to feel however I feel.
