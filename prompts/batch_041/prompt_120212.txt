I'm a retired physical chemist — 35 years of spectroscopy and stat mech — and I've recently fallen down the philosophy of science rabbit hole. The fight I keep tripping over is reduction vs emergence, and I can't tell how much of it is a real disagreement and how much is people using "reduce" for five different things. Take the role of a philosopher of science who works on inter-theoretic reduction and knows the antireductionist literature cold. Think carefully before writing, and start with a short brainstorm: list the seperate things people might mean by "reduce" (deriving one theory from another, explaining a macro-regularity, property identity, ontological dependence, prediction) and flag which of them the two sides actually disagree about, since I suspect alot of the shouting is verbal.

Then draft the joint statement that a committed reductionist and a committed emergentist could both put their names to. The reductionist: every special-science generalization is in principle derivable from and explained by physics, given the right bridge principles, and autonomy talk is a promissory note on our ignorance. The emergentist: at least some higher-level regularities are explanatory in their own right, and no amount of micro-detail hands you them for free.

Include: (1) numbered agreed claims — covering supervenience, what a bridge principle would have to be to do the work, and what neither side is committed to (e.g. does either of them have to buy downward causation? say if you think not); (2) a disagreements table, each view phrased so its own side would accept the wording; (3) the cruxes — I'd guess one is whether multiple realizability really blocks reduction, and another is what it shows that the same macro-behaviour keeps recurring in systems with completely different micro-physics — and for each crux a case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Say which parts, if any, are merely verbal. No citations or reading lists please, I've got no internet out here.
