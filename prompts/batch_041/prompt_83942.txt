I run a casual reading-and-discussion night out of the co-working space where I rent a desk — mostly software people, a couple of econ PhD students, and one retired guy who insists on correcting everyone and who will probably tell me this is "just a public goods game." There are about 18 of us, we get 90 minutes on a Thursday evening, and this month it's my turn to lead. I want to do something hands-on instead of the usual someone-presents-a-paper-and-we-all-nod format. The theme is cooperation, and there's a specific thing I want people to feel rather than just hear. It's easy to say "in repeated interactions you cooperate because you'll see the person again," and everyone nods, and then the moment you add any real messiness — where you genuinely can't tell whether someone stiffed you on purpose or whether their help just didn't come through, or they think they helped but it never landed — the whole thing falls apart and everyone starts punishing everyone and cooperation dies. I've definately watched this happen in my actual work life more than once. What I want is for the room to live through that collapse and only afterwards realize it was about information, not about goodwill. And there's a second layer I'd love them to bump into: that the obvious fix — be forgiving, give people the benefit of the doubt — also opens you up to getting played by someone who's just pretending it was an accident, so forgiveness isn't free, your trading one failure mode for another.

So what I'm asking for is a runnable game for about 18 people in 90 minutes, setup and debrief included. The backbone should be a prisoner's-dilemma-style payoff (cooperate or defect; mutual cooperation beats mutual defection, but defecting on a cooperator is the single best outcome), played over a bunch of rounds, with no real money — points or tokens, and I'll put up a bag of good coffee as a prize for whoever finishes with the most, which people take way more seriously than I expect. The key requirement: the "noise" has to be baked into the mechanics so it isn't just me standing at the front narrating mistakes. I want some genuine randomness that sometimes makes a cooperation fail to register, or makes a player mis-see what their partner did, and I need you to decide who knows about that randomness and when — does everyone know the error rate from the start? does the person whose cooperation got eaten know it wasn't the other person's fault? do the two players ever find out they disagree about what happened? I'd rather the mechanics do the work than a lecture, so keep any explanation up front to a bare minimum and let the weirdness emerge on its own.

Please give me the full rules and a payoff table that doesn't need cash; the materials and how to set up 18 people (do they move around, are they paired at random each round, is there a public scoreboard?); a round-by-round timeline that actually fits in 90 minutes including the debrief; and then the debrief itself, which should map specific moments from the game onto the underlying idea — strict you-did-X-so-I-do-X reciprocity versus forgiving strategies, why noise is what breaks the "you'll see them again so you behave" logic, and the exploitability trade-off. Also tell me the failure modes: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this (I'm betting at least one person games the noise and everyone gets furious about it, and that the correcting guy declares the whole thing trivial), and how I should handle each. And be straight with me about what this activity can't show — I assume it can't really show the evolution side, like how strategies spread through a population over generations, but tell me what else it leaves out so I don't oversell it in the wrap-up.
