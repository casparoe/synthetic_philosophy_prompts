I have been trying to state the principle behind my own archaeological inferences, and I keep failing. Here are the cases and my verdicts.

A burial with a wagon, horse harness, and a few personal ornaments. I think we are warranted in inferring that the mourners intended to equip the dead for some continued journey or use, but not in inferring a specific doctrine of an afterlife.

A hoard of coins with the emperor's face scratched off, buried in a pot. I think we are warranted in inferring a deliberate, socially meaningful act of rejection, perhaps political, but not in inferring the individual beliefs of whoever scratched them.

A foundation deposit of miniature axes, too small to use, buried under a house. I think we are warranted in inferring a protective ritual at construction, but not a belief in a specific deity.

A single shoe hidden in a chimney. I do not think we are warranted in inferring a protective ritual; it could easily be lost or discarded. I notice that this verdict sits awkwardly with the previous one, since both involve a concealed object in a building.

A large pit filled with the butchered bones of many animals, some still connected at the joints. I think we are warranted in inferring a communal feast, and perhaps an institution of feasting, but not a belief about the animals.

A cave with handprints. I do not think we are warranted in inferring a ritual, because the production could be play, marking, or something else.

A building with a central hearth, many storage jars, and no obvious sleeping areas. I think we are warranted in inferring a communal or institutional function, such as a feasting hall, but not a specific political system.

A single seal impression on a jar handle. I do not think we are warranted in inferring a bureaucratic institution; it might be a trader's mark.

A cemetery in which some graves have many goods and others none. I think we are warranted in inferring some social differentiation, but not hereditary inequality.

A cache of unused arrowheads under a burned floor. I think we are warranted in inferring deliberate storage, but not a ritual deposit, because the arrowheads could have been kept for later use.

My tentative principle is this: an inference to a belief is warranted only when the object's production would be irrational or unintelligible without that belief; an inference to an institution is warranted only when the distribution of objects across a site shows a pattern that no individual practical reason could produce. But this principle seems to fail on the foundation deposit versus the hidden shoe, and perhaps on the large pit versus the cave.

Is there a principle that gets all or most of these verdicts? State it precisely, with the case that motivates each clause. What does it predict for cases I have not listed, such as a temple with thousands of identical figurines, or a house abandoned with pots still on the floor? Which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? If no single principle works, give me the smallest set of principles and say which verdict each fails to accommodate. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Do not give me citations or a reading list. Please write in American English.
