Here's the thing I keep noticing.

Last year I had to pick between two apartments and I picked the one with the big kitchen window. If you'd asked me why, I'd have said: the light, obviously, I need light in the morning. It felt completely true. Then months later my girlfriend pointed out that the place I turned down was actually sunnier, and that the real reason was probably that the landlord of the other one reminded me of a guy I hated at work. She was right. But here's what gets me: the reason I gave at the time didn't feel like a guess. It felt exactly as solid as any reason I've ever given for anything. Looking back at it from the inside, I can't find any difference between that reason and a good one. There's no little marker that says "made up."

Same thing happens when I argue with my brother about politics. He gives a reason, I knock a hole in it, and instead of backing off he's got another reason ready, then another. And I'm sure he's not lying — he looks like he's genuinely reading something off a screen inside his head. But then I catch myself doing the exact same thing. If someone asks me why I think some policy is a bad idea, I can always produce a reason, and it always feels like I'm reporting something I found rather than making it up on the spot. When I watch other people do it I can see the machinery — I can tell he's just grabbing whatever reason is handy. But when I do it, it feels like reporting. Which is obviously a weird double standard to have.

And the part that really bugs me is that when I turn inward it never comes back empty. It never says "no idea." I ask myself why I feel a certain way about someone, and there's always an answer sitting there, fully formed, delivered with total confidence, whether or not it's anywhere near the truth. It's like a smoke detector that goes off at the same volume whether there's a fire or you're just making toast. If the alarm sounds identical either way, then the alarm isn't telling me anything — and yet I keep trusting it, because from the inside there's nothing to distrust.

The closest word I've found is "confabulation," but that seems to be about people with brain damage who invent stories about why they did things — the split brain patients, that kind of thing. I'm not brain damaged. This is just a Tuesday. And "lying to yourself" doesn't fit either, because lying seems to require knowing the truth and hiding it, and I honestly don't feel like I'm hiding anything from myself.

So: does this have a name? Is there a literature on it that I've just never run into?
