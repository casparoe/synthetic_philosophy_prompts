So I've basically talked myself into a hard-line methodological individualism about the social sciences, and I want you to tell me what it's exposed to. The view: if you want to explain some social outcome — a riot, a bank run, a norm that sticks around for three generations — the actual causal story has to bottom out in facts about individual people, what they believed, wanted, and did. Everything else — "the market punished the reckless banks," "the institution incentivized X," "the culture of honor demanded Y" — is either shorthand for a pile of individual-level facts or a placeholder for an explanation nobody's finished yet. I'm not even inclined to soften it with an "in principle" hedge; I think the micro story just is the causal story.

Don't give me the lazy objections — I know the "society is more then the sum of its parts" stuff and it doesn't move me. I want the cases and arguments a serious defender would actually have to work for, sorted from "this would make me give it up" through "serious" to "annoying but survivable." Some places I'd like you to press specifically:

- Cases where you hold all the individual-level facts fixed and the outcome still changes because the arrangement of those individuals changed. Does that even make sense under MI, or does MI have to redescribe the arrangement as itself an individual-level fact (somebody's belief, somebody's disposition)? And if it does, is the redescription doing any real work, or is it relabeling?
- Social facts that seem to need mutual or collective attitudes rather than any one person's attitude — the common-knowledge thing, where the fact that everyone knows that everyone knows is what's doing the causal work, and no single head contains it. Is that a genuine defeater, or does it dissolve into individual beliefs at the limit?
- Predicates defined by collective acceptance rather than by anything natural — money, a marriage, a legal person, an unemployment rate. When an explanation cites one of these, is it citing an individual-level fact at all, or a social kind that individuals merely track? This is where I'm least comfortable.
- Structural interventions that work without changing anyone's psychology — flipping a default, changing a rule, changing who is connected to whom. If you can move the outcome with no change in any individual's beliefs or desires, what's the causal story?

For each item, tell me (a) how decisive it really is — fatal, serious, nuisance — and (b) what MI's own defenders would concede rather than deny. I care less about which objection sounds strongest and more about which ones MI can't wriggle out of.

Then the honest part. I've got a nagging suspicion that "every social explanation reduces to individual-level facts" is the kind of claim that can't lose, because whatever the counterexample, you can always say there's *some* individual-level story underneath, we just haven't found it yet. If that's the shape of the view, say so straight, and then give me a verdict rather than a menu: is that a defect, or is it fine for a thesis of this kind to be true by construction? And if you think the view only survives in a weakened form, name the form and tell me why the weakening isn't just a concession to the holists.
