A coin is about to be flipped. On Heads the world contains exactly one observer; on Tails it contains a trillion observers, all in evidentially identical situations, each with a life worse than the Heads observer's by a margin too small for anyone to notice or care about. You are one of the observers, and your only information is that you exist.

Give me your credence that, on that information alone, you should be close to certain the coin landed Tails — equivalently, that the self-indication assumption (your existence is likelier in worlds containing more observers) is the right treatment here. I want the number and its decomposition, not the number alone. At minimum: (i) the probability that the correct general account of self-locating belief is SIA rather than the self-sampling assumption, on which bare existence is evidentially inert; (ii) the probability that the reference class can be specified tightly enough to make either account determinate — which observers count, whether merely possible ones are included, and how a single subject of experience is individuated; (iii) the probability that the fact of one's existence is a legitimate likelihood-bearing datum rather than an indexical necessity that cannot confirm anything; (iv) the probability that SIA withstands its own wider implications, such as its pressure toward expecting oneself to be a Boltzmann brain or toward the conclusion that the world is far larger than it appears; plus any further term you think belongs. Give each term a number and one line of justification, then combine them. Finish by naming the term your final number is most sensitive to, saying what would move it most, and stating whether the number commits you to the presumptuous philosopher's verdict that a world of vastly many marginally worse-off observers is to be preferred.
