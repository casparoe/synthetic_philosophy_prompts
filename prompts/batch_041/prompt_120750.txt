So I've been trying to figure out whether I actually like my job or whether I've just been telling myself a story about it, and my therapist suggested I start noticing what I'm feeling *while* I'm feeling it, instead of reconstructing it at the end of the day. That sent me down a rabbit hole about whether people can even do that. Two things I read have been bugging me. First, there's this researcher who hands people beepers that go off at random moments and has them jot down whatever was going through their head at that exact instant, and apparently alot of people come back shocked — some discover that at those sampled moments there was no inner speech at all, even though they'd have sworn they talk to themselves constantly, and others report these wordless shapings-toward-a-problem that they'd never have listed as "thinking." Second, there's the experiment where people pick which of two faces they find more attractive, then the experimenter secretly swaps in the face they *didn't* pick and asks them to explain their choice — and a decent chunk of people happily explain why they chose the face they actually rejected, complete with confident, made-up reasons.

Now I'm in a seminar where we're reading about introspection as a source of evidence, and I keep going back and forth. A friend of mine says the beeper stuff doesn't show introspection is unreliable at all — it just shows people usually don't bother to introspect, they guess from their pet theory about themselves, and when you force them to attend to the present moment they're actually pretty good at it. That feels like a dodge to me, but I can't articulate why, and honestly I'm not sure it's wrong.

Anyway, please write an essay on whether introspection is a reliable source of evidence about one's own current mental states. Weigh the best arguments on both sides — including the suggestion that we need to seperate occurrent introspection from our standing beliefs and theories about our own minds — and reach a verdict rather than a both-sides mush. If you think it's reliable about some contents (say, what you're currently seeing or feeling) but not others (say, why you made a judgment, or whether you're really thinking in words), say so and defend it. I'd especially like you to deal with the dodge: when someone's report about their own experience turns out to be wrong, does that show introspection failed, or only that they never introspected in the first place? And does it matter for the reliability question that some people seem to have no inner speech at all while others can't imagine not having it?
