Next month I'm doing a long interview for my tiny podcast with this guy who's been running an online Nomic league for like a decade -- you know, the game where changing the rules is itself a move, and the rules can even change the rule that tells you how to change rules. He's got this whole thing where he thinks Nomic is a working model of how a real constitution can legitimately rewrite its own foundations, and he gets kinda smug about it. I never went to law school and honestly half the fun for me is watching him have to defend the thing under pressure, so I want questions that dig into the guts of it instead of letting him give the standard "well that's just the paradox, isn't it" speech. Can you draft me 9 or 10 questions, and for the ones where there's an obvious dodge, a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give?

I definately want at least one question each on these, plus whatever else you think earns its place:

- the whole mutable vs. immutable rule thing. In the original setup some rules are "immutable" and take precedence over the others, but you can turn one into a mutable rule first and then change it. So is immutability a real difference or just a higher vote threshold wearing a costume? And if a majority can vote to make the immutable rule changeable, wasn't it really changeable the whole time? Follow-up for if he says it's a genuine difference in kind.

- the exact spot where the self-amendment paradox bites. A rule that lays out how rules get changed, applied to itself -- can it authorize its own change, or does it need to already have the authority it's handing out? Make him point to the precise step where the circle closes. If he says "that rule just doesn't apply to itself," ask how it knows to exempt itself without already having the very authority it's trying to grant.

- whether there's always some background rule that never gets touched -- the rule about which rules count as rules, or who gets to say a vote was valid. Is that part of the game or outside it? If he says there isn't one, how does he stop an infinite regress? If he says there is, get him to name it and say how you'd know when you'd hit bottom.

- a scenario I want to spring on him: a player proposes a rule that says every rule change passed by a simple majority before this rule takes effect is now void, and it passes by a simple majority. Is it in force? Does it wipe itself out? Does what the written rules say settle it, or does it come down to what the players decide afterward? I want him to actually rule on it and defend the ruling.

- the rule that says you have to obey the rules. Turns out you can change that one too. So if the players vote to stop obeying the rules, is that a legal move inside the game or is it the game ending? And what does that say about whether a legal system's authority can come from inside itself?

- how far any of this carries over to real constitutions, especially the ones that try to lock a clause so it can never be amended. Does Nomic say that lock is real or just a speed bump? Would a later amendment repealing the lock be valid, and who decides?

- a rule reaching forward in time: suppose a rule says no rule passed before turn 10 may be changed. On turn 15, does it still bind? Why or why not? I want to hear how a rule can tie the hands of people who never agreed to it.

- and then the ending: I want a question that asks him what would actually count against his claim that Nomic is a genuine model of self-amending law -- what he'd have to see or hear that would make him drop it. And maybe one about the strongest disanalogy, since in Nomic you can always just stop playing and walk away, and real legal systems don't let you do that so easily.

Keep them conversational enough that I can actually read them out loud. Also flag for me which two or three of these he's prolly most likely to wriggle out of, so I can save them for the end.
