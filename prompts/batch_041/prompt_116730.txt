I've somehow ended up running the Thursday night 'big questions' group at our local library, and next month I've said I'd do Molyneux's problem — the one about the man born blind who's learnt to tell a ball from a cube by touch, then gets his sight and is asked which is which by looking, before he's allowed to feel them. The audience is about fifteen retired people, one ex-nurse, one retired engineer, all of them happy to argue the toss. I've got forty minutes and a flipchart.

I don't want you to explain it to me, I want a plan for how I explain it. So: the order the bits have to go in and why that order — what has to be understood before the question even counts as a question. I keep finding that if I start it wrong it comes out as 'would a man who's never seen be amazed by colours', which isn't the puzzle at all, so I need to know which idea has to be planted first to stop that happening.

Then the one example to carry the whole thing. Everyone's heard the ball and the cube, so if that's still the one, say why, and how to keep it from feeling like a party trick; if it should be something else, tell me what the replacement has to have and what it mustn't have. I did wonder about a swap: a woman blind from birth with no hands, who can tell the ball from the block by the sound each makes when it's rolled across a table, and then gets her sight. Does that keep the question or change it? If it's any use, where in the sequence does it go?

Then the misconceptions, in the order they'll actually come up, and the exact point in the talk where I should head each one off. My guesses: 'he's never seen, so obviously he couldn't know, end of story'; 'this is really about whether ideas are innate'; and someone who's heard about the cataract operations and thinks that settles it.

Two things also trip me up in my own telling: with only two shapes he's got a fifty-fifty chance of being right, so what am I actually asking whether he can do — and how would I tell 'he knew' from 'he guessed'? Where does that go in the order?

Last thing: one question to put to the room at the end that would show me whether they've got the puzzle rather than just enjoyed the story, and whatever I should openly refuse to go into at this level, and say so.
