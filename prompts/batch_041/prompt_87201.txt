Imagine that you are advising a foundation that can support a focused five-year program on the moral status of invertebrates, with a budget for a small interdisciplinary team, on the order of eight to twelve researchers. The foundation's aim is to reduce decision-relevant uncertainty about the scale argument: the claim that the sheer number of insects and other invertebrates could give them enormous expected moral importance even if the probability that any given individual is sentient is very low. It wants a defended research agenda, not a survey, a literature review, or a reading list. Rank the open questions and lines of work that most deserve support, and defend the ranking. Treat as genuinely open, and as central to your ranking, at least the following issues: whether sentience is a threshold property or a matter of degree, and how that interacts with the many-individuals-low-probability structure; whether moral weight attaches to individuals, species, neurons, or some measure of valence-processing or integrated information; whether the morally relevant harms include only pain and death or also deprivation, frustration, and thwarted preferences; and whether the scale argument should be modeled as expected-value maximization under deep uncertainty or instead through a decision rule such as maximin, precaution, or robustness across plausible moral theories. You may find it useful to treat the scale argument as a welfare function that multiplies the number of individuals by a probability of sentience and by some measure of intensity and duration, but you should also assess whether that functional form is itself one of the things at issue. For each item in your ranking, explain why it is upstream of other work, how tractable it is for a small interdisciplinary group, how neglected it is, and what practical decision about research funding, advocacy, or intervention would change if it were resolved. Identify which conclusions or interventions are robust to almost any answer and which are highly sensitive. Begin your response with a single sentence stating your ordered agenda, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of this question. Close by naming the two or three empirical or conceptual findings that would most move your ranking. Do not provide citations, bibliographies, or suggested readings; reason from first principles and from whatever background you already have.
