I got into a stupidly long argument with my flatmate last night and I'm still chewing on it. He used to be vegan, now he eats a lot of beef for what he insists are animal-welfare reasons, and the whole thing rests on one line he kept repeating at me: 'growing crops kills more animals than raising cattle'. Whenever I pushed on it he'd just say the mice and the rabbits and the birds never show up in my accounting, that a pasture-raised cow on land you couldn't grow soy on anyway is the least-harm option, and that we should care about wild animals' own lives too, which apparently makes the whole picture worse for me. I'm not even sure he's wrong, but I'm also not sure hes saying anything in particular -- it felt like three or four different claims wearing one coat, and at least one of them seemed to cut against the conclusion he wanted.

So I'd like you to act as someone who has spent years on the ethics of animal agriculture and on welfare comparisons under empirical uncertainty, and treat 'growing crops kills more animals than raising cattle' the way a philosopher treats a slogan. First, brainstorm quietly: what are all the genuinely distinct things a sentence like that could be claiming? Then lay them out for me as a numbered menu, and for each one tell me (a) whether it's a straight empirical claim, a conceptual one, or a covert moral-weighting choice, (b) whether it's trivial, contentious-but-defensible, or flat false, and (c) whether it would actually license his conclusion that he should keep eating beef -- or whether it collapses into supporting something else entirely. The obvious readings are the body-count-per-calorie one and the per-acre one, but I want the ones hiding underneath: death vs. suffering as the currency, trading a field mouse's short life against a steer's eighteen months of confinement, how much of the total hangs on whether wild animals' lives are net good or net bad, whether 'unintended byproduct of a harvester' and 'bred into existence for slaughter' come out different under the same arithmetic, and whether the deciding numbers are even the kind anyone could know. Show me where the verdict flips as you change the unit (per meal, per calorie, per acre, per dollar) and tell me which unit the slogan actually needs to be true. Please don't just hand me the EA-adjacent version of this, I've read the blog posts -- I want the boring philosophical scaffolding underneath, with verdicts, so I can go back to him with an actual menu instead of another shouting match. And at the end, tell me which reading you think he really meant, and whether that one survives.
