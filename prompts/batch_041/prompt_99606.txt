I'm trying to get straight on the 'low-impact AI' idea before I commit to writing about it, and I keep going round in circles. Everyone says an agent should avoid side effects, but nobody tells me what a *definition* of side effects would have to do to count as any good. So write me the desiderata: what any adequate formal account of 'avoiding side effects' -- or an impact measure, same thing for my purposes -- has to deliver. The cases it must get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the constraints it has to respect, and the consideration behind each requirement.

Some things I want in the list: it has to score a plan the agent is actually considering, so it must be estimable from the agent's own model rather than from the true values (the agent doesn't have those); it shouldn't be satisfiable by the agent just doing nothing, or by freezing the world; it has to cope with impact being a matter of degree, and with cases where each of two agents does something individually harmless but together they wreck the thing; and it should say something about whether the agent's own body and its intended goal count as 'impact' or not. I'd also like it to handle the vase-in-the-box sort of case, where the harm is real but no one intended it.

Then tell me which requirement is doing the most work, and whether you think any of them are jointly unsatisfiable -- because my suspicion is that you can't define impact without smuggling in a value specification, and if thats right I'd rather the list make that a result than quietly assume it away. British English please. Keep it tight, I don't need a history of the field.
