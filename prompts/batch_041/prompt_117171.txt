I am closing a seminar on whether mental content can be naturalized, and I want to end with a poem rather than with a summary. But I want the poem to be the case itself, not a restatement of it, so let me set the case out first.

A toad has a single internal state — call it the strike state — that goes off when a small dark moving speck crosses its visual field. Usually the speck is a fly, and the strike that follows usually pays. Sometimes the speck is a wind-blown leaf; sometimes the shadow of a hawk; sometimes the shadow of another toad. The state goes off identically in all four cases, and there is nothing anywhere inside the toad that marks the difference: no tag, no second state, no residue, no variable that takes a different value. There are then two things one might say the state is about. If it is about the fly, then when the speck is a leaf the toad is wrong — the state misrepresents. If it is about the speck, the toad is never wrong about anything, only sometimes unlucky; and a state that cannot be wrong about anything is a strange candidate for a representation at all. The state itself cannot adjudicate between these readings. It fires either way, and it has no view about which one is correct.

I would like a pantoum spoken by that state.

The form, as I want it. Five quatrains. Lines two and four of each quatrain come back as lines one and three of the next. In the final quatrain the second line is the poem's first line and the fourth line is the third line of the first quatrain, so the poem ends on words it has already used. Rhyme if it helps, never as filler.

The constraint that matters most: the line reporting what is crossing the visual field must not name any of the things seen — no fly, no leaf, no shadow, no hawk — so that what the line is about has to be carried by the lines around it. Let that line be the poem's opening line. It first means fly. It must come back in the final quatrain meaning leaf, with its words in the same order and only the punctuation free to shift, and the reader who knows this material must feel, at that return, the precise moment the state is wrong — with nothing in the poem having altered to mark it. The transformation should be the whole poem's work, not a single line's.

Some further things I care about:

The state cannot say what it is for. Any line in which it tries — I am for this, this is what I am for — should be quietly overturned by the return of the recurring line rather than by argument. It should not sound coy or withholding; it should sound like something that has no way of knowing and is not troubled by that.

It may register that the firing is old, that it was going on long before and will go on after, but it must not use the vocabulary of function, design, selection, purpose, information, or representation, and it must not mention any theorist or position by name.

No quatrain may explain the one before it. If the poem has a turn, I would like it to be the moment a reader sees that the state's indifference — the thing that looked like a defect in the speaker — is exactly what makes its error possible. I would rather you find that than announce it.

Leave the question open in the poem's words and closed in its form. Nothing in it should say that the state is about the fly. It should simply have been about the fly when the thing in the air was a leaf. Someone who has never heard of any of this should still find it a good poem; someone who has should find it a correct one.
