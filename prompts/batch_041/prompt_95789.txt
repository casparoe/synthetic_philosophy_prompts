I teach a 400-level epistemology seminar and I'm writing the final. I want one essay question on the bootstrapping problem and easy knowledge. The trouble is my students have all read the standard case -- you look at a red wall, believe it's red, and then feed that in as a data point for "my colour vision is reliable" -- and they can recite the "easy knowledge" label, but they can't actually say what's supposed to be wrong with the reasoning. So I've made up a variant to hang the question on:

Sam wants to know whether his memory for his dreams is reliable. Each morning on waking he writes down everything he can recall from the night's dreams. The next morning, before looking back at the entry, he tries to recall the same dream, then reads what he wrote and asks whether his current memory matches the entry. It almost always does. After a few months of this he concludes his dream memory is reliable.

I want the question to force them to (a) say whether Sam's procedure is a bootstrapping case at all, and if so in what sense; (b) work out what's actually doing the work in a bootstrapping objection -- is the problem that the reasoning is circular, or that the "evidence" is rigged to come out favourable no matter how the faculty is, and which of those Sam's case is; and (c) say what Sam would actually have to do to get non-circular evidence that his dream memory is reliable. A top answer should also be able to say whether Sam's case is better or worse than the standard colour-vision one.

Give me the essay question itself (aimed at about 60 minutes), a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme. Please format the whole thing as a JSON object with these fields: "question" (the essay question as it'd be printed on the exam), "model_answer" (the ~600-word answer), "marking_scheme" (an array of objects, each with "point" -- the specific move the answer has to make -- "marks" -- what it's worth -- "top_answer" -- a sentence on what earns full marks -- and "common_error" -- a one-line note on how students usually get this one wrong), and "total_marks" (the sum, which should come to 20).
