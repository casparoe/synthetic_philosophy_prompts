I'm a second-year politics student and I've somehow ended up running the sex-work session for our policy society's debate night, and it's doing my head in because everyone in the group just talks past each other. Half of them say 'the Nordic model' or 'like Germany' or 'like New Zealand' as if those are three flavours of the same thing, and I've realised none of us can actually say what the disagreement is — what you'd have to believe about sex work to end up endorsing one of them rather than another. So what I want from you is a proper decision tree or taxonomy I can hand round, built out of the logical space first and only then from the labels. Start with the branch questions — the ones that genuinely split the positions — and phrase each one so a reader can answer it without already knowing which camp it puts them in, so no node that just asks 'do you support the Nordic model?'. I'm thinking along the lines of whether the sale of sex is inherently harmful or only harmful under certain conditions; whether the person selling is best understood as a worker or as a victim of coercion; whether the criminal law should aim at the seller, the buyer, the third parties, or nobody at all; and whether the goal is abolition of the trade, harm reduction inside it, or full recognition and normalisation — but use your own judgement about which questions really do the separating and drop any that turn out to be doing no work. Aim for maybe five or six branch questions and no more than eight or nine leaves.

Then put the actual positions at the leaves — buyer-only criminalisation, full legalisation with regulation, decriminalisation, plain criminalisation on both sides, and anything else that falls out — each with a one-line statement of its most awkward commitment, i.e. the case it has the hardest time explaining. I'd like you to flag any combinations of answers that nobody seems to occupy, and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, just unattractive, or simply unexplored; and flag any two positions the tree can't pull apart rather than papering over it. It'd help alot if you made it bite on a few concrete cases instead of staying abstract — say a migrant worker with no papers, someone who says they'd stop tomorrow if the money weren't so good, a regular client with a disability, and street-based versus indoor work — and told me which branch each case actually tests, and where the tree forces you to a leaf you might not like. The audience is smart undergrads who don't know the literature, so keep the jargon light but don't dumb the distinctions down. British English please, and don't worry about length — I'd rather it be too detailed than too thin.
