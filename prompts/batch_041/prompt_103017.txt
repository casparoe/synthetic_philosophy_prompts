I'm building a case for a paper on the problem of old evidence, and I need a scenario engineered to do a job rather than merely illustrate the puzzle. Construct an ordinary-life situation in which a single agent holds a single body of evidence and a single hypothesis, and in which two accounts of confirmation return opposite verdicts on whether that evidence confirms that hypothesis: on a strict conditionalization account, evidence the agent has already known for a long time cannot raise her credence in a hypothesis she only frames afterward, since conditioning on what is already certain moves nothing; on a counterfactual or learning account, the same evidence does confirm the hypothesis, because if we strip it out of her background and imagine her encountering it for the first time, her credence in the hypothesis would rise. Constraints: no scientific exotica and no famous textbook illustrations; the episode should be something an ordinary person could actually live through; and everything except the timing of when the evidence became known relative to when the hypothesis was framed must be held fixed between the two accounts. The point of the case is to show that the disagreement is about what question confirmation is meant to answer rather than about the numbers, so a case that simply stipulates the two verdicts is worthless — the verdicts have to fall out of the accounts themselves.

Give the answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion: (1) the scenario; (2) the verdict of the strict conditionalization account and the reasoning behind it; (3) the verdict of the counterfactual account and the reasoning behind it; (4) the verdict of a third, order-indifferent account on which confirmation is just the likelihood ratio of the evidence given the hypothesis versus given its negation, together with a judgment on whether that account treats the split between the first two as substantive or as an artifact; (5) the single feature of the case that is doing the work, what becomes of the divergence when that feature is removed, and how a defender of the strict conditionalization account would try to disarm the case.
