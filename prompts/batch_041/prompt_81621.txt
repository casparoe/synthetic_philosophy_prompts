I've been drafted into a debate on commercial surrogacy and egg donation and would like to know whether my position is stable before I defend it. Here is the package I would present.

(1) Competent adults own their reproductive labor: contracts for gestational surrogacy and for egg donation should be legally available and binding, and the presumption against state interference with them is strong.
(2) A transaction is wrong when it exploits someone, and exploitation consists in taking advantage of the other party's constrained options, not in the exchange being commercial as such.
(3) Paternalistic bans that shield women from their own competent choices treat them as less than full agents; that is my objection to the criminal prohibitions across much of continental Europe, and my reason for preferring the open American market, warts and all.
(4) All the same, I cannot shake the sense that gestation is not just another service, and that the European refusal to let it be bought registers something my (1) and (3) cannot state.
(5) A contract cannot be specifically enforced against a woman's body, so the only remedy I can defend is money against the intended parents.
(6) Because bans in wealthy countries push demand toward poorer ones, the right cure for exploitation is regulation — capped fees, guaranteed health care, independent counsel, a real exit — rather than prohibition.

Which of these actually conflict, which only appear to, and how severe is each genuine conflict? Rank the genuine ones by severity, and for each give the cheapest revision and what it would cost me. I would like you to discriminate rather than flag everything as equally inconsistent; I genuinely do not know which of these is doing the work. End with a final line beginning "Verdict:" that names the single commitment most in need of revision, followed by a phrase describing the revision.
