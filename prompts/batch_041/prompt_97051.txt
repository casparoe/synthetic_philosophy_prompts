I run a small aesthetics reading group and I want to do a session on the paradox of fiction, so take the role of someone who's taught this at grad level and thinks carefully about the structure of a debate before writing anything. The paradox, roughly: (1) we genuinely feel emotions like fear and pity toward characters we know aren't real, (2) we only feel genuine emotions toward things we believe exist, (3) we don't believe fictional characters exist. Pick any two and the third looks false. Before you write, brainstorm the logical space first: which premise each familiar move denies, and which combinations of answers nobody seems to have bothered occupying.

What I want out of you is a decision tree or taxonomy that sorts a reader into a position by asking them concrete cases, not by asking them to pick a label. Every branch node should be a question an ordinary person can answer without knowing any of the literature, and every leaf should carry the name of a position, or be marked unnamed if the combination is unoccupied, with one line on why nobody's there (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored).

Cases I want built in, because I think they do work the stock examples don't:
- someone who sobs at a Netflix finale, then twenty minutes later feels nothing about it, but insists the sobbing was real when it happened
- rereading a novel where you already know the beloved character dies, and finding you still dread it and still cry
- someone who talks to an AI companion app every night, knows full well it's a language model, and says they'd be genuinely gutted if the company shut it down
- a viewer who knows a scene was engineered in a writers' room for no reason except to make people cry, and cries anyway
- being moved more by a fictional famine than by a real one you read about the same afternoon

Leaves I need to see: the quasi-emotion / make-believe line, the thought-theory line (emotions take thoughts or propositions as their objects, not beliefs), the half-belief or inconsistent-belief line, the illusion-theory line (we don't really feel anything and we misdescribe ourselves), and the no-paradox line that just denies premise (2) outright. If two of those can't actually be separated by your questions, flag that instead of papering over it, and tell me which pair it is.

One more thing on the epistemics. Please seperate what's pretty settled from what's genuinely contested, and don't hand me a contested view as if it were the consensus. Settled-ish: that each premise is independently plausible, that the trilemma is real, that the classic cases (fear of the green slime, pity for a doomed heroine) pull in different directions. Contested: whether quasi-emotions count as emotions at all, whether the thought theory is genuinely distinct from the make-believe account, whether half-belief is even a coherent mental state. Mark those as disputes. And don't guess at who held what or invent references, I don't want a bibliography, just the structure. Keep it to about 6 to 8 questions and 5 or 6 leaves.
