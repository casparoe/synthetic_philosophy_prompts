A friend from my old reading group has started writing a blog about the philosophy of mind, and his latest post is on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. The gist runs roughly as follows. Illusionism, he says, is the view that consciousness does not exist: we believe there is an inner glow, a felt quality to experience — the redness of red, the sting of a pain — and the illusionist holds that this is simply an error, that there are no such things as phenomenal states, only the brain processes that give rise to our talk about them. It is eliminativism applied to the mind's most private domain. What makes it clever, on his telling, is that it dissolves the hard problem rather than solving it: once you accept that 'phenomenal consciousness' names nothing, there is nothing left for a theory to explain, and the explanatory gap turns out to have been an artefact of language all along. As for the illusion itself, he says it is just the brain's higher-order machinery representing to the rest of the brain that it has a quality it does not have — so illusionism is really the higher-order thought theory under a new name, with the added twist that we are all, in effect, zombies who are mistaken about being conscious. He concedes that this sounds deranged but insists it is what the serious people now believe, and concludes that anyone still troubled by the hard problem is in the grip of a pre-scientific intuition.

I have read enough to suspect this is not quite right, but not enough to say where. Could you audit the summary for me? Go claim by claim: tell me which parts an illusionist would happily endorse, which would make them wince, and which are straightforwardly false. I am particularly interested in how illusionism actually differs from eliminativism about experience, from the view that the hard problem is merely verbal, and from higher-order theories of consciousness — the post runs these together, and I suspect that is where the damage is done. Then tell me whether his closing claims survive once the summary is corrected: does illusionism as its defenders understand it really leave nothing to explain, and is it fair to describe those who still feel the pull of the hard problem as simply pre-scientific? For what it is worth, I find the view distasteful, but I take it that distaste is not an argument. Please write your reply in British English, and be specific rather than reassuring — I would much rather have the awkward distinctions spelled out than a verdict that it is 'broadly right'.
