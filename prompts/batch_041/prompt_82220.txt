I moved to Amsterdam three months ago for my partner's job and I keep tripping over the word 'liberal'. Last week a colleague called the VVD 'the liberal party', I said something dumb about how strange it is that a right-wing party gets the L-word, and two other people at the table then went at it for twenty minutes while I mostly just watched. I found myself nodding at both of them at different points, so I'd definately like an actual ruling rather than a vibe.

(1) Anna, who's American like me, said: liberal in English means someone who wants the state to do more about inequality and social/cultural issues -- that's the sense in the country that produces most of the world's political journalism, so that's the sense; calling a free-market party liberal is at best a translation artifact, and in plain English you'd say conservative.

(2) Bram, who's Dutch, said the American usage is the odd one. Liberalism is a European nineteenth-century formation -- constitutions, individual rights, free markets, keeping the church out of the state -- and it sat on the left back when the alternative was kings and clergy. What changed is that the left later went social-democratic and kept the name in America. So the European usage is the original, and America is the one that drifted.

(3) Anna said that's history, not meaning, and Europe isn't consistent anyway: Venstre in Denmark literally means 'Left' and is a centre-right party, the British Lib Dems aren't the same animal as the Australian Liberal Party, and the languages themselves split the concept -- French libéral vs libertaire, Italian liberalismo for the political thing vs liberismo for the market thing. If Europe can't agree with itself, why is American usage the anomaly?

(4) Bram said all of those are still recognisably about liberty in a way 'American liberal' isn't, since an American liberal is often fine with a lot of state power over markets and gets called a leftist by other Americans. Anna said that just concedes her point that the word means different things in different places, and then it devolved into whether 'liberal' names anything at all or is just a team jersey.

Can you go move by move, rate each one, and say what both of them are missing? Also flag which of your calls you're least sure of and what would actually change your mind on it.
