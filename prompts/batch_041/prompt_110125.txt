I am a graduate student in philosophy of physics, and I have to present my own view in a seminar next month, so the topic has become personally urgent. I would be grateful for a rigorous audit of the package I have arrived at. First, I am a physicalist about quantum mechanics: the wavefunction and its unitary evolution exhaust reality, there is no collapse, and there is no additional probabilistic fact in the world over and above the wavefunction. Second, I accept the many-worlds interpretation: every branch is equally real, and talk of the actual outcome is indexical rather than a report on which branch alone exists. Third, I think the Born rule is not a fundamental postulate but a theorem about rational action, so an agent who knows the wavefunction should weight branches by the squared amplitude rather than by anything else. Fourth, I hold a principle I call successor indifference: I do not think personal identity across branches is what matters; what matters is that each successor carries forward my projects and values, and I should be indifferent between branches that are alike in what they let my projects become. Fifth, I think that before a measurement I am genuinely uncertain about which successor I will be, but this uncertainty is not about which branch exists, since all of them exist. Sixth, I think the fundamental dynamics is deterministic, yet the probabilities are objective in the sense that any rational agent must use them. Seventh, I think probability is epistemic: it is a feature of rational credence, not a physical field. Eighth, I think that if all branches are equally real, the natural default is to count branches equally, and I do not see why amplitude should matter unless something in the decision theory puts it there. Ninth, I think the empirical success of quantum mechanics supports many-worlds because it takes the formalism literally. Tenth, I think a good interpretation should not add extra normative facts to physics; the Born rule should fall out of physics plus rationality alone.

I suspect there is at least one genuine tension in this package, but I cannot tell whether it is the obvious one or something less obvious, and I do not know which commitment I would give up if pressed. Please audit the set. Which pairs are actually in tension, which only appear to be in tension, and how severe is each? For each genuine tension, what is the cheapest revision that would restore consistency, and what would that revision cost me elsewhere in the package? I would also like the tensions ordered by severity rather than treated as equally bad. Finally, if I wanted to make one change that cleans up the most, which single commitment should I give up or modify, and what would I lose by doing so? Please write in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way you would explain it in a letter.
