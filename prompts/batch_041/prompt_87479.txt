ok so I've been reading around the free energy principle / predictive processing stuff and I built an argument that I think shows the whole framework proves way too much, and I want you to tell me where it breaks. Here it is:

(1) Any system that keeps itself going, far from thermodynamic equilibrium, has to have some kind of boundary -- a Markov blanket -- separating its internal states from the external states.
(2) Once you've got a Markov blanket, you can always write the system down as minimizing variational free energy, because free energy is an upper bound on surprisal and a system that stays alive stays in low-surprisal states.
(3) Minimizing variational free energy is just maximizing the evidence for a generative model. That's Bayesian inference, by definition.
(4) So any persisting system is doing Bayesian inference -- it's updating a model of its environment.
(5) Bayesian inference is what it is to have beleifs, and to have beliefs that track the world is to be at least a bit rational.
(6) So every persisting system -- a bacterium, a plant, a flame, even a whirlpool that holds its shape for a while -- has beliefs about its environment and is rational.
(7) But that's absurd. A flame doesn't believe anything.

I'd like to keep 1-3 if I can, they seem like the technical core and I don't want to just wave my hands and say the maths is wrong. So which step do I give up, why did it look fine, and what does giving it up cost me? Like if I drop the move from 3 to 4, does the FEP still say anything about minds at all, or does it collapse into a fancy way of saying "things that last, last"?

And please don't just pick whichever step is easiest to reject -- I want to know if this is even the right way to press the worry, or if I'm attacking a version of the view nobody actually holds. If the whole framing is off, say so instead of humoring me.
