So there's this argument in my reading group that's been grinding for months and I want an actual diagnosis, not another summary of the positions.

Here's the setup as I understand it. Take the sorites for 'heap' or 'bald': everyone agrees one grain or one hair never makes the difference, and everyone also agrees that 'either it's a heap or it isn't' is true of every case, even the borderline ones. The epistemicist says: fine, so there really IS a sharp cut-off, some exact number of grains, it's just that we can't know where it falls, and 'vague' is just the name for our irremediable ignorance rather than any wobble in the world. The supervaluationist says: no, there's no fact of the matter about the cut-off; 'heap' is true if it's true on every admissible way of making the predicate precise, false if false on every such way, and neither otherwise, but 'either a heap or not' still comes out true because it's true on every precisification even though neither disjunct is. So both camps save classical logic and both refuse to accept the tolerance premise as straightforwardly true, and yet they hate each other's story about *why* they get to do that.

My hypothesis, and I want you to take it as a hypothesis and not as a settled thing: the fight isn't about the sorites at all (they handle it the same way) and isn't even really about whether a sharp boundary exists. I think it's about whether 'there's no fact of the matter' is a coherent thing to say about a borderline case, or whether it's just a fancy way of saying 'we don't know' -- in other words a disagreement about what truth and knowledge are doing in the explanation, not about heaps. But maybe I'm wrong. Maybe it's a genuine disagreement about whether vagueness lives in the world or in us, or maybe the two sides are definately answering different questions (one about the semantics of 'heap', the other about which logic you're allowed to use) under a single heading.

What I want from you: diagnose teh disagreement. Verbal, substantive, or two questions wearing one name? Is there a hidden empirical or formal question doing the work here? My suspicion is it's higher-order vagueness -- both sides seem to owe us an account of the vagueness of 'admissible precisification' on one side and of 'knowable' on the other, and that's where the real action is -- but tell me if that's a red herring. Back the diagnosis with what each side actually says, not just the labels. And then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress here: a distinction, a formal result, a change of question.
