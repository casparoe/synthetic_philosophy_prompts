I've been stuck in a months-long argument with a friend about Dennett's two-bitser and Searle's Chinese room, and neither of us is moving. The usual lines: Dennett says a two-bitser can be treated as an intentional system, so its aboutness is real enough for prediction; Searle says that aboutness is derived from us, and only brains have original intentionality. The twist that keeps us stuck is a case neither side talks about much: a thermostat that learns a small predictive model of the room and adjusts its setpoint, or a bacterium that follows a chemical gradient by tumbling. Dennett can call both intentional; Searle says any intentionality there is derived or missing. So why has this debate stalled? Is it a substantive disagreement about whether intentionality is intrinsic, a verbal fight over original vs derived, or two different questions -- one about the explanatory stance, one about the metaphysics of content -- sharing one label? My hunch is the last, but I want a diagnosis, not a survey. Use what each side actually claims about the two-bitser, the Chinese room, thermostats, and simple organisms. Then tell me what would count as progress: a distinction, a new case, a formal result, or nothing. And say what would change your mind and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. American English, please.
