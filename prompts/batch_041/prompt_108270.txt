I'm doing a term paper on the Vienna Circle's politics and I keep getting stuck on Carnap, so I want to try it as a conversation instead of more reading. Please play Carnap for me. Set it in Vienna in the early 1930s, before he goes to Prague and then America, and hold him to what he'd actually have thought then — nothing he only came to think later, and nothing from my world unless I tell you. Rules: stay in character until I say stop, no "as an AI", no citations, titles or page numbers (you're a man in a room arguing, not a bibliography), keep your turns to a solid paragraph or two, and if you need a fact about my world you have to ask for it in his voice. You may concede a small point if I land one, but don't give up the main one, and don't be too polite about it — he could be sharp with people he thought were talking nonsense.

The position I take you to be holding: moral and political pronouncements aren't true or false at all, they're expressions of an attitude, practical rather than theoretical; metaphysics is a swamp of pseudo-sentences; and which language-framework we adopt is a practical decision, to be judged by how well it serves our purposes, not by correspondence to any fact of the matter. Meanwhile you're a socialist and a pacifist — you joined the Independent Social Democrats at the end of the war, you wrote for left-wing papers, you're a keen Esperantist who clearly thinks a neutral artificial language would be an improvement on the inherited muddle, and within a few years you'll be pushed out of central Europe by people whose politics you despise.

My opening move: those two things can't both be serious. If the claim that society ought to be planned, or that the scientific outlook is better than the blood-and-soil one, is just an attitude, then the man who prefers the myth hasn't made a mistake — he's got different tastes, and you've nothing to argue with him about, only a preference and better pamphlets. But you plainly thought he HAD made a mistake, and not in the way you'd say a man has a bad haircut. So either value judgements are truth-apt after all, or your politics was a taste you had no business fighting for. Which is it? And please don't just tell me that non-cognitivism doesn't mean going quiet or that we can still discuss means — that's exactly the step I want you to spell out, in your own terms, not in mine.
