Write me an Onion-style news report, headline plus 600ish words, about a mid-sized city council that has just voted to replace its alcohol ordinance with Plato's actual drinking rules from the Laws. The jokes should come from getting the doctrine right, not from the fact that it's old. The stuff that needs to be in there, in the background: wine is treated as a drug that removes fear, so a supervised drinking party is a test of what someone's really made of and a gymnasium for self-control, and the party's purpose is education in the right pleasures and pains rather than pleasure as such; somebody sober and qualified has to run the thing and decide the mixing and the pace; there are age tiers, with nothing for the youngest and the "chorus of Dionysus" reserved for the old; the theory behind giving it to the elderly is that they've gone dry and rigid and the wine softens them the way fire softens iron, so they loosen up and stop being embarrassed to sing and dance; and shame or modesty is what keeps a drinker from tipping over into shamelessness. Also that it's a state institution with inspectors, not a hobby.

So the councilpeople and residents should be fighting over the real mechanics: who gets certified to run a party, whether the party inspector can shut down a dinner party, what "moderate" means for the middle bracket, whether the old folks' chorus is mandatory or opt-in, what a 39-year-old does on a Friday night when he's aged out of one tier and isn't old enough for the chorus, and the awkward fact that the whole scheme is aimed at making citizens better rather than letting them have fun. Include a line from an invented city official defending it as "basically what we already do, just with paperwork," and one from an invented local who's actually read the Laws and thinks the council missed the point — that the drinking party is where you find out what you're made of, not a licensing regime. Maybe a bartender who is now technically an unlicensed gymnasium.

Don't quote Plato. No quotation marks around anything that's supposed to be from the Laws, since you can't check it — paraphrase the views in your own words. If you bring up the age numbers, get them right or just say "the tiers" and move on. Keep it deadpan, no winking, and don't open with "in ancient Greece..." throat-clearing. Around 600 words. It's fine if the reporter sounds a little annoyed by the end.
