I hold a chair in philosophy of science, and for the past several years I have been collaborating with a computational-statistics group on problems of model selection, so I have become reasonably fluent in the vocabulary of priors and likelihoods, regularization and the bias–variance trade-off, minimum description length, and structural causal models with their interventionist semantics — even though I remain, by training and temperament, a metaphysician. I am assembling material for a mixed seminar whose students arrive mostly from statistics and machine learning, and I want to do something more demanding than the customary survey. Please restate the two dominant positions in the metaphysics of laws of nature as faithfully as you can in that statistical vocabulary. On the one side is the Humean best-system tradition, on which laws are whatever general axioms achieve the optimal trade-off between simplicity and strength in systematizing the entire history of the world, and on which the laws therefore supervene on the total distribution of local matters of fact; on the other is the anti-Humean or governing conception, on which laws are features of the world that necessitate or produce the regularities rather than merely summarizing them. Use each account's own internal structure rather than a loose analogy: map the simplicity–strength trade-off onto what it genuinely resembles — a compression problem, a description-length constraint, a model-selection criterion — and map the anti-Humean's governing role onto whatever truly corresponds to it, whether that is an invariant structural equation, an intervention-stable mechanism, or something else. I want the translation to be honest, and in particular I do not want the target vocabulary's assumptions smuggled in and then presented as if they were neutral renderings.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Which commitments of each side survive the translation without distortion, which survive only if I allow the statistical vocabulary to do work it was never designed to do, and which have no counterpart in it at all? I am especially interested in whether a law's necessity — as opposed to its mere invariance or its predictive success — can be expressed in these terms at all, or whether the translation quietly converts the anti-Humean into a Humean by substituting robustness under intervention for governance; and, symmetrically, whether the Humean's insistence that the laws supervene on the total mosaic can be distinguished from the ordinary point that a model is fitted to, and answerable to, a fixed dataset. Where the residue is real, tell me whether it indicates a defect in the statistical framework — something it cannot say that a theory of laws ought to say — or a sign that the metaphysical dispute was doing less work than its participants supposed.
