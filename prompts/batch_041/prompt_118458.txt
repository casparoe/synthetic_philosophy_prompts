I convene a graduate seminar on the constitutional politics of European integration, and I am looking for a written exercise that will separate those who have actually read the treaties from those who have merely read about them. I should therefore like you to compose a satirical piece in the form of a European Commission press release, together with the annexed proposal for a regulation, announcing a new instrument for the reduction of the Union's democratic deficit. The instrument is to be called the Democratic Deficit Amortisation Facility. The piece should be written in the register of the real thing, and the comedy should come entirely from the institutional detail.

A few specifications.

- Form and length: the press release (headline, dateline, body, a quotation from an invented spokesperson, and a Background note) followed by the recitals and three to five operative articles of the annexed proposal. Roughly seven hundred to eight hundred words in total.
- Register: deadpan institutional English, the idiom of the Official Journal. No winking, no editorial asides, and no closing paragraph that tells the reader what to conclude. The irony must be legible without the author's assistance.
- Target: a practice rather than a person — the reflex by which any shortfall in democratic authorisation is answered with a further instrument, a further committee, and a further consultation that cannot bind; and, at the same time, the complacency of the output-legitimacy defence, which treats the deficit as a problem of delivery rather than of authorisation. Satirise the machinery fairly. A committed federalist should laugh and wince in roughly equal measure; a committed critic should be made uneasy at how readily the complaint has been converted into an agency with a budget line.
- Things to get right: the Commission's near-monopoly of legislative initiative, and the fact that its own proposal would be adopted by a procedure in which it does not vote; trilogues; comitology; the reasoned opinion by which a national parliament may raise a subsidiarity objection, and the ease with which it may be noted and set aside; the dual legitimacy of Council and Parliament, and what is genuinely added by a third, participatory channel; conferred competences and the vocabulary of pooled sovereignty; and the teleological pull of the treaties' aspiration to ever closer union among the peoples of Europe.
- The invented mechanism should have an internal logic of its own, so that each absurdity follows from a real feature of the Union's design. You might, for instance, give it deficit units that member states may accumulate, trade or offset; a democratic reserve that is faithfully recorded and expressly non-binding; an amortisation schedule running comfortably beyond any electoral horizon; or a facility whose enabling regulation is adopted without a vote by the institution that drafted it. These are suggestions, not a template.
- Do not name living officials, and do not attribute positions to real courts, governments or institutions. Invent your dramatis personae, or refer to them generically, as in a large member state or the constitutional court of a founding member.
- No citations, bibliographies or reading lists are required, and none should be implied.

British English throughout.
