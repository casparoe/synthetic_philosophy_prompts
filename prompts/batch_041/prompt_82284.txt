I cover science and ideas for a magazine, which means I spend a lot of time at the kind of dinner where someone brings up Dennett's ladder of minds — Darwinian creatures that are just wired the way selection wired them, Skinnerian ones that learn by reward and punishment, Popperian ones that can run the experiment in their heads and let the hypothesis die instead of themselves, and then Gregorian creatures, whose minds are stuffed with tools they get from culture: language, notation, maps, math, other people's stored-up thinking. I've used that ladder in a piece or two and I mostly buy it, but a philosopher friend and I got into it over drinks and now I want an outside verdict.

Here's the thing I want priced. Suppose I propose a fifth grade, provisionally, and I want to know whether it's a real addition or just Gregorian creatures with extra syllables. The grade would be: creatures whose cognitive tools arrive from outside already finished, and whose competence with those tools is borrowed rather than earned — nothing in the creature's own history, and nothing in the history of the tradition it inherited, is under any selection pressure from the way the creature uses the tools. The picture case is a kid raised inside a mature scientific or legal or computational tradition who can run its apparatus flawlessly without ever having discovered any of it, and whose use of it doesn't feed back to reshape it. My friend says that's just what Gregorian creatures are. I say the interesting difference is the direction of dependence — in Dennett's story the tool and the mind co-evolve, and in my grade the mind is downstream of the tool and stays there, which should show up as a difference in what the creatures can do when the tradition starts to rot.

So: give me your actual credence that this is a genuine further grade rather than a sub-species of Gregorian, and please don't hand me "it depends how you draw the categories." Decompose it. How much of your number rides on whether Dennett's four are meant as a real taxonomy of kinds at all or just a loose ladder of increasing behavioral plasticity? How much on whether my proposed distinguishing feature — inherited, unselected, one-way tool dependence — is actually absent from Gregorian creatures as Dennett describes them, rather than just underspecified? How much on whether the new grade does any explanatory or predictive work that "Gregorian plus a modifier" doesn't? And how much on whether kind-hood here is even a matter of sharp categories versus a continuum, which if it's a continuum might make my whole question malformed? Give me a rough probability for each term, one line of justification for each, show me how they combine, then tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to and what would actually move it — a finding, a thought experiment, an argument. And commit. If you think it's a genuine fifth grade, say so and defend it; don't lay out both sides and leave me holding the tab.
