I'm the parent rep on my school district's technology committee — no philosophy background, I got appointed because I complained about the last software rollout. We meet Thursday and I have to circulate a written recommendation before then, and I'm in over my head.

Here's the thing. The district is buying an early-warning system that flags kids for the limited spots we have in counseling and after-school tutoring. It isn't the flagging I care about, it's that the vendor lets you install exactly one of two decision engines and then it's locked in for the life of the contract.

Engine A scores every kid on how much they'd gain from the spot, and takes the top ones until the spots run out. Engine B doesn't score anything. You write it a list of rules and it checks each kid against them; if two of your rules point different ways it just stops and kicks the case to a human. And it literally cannot make an exception — no matter what the numbers say, if a rule says no, it's no.

The vendor ran both engines over all forty of our test scenarios and they made the identical call on every single one. My chair says fine, flip a coin. I don't think that's right but I can't say why.

The other thing is, two years ago the board passed a rule that any automated decision about a student has to be explainable in one sentance that the kid's parent could read out loud at a meeting. I dont think anyone has thought about what that does to this choice.

So: which engine do we install, and why. Please actually pick one — don't give me a both-sides thing and leave it to me. Tell me honestly what the best case for the other engine is and why it doesn't win. And tell me what our one-sentence rule really rules out, because I have a feeling it kills more options than people realize. If you think there's a third option — some hybrid, or just refusing to buy either — say so and say whether you'd take it over your pick.
