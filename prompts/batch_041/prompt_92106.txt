I've been chewing on Hofstadter's careenium setup — the billiard table full of little magnetic balls (simms) that clump into higher-level patterns he calls simmballs, standing in for symbols. His question is which level is doing the pushing, and I've landed on the view that the simmballs really do push the simms around: the symbol level is genuinely causally efficacious, not just a convenient redescription of what the simms are up to. I don't think it's merely a level we find useful for prediction.

What would count against that? I want the evidence and arguments that should actually move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want you to be honest about which ones a committed defender of simmball-level causation would themselves concede versus wave away. A few things I'm especially unsure about, so please address them if they're relevant: (1) whether the view collapses into the claim that the simm-level description was just incomplete, rather than that there are two real levels; (2) whether it can survive the standard exclusion-style worry that if the simm-level facts already fix the outcome, the simmball-level facts are doing no extra work; and (3) what would have to be true of the simm-to-simmball mapping for the simmball level to earn its keep — for instance if the mapping is unstable or many-to-one, or if you can nudge the simms in ways that leave the simmball description untouched but change what happens downstream.

I'd also like a variant to think through: suppose you could build a second careenium out of completely different simms that nonetheless settles into the same simmball dynamics. Does that help the simmball-cause view or hurt it? And tell me whether the position I've landed on risks being unfalsifiable, and if so whether that's actually a problem here or just the price of a level-of-description claim.

Please write in American English.
