ok so im teaching a unit on quantum immortality next term and ive realized i cant make the students do anything useful with it until we've settled what the thing is even supposed to be. everyone in this literature argues from the conclusion -- either obviously you always survive, or obviously the whole idea is a reductio of many worlds and thats that. so before we read anything, give me the desiderata: what would any adequate theory of personal survival across branches -- the thing the suicide argument actually leans on -- have to deliver? i want the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it cant collapse, the physics and decision theory it cant contradict, with the specific case or consideration behind each requirement, and a note on which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just house rules of one camp.

the cases i care about, in no order: the spin-triggered gun and the poison pill; whether the argument applies equally to a classical coin flip in a deterministic world, which i think is where most accounts quietly cheat; the fact that no branch ever recombines and no consciousness collapses anything; the measure or born weights and what exactly they are weights of; the third-person fact that most of you dies versus the first-person fact that you dont; the fission and duplication cases, since branching is basically Parfit's problem with a physics budget; and the survivorship-bias objection, which i suspect is either fatal or trivially answerable depending on how self-locating credence gets set up.

then flag any two requirements you think cant be jointly satisfied. my hunch is that using the born rule as the rational credence for your own future and being certain of survival in the suicide case are the pair that cant both hold, and i want you to tell me whether theres any way out that isnt just positing two kinds of probability and hoping nobody asks how they relate.

finally, which single requirement does the most work excluding candidates, and briefly, how do the main lines -- the many-worlds-plus-self-locating-uncertainty view, the survival-is-certain reading, and the collapse/no-branching reading -- do against the list? keep the scoring short, the list is the deliverable.
