Right, so I've definately talked myself into risk-weighted expected utility and I want you to try to talk me out of it, or at least tell me what should.

Quick version of the view in case it's not fresh: you rank a gamble's outcomes worst to best, and instead of just adding up probability times utility, you apply a risk function r to the chance of doing at least as well as each step, so the value is u(worst) + r(P(at least 2nd worst)) x [u(2nd worst) minus u(worst)] + r(P(at least 3rd worst)) x [u(3rd worst) minus u(2nd worst)] + ... The whole point of r is to make risk attitude a separate, explicit ingredient rather than something smuggled into the shape of the utility curve. I think that's right: being risk-averse is a rational attitude, not a computational error, and plain expected utility theory gets this wrong by baking risk-neutrality in at the ground floor.

So: what would count against this? Some things I already half-suspect are problems, tell me which are fatal and which are just awkward:

(1) A friend says it isn't even a new theory. She says any rule shaped like that is just a theory that transforms cumulative probabilities instead of individual ones, with r renamed, so it collapses into that older family and has whatever problems that family has. Is she right, and if so, does it matter?

(2) Someone else says a risk function like this makes you exploitable across time, that you'll take a gamble and then pay to undo it, or something money-pump-ish, and that expected utility maximisation is the only rule that survives sequential choice. I genuinely can't tell if that's a real result or just folklore people repeat.

(3) It's a theory about known probabilities only. Does that mean it can't say anything about decisions under ambiguity, and is that a defeater for it or just a different question it wasn't trying to answer?

(4) There's one global r for all gambles in all domains. Doesn't that commit it to the prediction that my risk attitude, in the probability-transform sense, is the same about money and about my health and about everything else? That seems obviously false of me.

Then the general ask: rank these from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me what a committed defender of the view would themselves concede rather than what critics say about it. Also, honestly, does the view risk anything at all, or has it been quietly insulated, e.g. by the move that says risk attitude is just a primitive preference so nothing can count against it? If it's insulated like that, is that a problem for a view of this kind? And what does it predict that a plain expected-utility person wouldn't, that I could actually go check?
