I'm puting together a handout for a workshop on Kant's Perpetual Peace and I want your help ranking some stuff. Take the role of someone who's taught the text for years and also works on contemporary just war theory, and think carefully before you commit to anything. Below are eight proposed additions to Kant's articles. I drafted them — some are meant to be genuinely Kantian, some I suspect are just modern policy preferences dressed up in Kant-speak. Sort them into tiers by how Kantian they really are: could you derive the thing from the same principles Kant is actually working with (the form of law, publicity, the autonomy of states, cosmopolitan hospitality, the republican constitution), or not? Start by writing out the principles you'll test each item against, then give a one-line verdict per item, then flag (a) anything morally attractive that Kant's own framework would rule out, and (b) the two hardest to place, and why. I dont need citations, I only care about the reasoning.

1. A ban on weapons that select and kill targets without a human deciding to fire.
2. Treaty negotiations must be public — draft texts published before ratification.
3. A world state with a monopoly on legitimate force is the only real guarantee of peace; the federation of free states is just a way station.
4. Wealthy states must admit people displaced by climate change as permanent residents, not only as temporary guests.
5. No state may sell arms to a non-republic.
6. A global tax on financial transactions, funding an international court with compulsory jurisdiction.
7. Military intervention to restore a republican constitution after a coup.
8. Citizens must vote in a referendum before their state declares war.
