Suppose we could put a question to Descartes that only became askable in the twentieth century, and that he had to answer as a philosopher rather than as the custodian of a system.

The question concerns pain asymbolia. Damage to the insula, and in some cases surgery that severs the cingulate or the frontal pathways, can abolish the aversive quality of pain while leaving its location and intensity intact: patients still detect and localize the noxious stimulus, still call what they feel pain, still rate it as intense, and yet are not bothered by it, do not withdraw, and sometimes smile while it is being inflicted. The sensation survives; the awfulness is subtracted.

What would Descartes have made of such a patient? Two elements of his own view look to me to be in tension here, and I want to know which one he would sacrifice. He treats the sensation of pain as a confused mode of thought, instituted by the union of mind and body for the preservation of the composite: the hurt is precisely how the body's interests intrude upon the mind. But he also concedes that this institution can misfire — the dropsy patient's thirst is attached by nature to a bodily state that drinking will worsen — and he handles that case by saying the senses are given for the preservation of life rather than for the truth. If the hurt simply is the institution at work, then a pain that no longer moves the patient looks like a sign detached from what it signifies, and the asymbolic patient is arguably not in pain at all, merely reporting a local quality that has lost its point.

Three things I want from the answer. First, is asymbolia evidence that the badness of pain is not intrinsic to the sensation but belongs instead to a further state — aversion, appetite, an evaluative response — that the lesion removes; and is Descartes committed to denying that, or is he free to accept it? Second, at what joint would his theory have to bend, and what would he give up in order to keep the rest? Third, what would he make of the therapeutic prospect: if the sting can be stripped out while the sensation remains, is the patient cured or diminished, and does that reveal what he took the badness of pain to consist in? I care more about locating the joint than about a verdict.
