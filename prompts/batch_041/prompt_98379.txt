Okay so I have zero formal background in this, I just read a lot and I've gotten kind of obsessed with the sortition thing lately — picking lawmakers by lottery, like in Athens. My whole reason for caring is the career-politician problem. I don't think politicians are bad people, I think the job is a job: you have to win the next election, keep your donors happy, and your already thinking about your next office or your book deal or your lobbying gig. Somebody who serves one term by lottery and goes back to their normal life doesn't have any of that. Term limits seem like the weak version of the same fix to me, but I could be wrong about that too.

Here's the thing I keep going back and forth on, and it's a bit of a weird idea so bear with me. What if you don't sortition the people who *vote* on laws, but only the people who decide what gets voted on? Like a randomly chosen "agenda council" that picks, say, the ten or fifteen bills the elected legislature has to take up that session. Keep the elections, keep the professional politicians, but they can only act on stuff a random sample of citizens put in front of them. My gut says this gets you most of what I actually care about — the agenda stops being for sale — while dodging all the obvious objections to amateur lawmakers who don't know anything. But I have no idea if this is a real idea or if I'm just fooling myself.
