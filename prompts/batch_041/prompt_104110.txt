I want a credence, decomposed, not a survey of the literature. Here is the setup. Consider three ideal types of moral culture, distinguished by how each grounds personal worth and how each responds to insult. In an honor culture, worth is a matter of publicly recognized standing, and a slight must be answered — typically by the person slighted, and often in kind — because an unanswered slight diminishes one's standing. In a dignity culture, worth is intrinsic and inviolable, so a slight is best met with forbearance, indifference, or ordinary legal process rather than private retaliation. In a victimhood culture, worth is bound up with the recognition of one's suffering, so a slight is a moral violation that calls for institutional remedy and public acknowledgment rather than either a duel or a shrug.

Take the proposition: dignity culture is morally superior to both honor culture and victimhood culture, where "morally superior" means that it performs better on welfare, autonomy, and justice considered together.
