Forty years in a wet-lab and I retire straight into philosophy, which my wife says serves me right. Anyway, I've got an argument I can't break and its making me a little crazy. Setup: a guy in a parking garage offered me something absurd -- hand over my wallet and he'd fix my life in some enormous way -- and obviously I laughed and walked off. But by the time I got home I'd built the case for paying him, and I can't find the leak:

(1) Do whatever maximizes expected value; that's just decision-making.
(2) There's a nonzero chance the offer was real -- you can't rule it out with certainty.
(3) For any payoff I care to name, there's some tiny chance, small enough to be believable, that multiplied by that payoff still beats keeping the wallet (and beats anything else I'd do with the money).
(4) So paying him was the correct move.
(5) The same reasoning works for the next guy with an even grander story, and the next, so I should empty my accounts into any stranger with a big enough promise.
(6) That's nuts. So something in (1)-(4) is off.

Where? My buddy says "he was lying, duh," but that can't be it -- (2) already grants he's probably lying, and (3) is supposed to carry the day regardless. I'd like to keep (1) if I can; I've done expected values my whole life. So which step do I drop, why did it look so innocent, and whats the price?
