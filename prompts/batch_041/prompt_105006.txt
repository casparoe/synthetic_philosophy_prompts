I'm a retired analytical chemist — 34 years in a lab, mostly spectroscopy — and since I hung up the safety glasses I've been trying to teach myself philosophy, working through a couple of books and an online lecture course on mind and language. I just hit the section on Putnam's Twin Earth argument and there's a summary of it that's been bugging me, and I cant tell whether my unease is just me being new at this or whether the guy really did oversell it.

The way he told it: Putnam's Twin Earth thought experiment refutes the idea that meanings are in the head. Oscar on Earth has a molecule-for-molecule duplicate on Twin Earth. Earth-Oscar's word 'water' picks out H2O; Twin-Oscar's word 'water' picks out XYZ, some other compound that looks and tastes and behaves just like water. Since the two men are internally identical but their words mean different things, meaning has to be fixed from outside the skull, by the environment. And the upshot is that mental content is broad — your beliefs and thoughts are partly constituted by the stuff around you, so two people with identical brains can literally believe different things when each one says 'water is wet.' He said it also shows that meaning is a social thing, settled by your community rather than by you, and the big moral is that meaning, all the way down, is a public, external matter, so there's no such thing as a private language.

I can swallow part of that — I spent my whole career believing 'gold' means the element with atomic number 79 whether or not anybody in the room knows it, so I'm not exactly allergic to the environment doing some of the work. But something about the way he slid from the water case to the claim that my beliefs are made of the stuff around me, and then to the community bit, feels like two or three different claims wearing one coat. Could you audit the summary for me — go claim by claim and tell me what a philosopher of language would nod along with, what they'd wince at, and what's just wrong — and then tell me whether the community part and the no-private-language part really follow from the water-and-XYZ part, or whether he smuggled them in? I'd rather not walk away with a tidy slogan I can't defend.
