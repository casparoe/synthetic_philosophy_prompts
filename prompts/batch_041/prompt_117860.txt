I built this argument for a seminar thing on killing and letting die, and now I can't get out of it. Every step looks fine to me, the conclusion is obviously false, and I've been going in circles for about a week. Here's the chain:

(1) When I let something happen, nothing happens that wasn't already happening -- the world just carries on the way it was going. (2) Everything that does happen, happens in virtue of positive facts: events, states, bits of stuff doing things. There are no negative facts out there leaning on the world. (3) So if it's bad of me to keep walking while the guy on the pavement dies, the badness has to land on something positive about me: that I kept walking, that I didn't cross the road, that I didn't take out my phone. (4) But keeping walking and not crossing the road are things I do. Those are actions. (5) So letting die is a species of doing -- just one more description of the same bit of behaviour. (6) And if one and the same bit of behaviour can be described either as a doing or as an allowing, then the doing/allowing line can't be carrying any moral weight by itself; it lives in our descriptions, not in the world. (7) So killing and letting die come out morally on a par unless something else in the case is doing the work, and (8) a doctor who could have saved a patient with a phone call and didn't is as much a murderer as one who injects air into the drip, and I commit battery every day I don't hand a stranger one of my kidneys.

(8) is nonsense, so something up there is broken. Where, and what does fixing it cost me?

A warning about the cheap exits. The premise I really don't want to give up is (2) -- I've argued seperately, in a paper I still stand behind, that absences aren't causes and that causal claims are made true by chains of positive events, and that's exactly what (2) is doing here, so please don't just wave it away. Beyond that I'd take (1) through (6) over (8) any day, though I know that's not a reason. And don't tell me the distinction has to matter because of how bad things get if we drop it; that's a reason to want an answer, not an answer. If it turns out the chain is actually valid and the only way out is to give up something I like, say which thing, what I lose by giving it up, and whether the doing/allowing distinction survives in any form on the other side. Also tell me why the bad step looked fine to me -- I want to know what it was doing to my eye.
