I run compliance for a mid-sized international NGO — about 400 staff across a dozen countries, mostly in places where the lines around gifts, favors, and family obligations don't look like they do back at our HQ. Every week something lands on my desk: a country office wants to approve a gift to a local official, or a project lead hired somebody's relative to keep the village council on side, or a finance officer flags a payment to a customs clerk that "everyone does." Right now we're deciding these case by case and the answers are all over the place, which is a problem because people get fired over them and programs get shut down.

I need a decision procedure my field staff can actually run — people with no philosophy background, working in a hurry, sometimes under pressure from a government office. Something like: the questions to ask, in order; what a sufficient answer to each one looks like; where the procedure just stops and says no; and where it has to hand the decision to a person because it's turned into a question of values rather than facts. And I want it grounded in the actual thinking about this stuff — what clientelism really is versus a gift economy, whether a gift and a bribe are different moral categories or the same one with better manners, the argument that anti-corruption standards are just Western norms in a suit, the counterargument to that. Don't dumb it down to the point where it stops tracking the real distinctions, but don't make me hand my field staff a seminar either.

Then run it on three cases: a genuinely customary gift to a local official's family at a wedding, a cash payment to a customs clerk to get medical supplies released, and hiring a village elder's nephew because the elder controls access to our project site. At least one of those should come out with an answer my country directors won't be happy about — say which and why. Also be honest about where the procedure can't decide for us, especially around the universality question, and tell me who should be signing off when it hands the call to a human.
