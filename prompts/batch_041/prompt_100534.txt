I share a corridor with a colleague, Dana, who's spent about a decade writing on rationality and time, and she's become the person I bring my decision-theory puzzles to, mostly because she gives verdicts fast and doesnt hedge. Her record so far, as best I can reconstruct it: Ted, who'll trade his tea for a coffee plus a nickel, then the coffee for a water plus a nickel, then the water back for tea plus a nickel, and says he'd keep going as long as I keep offering — Dana says irrational, no qualification. Maria, who has a strict ranking of everything she cares about and simply says no to any swap that isn't an improvement — rational, obviously. Priya, who is forever promising to save and then spending, and who fixed it by setting up an automatic transfer she can't reverse without a week's notice — Dana thinks that's the most rational thing on the whole list; she says tying your own hands is fine and often required. And Sam, who has the same shape of cycle as Ted, except over three cities he might move to, and who told me cheerfully that he'd pay a small amount to make any one of the three swaps, forever — irrational again. I've never heard her give a reason that didn't sound like it could really be about two or three different things, which is why I'm writing to you instead of just asking her.

Next week I'm bringing her something I honestly can't call. A former student of ours, Rina, has a clean cycle — she'll tell you, and she's thought about it a lot, that she prefers the mountains to the sea, the sea to the desert, and the desert to the mountains. But she also knows this about herself, and about two years ago she decided she'd never make any swap among those three; she keeps a card in her wallet saying as much, and she's turned down a real offer — someone offered her fifty dollars to trade her desert trip for a sea trip, which by her own ranking is an improvement, and she said no. Nobody has ever put a full cycle of trades to her and nobody easily could, since the three are on different continents and you can basically never hold all three at once anyway. She says she refuses in advance so that no one can ever run her around in a circle.

So what do you think Dana will say about Rina — irrational, rational, or something in between — and what's the rule she's actually been running all along? You're the philosopher of action here, so think it through carefully rather than going with your first instinct.
