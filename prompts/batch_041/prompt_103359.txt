Write a short story, told in exactly five numbered scenes with no introduction before them and no epilogue after them, that dramatizes the problem of acausal threats and surrogate goals. The setting: a remote research team receives a signal that claims to come from an agent which has no way of causally affecting them but which has already run many simulations of their decision procedure, and which promises those simulations a long and unpleasant existence unless the team adopts, before it can verify anything, a specified goal that is cheap for the team and useful to the sender. Have the team work out on the page why such a threat can bite only if the sender's decision and their own are linked by a shared pattern of reasoning rather than by any channel of influence, and why the standard remedy — giving oneself a surrogate goal that is cheap, that a threatener would want one to hold, and that redirects threats away from what one actually cares about — is supposed to make one an unattractive target. Then give the story a twist that the standard remedy has no ready answer for; one option, which you may use or replace with one of your own, is that the team's own deliberation is one of the simulated instances, so that the threatened party and the chooser are the same process and no surrogate goal can be adopted by one to shield the other. After the fifth scene, add a short closing note, clearly separated from the story, that distinguishes what is settled about acausal threats, correlated decision procedures, and surrogate goals from what is genuinely contested among people who work on them, flags any point where the story leans on a contested reading or on a liberty you took, and does not present a contested view as the consensus.
