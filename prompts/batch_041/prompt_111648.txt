Okay so I'm trying to get the central argument of a chapter straight and I want someone to tell me whether it actually works. Here's the sketch — be as brutal as you like.

Mary's room, standard setup: Mary knows every physical fact about colour vision, then leaves the room, sees red for the first time, and (supposedly) learns something she didn't know before. Conclusion: physicalism is false, because there are facts that outrun the physical facts. My move: I don't think the intuition, if it's worth anything, cuts against physicalism specifically. Take *any* complete description of the world, physical or otherwise. Imagine we had a complete dualist description — every fact about the non-physical properties, written out in some canonical notation. Mary's still in the room, still has never seen red. Does the dualist description close the gap? I say no. Descriptions don't seem to be the sort of thing that can hand you what-it's-like; you can be in full possession of the description and still be owed the experience. So the intuition is pumping on something quite general about the knowledge-that / descriptive mode, not on physicalism's failure in particular. If that's right, the knowledge argument can't be run as an argument *for* dualism or *against* physicalism — at most it shows that phenomenal knowledge isn't derivable from any complete description, which is a claim about description, not about the physical. I'm calling this the symmetry objection.

What I want: comments on whether it goes through. Specifically — (a) am I begging the question by assuming a dualist description would be 'complete' in the relevant sense, or wether a description of the mental is exactly the kind that closes the gap by construction, in which case my symmetry is fake and there's a real asymmetry after all? (b) Does the objection prove too much, or does it just collapse into the phenomenal-concept reply that physicalists already give, in which case it's doing no work against them? (c) Is there a version of the knowledge argument where the asymmetry between physical and mental descriptions survives, so it still has dualist teeth? I think it's definately worth trying to save the argument this way, but tell me if I'm wasting my time.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — does the symmetry objection work, yes or no — then the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate my question back at me. British English please.
