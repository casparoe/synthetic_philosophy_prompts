My friend Dara is an expressivist — she thinks a sentence like "lying is wrong" is more like booing than like describing — and she's been trying to get out from under the Frege-Geach problem for a class of words she's writing her dissertation on. Her verdicts on the cases she's done so far:

- "Lying is wrong, and if lying is wrong then getting your little brother to lie is wrong, so getting your little brother to lie is wrong." She says this is valid, and the antecedent isn't just being asserted over again. Embeds fine.
- "Boo for lying!" and "Hooray for honesty!" — she says these can't go inside a conditional or under a "not" at all; "If boo for lying, then..." is just noise. Not a candidate for logic.
- "That damn dog is loose." She says this embeds fine ("If that damn dog is loose we're in trouble," "That damn dog isn't loose"), but the damn bit isn't part of what's true or false, it just rides along outside. So embedding by itself doesn't hand you truth-conditional content.
- "She believes that lying is wrong" and "It's true that lying is wrong." She'll say both without blinking, but she insists "believes" and "true" are doing very thin work there — no property of wrongness, no truthmaker, none of that.
- "Murder is wrong but I don't disapprove of murder." Not a contradiction, she says, just a weird thing to say.

Now she's stuck on a new case. She's been transcribing a dialect that has a word, "smunt," which is a pure venting word that got grammaticalized into a predicate. "That's smunt" just vents disgust at the thing; there's no descriptive content, no property of smuntness, nothing you could be right or wrong about. The speakers are perfectly happy with "If that's smunt, don't touch it" and "That's not smunt, you're just being squeamish." But they flat out refuse "She believes that's smunt" and "It's true that that's smunt" — they say you can't believe or know that something's smunt any more than you can believe a sneeze.

What's Dara going to say — is "smunt" a candidate for logic, i.e. is Frege-Geach solved for it, or not? I care a lot less about the yes/no than about which rule she's actually running, which other rules fit her earlier verdicts just as well, what each of them predicts here, and which further case would best make her pick between them.
