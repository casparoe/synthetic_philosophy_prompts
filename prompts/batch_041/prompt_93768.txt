The trick is that the "uncountable" set sitting inside the countable model just has no bijection to the naturals that the model itself can see; the bijection exists in the bigger world, not in the model.

Two characters, evenly matched, no strawmen, and please make them actually take up each other's points instead of delivering back-to-back lectures. Character two is an absolutist: they think that's a massive overreaction, that the paradox is fully explained by the model being an incomplete picture, and that uncountability is as objective as anything — you just have to say it properly ("no bijection exists") instead of pretending the model contains every function.
