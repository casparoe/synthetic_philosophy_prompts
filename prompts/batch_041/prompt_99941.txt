Bit of an odd one, but this has been eating at me and I don't really have anyone to ask who won't just tell me what they think I want to hear. When I was 20 my dad was dying — six weeks, we knew — and one night in the hospital he made me promise to "keep the family together," which in his mind meant look after my little brother, don't let the three of us drift apart, and don't sell the house. I said yes. I was wrecked, I'd been sleeping in a chair for a week, and honestly I'd have agreed to anything to make him stop looking so scared. I'm 41 now. Mum's gone. My brother has been in and out of trouble his whole life, and a few months back he did something — not violent, but bad enough that I've cut him off for now, because my own kids were getting hurt by it. My aunt says I'm breaking a promise to a dead man; my partner says a promise to a corpse shouldn't run my life. I feel about half of each. I did one term of an intro philosophy course years ago and there was a week on promises, but all I remember is that everyone seemed to disagree with everyone.

And here's the part that really bugs me. When I told my brother I was stepping back, he said "you're released, I never asked for a minder." So the person the promise was *about* says I'm off the hook. But he isn't the one I made it to. So whose promise is it, exactly? What I want is the real answer, not the therapy answer: if that promise still binds me, what is actually doing the binding — and does it matter that he's dead, that I was falling apart when I said it, that the promise was vague to begin with, that everything it was about has changed, or that the only person still holding me to it is my aunt? And if it doesn't bind, then what *was* I doing when I said yes? I don't need a reading list, I just want someone to lay out what's genuinely at stake here and where the weight really sits.
