Construct a market scenario to order, for a unit on why competitive markets trade at all rather than settling into silence. The principle you are working against is the no-trade result: if the participants share a common prior over the states that matter, if the structure of the market is common knowledge, and if it is common knowledge that every participant is rational and uncoerced, then no purely speculative trade can take place, because any offer to trade is itself informative — the party receiving the offer reads it, revises, and declines. Your task is to build a case in which trade nevertheless occurs and leaves both sides better off, while all four of those conditions continue to hold.

Constraints. The setting must be ordinary and free of technical machinery: a face-to-face exchange such as a used-car sale, a small mutual insurance pool, a forward contract between farmers, or a betting table among friends — not an abstract state space or a laboratory market. The parties must genuinely share one prior over the payoff of whatever changes hands; none may be irrational, deceived, or acting under duress; and the trade must not rest on any difference of opinion about what the thing is worth. Hold the scenario to a single paragraph.

Then do the accounting. Identify exactly which premise the no-trade result must be suppressing in order for your case to be possible, and show that the suppression is real rather than smuggled in through a stipulation. Name at least two rival explanations of the trade — for example, that the parties simply disagree, that one is being misled, or that one is compelled — and show that each is ruled out by your setup. Then isolate the single variable that is actually doing the work: say what the private information in your case is about, and explain why information of that kind fails to trigger the adverse-selection reasoning that kills trade when the private information is about the asset's value instead.

Finally, run the contrast. Keep the same two parties, the same asset, the same common prior, and the same rationality assumptions, and move only that one variable — relocate the private information from where you placed it onto the asset's payoff. Show that trade now unravels, say at which step it unravels, and argue that the unraveling is forced rather than a matter of the parties being slow to see it. Close with an honest note on how a defender of the no-trade result would attack your original case: which stipulation they would challenge, and whether conceding the challenge costs them the result or only a loose paraphrase of it. A case that asserts the trade instead of generating it from the traders' positions does not meet the brief.
