Write a satirical corporate policy document: the newly revised "Section 7: Conduct, Commitment, and Corrective Action" of the employee handbook of a fictional neuroscience-adjacent firm that has adopted eliminative materialism about the propositional attitudes as official company doctrine. Around 700 words, in the deadpan register of real human-resources prose, formatted as numbered subsections.

The satire should be legible as fair to someone who knows the eliminativist literature and follow from the doctrine rather than from the document announcing its own absurdity. The handbook should repeatedly legislate against folk-psychological posits — beliefs, desires, intentions, expectations, trying — while proving unable to state a single rule, expectation, or disciplinary procedure without quietly relying on them; it should include a clause requiring employees to affirm, sincerely, that sincerity is a prescientific fiction; a clause governing promises, contractual intent, and what "meant to" can mean now; a clause on how performance will be assessed once "knowing how" and "trying" have been reclassified as pre-theoretic relics awaiting replacement by the relevant neuroscience; and a grievance procedure for employees who report that a manager keeps attributing beliefs to them. It may also gesture at the instrumentalism retreat — that the vocabulary is retained as a useful fiction — provided the document cannot quite keep its own fiction straight.

No line should wink or declare itself ironic. The comedy must come from the doctrines being taken seriously and pressed to their institutional conclusions: the theory-theory framing of folk psychology as a predictive theory that will be displaced, the phlogiston-and-caloric analogies, the self-refutation and indispensability objections, and the still-unsettled empirical bet on future neuroscience. A reader who does not know the debate should find it funny as a portrait of bureaucratic self-contradiction; a reader who does should recognize that each absurdity is a real pressure point in the eliminativism dispute. The target is the practice of installing eliminativism as a governing doctrine, not any individual.
