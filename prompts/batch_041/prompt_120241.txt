Write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the debate over expected value fanaticism. The first camp are committed expected-value maximizers who hold that a sufficiently large payoff at a sufficiently small probability can outweigh any ordinary certain good, and who are willing to follow the arithmetic wherever it leads. The second camp are critics who accept expected-value reasoning for ordinary probabilities but reject it for vanishingly small ones, whether because they impose a probability threshold, bound the utility scale, or treat some values as not to be traded off against any probability, however small. Both camps should be presented at full strength. The collaboration was prompted by a concrete choice: a funder can either secure a guaranteed modest benefit or buy a lottery ticket with a one-in-10^40 chance of a benefit 10^80 times larger; both sides accept the arithmetic but disagree about whether the lottery gives a reason at all. Write the document they could both sign, in their voice rather than an outside adjudicator's. Format it as exactly five numbered sections, each a compact paragraph, with no introduction or conclusion. Section 1 lists the claims both accept about the formalism, the cases, and the burden of proof. Section 2 states their remaining disagreement in wording each side would accept for its own position, and says which part of the dispute, if any, is merely verbal. Section 3 identifies the cruxes, including whether tiny probabilities can generate reasons and whether unbounded value aggregates are well-defined. Section 4, for each crux, records a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Section 5 is a self-critical note: say where the document is most likely to fail, which formulations are most vulnerable to being vacuous or slanted, and what argument or consideration would lead each side to change its verdict.
