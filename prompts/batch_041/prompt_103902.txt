I'm building a little writing exercise and could use a hand. Write me a mock FAQ page — the kind of slick help-centre / "sustainability commitments" page you'd find on a big agri-chemical company's website — for a made-up company (call it whatever you like) that has decided to fold insects into its welfare accounting. The joke is that they've actually absorbed the scale argument — astronomically many insects, a small and deeply uncertain probabilty that any one of them is sentient, so the expected amount of morally relevant suffering comes out enormous — and instead of being radicalised by it they've turned it into a spreadsheet, an actuarial model, and a press page. The satire should land on the practice of taking the scale reasoning seriously in a corporate-accounting register (welfare offsets, expected-value line items), not on the insects.

Format: Q&A, 6–9 questions, roughly 500–700 words. Voice: deadpan corporate comms. No winking, no nudges to the reader that this is silly, no "of course, this is absurd" asides. The humour has to come from the reasoning being actually right, so that someone who knows the debate laughs instead of wincing.

Some things I want in there, worked in naturally:

- One question where they just show the arithmetic: insects affected per hectare per application, times hectares treated, times an explicit per-insect probability of sentience, equals an expected-welfare figure. Their numbers can be their own invented assumptions (it's a fictional company), but the multiplication has to be internally consistent and the order of magnitude has to be plausible.
- A question where their own actuary or in-house ethicist pushes back on the correlated-uncertainty point: if the probability of sentience is driven by facts common to all insects, you can't just multiply it by the headcount, and the giant number collapses. The company's answer has to be a real answer, even if it's a self-serving one — dont just have them dodge it.
- A question on whether the probability is per individual, per species, or per unit of nervous tissue, and why their particular choice happens to lower the figure.
- Something on offsets / credits / why they don't simply stop spraying, and what an "insect welfare offset" would even mean.
- A customer-facing question, like whether any of this changes the potatoes.

Please don't invent studies or citations; if it needs a source, make it an obviously fictional internal document ("our 2023 methodology note").

Then, after the FAQ, add a short note (3–4 sentences) on where the piece takes liberties with the actual scale argument and which bit of the reasoning you're least confident you got right.
