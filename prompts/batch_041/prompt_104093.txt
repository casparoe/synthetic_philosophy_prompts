I'm trying to state my hedonic theory cleanly for a paper and want to check whether it is actually a coherent package before I commit to it. Here is where I have landed. (1) Welfare hedonism: a life goes better for the one living it exactly to the extent that it contains more pleasure and less pain, and nothing else counts in itself. (2) Pleasure admits of degree on a single scale, so that any two pleasures can in principle be compared and added, and a well-defined quantity of net pleasure attaches to a life. (3) There is no one introspectable quality shared by all pleasures: the pleasure of a hot bath, of finally seeing a proof go through, and of hearing that a friend got the job have no common felt character. (4) What makes them all pleasures is that the subject is pleased by something; pleasure is always pleasure taken in an object, whether a sensation, an activity, or a fact. (5) Pleasure is not the satisfaction of desire, since being pleased by something is a different state from getting what one wanted, and the two come apart.

I expect a reader to pounce on (3) as the one that fights with (1), but I want to know where the real trouble is. Which of these pull against each other, which pairs only look as though they do, and what is the cheapest revision that would restore consistency? Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and tell me which single commitment you would give up if you had to. Take a definite position and defend it rather than laying out the options for me to choose between. Write it as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
