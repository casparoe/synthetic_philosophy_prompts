I've been teaching myself reinforcement learning in my spare time -- the kind of system that takes an action, receives a number that can be positive or negative, and gradually adjusts its behavior so that the number comes out larger -- and the way the people who build these systems write and talk about them has started to bother me. The agent is said to want the reward, to be punished when the number goes negative, to seek one thing and avoid another. When a researcher wants the agent to explore, a bonus for novelty is added and called curiosity; when the researcher wants it to stay near a supply of something it needs, the bonus is called hunger. And then, if you ask the same researcher directly, you usually get told that nothing is going on inside -- that there is no one home and nothing it is like to be the agent. I would like to know what view of pleasure, pain, and mattering this whole way of building and describing agents presupposes, and whether the practice is even coherent given what its own practitioners say about it.

The thing that first made me suspicious is that these separate signals -- the task reward, the curiosity bonus, the hunger bonus -- are simply added together into one number that the agent maximizes, so that a unit of curiosity gets traded off against a unit of hunger as if the two sat on the same ruler. That looks to me like a commitment rather than an engineering convenience: it seems to assume that everything an agent could possibly care about can be lined up along a single scale, and that a thing's position on that scale is what makes it good or bad for the agent. So please reconstruct the theory that is actually at work. Does the design assume that the positive-or-negative quality of an experience is a property of the number itself, or a property of some subject that feels it? Does it assume that the only thing that matters to a creature is how much of this one quantity it accumulates? Which rival views does it quietly rule out -- that suffering requires someone to suffer it, that the felt character of an experience is essential rather than incidental, that some goods simply cannot be weighed against each other? And is the practice internally consistent: can you keep the entire engineering setup and still deny that these agents have any welfare at all, or does the vocabulary of punishment, curiosity, and hunger commit you to more than you meant?

Be careful to separate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits comfortably with -- I suspect that several quite different theories would license the very same code, and I want to know where the design is underdetermined. Then tell me what would have to change in how such systems are built and described if we replaced the presupposed view with the rival I find most plausible, namely that a feeling has to be felt before it can be a feeling. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
