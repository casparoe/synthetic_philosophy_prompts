Take the four cases one at a time and say which of my instincts are doing real work and which are just me forcing a metaphor — I suspect Germany looks like full recognition and provisioning plus a compulsory independent audit, Japan like something kept contingent or amortised for decades, Turkey like a refusal to recognise the loss combined with pressure on anyone who tries to audit it, and the South like a restatement that a large block of shareholders still contests. Don't smooth over the places where the framework quietly assumes its own metaphysics (that losses are quantifiable, that there's a single entity doing the reporting, that a debt can in principle be discharged) — flag them when they're doing the work.
