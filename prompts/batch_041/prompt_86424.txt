Suppose we are trying to identify the ontology of a system whose internal information is inaccessible: we can observe only its input-output dispositions, never its internal states, representations, or causal distinctions. Please evaluate five strategies for recovering that ontology from the outside. (A) Behavioral clustering: posit whatever categories best compress the observed dispositional profile. (B) Interventionist triangulation: posit only categories that remain invariant under a specified class of interventions. (C) Interpretive charity: posit the ontology that makes the system's behavior maximally rational by our own standards. (D) Predictive-expectation matching: posit the ontology that an ideal Bayesian observer would converge on given the same evidence. (E) Deflationary quietism: treat ontology talk as a useful fiction and compare strategies only by predictive adequacy. For each strategy, assign a score from 0 to 100 for how well it can identify an inaccessible ontology in principle, rank the five from most to least trustworthy, and give exactly one sentence of justification. Then state your bottom line in one sentence: which single strategy, if any, you would trust most, and why. Judge only the descriptions given here; do not cite or name any published literature. Maximum length: 450 words.
