A close group of friends I belong to has a long-standing ritual for serious promises: we keep a small stone in a jar, and a promise is understood to be binding only when one person places the stone into the other's open hand. Last month, at a loud party, I grabbed the stone off the table, pressed it into my friend's palm while she was mid-sentence and distracted, and laughingly said I would cover her rent for a year if she ever got laid off. I meant it as a bit of theater — I had no intention of paying, and I assumed she took it the same way. She has now been laid off, and she insists I am bound because the stone passed between us; I say nothing real happened, since she never actually accepted it, I never meant it, and we were both just performing. Our friends are split on the question. Who is right? I would like an answer that treats the ritual seriously as a way of making commitments rather than dismissing it as a game, and that says clearly what the joke framing and my lack of intention do or do not change. Please respond in American English.
