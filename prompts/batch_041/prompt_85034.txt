I am drafting a position statement on skill and knowledge to circulate before a workshop, and I should like it audited before anyone else sees it. Please take the role of a philosopher of mind and action who works on the epistemology of skill and who knows the intellectualism debate in detail. Think carefully before you commit to a verdict, and begin by restating my position in your own words and noting anything you find ambiguous, so that I can see how you have read me. I do not need a reading list or attributions — I want the arguments worked out from the commitments as I have stated them.

Here is where I have landed.

1. Knowing how to do something is a species of propositional knowledge. To know how to F is to know that such-and-such is a way for one to F; I take this to be the correct account of the ascriptions themselves, not a reconstruction of them.

2. I reject the idea that the proposition known in a case of know-how is a special, practical proposition, or that it is grasped under a distinctive practical mode of presentation which the instructor who can only describe the stroke lacks. The content is ordinary, and so is the attitude taken towards it.

3. Skilled performance is largely unreflective. The expert does not consult representations as she goes, and the familiar findings about automaticity and the chunking of action sequences show that guidance by explicit content drops out as skill is acquired.

4. Know-how ascriptions are gradable and context-sensitive in a way that knowledge ascriptions are not. "She knows how to play the piano" admits of degrees, and whether someone counts as knowing how to do something shifts with what is being asked of her and with the standards in play.

5. Know-how cannot be acquired from testimony alone. One can be told, exhaustively and accurately, how to do something and still not know how to do it; practice is indispensable.

6. Ability and know-how come apart in both directions. A skier who cannot say what she does need not know how to ski, while an instructor who cannot ski at all may nonetheless know how to.

Now: which of these pull against one another, and how badly? I should like the genuine tensions ranked by severity, each with an account of what would have to give and what that would cost me; the pairs that only look as though they are in tension, with an explanation of why they are not; and, at the end, your judgement as to which single revision would clean up the most. I should also like to know which of my commitments you would want to press me on even if it is not strictly inconsistent with the others.
