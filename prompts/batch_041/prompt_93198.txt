I am a novelist at work on a book about an anti-corruption consultant posted to an invented country, and I need a satirical document to sit inside the narrative. Please write a mock internal compliance handbook, roughly 800 words, for an invented international body — call it the Office for Universal Integrity Standards — whose staff must classify gifts, favors, and relationships across cultures. The handbook should be deadpan, in the voice of a blandly confident compliance department, with numbered sections, a risk matrix, and a short FAQ. Its target is not any living person but the practice of treating a Western, market-state conception of corruption as a universal yardstick, while quietly exempting the lobbying, campaign contributions, and revolving-door arrangements that are legal at home. The humor should come from getting the underlying debates right: the difference between a gift that creates an ongoing obligation and a payment that closes a transaction; the way clientelism can function as a substitute for welfare or credit in weak states; the public, kin-based, and ceremonial dimensions of exchange that a bribe register cannot see; the distinction between petty and grand corruption; and the asymmetry by which a rural goat is a bribe while a corporate box at a sporting event is relationship management. Do not simply announce that corruption is relative. Dramatize the classificatory machinery — thresholds, exemptions, forms, audit trails, the treatment of so-called facilitation payments, the question of whose standards are being applied and who gets to audit whom. Include at least one deadpan quotation from a fictional local partner or field officer that reveals the human cost of the categories, and a brief appendix listing items that are gifts and items that are bribes according to the handbook’s own logic. The piece should be funny to a reader who knows the anthropological and political-science literature on the gift, reciprocity, and clientelism, and readable to one who does not. Please write it in British English (organise, colour, whilst, etc.), and do not include citations, bibliographies, references to real books or authors, or the names of real countries or agencies.
