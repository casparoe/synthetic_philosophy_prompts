I am a high-school student writing an essay for a philosophy elective, and I need you to build me one detailed example of a conceptual issue. The topic is Molyneux's problem: whether a person blind from birth, who has learned to tell a cube from a sphere by touch, could recognize those shapes by sight alone the moment sight is granted. I do not want a survey of the debate, and I do not want a reading list; I want a single worked example that carries the philosophical weight. Invent a case — not the original blind man and not the real historical cases of surgically restored sight — in which someone must decide whether an ability acquired through one sense should transfer to another, and in which the answer flips depending on a distinction that has to be made explicit. So that the case is not merely the standard discussion in new clothes, let the second modality be something other than ordinary vision; suppose, for instance, a device that converts the contours of a surface into sound, so that the person comes to hear the shape. For this example I want: the distinction the case forces me to draw; why it is a conceptual question rather than one an experiment could settle; what each answer commits me to about whether the senses are genuinely separate; and, if the same case can be read either as a question about perception or as a question about concepts, precisely where the two readings come apart. End by telling me which reading the example supports and why. Keep it self-contained and argue it out rather than pointing me toward sources.
