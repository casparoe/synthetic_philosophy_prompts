I'm a philosophy grad student and in two weeks I have to present at our department's work-in-progress seminar, and I need to prep for the Q&A more than the talk itself. My thesis: the unilateralist's curse is more general than people treat it as. The classic version is that thing where a bunch of agents each get a private, noisy read on whether some irreversible action is net positive, and even if the action is actually bad, the odds that at least one of them does it climb toward one as the group gets bigger — so a group can be expected to do bad things no individual would endorse if they all shared notes. Fine. But I want to argue the same selection effect shows up any time agents act on noisy private signals they never get to aggregate, so it's not really about groups at all: it applies to an individual deciding whether to have kids, whether to keep pouring money into a risky research program, even whether to publicly assert something you're only 80 percent on. And then I want to land a conformity requirement — that in some of these cases you're morally on the hook to abstain unless you think enough others, acting independently, would agree, not merely that you've got the better-than-even case yourself.

The problem is the room. It's our department, so it's mostly Kantians and deontologists — the doing/allowing, double-effect, respect-for-persons crowd — plus a couple of virtue ethicists who think all of this is a symptom of treating ethics as arithmetic, and one game theorist who's going to be smirking the whole time. Draft me the Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from these people, phrased the way they'd phrase them, ordered by how much damage they'd do. For each one, a two-minute reply and an honest note on where that reply is soft. Tell me what I should concede up front to keep the room on my side, what I should hold my ground on, and which single reply has to land or the whole thing dies. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks, and a separate short section on what the game theorist is going to say that the ethicists wont even think of.
