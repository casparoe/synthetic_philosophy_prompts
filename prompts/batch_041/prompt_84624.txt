A few of us have a standing argument in the pub about whether pleasure and pain are even the same kind of thing — whether a big pile of mild enjoyment can ever cancel out a stretch of real misery, or whether saying it cancels out is a category mistake. I want to stop rehasing it and just make a one-page decision tree we can all fill in, so we can see which position each of us actually ends up at.

The view I want to build the thing around is what I've been calling lexical threshold negative utilitarianism — I may have the name wrong, it doesn't matter, treat it as a stipulation. The idea: the disvalue of suffering doesn't trade smoothly against pleasure. There's a floor, so that below some intensity (or duration, or kind) a pain is basically noise and counts for nothing. There's a ceiling, so that above some level no amount of pleasure compensates for it, not one unit, not a googol of mild delights. And in between the two there's a band where the two are traded off at some rate. I want the tree to map out that view's variants and its nearby rivals.

So: a taxonomy as a tree, each branch labelled with the question that splits it from its siblings, and each leaf a position with a short descriptive name (make the names up if you have to — no need to tell me who held what, or cite anything) plus one sentence on the most awkward commitment that leaf has to swallow.

Branch on things like: is the claim that pain and pleasure are commensurable a claim about value itself, about the structure of experience, or about what we can actually measure and compare? Is the asymmetry between them a matter of an exchange rate (pain is worth k times an equal pleasure, finite k) or of a line (below/above, no rate at all)? Are the floor and the ceiling independent parameters, or does setting one fix the other — and can you have only one of them? Is the threshold absolute, or does it slide with what's on the other side of the ledger, so the ceiling rises when there's alot of pleasure at stake? Is the lexical part strict — nothing in the lower tier ever compensates — or only threshold-lexical? And is the unit the moment, a whole life, or a per-person total, and does the structure change once you're adding across people rather than within one life?

I want at least these somewhere in the space: straight Bentham-style commensurability; pure negative utilitarianism with the line sitting at zero; finite negative weighting; a floor-only view with no ceiling; a ceiling-only view with no floor; a view where pleasure satiates but pain doesn't; parity or vagueness rather than full comparability; flat-out incommensurability; and a view where the asymmetry is intrapersonal only, so no compensation within a life but aggregation allowed across lives.

Keep it to 10 to 14 leaves, no more than about five questions deep, and if two leaves are ones your tree can't really pull apart (I suspect the measurement skeptic and the incommensurabilist will be like this), say so instead of papering over it. For any combination of answers that doesn't correspond to a view anyone would hold, leave the branch hanging and say in a few words whether it's empty because it's incoherent, because it's obviously unattractive, or just because nobody's bothered. This is for a group of about eight of us, most of whom aren't philosophers, so phrase every question as something a person could answer without already knowing what the answer is called.
