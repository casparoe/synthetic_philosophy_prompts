Explain the conceptual difference between explaining a moral revolution and justifying one. Take the case of a practice like slavery or dueling that was once widely accepted and later became unthinkable. Suppose someone argues that such changes are not driven mainly by arguments — the arguments against the practice were available long before it ended — but by a shift in what the relevant community took to be honorable, so that continuing the practice came to signal weakness or degradation rather than mastery. Explain what it means to say that the new outlook cannot be vindicated in terms of the old one's standards, and then address whether this makes the change irrational or merely non-argumentative. In doing so, distinguish the causal roles of argument, interest, and honor, and say whether a transition driven by honor can count as a genuine moral revolution rather than a change in taste or a side effect of shifting class interests.
