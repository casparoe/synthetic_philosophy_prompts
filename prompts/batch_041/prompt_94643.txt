Construct a thought experiment in which the principle that mere temporal position is no reason to value a good less — zero pure time preference — is forced to prescribe an action that almost everyone, including most defenders of that principle, would refuse, while every other ground for discounting is stipulated out of the picture. Constraints: one ordinary person; goods that are ordinary and equally usable at both dates; the beneficiary at both dates is that same person, so nothing may turn on future generations, population size or non-identity; the later date is at least twenty years off; the later receipt is certain, as is the person's survival to it; no borrowing, lending or investing; no change in the person's tastes, values or information between the dates; nothing larger than a year's income at stake; and the scenario itself no longer than one paragraph.

Then, in order: (a) lay out the two options and give the verdicts — what zero pure time preference says, what a small positive rate says, and the exact rate at which the verdict flips; (b) name the single feature of the case that is doing the work, and say what becomes of the case if that feature is removed; (c) give the strongest reply available to someone who denies that the case exhibits pure time preference at all, and say whether that reply succeeds.

Keep the whole answer under 600 words. Start with the scenario; leave out definitions and framing.
