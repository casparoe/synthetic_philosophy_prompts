The position I've come to hold, roughly in Quine's spirit: moral judgements aren't in the business of tracking truth the way scientific claims are. They're more like expressions of preference, or directives for coordinating behaviour. Quine's naturalism says reality gets described from within science, and ethics isn't part of that enterprise — it's something else we do with language, continuous with wanting rather than believing. Moral disagreement, on this picture, isn't a disagreement about facts that one side might be getting wrong; it's more like a clash of attitudes. The upshot is that there's no moral reality for our moral claims to answer to, and no truth of the matter for the philosopher to uncover. Ethics becomes a kind of engineering: shaping desires, not discovering truths.
