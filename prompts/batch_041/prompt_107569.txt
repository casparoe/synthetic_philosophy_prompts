Ok so I've been going around in circles on this one for months and I need someone to just rank these for me. Everyone says compromise between people with totally different moral views is good because of "gains from trade" — like two countries trading, both end up better off. But I can't make that work for morals, because there's no price on any of this stuff, people say their values are priceless, and on top of that neither side actually knows how much the other side really cares. Everyone has an incentive to act like they care more then they do, especially if they're a politician talking to their own voters rather than to the other side. So is there even a gain to trade, or is it just whoever is worse at posturing getting rolled?

Anyway I made a list of the five moves people actually make when they "compromise," and I want them ranked from most to least real gain from trade — meaning most to least actual value created, rather than one side quietly losing or the same pie just getting cut into different shapes.

The five moves:
(1) Splitting the difference — each side gives up half of what it wanted.
(2) Swapping — you get your way on issue A, I get my way on issue B, and we call it even.
(3) Kicking it to procedure — we stop arguing about what should happen and agree instead on who decides, or how it gets decided.
(4) Nesting — both sides keep their principle completely intact, but one of them gives up the right to act on it here, in this case, for now.
(5) Side payments — the side that loses gets paid off in some other currency, could be money, could be an apology, a plaque, a holiday, a promise about something else entirely.

Two things I want doing the work when you rank them. First, the posturing test: imagine both sides suddenly find out how much the other side actually cared, now that nobody has any reason to exaggerate anymore — does the deal still look like a win for both, or does one side realize it gave up something huge for nothing? Second, does the move actually grow the pie, or does it just slide slices from one plate to the other? I suspect 2 and 5 fail the first test badly and 3 and 4 fail the second, but I might have it backwards, so don't just tell me they're all fine in different ways.

Give it to me as a numbered list, exactly five items, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up.
