My statement of it is this: two parties must divide some surplus; each has available a move that locks it into a demand it could not credibly abandon; whoever locks in first typically ends up with the better share, because the other party must then either give way or accept a deadlock; so both parties have reason to lock in before the other does, and a race ensues.

My question is how to win such a race. The reasoning I have been working with is this. Since locking in earlier is always better than locking in later, and since the only obstacle to locking in early is the agent's own hesitation, a commitment race is essentially a contest of resolve: the decisive party wins, and the ideal commitment device is one that is instantaneous, costless, and irreversible. Two consequences seem to follow, and I would like them confirmed. The first is that a party able to commit should always commit at the earliest logically possible moment, so that any delay is a failure of nerve rather than a strategic cost.
