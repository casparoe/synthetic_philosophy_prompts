Im a third-year phil student and next week I have to present my term paper at the joint session my aesthetics seminar runs with the art history department, so im basically talking to a room of people who spend their lives handling actual objects. My paper is called The Original Doesnt Do Any Aesthetic Work. Claim: once you fix the experience a painting affords to a viewer who knows everything relevant about it, there's nothing aesthetic left that the original has and a perfect forgery lacks. The extra money we pay for the real one buys three non-aesthetic things, and this is the bit I'd like you to take seriously: evidence value (the object is a trace of the past that tells you things no copy can), contact value (the odd charge of being causally hooked up to the actual moment of making), and scarcity value (there's only one, so it's a positional good). My main move is that critics who insist the original is better aesthetically are smuggling contact value in and then relabeling it. The only genuinely mine part is a diagnostic I run: put a heavily over-restored picture next to a perfect forgery — the first has the contact but what you see is mostly 19th-century repaint, the second has the experience but no contact. Ask which one you'd rather have in the gallery. My claim is people flip depending on which example you describe first, which shows the premium isn't tracking anything aesthetic at all.

The audience: mostly art history grad students who work on conservation and provenance, one curator from the university museum who has to justify acquisitions to trustees, my professor (analytic aesthetician, works on the ontology of art, will like the framework and hate where I take it), and one person from archaeology who thinks about looting and repatriation and will definately think I'm a monster. What they care about: whether restoration destroys the work, whether a painting just is its material history, and whether anything I say would survive contact with a court. In their world authenticity isn't a puzzle, it's a thing you can be sued over.
