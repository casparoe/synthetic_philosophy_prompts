A guy in my alignment reading group swears this is a proof and I can't find the hole. Setup first, because it's the whole point: you take a human supervisor who's allowed to consult an AI assistant; the two of them work on a problem too hard for the human alone; the human breaks it into subquestions small enough that they can check each answer themselves (nobody checks whether that was the right way to break it up, only whether each answer holds up); then you train a fresh assistant to imitate the human's answers on those subquestions, and start over with the new assistant. That's the iterated amplification and distillation loop.

His argument: (1) if a supervisor can decompose a task into pieces they can each check, and they do check them, then the task is in effect solved by the supervisor, with the assistant supplying only labour. (2) So every output of the amplified system carries the supervisor's endorsement. (3) Training an assistant to imitate endorsed outputs puts nothing into the assistant that wasn't already endorsed. (4) Endorsement is preserved by both steps, hence by any number of rounds, so the final model is exactly as aligned as the human was. (5) The final model is also far more competent, since at each round the assistant is at least as good as the human at the subquestions -- it's trained to match the human and it has vastly more compute. (6) So the loop delivers something superhuman that is still on our side, and alignment is solved by construction.

I'd like to believe it. What bothers me is that I can swap a single bored annotator in for the supervisor and "whatever humans already wrote" in for the decomposition, and the same five steps prove that ordinary fine-tuning on human text yeilds a trustworthy superhuman system, which it plainly doesn't. So some step is doing work it hasn't earned. Which one, why does it read as a truism when you hit it, and what does patching it cost me? I'd rather lose (5) than (4) if that turns out to be the choice, so tell me whether I can. Continuous prose please, no headings, bullets, bold or tables -- just write it out like an email.
