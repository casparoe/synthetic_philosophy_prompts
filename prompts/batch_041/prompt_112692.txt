My friend group has this recurring fight that always ends with someone saying "when in Rome, do as the Romans do," like that settles it. Last month it was about whether a country can expect newcomers to adopt its customs (friend A: obviously, when in Rome). This week friend B used the exact same sentance to argue we shouldn't judge the customs of other cultures at all. Same proverb, opposite conclusions, both delivered with total confidence.

Can you take that one sentence apart and show me the different precise things it could be claiming? I want a menu, not one reading explained at me. Off the top of my head the variables seem to be: who the "you" is (a tourist? someone who just moved there? a third-generation kid who'd rather not? someone who's been there all along and disagrees with the majority?); what counts as "Rome" (a whole country, a neighbourhood, a workplace, the majority's norms, or just whoever's loudest); and whether "do as the Romans do" is advice or a demand -- i.e. is the claim that conforming is prudent, that it's morally required, or that the local norms are actually *correct* because they're local? Also: do the Romans all do it? What if the locals are split, or the norm is one they only enforce on outsiders? And does it cover behaviour only, or does it reach your opinions and beliefs too?

There's also a background thing I half-remember about cultures differing in how tight or loose their norms are -- how much rule-following is demanded and how much deviance is tolerated -- and I'd like the readings sorted against that: which ones presuppose a tight society, which only make sense in a loose one, and which are really just claims about how norms work anywhere. My hunch is that the proverb gets used by people on opposite sides of the assimilation argument because it's actually two or three different claims wearing one coat, but I'd like to see it done properly.

For each reading, say whether it's a platitude, a real and defensible claim, or just false, and tell me which reading each friend has to be relying on for their conclusion to follow. And please be upfront about where people who actually study this are agreed vs. where it's definately still contested -- I don't want a contested view dressed up as the settled consensus.
