I'm not a philosopher, just a guy who reads too much and then argues with his friends about it. Heres my problem: everyone I know agrees we have a duty to remeber — remember the wars, remember the bad stuff so it never happens again. But I've also read a couple of books arguing that forgetting gets a bad rap, and that all this remembering mostly just keeps old wounds open and old grudges ticking. Both felt convincing while I was reading them, which I take as a sign I don't understand the question yet.

So here's what I want. I made a list of the reasons people actually give for keeping a painful memory alive. Please rank them from most to least defensible, and give me a couple of sentences on each so I can see why it landed where it did:

1. Debt — we owe the victims something, and remembering is the only way left to pay it.
2. Warning — if we forget, it will happen again.
3. Identity — without that memory we wouldn't be who we are; the group is built out of it.
4. Truth — it happened, and pretending otherwise is a lie, whatever good the lie does.
5. Ritual — remembering isn't supposed to achieve anything, it's just what a community does with its dead, and asking what it's for is already the wrong question.
