I am a third-year PhD student in heritage studies, currently serving as a research consultant to the restitution working group of a mid-sized encyclopedic museum. The group receives roughly one formal claim a month, and the board has asked us to replace ad hoc case-by-case decisions with a defensible procedure. The chair has proposed that we adopt a principle she calls reparative relationalism: cultural objects are not merely property but nodes in a continuing relationship between a community and its past; a claim for return is strong to the extent that (a) the community's relationship to the object is constitutive of its identity, (b) the museum's continued possession actively sustains a wrong by denying recognition, blocking access, or benefiting from a history of expropriation, and (c) return would repair that relationship rather than merely transfer title. The working group is not philosophically trained, and I need to translate this into something they can actually run: an ordered set of questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, where it must hand the decision to the board because the question is one of values rather than facts, and how to record dissents. Please write that procedure, keeping as much of the principle's substance as survives operationalization, and be explicit about which distinctions you had to drop, where the procedure is likely to misfire, and what it cannot settle at all.

Then run it on three cases. The first is the Benin Bronzes, taken in the British punitive expedition and now dispersed across several museums; the second is the Parthenon Marbles, removed under Ottoman authority and now in a single encyclopedic museum; the third is a hypothetical collection of ceremonial objects from a community that was missionized in the nineteenth century, where the claimant is a diaspora organization and the local community is divided about whether return is desirable. I should say where my sympathies lie so you do not soften the result: I think the Parthenon Marbles should go back, and I am inclined to give diaspora claimants standing even when the local community is ambivalent. If the procedure delivers a weaker answer in either case, I want that stated plainly, not hedged. The board meets in six weeks, the working group has no authority to settle legal title, and the external ethicist on the committee will want to see the value judgments separated from the factual findings. At the end, please state how confident you are in the procedure you have given and identify the single step you are least sure about.
