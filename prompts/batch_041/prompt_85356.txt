I've argued myself into Gödel's conclusion about time and I want you to try to talk me out of it, or at least tell me what should. The way I see it: he cooked up that rotating-universe solution (the one where the whole cosmos has to be spinning and you need a nonzero cosmological constant), and the point of it isn't the time travel gimmick, it's that in that spacetime there is no global time function at all — you can't foliate it into a succession of nows — and that's exactly the structure an objective lapse of time would need. Special relativity only took away the privileged now; this takes away any global now whatsoever, and the closed timelike curves make it vivid, since the past can't be simply gone if you can loop back into it. So becoming is a feature of how we describe the world rather than of the world, and time in the robust sense is ideal. That's roughly the shape of his argument, I think.

What I want is a map of what counts against it, ranked by how much it should actually move me, and sorted into the stuff the position's own defenders would concede versus the stuff they'd wave away. A friend of mine says the whole thing is a modal cheat: even granting that a rotating universe solves the field equations, going from the theory permits a world with no objective time to time in our world is an illusion looks like exactly the fallacy people commit when they argue from the bare possibility of a designer to the existence of one. Another friend says the opposite, that it isn't the rotating universe doing any work, that SR alone already kills the objective now, so the rotation and the closed curves are decoration and the argument should be statable without them — and if it can't be, that's a problem for it. I'd like to know which of those bites, whether the no-global-time-function point survives once you're allowed to say becoming is a local and perspectival matter rather than a global one, and whether the conclusion is even the sort of thing evidence could touch, or whether it's been fenced off as unfalsifiable — and if it has, whether thats a real defect here or just what you'd expect from an interpretive claim about a physical theory. Then, if you can, give me the strongest version of the view that survives all of that, and tell me what it gives up to survive. Don't tell me to go read anything, I can't.
