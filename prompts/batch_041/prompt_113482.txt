I'm in my school's philosophy club and I have to write a dialogue to read out at the next meeting. The topic is the thing my friend and me argue about constantly: when do some things add up to a bigger thing? He says there's literally no such thing as a chair, there are only the tiny particles arranged chair-wise, and I say if thats right then there's also a thing made out of my shoe plus the moon, which is nuts. So write the dialogue between two smart people, one who thinks composition is completely unrestricted (any two things whatsoever always make a new thing) and one who thinks it never happens at all (only the basic particles exist, nothing is ever made out of anything). Neither one should be a pushover, and they should actually answer each other instead of trading speeches. I want them to fight about two cases: a candle flame, which stays the same flame even though the wax in it is constantly burning away and being replaced, and a bunch of single-celled things that live separately and then all clump together and crawl around as one blob for a while before splitting up again — is the blob a new object that wasn't there before, or just the cells arranged blob-wise? At some point let one of them try the escape hatch of saying maybe there's no fact of the matter here and it's only about how we talk, and let the other one say whether that works. No quotes or reading lists, just the dialogue.
