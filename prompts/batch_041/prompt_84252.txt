Max Weber died in 1920, well before any of the following existed: France's statutory 35-hour week, the legal right to disconnect from work communications outside working hours that several countries now recognize, and karoshi — the Japanese category of death from overwork that can qualify a worker's survivors for compensation. Suppose he could observe all three. Would he read them as a continuation of the ascetic ethic he traced to Calvinism and Puritanism, or as a reversion to the pre-capitalist traditionalism he illustrated with harvest laborers who cut back their hours when piece rates were raised? I want his likely verdict on whether anyone has a right to leisure, on whether karoshi is the work ethic outliving its religious reward or a modern pattern of overwork his framework cannot explain, and on what in his own account he would have to give up if the 35-hour week turned out to strengthen rather than weaken the work ethic. Be explicit about which of his claims you are extrapolating from.
