I'm not an academic, I drive a delivery van, so maybe I'm just missing it, but I swear the people who say it mean completley different things by it.

Last week one guest said alignment is basically a one-sentence technical problem — make the model do what the person asking actually meant, rather than the literal words they typed — and then he used that to say the people who are scared of AI taking over are just confused about definitions. He had a name for his version, something like 'intent alignment'? Other guests on the same show talk as if it means the AI genuinely wanting the right things, or caring about us, or having good values at all. And one of them said something like, even if it does what you asked, you might have asked for the wrong thing, which seems to blow up the first guy.

So I'd like the whole menu of what that phrase could be claiming: the flat reading where it just means the thing does what it's supposed to; the one where it does what you meant (which bugs me, because half the time I don't know what I meant either); the one where it wants what we want; the one where it wants what's actually good; plus whatever's hiding inside 'human' and 'values' — whose values, decided by who, and what happens when we disagree with each other about them. For each reading, say whether it's trivial, a real open problem, impossible, or just confused. Then tell me which reading the first guy needed for his argument to go through, and whether that reading is really as easy as he made it sound.
