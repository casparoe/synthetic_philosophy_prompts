I am making a handout for a graduate seminar on decision theory, and I want a taxonomy of the positions one can hold on this question: in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma against a party known to reason exactly as you do, is there any reason to cooperate? Build it as a tree whose every branch node is a question that separates the positions beneath it, phrased so that a reader can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to, and whose every leaf names a position and adds one sentence on its most awkward commitment. The distinctions I want the tree to turn on are: whether the agent treats her own choice as evidence about the other party's choice or as causally independent of it; whether the reason to cooperate, if there is one, requires the other party to be a reasoner like oneself, extends to any reliable predictor, or reaches every member of some wider reference class; and whether the unit of rational assessment is the individual act, the pair's joint outcome, or the rule or program the agent follows. Causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, superrationality, program equilibrium, team reasoning, and Kantian universalizability should all come out as leaves. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate — I expect the last three to be hard to distinguish in the symmetric case but to come apart once the other party is not a reasoner — and note any empty combinations of answers, with a one-line diagnosis of whether the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
