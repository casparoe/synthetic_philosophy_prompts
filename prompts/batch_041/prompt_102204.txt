Alright, this is a puzzle I keep running into and I don't know if it has a name or if I'm just confusing myself.

Say I'm stuck between two ethical views. Call them A and B. A is roughly 'what matters is suffering — beings that can suffer are the ones that count, and how bad a world is basically comes down to how much suffering is in it.' B is roughly 'what matters is flourishing — suffering is bad mainly because it blocks a good life, but the good life is the real thing of value.' I hold both with some credence and I don't know which is right. So I do the sane-sounding thing: I act as if each one might be right and pick whatever scores best on average across the two.

Here's where it breaks. A and B don't just disagree about which outcomes are good; they disagree about how much anything matters, and I can't line the two up. Under A, going from a world with a bit of suffering to one with none is the entire point of ethics. Under B that same change is real but pretty small next to the gap between a flourishing life and a merely okay one. So when I average, B doesn't win because I think it's likelier — it wins because B's numbers are just bigger. If I rescale B so that its small equals A's big, then A wins instead, and I haven't changed a single thing about what I believe. The winner is being decided by an arbitrary choice of units, like measuring in inches vs centimeters and getting a different result.

Same thing happens when I think about future people. One view says a life a thousand years from now counts exactly as much as a life now; another says it counts a lot less, because of how far away and uncertain it is. Again, whichever view I scale up wins, no matter how likely i think it is.

I know this is near stuff people already talk about — Pascal's wager, the idea that a tiny chance of some huge or infinite bad thing should swallow every other consideration, expected-value reasoning under uncertainty. But it doesn't feel like quite the same problem, because here neither view is claiming anything infinite or astronomically unlikely. Both are ordinary and both are plausible, and the trouble is only that I can't put their values on one ruler.

So: is there a name for this? Is it a genuinely seperate problem from the Pascal's-wager/fanaticism thing, or the same problem in different clothes? And if it does have a name, what do people actually do about it — is there a non-arbitrary way to fix the scale, or is the honest answer that 'do whatever scores best on average across your moral views' is just not well-defined here? I care way more about the carving than the label, so tell me exactly what this is, what it isn't, and where the edges are.
