Write a joke about the thesis that consciousness is a user illusion — the view, imported from interface design, that introspection delivers a simplified model of the brain's own processing and that the felt subject of experience belongs to that model rather than standing behind it. The joke should be funny to someone who actually argues for or against the position: the punchline should turn on the view's real commitments (the transparency of the illusion, the denial of phenomenal properties, the meta-problem of why we report any of this at all), not merely on the phrase user illusion. One angle worth exploiting: if the interface is transparent and no user sits behind it, then whoever is telling the joke is part of the rendering too. If it sharpens the punchline, you may introduce a small piece of invented jargon, provided the setup makes its meaning clear.
