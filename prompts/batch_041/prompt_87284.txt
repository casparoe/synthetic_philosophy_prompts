I am assembling materials for a session on the metaphysics of meaning, and I want the requirements on the table before we look at any particular account. The question is where meaning resides — in the message, in the decoder, in the relation between them, or somewhere else entirely. The vocabulary I have in mind, which you should feel free to replace if it obscures more than it helps, comes from the chapter of Gödel, Escher, Bach titled The Location of Meaning, where three layers are distinguished in any information-bearer: a frame message, which announces only that there is something here to be decoded; an outer message, which carries, implicitly, instructions for how to decode it; and an inner message, the content itself. I want the desiderata for any account of meaning that takes such layers seriously.

Write the requirements an adequate account must meet, each paired with the case or consideration that generates it. I want at least the following cases handled explicitly, since each has been used to embarrass one position or another: an inscription in a script no one can read and for which no bilingual key survives; a stretch of DNA, read and acted upon by machinery that understands nothing; a musical score, which underdetermines every performance that still counts as a performance of it; a string of symbols produced by an uncomprehending process that happens to be a valid derivation in some formal system; a sentence whose speaker meant one thing and whose competent hearer took another; and a signal that is a message only relative to a code that has since been lost. For each, say what the account must deliver and why that is a genuine requirement rather than a preference of one school.

I also want the distinctions the account may not collapse, each again with the case that forces it: between meaning and information; between what a message means and what it causes; between being interpretable and actually being interpreted; between what a sender intends and what a convention fixes; and between the content of a message and the content of its frame.

Then the part I care about most. Say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are negotiable, and whether any subset of them is jointly unsatisfiable. My own suspicion is that the layered picture buys its explanation of the inner message at a price it cannot pay: if the outer message tells you how to decode the inner one, then the outer message must itself be read, and the frame must be recognised as a frame, so the account threatens to generate a regress of messages — one that can be stopped only by a layer that is not a message at all, whose uptake is not interpretation. If you think the regress is benign, say precisely what stops it and why the stopping point is not itself an unread message; if you think it is fatal, say which of the other requirements it takes down with it. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude otherwise plausible accounts.

You may close by scoring one or two well-known positions against the finished list, briefly, but the list is the deliverable — do not let it turn into a survey. Throughout, state how much confidence you place in each claim you make, and single out the requirement you are least confident belongs on the list.
