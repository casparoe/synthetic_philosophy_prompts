I sit on the diagnostic standards committee of our national psychiatric association, and last night an old friend who sits on the same committee and I argued for two hours about the proposal we vote on next month: whether to add a new category — call it "attentional depletion syndrome" — to the next revision of the manual. The proposed criteria are almost entirely self-reported: a persistent sense of mental exhaustion after sustained attention, together with a tendency to reorganize work and social life around the label. What unsettled us both was the field trial. Patients diagnosed at intake scored closer to the prototype profile twelve months later than at baseline, and the clinicians reported that patients seemed to grow into the diagnosis. My friend calls this a textbook looping effect and says the category manufactures the very cases that then appear to validate it; I think he is conflating the way a category shapes people with whether it tracks anything real, and that several categories we already accept behave the same way. Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote. I need a concrete recommendation — adopt, reject, or adopt only with specified safeguards — with reasoning the other members can actually weigh, an honest statement of the strongest version of my friend's objection, and, if you recommend adoption, exactly what the safeguard should be and how we would know in five years whether it had failed.
