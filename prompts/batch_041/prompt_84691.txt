Write me an essay about the hedonistic imperative, i.e. the idea that instead of just treating suffering we should engineer it out of existence with biotech — gene editing, mood drugs, whatever works. I'm broadly in favour but too things bug me and I want you to actually dig into them instead of hand-waving.

First one: say we could rewire pain so it stays informative — you still get the signal when your hand is on a hot stove, you still flinch back — but the signal loses the awful can't-stand-it quality. Have we abolished suffering there, or just relabelled it? Does it matter whether the person still wishes it would stop?

Second one: say the tech is cheap and safe enough to just put in the water. Nobody chooses it, everyone's baseline mood goes up. Is that a different question from offering it? And is there anything genuinely valuable we'd lose — meaning, art, grief, sympathy, motivation, the whole romantic story about suffering making us deep — or is that just a cope?

Don't end on "it's complicated". Take a position on whether the project should be pursued, and under what conditions (opt-in vs imposed, present people vs people not born yet).

Format, in this order, with roughly these lengths:
- What's actually being claimed — 150 words. Separate the modest version of the view from the ambitious one.
- The case for abolition — 350 words.
- The case against — 350 words.
- Consensual vs imposed — 250 words.
- My verdict — 300 words. Say what you'd actually do if you were in charge and the tech worked.

Use the hot stove example and at least one example about love or grief or art.
