I have no formal training in philosophy or decision theory, but I have been working through the Dutch book argument on my own, and I want to turn a half-formed idea into a short research proposal, no more than two pages, for an independent scholar grant. The standard story, as I understand it, is that if your credences violate the probability axioms, a bookie can offer you a set of bets such that you lose money no matter what happens, and that is supposed to show your credences are irrational. My worry is that the argument quietly bundles together two different claims: first, that your credences are internally inconsistent in a way that can be described without any bookie; second, that they are exploitable by an opponent who knows them and can choose the stakes, the direction, and the order of bets. I suspect these can come apart. A set of credences might be internally inconsistent but not exploitable if the bookie must offer a single take-it-or-leave-it package, or if your utility for money is not linear, or if you are allowed to revise between bets. A coherent set might still be exploitable if the bookie can control framing or order. I also wonder whether the argument is really about money or about utility, and whether a sure loss of money counts as a sure loss of utility when money has diminishing marginal value.

Please write the proposal. I need a precise question with a determinate answer, an explanation of why the usual defenses and objections leave it open, a concrete method—what cases or distinctions I would construct, what a decisive example would look like, and how I would tell whether the two claims come apart—what would count as the project failing, and a realistic scope for six months of part-time work. Do not include a bibliography or a literature review; I will add references myself, and the proposal can refer to positions and arguments without citations. At the end, say how confident you are in the main claims and single out the point you are least sure of.
