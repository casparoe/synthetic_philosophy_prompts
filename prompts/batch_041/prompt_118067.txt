I'm a novelist — literary stuff, nothing you'd have heard of — and through a series of small civic accidents I'm now the lay member on our state's substance policy review commission. We don't write law, we make recommendations, and the legislature mostly follows them. I've been on it four years and I still can't tell you what our actual rule is. Next month we vote on the big one, and I'd like to know what we'd be committing ourselves to before I open my mouth.

Our track record, roughly in order:

- Cannabis: we recommended full legalization. Regulated retail, taxed, home grow allowed. 6–1.
- Psilocybin: we recommended decriminalizing possession and allowing it in supervised clinical settings, but we deferred retail sales "pending dosing standards." 5–2.
- Kratom: a bill to ban it came to us. We rejected the ban and recommended keeping it legal for adults, with purity and labeling rules and an age limit. Unanimous, which never happens.
- Synthetic cannabinoids, the "spice" stuff: we recommended criminal penalties for manufacture and sale, but possession stays a civil fine — no arrest, no record. 6–1.
- Heroin: we recommended decriminalizing simple possession with a mandatory treatment referral instead, and we endorsed a supervised consumption site. We also recommended against any legal sales, and that part wasn't close. 4–3.
- Alcohol: we approved a small excise increase and rejected an advertising ban, on the grounds that the evidence for the ban was weak.

The chair opens every meeting with the same question — "if we say no, what does the black market do?" One member brings up Portugal every single time. And there's a guy who votes on harm to the user and basically nothing else.

The new one is a draft bill to create a legal, regulated cocaine market. Pharmacy-only, no advertising, potency tested, a monthly cap per person, tax earmarked for treatment, and — this is the part everyone's yelling about — powder only, nothing smokable.

So: what do I vote for if I want to be consistent with those six decisions? Tell me the rule or rules our record actually fits, what each one predicts about the cocaine bill, and which you'd bet on and why. Also tell me which past decision is the closest analog here, and if the record honestly doesn't decide it, what principle we'd be adopting by voting yes and what we'd be adopting by voting no. And name one future case that would definately tell the rules apart — I'd rather find that out on something small than on cocaine.
