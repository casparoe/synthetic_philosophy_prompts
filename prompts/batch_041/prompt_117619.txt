Me and my mate Dan had a massive row about risk last night and I'd like to settle it properly instead of by who's louder.

The short version: he pays through the nose for insurance on stuff he'll almost certainly never claim on, and I said he's basically paying to avoid a bad feeling, which is a bit daft. He said no, he's buying off a real risk, and if I really thought the odds were that good I should be happy to take the other side of the bet myself. Then I said it depends whether caring about risk is a proper part of caring about how your life goes or just an arithmetic slip, he said obviously the second, and we spent alot of the evening going round the Allais thing and the urn with the unknown mix of balls, both of us defending positions we couldn't name. What actually ended the evening was him saying he'd take a one-in-ten-thousand chance of dying to knock an hour a week off his commute, me saying no, and neither of us being able to say what we'd disagreed about.

So: build me a quiz that sorts people like us into the positions. Six to eight questions, each one a concrete choice between two or three options — an insurance premium, a job with a bonus lottery, a medical gamble, a risky move to another city, that sort of thing. Every option has to be something a person could actually say out loud, and no question may just ask whether you're risk averse or name a theory at the reader. Then a scoring key mapping patterns of answers onto positions. Our lot are mostly people who did a bit of economics at uni and nobody's a philosopher, so keep it in plain English.

The positions that have to come out the other end, as scoring outcomes: (i) risk aversion is just a mistake in the maths and expected value is the whole story; (ii) risk aversion is a rational attitude towards the good itself, so a chance at a good thing is part of what you're getting and not merely a means to it — I think there's a name for this, risk-weighted expected utility or something like that; (iii) a version of (ii) where the re-weighting falls on the odds of doing at least this well, so the worst outcome just keeps its plain odds, rather than on each outcome's odds taken on their own; (iv) unknown odds are a different thing from risky odds, and only the first licenses caution; (v) throw the odds out and just protect the worst case; (vi) risk aversion is only rational where the bad outcome can't be compensated or is the sort of thing that changes who you are — fine to gamble with money, not with your kidneys.

Then tell me which combinations of answers nobody lands in, and for each of those whether it's because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. If two of the positions above come out identical on every question you've written, say so rather than pretending the quiz separates them, and tell me what sort of question would be needed to prise them apart.

One more thing: the questions do need to separate (ii) from (iii), which I'm fairly sure is a real fork and I can't for the life of me turn into a case. And please don't just trot out the famous paradoxes as the questions — I want cases I could actually put to Dan over a pint.
