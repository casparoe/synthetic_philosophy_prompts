I'm a second-year PhD student in sociology, and next week I have to run the session of our department's theory reading group that's devoted to the individualism–holism dispute in social explanation — the question of whether social phenomena must ultimately be accounted for in terms of the beliefs, dispositions, and actions of individual people, or whether some social facts are legitimately explained by other social facts with the individuals left out of the causal story altogether. The group is a mixed crowd of about a dozen: roughly half are quantitative sociologists and demographers who take it as obvious that the individualist demand is just what serious explanation means, and half are historians and ethnographers who find that demand vaguely ideological and suspect it smuggles in a politics. I have seventy-five minutes and a whiteboard, and I don't want to be talking at them for more than a quarter of it. Don't explain the dispute to me — I know it — plan how I should explain it to them.

Give me the order the pieces have to go in and what depends on what, so that by the time they hear the holist option it doesn't sound like mysticism or a plea for group souls. Tell me the single case to build the session around and what it must have and lack; I had been going to use a bank run, where each depositor's belief that the others will withdraw is itself part of what makes withdrawing rational, but I have a nagging feeling that example quietly rigs the game toward holism, so tell me whether that's right and what I should use instead if it is. Then map the misconceptions this particular crowd will bring or form — I'm anticipating "holism just means believing in group minds," "individualism just means assuming everyone is selfish," and "this is a political quarrel wearing a methodological costume" — and tell me exactly where in the sequence I should head each one off. Finally, give me the one question I can put to the room at the very end that would actually show me whether they can now run the distinction themselves rather than just recite it, and tell me what I should deliberately leave out at this level and announce that I'm leaving out. Keep the whole plan to at most 800 words, and lay the teaching sequence out as a numbered list of moves.
