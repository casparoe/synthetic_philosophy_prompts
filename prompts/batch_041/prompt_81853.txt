I got into this argument with my roommate after a seminar and I can't let it go. The position I've landed on is that pure time preference is irrational -- that once you've stripped out uncertainty, the chance the good never arrives, changing circumstances, and the fact that a later good might be less useful to whoever receives it, there's nothing left over that "later" alone could rationally do. A pain in ten years isn't a smaller pain than the same pain tomorrow just because it's further off. My roommate says I'm just reheating some Victorian utilitarian impartiality thing and that it's obviously true, which honestly makes me more suspicious, not less.

So what I want is the case against me. What would count against the claim that pure time preference is irrational -- not the standard "but people do discount" stuff, I know people do it, I'm asking what should move me. Which observations, arguments, intuitions, or formal results actually bear on it? I'm thinking of things like the spatial analogy (if it's irrational to care less about a good because it's in another country, why is it rational to care less because it's in another century -- and does the analogy break because you can travel in space but not in time, or because your causal reach differs?), the metaphysics-of-time angle (does it matter whether the future is a real location in the way the present is, or whether the A-theory or B-theory is true -- and does my view secretly presuppose one?), the worry that "pure" time preference is never actually isolable from epistemic discounting, the asymmetry between how we feel about past versus future pains, and the person-affecting vs. impersonal reading of who is being wronged by discounting. I also want the demandingness objection taken seriously: if temporal neutrality is true, what absurd obligations does it stick me with toward the very distant future, and is that a reductio or just a bullet I have to bite?

Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about which ones a committed time-neutralist would themselves concede versus wave away -- I don't want only what critics say. Then tell me straight whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability (say, by defining "pure" so narrowly that no real discounting behavior can ever touch it), and whether that insulation is a genuine problem or just what a conceptual thesis looks like. I'll say up front that I'm most persuadable by anything that doesn't lean on controversial metaphysics of time, so weight accordingly. And please tell me how confident you are in each of these verdicts, and single out the one point you're least sure about.
