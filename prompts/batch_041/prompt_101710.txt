For my high school independent-study project in philosophy of science, I have to write a satirical piece that shows I understand a theory well enough to exaggerate its actual logic rather than inventing jokes about it. Please write a leaked internal memo, 550–600 words, from the Office of Strategic Silence of an interstellar civilization to its sector commanders, recommending the immediate preemptive destruction of a newly detected radio-loud civilization. Use deadpan corporate-bureaucratic prose, with a one-sentence Recommendation at the top, numbered sections, and a closing Risk Assessment paragraph. The target is the dark forest hypothesis: the memo should satirize the reasoning that cosmic silence proves universal predation, that first-strike advantage is decisive, and that any detectable civilization is either a threat or bait. To keep it from becoming a generic space-war joke, build the satire out of two invented doctrines you should treat as settled internal policy: the Opaque Canopy rule, according to which any civilization that has survived long enough to transmit has probably already survived a dark-forest encounter and is therefore more likely to be a hunter than a child; and the Glass Canopy corollary, according to which the absence of detected attacks is itself evidence that the attackers are better at hiding than we are. Include a dissenting minority report from a junior xenolinguist who argues that the policy makes every observation consistent with itself, so it cannot be falsified and therefore cannot guide action; make his objection technically serious by having him distinguish the probability that a civilization is predatory from the probability that we would detect a predatory civilization, and note that the memo treats the two as interchangeable. The humor should come from the policy's own logic: the chain of suspicion, the communication lag that makes every treaty obsolete before it arrives, the fact that a civilization's silence is read as either fear or ambush, and the bureaucratic conversion of an unfalsifiable fear into a budget line. Do not mention real people, real books, or real historical events, and do not use quotations from any actual source. End with a bottom-line recommendation of no more than two sentences.
