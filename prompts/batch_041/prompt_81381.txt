Two of my friends have been arguing about Sen's liberal paradox for about three years now, and I want them to stop and finaly write the document down together and sign it.

Quick version of the setup so you can keep them honest. Three outcomes, two people. Weak Pareto: if everyone strictly prefers X to Y, society ranks X above Y. Minimal liberalism: at least two people each get one pair of outcomes over which they're decisive, meaning their own strict preference between those two fixes the social ranking of that pair no matter what anyone else prefers. Unrestricted domain, and social rankings only need to be acyclic. Sen's theorem: you can't have all three. Both of them know this part cold, so don't spend the document re-deriving it.

What they don't agree on. One of them thinks the impossibility is real: a genuine antinomy in political morality, since minimal liberalism and weak Pareto are each independently binding on any decent politics, so something has to give -- she thinks it's the unrestricted Pareto principle, or at least the idea that a right has to be honoured whatever anyone's preferences happen to be. The other thinks it's an artifact of how rights get written down, as a restriction on a social preference relation over complete outcomes, when a right is really a person's control over one coordinate of the outcome -- a game form -- and once you formalize it that way the impossibility dissolves and nothing much about political morality follows.

Draft the joint statement they could both sign. Numbered agreed-claims section first: what they jointly accept about the formalism, what the theorem does and doesn't assume, what it rules out, and where they now think the residual dispute actually lives. Then the disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept as its own.

Then the cruxes, and I suspect there are two or three. My guesses: (i) whether the cycle needs at least one person's ranking of their own pair to depend on what the other person does -- call that a meddlesomeness condition -- and what happens in the fully separable case where nobody's ranking of their own pair is conditional on anyone else's choice; (ii) whether the paradox survives once rights are modeled as coordinate-ownership, and if it doesn't, whether that shows rights were misformalized or just changes the subject; (iii) whether minimal liberalism names the same thing as liberalism as a political doctrine at all, in which case part of the fight is verbal -- say which part if so. For each crux: the concrete case or arguement both of them would accept as bearing on it, and each side's prediction about it, written down in advance.

Keep it to their own views -- no bibliography, no reading list, they've both read everything anyway. And end with a section on what each of them would count as a reason to abandon their own position.
