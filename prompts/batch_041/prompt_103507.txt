I am a graduate student in philosophy of science, and I am trying to get clear on the reference class problem. In a seminar last term the instructor gave a thumbnail sketch that has been bothering me. He said, roughly—this is my paraphrase, not a quotation from any text—that the reference class problem is the problem that to assign a probability to a single event you must put it in a class of similar events; but every event belongs to indefinitely many classes, and those classes give different frequencies. So frequentist probability cannot handle singular events. And since every probability is ultimately a frequency, there is no fact of the matter about the probability of a singular event. Bayesians escape the problem because priors are not frequencies and need no reference class; hence Bayesianism is the only fully coherent interpretation of probability. The practical lesson, he said, is that in clinical or legal settings any probability you report is arbitrary, and you should reason about the individual case without statistical generalization.

I suspect this is a mix of truth and overstatement, but I cannot tell where the joints are. Please audit it sentence by sentence: what would a philosopher of probability accept, what would they wince at, and what is simply wrong? I am especially interested in whether the summary conflates the reference class problem with the problem of the single case or with the problem of induction; whether the move from “frequentism has trouble with singular events” to “there is no fact of the matter about singular probabilities” is valid; whether the Bayesian escape is as clean as claimed, or whether priors, exchangeability assumptions, hierarchical models, and the choice of a statistical model merely relocate the problem; and whether the clinical or legal conclusion follows from anything true in the summary. Where a defender of frequentism or a Bayesian would object, say what the objection is and whether it succeeds. I do not need citations, reading lists, or a history of the literature; I want the conceptual map and a verdict on the summary’s fairness, with the parts that survive correction separated from the parts that do not.
