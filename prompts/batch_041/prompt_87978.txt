Im putting together a handout for the first session of a grad seminar on expressivism, and I want to teach the Frege-Geach problem as a forking problem rather than as the usual parade of five labelled replies. So please build me a decision tree whose leaves are positions on the embedding problem, where every internal node is a question a student can answer before they know which -ism it leads to. No node should just be 'are you a noncognitivist?'

Start from the thing that's actually at issue: what is shared between a moral sentence as asserted and the same sentence as it occurs embedded -- under negation, in the antecedent of a conditional, and inside an attitude report like 'Sam thinks lying is wrong'. Make the first split the one I care about most: is the thing that travels across embeddings a state of mind, a content, a truth-condition, a rule of inference, or a pretense? And at every leaf, please note whether that view treats the embedded occurrence as having the same meaning as the unembedded one or a different one. I think that uniformity question is the real fault line and nobody seems to draw it cleanly, so I want the tree to make it explicit.

The handout is two sides of A4, so keep it to roughly 8 to 12 nodes and 8 to 10 leaves. I need these to show up somewhere as leaves: the consistency-of-attitudes reply, the minimalist-truth reply, hybrid/ecumenical views that build a descriptive component into the moral term, pretense-or-fiction views, inferentialist views that treat the meaning as a role in inference, the 'the problem is an artifact of truth-conditional semantics' reply, and a leaf for views that say moral discourse just isn't in the business of logic at all. Each leaf gets the position in a sentence, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment -- I especially want the ones that handle negation fine but go quiet when you ask about attitude reports.

Where the tree leaves a combination of answers unoccupied, say so and diagnose it: is nobody there because the view is incoherent, because it's coherent but nobody wants it, or just because nobody's tried? And flag any two leaves the tree can't actually pull apart, instead of pretending the questions separate them.
