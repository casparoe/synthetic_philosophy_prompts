I've been trying to work out what I actually think about ambiguity, going on gut reactions to cases, and I want to know whether there's one principle behind them.

Urn A has 50 red balls and 50 black. Urn B has 100 balls, red and black in some proportion I haven't been told. Betting £100 on red, I'll take A. Betting on black, I'll take A as well. If I only have B, though, and someone makes me pick a colour, I don't mind it at all -- red or black, same to me. It's only when A is sitting there next to it that B starts to feel like a bad deal.

A coin I'm told is either fair or double-headed. Betting on heads, I'd rather have that than a fair coin. So ambiguity that can only work in my favour doesn't bother me.

A coin drawn from a jar of coins, some fair, some weighted towards tails. Betting heads, I'd rather have a known fair coin than that one.

Now the payout instead of the odds: I can have a flat £50, or a coin flip between £80 and £120 depending on a coin I haven't seen. I'll take the flip. Unknown amounts dont bother me the way unknown odds do.

And the one that nags at me: a stranger offers to bet me on whether the next card off the deck is red, having already looked at it. No thanks -- I want out of that far more than I want out of urn B.

What principle gets all of that? I suspect it isn't really 'ambiguity' at all, but something about whether the hidden fact could be stacked against me, or about me being exposed to someone else's information -- but I can't get it into a clean statement, and I've no idea what it implies when the ambiguity is symmetric and the stakes are big, or how much I ought to pay to have B's composition revealed to me.

So: tell me what the principle is -- stated properly, with the case that motivates each clause -- or the small set of principles if no single one does it. Say whether any of my verdicts has to go, and what the principle predicts about two cases I genuinely can't sort out: (i) B and B', both filled from the same barrel so their compositions are identical, and I'm offered a bet on red in B and a bet on black in B'; (ii) the same stranger, except he hasn't looked either.
