I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher, and I want to pressure-test an argument I keep bumping into. As I understand it, Galen Strawson's Basic Argument runs roughly like this: you do what you do because of the way you are; so to be truly responsible for what you do, you would have to be truly responsible for the way you are; to be truly responsible for the way you are, you would have to have chosen it; and to be truly responsible for that choice you would have to be truly responsible for the way you were when you made it — which pushes the same question back one step, and then another, without end. Since nothing can be its own cause, the conclusion is that no one is ever ultimately responsible. I find this both compelling and suspicious, and I think of it the way I think about bootstrapping a compiler: somewhere there has to be a first compiler that nothing compiled, and I want to know whether responsibility has exactly that problem. Please build me one thought experiment to the following specifications.

1. It should involve a single ordinary adult in a realistic setting — a job, a family, a decision with real consequences — and it should say precisely what act she is being held responsible for. No time travel, no brain surgery, no clones, no gods, and no deterministic clockwork universe imposed by fiat.

2. The case has to make three verdicts come out simultaneously, and each must fall out of the position rather than being written into the scenario. First, the Basic Argument concludes she is not ultimately responsible. Second, an ordinary compatibilist — someone who says she is responsible because her action flowed from her own settled, endorsed desires and she was responsive to reasons — concludes that she is responsible. Third, an event-causal libertarian — someone who says the choice was not determined by prior events and that she was its source — concludes that she is responsible. The compatibilist and the libertarian must have a genuinely strong case; do not rig the scenario so that they look naive.

3. Include an element of deliberate self-authorship: at some earlier point the agent consciously worked to become the kind of person she now is — say, she deliberately remade her values and habits in the image of a mentor she admired, and sustained that project over years. The purpose of this element is that it looks as though it should stop the regress, because she really did choose her character.

4. Then show that it does not stop the regress, and identify exactly which step of the Basic Argument is doing the work once self-authorship is on the table. Make the regress concrete: if she chose her character for reasons, where did those reasons come from, and what made her the sort of person who would weigh them the way she did?

5. Isolate one variable. Construct a near-twin of the first agent who is identical in every respect except that she never engaged in any self-authoring — her character is simply given, and she acts from it just as the first agent acts from hers. Then state what each of the three positions says about the twin. The Basic Argument should return the same verdict for both agents; the compatibilist should treat them differently. Say plainly which feature of the case is carrying the weight, and what becomes of the whole example if that feature is removed.

6. Keep the case neutral on whether determinism is true. I want to see that the Basic Argument bites even if the agent's choice was genuinely undetermined, so that the libertarian's escape route is closed by the structure of the case rather than by assuming determinism.

7. Avoid the stock examples that circulate in this literature; I want something built for this question, not borrowed.

8. Finish with the strongest reply available to someone who wants to resist the conclusion — not a caricature, but the best line a defender of real responsibility would take against this specific case (for instance, rejecting a premise, or arguing that the standard of ultimate responsibility being demanded is one no one ever needed to meet) — and tell me whether that reply actually succeeds.

Deliver the scenario itself in a single paragraph, then the walk-through for each of the three positions, then the note on the working feature and the defender's best reply.
