I'm in an epistemology seminar and we've spent the last three weeks on Fitch's paradox of knowability — the one where if you grant that every truth is knowable in principle, and you also grant the pretty boring-looking facts that knowledge is factive (you can't know something false) and that knowing a conjunction is just knowing each bit separately, then it falls out that every truth is actually known, which is obviously absurd. So the anti-realist or verificationist who wanted 'all truths are knowable' as the cheap, deflationary alternative to realism ends up committed to the idea that there are no unknown truths at all, and everyone agrees that's too much. We've been fighting about whether this is really a paradox about knowability or just a fact about how knowledge behaves when you conjoin things, and whether the standard fix — restricting the knowability principle so it doesn't cover the awkward cases, the ones whose being known would somehow undercut them — is a principled move or just a patch.

Anyway, our seminar has this running joke where whoever's turn it is has to write an actual joke about whatever we just finished, and this unit is mine. I need a joke that genuinely turns on the content of the paradox, not a generic "philosophers are weird" gag and not something that just drops Fitch's name and hopes for the best. It should land for someone who's actually worked through the proof, so the punchline should ride on something real — e.g. the fact that the culprit is the unknown-truth case, or that the whole thing only bites because of the interaction between the knowability claim and the way knowledge distributes over conjunctions, or the intuitionist background the proof grew out of and why it's usually said to be worse news for verificationism than for realism. Format is totally up to you: one-liner, two-hander in a bar, a dialogue between a grad student and their very patient advisor, a shaggy-dog proof, whatever works. Just make sure a non-philosopher overhearing it could at least tell that I know what the paradox actually is. If you can sneak in a nod to why restricting the principle feels like cheating to some people and like the only honest move to others, bonus points.
