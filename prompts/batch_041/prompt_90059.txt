ok so here's a view about meaning i've drifted into and i want you to try and break it.

the setup first. take any message and i claim there's three layers to it. (i) the frame message: the stuff that tells you this is a message at all, and roughly what kind of thing it is -- it's in english, it's a traffic sign, it's a text from my sister, it's a song. (ii) the outer message: the stuff that tells you how to get at the content -- the header on a file saying it's gzipped, the fact that it's written in a particular alphabet, the tone, the formatting, the fact that quote marks around a sentence change what that sentence is doing. (iii) the inner message: the actual content, the thing you're left with once you've decoded. (i half remember getting these labels from a book about self-reference, but take the distinction on its own terms, the labels don't matter.)

my position is this: the meaning is the inner message. frame and outer are scaffolding -- necessary maybe, but not where the meaning lives. they're the wrapper, the meaning is the thing inside the wrapper. two texts with the same inner content have the same meaning however differently they're packaged, and a message nobody can currently decode but that's correctly framed and correctly headed still has its meaning, it's just not been recovered yet.

what should count against that? i want the counterexamples and arguments that should actually move me, sorted by how decisive they are. i'm already aware that sarcasm and air-quotes are supposed to be trouble here, so don't stop at "context matters" -- tell me which specific cases do real damage and which are just noise. in particular i want to know:

- are there cases where the inner message is empty, or trivial, or basically nonexistent, and the thing still means something? and if so is that a counterexample or just a different kind of message that my scheme was never about?
- can the outer message ever be wrong -- it tells you to decode in a certain way, you do, you get a garbled inner message, and yet the real meaning is still sitting there. if that's possible, whats the thing that fixes the correct decoding, and does my story have room for it?
- what happens when i keep asking what frames the frame? every outer message seems to need its own outer message, all the way up. does that regress make "the inner message" a fiction -- no bottom level, so my three tidy layers are really one thing descending forever? or does the regress actually terminate somewhere honest, and where?
- does my view predict anything its rivals don't, or have i set it up so nothing could ever count against it? if a defender says "fine, the outer message is part of the total meaning, but the meaning *proper* is the inner one," is that a real distinction or have i just bought unfalsifiability with a word? i'd rather know now than find out in an argument.

and tell me which of these a defender of my view would themselves concede counts against them, versus which they'd wave off as cases of a different kind of message and not real defeaters. be honest at the end about whether the view is doing any work at all or whether it's just a redescription of "content versus packaging."
