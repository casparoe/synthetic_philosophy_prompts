I'm giving a short talk on the two failure stories people tell about AI — the loud one, where a system gets quiety, terrifyingly good at grabbing influence and the world ends up optimized into something nobody asked for, and the quiet one, where nothing dramatic ever happens and we just drift into a world that belongs to nobody in particular. I want to close with a poem, 18-24 lines, that holds both without picking a winner, and I want the form to do the arguing instead of the vocabulary.

Two constaints. First, the drift has to be in the form: start it in tight rhyme and meter and let the constraints go slack line by line, so by the end it's basically free verse and the reader feels the thing loosen in their mouth before they could name it. Second, the loud failure gets one line that comes back — a refrain that returns completely unchanged every time, after the poem has moved on around it, so by the end it sounds like the only thing still standing while everything else has softened. Dont label either one, and keep the words "alignment" and "optimizer" out of it. Someone who knows the two stories should be able to say which is which; someone who doesn't should just think it's a good poem.
