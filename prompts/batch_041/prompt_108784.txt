I teach an undergraduate philosophy of science seminar and want a one-page decision tree that sorts students by the position they actually hold on what makes a claim, a theory, or a whole discipline scientific. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names: the first node should separate those who think a single necessary-and-sufficient criterion exists from those who think scientificity comes in degrees or by family resemblance, and every later node should be a question a student can answer without already knowing which position it leads to. Make the tree turn on at least these distinctions: whether the criterion is offered as necessary, as sufficient, or as merely typical; whether demarcation is a descriptive classification of a body of work or a normative verdict on how much to trust it; whether the thing being judged is a single claim, a theory, a research programme whose track record can improve or decay, a discipline, or the conduct of individual researchers; whether the operative factor is logical form (riskiness, falsifiability), historical performance (novel prediction, problems solved that were not built in to be solved), social and institutional organization, or the honest pursuit of inquiry whatever the results; and whether the standard is purely epistemic or also pragmatic (what to teach, fund, or admit as courtroom evidence). Use these cases as branch tests, and make sure each one actually does splitting work rather than decorating a leaf: astrology; an unproved mathematical conjecture; a stalled programme that survives only by patching itself after each failure; string theory in advance of any test; homeopathy; and a social or historical inquiry that cannot run controlled experiments. Each leaf should be a named position (or 'unnamed' if nobody holds it) with one sentence on its defining commitment, one on its most awkward commitment, and the test case that lands there. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and list the combinations of answers that correspond to no position, diagnosing in a line each time whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Return the whole thing as a JSON object with fields: nodes (id, question, yes_branch, no_branch), leaves (id, name, defining_commitment, awkward_commitment, landing_case), inseparable_pairs (leaf_ids, why), empty_combinations (answers, diagnosis).
