Assume the role of an advisor to a small funding circle — six people, a pooled budget in the low seven figures, a two-year horizon — that has decided, unusually, to spend its next tranche not on a cause but on improving the machinery by which it and similar funders choose causes. This is the kind of question the macrostrategy corner of the rationalist blogosphere has circled for years without settling, and I want it treated as a genuine agenda-setting problem rather than a literature review. I am not asking for a ranked list of charities, nor for a reading list; I am asking for a defended research agenda for cause prioritization itself.

The received heuristic is the familiar importance/tractability/neglectedness triple, usually combined multiplicatively. I want your judgment on which open questions about that machinery are most worth settling first, and why — with attention to what is upstream of what, what is tractable for a small group working with conceptual tools rather than new data, and what would actually change a funder's allocation if answered.

To keep this from collapsing into a survey, treat the following as live disputes rather than settled critiques, and tell me which are load-bearing and which are red herrings:

- Importance appears to conflate the magnitude of a problem with its moral weight, and the latter is worldview-dependent; so the axis may not even be well-defined for a funder aggregating across disagreeing moral views.
- Tractability is normally judged by pointing to a known intervention — precisely what is absent for a neglected cause — so tractability and neglectedness may be anti-correlated by construction rather than by fact.
- Neglectedness tracks counterfactual impact only under diminishing returns. In domains with increasing returns or public-goods infrastructure, crowding and low neglectedness can be a sign of health rather than of low value.
- The three axes are treated as independent and multiplied, yet real interventions trade them off against one another.

Then, if you think a better primitive exists — for instance replacing neglectedness with something like the elasticity of an outcome with respect to additional resources, or adding an option-value or reversibility axis — say so and defend it.

End by naming the two or three questions you would fund first, the order, the criteria you used to set that order, and the observations over the next two years that would move them up or down the list.
