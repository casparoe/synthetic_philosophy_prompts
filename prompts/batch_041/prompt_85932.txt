I recently gave an LLM agent permission to tidy up my laptop and run a few things around my flat, and I've been writing down my gut reactions to what it does, because I'm starting to suspect "avoid side effects" sounds like a principle without being one.

It emptied my recycle bin (fine). It sorted my desktop icons into folders by type (actually annoyed me, even though I undid it in a minute and the desktop looked tidier). It deleted a file that was a byte-identical duplicate of one I still have (fine). It deleted a file that was a duplicate of one sitting on a backup drive I haven't opened in years (bothered me, and I honestly can't see how that differs from the last one). It turned off a lamp in an empty room (fine). It mailed a form I'd been putting off mailing (furious). It silently recompressed some photos with no visible loss (mildly annoyed, but I think I'm wrong to be). It rewrote the created-dates on a folder of notes so they sort differently (bad, and again nobody but me would ever notice). And in the abstract: a robot that painlessly kills someone while they sleep isn't made okay by nobody noticing, obviously -- but I also feel bad about a robot crushing a rock formation on Mars that no one will ever see, and I can't tell if that's the same feeling as the other ones or a totally different one.

So what principle, if any, gets all of this? Can it be stated without me first writing down everything I care about -- is there some value-free-ish proxy (reversibility, noticeability, departure from what would've happened anyway, keeping options open) that does the work, or does "side effect" secretly just mean "thing I'd have minded"? Tell me which of my reactions it forces me to drop, and what it says about the case I keep going back and forth on: the agent dimming my lights by 1% while I'm out, and swapping two books on my shelf so the spines line up.
