I'm a PhD student in philosophy of logic, and I want help diagnosing a disagreement in my own area rather than resolving it. The dispute is between dialetheists and classical logicians. Dialetheists hold that some contradictions are true — that the Liar sentence, together with the ordinary truth rules and ordinary negation, yields a genuine case in which a sentence and its negation both hold — and they construct paraconsistent logics in which a contradiction does not entail everything, so that such a case can be tolerated without collapsing into triviality. Classical logicians hold that any derivation of a contradiction is a reductio: it shows that at least one of the ingredients must be given up, and that the dialetheist pays for consistency of the logic by quietly changing the subject — that the paraconsistentist's negation, or validity, or consequence, is not the one we were talking about.

The debate has run for decades, both sides know the standard moves, and nobody converts. My own hypothesis is that it is not, at bottom, a disagreement about logic. It is a disagreement about what counts as data for a theory of logic. Put the Liar, the truth rules, and classical negation together and you derive a contradiction; the classicist reads that derivation as a reductio of one of the ingredients, and the dialetheist reads it as a discovery about which sentences are true. From there the two sides cannot agree on what a counterexample to a logical law would be, and so cannot agree on what would settle the question. If that is right, the impasse is not a sign that the problem is hard; it is a sign that the two sides do not share a criterion of success.

I would like you to test that hypothesis and, if it fails, replace it with a better one. In doing so, work through the following, taking each seriously as a possible diagnosis rather than as a preliminary to a verdict.

First, the meaning-change charge. Is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or two camps answering different questions under a shared vocabulary? If part of it is verbal, identify exactly which part, and say whether the charge that the dialetheist has changed the subject can be made precise without begging the question in favour of the classical reading of negation.

Second, the possibility that this is two questions under one heading: one about which sentences are true, another about which sentences may be asserted or denied, or must be accepted as a norm of reasoning. It seems at least open that the dialetheist is right about the first and the classicist about the second, and that the dispute persists because the two are run together under the single word logic. Assess that.

Third, whether a hidden formal question is doing the real work. I have in mind the Curry-style trivialisation results: with a transparent truth predicate, a conditional that sustains modus ponens and contraction, and the truth rules, you get triviality, so the dialetheist must give something up. The live question is whether any of the available retreats — weakening the conditional, restricting contraction, restricting detachment, distinguishing a negation that tolerates overlap from an exclusion negation that does not — leaves a position that is both stable and recognisably a theory of truth, or whether each retreat simply relocates the problem, as the strengthened Liar threatens to. If the action is there, then progress in the philosophical debate is downstream of a technical result, and I want to know which result.

Fourth, whether part of the standoff is about what an agent could do rather than about what is true: if a logical law is something one reasons with and not merely something one reasons about, what could justify revising it, and can either side give an account of revision that is not circular? Is that a genuine crux or a red herring?

End with a verdict on what kind of disagreement this is, and on what would count as progress given the diagnosis — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question, or nothing at all, with an argument for the last if that is where you land. I am not asking for a survey of the literature or a reading list; I know the literature. I want the diagnosis argued from what each side actually says, and I want you to be willing to tell me that my hypothesis is mistaken.
