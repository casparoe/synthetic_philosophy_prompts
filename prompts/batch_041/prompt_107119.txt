For the final portfolio of my decision theory seminar I want to write a poem about the result that the expected value of information is non-negative — the one whose proof is simply that an agent who looks and then chooses optimally can always do whatever she would have done without looking, and so is never worse off than her uninformed self, state by state. What I want the poem to press on is the price of that guarantee: it holds only for an expected-utility maximizer whose preferences and available actions are untouched by what she learns, in a problem with no other choosers in it, which is to say it protects precisely the person the information does not change. Write it as a pantoum, with the speaker standing before something that will tell her a fact about herself and deciding whether to look, and let the form carry the argument: the line that states the reassurance should return verbatim in the second half, where it should read as a condition no one can meet, and the closing stanza's return to the opening line should close the loop without recovering anything. Keep the two halves distinguishable and the refrain doing the turn rather than commenting on it, and make it accurate enough that a reader who knows the theorem can tell which half is which, and can see that the second half is not an objection to the first but an inventory of what it quietly assumes.
