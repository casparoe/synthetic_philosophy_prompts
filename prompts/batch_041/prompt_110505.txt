ok so I've been going back and forth in the comments for like three threads now and I want to write down where I've actually landed on the regress of justification, because I keep feeling like I'm cheating somewhere.

(1) The regress has to stop somewhere. Infinitism is definately out — no finite creature can hold an infinite chain of reasons, and a justification I could never in principal finish isn't one I have. Pure coherentism is out too; a maximally coherent fiction is still a fiction.
(2) There are no self-justifying beliefs. A belief that comes with its own warrant built in, as an intrinsic property, is magic — a label where an explanation should be.
(3) No unjustified justifiers: a belief only justifies another belief if the first one is itself justified.
(4) I'm an internalist about justification. Whether I'm justified depends on how things look from my side, not on whether my belief-forming process happens to be reliable. BIV-me is justified in thinking there's a hand in front of him.
(5) In practice the regress stops because we decide to stop asking. You can demand reasons forever; at some point you take something as a starting point and move on, and that taking isn't itself something you can justify without going in a circle.

I have a feeling (3) and (5) are doing the real damage, and that (1) and (2) only look like they clash. Is this package consistent? If not, rank the tensions by how bad they actually are, tell me the cheapest fix for each and what it costs, and say which single revision would clean up the most.
