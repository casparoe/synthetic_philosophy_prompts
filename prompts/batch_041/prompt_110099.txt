My uncle spent thirty years selling agricultural chemicals and we get into it every summer when the town foggs the park and the school field for mosquitoes. I said something about not loving the idea of my kid rolling around on that grass the next morning, and he did the thing he always does: "The dose makes the poison." Everything is toxic at some dose, he says -- water, oxygen, vitamin A. The spray goes out at a concentration thousands of times below anything that's ever so much as made a rat sneeze, and if I want something to be scared of I should be scared of the car ride over there. I know he's not just making it up; I'm fairly sure that line is the founding slogan of toxicology, something from a Renaissance alchemist. But it feels like it's doing about six jobs at once and I can't tell which of them he actually needs for his argument.

So lay out for me the different precise things "the dose makes the poison" could be claiming, roughly from the boring to the ambitious, with a verdict on each -- platitude, genuinely substantive, defensible but narrow, or flatly false. Things I'm chewing on: does it just mean any substance can hurt you if you take enough, which seems almost trivially true and tells me nothing about this particular spray? Does it mean toxicity is a matter of degree rather than kind? Does it smuggle in the stronger claim that there's always a threshold dose below which nothing whatsoever happens -- and is that true across the board, or is that exactly where things like hormone-disrupting effects get strange, with effects at low doses that don't show up further up the curve? And which dose are we even talking about -- the amount in the jug, the amount in the air at the fence line, the amount in my kid's blood, the amount accumulated over a childhood of summers? How much of the slogan is really a claim about biology versus a procedural rule, i.e. that risk = hazard x exposure, so nobody gets to object to a chemical until they've named a dose -- which is a rule about who has to prove what, not a fact about bodies. I also keep hearing people amend it, "the timing makes the poison," "the mixture makes the poison," and I can't tell whether those are corrections of the slogan, narrower versions of it, or just the same idea with extra words. And something in the back of my head says the EU regulates pesticides by hazard (banning whole categories outright) while the US regulates by risk, which makes me suspect the slogan isn't neutral science but the flag of one particular regulatory philosophy.

Finish by telling me which reading my uncle is actually running, whether that reading gets him his conclusion ("so stop worrying about the spray"), and what I could say back that isn't just me asserting the opposite. A numbered menu with verdicts would be ideal -- I want to bring it to dinner, and I'd rather not be the guy who just looks anti-chemical.
