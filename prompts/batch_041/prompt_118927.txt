I've been in a reading group on Mencius and Xunzi for a few months and we've completly stalled on the human nature thing. You know the shape of it: Mencius thinks our nature is good, or at least that it contains the beginnings of the virtues; Xunzi thinks our nature is bad and that goodness comes from deliberate effort and ritual shaping. The standard gloss is that they disagree about whether people are innately good. But at this point nobody in the group believes that's the real disagreement, and yet we can't agree on what is, so I'm asking you for a diagnosis rather than a verdict.

Here's my hypothesis, tell me if it's right and where it's too crude. I think the impasse is largely verbal, or that a big chunk of it is: the two are using the word xing for different things, and once you pry apart (a) the descriptive claim about what motivational stuff a human is born with, (b) the claim about what's distinctive of humans as opposed to other animals, and (c) the normative claim about what a human is for, or what counts as a well-formed human, the apparent contradiction mostly evaporates. On this reading Mencius is mostly in (b) and (c) and Xunzi is mostly in (a), so they're answering different questions under one heading and the headline contradiction is an artifact.

But I'm not confident, because there's a version of the dispute that still looks substantive to me, and it's the reason I don't want to just declare it verbal. It's about what a human would be like with all the social shaping stripped away: whether the raw endowment already contains something that reliably grows into virtue on its own, or whether it contains only self-interested desires that have to be bent from outside by ritual and effort. That reads like a real disagreement about the structure of human motivation, and it also reads like something empirical-ish, or at least like a disagreement about unobservable counterfactuals (what would the pre-social human do), which might be exactly why it never gets settled.

So: diagnose the stall. Is it verbal, is it two questions under one heading, is it a hidden disagreement about the descriptive facts of motivation, is it a disagreement about whether a normative claim about flourishing can be read off a descriptive claim about endowment at all, or is it some mix? If you think part of it is verbal and part substantive, say which part is which. And whatever you conclude, tell me what would actually count as progress in the group: a distinction that dissolves one of the horns, a way of making one side's claim testable or at least falsifiable in principle, an argument that the question is ill-posed, or an honest admission that nothing will move it. Please engage what the two actually argue, not just the labels good and bad: the role of ritual and deliberate effort on Xunzi's side, the growth of the moral beginnings on Mencius's side, and the way each of them treats the gap between what people actually do and what they're capable of becoming. Don't give me a reading list or citations, I can't look anything up right now.
