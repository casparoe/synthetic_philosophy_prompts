I spent thirty-odd years in a physics department and retired a few years back; lateley I've been reading economics, which is a humbling experience for someone who thought he knew what a model was. Several of my old grad students went into finance, and they all eventually bring up Long-Term Capital Management — the fund with the two brand-new Nobel laureates on its board, the one that bet on spreads between nearly identical securities, borrowed enormously to do it, and then watched the Russian default come along and finish it off in a few months.

What I'd like to do is run that episode through the no-trade theorem, which I think I finally understand: if the traders are rational and it's common knowledge how everyone gets their information, then nobody can profit from private information, because the mere fact that someone is offering you a trade tells you they know something you don't — so you decline, and they decline, and the trade never happens. Any trade that does happen has to be explained by something outside the model: different priors, hedging, a need for cash, or a fool.

So take LTCM and tell me what comes out. Who was on the other side of those convergence trades, and why were they selling something that, by the fund's own account, everyone agreed was mispriced? Is the collapse a confirmation — that the partners were never paid for being right about value, only for bearing risk, and mistook one business for the other — or is it an anomaly, since the theorem needs rationality to be common knowledge and what actually happened was that it stopped being common? Or is this just a case the theorem never contemplated? And then the other direction: what does the episode do to the slogan that the price is a good summary of what everybody knows? People keep telling me that if you think the price is wrong you should ask what the person on the other side knows that you don't. Is LTCM an argument for that advice or against it? Say where the theorem is silent instead of making it talk, and skip the history — I know the story, I want the mapping.
