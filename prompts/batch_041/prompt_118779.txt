so i'm in a reading group that's been doing philosophy of physics all semester, and last week was the hole argument — the old one from the early days of general relativity, where you take a spacetime, fix everything outside some region, and then inside the region you permute which point carries which field values. Because the field is all there is, you get a second model that says exactly the same thing as the first about every observation anyone could ever make, and yet disagrees with it about what's true inside the hole. Half the room thought these were obviously two descriptions of one situation, the other half thought they were obviously two situations, and one guy said it was really just a fight about the word "same", which i thought was a cheap move, you can seperate any disagreement that way. Anyway, i promised to close the last session with a poem, so here's what i want: a sestina about it, in American English.

The sestina part isn't decoration, it's the whole reason i'm asking for that form. Six end-words coming back in a fixed shuffle, six times over — same six words every stanza, different arrangement, nothing new added — and then the envoi, where all six finally crowd into three lines. That's the relabeling, and i want the form to carry it. So pick the six end-words with that in mind; something like hole, point, field, name, same, world would work, but swap any of them if a different set does the job better. Across the six stanzas nothing should happen that wasn't already happening in the first one, the only thing that changes is which word is doing what and where. And i want it accurate enough that the person in the group who actually knows this stuff wouldn't wince: the hole, the two models, the fact that no observable can pull them apart, and then the price — either the points don't have identities independent of the field they carry, in which case there was only ever one model, or they do, in which case the theory leaves the inside of the hole wide open even after everything before it is fixed. If words like determinism or Leibniz equivalence turn up, they should earn their keep rather than get name-dropped.

No citations needed, obviously — i don't need you to tell me who wrote about this, the group knows. And please don't let it resolve. The last three lines should set the two readings side by side and just leave them there, the way our discussion actually ended.
