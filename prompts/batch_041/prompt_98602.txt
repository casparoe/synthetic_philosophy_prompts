I am assembling a position statement on art and politics for a graduate seminar, and I should like an audit of it before I circulate it. Here is what I hold.

(a) Every artwork carries and transmits a conception of the good life. There is no ideologically innocent art, and the pose of the disengaged artist serves the powers that be.
(b) It follows that an artist working on behalf of an oppressed people has a duty to make that inevitable transmission serve that people's liberation.
(c) Art must be truthful: it must render a people as they actually are, including what is petty, vicious and ordinary, and not only as noble, long-suffering and admirable.
(d) A work's standing as art is settled by its beauty, and beauty is not a function of political utility. A beautiful work that advances no cause is still art; a crude work that advances a good cause is not thereby redeemed.
(e) The audience that matters most for art of this kind is the dominant group, whose false picture of the oppressed must be corrected, so the artist's chief task is to make the oppressed legible and admirable to their oppressors.
(f) As a verdict on a case: a novel that renders the internal cruelty and petty vice of a poor community truthfully and beautifully, but supplies no countervailing image of dignity and no redemptive arc, fails in the artist's duty.

I hold all of these, and I suspect I cannot hold all of them. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which merely appear to be; rank the genuine tensions by severity; and for each, say what would have to give and what that concession costs. Finish by naming the single revision that would resolve the most at once. Please lead with the ranking and keep the whole reply under 450 words.
