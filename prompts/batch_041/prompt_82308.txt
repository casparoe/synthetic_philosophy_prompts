Random question from someone who reads too much pop science and no philosophy: is there a name for this? Every year or so I read another round of whether viruses are alive, whether prions count, whether a mule counts (living parents, no offspring), whether a seed someone got to sprout after two thousand years counts, whether those giant viruses count. What gets me is that in these arguments the two sides agree on every fact. Nobody disputes that the virus can't do anything on its own, that the mule is sterile, that the seed sat inert for two millennia. They just disagree about the verdict. And the definitions never settle it: someone offers a list -- metabolism, reproduction, evolution, a membrane -- a case shows up with most but not all of them, and instead of saying well then it isn't alive, they either say it's close enough to the paradigm or they bolt on a clause, and the clause is almost always about where the thing came from rather than what it does now, something like "or came from something that does". The list grows, the new clause has borderline cases of its own, and nobody treats any of that as a problem. I've seen the same shape in arguments about when someone counts as dead, so i dont think it's just biology.

I'm not after a definition of life. I want to know what to call the thing happening in these arguments, and how it differs from just saying the word is vague -- is it the same as a word that's vague because we don't know enough yet? If there are a few nearby ideas, which one actually fits, what do the others get wrong, and where does the fitting one stop: is bolting on a clause ever the right move rather than a dodge? A bare label won't help me.
