I write a science-and-ideas column, plus a little satirical newsletter on the side, and for the next issue I want a piece set in a world where the "accuracy-first" program in epistemology actually got picked up by institutions -- banks, insurers, HR departments -- instead of staying in the journals. The conceit I've landed on is a straight-faced consumer product review of a personal-finance app, let's call it CalibraScore, which rates you on how calibrated and refined your credences are, hands you a "Brier rating" the way a credit bureau hands you a FICO score, and then sells you products off the back of it. I want the humor to come entirely from getting the technical stuff right -- proper scoring rules, why calibration by itself is cheap (you can be perfectly calibrated and still useless), the calibration/refinement split, the immodesty requirement, the dominance arguments that are supposed to pin your credences down -- so that somebody who works on scoring rules laughs instead of wincing. Deadpan the whole way, no winking, around 700-800 words, formatted like an ordinary review: a star rating, a "who it's for" section, the works.

Three things I'd like it to smuggle in without ever stating them outright: first, that if accuracy really is the only thing that matters epistemically, then this app is the honest institutional form of that view, not a betrayal of it; second, that the app's own rating rule quietly isn't proper -- it rewards confident people -- which is exactly the sin the theory is built to forbid; and third, that the single "score" has to pretend there's one right way to trade off calibration against refinement, when the theory itself only ever gives you a partial order. Also, can you make the reviewer a sympathetic user who genuinely likes the app and is annoyed that the details keep bugging him? That seems funnier than a hatchet job.

Don't name any living researchers, don't put real citations or further-reading lists in it, and don't let it drift into being an essay about the view -- it has to stay a review the whole way through.
