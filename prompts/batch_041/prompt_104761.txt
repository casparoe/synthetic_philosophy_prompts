For several years a colleague of mine at a state secondary school has run a free Saturday tutoring programme for pupils from the nearby estate — four hours every week, through the holidays, with no extra pay and nothing about it in her contract. She is now unwell and has asked the rest of the science department to cover for a term. Two of my colleagues disagree sharply about what this means for the rest of us. One says we plainly ought to help: the children would lose out, and each of us could easily spare a couple of hours a week. The other says that what she did was far beyond anything anyone had a right to demand of her, and that this is exactly why it cannot now become a demand on the rest of us — otherwise she has, in effect, unilaterally rewritten all our obligations. I feel the force of both, and also a nagging worry I cannot put into words: we admire her precisely because she did more than she had to, yet if the rest of us decline, we are surely letting the children down. Was she doing something she was required to do, or not? And once that is settled, does it really settle what the rest of us owe — or is my sense that 'both cannot be right' itself the mistake?
