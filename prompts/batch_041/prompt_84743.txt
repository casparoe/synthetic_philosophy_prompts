hey — I have to present my own take on van Fraassen's reflection principle at a seminar next week, and before I do I want a consistency check on the pile of stuff I actually believe about it. Here's the pile:

(1) Reflection itself: if I know that at some later time I'll be at credence x about P, then I should be at x already now. Same evidence, same credence — anything else looks like planning to change my mind for a reason I can't state now.

(2) I'm a fallibilist about my own future reasoning. More evidence usually helps, but I dont think there's any general presumption that future me is the wiser one. I could get worse at this.

(3) But when I know the cause of a future shift is something I'd now call noise, I don't have to slide toward it. I'm doing a ten-day silent retreat next month and my freinds who've done it all came out more confident in whatever they walked in believing. Or the pill case everyone uses — take a pill you know makes people credulous and you'll come out at 90% on something you're now 50/50 on. I don't think Reflection binds me in those cases. It needs an escape clause for known head-messing.

(4) What actually sells me on Reflection is the money pump, the Dutch book arguement van Fraassen gives: if future me will bet at odds I now think are wrong, someone can arrange to bleed me dry, and I'd rather not be that guy. That's what makes it feel like a requirement instead of a nice-to-have.

(5) I also don't think post-pill me is making a mistake. His credences are fine given what he's working with. The mistake is all mine for taking the pill. Irrationality is time-slice-relative, basically.

(6) Deference to my future self and deference to expert consensus are the same shape for me: go along with it unless you have specific reason to think the process generating it is compromised. I lean on that move in a bunch of other arguments, so I'd rather not lose it.

So: which of these are actually pulling against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix for each real clash? Rank the real ones by how bad they are, say what has to give in each, and tell me if the fix for one of them quietly costs me something elsewhere on the list. I don't want a lecture on the literature, I want the audit — and please don't just tell me every pair is in tension. Some of these are fine together and I want to know which.
