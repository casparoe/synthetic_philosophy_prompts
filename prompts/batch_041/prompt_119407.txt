Say Leibniz got a peek at todays panpsychism debates. No need for names — just the setup: everything has some inner experience, and the hard part is how a bunch of tiny experiences add up into one big unified experience. What's his verdict? Put this case to him: a worm where each segment has a faint experience of its own, and once the segments get wired into the right feedback loop the whole worm reports one single unified experience; then the worm gets cut in half and each half reports its own unified experience, then the halves get reconnected. Would he say a new subject popped into being at the first step and died at the cut? Or does he just deny that any real combining ever happens — the worm's experience being one monad's representation of an aggregate rather than a new monad? Is he even willing to call the worm a substance, or only an appearance grounded in facts about the parts? And does his own mill argument (you could walk around inside the machinery and never see a thought) create trouble for him, since he wants simples yet still lets a dominant monad unify a whole body? Give me his answer in his voice, and flag where his commitments would embarass the panpsychists and where they'd embarass him.
