I write about science and ideas — long features, mostly, for a general but reasonably smart readership — and I'm working on a piece about the causal revolution. You know the shape of it: Pearl's structural-equations machinery and the do-operator, the interventionist idea that a cause is just the thing that makes the right kind of difference when you intervene, and then the last few years where everyone in machine learning decided to learn causal graphs from data and to argue about whether LLMs are doing anything causal at all or just parroting the vocabulary.

Here's the gimmick I want to try. David Lewis has been dead for a bit over twenty years — he went just as this whole thing was getting going — and he's still the guy every causal-modelling paper has to nod to or push against, because his whole story was that causation is counterfactual dependence applied along a chain, and the modellers mostly keep the counterfactuals but rebuild them out of equations and interventions instead of possible worlds. So I want you to drop him into a room in the present day and tell me what he'd actually make of it.

I dont want a survey of the literature — I want your best guess at his views, and I want them broken down rather than blended:

- Does he read the structural-equation definitions of actual causation (the ones that keep his preemption cases, late preemption and trumping and all that, and try to fix them by fiddling with the equations) as his own view in different notation, as a genuine rival, or as a notational shuffle that changes nothing?
- Does he stay a purist about counterfactual dependence, or does he drift further in the direction he was already going at the end, where he'd started talking about one event influencing another and about how fragile events are, rather than about dependence?
- Would he even care about the machine-learning side — causal discovery, learning causal representations, the claim that a language model which talks about causes beautifully is doing nothing but parroting — or does he wave it off as engineering and not philosophy?
- And the one I actually care about: would he be annoyed, flattered, or unmoved that his problem cases are now handled by tweaking a definition rather than by rethinking what causation is?

Then be straight with me about the seams. Which of those predictions would you actually bet on, which are you dressing up as predictions because you can't just say who knows, and where am I most likely to embarass myself if I print this. Casual is fine — I'm not going to quote you.
