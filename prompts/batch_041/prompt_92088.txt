My department's curriculum committee got into a fight last week and I've been roped into writing up who was right, which is how I ended up here. The fight was about whether we keep organizing the first-year survey as Medieval → Renaissance → Scientific Revolution, or drop those units and go thematic instead.

Maya opened by saying the whole scheme is a later invention: 'medieval' started out as an insult thrown by people who wanted to claim they'd surpassed it, and 'the Renaissance' and 'the Scientific Revolution' were more or less coined afterwards and then read back into the sources. So the units teach a rupture story the evidence doesn't support, and we'd do better to organize by themes and let any breaks show up where they actually are.

Ravi said fine, all periodization is invented, thats what periodizing is — 'the nineteenth century' is also an invention and he's not giving up centuries. Periods are filing systems, and you judge a filing system by whether it helps you find things, not by whether it names a natural kind. And something did change — printing, new reading publics, Greek texts coming back west, painting doing new things with space — so a unit on that isn't a lie.

Maya: but 'the Renaissance' isn't a neutral drawer label, it comes with a built-in claim that this was a rebirth after a dead stretch, and that claim does work — anything that looks continuous with what came before gets demoted to a 'precursor.' You can't borrow the word and refuse its meaning. And the geography is an Italy story; it fits Byzantium and the Islamic world badly, if at all.

Ravi: that's an argument for teaching it as one regional story among others, not for throwing the drawer out. And if we drop the labels, students lose the handle they need in order to even ask whether there was a break.

Maya: then call it 'the long fourteenth to seventeenth centuries,' drop the rebirth language, and see how many of your 'facts' about it survive the renaming.

Neither would budge and now the commitee wants a ruling. Can you go through it point by point and say who's right at each step? What I'd most like is for you to pull apart what they're actually disagreeing about — I think there's a difference between (a) whether change happened at all, (b) whether it was concentrated in a short window, (c) whether the people living through it thought of themselves as new, (d) whether it was one place or many, and (e) whether the label is useful for a given purpose — and that they keep sliding between these. Say which one each move is really about, flag any factual or conceptual claim either of them is smuggling in undefended, and tell me who's more right overall.
