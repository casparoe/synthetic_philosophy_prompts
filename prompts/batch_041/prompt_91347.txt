I've been reading about how different places handle surrogacy and egg donation, and the pattern looks strange to me. In a lot of Europe surrogacy is either banned outright or legally unenforceable, and where it is allowed, like in the UK, the surrogate is supposed to get her expenses covered and nothing more. Egg donation goes the same way: altruistic only, and in some countries (Germany, I think) not allowed at all. In the US, meanwhile, there's a full-blown market, with agencies, contracts, surrogates paid a fee on top of expenses, egg donors getting paid thousands, and donor profiles advertising height, college, and test scores.

What does this split presuppose about persons, bodies, or parenthood? Does the European approach assume some things just shouldn't be bought and sold even when everyone involved agrees, and if so, what makes reproduction different from the other risky things people get paid to do? Or does it assume women can't really consent when money is on the table? Is the American side committed to the opposite view, that these are simply services and autonomy settles it? Are the bans internally consistent, given that the same countries let their citizens travel abroad for the same procedures, or allow the donation while forbidding payment for it? And how much of this is really forced by the practice rather than being one option among several, say an anti-exploitation view that wants more support for the surrogate instead of prohibition?

Tell me where the practice is philosophically underdetermined. Write in American English, and keep it focused on the important issues.
