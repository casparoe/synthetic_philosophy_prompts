I'm giving a paper at a small interdisciplinary workshop next month and I want a Q&A prep sheet rather than a defense. My thesis is that hyperbolic discounting is neither necessary nor sufficient for akrasia. What the discounting literature calls preference reversal is, in most of the standard cases, just the later self having a different preference, and a different preference is not yet a violation of the agent's own judgment about what to do. So I introduce a distinction between synchronic and diachronic akrasia and propose an endorsement test: a later choice counts as akratic only if the agent, at the moment of choosing, still endorses the judgment she is acting against — feels its authority, even as she goes the other way. On that test Ulysses at the mast is not akratic, and neither is someone who simply changed her mind about the cigarettes; the akratic case is the one where the judgment stays live and loses anyway. I also want to say something about why the naive/sophisticated distinction everyone uses is orthogonal to whether anything akratic is happening.

The room will be about fifteen people. Mostly behavioural economists who live inside the β–δ model and treat the preference-reversal data as the whole explanandum; two Aristotelian virtue ethicists from my own department who will want the practical syllogism, the akratic's clouded or half-present knowledge, and to ask wether my two time-slices count as a single agent at all; and one philosopher of action who will ask what the akratic intends.

Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way those people would actually phrase them, ordered by how much damage they do rather than how interesting they are, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is thin. Tell me what to concede up front, and finish with the one question I'm hoping nobody asks.
