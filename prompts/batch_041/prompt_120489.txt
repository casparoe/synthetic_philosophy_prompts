For years I have been arguing with a friend about what is really there in the world, and I have taken to writing down her rulings. The whirlpool that forms when a bathtub drains: real. The center of mass of the solar system: real. The average household of the statistician's tables, with its fractional children and cars that nobody owns: not real. The border between two countries: real. The national mood as reported by a polling firm: not real. An electron: real. When I pressed her about the rules of chess she changed the subject.

She keeps a second list as well, of when it is legitimate to say that something believes or wants something. A dog that scratches at the door wants to go out. A chess engine that plays brilliantly has no beliefs and merely computes. A corporation can believe things and can be blamed. A market cannot, so talk of a market fearing a rate hike is metaphor rather than description. A bacterium wants nothing. She insists the two lists are really one list, which I doubt.

Next month she will be asked about two new cases, and I want to place my bet before she rules. The first is the unemployment rate. The second is the exact arrangement of craters on one particular asteroid: every pit in its precise position, with no description of the arrangement shorter than the list of pits.

Tell me what she will say about each and, more to the point, give me your best guess at the rule she is actually running, with your reasons. I can see at least two candidates. One is that a pattern is real when describing the system in terms of it is a genuine compression of the data and supports counterfactuals; the other is that it is real when it does not depend on anyone's interest or convention, though her verdict on the border suggests that this second candidate needs patching, and I would like to know which patch. There may be further candidates, including one that covers the second list as well. For each rule you consider, say what it predicts for the unemployment rate and for the crater field, which of those two you would stake more on, and whether any single rule can genuinely cover both of her lists or whether she is quietly running two. Then name the further case that would actually separate the surviving rules rather than being absorbed by all of them, and tell me where your reasoning is likeliest to be wrong and what would change your mind about which rule she is running.
