I need help settling an argument that has somehow taken over my house. I live in a housing co-op — 28 adults and a handful of kids in one big creaky building — and we vote next week on whether to keep our internal economy. Right now everything runs on "beads": everyone gets 200 a month, chores have posted prices (cleaning two bathrooms earns you enough to buy someone else's cooking shift), you pay a fee to book the common room, and if you hate doing your share you pay a neighbour who doesn't mind doing it. A group of newer members wants to scrap the whole thing. Their pitch: the beads are Monopoly money, they only circulate among us, they reward whoever happens to be good at the chores they don't mind doing, and they've turned the laundry rota into a tiny stock exchange. Instead, a rotating planning committee would look at what needs doing each month, assign it, and also decide how we spend the house's actual money — the boiler, the garden, the food budget.

What I cant sort out is the theoretical fight underneath it. My friend Priya says the plan is a fantasy: she says economists settled this a century ago — once you abolish prices for the things you produce, you have no way of knowing what anything is worth relative to anything else, so you literally can't tell a good plan from a bad one, and a committee ends up either flying blind or just guessing. My friend Marco says that's ancient history, and that a central board can post provisional prices and then keep nudging them up or down until what people want matches what's around, more or less the way a market does it — and anyway we have laptops now, which the people who made Priya's argument didn't. Marco also keeps pointing out that our beads aren't real prices in the first place: the board sets them by vote, half of them haven't moved in three years, and nobody can spend their beads anywhere outside the building.

I'd like to actually understand this before I vote, instead of siding with whoever I like more at dinner. Could you take the role of someone who knows this debate inside out — part economist, part philosopher — and think it through carefully before answering? Start by brainstorming what's really at stake on each side, then tell me which parts of Priya's and Marco's arguments survive scrutiny and which don't. Then bring it home to us: does it matter that our prices are fake, set by vote, and non-transferable, and does it matter that we still have to buy our heating oil and groceries from the outside world? I want the sharp version of the disagreement, not a both-sides summary. And please don't just list off famous book titles or names for me to google later — I have no internet tonight, I want the arguments themselves.
