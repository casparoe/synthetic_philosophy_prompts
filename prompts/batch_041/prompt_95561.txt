Ok, I'm workshopping this in a seminar on collective responsibility and I keep running into the same move from a colleague of mine — call her Dana. Her line is that a state apologising for a historical wrong is just the ordinary case of one person apologising to another, scaled up. The state, on her view, is a single moral agent that persists through time the same way you and I do; the citizens alive now aren't a different person from the citizens who did the wrong, they're the same agent at a later timestamp. So "it wasn't us" is exactly as good a dodge as me saying "the guy who broke your window was 2019-me." And she rides that to a pretty strong conclusion: same agent, therefore a real apology is owed and real amends are owed, and the passage of generations doesnt dilute the debt one bit.

I'm suspicious but I can't say why. Can you do the audit? Give me the correspondence — who or what plays the wrongdoer, the victim, the remorse, the amends, the memory — a table is fine if that's easier. Then where the mapping actually breaks, and split those into the ones that matter for her argument and the ones that are just noise. Press on the ones that only look like differences until you find out whether they are: does the state "remember" the wrong in anything like the sense a person does, or is it more that an archive remembers on its behalf? Does it have to feel anything for the apology to be sincere, or is the sincerity requirement itself just a leftover from the person case? Who's actually being addressed, given that the victims are gone? And what's the analogy quietly smuggling in that the state case doesn't have?

Then the payoff: does her conclusion survive once the breaks are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it the whole time? If it was — is there a better one? I floated something at her that she hated: that the state isn't like a person but like a firm, which has no feelings, survives complete turnover of its staff, and yet we hold it to its contracts and its apologies without blinking. Is that any better, or does it just relocate the problem? And be honest with me about which parts of this are the standard collective-responsibility stuff and which bits are actually relevent.
