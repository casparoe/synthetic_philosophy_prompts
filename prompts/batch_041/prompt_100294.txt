His line is basically: iterated amplification is just division of labor, and we already know division of labor works. So if you start with an overseer you trust, have it break a task into subtasks, hand those to copies of itself, and distill the results back into one model, you should end up with something both more competent and still aligned, because the whole thing is just a firm, scaled up. He leans on this to argue we shouldn't worry so much about decomposition -- competence and alignment both ride through on their own.
