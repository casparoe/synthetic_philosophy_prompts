Here's the idea. The fight over whether pure time preference is rational seems to me to be going in circles, and I think the reason is that "pure time preference" is being used for at least three seperate things. First, a preference about the *location* of a good — wanting the pleasure now rather than later, same pleasure either way. Second, a preference about the *shape* of a stream of goods — wanting things to improve rather than decline, wanting a life that gets better, which is not obviously a preference for earlier over later at all. Third, a kind of partiality toward the present self — weighting my current self's claim more heavily than my future self's just because it's current. My hunch is that only the third is "pure time preference" in the sense the irrationality charge is aimed at, and that the first two are either harmless or actually defensible, which would make the standard charge land on a much narrower target than its proponents think. But I'm not sure the narrower target even survives — maybe once you strip away shape and location, there's nothing left that's recognizable as a preference, and the whole notion dissolves.
