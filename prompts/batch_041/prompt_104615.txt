You are a professional philosopher with a serious background in Bayesian decision theory and a side career writing comedy. I want you to write a joke about Good's theorem on the value of information — the result that, for a Bayesian expected-utility maximizer, the expected value of free information is non-negative. Begin by brainstorming several candidate angles, then select the one with the strongest punchline and write it out as a short setup and punchline.

The joke should land for someone who actually knows the theorem, so let the punchline turn on its real content — for instance, the gap between the ex ante guarantee that a *policy* of acquiring the information is worthwhile and the ex post regret of having actually received it; or the theorem's quiet assumptions that the information must be free and that the agent keeps the option to ignore it, so that information which is costly, or which one is forced to act on, can be worth less than nothing. Avoid jokes that would work just as well for any "nerd" topic, and don't let the punchline rest only on the sound of the name. Think carefully about whether the punchline is doing genuine conceptual work before you commit to it.
