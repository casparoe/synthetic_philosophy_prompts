I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of mind assembling materials for a workshop, and I need a document rather than an essay. I have invited a heterophenomenologist, who holds that a science of consciousness should treat subjects' reports as data about what they believe while suspending judgment on whether those reports reveal anything about experience, and a first-person methodologist, who holds that experience itself is the explanandum and cannot be suspended in this way. They have agreed to co-sign a joint statement, and I would like you to draft it. Begin with a numbered section of claims both can endorse: what the primary data are, what the method does and does not purport to establish, and which of the usual disputes are empirical rather than conceptual. Then give a table of the residual disagreements, each stated in wording its own side would accept. I would like the statement to test a framing of my own, which may be mistaken: I suspect the real disagreement concerns not whether experience exists but what inferential license a report gives for a claim about experience, and that treating reports as testimony, defeasible only by specified defeaters, rather than as either fictional posits or theory-neutral observations, relocates the dispute. Identify the cruxes under that framing — I expect one about how to distinguish a subject who misreports from one whose experience is nonstandard, and one about whether a reporting criterion can be validated without circularity — and for each supply a concrete case both sides would accept as bearing on it, such as blindsight or two subjects who disagree about an ambiguous color boundary, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Where you judge a disagreement to be verbal, say so and give the reformulation that dissolves it. Omit citations; I will add them.
