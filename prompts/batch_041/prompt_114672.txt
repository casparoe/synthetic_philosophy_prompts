I'm on the audience standards panel at a public broadcaster, and I have to draft our written decision before Thursdays meeting. The case is a four-part drama, already shot and scheduled for March, about a real woman who ran the hospital in a mining town around 1900 — harsh with her staff, cold to her own children, died alone, all of that is on the record. The drama invents a long affair for her and a final act of atonement at a dying miner's bedside. It also takes a real pit disaster in which eleven men died, moves it a year earlier, and hands her the discovery of it; the person who actually raised the alarm was the mine's engineer, and we've cut him. Our historical consultant quit in the edit and has written to us saying we've defamed a woman who can't sue. Her great-granddaughter's lawyer says the dead still have interests and that we've put words in her ancestor's mouth; the engineer's great-grandson wants the scene reshot rather than captioned; and the family of a nurse our lead character sacks on screen is delighted, because the drama shows the sacking was unjust. Legal says the dead have no standing and that we're covered by a "loosely inspired by" disclaimer, which I think is thin given we use her real name, her real town and her photograph in the titles. Our own code says drama must not "materially mislead" — about what, and whom, nobody has ever said. Give me the panel's opinion: what we recommend, what the production has to do by when, and the honest best case for the side I'm inclined to rule against. I don't want a survey of the literature, I want something the panel can vote on.
