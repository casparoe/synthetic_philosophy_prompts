And here's what the student wrote:

"(i) The looping effect is Hacking's name for the way a classification can change the people who fall under it, which then forces the classification itself to change. It's a feedback loop rather than one-way labelling. His stock example is multiple personality disorder (now dissociative identity disorder): once the diagnosis was available and publicised, patients started presenting with the symptoms the diagnosis described — more alters, different alters — so the profile of the disorder shifted and the diagnostic criteria had to be rewritten. This is really just Hacking's version of the old dictum that if people define a situation as real, it becomes real in its consequences. The category doesn't just describe; it helps produce the behaviour it describes.

(ii) This is not the same as saying the illness is fake. Hacking is careful to separate making up people from the idea that the people are made up in the sense of not existing. A looping kind is real — it's just that its reality is partly constituted by the classification and by people's uptake of it. That's why he calls these interactive kinds: the classified respond to being classified, unlike indifferent kinds such as quarks or chemical elements, which don't care how we describe them. So the looping effect is a point about the ontology of the category, not a debunking of the suffering.

(iii) Since interactive kinds are exactly the kinds that get looped, and psychiatric categories are interactive, it follows that they aren't natural kinds, and Hacking concludes that psychiatry therefore can't be a science in the way physics is. The whole point of the essay is that the human sciences deal with moving targets, so any classification they produce will be historically local and will eventually dissolve as the loop runs its course."
