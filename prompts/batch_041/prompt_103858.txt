Ok so I just spent two months of my life agonizing over whether to take a funded PhD offer on the other side of the country or stay home and help my mum while my dad's recoving from surgery, and the whole time alot of people kept asking me which one was better and I kept saying they're not even on the same scale. Then I realized I have literally no idea what I mean by that, and neither does anyone I argue with. So I want to build a little decision tree (or quiz, whatever works) that sorts people, starting with me, by what they actually think is happening in a hard choice like mine.

Here's what I want sitting at the leaves, because these are the views I keep sliding between without noticing:

- the two options genuinely can't be ranked: there's no fact of the matter about which is better, and that's not me being confused or ignorant, it's just true (a real gap, not a blind spot);
- they can be ranked but only roughly: it's definite that neither beats the other, yet they're not exactly equal either, so there's still a reason to pick one over the other;
- they're exactly equal, so flipping a coin is as good as deliberating;
- there is a right answer, I just can't see it (ignorance, not metaphysics);
- there's really only one scale — call it well-being, or utility, whatever — and 'incomparable' is just a word for 'I haven't done the math yet';
- the act of choosing is what makes one option better: before I commit there's no fact, and my commitment creates the value;
- 'which is better' is the wrong question; what I should be asking is which rule or policy I want to live by.

Every branch node has to be a concrete question about a case, not 'do you believe in incomparability?' — I want it to catch what people say about cases, not which label they like. Use the diagnostic cases, like the one where I say A and B are neither better nor worse, and then someone improves A by a hair and suddenly I'll say A is clearly better (I'm told that's supposed to show they were comparable all along, so not a real gap), versus the version where the tiny improvement changes nothing at all. I want questions that force people to commit on stuff like that.

Also: flag which leaves the tree can't actually tell apart and why, and note any combination of answers that nobody seems to hold, with a guess at whether that's because the view is incoherent or just nobody's bothered with it.

Aim for 8-10 branch questions. Don't worry about naming philosophers or citing anything, I just want the thing to work on me.
