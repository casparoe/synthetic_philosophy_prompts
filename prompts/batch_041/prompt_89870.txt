I've been going through Carroll's tortoise-and-Achilles bit with a couple of people from a reading group — you know the one, the tortoise accepts P, accepts "if P then Q," but won't accept Q until you add the conditional as a further premise, and then won't apply that premise without yet another one, forever. A friend of mine (call her R) thinks the whole thing is a category error: she says the tortoise is treating a rule of inference as if it were just another premise, and that "Q follows from P and P→Q" was never a third fact you had to believe — it's the license to move from the first two to the third, so demanding a premise that justifies it is like demanding a proof that proofs work. On her view the regress only gets going if you grant the tortoise that rules are premises; refuse that and it never starts. I'm not so sure — it feels like she's just relabelled the problem, because now the tortoise can ask what justifies adopting the rule, and if the answer is "nothing, it's constitutive" that sounds like the regress went underground rather than away.
