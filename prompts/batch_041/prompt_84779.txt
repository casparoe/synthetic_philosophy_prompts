My brother and I had our annual Thanksgiving blowup about moral progress — he thinks the whole expanding circle story is just a nicer name for one generation's taste beating the next generation's, and my best counter was the usual slavery-to-suffrage-to-animal-welfare recital, which he's heard and has canned replies to. So I want a case that actually does some work, and I want you to build it to order.

The spec: a thought experiment, one paragraph, no sci-fi. No aliens, no uplifted octopuses, no hypothetical neuroscience, no machine that reveals hidden minds. Ordinary human community, ordinary amount of time passing.

Here's the trick I'm after: the circle doesn't get any bigger. Beginning and end, the community counts exactly the same beings as morally considerable — nobody added, nobody dropped, same creatures, same number. And yet something happens in between that anyone who beleives in moral progress has to call progress, and that my brother can't wave off as a change in taste.

Constraints, because I know how these go wrong. (1) Not intensity — they don't come to care more or less; the strength of concern is fixed. (2) Not information — same empirical beliefs about the beings at the start and at the end, nobody discovers the cows can do arithmetic. (3) Not sincerity — nobody was faking it in either version, both communities mean it. (4) Nothing that lets you smuggle in a widening: no new beings arriving, no old beings dying off, no discovering that two groups were secretly the same kind. And please dont lean on slaves-then-women-then-animals; if you use one of the classic cases it has to be doing something new.

Then the post-mortem. Say in a sentence or two what actually changed, since I suspect it's about the shape of the rule rather than its scope but I don't want to lead you. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens if I pull that feature out — does it just collapse back into a boring widening story? Then give me a stress test: a new being shows up after the story ends, and say whether the two versions of the community would part ways over it. If they do, tell me straight whether that means my same-size case was a widening case in disguise. And finally, play my brother for me — what's his best move to insist the circle did widen after all, just along an axis I didn't notice, and does that move work, or is it a redescription dressed up as a discovery?
