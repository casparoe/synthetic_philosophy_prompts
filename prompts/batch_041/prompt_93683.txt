Prepping notes for a workshop and I want to catch myself out before someone else does. Here's my package on time and direction, roughly in order of how confident I am:

(1) the fundamental laws are time-reversal symmetric, so any arrow has to come from somewhere other than the dynamics; (2) the arrow comes from thermodynamics — the second law is a statistical regularity underwritten by a very special low-entropy initial condition (people call it the past hypothesis); (3) I want to say that initial condition is itself a law of nature, not just one more gigantic accident about how the world happens to start; (4) causation is objective and directed — the cause really does bring the effect about, and not the reverse; it's not just us projecting our habit of inferring effects from causes onto the world; (5) I'm a Humean about laws — laws are just the best systematization of the total mosaic of particular facts, and there are no necessary connections out there over and above the regularities; (6) a verdict I'm fond of: if some pocket of the universe had its entropy gradient running the other way, there'd be no fact about whether it was going forward or backward relative to us — its future would just be our past, neither of us would be the one getting it right, and I think the same goes for a brief fluctuation that runs backwards inside our own region.
