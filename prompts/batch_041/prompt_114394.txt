I am a retired natural scientist — I spent thirty-five years in atmospheric chemistry — who has, since retiring, been sitting in on a graduate seminar in philosophy and economics. The convenor has asked me to set the final take-home essay because I kept pressing the students to say what a model actually represents rather than reciting its standard interpretation. The unit we have just finished is on the dollar auction and escalation of commitment, and I want a question that cannot be answered by repeating the familiar two-bidder story. Because the students will not have web access, the question must be self-contained; please avoid requiring citations, reading lists, or references to specific authors or papers.

Please write one essay question suitable for a 90-minute take-home, together with a model answer of about 800 words and a marking scheme worth 20 marks. The question should be built around the following variant. A dollar auction is held for a prize of £100. Bids are in £1 increments. The highest bidder pays their bid and receives the prize. The second-highest bidder also pays their bid, but the auctioneer places that payment in escrow. If the winning bid is £100 or more, the second-highest bidder’s escrow is returned in full; if the winning bid is less than £100, the escrow is donated to a charity that both bidders have publicly declared they oppose. Before bidding, each bidder may sign a binding declaration that they will not bid above a stated ceiling; if they later bid above their declared ceiling, the bid is void and they must pay a penalty of £20 to the other bidder. The declaration is private until the auction ends. There are two bidders, both risk-neutral expected-money maximisers, and both know all of the above. The prize is worth exactly £100 to each, and there is no resale value. The question should ask students to analyse whether the escrow rule plus the possibility of a binding ceiling eliminates the escalation trap for fully rational bidders; if so, at what bid the auction ends and what role the penalty plays; if not, what the equilibrium path is and why. It should then ask them to contrast that rational-choice analysis with a behavioural analysis in which bidders are susceptible to sunk-cost reasoning and to a strong desire not to be the one who funds the opposed charity. Finally, it should ask what the comparison shows about the claim that the dollar auction demonstrates the irrationality of escalation, rather than merely modelling a game with a bad equilibrium.

For the model answer, please give the reasoning a strong student should produce, not just the conclusion. For the marking scheme, build it around five or six must-hit points, assign marks to each so that the total is 20, and for each point add a one-line note on the mistake that typically costs students marks. Please make the scheme separate a first-class answer from a merely competent one, in particular by requiring the student to say whether the ceiling is doing any independent work once the escrow rule is in place. At the end, state how confident you are in the model answer and in the marking scheme, and single out the point you are least sure about.
