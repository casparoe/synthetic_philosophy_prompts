I have no background in philosophy, but I have spent a long while working through, on my own, the question of what actually separates science from pseudoscience, and why so much of the answer seems to come down to the demand that a claim be the sort of thing that could, in principle, be shown to be false. I would like to give a friend, who knows this material far better than I do, a joke that genuinely earns its laugh from the content of that idea rather than from the general observation that scientists can be smug. The picture I keep returning to is of the falsifiability criterion as a doorman at a club called Science, working his way along a queue of applicants. I would like the joke to turn on his being a bad doorman: admitting people he ought to turn away and refusing people he ought to let in, and doing both for reasons that someone who knows the debate would accept as fair rather than arbitrary. At least one applicant should be genuinely hard to place, so that the joke cannot be carried by the stock examples alone, and I would be delighted if the ending were something other than the familiar gag that the criterion cannot pass its own test. Please write the joke, and keep it short enough to tell aloud.
