For a graduate reading group on anthropic reasoning, please produce a one-page decision tree that leads a reader from a willingness to treat their own position among observers as a random draw to a named position on the doomsday argument. Construct it from the logical space rather than from a list of authors, and phrase each internal node as a question the reader can answer without knowing the labels, anchored where possible in a concrete case: whether learning that the human species will survive for a very long time should by itself count as evidence against your being an early member of it; whether a coin that creates either one person or a great many, together with your existence as one of the created, shifts the odds toward the many; whether the reference class is persons, person-moments, or all observers anywhere in the universe; whether the prior over the total number of observers is uniform, logarithmic, or simply unavailable; and whether a verdict here must be consistent with one's verdicts on Sleeping Beauty, on fine-tuning, and on the same argument run against the length of a single life rather than the species. The leaves should be the positions themselves — the self-sampling reading that accepts the doomsday conclusion, the self-indication reading on which the argument cancels, the view that indexical uncertainty is a confusion and the puzzle dissolves, the view that the reference class problem leaves the argument vacuous, and the view that it is valid only relative to a prior no one can defend — each carrying one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Note any combination of answers that no one occupies, saying briefly whether it is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate. Keep it to six internal nodes or fewer, and do not cite literature or attribute views to named authors unless you are confident of the attribution.
