I run a small rationalist/EA meetup thing — 12ish people, mixed crowd (a couple of vegans, a few ex-vegans, a few who've never thought about food ethics, one person who works in ag policy), 90 minutes in a pub back room. I want a session on wild animal suffering that doesn't turn into the usual lecture, and I've been kicking around a mechanic. Call it the death ledger. Each table gets a deck of food cards, one card per way of getting 1000 kcal (pasture beef, feedlot beef, chicken, eggs, wheat, soy, whatever) and each card has two numbers: hectares of cropland per 1000 kcal and hectares of pasture per 1000 kcal. Tables plan one person's week off the cards and tally their land. Then I flip a second deck: a per-hectare wild-animal mortality card for each land type — cropland presumably kills alot of small stuff per hectare (mice, nests, birds, insects), pasture presumably kills less per hectare, though I have no idea by how much, and pasture beef takes an absurd amount of land per calorie. Total is cropland hectares times crop mortality plus pasture hectares times pasture mortality. What I want them to walk into is the moment their obviously-better card — the grass-fed one, the one the vegans in the room half-approve of — tops the death column, with cheap chicken somewhere in the contested middle and the wheat-and-soy plate not at zero either. Before I flip the second deck, each table writes down which card they think will win, so the reveal lands. I've been calling the underlying thing land-mediated death: harm that tracks how much land a food takes rather than how many animals you directly eat. Then a third flip, a card that just says nobody knows whether wild animals' lives are net positive or net negative, so nobody can say which direction the ledger should point — redo the ranking with the sign flipped and watch it scramble.

Build it out: rules, materials, the numbers to print on the cards, timing for 90 minutes, debrief. Don't hand me real figures you can't back up — instead tell me the inequality the card values have to satisfy for the inversion to appear at all, and what the session becomes if that inequality fails, because I'm genuinely not sure the inversion is real and I'd rather the thing be honest than punchy. No lecture up front; field mortality, wild animal suffering, replaceability and all the rest only get named in the debrief. And the debrief should be straight with me about what this setup can't show — I already know it makes deaths commensurable by fiat and treats one person's weekly basket as if it seperate from the food system around it.

Keep the prose inside the fields tight.
