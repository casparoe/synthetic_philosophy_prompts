I am taking a seminar on memory, identity, and ethics, and we have just finished the exchange between Avishai Margalit's The Ethics of Memory and David Rieff's In Praise of Forgetting. Margalit, as I understand him, grounds a duty to remember in what he calls thick ethical relations -- the ties of family, community, and nation that make another person's past genuinely mine to keep -- and treats the memory of radical evil as a burden that only such relations can be trusted to carry; Rieff argues instead that the modern cult of commemoration has turned memory into a way of keeping old grievances alive, so that forgetting is frequently the more humane and the more honest response. For my final essay I want to read Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind through this debate rather than through the philosophy-of-mind angle about personal identity that everyone reaches for. In the film, Joel and Clementine each hire a company to erase the other from memory after a painful breakup, and most of the story unfolds inside Joel's head as he races the erasure, trying to hide Clementine in recollections the procedure cannot reach.

What I want from you is a reading that does justice to both the film and the positions. Several things keep troubling me. The Margalit-Rieff argument is about the collective memory of atrocity -- the Holocaust, the Balkans, Northern Ireland -- whereas the film concerns two private people erasing a failed romance, so I cannot tell whether it simply changes the subject or whether the intimate case tests something the political case obscures. I would also like you to sort out what the film shows about the difference between choosing to forget and being unable to remember, and whether the procedure is better described as a right to be forgotten or as a wrong done to the self who consented to it. And there is an easy reading on offer in which the ending -- Joel and Clementine deciding to begin again in full knowledge of how it ends -- vindicates memory over forgetting; I would like you to press on whether the film earns that or whether it is genuinely ambivalent.

Where the film resists the Margalit-Rieff framing altogether, please say so plainly rather than forcing a fit, and where it dramatizes something the arguments alone miss, name that too. I would rather have an honest reading that concedes the mismatches than a tidy one that reduces the film to an illustration of a thesis.
