Years ago I came across the scenario in which a dead thinker's brain is kept working and goes on answering questions, together with the suggestion that a book containing the same answers would be no less remarkable. I want to push that scenario further and hear the case for a conclusion I resist. Take the book to be a machine rather than a stack of paper: a mechanism turns its pages in response to whatever I say, the pages in turn drive what it says back, so its parts really do push on one another. Take the input to be open-ended rather than a fixed list of questions, so the book has to carry, along with its answers, whatever is needed to find them -- an index made of further pages, read by the same mechanism. And take it that the book must also answer questions about the conversation so far, so the pages it opens later depend on the pages it opened earlier.

Please argue that a book of this kind would be conscious. Commit to whatever you think consciousness requires and defend that commitment rather than hedging about it. Give me the argument as exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no conclusion: the first point is the position itself and why it is not as absurd as it sounds, and the remaining four are the strongest objections, in the order they would actually be raised against you, each followed by your reply.
