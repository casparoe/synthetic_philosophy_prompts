I train reinforcement-learning agents, and I'm fluent in the standard apparatus: states, actions, policies, value functions, model-based planning, counterfactual rollouts, exploration, and the distinction between an agent that is merely stochastic and one whose behaviour is genuinely reason-responsive. I'd like the free-will debate — and in particular the family of views Daniel Dennett gathers in Elbow Room (1984) under the heading of the varieties of free will worth wanting — restated in that vocabulary as faithfully as the translation can bear.

Take the main positions one at a time — hard determinism (or hard incompatibilism), libertarian free will, and Dennett's compatibilism — and, if the vocabulary can carry it, the hierarchical or self-governance line that locates freedom not in the metaphysics of the will but in an agent's endorsement of its own desires. For each, give me the rendering in my terms: what is the agent, what is the policy, what is the environment, and what does the position's central claim become?

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Which claims cross over cleanly — for instance, is "could have done otherwise" simply the counterfactual sensitivity of a policy to its inputs, and is "acting freely" simply a policy's being responsive to reasons rather than hijacked? Which claims cross over only under a distortion you should flag explicitly — is a stochastic policy an adequate stand-in for libertarian indeterminacy, or is injected randomness the opposite of control? And which claims have no counterpart in the vocabulary at all — I am thinking especially of desert and moral responsibility, and of what "worth wanting" could mean when the framework supplies no notion of which objectives are the right ones to have. Where something drops out, tell me whether that is a gap in the RL vocabulary or a sign that the original claim was doing less work than it appeared to.

Keep the translation honest: don't let my framework quietly settle the original question by assuming, say, that any behaviour a policy produces is thereby free. State how confident you are in each part of the translation, and single out the one point you are least sure of.
