I'm building a diagnostic for a graduate seminar on causation by omission, and I want a decision tree that takes a reader from a few starting commitments to a named position on whether omissions can be causes. Build it out of the logical space rather than a list of authors: the first branch should separate those who think the question is whether there are negative events or absences among the causal relata at all from those who think the situation contains plenty of causes and the real question is how omission-talk selects one of them; further branches should separate plain counterfactual-dependence accounts, accounts that need a normality or default ordering over the variables, and accounts on which the causal claim is true only relative to a norm the agent was expected to meet. The leaves must include the eliminativist position (nothing is ever caused by an omission), the counterfactual view, the dependence-plus-normality view, the normative-relation view, and the contrastive or causal-selection view; give each its standard name where there is one, and mark any leaf whose label is contested or whose attribution you are unsure of. Phrase every node as a question about one of these cases, so that a reader can answer without knowing which leaf it leads to: the gardener who does not water; the parent who leaves a child in a hot car; the doctor who omits a test that would not have changed the outcome; the person who believes she pressed the button, did not, and the machine runs anyway; an institution that never built the levee, where the omitter is not an agent; and an omission-shaped case of preemption in which either of two failures would have sufficed. Note which combinations of answers are empty and, for each, whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate on these cases. Close by telling me which branch node you are least confident about, and what a single case would have to look like for you to place a position on a different leaf — that is, where this instrument is most likely to misclassify.
