I run a third-year seminar on rational choice and social ontology, 18 students, 90 minutes, and I want a game that makes them feel a multipolar trap from the inside -- the kind where nobody is cheating, everyone would rather be in the other outcome, and they end up in the bad one anyway. They should hit the wall themselves before anyone in the room says the word Moloch.

A few things it has to get right, because these are exactly what students flatten. It must not be a prisoner's dilemma: no dominant strategy, and the bad outcome should come from competition rather than from someone defecting. So make the payoff depend on relative standing -- each player wants to be ahead of the others, not merely well off -- which is why a shared target everyone agrees on doesn't dissolve the race. Second, include a round where they're free to talk, negotiate, and promise, and make that round fail anyway, so they can't walk away thinking the problem was just poor communication or bad vibes. And put most of the harm on people who aren't in the room, so the players can't simply feel the cost and stop.

Give me rules, materials, a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the structure. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about what the activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to say out loud myself.
