Theres a four-way sorting of minds I keep coming back to: Darwinian creatures that are just hardwired by natural selection, Skinnerian ones that learn by getting burned or rewarded for what they actually do out in the world, Popperian ones that can run the trial and error inside their own heads and kill the bad options off before they cost anything, and Gregorian ones that get their minds rebuilt by picking up thinking tools from other people. Now bolt on something the original picture didn't have: a cheap external simulator. Suppose I'm basically a Skinnerian creature with no inner critic worth the name, but I've got a box on my desk that will run any action I feed it a thousand times in a simulated world and hand me back the payoff, so I never have to risk the real thing. What are the most important implications of that setup -- for what I actually turn into, for where the line between the four grades now sits, and for whether being a Popperian creature is a fact about me at all anymore or just about the box I'm holding? Tell me which of the implications are load bearing and which are hype.
