I am a novelist at work on a book in which one of my characters, a comparative neuroanatomist with a taste for provocation, argues at a dinner party that moral standing tracks brain size. His analogy, which he returns to more than once, is that a brain is like an engine and intelligence is like horsepower: just as a larger-displacement engine can do more work, a larger brain can support a richer mental life, and so a creature with a larger brain has a greater claim on our moral consideration. He concedes that body size complicates the comparison, but he treats that as a technical correction rather than a threat to the analogy, and he waves off objections about whales and elephants by saying that the relevant measure is the encephalization quotient, or perhaps the absolute number of cortical neurons, depending on which way the argument is going. I want the novel to be intellectually honest, so before I put this speech in his mouth I need to know how much of it the analogy can actually carry.

Please lay out the correspondence explicitly — a table of correspondences is welcome — showing what plays the role of engine displacement, what plays the role of horsepower, what plays the role of the vehicle or the load, and what plays the role of the road, then identify where the mapping breaks: which features of engines and horsepower are quietly imported into the brain case, which comparisons (absolute size, relative size, encephalization quotient, neuron count, cortical neuron count, connectivity, glia) come apart from one another, and which of those differences matter for the moral question rather than merely for the biology. I am especially interested in whether the conclusion he wants — that brain size is morally relevant, and perhaps that it is the relevant measure of moral standing — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was doing all the work. If a better analogy exists for the moral point he is trying to make, I would like to see it, but I would also like to know what it would leave out. Throughout, please say how confident you are in the claims you make and single out the point you are least sure of.
