Write a Help Center FAQ of roughly 700 words, in the flat, relentlessly cheerful register of a real software vendor's support documentation, for a company that sells apologies, promises, and congratulations on demand — call it Sincero, Apology-as-a-Service — and whose actual value proposition is that none of its speech acts ever take effect. Treat this as a straight-faced reductio of the corporate non-apology and of deniability-preserving customer-relations language, and let the humour come from getting the felicity conditions right rather than from winking at the reader: apologies issued to the wrong audience or only after the injured party has gone, "apologies" whose content is in fact a prediction about the complainant's feelings, promises conditioned on the promiser's future preferences, thanks that presuppose a favour never received, and an optional Sincerity add-on whose terms quietly suspend the sincerity condition. Hold the corporate register throughout and keep the narrator serenely unaware that anything is amiss, so that a reader who knows Austin's distinction between misfires and abuses, the requirement of uptake, and Searle's notion of direction of fit can tell that the author understands the theory, while a reader who knows none of it still finds the escalation funny. Include at least one clause that is performative in form but constative in content, and close with a refund policy that turns on whether the customer can be said to have accepted the terms at all.
