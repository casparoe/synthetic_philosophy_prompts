I have read that intuitionistic logic rejects the law of excluded middle. But bivalence seems impossible to deny: Goldbach's conjecture is either true or false whether or not anyone has settled it, and the same goes for whether the decimal expansion of π contains a run of a hundred consecutive 7s. So I take it that intuitionists cannot seriously mean that such a statement lacks a truth value; they must merely be refusing, out of intellectual honesty, to assert what they cannot prove. On that reading, intuitionistic logic is just classical logic together with a rule against asserting a disjunction until one of its disjuncts has been established. Is that the right way to read the position — and if so, is the "different logic" nothing more than this added discipline of assertion?
