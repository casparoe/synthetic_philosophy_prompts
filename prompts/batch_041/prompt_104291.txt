Okay, so — I'm a science and ideas journalist, and I'm building an interactive flowchart for readers of the magazine I write for. Working title is "which theory of manipulation do you actually beleive?" The hook is that I've spent three months interviewing people about dark patterns, nudge-y app design, political messaging, all of it, and everyone has a rock-solid opinion about which things are manipulative, but the second you ask them *why*, they turn out to mean completely different things by the word and none of them notice.

So what I want from you is the actual instrument: a decision tree, about ten branching questions, ending in six or seven named positions on what makes influence over someone manipulative rather than legitimate persuasion. The thing I care about most: every node has to be a concrete case, or a yes/no about a case, phrased so a reader can answer it without knowing which position it leads to. No node that just asks "do you think autonomy matters?" or "is deception wrong?" — I want people sorted by their gut reactions to situations, not by which label they like the sound of.

Cases that have to be in there, because these are the ones that split my sources: the supermarket that puts the milk at the back of the store; a city mailing you a letter saying your neighbours used 20% less electricity last month; a bank app that plays a sad violin sting when you hover over the cancel button; a chatbot companion that remembers your mum's name and flatters you into staying subscribed; a friend who lies to you to get you to a surprise party; a surgeon describing the same odds two ways; a salesperson who asks whether you want the medium or the large; a casino comping drinks for someone it knows is a problem gambler; a canvasser who uses a true statistic and your own stated values to get you to a rally; a podcast host who sincerely cites only the studies that suit him; and a partner who keeps insisting you're misremembering an argument you're pretty sure happened.

The distinctions I want the tree to actually carve up, since these are the ones my sources talk past each other on: does it have to involve a false belief, or can influence be completely truthful and still manipulative? Does it have to be hidden, or can entirely open influence count? Does it work by bypassing someone's reasoning or by exploiting it? Is the wrong done to the target's autonomy, or is it really a claim about the influencer's character? Does it matter whether the target has some vulnerability or desire that's being leaned on? And where do flat threats and honest argument sit — are those just the two poles, or is there stuff in the middle that neither captures?

For each leaf, give me the name of the position (or "unnamed" if you genuinely can't find anyone who defends it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Then, at the end: tell me which of my leaves the questions can't actually pull apart and why, and which combinations of answers are empty — and for each empty one, your best guess whether that's because the view is incoherent or just because nobody's bothered. Audience is smart non-philosophers, so keep the register plain; I'll do the writing, you do the sorting.
