I recently became a volunteer moderator for the clinical-questions section of a nursing forum, and our worst problems are not obvious misinformation but answers that are true, or sincerely believed, or confidently stated by someone who was not in a position to know. I want a decision procedure my co-moderators and I can actually run on a reported post, grounded in the philosophical debate about whether assertion is governed by knowledge, truth, or belief, but usable by someone who has never read that literature. The forum already distinguishes clinical claims from experience reports; the procedure applies only to the former, and it should also say when a poster must retract a claim rather than merely edit it. Give me the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops with a clear leave-up or remove, and where it must hand the decision to a human because the remaining question is one of community values rather than fact. Then run it on three cases: a true clinical claim whose poster admits they were guessing; a false claim that is sincerely believed and carefully hedged; and a true, believed, confidently stated claim based entirely on one anonymous online comment. I expect the procedure to give me at least one result I will not like, and I want you to say plainly where it will misfire and what it cannot settle.
