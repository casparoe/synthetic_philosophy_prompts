I have been reading a popular essay on Rorty—the author is a respected generalist who writes about philosophy for a broad audience rather than a Rorty specialist—and I would like a careful audit of its central summary before I take it on board. Here is my paraphrase of the essay's account.

Antirepresentationalism, on this telling, is the thesis that the mind does not mirror nature: there is no such thing as a belief's accurately representing a mind-independent world, because truth is not a correspondence relation between sentences and facts but simply the honorific we confer on whatever our community currently accepts. It follows that science has no special claim to describe reality; it is one vocabulary among many, no closer to the way things are than poetry or politics. Since the world cannot tell us which of our vocabularies is correct, the choice among them comes down to what serves our purposes, and Rorty cheerfully concedes that this makes him a relativist—a relativist who thinks we should stop fretting about the label. His target is the Cartesian picture of a mind set over against a world it tries to copy, and his remedy is to replace epistemology with hermeneutics, conversation, and what he calls edifying philosophy. The essay then draws its political upshot: because there is no way things really are, liberalism cannot be defended as true, only as ours, and Rorty's politics therefore reduces to tribalism with a friendly face.

I am broadly sympathetic to the pragmatist project and do not want to dismiss this out of hand, but two things bother me—the readiness to call Rorty a relativist, and the suggestion that he puts science on all fours with poetry—and I suspect at least one of these worries rests on a conflation I cannot quite locate. Please go through the summary claim by claim. Which parts would a careful reader of Rorty accept as fair, which are the standard simplifications a popularizer is entitled to, and which are genuine misrepresentations of the position? Where the essay uses a term of art—representation, truth, relativism—I would like to know whether it is using it in Rorty's sense or in its everyday one. Finally, tell me whether the political conclusion the essay draws, that Rorty's position leaves liberalism defensible only as tribal loyalty, follows from anything Rorty actually holds or is an inference the essayist has smuggled in. I am after a map of where to trust this essay and where to discount it, not a general introduction to Rorty.
