Here's the job. You're a philosopher of science who's been refereeing for journals for about 20 years and is very, very tired. Write me a mock referee report -- the thing a journal emails to authors -- on a manuscript that claims to have *measured* how close two rival scientific theories are to the truth and ranked them. Like 600-700 words, formatted as a real report: the manuscript's (made-up) title, a one line recommendation, then numbered comments. Deadpan the whole way through. No winking, no "of course this is impossible!", no asides to the reader explaining the joke. Things I want smuggled in. Priya explained these to me so I'm pretty sure they're right, and if you know the real versions, use them, but please don't cite anything, I don't want a bibliography, and if you need a philosopher's name just invent one rather than half-remembering a real one:

- there's an old proof that on the natural definition of "closer to the truth" (A beats B if A keeps all of B's true consequences and adds some, while A's false consequences are fewer), no false theory can ever come out closer to the truth than any other false theory. Something about how you can always manufacture an extra false consequence of A that B doesn't have. The referee should ask the authors to reconcile their ranking with this, in the tone of a man asking them to fix their formatting.
- the score depends on the vocabulary you describe the theory in. Same theory, equally expressive re-description, different ranking. Have him politely request that they re-run their metric in a second language and report both numbers.
- the triviality thing: you can raise your truth-content by conjoining boring truths (2+2=4, that kind of thing), so he should be gravely unsure whether the theory in their appendix is closer to the truth just because they added more stuff to it.
- they claim the measure "rewards content", and he notices that adding content also added false consequences, so he asks them to restate the whole result as a partial order.

- at some point he should note that the two theories being ranked are both false (the authors admit this in §2), i.e. the ranking is being done in exactly the case the proof covers.
- somewhere he should be unable to say whether his own report is true and treat that as administratively fine.

Think carefully before writing.
