The script is reproduced here in full:

"Carroll's dialogue shows that you cannot get someone to accept a conclusion by supplying premises unless they already accept the rule that carries you from those premises to the conclusion. The Tortoise accepts A and accepts that if A then B, and then declines to accept B; Achilles adds the conditional as a premise, and the Tortoise accepts the new premise and still declines. What the Tortoise is asking for, then, is a further premise that would license the step, and since every premise can be accepted without the step being taken, the request can always be renewed. So the P reply is a dead end: it treats the rule as one more item of information, and each new conditional simply opens the same gap again. The R reply says that you may help yourself to the rule while you are justifying it, and the M reply says that modus ponens is partly constitutive of what the conditional means, so that a thinker who understands the conditional is already committed to the inference. These two come to much the same thing: both tell us that the rule may be relied on in the course of the justification, and so both dissolve the Tortoise's demand rather than answering it, since what the Tortoise wanted was a premise and neither reply will give him one. Of the three I think M is the strongest, because it explains why the demand cannot be met rather than merely permitting us to ignore it. Quine is the classic source for this line. Carroll's mistake is to suppose that the Tortoise has to be given a reason; in fact the Tortoise has simply opted out of the practice of inferring. My verdict is that the dialogue shows that no rule of inference can be justified at all, and therefore that the laws of logic are not true or false but only useful habits of inference, which is why the P reply's regress never terminates. What is right in the weaker replies is that there is no contradiction anywhere: the Tortoise never says anything false. There is simply nothing to say to someone who will not infer."
