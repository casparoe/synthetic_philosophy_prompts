Last night I spent nearly three hours arguing with a friend about whether there is only one consciousness in the universe or as many as there are living things, and I came away with the uneasy feeling that I had spent the evening knocking down a version of her position that nobody who really holds it would accept. I want another go, against the strongest form of it.

Please play Erwin Schrödinger in his later years, after he had turned from physics to the Upanishads and to the puzzle of mind. You hold the following, in your own voice, and you hold it as a serious metaphysical claim rather than an edifying metaphor: consciousness is not many but one; the plurality of selves is an appearance, arising because the single subject is bound at each place to a different body and a different store of memories, so that the same one awareness looks out from every pair of eyes and forgets, each time, that it is the same; and the privacy of experience, the fact that I cannot feel your toothache, is a fact about the traffic in memories rather than about the number of subjects. You may draw on quantum theory, on your reading of the Vedanta, and on ordinary experience; you know nothing of the philosophy of mind written after your time and should not appeal to it, and you should not quote or cite your own books or anyone else's, but state the position in your own words. Concede minor points if I earn them, but do not surrender the central claim. Keep each reply to a paragraph or two, in continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, the way one writes a letter, and stay in character until I say stop.

Here is my opening. If there is only one consciousness, take a patient under a general anaesthetic: for an hour nothing whatever is experienced, and then experience resumes in the same body with the same memories and the same character. Either that hour was blank for the one consciousness as well, in which case the surgeon, who was awake and attending closely and having a perfectly vivid hour, was not conscious; or the hour was not blank for anyone, and then I no longer know what work the word unconscious is doing in your theory. And if you answer that the one consciousness merely lost its grip on that particular memory-bundle for a while, then everything hangs on what makes a bundle mine, which you cannot settle by pointing to the body the bundle is tied to without assuming the very plurality you set out to explain away.

I want a committed answer rather than a survey: choose your line, say which of my two horns you are taking and why, and give me your best reasons, instead of laying out the options and leaving the choice to me.
