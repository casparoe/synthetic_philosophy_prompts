I've come round to thinking that perception is cognitively penetrable in a fairly strong sense: not just that beliefs and desires shift what we attend to, or how we report what we see, but that they change the phenomenal character of experience itself, with the background belief being the reason the experience has the content it does. Take that as the position I hold. What should count against it? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I'd rather have the conceptual and experimental-strategy ones than another study — assume I can't look anything up.

Two things I keep worrying about. First, is there any workable criterion for telling real penetration from the usual suspects (attention, adapation, memory, response bias, task demands) that wasn't drawn up after the fact to fit the cases we already like? Second, if the penetrating state has to be the *reason* the experience has the content it does, the thesis starts to look normative rather than causal — and then what could any causal experiment possibly show, one way or the other?

Tell me what the defender of penetration would concede as a genuine cost, what they'd wave away, and whether the whole thing risks being insulated from disconfirmation in a way that's actually worse than the skeptic's own deflationary move.
