I've been in a reading group on On Certainty for a term now and we've gotten completely stuck on the same question, and I suspect it's stuck for everyone and not just us. Here's the shape of it. Wittgenstein has these "hinge" remarks — that a lot of what we take for granted, like that the earth existed long before I was born, or the sort of thing Moore pointed at with his own hands, isn't something we know or doubt or even really believe in the ordinary sense; it's more like the frame that makes knowing and doubting possible at all, and it's held up not by evidence but by our acting, our training, the whole practice. One camp in our group reads that straight: hinges aren't propositions at all, they don't have truth values the way ordinary claims do, and calling them "known" is a category mistake; the certainty here is arational, a kind of animal sureness. The other camp says that's mystification — fine, hinges have a special epistemic status, we're maybe entitled to them in a way that doesn't need evidential support, but they're still perfectly good propositions, they're true, and the interesting question is what kind of warrant we have for them. And then we go round in circles, because every time the second camp gives an account of the warrant, the first camp says that's just smuggling evidence-based epistemology back in, and every time the first camp says hinges aren't propositions, the second camp says you're using "proposition" in a way nobody else does.

Here's my hypothesis, and I'd like you to test it rather than just agree with it: I think the disagreement is partly verbal — a fight about how to use the words "proposition," "knowledge," and "belief" — but that underneath there's a real substantive disagreement about whether justification has to be evidential, and maybe also about whether epistemology is supposed to describe how we actually hold our certainties or to justify them. Is that right? Or is there a hidden empirical/formal question doing the work, or are the two sides actually answering two different questions under one heading (one about the grammar and phenomenology of certainty, one about the structure of warrant)? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of who said what, and an honest answer about what, if anything, would count as progress here — a distinction, a change of question, a concession on one side — or whether the impasse is just what you get when a question is under-specified.

One more thing that might or might not be relevant: does it matter that "hinge" might not name one kind of thing at all but a grab-bag, so that the two camps are each right about different members of the set — some hinges being non-propositional practical certainties and others being ordinary propositions with an odd warrant? If so, tell me which way that cuts, because it feels like it could dissolve the fight or just relocate it.
