The whole point is: a reason, but no reasoner anywhere in the loop.

For the group I have to bring in a satire piece and I want it to actually be about the concept, not just lol bureaucracy. Can you write me this:

A deadpan regulatory guidance document from an invented agency whose job is to decide, for any process anyone submits to it, whether that process has a rationale. Real guidance-note voice — purpose, definitions, criteria, an appeals procedure, a table of worked examples with the agency's verdicts on each, a short closing section. Describe a flowchart in words rather than drawing it. Put a rival body in the background that insists reasons require reasoners, with the agency repeatedly writing memos telling it to stay in its lane — that's most of the running gag.

The target I want skewered is the practice of crediting a process with a reason just because you can tell its history as a selection story — i.e. handing out rationales so freely that the category stops doing any work. So the agency should be competent and even-handed, and its criteria should be recognisably the real ones: selection history, proper function, behaving as if designed, tracking versus representing, and so on. The funny verdicts have to follow honestly from applying those criteria, not from the agency being dumb — a phil of bio person should laugh rather than wince. Let the tests disagree on at least one case, so the agency has to pick a side and justify it in a footnote. End with the agency applying its own criteria to this very guidance note, and dont resolve it — by the last line I want the reader to be the one stuck with the question of where reasons actually live.

Worked examples I'd like in the table (use most of them, plus at least one of your own that I havent thought of): a beaver's dam, a thermostat, a chess engine, a river's meander, a price in a market, an immune system, a coin that's come up heads ten times running, and a company's hiring policy.

About 750 words, section headings fine. Readable to someone who's never heard the term, and never wink at the reader — no asides, no jokes about the agency being Kafkaesque, no real people as characters, no citations. Thanks!
