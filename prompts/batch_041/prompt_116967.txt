I keep seeing the experience machine used as a one-line refutation of hedonism, and I want a more careful reconstruction. Suppose Epicurus were handed a clear description of the machine — no new texts, no experiments, just his own system plus the setup — and asked whether to plug in (or, in the local jargon, whether he would wirehead). Work out what he would actually have concluded, and make the answer turn on his own hedonism rather than on generic worries about manufactured happiness. In particular, how do the pleasures of rest — the mere absence of bodily pain and mental disturbance — compare with the pleasures of active enjoyment for him, given that he treats the mind's pleasures as resting on true beliefs about nature, death, and the limits of desire, and that once pain and disturbance are removed pleasure can be varied but not increased? Then take the harder question: could he refuse the machine without inconsistency, or does his hedonism commit him to accepting any experience that reliably delivers tranquility, even one he knows to be manufactured? Close by naming the single point at which your reconstruction is most likely to be wrong.
