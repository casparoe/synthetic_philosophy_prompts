Some friends and I have been going around in circles for about two years on whether generations are real things or just marketing. The generationalists say a cohort that comes of age during some formative stretch — a war, a crash, a pandemic, the arrival of the internet — gets stamped with a durable outlook, so that Gen Z or the boomers name something genuinely out there. The critics say the boundaries are arbitrary (why eighteen years? why that cutoff year?), that the spread within any cohort swamps the differences between cohorts, and that the whole thing is a media product that sells itself back to us. Nobody has moved an inch, and everyone's read the same sociology.

I want a diagnosis, not a verdict. Is this a verbal fight about the word real — one side meaning causally efficacious, the other meaning cutting nature at its joints? Is it a hidden empirical dispute about effect sizes that neither side has actually settled? Or are they answering two different questions under one label, one about how people really differ and one about why we keep talking as if they do? I have a hunch it's that last one, plus something like a looping or interactive kind — a category that, once people are sorted into it, changes how they act, so both sides can be right. Tell me if that's a cop-out. And say what would actually count as progress here: a study, a distinction, or just owning up that the question was ill-posed from the start.
