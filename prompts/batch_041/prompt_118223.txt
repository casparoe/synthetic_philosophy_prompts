ok so context: i sit on the ethics committee at a mid-sized hospital and we're rewriting the consent language for a biobank, where people agree that leftover tissue and blood can be used for research we haven't designed yet. the current form says future research and everyone knows thats meaningless. so i've been reading around on what makes consent valid -- deception, coercion, competence -- and i've ended up somewhere i honestly can't tell if it's a real position or just me being difficult. here it is: consent isn't a mental state or an act of will or a signature, it's a relation between the reasons the person actually has and the act under the description it will actually bear at the moment it's carried out. so a signature is valid only if the person's reasons would still support the thing if they were handed an accurate description of it at the time it happens, not at the time they signed. i've been calling it reason-robust consent. it's supposed to make open-ended biobank consent invalid no matter how clearly you disclose it, which feels right to me, but it also seems to make a lot of ordinary medicine invalid (the surgeon finds something unexpected mid-operation, the patient never consented to that specific thing), which feels wrong. i suspect the seperate issue is that some of those cases are about a person being unable to recieve a description at all, and some are about the description changing after the fact, and my view is treating them as one thing.

What are the most important implications of this view for how we should actually write the form and run the biobank? I want the ones that a standard disclosure-plus-competence framework gets wrong -- especially anything about participants who later object to research done under their original signature, and about whether we can ever honestly say someone consented to a study that didn't exist yet. And i want to know where the view collapses: what does it forbid that we obviously should allow, and is there a patch that saves the ordinary medicine cases without letting the biobank back in?
