I am in the final weeks of a graduate seminar on Wittgenstein and on Kripke's reading of him, and for our last meeting I would like to open with a poem rather than a summary, in part because the paradox at the center of the material seems to resist summary. The strand I keep circling is the quus scenario. We are asked to imagine a speaker who, by every bit of evidence in their past behavior, has been adding, and whose entire history with the word plus is equally compatible with a deviant function on which any two numbers below fifty-seven are combined in the ordinary way while every other pair returns five. Kripke stages the crisis at the instant the speaker is asked for sixty-eight plus fifty-seven and answers a hundred and twenty-five, insisting that addition was what they meant all along, while the skeptic replies that nothing in the whole record of their use settles that rather than the deviant reading. What I find both thrilling and maddening is the further claim that at the crucial moment there is no fact about the speaker that makes the answer correct — they simply go on as they were trained to, and correctness turns out to be a matter of what the community does rather than of what is true.

Please write a sestina in the voice of that speaker, set at the threshold itself: not after the philosophy has been digested, but in the moment of being asked for sixty-eight plus fifty-seven and discovering, to their own alarm, that they cannot point to anything inside themselves that rules out the deviant reading. Let the form carry the argument. The six end-words should be the load-bearing terms of the scene — I would suggest same, next, plus, quus, nothing, and go, though you may trade one or two if the poem pulls against them. The reason for choosing a sestina is that its permutation is a rule the reader can check: the pattern is public, repeatable, and either followed or not, which is precisely the kind of thing the skeptic says we can no longer take for granted where meaning is concerned. Make that do work. I want each end-word to shift sense as it recurs — nothing should begin as the absence the speaker hunts for in their own history and end as the substitute ground they are offered instead, and go should move from a private intention to a communal practice — so that by the closing lines the same six words are doing something they could not have done in the first stanza. The envoi should turn on the asymmetry the poem has quietly been demonstrating all along: the permutation was obeyed correctly and in the open from beginning to end, and yet nothing in the speaker's head ever fixed what obeying it meant.
