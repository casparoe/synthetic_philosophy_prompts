I chair the graduate medical education committee at a mid-sized teaching hospital and I have to circulate a written opinion before Thursday's meeting, so I could use a hand drafting it. Quick background in case it helps: after a bad burnout report a few years back, our internal medicine program replaced the hard duty-hour caps on the ICU rotations with what they call "flexible call" -- the cap is still printed in the handbook but residents can request a lighter schedule, and the chief resident and the attending on service grant or deny the request at their discretion. Formally there's no penalty for asking, nobody has to justify anything, and the attendings insist they've never turned anyone down. In practice almost nobody asks. We ran an anonymous survey last spring: 68% said asking would be "career suicide," several said they'd rather be exhausted than be known as the resident who can't hack it, and one wrote that the attending "would never say no to my face, but he doesn't have to -- I just know." The one resident who did ask last year got a noticeably tepid fellowship letter the following autumn, though his evals were mediocre anyway so who knows. The attendings are adamant that the system respects residents' freedom: nobody is forced to work the long weeks, the lighter option is right there, and some residents genuinely want the extra ICU time for fellowship applications -- one told the committee that a flat cap would take that away from her, and that a room full of administrators deciding how much she's allowed to work is its own kind of paternalism. One attending, who did a politics degree before med school, keeps calling this the "eyeball test" -- something about whether you can look the person who holds your future in his hands straight in the eye without having to be deferential, and he says the answer here is obviously no. I think he got it from a philosopher, Pettit maybe? Anyway the chief residents' association wants us to either keep things as they are or go back to a flat cap with no permission needed. I want to propose a third thing: a default cap that applies automatically, plus a written opt-in for residents who want to exceed it, filed with the GME office instead of the attending and not visible to the attending until after that rotation's evals are in.

Please draft the written opinion I'll circulate. It should recommend a concrete option and give a time frame -- whether we phase it in, when we review it, that sort of thing -- and it should take the attendings' side seriously, especially the resident who says the flat cap takes away something she definately wants and that we'd be deciding for her, rather than just waving at "wellness." It also needs to be usable by committee members who have never read any political philosophy, so define your terms in plain language and don't assume anyone knows what "non-domination" means. And if you think my opt-in idea is a fig leaf that doesn't really change anything, say so and recommend something else -- I'd much rather be told my proposal is bad now than in the meeting.
