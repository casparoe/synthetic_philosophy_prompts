My old dissertation advsior died last month. His whole career was built on the careenium: the giant sphere packed with little rolling balls (simmballs) that knock each other around, and the question of whether the symbolic patterns at the higher level actually push the balls or whether it's just balls pushing balls all the way down with the symbols as a useful redescription. He insisted there was no central controller in there, no homunculus, no ghost in the sphere. I'm giving the eulogy at his memorial. The room splits roughly in half: cognitive scientists who know the careenium backwards, and his family and old friends from before academia who have never heard of it. I need a five-minute eulogy where the careenium does real structural work, not decoration, because the main thing I want to avoid is saying anything he would have thought false. That rules out the usual moves -- he's looking down on us, his soul, he's in a better place, even 'he was the heart of the lab' if that implies a central controller. But I don't want a dry lecture either. Write the eulogy. It should land for the family without being dishonest to the philosophers, and the simmball picture should shape how the whole thing answers whether he's still here, and in what sense.
