I'd like help identifying a pattern that keeps surfacing in how my team reasons about evidence, and I'm no longer sure whether it is one phenomenon or a cluster of three. I should say up front that I have no formal background in philosophy.

The setting is ordinary. We want to know whether some property holds of a small, awkward-to-test class of things — say, that no request reaching a particular legacy service ever ends in an unhandled exception. We cannot easily exercise that service. What we can do, cheaply and at volume, is exercise everything else. And here is what unsettles me: a passing test on a code path that never touches the service does appear to count, in some small way, as evidence for our claim about the service, since 'every request is either handled or never reaches the service' says the same thing as 'no request that reaches the service is unhandled'. The cheapest evidence available to us is therefore evidence about everything except the thing we care about, and there is vastly more of it. A team that never tested the service at all could in principle accumulate more apparent support for a claim about it than a team that tested it carefully.

Two further observations, which may or may not belong with the first. The support we gain seems to depend on how the claim is worded rather than on what we actually observed: the same claim admits formulations under which almost nothing we did counts, and formulations under which almost everything does. And the effect scales perversely — the rarer and harder to reach the target class, the more free evidence is lying around, so the cheapest route to confidence is strongest precisely where direct checking is hardest.

I have come across the raven paradox in passing and assume it is the ancestor of this, but the textbook version is a logician's puzzle about a hypothesis nobody needs, whereas what I am describing is about where an engineering team's evidence actually comes from, and which evidence gets collected. Is there an established name for this — either for the whole cluster or for the specific inversion in which cheap, technically-confirming, substantively empty evidence crowds out the expensive, informative kind? If there is no name, please coin one and define it. Either way, I want the boundaries drawn: which parts of what I have described are the familiar paradox, which are some other established idea, and which (if any) are not covered by anything I could have found. The label matters less to me than the carving.

Finally, please state how confident you are in each claim you make, and single out the point you are least sure of.
