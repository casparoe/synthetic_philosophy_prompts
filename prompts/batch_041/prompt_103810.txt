I am a high-school student and a member of a small after-school philosophy club, and a thought experiment we worked through last week has left me genuinely stuck. One of our members proposed it as follows. A fair coin is about to be flipped. If it comes up heads, then exactly one person will be created and placed alone in a room; if it comes up tails, then one million people will be created, one per room, and none of them will be told how many others there are. You are one of the people so created; you wake up in your room and your only relevant evidence is that you exist. Before the coin is even flipped, someone offers you a bet that pays out if the coin was heads and costs you if it was tails, and you have to decide now whether the reasoning favors taking it.

Two of my friends have staked out opposite positions. The first says that once you find yourself existing, you should be about a million times more confident that the coin was tails than that it was heads, since a world with a million people gives you a million more chances to be someone, and so you should decline the bet. The second says this is plainly confused: you would have existed either way, so the bare fact that you exist is equally likely on both hypotheses and tells you nothing about the coin, leaving you at fifty-fifty. They cannot both be right, and when I try to adjudicate I find I can argue myself into either one. A third member then extended the first friend's reasoning and claimed that if it is sound, we should also believe it overwhelmingly likely that the universe contains an astronomically large number of people, because our own existence would be a far less probable coincidence in a small universe; and that if the second friend is right, we have no such reason. This extension is what I actually want to understand.

Please tell me who is right about the bet, and whether the reasoning does or does not carry over to the question about how many people the universe contains. Be concrete about the exact step where the two friends diverge, and do not just say that it depends on which assumption you adopt -- I want to know which assumption you think is actually correct, and why. Open with a one- or two-sentence verdict and then justify it, and keep the whole answer under about 450 words.
