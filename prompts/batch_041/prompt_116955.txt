I am a software engineer by training, and for the past two years I have sat on the governance committee of the professional association I have belonged to since graduate school. I have no background in political theory and I am somewhat out of my depth. The association is a nonprofit with roughly forty thousand members, a nine-member elected board, and a bylaws amendment process that lets members petition for changes. A petition has just cleared the signature threshold, which means the committee must take a position before the annual general meeting, and I have been asked to write the opinion we will circulate to the board and to the petitioners.

The petition bundles two changes. The first would convert three of the nine board seats into seats filled by lot: any member in good standing would be eligible, the drawing would be stratified so that the three selected directors roughly mirror the membership by region, career stage, and area of practice, and each would serve one two-year term with no possibility of renewal. The second would cap the six remaining elected seats at two consecutive terms, followed by a two-year cooling-off period. The petitioners argue that the board has become a small, self-perpetuating group who have effectively made a career of governing the association, that they recruit and endorse one another, and that an ordinary member has almost no realistic path onto it. They invoke the Athenian use of selection by lot and the recent citizens' assemblies as evidence that this is not a fringe idea.

The board is divided and I am honestly unsure. Draft the written opinion I will circulate. I need a clear recommendation with a time frame — what the committee should put to the membership at the annual general meeting, in what order, and what should happen if the membership rejects it — followed by the reasoning that supports it, and a serious statement of the strongest case for the losing side that a board member on the other side could recognize as fair.

In the reasoning, please work through the parts I find hardest to sort out on my own. Are the two halves of the petition really one remedy or two different ones, and would it be coherent to recommend one and reject the other? If the point of choosing by lot is that every member has an equal chance, does stratifying the draw to mirror the membership quietly undermine that justification, or does it repair it? Can a voluntary association oblige a randomly selected member to serve at all, given that directors carry fiduciary and legal duties — and does the answer change if the body is a one-off deliberative group with a bounded question and paid staff rather than a standing board that must handle audits, litigation, and personnel matters? And is there a real risk that term limits do not end the careerism the petitioners describe but simply relocate it, strengthening the permanent staff and the informal networks that never appear on a ballot?

I do not need a bibliography or citations; I cannot check them, and I would rather have the argument than a reading list. What I need is something I can defend in a room of nine people, several of whom have already made up their minds.
