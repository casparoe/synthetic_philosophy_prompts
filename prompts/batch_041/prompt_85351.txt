Ok so I've been going down an infinity rabbit hole and I've been keeping a list of my gut reactions, and I want to know if there's one principle behind them. My suspicion is there isn't and I have to give something up, so tell me which.

The hotel with rooms 1, 2, 3, ... all full, and one guy shows up at midnight: fine, everyone moves up a room, he takes room 1. No contradiction, I don't see the problem.

That same hotel, and a bus with a seat for every number 1, 2, 3, ...: also fine. Double the room numbers, done.

The hotel where the rooms are numbered by every fraction between 0 and 1, so packed that between any two rooms there's another room, all full, and one guy shows up: I say fine, same reason as before. But I honestly can't picture the shuffling and it makes me nervous in a way the first two don't.

The ordinary hotel from the top, and a bus with a seat for every real number between 0 and 1: impossible. Not hard, impossible. I want to know what makes that bus possible and this one not, in a way that isn't just pointing at a proof.

The even numbers and all the numbers: I do not believe those are the same size. It feels like a trick delivered with a straight face.

The square numbers and all the numbers: same reaction.

The points in a one inch line and the points in a two inch line: same number, obviously, and I have no problem with it at all.

Point nine repeating and one: same number, fine.

There is no biggest number: fine, and it doesnt feel like it commits me to some giant completed infinity existing somewhere.

The lamp that gets flipped on, off, on, off, each flip taking half as long as the last, for a minute total: I say there's no fact of the matter about whether it's on when the minute is up. Not on, not off, just nothing.

The counter that ticks up by a half, then a quarter, then an eighth, and so on, for a minute: I say when the minute is up it reads exactly one. Definite answer, no mystery.

Achilles and the tortoise: he catches it, no paradox there.

And nothing physical is actually infinite, I'm pretty sure. No infinite piles of coins, no infinite past. But it's just plain true that there are infinitely many numbers. I dont feel any tension there, though maybe I should.

Here's the principle I tried to write for myself: two collections have the same number of things exactly when you can pair them off one to one, except when one collection is literally a part of the other and you got it by leaving members out, in which case the bigger one is bigger. I already know this blows up somewhere, probably on the two lines.

What I want: the principle, or two or three candidate principles, stated precisely, with which of my reactions each one saves and which it has to overturn. Someone will say my reactions about the even numbers and the two lines contradict each other, since the short line is part of the long one. It doesnt feel that way to me, the points aren't laid out in a counting order the way the numbers are, but tell me if that's a dodge. And say what your best principle predicts for a couple of cases I havent thought about: a hotel whose rooms are numbered by every real number between 0 and 1, with a bus that has a seat for every real number between 0 and 1; and a hotel whose rooms are numbered by all the negative whole numbers, so there's no first room, with one guy showing up at midnight. Please dont tell me it's all just definitions, because if it were I wouldnt have these reactions.
