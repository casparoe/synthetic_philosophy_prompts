Ok so this is an arguement a friend of mine swears by and I cannot find the hole in it, and it's bugging me. We were arguing about whether anyone will ever actually know why some countries are rich and others aren't — institutions, geography, culture, whatever — and he says the honest answer is no, and here's the proof:

(1) To know that X caused Y, you have to rule out everything else that could have caused Y and that moves together with X.
(2) In the historical record, everything moves together with everything. Institutions, geography, culture, disease environment, colonial history, religion, latitude, legal system — every variable anyone has proposed is correlated with all the others, and all of it is measured at the same coarse country-level grain.
(3) The only way to actually rule those rivals out is to randomize X, or find something that randomizes it for you — an experiment, or a natural experiment that's as good as one.
(4) Nobody randomized institutions or geography or culture across countries. And every natural experiment on offer — the usual instrument (how deadly a place was for European settlers), or a border discontinuity, or a colonial-era rule change — leans on an assumption, that the thing affects prosperity only through X and not through anything else, that can't be tested from inside the data. (He also says the geography people control for current institutions, which are themselves partly caused by geography, so of course the geography effect shrinks — but I think that's a seperate gripe.)
(5) So we can never know why some nations are rich. We can tell stories that fit and that's it.

My problem is that (5) proves way too much. If (1)-(4) are right then we also can't know that smoking causes lung cancer, or that lead in petrol knocked down kids' IQs, or basically any causal claim in epidemiology or historical science — and I'm not giving those up. So something in there has to go. Which step do I drop, and why did that step look fine until I stared at it? And once I drop it, what do I lose — specifically, do I lose my ability to brush off the arguments I think are junk, the ones that just throw in a pile of controls and declare the question settled, and does the repair actually do anything for the institutions-vs-geography fight, or does it just make me feel better about not knowing? Also, tell me whether you think that settler-mortality thing is really a geography variable in disguise, because if it is, doesn't that break the whole style of argument and not just this one case?
