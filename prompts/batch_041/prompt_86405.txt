Two agents, A and B, are ideally coherent Bayesians: each knows the other's evidence, they agree on all likelihoods, and each is certain of the other's rationality. They nonetheless assign different probabilities to a hypothesis H, and the disagreement is common knowledge between them. Neither prior is stipulated. Each agent's prior is the output of running the same learning rule over a different stream of data, and each can inspect the other's prior and the other's data.

I want your credence that this situation is stable, meaning that A and B can go on assigning different credences to H indefinitely without either having erred in reasoning, updating, or the representation of their evidence. Allow that each may update on the other's credence, so that stability means a fixed point of that mutual updating at which they still differ. Give me a number, and decompose it. I care about at least the following terms, though you should add whatever else belongs and state how the terms combine, since they are plainly not independent:

1. The probability that coherence and the probability axioms leave an agent's prior underdetermined, so that two coherent agents can start from different priors without either being irrational.

2. The probability that, given full mutual transparency about priors, evidence, and the disagreement itself, some further norm of rationality forces convergence regardless.

3. The probability that the shared-evidence condition is well defined, in particular whether facts about an agent's own learning history count as evidence, and whether sharing all evidence can be specified without already presupposing one of the two priors.

4. The probability that both learned priors are conditionalizations of a single hyperprior over data streams, so that the common prior assumption can be reinstated one level up, over the processes that produced the priors rather than over the priors themselves, and convergence follows from there.

Then combine the terms, tell me which one the final number is most sensitive to, and say what argument, proof, or counterexample would move that term most. State also what would count as settling the question in either direction: what would an impossibility result, or a constructive example, have to establish? For calibration, I put the probability of stable disagreement at about 0.45; if you land far from that, tell me which term we most likely disagree about.
