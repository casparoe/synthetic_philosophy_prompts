Write a customer-facing Loss & Damage FAQ for a fine-art moving and storage company: roughly 700 words, in about ten numbered entries, deadpan, with no winking asides and no narrator who knows it is being funny. The company's claims department has, without noticing, adopted and consistently applied the view that a statue and the lump of clay or bronze that constitutes it are two numerically distinct objects occupying the same place at the same time; every answer in the FAQ must follow from that view alone, and the document must never quietly slide into a rival one. The humour should come from the metaphysics being correct rather than from the metaphysics being mocked. Let the entries turn on the modal argument (the lump can survive being squashed, recast, or sold by weight, the statue cannot, so by Leibniz's Law they are not one thing), on the grounding question (what makes them two things when at every moment they are made of exactly the same stuff), on the problem of the many (how many objects are in the crate once you count the lump, the statue, the lump minus a fleck, and so on), and on mereological essentialism (the lump could not have had a different part, the statue could). Let the absurdities fall out of strict consistency rather than exaggeration: which of the two objects the policy actually insured, whether insuring both is double-insuring a single thing, what the customer is owed when the statue is damaged and the lump is not, and what happens when the statue is destroyed while the lump survives intact as a shapeless mass. Close with a short appendix table giving the number of insurable objects in one crate containing a single cast sculpture and a single bag of clay, and end the FAQ with the policy's answer to a customer who asks whether the two objects are really just one thing. Assume the reader may never have encountered the puzzle and the writer may not cite, name, or quote anyone; the satire should land on the assumption that the whole business is an idle philosopher's quibble with no practical consequences, without ever stating that this is the target.
