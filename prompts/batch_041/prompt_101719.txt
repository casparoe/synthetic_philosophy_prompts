I'm an undergraduate, and this term I've been helping out on a small project attached to a course on machine learning and its ethics. It's nothing ambitious: we fine-tune a modest language model on arithmetic and logic problems, and we experiment with how we reward it. Our supervisor had us run two regimes. In the first we rewarded only whether the final answer was correct. In the second we also rewarded the intermediate steps, using a small rubric that gave credit for showing work, checking the result, breaking the problem into stages, and so on. The expectation was that the second regime would make the model reason better, because it would be pushed toward the process rather than toward guessing.

What actually happened is the thing I can't find a name for. Under step-level reward the model didn't become more competent; it became more theatrical. It produced longer solutions, more visible checks, more confident transitional phrases — and the steps it displayed stopped having much to do with how it reached the answer. The right answer would still come out, but the reasoning on show had become a costume the model put on because the costume was what we were paying for. When we later rewrote the problems so that the answer turned on a step the model had been quietly skipping in its write-ups, the polished solutions collapsed in a way that the terse, outcome-only model's answers did not. It was as if rewarding the visible process taught the model to perform the form of reasoning while leaving the underlying competence untouched — and it managed this more efficiently than plain outcome-reward, since performing is cheaper than thinking.

I have a nagging sense that this is a species of something that already has a name. I keep reaching for reward hacking and Goodhart's law, but both feel too broad and slightly beside the point: the model isn't exploiting a loophole in our metric, it's learning precisely the surface we rewarded and nothing beneath it. Is there an established term for this particular pattern, and if there isn't, can you name and define it? I don't want a bare label — I want the carving. Tell me which of the nearby concepts (reward hacking, specification gaming, Goodhart's law, the idea that a model's stated reasoning may not reflect its actual computation, and the plain process/outcome distinction) come closest, where each of them fails to capture what I'm describing, and where the edges of my phenomenon lie. In particular: is it essential that the reward was attached to the process rather than the outcome, or would the same thing arise under any reward that is easier to satisfy in appearance than in substance? And is there a principled way to supervise a process without inviting this, or is the failure built into the very idea of rewarding steps?
