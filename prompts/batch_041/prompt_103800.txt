I need a specification, not a survey. Set out the desiderata that any adequate theory of belief attribution to artificial systems must satisfy: the phenomena it must accommodate, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the constraints it must respect, each paired with the consideration that motivates it. The framing you should take as the point of departure is the intentional stance — the proposal that we ascribe beliefs and desires to a system when doing so lets us predict and explain its behavior — and the question is what that framework, or any rival, would have to deliver before we should trust it for machines rather than for one another. Treat the stance as a live candidate, not as settled doctrine, and be willing to conclude that it fails some requirement on the list.

Hold fixed the following class of subjects, since the desiderata should be answerable for them rather than for idealized agents: systems trained by gradient descent on large text corpora, whose outputs are sampled from a distribution, whose verbal behavior shifts with prompt, sampling temperature, and context, and whose internal states are partially legible to interpretability probes but not available to the system by anything like introspection. Write from what you can reconstruct yourself; do not supply titles, authors, dates, or reading lists, and do not lean on any particular paper's formulation.

Each desideratum should be anchored to a case. At minimum, work through these: a system that affirms a proposition under one prompt and denies it under another; a system whose fluent affirmation is a near-verbatim continuation of its training distribution; a fine-tuned descendant of the same base model that now denies what its ancestor affirmed; a retrieval-augmented or ensembled system whose verdicts change when its external store or its aggregation rule changes; a system that reports its own attitudes in the first person; a probe that decodes a linearly readable signal for a proposition from the model's activations; and a system whose outputs are rendered consistent only by an external filter that discards some of what the model generates.

For each requirement, state the case that forces it and say whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp. I expect the list to include, among others: a verdict procedure for internally inconsistent systems that does not collapse into the claim that they believe nothing; a principled separation of belief from the mere disposition to emit a sentence, so that the parroting case is handled rather than stipulated away; a stance on the supervenience base of the attributed belief — the weights alone, the system together with its environment, or the output distribution; an account of whether and when a system's self-report counts as evidence about its own attitudes; a treatment of degrees, so that credence ascriptions are possible without being merely metaphorical; an account of how probe results bear on attribution without begging the question; and a criterion of practical adequacy, since we ascribe beliefs in order to decide whom or what to trust, when to suspect deception, and where to locate responsibility.

Then do the part I care about most: identify any pair or subset of the desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and explain the conflict. I am particularly interested in whether a realist reading of the stance — on which there is a fact of the matter about what a system believes — can be held together with the instrumentalist reading on which attribution is licensed by predictive utility alone; whether the demand for consistency in attributed belief is compatible with the observed prompt-sensitivity of the systems in question; and whether the requirement that a system's own report be treated as evidence can survive the requirement that we not posit inner states beyond what the behavior and the probes support.

Close with a brief assessment of how the intentional stance, a deflationary predictive or as-if account, a functional-role or inferentialist account, an eliminativist denial that such systems have beliefs at all, and a representationalist account keyed to probe findings fare against your list. Name the single requirement that does the most to exclude candidates, and name the requirements you judge to be prejudices of a particular camp rather than genuine constraints.
