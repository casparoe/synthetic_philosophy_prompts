I've been trying to write down my own view on equality for a grad seminar I'm teaching, mostly because a colleague keeps telling me my position is just three positions stapled together, and I want to know if she's right before I expose myself in front of students. Here's the package, warts and all. (1) The currency of distributive justice is capabilities, not resources and not welfare -- what matters is what people are actually able to do and be. (2) But I've recently been convinced that a capability shouldn't count unless it's one the person could be brought to care about exercising; a capability that's completely alien to someone's motivational set isn't really an advantage for them. I've been calling this effective access. (3) I'm a relational egalitarian at bottom: what's bad about inequality is hierarchy, domination, and the way some people get to set terms for others, not that some bundles are bigger than others. (4) I'm also a luck egalitarian about responsibility: inequalities traceable to brute bad luck are unjust, inequalities traceable to people's own choices are not. (5) I think equality is intrinsically valuable, not just instrumentally useful for other goods. (6) And I think a society can be fully just without any interpersonal comparisons of welfare, because the capability metric plus effective access does all the work.

Can you audit this? I don't want a survey of the literature -- no reading lists, no citations, I'm offline and just want the logic. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like they are, rank the real tensions by how much damage they do, and for each say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair costs. Also tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what I'd be losing by making it. I suspect (4) and (3) are the problem, but I genuinely can't tell if that's just because I've been reading to much about the harshness objection, so don't take my suspicion as a guide -- if the real crack is somewhere else, definately say so.
