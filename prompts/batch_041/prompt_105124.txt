For the past several months I have been sitting in on a small reading group that is working through Jain accounts of many-sided truth, and I have been asked to draft the specification we will use to judge any theory of it we consider. I do not want a history of the doctrine or a survey of who held what; I want the desiderata — what any adequate account of many-sidedness must be able to do.

Set them out, each with the consideration or case that generates it. The requirements I care most about are these: that a single claim be affirmable and deniable without either collapsing into flat contradiction or simply repealing the law of non-contradiction; that the inexpressible limb of the standard sevenfold predication be handled as something we cannot state rather than as one more statable value; that the view survive its own application to itself; and that a partial standpoint be distinguishable from complete knowledge without complete knowledge becoming just another standpoint. I would also like a requirement governing how the qualifier is read — as a modal operator of bare possibility, as an indexical marker of standpoint, or as something else — because our group keeps sliding between the readings without noticing.

To keep this from turning into a recital, the requirements should be answerable to three cases: a colleague who says a given surgery is both healing and harming, and takes many-sidedness to license asserting both with no qualification; a dispute in which the parties agree on every fact but classify the same thing differently — whether a virus is alive, say — so that the disagreement is not about information at all; and a speaker who declares every position true from one standpoint or another in order to avoid committing to anything. Say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of one camp, name any pair you think cannot be jointly satisfied, and tell me which single requirement excludes the most otherwise attractive theories. Finish by scoring, briefly, the sevenfold-predication account, a frankly relativist reading, and a deflationary reading on which there is one world under many descriptions — I expect to argue about the scores myself.
