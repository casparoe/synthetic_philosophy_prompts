I'm a systems engineer. My working life is multidisciplinary design trade studies, so I think in the vocabulary of multi-objective optimization: a design vector, a feasible set cut out by constraints, an objective vector, Pareto dominance, the Pareto front, supported versus unsupported points, weighted-sum scalarization and its inability to reach solutions in the non-convex parts of the front, the epsilon-constraint method, lexicographic and goal-programming formulations, knee points, sensitivity analysis, and the difference between generating the whole front a posteriori and fixing preferences a priori or interactively. I have no philosophical training beyond one undergraduate ethics course.

A friend who is finishing a PhD in philosophy and I argued about this for two hours last week. My line was that what philosophers call hard choices between incommensurable values are just multi-objective problems whose trade-off structure and weights have not been elicited: specify them, and the difficulty dissolves into a scalarization. She said that was a category error, and she kept deploying machinery I could not place. She wanted a distinction between two options being better, worse, or equal, and two options being comparable at all, plus a third case in which they are neither equal nor incomparable but roughly on a level with each other. She said some reasons are silenced or excluded by a choice rather than outweighed by it. She said some commitments are partly constitutive of who the agent is, so that violating one is not a low-scoring move but a different kind of move. And she said regret in a hard choice is not the regret of a forgone optimum but a sign that something was genuinely destroyed. I could not tell whether she was describing real structure or simply refusing to state her utility function.

I want two things. First, restate her position as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary, laying out the correspondences explicitly, and stating her claims in my terms rather than paraphrasing them into mine. Second, give me the residue report: for each claim, what maps cleanly, what maps only under distortion, and what has no counterpart in the multi-objective picture at all. Be explicit about where the translation fails because my framework is missing something and where it fails because her formulation was vaguer than it sounded.

Then adjudicate. Is her position just the observation that preference elicitation is incomplete, in which case my deflationary reading survives? Or does the residue point to structure no amount of elicitation would capture — choices no weighting can reach, objective sets or feasible sets that are revised by the act of choosing, options that are ruled out rather than outranked, standpoints that are not agent-neutral? Tell me which of her claims do real work against my picture and which are, as I suspect, my own machinery described from the outside. Note also anything running the other way: features of my framework, such as indifference among points on the front or the front being something you can know before you choose, that her vocabulary seems unable to express.

You need not cite sources; I have no way to check them where I am.
