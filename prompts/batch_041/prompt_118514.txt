So my friend Priya and I spent like two hours last night arguing about grounding — the in-virtue-of relation, the non-causal one where you say the fact that the table is stable is grounded in facts about how its legs and top are arranged. I've been writing up my own take for a metaphysics reading group and I went in thinking my picture was pretty tight, but she came at me from about six directions and now I'm less sure. I'd rather get audited now than get picked apart in front of everyone, so here's the package as I'd actually state it:

(a) There's one grounding relation, not a family of them. Composition, disjunction, realization, the mental-physical case — same relation, different relata. (b) It's hyperintensional. Meaning: two facts can be necessarily equivalent and still ground different things, and a fact can ground one thing without grounding something else that's necessarily equivalent to it. So grounding isn't just necessity in a trenchcoat. (c) It's a strict partial order — irreflexive, asymmetric, transitive — and I mean fully transitive. If a grounds b and b grounds c, then a grounds c. No exceptions for weird cases, no "well, for full grounding only" hedge. (d) It's explanatory and it's worldly, both at once: to say a grounds b is to say b holds because a holds, and that because is a feature of the world, not of us or our interests or our explanatory practices. (e) The world is well-founded: every chain of grounding bottoms out in facts that aren't themselves grounded, and that's just what fundamental means. (f) Physicalism, where physicalism is a grounding claim rather than a supervenience claim — mental facts are grounded in physical facts. (g) Grounds necessitate: if a grounds b, then it's impossible for a to hold and b not to hold.

Priya's big move was that (b) and (g) can't both be true, since necessity is coarse and hyperintensionality is fine-grained, and when I said (g) is only a necessary condition and not a sufficient one she acted like that was a dodge. She definately thinks (f) makes the mental epiphenomenal, which I keep telling her is just what physicalism is, and she kept going on about my bottom level being "brute facts all the way down" like that's cheating — which, yeah, the fundamental is ungrounded, that's the whole point, but she wouldn't drop it.

So what I want from you: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each one. Rank them by severity instead of telling me they're all a bit tense. For each real tension, say what the cheapest thing to give up is and what it costs me — and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most mess with the least damage, cause I'd alot rather change one thing than three.
