I'm a second-year PhD student in evolutionary anthropology, coming out of a behavioral ecology background, and I keep trying to drag signaling models into human material, which may be my first mistake. I've got a pattern I can't find a clean name for and it's been bugging me for a couple weeks, so I figured I'd ask someone who actually knows the literature.

The handicap principle, the way I was taught it, says a signal is honest because it's costly, and the cost has to bite harder on low-quality senders than high-quality ones -- thats the engine. But I keep running into signals where the credibility is definately not coming from how big the cost is; it's coming from the fact that the thing can't be taken back. Two things that made me notice. First, a friend got a fairly cheap, very visible tattoo committing herself to a political cause, and people treat it as a much stronger sign of commitment than the expensive watch another friend bought, even though the watch cost maybe a hundred times more and can be pawned tomorrow. Second, out in the field people make promises in front of the whole village; uttering the promise costs nothing, but once it's said it's said, and everyone treats it as binding in a way a private resolution isn't. Meanwhile the textbook cases I was trained on -- the peacock's tail, a stotting gazelle -- the cost is obviously real, but it's also sunk, and I honestly can't tell whether the theory is leaning on the cost or on the irreversibility.

So is there a name for this? I want to know if there's an established concept for credibility-from-irreversibility as distinct from credibility-from-cost, and how it sits relative to the handicap principle: is irreversibility just a special case of cost (sunk cost), or a separate axis, or is it actually the thing doing the work in the classic handicap cases and the cost is partly a red herring? I know about commitment devices and Schelling's burning-bridges idea, and I know index signals are supposed to be the can't-fake-it category, but neither of those feels like exactly this. If there's no established name, coin one, define it, and tell me what it excludes -- is a reversible-but-huge cost really a weaker signal than an irreversible-but-tiny one, and is that the right way to carve it? I don't want a bare label, I want the joints: where cost and irreversibility come apart, which one the evidence actually supports, and whether this is a real gap or whether I'm just confusing a signal with a commitment. Give me a one-sentence verdict, then put the term -- established or coined -- on the last line as \answer{...}.
