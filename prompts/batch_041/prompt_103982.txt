Explain the conceptual problem of higher-order vagueness. If a predicate is vague, there is no sharp line between the cases that clearly fall under it and the borderline cases; but there also seems to be no sharp line between the definitely-satisfying cases and the borderline ones, so that the status of being borderline is itself vague. Explain why this is a genuine difficulty rather than a technicality, why it threatens to recur at every further level instead of resolving at the second, and why it is especially awkward for views that try to tame the sorites by locating the vagueness in one specific place — for instance the epistemic view, which posits a sharp but unknowable cutoff, or supervaluationism, which explains vagueness as a gap in truth value. Then say whether any such view can stop the regress without giving up what made it attractive.
