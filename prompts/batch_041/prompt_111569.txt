I'm in the sixth form and have been reading around decision theory on my own, so I recognise the shape of a paradox when I meet one, but I cannot find the name for the pattern I keep noticing.

A friend of mine will swap one of her possessions for another if the other person throws in a small fee, and asked about three of her things in a single sitting she will trade the first for the second, the second for the third, and the third back for the first, paying a fee each time — so someone patient can take her money in a circle and leave her holding exactly what she began with. A second friend looks the same from the outside but is not: she would never run the circle in one sitting, since at any one moment her rankings are perfectly consistent, yet her tastes drift over the weeks, so a trader willing to wait between steps can still walk her round the loop and pocket the difference. A third version: she sees the drift coming, knows she will regret it, and goes along with it anyway.

Is there an established term for the exploitable structure in the first case, and does it extend to the second? My suspicion is that the two come apart — one is about a single set of preferences, the other about preferences held at different times — and I would rather have the carving than the label: what exactly each concept covers, why the second case is or is not an argument that she is irrational rather than merely unstable, what the third case adds, and where the terms stop applying. If nothing established covers all three, say so, and name and define what does.
