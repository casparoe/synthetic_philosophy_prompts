I'm a fourth-year PhD student in metaphysics, currently seconded half-time to a rare-disease data consortium, where I serve as the team's metaphysician in residence. The curators I work with are biomedical informatics staff with no philosophical training. They add edges to a knowledge graph at a rate of roughly forty a week, and each edge ships in a quarterly release that is queried both by clinicians using the registry and by an automated phenotyping pipeline, so a mis-typed edge does not stay local. Our graph currently has a single generic relation, dependsOn, and my PI wants it split into four: GROUNDS, CONSTITUTES, CAUSES, and CORRELATES. I need to hand the curators a procedure they can run themselves in about twenty minutes per edge, without me in the room and without internet access; I will review only the edges they flag.

Please write that procedure: the tests in the order they should be run, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the conditions under which it stops and assigns a label, the conditions under which it stops and refuses to assign one, and the points where it must hand the edge to a person because the question is no longer graph-internal. It has to survive the cases we actually fight about: a pathogenic variant and the disease it is said to cause; a symptom cluster and the syndrome it is said to constitute; a social determinant and a health outcome; a lab measurement and the diagnosis it supports; and two predicates that are necessarily coextensive across our data but where only one direction seems to be dependence.

Two further constraints. First, the PI insists the graph be well-founded, with a base layer of nodes that depend on nothing, and the team is split over what belongs in that layer: the gene, the patient, or the whole registry treated as a single thing. The procedure must say how a curator is meant to adjudicate that, or admit that it cannot. Second, the curators will inevitably treat 'we need this edge to explain X' as evidence of grounding, and the procedure has to disarm that move without insulting them. Do not give me a reading list; nobody on the team will read it.

Then run the procedure on three edges of your choosing, at least one of which yields a verdict the team will not want to hear. Finish by telling me what the procedure cannot do and which of its steps are judgment calls wearing the costume of a test.
