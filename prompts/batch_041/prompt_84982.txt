I'm supposed to present my thesis chapter at a work-in-progress thing next Thursday, and my advisor keeps telling me to just write down what I actually think and then see whether it holds together, so here's my attempt. I came to this from general relativity rather than from Newton, and I've read enough Leibniz to be permanently nervous about the whole subject. (1) Substantivalism, in the plain sense: spacetime is an entity in its own right, over and above bodies and their relations, and a claim to the effect that the water is rotating relative to spacetime is literally true rather than a shorthand for something about the relations among pieces of matter. (2) What convinces me is the bucket: a bucket of water spinning in an otherwise empty universe still ends up with a concave surface, so whatever the water is rotating relative to can't be other bodies, and relationism about motion looks dead to me. (3) But I don't believe in absolute rest or absolute velocity for a second: a uniform boost of everything in the universe is a redescription and not a new possibility, and I'm happy to swap Newton's spacetime for the Galilean/neo-Newtonian one in which only acceleration is absolute. (4) The same lesson, harder, from GR: I take the gauge structure of the theory seriously, so two models related by a diffeomorphism are two seperate presentations of one and the same physical situation, and the theory doesn't privilege any particular labelling of the points. (5) I'm a determinist and I mean it: if a fundamental theory says that the complete physical state at a time leaves the future genuinely open, I take that to be a defect in the theory or in my understanding of it, not a discovery about the world. (6) I'm a haecceitist about spacetime points: there's a fact of the matter about which point is which, and that fact isn't exhausted by the pattern of metrical and material facts at and around the points; without something like that I don't see how (1) is anything more than a manner of speaking. (7) And I've never been able to shake the thought that inertia shouldn't be taken as primitive, that the local standard of non-acceleration ought to fall out of the global distribution of matter and energy, which I know people call Mach's principle and which is a big part of why GR feels to me like the right theory rather than a lucky formalism.

What I'd like is an audit, not a lecture. Which of these seven actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict, and how bad is each real conflict? Order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one tell me what would have to give and what I'd be paying, including, if it comes to that, what's left of (1) after the surgery. Please don't tell me it's all fine and please don't tell me it's all broken; my suspicion is that one or two of these are doing the real damage and the rest are noise, but I couldn't tell you which ones. And if a single revision would clean up more than any other, say which and why.
