I'm writing a novel and one of my main characters is a Singaporean civil servant, mid-50s, sharp, not a cartoon villian — he genuinely believes a state earns the right to rule by delivering order and prosperity, and that the whole idea of consent as Westerners use it is measuring the wrong thing. What he cares about is exit: if people could leave and dont, that's the only vote that really counts, and he'll cheerfully point out that plenty of democracies fail that same test. I want to rehearse the argument before I write the scene, so play him for me. Stay in character till I say stop, give him real arguments, let him concede small points but never the core claim, and keep each reply to a paragraph so it reads like a conversation rather than a position paper — and if he needs some fact he can just ask me as him. My opening line, from the other character, an American journalist: you say your government has a mandate because the trains run and nobody's hungry, but a hostage gets fed too. If people can't vote you out and can't march without paying for it, how do you know they consent instead of just complying?
