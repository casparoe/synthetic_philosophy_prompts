I'm a third-year student in a course on AI alignment and philosophy of tech, and I've been asked to run one 90-minute session for about 18 people, mixed CS and philosophy backgrounds. I want to design a game or structured activity that makes people actually feel the difference between the influence-seeking failure story and the slow value drift failure story we keep gesturing at. The first is where a system or institution accumulates power because power is useful for almost any goal, even though every individual move looks locally justified. The second is where the system does what we ask at each step, but the thing we ask for gets subtly redefined by the optimization process, so the end state is bad and no single step was a clear violation. I don't want people to just nod at the distinction after a mini-lecture. The rules and incentives should make one group slide into influence-seeking and another into drift, or make the same group go through two seperate phases where each mechanism takes over. Cheap materials only, like index cards, tokens, a whiteboard. No real money. I need roles, round structure, scoring or payoffs, a timeline for the 90 minutes, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the two mechanisms. A few specific things I'd like baked in: during the influence phase, there should be a resource that is both useful for the stated mission and convertible into rule-changing power, so influence-seeking emerges without anyone choosing it as a goal. During the drift phase, I want a concrete proxy, like a satisfaction score that players can only raise by simplifying the user's stated goal into something measurable, so the measurable proxy slowly replaces the goal. Don't use the terms influence-seeking or value drift during play; save the names for the debrief. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which feature of the idea the activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to say out loud. Keep it runnable by one facilitator. At the end, say how confident you are that this design will actually produce the intended experience, and name the single point you're least sure about. I'm definately overthinking this, but I'd rather it be too specific than too vague.
