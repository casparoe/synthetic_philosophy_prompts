I am a retired field geologist who has spent the past two years reading Hume on money and trade, and I would like a short story—no more than 800 words—that dramatizes a problem I have been unable to resolve. Set it on a small island whose currency is a perishable mineral that is also genuinely useful: a salt used both as money and as a preservative. A new trade route doubles the supply. The story should follow a visitor, perhaps a naturalist, who tries to explain to the islanders why their sudden abundance of tokens does not make them richer, and why the first effects will be uneven—some will gain, some will lose, and prices will not adjust all at once. But the twist is that because the money is also a consumption good, an influx of money really is an influx of wealth, so the distinction between money and real wealth cannot be drawn as sharply as Hume's essays suggest. Let this emerge through a concrete dispute—someone hoards the mineral, someone else consumes it, a merchant reprices debts, a magistrate must decide whether to accept a foreign token that cannot be eaten—rather than through a lecture. Do not include citations, footnotes, or a bibliography. End with one sentence that states the unresolved question the story leaves behind.
