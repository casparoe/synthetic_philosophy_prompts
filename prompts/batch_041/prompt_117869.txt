A constitutive panpsychist and a cosmopsychist — both hold that experience is fundamental rather than emergent, but they disagree about the direction of derivation — have spent years talking past each other, and I want the document they should have produced instead. The constitutive panpsychist begins with fundamental micro-subjects and owes an account of how a unified macro-mind is assembled from them; the cosmopsychist begins with a single cosmic subject and owes an account of how individual minds are derived from it. Draft their joint statement. Give me, first, a numbered section of claims both sides can sign: what calling experience fundamental rules out, why neither will accept emergence, what it takes for something to count as a subject, and where each side locates the other's derivation problem. Then give me a table of the exact remaining disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would endorse the wording. Name the cruxes — I expect one to be whether subjecthood can be had derivatively at all, and another to be whether combination and decomposition are one unsolved problem under two names — and for each crux supply a case or consideration both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. The joint statement has to face the shared hard cases, including a single subject that seems to host contradictory experiences at the same moment and the evidence that one brain can support more than one subject. If part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part. Neither side should be made to concede more than its position actually requires.
