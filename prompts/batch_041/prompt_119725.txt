A friend of mine dropped out of our stat mech program to found a startup, and I keep telling him that his whole pitch is just Maxwells demon with a business model bolted on, and he keeps telling me I'm stuck in the 19th century and don't understand the information-theoretic revolution. Anyway, his investor deck has a "Frequently Asked Questions" section at the bottom that is unintentionally the funniest thing I've read all year, and it's made me want to write a version that's actually good, so I'm asking you instead.

Write me that FAQ page -- 600 to 700 words, numbered Q&As, deadpan corporate register, the way a real early-stage investor FAQ reads: breezy, evasive, faintly menacing, occasionally contradicting itself. Invent the company (something like Demonworks or Second Law Labs, your call), the founders, the funding rounds, the product names, the regulator, and any quotes you want to put in their mouths. The comedy has to come from the physics being right rather than from the physics being made up, so the answers need to quietly encode the actual resolution of the paradox while never admitting it: the demon is a measurement-and-feedback device, so the measurement has to be written down somewhere physical; the memory has to be erased eventually, and erasure is where the bill lands (my friend keeps waving away Landauer's kT ln 2 per bit and it would be great if the FAQ waves it away too, in exactly that tone); the demon is really a heat engine running on an information gradient rather than on a single heat bath, so the "we extract work from one reservoir" claim has to get retracted somewhere in the fine print without anyone ever using the word retract; and the reversible-computing loophole -- if you never erase, you never pay -- should be acknowledged and then buried under an implementation detail nobody will read. I'd love it if a thermodynamicist reads the thing and laughs rather than winces, which means the jokes should be about Szilard's one-molecule engine, Bennett's accounting of the demon's memory, and whether the demon is a demon at all or just a very small accountant. And make the final question, whatever you decide it is, leave a reader genuinely unsure whether the second law has been broken or merely invoiced to someone else.
