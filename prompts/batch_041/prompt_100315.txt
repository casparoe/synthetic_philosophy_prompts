I've somehow ended up as the unpaid, slightly reluctant adviser to a small humanities foundation that's putting up money for four fellows for two years to work on Kierkegaard's critique of the press and of the public, and they've asked me to help them decide what the fellows should actually be working on. I keep going back and forth on it, so I'd like your take.

The candidates I can see are roughly these. (a) The relationship between what he calls the public -- an abstract phantom, nobody, a monstrosity produced by the press -- and the leveling process he describes: is the press the cause of leveling, or just its most visible symptom? (b) Whether the critique is meant descriptively or normatively, and if normatively whether it lands him in a fairly straightforward elitism, the single individual set against the crowd. (c) Whether the public and the crowd are even the same target at all, since one looks like an abstraction that nobody belongs to and the other like an assembled multitude. (d) What his own authorial practice does to the coherence of the critique -- the pseudonyms, the indirect communication, the fact that he published relentlessly into the very market he was attacking. And (e) how far any of this transfers to a world of feeds and algorithmic recommendation, where something like the public is arguably reconstituted but in no shape he would have recognised. I have a hunch that (a) is upstream of most of the rest -- that if leveling is the real object and the press merely its instrument, then a good many of the political readings of him collapse -- but I don't trust the hunch, and I may just be importing a distinction that isn't his.

What I want from you is a definately defended ordering, not a survey. Assume the four fellows are philosophers with decent Danish-reading ability but no specialism in nineteenth-century Denmark, two years to work, and a funder who will want a concrete contribution at the end of it. For each question you'd prioritise, tell me why it's upstream of the others, how tractable it is with those resources, and what would genuinely move it up or down the list -- a conceptual distinction, a better rendering of some key term, whatever it happens to be. And say plainly which of the candidates you'd drop as not worth two years of anyone's life. One page, 700 words maximum, in British English please.
