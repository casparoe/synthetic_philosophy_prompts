A friend and I have been argueing about judicial review for months and we've started repeating ourselves, so we're trying the adversarial-collaboration thing. I think judicial review is basically legitimate: when a legislature passes something that violates a right, someone unelected has to be able to say no and mean it. She thinks final say over what the constitution means should sit with the elected branches -- courts can flag things, they can't settle them. Arguing in the abstract got us nowhere, so we want the document built around one concrete proposal I've been pushing: every constitutional rights ruling sunsets after twelve years unless the court re-affirms it, and in the meantime the legislature can re-enact the struck-down law by a two-thirds vote with published reasons, which the court can only overturn by showing the law burdens the 'core' of the right rather than its periphery.

Write the joint statement we could both sign. Start with a numbered list of what we actually agree on -- about what a constitution is for, about the countermajoritarian worry, about what the sunset proposal would do in practice. Then a disagreements table, each side's view stated in wording that side would accept, including whether the core/periphery line can be drawn without begging the question and whether 'final say' is even the right way to carve the issue up. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions where settling one would move at least one of us, and for each a case or piece of evidence we'd both agree bears on it, with each of our predictions written down in advance. If part of what we've been fighting about turns out to be verbal, say which part. And if you think we can't honestly sign a joint statement -- because the real disagreement is something neither of us has put on the table -- say that straight instead of manufacturing consensus.
