so there's something at work I keep bumping into and I want to know what it actually commits us to. I do trust and safety for a mid-size platform. Our integrity policy attributes "intent" to accounts in a pretty specific way. We obviously can't see what anyone was thinking, so instead a moderator can only record an intent finding ("this account intended to deceive") if the behaviour passes two tests. First a perturbation test: we re-run the account's behaviour with the wording, the timing and the target changed, and the same pattern has to reappear; if it only shows up for one exact message at one exact moment we log it as organic or noise and move on. Second a compression test: the intent finding has to let an analyst predict the account's next dozen actions better than a low-level mechanical description of those same actions would; if the click-level account does the job just as well, we're told to drop the intent finding.

Meanwhile the same document, in a completely different section, says our ranking model has no intentions and no goals, it's "just arithmetic" — even though the second we're debugging it the engineers are all saying things like "it wants to push this up" and "it's trying to keep you watching". And here's the bit that nags me: the intent findings are what get people banned, and the "just arithmetic" framing is what gets the company off the hook when the ranking does something awful.
