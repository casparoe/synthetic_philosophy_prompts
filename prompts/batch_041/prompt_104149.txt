For my high school philosophy club, I have to present where I've actually landed on whether technology shapes society or society shapes technology, so the others can push on it. Here is my current position, and I'd like to know whether it hangs together.

(a) Once a technology is widely adopted, the broad way society reorganizes around it is more or less inevitable, and different countries end up in similar places regardless of their politics.
(b) Artifacts have politics: design choices, like a bridge built too low for buses or a feed tuned to reward outrage, encode and enforce social arrangements, so a tool is never politically neutral.
(c) When a technology causes harm, it really comes down to how people choose to use it; the same app can be used well or badly, so blaming the design is a cop-out.
(d) Laws and standards genuinely redirect what technologies do; seatbelt and emissions rules changed cars, so outcomes are not dictated by the technology alone.
(e) Whatever politics an artifact has, that politics is not permanent; redesign the thing and you change the politics.

Tell me which of these actually pull against each other and how badly, which pairs only look like they conflict, and for each real tension what the cheapest fix would be, ending with the single revision that would clean up the most. Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion, in American English.
