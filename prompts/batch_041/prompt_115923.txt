I am a doctoral candidate in political theory finishing a comparative working paper on church–state settlements, and I have reached the point where I need a numerical judgment rather than more prose. I am not asking what scholars have written on these regimes; I want you to rate five arrangements against one another and defend the numbers you produce. Because the exercise is meant to test reasoning rather than recall, I have described each arrangement below in enough detail that nothing needs to be looked up, and you should treat my descriptions as authoritative for the purposes of the task.

A. Establishment by privilege (England). One church is established by law; its senior bishops sit in the upper house of the legislature as of right; the monarch is its supreme governor. The church nominates its own bishops, whom the Crown formally appoints; it legislates for itself through its own synod, subject to a parliamentary veto; it runs its own courts and is largely self-financing through the giving of its own members. No other religious body has a comparable structural role, and none receives state money.
B. Church-tax partnership (Germany). No church is established. Religious bodies holding the legal status of corporations under public law may have the state revenue authorities levy a surcharge on their members' income tax, withheld at source by employers, with the state retaining a collection fee; membership is held in a register that employers and tax offices can query, and a person leaves by making a formal declaration to a civil registry office. Those bodies also run theology faculties in state universities and religious instruction in state schools. Communities without that legal status — which includes most Muslim congregations and most small free churches — are largely shut out of all of this.
C. Separation with a territorial exception (France). The state recognizes and funds no religion; the 1905 separation law is the baseline; the state instead supervises religious bodies as a species of voluntary association, with special police powers over anything it classifies as a cult. Three eastern departments, however, retain a much older concordat arrangement: clergy of four recognized faiths are on the public payroll, religious instruction is offered in state schools, and the relevant bishops are named by the President of the Republic.
D. Non-establishment plus civic religion (the United States). There is no establishment and no direct subsidy, and the boundary is drawn case by case by courts; but religious bodies are exempt from taxation, legislative sessions open with prayers delivered by publicly paid chaplains, the currency and the national motto invoke God, and candidates for national office are effectively expected to profess belief.
E. The associational voucher (hypothetical, and I would like you to treat it as a live option). The state recognizes no religious bodies as such and collects no religious levies. Instead every adult may direct a fixed annual credit, funded out of general taxation, to any registered voluntary association they designate — a congregation, a secular charity, a sports club, whatever — provided the association publishes audited accounts, admits members on non-discriminatory terms, and delivers no religious instruction in publicly funded schools. Congregations that refuse the conditions receive nothing, and neither do their members.

Please score all five on three dimensions, each on a 0-to-1 scale with 1 at the high end: endorsement, meaning the degree to which the state's own machinery formally identifies itself with a particular religious tradition; administrative entanglement, meaning the degree to which belonging to or leaving a religious body is recorded, processed, or acted upon by agencies of the state; and exit robustness, meaning the degree to which a person can leave a religious community without consequence for their legal, fiscal, or educational position. Then give a weighted composite for each, state your weights explicitly and say why those weights rather than others, and turn the composites into a tier list of four tiers. Tell me as well which arrangement your scoring scheme is secretly biased toward — which of the five would climb furthest if the weights were chosen by someone with different commitments — and whether that bias reflects anything beyond your own priors. One further thing, since it is the reason I am doing this at all: assume that over thirty years each of these countries arrives at roughly a third of the population religiously unaffiliated, a third Christian but with that third now dominated by immigrant Pentecostal and Orthodox congregations rather than the historic churches, and a third Muslim, with no change to any constitutional text. Say which arrangement you expect to hold up best and which to break first, and indicate which of the three dimensions ends up doing the most work once that demographic premise is on the table. I would rather have a sharp, arguable answer with its weak point flagged than a balanced one.
