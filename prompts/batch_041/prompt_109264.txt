Okay, I've been going down a rabbit hole on this Kelly betting stuff and I want to figure out what I actually believe, because my gut keeps giving me answers that dont obviously fit together.

The setup: someone offers you a bet where you win or lose a fixed fraction of your current bankroll, the odds are in your favor, and you get to repeat it over and over. My gut says bet some fixed fraction of your roll each time, never all of it, even though every single bet has positive expected value. And I get the reason — a friend explained it as: if you bet everything, your wealth over the rounds gets multiplied by a string of numbers, and it's the average of the logs that compounds, so the typical path goes broke even though the average across all possible worlds is enormous. Fine, I'm on board with sizing down here. I even looked up the formula, and for a 60/40 even-money bet it says stake 20% of your roll.

But then my gut gets weird on these:

1. A single favorable bet, offered once and only once, ever — 60/40, even money. My gut says take it, and honestly take a big swing, since I'm never getting another shot. But that's backwards from the repeatable case, right? If anything I'd figure the repeatable one is where I can be aggressive, because it averages out over time, and the one-shot is where I should be careful. Instead I'm cautious on the repeatable one and bold on the one-shot.

2. A bet that wins big with 99% probability but wipes me out with 1%, offered once. My gut says no, even though the expected value is clearly positive. Feels like the ruin thing again, but there's no repetition here, so I dont know why I care.

3. Insurance. I'll happily pay a premium that's higher than the expected loss to protect against a catastrophe, and it feels smart. But that's a negative-EV bet, so it seems to cut against taking the positive-EV one-shot in case 1.

4. Two favorable independent bets available at the same time. My gut says bet both, with a smaller stake on each than I'd use for either alone. No idea if that's principled or just nerve.

5. A tiny edge — 51/49, even money, repeatable. My gut says stake small but positive, roughly in proportion to the edge.

6. A friend says he always bets half of whatever the formula says, for safety. Is that principled, or just jitters?

So what I want: is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? And does the one-shot/repeatable distinction actually do any work in it, or am I just telling myself a story? Also, my friend claims the same logic applies to anything you can 'bet' over and over — your beliefs, how much effort you put in, even how happy you let yourself be — and that the right rule is to maximize the geometric mean of whatever it is, not the arithmetic mean. Does that generalization actually follow from the money case, or is it a different claim wearing the same clothes?

Finally, tell me which of my verdicts I should give up if I want a clean principle, and what the principle says about a case I haven't listed.
