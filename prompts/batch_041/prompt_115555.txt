Ok, so this is going to sound like I'm just relabelling the standard planning-theory account of shared intention, but I think what I've got is genuinly a different view, and I want to know what would count against it.

The view: two or more agents are doing something together just when each is running an accurate enough predictive model of the other's behaviour and each knows the other is doing the same — mutual prediction, reciprocal and reliable, and that's the whole of it. We-intentions, joint commitments, shared goals: on my account those are usually downstream of successful mutual prediction, not the thing that makes an action joint in the first place. The we-talk comes after, as a gloss on coordination that's already happening.

What I'm most attached to, and what I suspect is doing all the work, is the normative part. On my view the obligations of joint action are real but derived: it's wrong to bail mid-plan not because you violated some primitive joint commitment, but because you wrecked a coordination equilibrium and forced the other person to rebuild their model of you. Betrayal feeling different from a failed forecast gets explained the same way. Real, but downstream.

Two cases got me here. One is improvising with a stranger — never played together, no shared plan, and it works; nobody would deny we were playing together. The other is the mirror image: a chess game, where I model my opponent extremely well and there is no 'us' at all. I take the first as support and I have a story about the second.

So what would count against it? I don't just want the standard "prediction can't capture normativity" objection, I know that one. I want the actual defeaters: cases, intuitions, findings from developmental psych or animal cognition or game theory, whatever the view is committed to getting right and might not. Rank them, fatal down to merely awkward. Say which of them a defender like me would concede counts against us and which we'd wave away. Be honest about whether my move to 'derived normativity' has made the view unfalsifiable, and if it has, whether that's fatal for a view of this kind or just the cost of doing business. And tell me what my view predicts that the we-intention accounts don't — if the answer is nothing, say so.

I'd like you to flag how confident you are in each claim you make, and single out the one point you're least sure about.
