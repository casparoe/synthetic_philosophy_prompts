I am a novelist at work on a book whose premise is that reality has a bottom floor — a lowest level of facts on which everything else depends — and I have spent the last few months reading around in contemporary metaphysics in the hope of getting the premise right. My previous books were domestic and had no cosmology in them at all, which is probably why I feel so far out of my depth here. A friend of mine who is finishing a dissertation in the field, and who is ordinarily very careful, sent me the following paragraph as a summary of what philosophers say about grounding and fundamentality, and told me it was "the standard picture." I have reproduced it more or less verbatim:

"Grounding is the relation between a less fundamental fact and the more fundamental fact that makes it the case — the fact that there is a novel on the desk is grounded in facts about paper and ink, those in facts about the cellulose molecules, and so on down. It is a kind of metaphysical causation, or if you prefer, a fancy name for supervenience: to say that one fact is grounded in another is just to say that you cannot have the grounded fact without the fact that grounds it. Since grounding is irreflexive and asymmetric, and since a chain of grounding relations cannot run on forever with nothing at the bottom to stop it, it follows just from what grounding means that there must be a level of absolutely fundamental facts — facts that ground others but are not themselves grounded in anything. The fundamental level is thus not a substantive hypothesis but a conceptual necessity, and the fashionable view that everything is grounded is self-undermining: if the whole is grounded in its parts and the parts in their sub-parts and so on without end, then no fact is ever actually grounded, because you never arrive at the grounder."

What I would like from you is an audit. Which sentences would a philosopher working on grounding accept as fair, which would make them wince or reach for a qualification, and which are simply mistaken? I am especially curious about three things: whether the claim that the fundamental level follows from the meaning of grounding is really as airtight as my friend makes it sound; whether "metaphysical causation" and "supervenience" are as interchangeable with grounding as the paragraph implies; and whether the closing argument about the endless chain is a genuine refutation of the view that everything is grounded or an artifact of how it is phrased. For what it is worth, I am inclined to accept the bottom floor — the alternative sounds to me like a building with no foundation — but I am suspicious of any argument that claims to settle a question about the universe by appealing to what a word means, and I cannot tell whether the paragraph is doing that or whether I am misunderstanding it. If the paragraph does overreach, tell me whether the modest core of it — that grounding is asymmetric, and that some things are more fundamental than others — is still enough to build a cosmology on, or whether the whole picture has to go. Be concrete, please; I would rather be told that three words are doing illegitimate work than be handed a survey.
