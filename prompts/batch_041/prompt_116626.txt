For an upcoming session of a monthly online colloquium I run for graduate students in analytic metaphysics, I've invited a philosopher whose work defends David Lewis's counterpart theory against the standard Kripkean line that de re modal claims require strict transworld identity — the objection usually pressed via the Humphrey case, where we seem to say that Humphrey himself, not merely some counterpart of his, might have won the election. Her own position is a deliberately hybrid one: counterpart theory for persons, on the grounds that persons are best treated as four-dimensional and so can be represented at other worlds only through similarity-related counterparts, but plain transworld identity for fundamental physical objects, which she thinks carry haecceities that counterpart relations can't capture. I'd like you to act as a professional philosopher specializing in modal metaphysics and the semantics of de re modality, and to help me prepare the interview. Please begin by briefly brainstorming, for your own use, the main pressure points in the contemporary debate — the counterpart-theoretic reading of de re modality, Lewis's contextualism about which counterpart relation is operative, haecceitism and the question of whether worlds can differ without any qualitative difference, the translation problem that arises when quantified modal logic is recast in counterpart-theoretic idiom, and the contrast between concrete and ersatz conceptions of possible worlds — and then use that brainstorm to draft eight to ten interview questions.

The questions should go well beyond having her restate the view. I want at least one question that presses on whether her split treatment of persons and particles is stable, or whether the same considerations that push her toward counterparts for persons (four-dimensionalism, the rejection of bare haecceitistic facts) will generalize to the particles and undermine the hybrid; and a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. I also want a question on the contextualism charge — namely, that if which counterpart relation is in play varies with context, then "Humphrey could have won" and "Humphrey could not have won" may both come out true, and I'd like a follow-up asking whether that leaves de re modal discourse about a determinate subject matter at all, or whether it reduces to talk about our representational practices. Include a question on the translation problem that asks her to say what, exactly, is lost when modal formulas are rendered counterpart-theoretically, and one that forces her to distinguish her view from a merely notational variant of transworld identity. For each question, supply the likely reply and a follow-up that makes the reply harder to give. Please don't include citations, reading lists, or references to specific papers; I only want the questions, framed so that someone reasoning from the ideas themselves can engage with them.
