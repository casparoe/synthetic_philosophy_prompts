I'm putting together a spec for a theory of rational choice in what Hofstadter called the Luring Lottery and the Platonia-style variant of it, and I want the requirements list before I look at any more proposed solutions. The setup I'm working with: unlimited entries allowed, you submit one postcard with a number on it, the prize is a million dollars divided by the total number of entries, no communication, everyone acts alone, and there's a Platonia cousin where exactly one entrant wins and more than one entrant voids the prize entirely. The actual 1983 contest in Scientific American seems to have turned into a biggest-integer arms race rather than anything cooperative, which is the data point I keep circling back to. Superrationality is the obvious candidate theory here, but I don't want a defense of it yet — I want to know what any theory in this area has to be able to do to count as adequate.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, no preamble and no closing summary. Items should cover: the cases the theory has to get right with the case behind each requirement; the distinctions it can't collapse; any pair of requirements you think can't be jointly satisfied and why; which single requirement does the most work in ruling out contenders; and finally, what would change your mind about the list itself — which argument or consideration would make you drop or add a requirement, and where you think your reasoning here is most likely to go wrong. Be honest about how committed you are to the incompatibility claim in particular, since thats the part I'd most expect to be an artifact of how the requirements are worded rather than a real result.
