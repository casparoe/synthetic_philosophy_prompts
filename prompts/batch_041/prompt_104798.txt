A couple of friends and I have been going back and forth about Lara Buchak's risk-weighted expected utility stuff — the idea that a person has a risk function r that reweights the probabilities of outcomes depending on how good or bad they are, so that a risk-averse person (convex r) acts as if the chance of the best outcomes is smaller than it really is. I get the formalism fine on paper but I keep feeling like I'm missing what it actually does that plain expected utility can't, and I learn better from a concrete case then from the axioms. So I'd like you to build me one, to spec.

I want a single ordinary-life decision situation — realistic, the kind of stakes a normal person could actually face (a purchase, a job offer, a home-repair or medical choice, whatever), with explicit numbers: a small number of outcomes, stated utilities or at least a clear worst-to-best ordering, and stated probabilities. The constraint that matters most is that I want the case to isolate the risk function as the thing doing the work. So keep the utility ranking over final outcomes and the probabilities fixed, and show me the same agent with a convex risk function ranking the two options one way and with a linear risk function (i.e. plain expected utility) ranking them the other way — and then give me a second pair of choices, drawn from the same situation or a natural extension of it, such that the convex-risk-function agent's pattern of choices across the two pairs is one that no expected-utility maximizer with any utility function could reproduce. Don't just stipulate that the verdicts come out that way; derive them. After the scenario, walk through the REU computation for each option (show me the rank-ordered weighting, not just the final number), say exactly which feature of the case is doing the work, and tell me what happens if that feature is removed — does the case collapse back into something EU can handle? Then play devil's advocate: tell me how a committed expected-utility theorist would try to defuse the case, either by saying what's wrong with it or by redescribing the outcomes so that the REU agent turns out to be an EU agent with different utilities after all.

Also, be upfront about how confident you are in the claims you make here — especially the claim that the choice pattern really is impossible for any EU maximizer — and flag the one step in your reasoning you're least sure about. Write it in American English. End with a final line that starts with "Verdict:" and names, in a short phrase, the single feature of the case that makes REU and EU come apart.
