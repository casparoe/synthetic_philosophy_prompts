I recently moved to a four-day week, and the freed day has become an exercise in self-audit rather than the leisure Keynes imagined for a society that had solved its economic problem; the experience has sent me back to his distinction between absolute needs, which can in principle be satisfied, and relative needs, which are positional and therefore insatiable. Please write a villanelle for the reading group I am in on Keynes's conception of the good life. The two refrains should carry the two halves of that distinction, and the form's compulsive return to both should track the thought that a society released from scarcity may not become leisured but may instead relocate scarcity into comparison. Let the turn come when the poem stops treating free time as a reward and asks what Keynes took it to be for: the enjoyment of art and friendship, the pursuit of truth, and the possibility of valuing ends above means, against the background of his Moorean conviction that good states of mind are the ultimate goods and economic activity only a means. It should be recognizable to someone who knows the essays rather than merely the name, and it should not resolve into either a utopia or a complaint.
