Im a referee for a general math journal — I do maybe twelve, fifteen papers a year — and a big fraction of them lean on Zorn's lemma somewhere in the main proof, usually to conjure up some maximal gadget: a maximal ideal, a maximal independent set, a basis, a maximal extension of a partial map, that kind of thing. Im not a set theorist and I keep worrying Im either waving through proofs with real gaps or else flagging steps that are just routine for the intended audience. Can you write me a checklist I can actually run, in order, on any Zorn application in a paper Im refereeing?

What I want: for each step, what genuinely has to be true for the application to be legitimate, what I can let slide as routine, and what a failure would actually look like on the page so I can spot it. A few specific things I keep tripping on and want the procedure to handle: (a) the gap between the maximal element Zorn hands you and the thing the paper actually wants — 'maximal' is not 'maximum' and I suspect some papers quietly need the stronger one; (b) chains whose upper bound is built by taking a union, where it's not obvious the union lands back inside the poset, plus the annoying edge case where the chain is empty and that quietly forces the poset to be nonempty; (c) papers that advertise themselves as choice-free or constructive but then invoke Zorn, when I have a vague memory that Zorn and the axiom of choice are basically the same thing in disguise; and (d) an argument that looks like Zorn but might really be transfinite recursion or a well-ordering in a Zorn costume.

Then please run the checklist on two or three hypothetical papers: one where it comes out clean, one with a genuine gap that the author will swear isn't one, and one where the checklist tells me the proof is fine but only because the paper is leaning on more set theory than it lets on — which is the verdict I won't like, since it means I have to write a referee paragraph about something I don't fully understand. And be honest about where the checklist runs out and I just have to trust the author or punt to a set theorist.

Keep it in words, bare minimum of notation, and assume I've seen Zorn's lemma stated but have never actually proved anything with it myself.
