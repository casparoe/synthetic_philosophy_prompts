A friend of mine who's really into the whole wild animal suffering thing has been talking me out of veganism and I think his argument proves too much, but I cant find the bad step.

(1) The only thing that matters morally is the total amount of suffering in the world. How it's spread across lives, and how many separate lives it's spread across, doesn't matter in itself.
(2) Most of the suffering on Earth is out in the wild — mice, insects, birds — and it dwarfs everything farming does.
(3) Growing crops for people kills a staggering number of small animals: plowing, pesticides, combines, habitat loss. A wheat field is basically a mass grave.
(4) Raising a cow on pasture, by contrast, is one large animal eating grass we can't eat anyway.
(5) So a calorie of beef costs fewer animal deaths than a calorie of bread.
(6) So if (1) is right, eating beef does less harm than eating bread, and going vegan is backfiring.
(7) And it generalizes: chicken is worse than beef (more animals per calorie), so the most ethical diet is the biggest animals you can find — beef over chicken, and if we're being consistent, a whale beats everything.

But (7) is nuts. Nobody thinks the way to reduce suffering is to eat cows. So one of (1)-(6) has to go, and I'd really like to keep (1). Which step do I cut, and what does cutting it actually cost me?
