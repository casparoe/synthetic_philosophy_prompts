I'm a third-year student currently taking a course on decision theory, and I've been trying to find a name for a structure I keep noticing in my own life. I'd like more than the name, though: I want to know how the established concept, if there is one, is actually carved, because I suspect the thing I've noticed overlaps with it without being quite identical.

The cleanest case I can give is this. On my walk to the library I pass two side roads in succession. The two junctions are indistinguishable from where I stand — same corner, same signpost, nothing to tell me which one I am at. If I take the side road at the first junction I end up somewhere only okay. If I stay on the main road I reach the second junction, where taking the side road leads somewhere much better, while staying on past it leads somewhere bad. The best thing for me to do is to stay on at the first junction and turn at the second — which means that at the first junction the correct move is the one that looks worse from where I am standing, and I can only make it by fixing a rule in advance and then following it blindly, since at the moment of acting I cannot tell which junction I am at. Does this have a standard name and treatment?

A bare name would not satisfy me, because I can already see it sitting near three other things and I want to know which of them it really is. It resembles a commitment device — hiding the cigarettes, tying yourself to the mast — but in those cases you remember the commitment and can tell when it is operative, whereas here the whole difficulty is that I cannot tell whether the moment the rule governs has arrived. It resembles choosing principles without knowing which position in society you will occupy, but that is uncertainty about which person you will be, not about which of your own decisions you are making. And it resembles ordinary decision under uncertainty, except that what I lack is not knowledge of the consequences but knowledge of where in my own sequence I stand.

I have three variants, and I do not know whether they belong to the same phenomenon. First, suppose the junctions are distinguishable, but only by a cue that carries no information about which is the better one to turn at — a red signpost here, a blue one there. Does the structure survive, and does my ability to tell them apart change anything? Second, suppose there are many junctions rather than two, so that the rule must say when, across a long run of indistinguishable moments, to break off. Third, suppose it is not one forgetful walker but a relay of people, each seeing only their own stretch of road and unable to speak to the others, so that the sensible thing for the group can be achieved only if each of them does the locally worse thing. If these are one phenomenon in different clothing, say what the clothing changes; if they come apart, I want to know where the seams are.

So what I am really after is the carving: what the structure is and what it requires — blindness to which moment you are in, an asymmetry between the moments, a rule that must be fixed before you know where you are — what it does not require, and where its edges lie. If nothing established fits exactly, I would rather have a coined term with a definition and a case on either side of the boundary than a near-miss label.
