I am a PhD student in philosophy of science, and I am trying to understand why the debate over Goodman's grue has stalled. The problem is usually put like this: all emeralds examined before some future time t are green, and therefore support the hypothesis that all emeralds are green; but the same evidence also supports the hypothesis that all emeralds are grue, where something is grue just in case it is green if examined before t and blue otherwise. The literature has generated a long list of responses -- entrenchment, natural kinds, projectibility, Bayesian priors, and skeptical or pragmatic deflations -- and yet the parties do not converge. Roughly: entrenchment locates the difference in our linguistic habits, natural-kind views in the metaphysical structure of the world, Bayesian views in the prior, and skeptical views in the absence of a non-circular justification for induction. I have a hypothesis, but I want you to diagnose the disagreement rather than survey the options. My hypothesis is that the debate conflates two questions that I have been calling the ranking question and the entitlement question. The ranking question asks: given the evidence, which hypotheses does it support, and why does the green hypothesis rank above the grue hypothesis? The entitlement question asks: what entitles us to use any inductive ranking rule at all, including one that prefers green? Entrenchment and natural-kind answers look like answers to the ranking question; skeptical and pragmatic answers look like answers to the entitlement question. If that is right, the apparent disagreement is partly a category mistake, and the residual dispute is about whether the ranking question can be answered without presupposing an answer to the entitlement question. If it is not right, say why the two questions cannot be separated, or why some third question is doing the work. Diagnose the stall: is it substantive or verbal, are the sides answering different questions under one heading, or is there a hidden empirical or formal question doing the work? I want a committed diagnosis, not a neutral map, with reasons drawn from the actual structure of the grue problem. Finally, tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis -- a distinction, a formal result, a new case, or a change of question -- or whether no progress should be expected. End with a single line in the form \answer{...} containing a short label for your verdict (e.g., two questions, substantive, verbal, hidden empirical question, or a similarly brief phrase).
