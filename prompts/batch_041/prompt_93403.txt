The Rietdijk–Putnam argument, as I reconstruct it, says that because simultaneity is relative, an event that lies in my future can be simultaneous with my present for some other inertial observer, and since what is real for an observer is real, all events are equally real — so the block universe follows and tense is not objective. Please give me your credence that this argument, granting special relativity, actually establishes its conclusion. Decompose the number: the probability that the relativity of simultaneity genuinely rules out a privileged global present; the probability that the argument is valid conditional on that; the probability that presentism or the A-theory can be reconciled with relativity by some serious move (a privileged foliation, an indexical reading of 'now', a non-realist reading of tense); the probability that the step from 'real for observer O' to 'real simpliciter' is legitimate rather than a smuggled premise; and any further term you think belongs. Give each a number with one line of justification, combine them, then tell me which term your credence is most sensitive to, whether you read the argument as being about the reality of tense or about the ontology of spacetime, and what argument or result would move your number most.
