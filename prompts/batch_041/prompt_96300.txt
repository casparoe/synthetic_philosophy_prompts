Write an essay on whether Schlick's account of the meaning of life can survive the parasitism objection. Schlick basically says the meaning of life isnt found in work, because work is instrumental, aimed at something beyond itself, while play is activity done for its own sake, and so meaning gets tied to youth, where youth isnt an age but a mode of playful, self-justifying activity. The objection I want you to wrestle with is that play and creativity alot of the time depend on a background of instrumental labor that someone has to do. If that labor is meaningless, then the meaningful stuff is either a luxury built on meaninglessness or it secretly borrows its meaning from the labor it looks down on. Weigh that against the reply that Schlick can distinguish work in the economic sense from work in the existential sense, or that instrumental activity can be done in a spirit that makes it intrinsically valuable, or that caregiving and drudgery can be play-like when they are freely taken up. Dont just summarize Schlick. Take a side. Does the parasitism objection sink him, force a revision, or miss the point? Defend a verdict, and be specific about what counts as play, what counts as work, and whether the distinction can do teh work Schlick needs. Also consider whether the view is too individualistic or too aesthetic, and whether it can account for meaning in lives dominated by caregiving or drudgery. Argue from the ideas themselves.
