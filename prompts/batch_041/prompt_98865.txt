I've been arguing with my roomate about how much of everyday etiquette is actually morally required, and I keep landing on opposite answers depending on the case. Here's the pile I've got, with my gut calls on each. Tipping 20% at a US diner where the server's wage is set assuming tips: not leaving anything feels genuinely wrong to me, like stiffing a person who did their job, not just bad manners. But the same diner after the owner switched to a no-tipping, higher-wage model and put up a sign saying tips now go straight to the owner: suddenly I feel nothing at all about leaving zero. A friend visiting from a country with no tipping custom, eating at a normal US diner: I don't think they're obligated the way I am, even though the server is relying on the same tips from the same table. An American tourist in that friend's country leaving cash on the table: I think they shouldn't, it comes off as imposing our rule on someone who never asked for it. Jaywalking across an empty street at 2am, no cars, no cop in sight: totally fine, the light is just a suggestion. Cutting to the front of a crowded bus queue because I'm late: not fine, clearly, even though nobody's hurt in any lasting way. And blocking the walking side of an escalator in a city where walk-left is the norm, so people pile up behind me: feels like almost nothing, forgivable, especially for a visitor, even though I'm literally slowing specific people down.

The two that won't sit together are the queue and the escalator -- both inconvenience real people right behind me, and yet I feel bound by one and not the other -- and the tipping pair pulls in the other direction, since there the convention says tip but my obligation evaporates the moment the money stops reaching the worker. My roomate says it's all just one rule, follow whatever convention the locals follow and be extra lenient with visitors, but that makes my Japan-tipping tourist either rude or fine depending on nothing, and it can't explain why the no-tipping sign changes a thing. I also want it to tell me about a weird one: my friend's building has an unspoken custom that whoever boards the elevator first presses the button for everyone and calls out floors, and guests who don't do it get side-eye. Do I have to?

Can you figure out what principle, or small set of them, actually generates my verdicts, tell me which of my calls it forces me to give up, and predict what it says about the elevator one? Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro and no summing-up at the end.
