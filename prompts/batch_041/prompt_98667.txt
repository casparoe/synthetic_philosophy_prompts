Please take the role of a professional philosopher of mind with expertise in hedonic psychology and the philosophy of well-being, and think carefully before you begin; start by brainstorming the candidate points of agreement and the possible cruxes, then draft the document itself. I am a high school student preparing a joint statement for my philosophy club's adversarial-collaboration project, and I need you to write it. The two parties are a hedonic monist, who holds that all pleasures share a common phenomenal quality, or at least constitute a single natural kind, and a hedonic pluralist, who holds that pleasures are radically heterogeneous and are unified only by the subject's attitude toward them — by being liked, wanted, or welcomed — rather than by any shared feeling. Both are hedonists about well-being: they agree that pleasure is what makes a life go well, so their quarrel is not about value but about what pleasure is. The document should open with a numbered list of claims both sides can sign: what the dispute is really about, what each side concedes about the other's examples, and what any adequate theory of pleasure would have to explain. Then give a disagreements table setting out the precise residual disputes, each phrased in wording its own side would accept, and identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, give a case or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Please use the following case, which I invented, as one of the shared tests: experimenters induce in a subject a state that the subject sincerely reports as pleasant, but which shares no introspectively detectable feature with any other pleasure that subject has ever felt and which is produced by a wholly different mechanism; the parties must say what each of them predicts about whether this state counts as a pleasure. Also have them consider ordinary contrasts such as the pleasure of relief from pain, the pleasure of solving a proof, and the pleasure of a warm bath. Finally, if you judge that part of their disagreement is merely verbal — a quarrel about how to use the word pleasure rather than about the phenomena — say which part and why.
