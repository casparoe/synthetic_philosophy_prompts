A decision theory blog I read had a post on the Death in Damascus problem and I can't work out how much of it to trust. The setup he gives is: you're a merchant in Damascus, Death turns up and tells you he's coming for you tomorrow, and you can either stay in Damascus or get on a camel and go to Aleppo. Death is a perfect predictor -- he worked out in advance which way you'd jump, and he'll be waiting in whichever city that turns out to be. So if you stay he's in Damascus, if you go he's in Aleppo, and there's no city where he isn't. Then the post says:

'This is basically Newcomb's problem in a robe and a scythe, with Death standing in for the predictor. Causal decision theory comes out of it badly: it shrugs and says Death's position is a fixed fact about the world that your leaving Damascus can't causally budge, so both options come out fifty-fifty and you might as well stay where you are. Evidential decision theory does better -- staying in Damascus is evidence that Death is in Damascus, so EDT tells you to get on the camel and go, which is what the merchant in the old story does. Where CDT really falls apart is the ticket: a causal agent will happily pay $1 for a ticket that pays out $11 if it survives, because its own bookkeeping says it's 99.99% likely to live. That's a decision theory you can pump money out of. So Death in Damascus is the case that finishes off CDT, and the honest answer to what the merchant should do is flip a coin -- a decision that's genuinely random is the one thing a predictor can't get ahead of.'

I'm a two-boxer by temperament -- you can't cause a prediction that's already been made -- so I came in expecting to be on CDT's side, but the ticket thing sounds like a real problem, and something about the whole post feels to neat to me. Can you go clause by clause: what a decision theorist would nod at, what they'd wince at, what's just wrong -- and does the closing claim about the coin flip, and about this being the case that finishes off CDT, actually follow from anything true in the rest of it?
