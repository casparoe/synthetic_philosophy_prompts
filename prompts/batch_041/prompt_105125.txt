I have been trying, without success, to find a name for a pattern that keeps turning up in disputes I follow, and I suspect the philosophy of science has a term for it that I simply do not know. Here is the pattern, as concretely as I can put it.

Two camps disagree about something both claim to be settling with evidence. One recent example: whether a certain cognitive effect is a genuine feature of how people reason, or an artifact of the particular task used to measure it. Another: whether a market anomaly reflects a stable behavioral tendency, or a rational response to a risk factor nobody has managed to observe. In each case a new finding appears that neither camp predicted. Within a short time both camps are citing it. Camp A says the finding is just what its account implies, once you add the background assumption about measurement it has relied on all along. Camp B says the same, with its own background assumption. Neither is obviously cheating: grant each camp its background assumptions and the finding really does follow from its view, and really does count in its favor. Each camp then observes that the other's fit is contrived, that it was assembled after the fact, that the other camp would have told an equally smooth story no matter what the data had shown. And each camp is right about the other.

What strikes me is the symmetry and the invisibility. The retrofitting is available to both sides at once, and it is invisible from the inside: from within a framework, an implication you can derive looks like support, and you cannot tell whether you would have derived it before seeing the data. So the dispute swallows an indefinite stream of evidence without either side ever being moved, and yet neither side is merely being stubborn, because the moves each side makes are individually defensible.

I know the phrase 'underdetermination of theory by evidence,' and I know Popper's worry about theories that can absorb anything, but neither seems to match what I am describing. Underdetermination sounds like a fact about the logical relation between a theory and a body of data, something you could point to from the outside; what I am after is the thing that happens in the middle of a live dispute, where each side is genuinely confirmed and each side can see straight through the other. Nor is it the Kuhnian picture of rival paradigms with no common measure: these two camps share all their data and most of their methods, and they keep agreeing on what the evidence is while disagreeing about whose evidence it is.

Is there a name for this? If there is, I want to know how it differs from underdetermination proper, from an ordinary ad hoc hypothesis, and from plain motivated reasoning, since I can already see those are nearby and I do not think any of them is quite it. If there is no name, coin one, and define it so that I can tell when a dispute is of this kind rather than merely difficult, say where its edges are, and say what would have to change for a dispute to escape it. A bare label will not help me; what I want is the carving.
