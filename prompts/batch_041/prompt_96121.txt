Right — I'm setting the paper for our final-year philosophy of physics module (they've had a term of QM, so they know the formalism, roughly) and I need one essay question on whether the wavefunction is real, worth 25 marks, 75 minutes. No references needed, and I don't want them wasting words on who said what.

What I don't want is an invitation to write out PBR and stop. The question should be built around a short claim printed above it — to the effect that since Pusey, Barrett and Rudolph we know the wavefunction is a real physical thing, so the epistemic view of it is finished, and QBism goes with it — and candidates have to unpick the claim rather than restate the theorem. For full marks they should have to: say what the theorem's conclusion actually is, i.e. the disjunction between the state being ontic and the failure of preparation independence, and why that conditional structure matters; show they know what the engine of the argument is (anti-distinguishability) without me rewarding a rehash of the algebra; keep the psi-ontic/psi-complete distinction seperate and use it, Bohm being the obvious case; keep psi-ontic apart from the view that the wavefunction is a physical field in a high-dimensional space; say whether an epistemic reading can be rescued by letting go of preparation independence, and what that costs; and say something sensible about whether the theorem even bites on frameworks that dont posit ontic states at all, which is where the QBism part of the claim lives.

So: the question, a model answer of about 700–800 words, and a marking scheme out of 25 built on five must-hit points with the marks for each, a one-line note on how candidates usually get each one wrong, and separately the two or three errors that should cap someone at a 2:2 however nicely written. Finish the model answer with a couple of sentences on what would change my mind about the scheme — which argument would make me move or reweight one of the must-hit points — and where the answer is most likely to be wrong. British English throughout, please.
