Hey, I'm a second-year student in a human origins and inequality course and there's a pattern I keep running into that I don't have a name for. It goes like this. A whole population shifts to a new way of living that's worse for nearly everyone under it -- shorter lives, worse teeth, more infectious disease, more hours of grinding work for less food -- and it still spreads, and once it's spread you can't really go back, not because anyone's forcing you but because any group that opts out gets outcompeted, absorbed, or shoved onto worse land by the groups that stay in. Farming is the case we did in class, but I keep seeing the same shape elsewhere: everyone I know drifting into working nights and weekends even though none of us thinks it's a good life, or the SUV thing where each individual buyer is a bit safer and everyone ends up worse off. The part that trips me up is that my lecturer keeps reaching for tragedy of the commons and prisoner's dilemma and neither definately fits. There's no shared resource being depleted, and it isn't a one-shot game -- the total pie actually gets bigger while the average person's slice shrinks, and the payoffs themselves keep shifting as more people pile in, so the thing that locks you in is the competition among the people already inside. Is there an established name for this exact structure? If one of the standard ones fits, tell me which and why; if the nearest ones only partly fit, tell me precisely what they miss; if nothing fits, coin a term and give me its edges -- what it is, what it isn't, and what would count as a borderline case. And is reversibility part of the definition or just a common feature? Give it to me as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, no intro and no conclusion.
