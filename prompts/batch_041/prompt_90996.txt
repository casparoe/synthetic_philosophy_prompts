I'm a research assistant on a crustacean welfare project (mostly penaeid shrimp, the farmed prawn kind), and I've been volunteered to open our lab's seminar series with a joke before I do the boring part about welfare indicators. The room is mixed — half aquaculture people who've done time in a hatchery, half animal-welfare people who've never smelled a maturation tank. I want something that lands for the first half and at least makes sense to the second.

So here's the material I actually work on, so you don't have to guess at it. In basically every marine shrimp hatchery in the world, the way you get a female to spawn is to cut off one of her eyestalks; the eyestalk is where the hormone that suppresses ovary development gets made, so removing it — one eye, no anaesthesia, sometimes with a red-hot wire — is the standard trick, and has been since the 70s. There are alternatives (better broodstock feed, changing the male-to-female ratio in the tank) and a couple of big retailers have told their suppliers to go ablation-free by 2027, so the industry is in this weird spot where everybody knows the procedure and nobody wants to say it out loud. Second thing: our slaughter rules are all inherited from vertebrates, where "humane" means two steps — render the animal insentient, then kill it. A crab or a shrimp doesn't have one central switch to flip; the nervous system is more like a chain of ganglia running down the length of the body, so it's genuinely unclear what you'd aim at, or how you'd know you'd hit it. Third: a larval tank holds something like a million animals and the inventory line reads "3 kg." Which means per-individual welfare thinking — the entire grammar of animal welfare — kind of dissolves at the scale we work at.

Write me the joke. Setup and punchline, a few sentences, something I can say out loud in thirty seconds. The punchline should turn on one of those three things: an insider should laugh and be able to point at which one it's riding on, and a non-insider should at least get the shape of it. Please don't hand me a generic one that would work just as well if I'd said chickens instead of shrimp, and nothing that leans on the lobster-screams-in-the-pot thing. Then underneath, a short note for me: where the joke takes liberties with the science, and where it's resting on something actually contested (I'm thinking of whether any of this counts as sentience, and whether ablation is really the worst thing that happens in a tank — but you tell me), because I don't want to be the guy who opens a welfare seminar with a line that turns out to be flatly false. And if one of the three things I described is wrong, or if no good joke can be built on this without being in poor taste, just say so instead of forcing it. I'd rather find out now than in front of the room.
