I've spent too many evenings in the population-ethics threads on LessWrong and the EA Forum, and I want to build a decision tree for a reading group that sorts the positions on the procreation asymmetry — the claim that coming into existence can be bad for the person who comes to exist and can never be good for them.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names: each internal node a question a participant can answer without knowing which view it leads to, with the views located at the leaves only afterwards. Branch on the distinctions that actually do the work: whether comparing existence with nonexistence is well-formed for the person concerned; if it is not, whether what does the work instead is the impersonal badness of suffering, the wrongness of imposing a life, or a non-comparative wrong done to the child; if it is, whether the asymmetry is a claim about value or only about what can be demanded of agents; and whether it is brute, a corollary of the person-affecting restriction that only existing people can be made worse off, or an artifact of treating nonexistence as a welfare level of zero.

Probe the nodes with cases rather than labels: a child whose life is barely worth living versus waiting and creating a different child with a much better life, with no one worse off either way; a happy child at no cost to anyone; a miserable one; a pill that improves an existing person's life; happy people added to a distant island; the slide from mere addition to the repugnant conclusion.

Each leaf should carry the standard name of the view if it has one and a descriptive label if not, one sentence on the commitment that view has the hardest time defending, and a flag where two leaves agree on every probe and differ only in their grounds. Include at least the plain asymmetry, the stronger claim that all procreation is a harm, the denial of the asymmetry in either direction, and the view that keeps the asymmetry while rejecting any comparison between existing and not existing. Note which answer combinations come out empty, and whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
