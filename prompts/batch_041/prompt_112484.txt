I am an undergraduate taking a course on the philosophy of physics, and I have been persuaded by a position I would like you to stress-test. The position is this: the direction of time is fundamentally thermodynamic. There is no independent temporal arrow over and above the entropy gradient; the past is the direction of lower entropy, the future is the direction of higher entropy, and the psychological arrow (memory, anticipation, deliberation) and the causal arrow (causes preceding effects, interventions making a difference) are derivative from, or at least asymmetrically dependent on, that thermodynamic gradient. The fundamental laws are, or may well be, time-reversal invariant; the arrow enters only through a special low-entropy boundary condition at one end of time, often called the past hypothesis. I am not asking for a reading list or citations. I want to know what would count against this position.

Please give me a map of its exposure. What observations, arguments, formal results, or possible worlds would have to obtain for the position to be false? Which assumptions could fail, and what would the world have to look like for each failure? I am especially interested in defeaters that a defender would themselves concede as genuine, not merely what critics allege. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguish those that would refute the position from those that would force a qualification. Does the position predict anything its rivals do not, or has it been insulated by qualifications so that it risks nothing? If it has been insulated, say so plainly and say whether that is a problem for a view of this kind.

To make this concrete, consider some cases that I have been worrying about. First, a universe whose entropy increases away from both ends of time, so that there are two low-entropy boundaries and psychological arrows pointing away from each. Would that refute the thermodynamic account, or can it be accommodated by saying that there are two arrows and no global direction? Second, a world in which entropy increases but records and memories are systematically unreliable in a way that does not track the entropy gradient: does the thermodynamic account have the resources to say that such a world lacks a psychological arrow, or does it thereby show that the psychological arrow is not derivative? Third, if a future theory of quantum gravity made time emergent and the thermodynamic arrow merely approximate, would that count against the position or merely relocate it? Fourth, what should we make of the fact that statistical mechanics itself seems to need a time-asymmetric posit, such as a past hypothesis, to derive the second law? Does that make the thermodynamic arrow explanatory bedrock, or does it show that the position presupposes the very arrow it claims to explain?

I would also like an honest assessment of whether the position is empirically exposed at all, or whether it is the kind of view that can absorb any conceivable evidence. If there are developments in cosmology, statistical mechanics, or the foundations of causation that would genuinely move a committed defender, tell me what they are. If the most persuasive consideration against the view is conceptual rather than empirical, say what it is and why it should or should not worry me. Please answer in British English.
