I recently helped draft a policy on a risk committee — we accepted a measure that imposes a tiny, barely noticeable harm on a very large population in order to spare a small number of people something severe — and the old torture-versus-dust-specks puzzle has stopped being a puzzle for me and become a live question. I want the document that two people who have argued about it for years could actually sign. On one side, someone who holds that harms are commensurable and fully aggregable: enough trivial harms outweigh a torture, and the harm scale contains no discontinuity. On the other, someone who holds that there is a floor: trivial harms, however many, cannot be summed into something that outweighs a serious one. Draft their joint statement as follows: a numbered section of claims both can sign, covering what aggregation is, what the numbers do and do not settle, and which portions of their apparent disagreement are about words; then a table of the residual disagreements, each side's position rendered in wording that side would accept for itself; then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and for each crux, a case both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Two cases should be handled explicitly, because I suspect they split the disagreement rather than merely restating it. First, a version in which all the trivial harms fall on the same person, so that the aggregation runs across moments of one life rather than across lives: does either side change its verdict, and if not, on what grounds? Second, a version with a dial: the device either inflicts a fixed severe suffering on one person or gives each of N people a momentary trivial nuisance, and I want each side to commit to the shape of its verdict as a function of N — a threshold value, a monotone curve with no threshold, a limit never attained, or something else — and to state what would have to be true for that shape to be mistaken. I am not asking who is right, and I do not want citations or reading lists; I want the joint document.
