I've been going round in circles on something with a mate for weeks now and I'm hoping it has a name, because I can't carve it up properly on my own. What I keep running into is this. Take an immune system: it "remembers" measles and makes the right antibody thirty years later, and the reason it makes that particular one rather than some other is that it met measles before. That reason is doing real explanatory work — if you didn't know about the earlier exposure you'd have no idea why it responded that way — and yet you can go through every cell in the body and nowhere is the measles written down. Same with a tree's roots growing towards water, or a bird building a nest it's never seen, or the price of a thing settling where it does without anybody working it out. In each case the behaviour has a point, and the point explains the behaviour, but you can't find the point anywhere in the system. It's in the history — in what got kept and what got thrown away — not in the mechanism.

The trouble is I keep seeing two reactions and I don't like either of them. One is "oh, that's just a metaphor, the tree doesn't want anything, stop anthropomorphising" — but that can't be the whole story, because the water really is why the root grew that way, and that isn't just a manner of speaking, the way it would be if I said the river was trying to reach the sea. The other is "so there must be something inside that represents the water, or the pathogen, or the nest" — and then whatever does the representing has to be read by something else, and off we go, little man inside the little man. My half-formed thought is that the first reaction throws away a real explanation and the second goes looking for it in the wrong place: the reason is real, but it runs backwards relative to how we normally explain someones action, where the reason comes first and the doing comes after. I've a vague memory of a Dennett line about explanations of this sort running in reverse, but I couldn't tell you where, and it wouldn't have answered the question anyway. I'm not an academic, I just read too much of this stuff online.

So: is there a name for this? And more to the point, how do you draw the edges? Where does "the reason is genuinely there, it's just not in the system" stop and "this is just us talking" start — is a thermostat wanting the room warm the same kind of thing, and a river reaching the sea not, and if so what exactly separates them? What are the two mistakes above actually called, if they are mistakes? And does the same distinction apply when it's a machine rather than an organism — a chess engine protecting its queen, or a language model producing something that sounds like it had a reason for saying it? I don't just want the label, I want to know what it rules out.
