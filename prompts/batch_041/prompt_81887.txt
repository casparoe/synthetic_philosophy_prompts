I've been trying to pin down a reading of Machiavelli on fortune and virtù, and I'd like you to stress-test it rather than summarize the secondary literature. The reading is this: virtù in Machiavelli is not primarily courage, skill, or even the fox-and-lion adaptability usually assigned to it. It is the capacity to arrange matters so that fortune has as little left to decide as possible — to convert a situation that hangs on how things fall into one that hangs on choices the prince has already locked in. On this reading, the well-known image of fortune as a violent river that can be banked and dammed is not a metaphor for boldness but the program itself: the point of virtù is to shrink the number of future contingencies one will then have to respond to well. Let me call this the "closing the game" reading.

What I want from you is a map of its exposure. Specifically:

- What would have to be true for the reading to be wrong, and which observations, episodes, or arguments would count against it? Sort the defeaters by how decisive they'd be, from the ones that would sink the reading to the ones that would only make it awkward.
- Machiavelli's own narratives are the first place to look. Where do his successes and failures strain the reading? A prince who does everything right and is undone anyway cuts in one direction; a prince who wins by improvising at the last moment cuts in another. Say which episodes bite and how hard.
- There is an internal distinction the reading seems to need and may not be able to keep: between reducing fortune's role by removing contingencies in advance and reducing it by accumulating the wherewithal to absorb whatever fortune sends (one's own arms rather than mercenaries, reserves rather than dependence). If the reading cannot separate these, does it survive? If it can, on what basis?
- Finally, be honest about whether the reading risks becoming unfalsifiable. If every act of successful improvisation can be redescribed as a disposition-to-improvise that was already in place, has the reading been qualified into immunity — and is that a fatal problem for a reading of this kind, or an acceptable cost?

I'd rather you tell me plainly if the reading is confused at the root, or if the question as I've posed it can't be answered well, than manufacture a tidy set of defeaters to keep me happy. If the position turns out to be well insulated, say so, and say whether that insulation is a vice or just what a reading of this sort looks like.
