I keep getting stuck trying to explain to a friend why I believe anything at all — every reason I give leans on some other belief, and when she just says 'why?' one more time I've got nothing left. I read there's basically three ways people get out of this: stop somewhere and say that one just stands on its own, or let the reasons lean on each other in a circle, or admit it just goes on forever. I want a poem that is the problem itself, not a poem about it. Say 15 to 20 lines, no rhyme needed, but heres the shape I'm after: each line is the reason for the line above it, so it only really makes sense read from the bottom up. The bottom line is the foundation, the one that's supposed to stop everything, and I want it to feel like a cheat when I finally get there, like the poet ran out of breath and stamped her foot. So instead of committing to one, give me three different possible versions of that bottom line at the end: one that stops by fiat, one that loops back so the first line is the reason for the last, and one that just trails off like it could keep going forever. Then tell me which one you'd actually end the poem on, and why. Someone whos sat with this should feel all three exits in the thing, and someone who hasn't should still feel why it's maddening.
