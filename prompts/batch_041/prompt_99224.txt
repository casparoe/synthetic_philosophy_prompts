I've just started a PhD in history and philosophy of science, and my supervisor is a proper Lakatos person — everything gets sorted into 'progressive' or 'degenerating' — and honestly I can't work out what rule she's actually running when she says it. In reading group this term she's pronounced on a whole string of cases, and they don't obviously reduce to one criterion. Copernicus she calls progressive, and when someone objected that the system was still a mess in the 1540s she said yes, but the phases of Venus settled it — it called something nobody had seen. The late Ptolemaic epicycles she calls degenerating, just patching to save the phenomena. Bohr's 1913 atom she calls progressive even though she cheerfully admits the stationary orbits were bolted on and clashed with classical electrodynamics, on the grounds that it named the Balmer lines in advance. Phlogiston after the chemical revolution, degenerating. And then last week a programme that had only just welded two previously seperate domains into one framework, with no new prediction tested yet, she called progressive already — because she said the belt was growing in a content-increasing direction rather than just absorbing whatever got thrown at it. I genuinely can't tell whether the load-bearing thing for her is novelty of prediction, non-ad-hocness of the latest move, or the overall trend over time.

So I've built a case for my next seminar and I want to guess her verdict before I stand up. It's a toy programme I've constructed, but it's structurally realistic: it has spent roughly twelve years doing nothing but patching — a string of failed predictions, each one absorbed by yet another tweak to the protective belt — and then last month it added a new auxiliary hypothesis that is genuinely not ad hoc (it has independent testable consequences nobody was even looking for) and that also makes a novel prediction which won't be testable for another five years. Would she call this thing progressive or degenerating? I don't just want the verdict — I want the candidate rules that fit everything she's said so far, what each of them predicts about my case, which one she's most likely actually running and why, and the single further case that would tell those rules apart.

Give it back to me as a JSON object with exactly these fields: "verdict" (one of "progressive", "degenerating", "genuinely underdetermined"), "candidate_rules" (an array of objects, each with "rule" — a one-line statement of the criterion — and "prediction" — what that rule says about my toy programme), "preferred_rule" (the name/one-line label of the rule you think she's running), "why_preferred" (two or three sentences), "discriminating_case" (the further case, described concretely, that would separate the surviving rules), and "confidence" ("low", "medium" or "high").
