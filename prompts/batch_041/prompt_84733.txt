My reading group has been going through the Euthyphro stuff — whether God's commanding something is what makes it good, or whether he commands it because it was good already — and my friend Priya, who's a jurisprudence person, says the whole dilemma is a party trick that only looks scary if you've never thought about law. Her line is that divine command theory is just legal positivism applied to morals. God is the sovereign, his commands are the enacted statute, and the first horn (the good is whatever he says) is exactly the positivist thesis that a norm counts as law because of where it came from rather than because of what it says — the seperability of law and morality. Nobody thinks positivism gets refuted by pointing out that a legal system can enact monstrous laws; that's just a feature of the view. So the arbitrariness objection to divine command theory is the same objection, and it should die the same death: his commands obligate because he issued them, and whether they're nice is a separate question. The second horn, she says, is worse for the theist anyway, since it makes God a middleman and hands the standard off to something that isn't him. So: take the first horn, stop worrying. She says all this with a lot of confidance and my instinct is that she's buying a conclusion on the cheap, but I can't put my finger on where the analogy leaks.
