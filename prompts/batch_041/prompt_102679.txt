I'm writing the final for my upper-level seminar on markets and democratic governance, and the last unit is futarchy, so I need one essay question plus a model answer and a marking scheme.

I dont want a question they can answer by reciting the slogan (we vote on values, bet on beliefs) and then listing the usual objections -- manipulation, Goodhart's law, thin markets. The thing I actually want to force is the counterfactual problem, which I suspect most of them won't have thought about even if they read the proposal. Roughly: a decision market is supposed to tell you how some welfare metric would move *if* a policy were adopted, but in the world you only ever get to observe one branch, and the branch you observe is the one you chose. So the number the whole scheme turns on -- the difference between the two conditional forecasts -- looks to me half unobservable and, as far as I can tell, never actually scored. I want them to work out whether that kills the scheme or whether committing to the decision rule in advance rescues it.

Concretely, give them a setup: a city is deciding between policy A and a status quo B, and runs a conditional prediction market that resolves on a welfare metric measured a few years later, with the rule fixed in advance (adopt A if the market says A scores higher). Then the question should make them (a) say exactly what the market is pricing and why that's supposed to be informative, (b) locate precisely where the counterfactual problem bites and whether the advance commitment to the rule fixes it or just relocates it, and (c) say whether the standard "manipulators get arbitraged away" defense survives once they see the problem. A good answer should also say what would have to be true for the market to be informative at all -- I don't want them just declaring it broken.

Model answer around 500-600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 that names the specific moves separating an A from a B -- e.g. I'd expect an A to distinguish the conditioning/self-reference problem from the manipulation problem, and to say something concrete about what it would take to measure the counterfactual (randomization? a matched control? holding the decision rule fixed but varying something else?). Also have the model answer say how confident it is in its claims and flag the one point it's least sure of. Keep the whole thing under about 950 words.
