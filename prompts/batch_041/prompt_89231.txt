Write a dialogue between two philosophers of commemoration. The first holds that to commemorate someone is to perform a public act of endorsement: a statue, a street name, or a holiday says, in effect, that this person's life merits honor. The second holds that commemoration is better understood as a ritual through which a community constitutes and reproduces its collective identity; endorsement is neither necessary nor sufficient, and the ritual can be self-critical. They are debating a proposal in the invented city of Alder: the city council wants to remove a statue of a colonial governor and replace it with an empty plinth and an audio guide that plays conflicting testimony from descendants, including some who want the statue gone and some who want it kept. The first argues that the proposal is not commemoration at all, because an empty plinth cannot endorse anyone; the second argues that it is the most honest commemoration the city could adopt, because it makes the community's division part of the ritual. Let them press each other on whether an empty plinth can carry the same illocutionary force as a statue, whether a community can constitute itself through an act that refuses a single message, and what would count as the proposal's success or failure. Both should be equally competent and responsive; no strawmen. Structure the response as: Setting (one short paragraph), Dialogue (about 900 words), Unresolved question (one short paragraph).
