Every year in my Indian epistemology seminar I try to get across that the pramāṇa lists arent just inert taxonomy — deciding which sources of knowledge get to count is a political act, and the fight between Nyāya and Mīmāṃsā over whether there's a fifth (postulation, arthāpatti) is a real fight, not trivia. This year I want a satirical writing exercise instead of another essay.

So here's the setup. Imagine a mid-size company that has adopted, as official knowledge policy, the Nyāya four — perception, inference, comparison/analogy, and verbal testimony — and nothing else. Claims the org acts on have to trace back to one of those four. There's an epistemics department, a form per pramāṇa, an appeals process, the whole bureaucratic aparatus. A couple of employees keep filing under arthāpatti (postulation), which isn't on the approved list — the inference desk insists it's just inference wearing a hat — and the analogy desk is basically a punchline nobody respects. The org also has an official theory of error: error is misattribution, taking x to be y, rather than perceiving some non-existent thing. Nobody's read the memo on that one.

Write the onboarding handbook excerpt that new hires get on day one. Deadpan, with headed sections, around 650 words. The humor has to come from the material itself — the conditions each pramāṇa has to satisfy, why testimony is the philosophically loaded one (who counts as a reliable speaker, what the org does with hearsay), what the org does with doubt as opposed to error, and the postulation people being treated as cranks. Don't let it collapse into generic "corporations are dumb"; someone who knows this debate should definately recognize that whoever wrote it gets the dispute. And don't wink at the reader.

Begin with the handbook itself, no preface or commentary.
