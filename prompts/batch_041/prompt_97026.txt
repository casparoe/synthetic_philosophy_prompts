I am a computational physicist who spends most of my time building lattice-gas and cellular-automaton models of active matter. My working vocabulary is microstates and macrostates, update rules, coarse-graining, order parameters, effective dynamics, causal interventions, and the distinction between a fundamental description and an effective one. In a reading group on philosophy of mind we recently discussed a thought experiment about a careenium: a very large collection of tiny simulated billiard balls, called simmballs, that move and collide according to simple local rules. Over time, stable and repeatable collision patterns appear within the careenium, and these patterns can be described as symbols that seem to carry information about the careenium’s environment. The question that divided the group was whether those higher-level patterns—the symbols—can be said to push the simmballs around, or whether all of the pushing is done at the level of the individual simmballs and the symbols are merely along for the ride. I would like you to restate that debate, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually use. Assume I am fluent in the computational-physics terms above but have no real background in the philosophy-of-mind literature.

Then give me a residue report. When a symbol appears to cause a change in the simmballs, what is the best translation into my vocabulary: an intervention on a macro-variable that leaves the micro-dynamics untouched, a constraint on the space of micro-trajectories, a case of multiple realizability, or something else? Which parts of the claim that symbols push the substrate around translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and what exactly gets distorted in each case? Which parts have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary—for instance, if the debate depends on a symbol being about something, or on its being correct or incorrect, or on its having normative force, say so, and explain why coarse-graining and effective dynamics cannot express that. Finally, tell me whether the residue is a defect of the computational-physics vocabulary, something it would need to add in order to engage the careenium debate, or a sign that the original debate is less clear or less substantive than it first appears. I am not asking for citations, a bibliography, or a reading list; I want the translation and the residue.
