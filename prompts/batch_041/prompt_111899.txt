Im putting together a 2-page research proposal for a small workshop I'm trying to organize — mostly grad students, half philosophy half CS — and I want to pitch it on the problem of logical omniscience. Heres my half-baked idea and I'd like you to turn it into the actual proposal.

The standard complaint is that possible-worlds models (and structured-proposition models, and probabilistic ones) make agents logically omniscient, and the standard fixes are to bolt on impossible worlds, or awareness sets, or resource/complexity bounds, or else to just say the omniscience is a harmless idealization. I think all of these treat a symptom. My hunch: the real culprit is that we model 'believing P' as a relation between an agent and a *proposition*. But what an agent is actually related to is a way of being given P — a presentation, a format, a mental file — and two presentations of the same proposition can be arbitrarily hard or easy to connect to each other. If that's right, then logical omniscience isn't a bug in possible-worlds semantics specifically; it's a bug in propositionalism as such, and it should reappear, in different clothes, in every framework that takes content to be a proposition, structured or not. That would explain why each patch handles a few cases and then leaks.

So write me the proposal. I need (a) the question stated so it has a determinate answer rather than just a vibe — something like 'does logical omniscience follow from propositionalism on its own, or only from specific extra assumptions we could drop?' — (b) why the existing responses (impossible worlds, awareness, bounded resources) leave that question open instead of settling it, (c) what I'd actually do, i.e. a concrete method: say a small formal model, or a couple of worked cases where a propositionalist framework still generates omniscience even after you've applied the usual patches, (d) what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing — I'd rather find out now if the thesis is unfalsifiable — and (e) a scope that fits about three months part-time.

Don't bother with references, I'll add those myself. And if you think the hunch is just wrong, or is already somebody's point, say so plainly — I'd rather the proposal be honest about that than oversell it.
