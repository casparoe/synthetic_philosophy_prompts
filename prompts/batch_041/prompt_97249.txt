I'm on the board of a housing co-op, about 120 units that we collectively own, and we're about to vote on a twenty-year plan: a big loan to retrofit the building, which will raise everyone's monthly charges for years and lock in a layout we'll all have to live with. The fight isn't really about the plan, it's about who gets to vote on it. Right now only people who hold a share (current residents who bought in) can vote, but that seems arbitary to some of us. One board member says anyone whose life will be seriously affected should get a say — the people who rent from shareholder-members and actually live here, the guy who runs the ground-floor shop and depends on our foot traffic, even the neighbors on the block who'll have scaffolding and noise outside for two years. Another says no: only people who are actually bound by the rules we're voting on should vote, otherwise you let outsiders with no intention of staying stack the outcome. Then there's the future-member problem — whoever buys in three years gets stuck paying off this loan but obviously can't vote now. And someone floated weighting votes by how many years you expect to stay, so a recent buyer who plans to die here counts for more than someone selling next spring. Is there a principled way to decide who's in and who's out here, or is it just a line we pick and live with? I don't want "make sure everyone feels heard" — I want to know how you'd draw the line and how you'd defend it to the people left on the wrong side of it.
