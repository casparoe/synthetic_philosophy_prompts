Setup is Dennett's multiple drafts model: no Cartesian theater, no finish line where contents get displayed to an inner viewer, a content counts as conscious by being probed and made 'famous in the brain', and — the part that matters here — there's no fact of the matter about exactly *when* a content became conscious, since that gets settled by later probing. That's supposedly why the Orwellian vs Stalinesque distinction is bogus. Fine, I buy the gist. Here's his argument:

(1) A state is conscious only if there's a determinate fact about what it's like to be in it.

(3) If there's no fact of the matter about when a content becomes conscious, then there's no fact of the matter about whether it becomes conscious at all.
(4) So on multiple drafts there's no determinate fact about what it's like to be in any state.
(5) So by (1), no state is conscious — the model is eliminativism about consciousness in disguise.
(6) And if no state is conscious, then the reports and judgments the model leans on aren't about anything, so it saws off the branch it's sitting on. It proves too much.
