I'm a backend engineer at a mid-sized software company, and I have no philosophy training — I studied computer science and haven't taken a course in ethics since high school. For the last year I've set aside a fixed portion of my salary to give away, and I've been stuck on how to divide it between two programs. The first funds a surgical intervention that permanently cures a specific congenital condition in a small number of children; while it lasts, the pain is the kind that dominates everything else in a person's life. The second funds a much broader program that gives large numbers of people modest, everyday improvements — better sleep, somewhat less anxiety, a bit more energy. When I laid this out to a close friend who's finishing a master's in public policy, she said the choice isn't really a tradeoff at all: she claims that any amount of the first kind of suffering outweighs any amount of the second kind of good, no matter how many people are on the receiving end, so the first program wins by default. But then she added — and this is where she lost me — that this only holds above some severity line. Below that line, she said, the good and the bad really can be added up and traded off against each other like ordinary quantities. She talks as though she's stated one clean rule, but it sounds to me like two rules glued together, and I can't tell whether the glue is principled or arbitrary.

I'd like you to help me work out whether her position actually holds together and, if it does, whether it's one I should adopt. Concretely: does it make sense to say that pleasure and pain are sometimes weighable against each other and sometimes not, and if so, what has to be true about them for that to work at all? If suffering above the line really does swamp any amount of lesser good, does that commit her to anything strange — say, about how many people's mild misery it would take to cancel out one person's agony, or about whether we should care at all how the lesser goods are spread around? And if the two-part rule turns out to be unstable, what's the most defensible single rule in the vicinity, one that still takes severe suffering seriously without treating the milder stuff as worth nothing? I'd rather you reason it through with me than just hand me a name for her view.

Please be explicit about how confident you are at each step, and flag the single claim you're least sure of — I'd rather have an honest map of your uncertainty than a tidy answer.
