I write about energy and ideas, and I keep running into a move in the nuclear debate that I would like you to audit. Ask whether Germany's phase-out was a mistake, or whether France should keep its fleet, and the answer often arrives from nuclear advocates as a portfolio argument: an electricity system is like an investment portfolio, so you don't put all your eggs in one basket — wind and solar are one asset class, nuclear another, and a serious system hedges by holding several, which is supposed to be the lesson of Germany's exit on the one side and of French prudence on the other. I am not sure how much of the argument the metaphor is carrying, particularly since the hedge did not seem to behave as advertised in the winter when a large share of France's reactors went offline at once. Please set out the correspondence as explicitly as you can — what plays the role of the assets, the returns, the volatility, the correlations, the investor's risk appetite, the rebalancing, the time horizon — then tell me where the mapping breaks, what the metaphor quietly assumes about electricity systems that may not be true of them, which of those breaks matter for the conclusion about the two countries and which are idle, and whether the diversification case for keeping nuclear in the mix survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If some other comparison would carry the argument better, tell me what it is.
