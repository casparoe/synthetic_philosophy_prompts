I'm about two hundred pages into a novel and I've hit the scene I've been dreading: my main character has to pick between two things, and I dont want it to be a tie, and I don't want one of them to be obviously better either. What I actually want is for the two options to be genuinely incomparable — not equal, incomparable — and every version I write collapses back into one of those two boring shapes.

Here's the machinery I'm working with, tell me if I've got it wrong: two things can be incommensurable, meaning there's no common unit of value you could measure both with, without yet being incomparable in the strong sense. And there's a test I half-remember from somewhere: take one option and sweeten it a little — and the sweetened version still isn't better than the other one — then the two originals weren't equal, they're not comparable at all. I want a case where that test comes out that way.

So build me the scenario. Constraints: contemporary, ordinary life, no trolleys, no ticking clocks, no hospital beds, and both options have to be things a real person could want for their own sake. Don't give me the cheap version where the two things are obviously different kinds of thing (career vs. friendship) — they should look like they're on the same scale at first glance and the incomparability should still bite. No third option that dissolves it, and no common currency (money, health, years of life) quietly converting one into the other.

Then walk me through it: why the sweetening test lands where it does, which feature of the case is doing the work, what happens if I take that feature out, and what someone who thinks all values are ultimately comparable on a single scale would say to deflate the whole thing — because that's the objection my writing group is going to make. And please don't just have the narration assert that the two things are incomparable; it has to fall out of what they are.
