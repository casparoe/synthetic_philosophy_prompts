ok so I'm a novelist — about 70k words into this book about a tribunal, a committee of seven that goes case by case through a list of defendants and has to decide, for each one, (i) did they actually do the thing, (ii) was the thing ordered from higher up, and (iii) are they therefore guilty. And I've realised the whole plot turns on a question I definately don't have straight, so before I write myself into a hole I want a sanity check on what I actually think.

Here's my set:

1. A group can really believe things. The committee's verdict is the committee's, not just a tally of what the seven of them happen to think.
2. The fair way to get from the members' views to the group's is to majority-vote each question separately.
3. To count as rational at all, a set of beliefs has to be consistent — you can't hold P, and if P then Q, and not-Q.
4. I know enough to know that voting each question separately can land the group in exactly that: affirming P, affirming if P then Q, and denying Q — even when every single member is perfectly consistent on their own.
5. When it lands there, my instinct is that the committee hasn't reached a verdict at all. Not a wrong verdict — no verdict. There's nothing there to be right or wrong about, it's just incoherent noise in the shape of a decision.
6. And a legitimate verdict, for me, has to be one the members could own — nobody should be bound by a finding that not one of them actually holds.

So which of these can I keep? My gut says two of them can't both be true and I can't tell which one I'm really willing to give up, cause each of them is doing work in the book. Rank whatever's in tension by how bad it is, tell me the cheapest way out and what it'd cost me. And if some of these only look like they clash, please say so — I'd rather not gut a belief for nothing.

Oh, and one more thing: is 1 even doing anything next to 2? Half the time I think the group's belief is a real thing sitting there, and the other half I think it's just whatever the vote spits out. If that's a real tension tell me; if it's me being muddled, tell me that too.
