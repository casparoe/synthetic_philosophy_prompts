I've somehow ended up as the person deciding how a small foundation spends about £1.5m over three years on "analogy as the core of cognition" — basically the Hofstadter-and-Sander picture, the idea that analogy isn't a party trick but the engine of thought, that categorisation, memory retrieval, problem-solving and even perception are all analogy underneath. I'm not a cognitive scientist; I did a philosophy degree, read Surfaces and Essences on a beach one summer and got a bit evangelical about it, and now I have to justify a portfolio to a board who mostly want to know what we'd actually be buying. I dont want a literature review or a reading list — I want you to tell me which open questions in this programme are genuinely worth money, in order, and why.

To make it concrete, here's the mess I'm staring at. A colleague I trust keeps saying the field is stuck on retrieval and has barely touched what she calls "generative analogy" — the case where you build a new abstraction on the fly because there's simply no ready-made source concept to map from — and she reckons that's where the real cognitive action is, not the "this is like that" examples everyone trots out in talks. Someone else insists the whole thing founders on measurement: we've got no agreed way to say one analogy is deeper or better than another except by shrugging and asking a human, so every empirical claim is soft. And then there's the nagging worry about whether the current crop of large language models, which seem to do something that looks like analogy by gliding around in a vector space, either vindicates the programme or quietly shows that the interesting human capacity is something else entirely.

I'd like your prioritisation to weigh how upstream each question is (how many other things get easier if it's settled), how tractable it is for a small interdisciplinary team rather than a giant lab, how neglected it is, and roughly what kind of result over the next three years would move it up or down my list. Also tell me which of the things people get excited about in this area I should politely ignore, because their answer wouldn't change anything downstream. Feel free to add questions I haven't listed if they deserve to outrank mine — just show me the reasoning, not a survey.
