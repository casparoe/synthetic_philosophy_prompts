I sit on the collections ethics committee of a mid-sized museum, and before the next round of cases I want the specification rather than the theory: what must any adequate theory of the value of originals deliver? By that I mean an account of why an authentic object should be worth more, or worth something different in kind, than a perfect copy of it. Give me the requirements list.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that generates it, and say whether it is a genuine constraint on any view or merely a preference of one camp. Two things matter most to me. First, flag any subset of the requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied; if some pair pulls in opposite directions, that is itself the result I need to report, so do not smooth it over. Second, name the single requirement that does the most to eliminate candidate families.

The purpose fixes the standard of adequacy. I have to advise on acquisition and deaccession, on whether to insure an original above a replica, on restoration and on the display of replicas, on whether and how to label a copy, and on restitution claims. A view that settles the metaphysics and leaves the registrar with nothing to do is not adequate for me; a view that buys crisp verdicts by collapsing the distinctions listed below is worse.

The list should cover at least these cases:

- A duplicate of a painting identical in every respect any instrument can detect, including the isotope ratios that normally date canvas and pigment. Stipulate a replicator, not a forger: nothing about the object betrays it.
- A forgery that is not merely as good as the original but better — a finer composition, executed with more skill, in the same period, from the same materials.
- A print pulled from a plate the artist reworked a decade after the first impressions were taken, and an authorized posthumous cast made from a mold the artist left behind.
- A Roman marble copy of a lost Greek bronze: it fails as the work it copies and succeeds as an original in its own right.
- An artist's own late replica of an early work of hers, lost and known to her only from photographs.
- A work that exists only as a file, with no privileged token: every copy is a copy, and none is the original.
- An original now reduced to a ruin, exhibited beside a copy that shows what it once looked like; and a restored original that is by now half reconstruction.
- A fabricated natural-history specimen, and a cast taken from a genuine fossil.
- An object whose provenance includes both the artist's hand and a documented wartime looting.

I expect these to force positions on at least the following: whether the value attaches to the object or to its history; whether it is intrinsic or relational; whether authenticity admits of degrees; whether it is the same kind of value as aesthetic value or a different kind altogether; whether the market premium is evidence for the value or a debunking of it; and whether a history of wrongdoing is value-bearing in the way a history of authorship is.

Two constraints I will not budge on. The theory must be consistent with the fact that replication is now effectively free and perceptually perfect for most media while originals keep their premiums. And it must be consistent with the fact that people report the same experience of a work after learning that it is a copy while their valuation of it collapses; whatever the theory says the value is, it cannot treat that gap as mere error.

Distinctions I do not want collapsed: between the value of a work and the value of a token of it; between deception of a buyer and the wrong done to an artist, an audience, or a practice; between a copy and a restoration; between authenticity as a property of an object and authenticity as a relation to a community.

End with one line each on how the causal-history view, the experiential view, and the debunking view that treats the premium as pure positional signalling fare against the list, naming for each the requirement that does it in. Keep the weight on the requirements and their cases; I will run the scorecard myself.
