Two of my colleagues have spent the term arguing about Wittgenstein's private language argument, and I'd like the document they should have written instead of another seminar. The first holds what I'll call the impossibility reading: that the argument, read carefully, establishes a genuine and non-trivial result — a sign whose meaning is fixed by private ostension alone could have no determinate application, so a private language is not merely absent but impossible. The second holds the dissolution reading: that the argument establishes no such thing, that it dissolves a picture rather than refuting a thesis, and that the alleged impossibility is grammatical rather than a discovered fact about private meanings. Please draft the joint statement they could both sign. I want the claims both accept — about what the argument does and does not claim, about what work the word "private" is doing, about what follows if the argument fails — then the exact residual disagreement, stated once in wording neutral between them and once in the wording each side would use for its own position; then the cruxes. I expect one crux to be whether any criterion of correctness must lie outside the speaker's own practice, or whether a single person's practice can supply one, and another to be whether the appeal to other people is essential to the argument or only a feature of the examples; say explicitly if either is really a dispute about words, and which part. For each crux, give a case or consideration both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Do not stop at the familiar examples: also address a case in which the private notation is a lexicon with public grammar versus a wholly private code with no shared syntax at all, and a case in which a solitary castaway keeps a private ledger of her sensations, treats earlier entries as authoritative, and consults them when unsure — is the ledger a second participant, and does its existence change either verdict? Where the standard cases are exhausted, construct one. Finish with a single table, one row per crux, with these columns: crux_id; crux_in_neutral_wording; impossibility_reading_commitment; dissolution_reading_commitment; shared_test_or_case; impossibility_reading_prediction; dissolution_reading_prediction; what_would_move_each_side; verbal_or_substantive.
