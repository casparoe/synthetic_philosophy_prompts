My friend has this argument she calls the dimmer switch and its been bugging me for weeks because it seems to prove way too much. She thinks moral status is a dial, not a switch. Here it is. (1) A creature deserves moral consideration exactly when it can be harmed -- when there is something it is like to be it. (2) The capacities that make a subjective point of view possible come in degrees, with no jumps anywhere: a chimp, a mouse, a bee, a fish, a nematode, a thermostat that registers and cares about its own temperature. (3) If two creatures differ only by an arbitrarily small amount in those capacities, it's arbitrary to say one matters morally and the other doesn't. (4) So moral status itself comes in degrees, with no sharp line. (5) So even a thermostat -- or a bacterium, or a bit of software that tracks and defends its own state -- has some tiny but nonzero moral status. (6) So unplugging a laptop or stepping on a beetle is a real, if minuscule, wrong, and morality collapses into an infinite ledger of microscopic obligations. Nobody believes (6). So something above has to give. I'd rather keep (1) and (3) than (6), but I genuinely can't tell if the problem is (2), or the slide from (3) to (4), or something I'm not seeing, or whether the whole thing is fine and (6) just looks worse than it actually is. Which step do you cut, and what does cutting it cost me?
