I keep hitting the same snag in the "low impact AI" / "avoid side effects" stuff and I want to know wether the snag itself has a name. The setup is simple: you want an agent that does the thing you asked for without disturbing anything else along the way -- fetch me the coffee, dont knock the vase over on the way. What I can't get past is that "anything else" is only defined relative to what you asked for. Send the robot for coffee and moving the chairs is a side effect; tell it to tidy the room and fetching the coffee is the side effect. So which disturbances count depends on where you draw the line between the task and its surroundings, and tiny rewordings of the task move things back and forth across that line. And if the only non-arbitrary place to draw the line is "whatever I actually care about", then "avoid side effects" seems to quietly presuppose that I've already handed you a complete account of what I care about -- in which case the instruction is doing no work, because you'd just do the right thing anyway.

Is there an established name for this exact bind, specifically the part where what counts as a side effect is hostage to a task-or-value specification that the instruction was supposed to let you avoid needing? I've seen "side-effect problem" and "impact measure" thrown around but I don't know if either of them is this thing. If nothing established fits, coin something and show me its edges -- I'm not after a bare label, I want to know what's inside it and what isn't. Also tell me how it relates to the two neighbours it smells like: the old intended-versus-foreseen business in double effect, and that experiment where people happily say the boss harmed the river intentionally but didn't intentionally help it.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion.
