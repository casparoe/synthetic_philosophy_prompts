You are a philosopher of probability and decision theory, with a specialism in Bayesian epistemology and the dynamics of belief. I want you to help me set a research agenda.

Consider the Judy Benjamin problem. Judy is dropped into a territory divided into Red and Blue, and also into Second Company and Headquarters. She assigns equal prior probability to the four quadrants: Red-and-Second-Company, Red-and-Headquarters, Blue-and-Second-Company, Blue-and-Headquarters. She then receives a radio message that she cannot tell whether she is in the Red area or in the Second Company area. She interprets this as learning the conditional: if she is not in Red, she is in Second Company. The question is how she should update her probabilities. This is a case of updating on a conditional whose antecedent is uncertain, and it has generated a literature on whether to conditionalise on the material implication, use Jeffrey conditioning, minimise relative information, or adopt some other rule. Different rules seem to give different answers, and it is contested whether the problem is well-posed.

I want you to treat this problem not as a puzzle to be solved in one go, but as the centre of a research area: updating on conditionals, learning conditional information, and the foundations of probability kinematics. Your task is to identify the most important open questions, implications, and lines of work in this area, and to prioritise them. Assume the prioritisation is for a philosopher with a strong background in probability theory and a few years to devote to a book or a series of papers. The aim is not to survey everything, but to produce a defended agenda: which questions are upstream of which, which are tractable, which are neglected, and which would change the most downstream work if answered.

Before you begin, brainstorm. List the candidate questions you see, including ones that are not usually grouped under the Judy Benjamin heading. Then think carefully about the dependencies between them. Then write your answer.

In your answer, I want you to:
- Explain, for each question you prioritise, why it matters, what makes it tractable or intractable, and what would move it up or down your ranking.
- Say which questions you would deprioritise, and why.
- Discuss the relationship between the Judy Benjamin problem and adjacent problems: updating on indicative versus subjunctive conditionals, the status of conditional probability as a primitive, the problem of old evidence, the Sleeping Beauty problem, causal versus evidential updating, and the role of information minimisation in statistics and machine learning.
- Be explicit about the criteria you are using (importance, tractability, neglectedness, how many other questions depend on this one) and the trade-offs between them.
- Consider whether the Judy Benjamin problem is a genuine problem or an artefact of a particular formal framework, and what follows for the agenda if it is the latter.
- End with a concrete recommendation: if you had to pick two or three questions to work on first, which would they be, and what would a successful research programme on them look like over the next few years?

Write in American English. Be rigorous and specific, but avoid bibliographies, reading lists, and citations; I am interested in your reasoning, not in a list of sources. Do not assume the reader has access to the internet.
