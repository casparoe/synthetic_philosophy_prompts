Give me the specification for a theory of rational choice under indifference before I commit to one: what any adequate account must deliver — the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the constraints it must respect — with the case or consideration behind each requirement. The cases I will judge it by, at minimum: an agent with no preference between two equal options who takes neither and loses everything; the same agent who settles it by flipping a coin; an agent indifferent between two options equal in value but unlike in kind; an agent indifferent between A and B who can first take one of two pills, one of which will make her prefer A and the other of which will make her prefer B, and who knows this in advance; and a tie-breaker choosing on behalf of others, so that the arbitrariness produces winners and losers. Two distinctions of mine must survive: settled indifference (I have deliberated and found no difference) versus unsettled indifference (I have not looked), and having no reason to prefer either option versus having a reason to be indifferent. Tell me which requirements are genuine desiderata and which are the prejudices of one camp, and flag any pair you think cannot be jointly satisfied — that is the part I most want. Then identify the single requirement that excludes the most candidate theories.

Format the answer in American English with these sections and rough lengths: The Requirements — each stated, with the case behind it (about 600 words); Genuine or Borrowed — which demands are real desiderata and which are camp prejudices (about 150 words); The Collision — the requirements that cannot both be met, and what follows (about 250 words); The Most Exclusive Requirement — what it rules out (about 150 words). No citations and no names of theorists; I want the requirements themselves.
