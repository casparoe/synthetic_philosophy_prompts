I am trying to decide whether the fence argument has any conservative content of its own, or whether it is only a presumption in favour of whatever happens to be established. The case is meant to settle that, so a case that works by making one side ill-informed, or by making the merits of the two arrangements obvious, will not count.

Construct a case, in one paragraph, set in a realistic contemporary institution — no exotic technology, and no institution whose merits are settled by a plain question of justice — in which the fence argument and a straightforward cost–benefit calculation part company in this direction: the fence argument comes out in favour of keeping a recent reform, and the calculation comes out in favour of restoring the arrangement that the reform displaced. Take the fence argument in the form that a proposal to change an established arrangement carries the burden of proof, because the established arrangement has been lived with and observed, while the consequences of changing it can only be conjectured, and conjecture does not discharge the burden. Both sides must work from the same facts; the reform must have been in force long enough for its workings to be observed rather than predicted; and the case must not rely on the reform having acquired the kind of unspoken authority that the argument was framed to protect.

Then do the check. State what each side concludes and which premise carries it there. Identify the single feature of the case that makes the fence argument switch sides, say what becomes of both verdicts if that feature is removed while everything else is held fixed, and say whether the case shows the fence argument to be empty of conservative content or only shows that one formulation of it is. Finish with the strongest reply open to a Burkean who denies that the presumption transfers to a self-conscious reform — a reply from prescription and inherited practice, or from the thought that the argument presupposes knowledge we no longer possess — and whether the case survives that reply. Write in British English.
