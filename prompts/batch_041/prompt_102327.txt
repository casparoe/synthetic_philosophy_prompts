I'm putting together a seminar on where moral values get their authority, and before I wade through any more genealogies I want the spec first. So write me the desiderata -- the full list of what any adequate genealogy of a moral value has to deliver. Not one particular account; the criteria that any of them has to meet. Assume the point of the whole exercise is to let me tell, for a given value, whether an account of its history has actually undercut its authority or merely told me where it came from. That's the job I need done.

So: the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the explanatory work it has to do, each with the consideration behind it. The cases I keep coming back to are the drift in what counts as cruelty, the way a practice like punishment gets handed down and endlessly re-interpreted, and the awkward one -- whether tracing a value's origin is even the sort of thing that can strip its authority. On distinctions, I want at least these kept apart: explaining vs debunking, origin vs justification, a practice's original function vs its present one, and showing a belief is contingent vs showing it's false. Add whatever else you think can't be run together. And mark which of the requirements are non-negotiable and which are just the preferences of one camp.

Then the bit I actually want: tell me which of these requirements can't all hold at once. I suspect theres a tangle at the centre of it -- if the genealogist uses standards like truth, or non-cruelty, or reason to cut down the value she's examining, and those standards have genealogies of their own, does the whole programme eat itself? Say plainly whether it does, whether that's fatal or a bullet worth biting, and which single requirement does the most to knock out the usual contenders.

Then test your own list on a case that isn't the stock one. Take the modern ideal that your main job in life is to be true to yourself. Does the list tell me what a genealogy of that would have to do, and would the result be a debunking or just a story about where the ideal came from? If the list can't answer that, the list is wrong somewhere -- say where.

You can close with a short verdict on how Nietzsche's own way of doing it fares against your list, and how the more functionalist or evolutionary debunking styles fare, but keep that part tight; the criteria are the main thing.

Dont give me a menu of positions and leave me to pick. Commit: give me the list you'd actually defend, say where you'd dig in, and put your neck out on the self-undermining question.
