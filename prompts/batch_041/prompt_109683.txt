I want the specification before I look at any more criteria for morally relevant computation. Suppose I want a principle that says which computations make a system a subject of experience, and so a bearer of moral status -- candidates on the table include recurrent processing, global broadcast, higher-order representation, and integrated information, but do not spend the answer scoring them. Give me the desiderata instead: the cases the principle must classify correctly, with the consideration that fixes each as a fixed point (adult humans and many animals as paradigm inclusions; thermostats, plants, and immune or digestive processes as paradigm exclusions; and the contested middle -- insects, developing fetuses, anaesthetized, dreaming, and vegetative humans, cortical organoids, and a system that reports pain in fluent language); and the distinctions it must not collapse (nociception versus pain; having a capacity versus currently exercising it; being a subject versus merely carrying information; having status versus how much; a system's instantiating a computation versus that computation mattering to it; and whether the deliverable is a fact of the matter or a decision rule for acting under uncertainty about minds we cannot detect). Say which requirements are genuine constraints and which are one camp's prejudice, and -- the part I care most about -- whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable: in particular, whether anything selective enough to exclude a thermostat can still include an infant, remain neutral about the substrate, and be applied to systems that cannot tell us what they feel, given that we have only one confirmed case to calibrate against.
