I translate Latin and a bit of Old English verse for fun, nothing published, just for freinds, and I keep hitting a situation I don't have a word for. Latest one: a short Latin poem whose whole point is a pair of words that differ by a single letter, and English just doesn't have that pair. So instead of carrying the joke across I rebuilt the English so the two line-endings rhyme, and the rhyme does what the letter-swap was doing -- it makes you stop and look at the words as words. Same job, completely different mechanism. Earlier one: dactylic hexameter, which English basically can't do, so I did it in a loose five-beat line, and now the English sounds "poetic" for reasons the Latin doesn't, because what makes English sound formal isn't what made Latin sound formal.

Does this have a name? I've heard "dynamic equivalence," but that seems to be about the reader's response, and mostly about prose; and I have a vague memory of "compensation," but I think that's about patching a loss somewhere else in the text, not swapping one load-bearing device for a different one. What I want is the concept, not the label: what exactly is the move, how is it not those two and not just paraphrase, and where does it stop being translation and turn into something else? Related thing I can't place either: when the swap works, nobody reading my English can tell any of it happened -- the more engineering went in, the less it shows. Is that part of the same phenomenon or a separate one?
