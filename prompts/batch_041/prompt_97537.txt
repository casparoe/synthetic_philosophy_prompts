Hey — I write the science & ideas column here and the humor issue is coming up, and I want to pitch something better then the usual philosophers-are-weird filler. Can you write me the piece?

The idea: a fake investor prospectus (or shareholder letter — your call) for a fund whose whole thesis is that history bends toward justice. Moral progress as an asset class. Deadpan financial prose the entire way through, no winking, and every joke has to come out of the actual philosophical problem — I want the Kant-and-Hegel crowd to laugh and not just wince.

A few things it has to actually chew on, without ever naming them outright:
- a trend isnt a goal. Things having gotten better so far, and history heading somewhere good, are two different claims, and the prospectus should quietly conflate them.
- survivorship bias in the record — we remember the wins, and the timeline gets written by whoever won.
- theodicy. A fund that's long on justice has to explain the drawdowns, and it should do it in the smarmiest possible way, like a manager explaining away a bad quarter.
- who's the counterparty? If some people are getting more justice, is somebody else paying for it?
- the maturity date is longer than a human life. The payoff shows up after you're dead, and the prospectus just sort of waves at that and moves on.

Structure it like a real prospectus: investment strategy, a risk-factors section where the risks are the real objections (at least one should be that the arc might be a circle, and one about whether the trend line is just a story we're imposing), fees, and a closing pitch. Give it a name that sounds like an actual fund. Around 600-700 words, and keep it readable for someone who's never read a word of philosophy of history.

Two things: don't cite anyone and don't put real quotes in it — I can't fact-check from here and I really don't want a made-up quote floating around attached to a real person.
