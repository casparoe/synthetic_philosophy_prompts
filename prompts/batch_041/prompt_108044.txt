The gist the author gives is that Aristotle was basically the first anti-capitalist: he thought any exchange aimed at profit was unnatural, and that only producing stuff for your own household counted as natural; money, on this telling, is just a token people agree to use, and because it's sterile — it can't make anything — charging interest is the worst of all the unnatural ways of getting rich, since it makes money breed money. It's a punchy chapter, and honestly it fits the vague picture I got in an intro econ class of Aristotle as a snob about commerce.
