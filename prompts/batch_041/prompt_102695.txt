Ok, philosophy PhD student here (don't ask which subfield, it's a mess), and I'm trying to figure out whether there's a single principle behind my gut verdicts about creating people, because I have a feeling there isn't and I want someone to either find one or tell me which of my gut calls to abandon.

My verdicts. (1) A couple who knows any child they conceive would have a short, agonizing, terminal illness: conceiving is wrong, flatly. (2) A couple who could have a child who'd have a genuinely wonderful life but decides nope, careers instead: not wrong at all, definately not wrong, and I really don't feel anything was lost — the world isn't worse for that kid's absence. (3) A couple who does conceive a happy kid: good, lovely, but they didn't owe it to anyone, and if they'd turned out infertile instead I wouldn't say the world came out worse. (4) The one that trips me up alot: a woman is already pregnant, the kid's life would be barely worth living — real hardship, but on balance glad to be alive — and I think continuing is fine but not required; whereas if the life would be barely NOT worth living, continuing is wrong. So I've got a sharp line at zero and no idea why the line lives exactly there and not somewhere else.

And (2) and (3) seem to fight each other: if creating the happy kid is good, then not creating it leaves things worse, so how is it not even a little bad? Good compared to what, if the comparison is with the kid never existing at all?

My current stab at a principle, which I suspect is just me restating the puzzle: suffering is bad and it counts even when you're deciding whether to make someone, while happiness is good but only counts once there's already someone there to have it. Does that actually get all four, or is it circular / doing no work? And if some principle does save all my verdicts but has to get really baroque to do it, honestly I'd rather hear which verdict to give up and why. Also tell me what your principle says about a case I didn't list: a couple who could either conceive kid A now (fine life, nothing special) or wait and conceive kid B later (much better life, different person entirely) — is picking A wrong, and does your principle handle that without appealing to what A would've wanted?
