I am a high school student writing an essay for my philosophy club, and I would like you to compare two thought experiments rather than simply summarise them. The first is the utility monster: a being whose capacity for satisfaction is so enormous that any pleasure given to it outweighs the pleasure we could produce for any number of ordinary beings, so that a total utilitarian seems required to hand it the world. The second is a mirror image that I have only ever seen stated informally: a being whose capacity for suffering is equally enormous and whose suffering cannot be relieved, so that the kindest available act is to destroy it — and, if it cannot be destroyed alone, everything else along with it — as quickly as possible, because every moment of its life contains more agony than the rest of creation combined.

What I want to know is whether these are one argument in two costumes or two genuinely different objections. Can a utilitarian coherently accept the first and reject the second, and if so, which principle does the work — for instance, that suffering silences competing claims in a way that happiness does not? Or does the second case show that the monster was never really an objection to utilitarianism in particular, but only to any theory that lets one being's experience outweigh everyone else's without limit? I would also like to know whether the monster argument quietly assumes that the capacity for pleasure and the capacity for pain grow together, and what becomes of the argument if they come apart.
