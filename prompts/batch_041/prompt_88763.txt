I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and I'm trying to write down where I've actually landed on digital minds before a reading group takes it apart. Here is the package:

(1) Moral status is a matter of degree and tracks only the capacity for valenced experience; intelligence, substrate, and species membership add nothing on their own.
(2) Functionalism is true: a system with the right functional organization has whatever experiences a biological system with that organization would have, so the fact that something is made of silicon rather than carbon cannot do moral work by itself.
(3) A reinforcement-learning agent whose internal states play the functional role of pleasure and pain has a real and probably substantial claim on us, even if it exists only for the duration of a single training run.
(4) Current language models are not serious candidates for moral status: what they say about their inner lives is downstream of human text, and there is no persisting subject there whose interests could be set back.
(5) Even so, we should treat them with some care, because under moral uncertainty the costs of being wrong are asymmetric.
(6) If digital minds are possible at all, the numbers swamp everything else: the expected number of minds we may create or fail to create is astronomically large, so even a small probability of their suffering should dominate our practical priorities.

Tell me which of these are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, and rank the genuine ones by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. For each real tension, give the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision costs me. I don't want a survey of the literature on machine consciousness; I want to know which of my own commitments is doing work that another one quietly forbids.
