So my reading group has been chewing on Benatar's asymmetry for like eight sessions and nobody has moved an inch, and at this point I honestly suspect we're not even having the same arguement. Quick recap for context, in case it matters: pain's presence is bad, pleasure's presence is good, pain's absence is good even when there's no one around to enjoy that good, and pleasure's absence isn't bad unless there's somebody who's being deprived by it. The whole antinatalist conclusion is supposed to fall out of those four.

Here's the case I brought in that really split us. Two merely possible people: one whose life, if you made it, would be on balance mildly bad, one whose life would be on balance mildly good. Nobody's actual, nobody's being deprived of anything yet. The asymmetry seems to say it's good that the first person never exists (their pain is absent, and that absence is good without needing a subject) but not bad, and not good either, that the second person never exists (their pleasure is absent, and that absence only matters if someone's deprived). Half the room thinks this is just the asymmetry working as intended and the other half thinks we've been tricked by a stipulation dressed up as an insight.

The three camps that have formed: (a) the asymmetry isn't a consistent axiology at all, it's a bundle of intuitions tuned to get a pre-chosen answer, so there's nothing to argue about, just something to debunk; (b) it IS consistent, but only if you distinguish good-for-someone from good-period, and the fight is really about whether impersonal goodness is a coherent notion; (c) the whole thing is confused because "absence of pain" isn't a state anyone's ever in, so calling it good is a category slip and the debate should dissolve.

My working hypothesis is that it's mostly (b) in disguise and that (a) and (c) are both deflating a substantive disagreement about whether value can float free of a valuer. But I could be wrong and it could be a purely verbal fight about the word good, or a bedrock clash of intuitions nobody can argue for.

So: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or two groups answering different questions under one name? Diagnose it using what each side actually says, tell me which of my three camps is doing the real work, and tell me what if anything would count as progress here, a distinction, a case, a formal result, or just the honest admission that no progress is coming.
