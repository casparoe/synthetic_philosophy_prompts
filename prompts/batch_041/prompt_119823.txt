Two of my colleagues got into a fight about this at the department's end-of-semester party and I've been chewing on it ever since. Mira works on decision theory, Dev does logic and philosophy of math. The question was whether "heuristic arguments" -- the informal, non-proof arguments that make a conjecture look true -- can be turned into a legitimate epistemic principle, or whether they're just respectable-sounding pattern matching.

Mira's example was the standard heuristic for Collatz. Every odd step you roughly multiply by 3 and then halve a bunch of times, which averages out to about two halvings, so on average each odd step multiplies your number by 3/4. Treat the successive multipliers as independent draws and the orbit shrinks like a random walk with negative drift, so almost every starting point should eventually come back down to 1. She said what licenses that is a presumption of independence: we have no information about the correlations between one step and the next, so treating them as independent is the default, and that's precisely what lets a pile of loose heuristics add up to something you should actually believe.

Dev said that's the whole problem. Run the exact same argument on the 3n-1 variant and you get the same 3/4 drift, but 3n-1 has a cycle -- 5, 14, 7, 20, 10, back to 5 -- so the independence assumption is just false there, and the heuristic gives you no way of knowing when it's false. Mira said fine, hold the conclusion more loosely, but that's not a reason to junk the whole category; and if you demand a non-question-begging justification of independence before you're allowed to use a heuristic, you'd have to give up statistical mechanics and the probabilistic method too. Dev said those are different: stat mech earns its independence assumption from experiment, and the probabilistic method doesn't assume anything about a fixed object, it averages over objects you get to choose. Mira said the entire point of a presumption is that you don't need evidence when there's nothing on the other side, and refusing to presume independence just means having no opinion at all, which is worse.
