I'm a second-year bioinformatics student and I've somehow ended up as the person who has to write up the mess from our reading group. Six of us spent the spring going through Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach, and we are now split clean down the middle on the typogenetics stuff, and its gotten weirdly personal.

You'll need the toy system either way, so: typogenetics is a made-up biochemistry where strands are strings over a four-letter alphabet like DNA's (A, C, G, T), you read a strand off in pairs, each pair maps to an "amino acid" through a fixed code table, and each amino acid is a command that operates on strands -- cutting, deleting, switching, moving left or right, copying, inserting, and so on. A strand's enzymes are just the amino acid sequences you get by translating it, and a self-rep is a strand that, when its own enzymes are made and turned loose on it, yields a copy of itself. The claim the isomorphism people take from all this is that typogenetics, Gödel's self-referential sentence, and DNA replication are the same structure.

Team Isomorphism: self-replication just is self-reference. A self-replicator is a fixed point -- something that carries a description of itself and whose behavior is then governed by that description -- and a Gödel sentence, a quine, a self-rep strand, and a bacterium are all instances of one formal phenomenon.

Team Analogy: no. Gödel's construction is about provability inside a coding that's fixed in advance; DNA doesn't refer to anything; the copying is done by machinery the strand doesn't specify; and typogenetics proves the opposite of what Hofstadter wanted, since the code table and the surrounding soup are handed to you from outside. If anything, self-replication looks like von Neumann's description-plus-universal-constructor-plus-copier setup, which is deliberately not self-referential.

What I want is the document we should have written instead of shouting at each other: a joint statement both camps would actually put their names to. Numbered agreed-claims section first -- what's true about typogenetics, about the Gödel construction, about DNA, about what "isomorphic" could even mean here, and about what both sides agree would settle things -- then a disagreements table: each remaining disagreement stated in wording both sides accept as fair to their own position, the crux it turns on, and, for each crux, a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. My own suspicion, for what it's worth, is that half the fight is that Team Isomorphism has Kleene's recursion theorem in mind while Team Analogy hears Gödel's diagonal lemma, and those are not the same theorem. If a chunk of the dispute is just verbal, say which chunk and why; don't paper over the rest.

American English please, and no bibliography -- we've read enough.
