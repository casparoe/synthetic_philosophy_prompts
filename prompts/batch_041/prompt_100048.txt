OK so I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm trying to write a piece about karma as a theory of moral desert — you know, the idea that people actually end up getting what they deserve, in this life or some next round. Problem is I keep getting the bumper-sticker version from sources and I can never tell if they mean anything at all.

The one that's bugging me came from a guy I was profiling for a totally different story — a sort of longevity guru who's now big into "energy." He kept saying 'karma is just the universe keeping the books,' and when I pressed him he said it means nothing you do is ever lost, every kindness is a deposit and every cruelty a withdrawal, and the account always balances out eventually. He offered this as the reason he gives away a chunk of his company's revenue, as if it followed. I nodded and wrote it down but I honestly couldnt tell you which of like six different things he was claiming.

So: can you lay out the menu of precise claims 'the universe keeps the books' could be making when someone says it about karma? I want the readings from the boring to the wild. Is it a description of how the world actually works, or a prescription for how we ought to treat each other? Is there a bookkeeper — somebody who intends the balance — or is it more like a law of nature, the way gravity doesnt care about you? Does it need a soul, or some continuing self that carries the balance forward, or could it run without one? And if the payout lands on someone who doesnt remember doing the deed — a next life, say — is that even desert, or just a weird causal relay? Does it track what you did or what you meant? Is it proportional and retributive, eye for an eye, or more like you can offset a debt with later good behavior? And is the actual claim that suffering is deserved, or just that actions have downstream effects, which, fine, but who cares?

Then tell me which of those readings are trivial, which are substantive and defensible, which are just false, and which one my longevity guy most likely meant when he used it to justify writing checks. And flag the ones that arent really about desert at all, since that's my whole angle. Lay it out as a list I can check my own understanding against, not an essay.
