I am trying to build an index that would let me compare the quantity of violence in a medieval European region with that in a present-day industrial democracy, and I have concluded that I cannot design it until I know what any adequate theory of violence — the quantity the index is supposed to track — has to deliver. So before I read another study, give me the desiderata: the full specification of what a theory of violence must get right.

For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that motivates it. At a minimum I want the theory to handle the following: a surgeon's incision and a boxing match both parties wanted (harm no one would call violence); a government that quietly lets a vaccination programme lapse, and a famine engineered by blockade (harm by omission, with causation smeared across many hands); a slave economy and a system of segregated deprivation (harm that no single act inflicts); a parent who never strikes but isolates and terrorizes a child; and a suicide, where victim and agent coincide. Tell me which of these are genuine constraints on a theory of violence and which are merely the preoccupations of one camp.

I also want the distinctions the theory cannot collapse — between violence and force, coercion and harm, act and omission, intended and merely foreseen, physical and psychological — and the difference between a count of violent events and a rate per head of population, including what the denominator even means when populations are estimated rather than counted. Add any consistency constraint the theory must honour, and be explicit about what the surviving historical record actually permits. Finally, say whether the whole enterprise presupposes that violence is a natural kind with a fact of the matter, or whether "how much violence" is inescapably relative to a category we choose; if the latter, tell me what that does to the idea of an objective index.

Then: mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are parochial; flag any pair or subset you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied; and name the single requirement that does the most to rule out candidate theories. If it is cheap to do so, say briefly which broad families of approach survive your list.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the one requirement you take to be most consequential, or the impossibility result — then give the reasoning. No preamble, no restating my question.
