So I'm in this reading group that started as a book club and has now devolved into four of us arguing about whether anyone ever really deserves to suffer, and I've got a bee in my bonnet about karma. Not the bumper-sticker "what goes around comes around" thing, but karma taken seriously as a theory of moral desert: every bit of suffering you undergo is the ripening of some action that was yours, and so it's deserved in the strict sense, not merely caused. What I can't get past is that on the strictest version of the no-self idea there's no enduring someone who did the deed and later undergoes the fruit, so the whole ledger is causally real but the word "deserved" has nothing to latch onto. I want to see that tension play out in a concrete case instead of just waving at it.

So build me a case to order, and put three positions through it. (1) Ledger karma: suffering is always the ripening of the sufferer's own past action, therefore deserved, exactly and proportionally, memory or no memory. (2) Ordinary retributive desert, the secular kind: someone deserves to suffer for a wrong only if the same blameworthy subject who did it is still around to undergo the suffering. (3) Deflationary / no-self karma: the causal ripening is real, desert is a projection, nobody ever deserves anything. I want the base case built so that (1) and (2) land on the same verdict — the suffering is deserved, not an injustice — while (3) dissents. Constraints: one life, no on-screen reincarnation, no courtroom, ordinary setting (a family, a small business, a hospital), one paragraph, and please don't just stipulate the verdicts, they have to fall out of what actually happens in the case. Also I don't want it to work by having the person secretly remember everything and secretly agree it's fair — the desert should attach whether or not they know why.

Then, holding everything else fixed, give me the variant where you strip out the single feature that's doing the work and show the verdicts moving. And finish by telling me how a defender of whichever position comes out looking worst would attack the case, and be honest about whether the attack lands. Skip the reading lists and citations, I just want the reasoning. Give it back to me as a JSON object with these fields: scenario (the case, as a string); verdicts (an array, one entry per position, each an object with position, verdict, and why); working_feature (a string naming the one feature that does the work and saying why); variant (an object with changed and verdicts, where verdicts has the same shape as above); defenders_reply (a string).
