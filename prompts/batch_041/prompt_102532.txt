I am a high school student writing a philosophy club essay, and I need to argue for a position on a modified Molyneux question. In the familiar setup, a person blind from birth gains sight and must tell a cube from a sphere by sight alone, having known them only by touch. My version changes two things: the person has used a tactile-to-audio sensory substitution device for years, so surface curvature becomes pitch and texture becomes timbre, and the objects are a set of irregular chiral shapes presented in a mirror. After sight restoration, the person can still feel the objects but cannot see them while touching; they are shown the mirrored shapes and asked to match each to the object in their hands. Please argue for the following position: at first the person could not match them by sight alone, but with a few minutes of active, self-controlled eye movements they could learn to do so, because the relevant representation is neither purely tactile nor purely visual but is tied to sensorimotor contingency. Write the essay in these sections: Thesis (2-3 sentences); The modified thought experiment (1 paragraph); The case for the position (3-4 paragraphs); The strongest objection and my reply (2 paragraphs); Conclusion (1 paragraph). Do not cite sources or provide a reading list. End by naming the single empirical or conceptual assumption that would most change the conclusion.
