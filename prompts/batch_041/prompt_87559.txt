Ok so I need an outside opinion on an argument two people in my seminar have been having all week, because I got dragged in as referee and I honestly have no idea who's right.

The setup is this little vision illusion. You flash a dot in one place on a screen, then a fraction of a second later you flash a dot of a different colour somewhere else. Get the timing right and you don't see two dots — you see one dot travelling from the first spot to the second, and it changes colour about halfway along the trip. Which is bizarre, because the second colour wasn't on the screen yet at the moment you'd have to be seeing it.

Anna says this shows your brain rewrites experience after the fact. You genuinely had the experience of the dot at position one, then the brain went back and edited the record, so that now it seems like the colour flipped in the middle. Her line: "you can't see green in the middle of the path before green has ever been shown, so the only options are that your past experience got altered, or that you never really had the earlier experience at all — and you definately did, you'd have said 'blue dot' if I'd asked you."

Ben says that's backwards, and the second horn is the right one. On his view nothing gets rewritten: the brain just doesn't finish building the experience until it has both flashes in hand, then publishes one continuous scene with the colour change already baked into the middle. No time travel, no editing of a stored experience, just a short delay before anything gets "shown" to you. He thinks Anna's version invents a weird mechanism (editing a memory of an experience) where a boring buffer would do. He keeps labelling the two options "Orwellian" (hers) and "Stalinesque" (his), which he says he got from somewhere.

Anna's comeback: calling it a "delay" doesn't remove the weirdness, it just moves it. If what you end up experiencing at time 1 is decided by what happens at time 2, then in what sense was there an experience at time 1 at all? And if there wasn't, how does the brain know to put a dot at position one in the first place? Either way the content of your experience is being fixed by later events, and that's the actual jaw-dropper, whatever we label it.

Ben then proposes a test: run the illusion and, right after the second flash, flash a probe patch that's either the first colour or the second, and see whether people identify the second colour faster. If the green really was experienced mid-path, he says, it should prime them. Anna says fine, but you can't settle the question by asking people what they experienced or how fast they pressed a button, because both of those are downstream of exactly the process we're arguing about — if the record can be edited (her view), the report can be edited too, so the report is worthless as evidence. Ben says by that standard her hypothesis can't be tested at all, which he thinks means it isn't really a hypothesis. She says it means his test can't distinguish the two views, which is his problem.

That's where they're stuck. Can you go through it move by move and say who's right at each step — and, if neither of them has it quite right, what are they both missing? I'd especially like to know whether Anna's "delay just relabels the problem" point actually lands, and whether Ben's priming test could distinguish their views or not.
