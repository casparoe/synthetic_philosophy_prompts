I have no academic training in philosophy of science, but I have spent a long time reading on my own about how scientific traditions are judged across generations, and the account I keep returning to is Lakatos's notion of a research programme. As I understand it, a programme has a hard core of assumptions its defenders refuse to abandon, a protective belt of auxiliary claims they adjust when trouble comes, a positive heuristic telling them what to work on next, and a negative heuristic telling them what not to touch; a programme is progressive when those adjustments keep yielding new predictions and degenerating when they merely patch over the latest anomaly, and — the part I find hardest — Lakatos insists there is no instant rationality, so the two can be told apart only in hindsight.

Brecht's Life of Galileo strikes me as a near-perfect test case, and I would like you to read the play through this framework. It sets a new astronomy against an old one and lets an institution, rather than an experiment, decide the contest: the telescope demonstrations, the moons of Jupiter, the move from Padua to the Medici court, the plague, the trial and the recantation, and the final scene in which the blind Galileo has the Discorsi smuggled out of Italy. Is the Church's defence of the old system a textbook degenerating programme, kept alive by ad hoc belt adjustments? Is Galileo's the progressive one, and does its positive heuristic survive the recantation, or must Lakatos call the programme dead because its champion yielded? Where does the framework genuinely illuminate a scene or a choice, and where does the play resist it — does Brecht care about the social and moral life of inquiry in a way that Lakatos's internal account brackets out, and if so, is that a gap in the methodology or a division of labour it is entitled to make? Please give me a reading that is faithful in both directions — the play not reduced to an illustration, the framework not bent to fit — and end by saying what the play shows about how programmes actually die that the methodology alone cannot.
