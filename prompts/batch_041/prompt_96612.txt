I'm not a biologist, just someone who reads alot of pop science and argues on forums, and there's a thing I keep running into with the word "species" that I want to know the name of, if it has one.

Here's the shape of it. Everyone uses "species" as if it names a real division out there, the way "gold" or "water" does — you find out which species an animal is in, you don't decide it. But the same word is also doing a second, practical job: it's the unit conservation law protects, the thing on a birdwatcher's list, the thing you count when you say "three species went extinct this year." Most of the time the two jobs don't collide and nobody notices there are two of them.

The interesting part is what happens when they do collide. Case one: a group everyone treats as one species by the practical standard — same look, same role in the ecosystem, managed as one thing by everyone whose job depends on it — turns out, on the evolutionary standard, to be several lineages that stopped exchanging genes a long time ago. Case two, the mirror image: two things that look and behave like obviously different animals turn out to be one interbreeding group. Case three, the one that really got me: an environmental offset where the lawyer and the ecologist are both counting "species" and come out with different numbers from the same survey, and neither is making a mistake.

Now the argument breaks out, and the move I keep seeing is this. Whoever's losing retreats to "well, species is just a human convenience anyway, it's just a category we impose," which seems to settle it. But then a week later the same person is saying "we have to save this species" or "this species is a unique evolutionary treasure," talking about it as a real thing again, and nobody calls them on the switch. The word seems to slide between the two jobs mid-conversation depending on which one is handy.

What I want is a name for that specific pattern — a term that does two jobs at once (naming a real division in nature, and being the practical unit for law and counting and value), where the two jobs only come apart in hard cases, and where the definitional fight that erupts is really a fight over which job gets priority, dressed up as a fight about facts. I know there's something called the "species problem," but I think that's about how to define species in the first place, and what I'm after is narrower. I've also seen people wave "pluralism" at this, but I'm not sure that's the same thing — pluralism seems to say several definitions are each fine for their own purpose, whereas what I'm describing is one word quietly switching between them mid-sentence. So: is there an established name for exactly this, and if there isn't one, can you name and define it, tell me how it's different from the species problem, from pluralism, and from plain old equivocation, and give me a case that's on the boundary of your definition so I can see where you're drawing the line? Don't just hand me a label — I care about where the edges are.
