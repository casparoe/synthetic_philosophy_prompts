I'm in a weird arguement with my roommate, who works at a company that does AI safety and eval stuff. They've got this internal rubric for deciding which models get what kind of oversight, and it basically sorts systems into four bins: ones that just do trial-and-error and get selected by outcomes (Darwinian), ones that learn from reward/punishment signals (Skinnerian), ones that build an internal model and test hypotheses before acting (Popperian), and ones that offload cognition onto external tools, language, and cultural artifacts (Gregorian). The weird part is they use different bins for different parts of the same system. During training they treat the model as a pure Skinnerian thing — just reinforcement, no inner life, no understanding. During red-teaming they act like it's Popperian — they talk about the model forming hypotheses about the evaluators, updating its world model, planning. And once it's deployed with tools and memory they start treating it as Gregorian, and suddenly there are meetings about whether it can be deceived, whether it has interests, whether it's a moral patient. I keep saying you can't have it all three ways unless you tell me what you think a mind is and what makes a creature the kind of thing that can be wronged.

Can you reconstruct the philosophical commitments this whole policy presupposes? Specifically: what does each bin commit you to about representation, learning, and agency? Which commitments are actually inconsistent with each other and which just look inconsistent because the policy is mixing levels (the model as trained artifact vs. the model as deployed agent vs. the species or system as a whole)? Is there a coherent reading where the four labels are stages or layers rather than exclusive kinds, and if so what would it take for a system to graduate from one to the next? And where is the policy underdetermined — could a purely eliminativist or behaviorist view license the same practices without any of the mentalistic talk, or does the Gregorian bin force some notion of content and normativity that the Skinnerian bin was supposed to avoid? I don't need a reading list, just the implicit theory and where it breaks.
