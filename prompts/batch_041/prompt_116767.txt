For a rationalist reading group that has already been through the standard treatments of the unilateralist's curse — the pattern in which many agents are each independently in a position to do something worth doing if it is done once, so each has a reason to act, while enough of them acting is worse than none of them acting — I want a decision tree a participant can walk through at the whiteboard and land on a named position about what an individual in that position should do. Every branch node should be a question about the case rather than about doctrine, so that someone can answer it without already knowing where it leads; candidate nodes include whether the harm from duplication is a fact about the action or only about other agents' likely choices, whether the agent's estimate of the value must be robust across plausible error rather than merely positive, whether the relevant unit is this act or a standing policy the agent would have everyone adopt, and whether the failure is located in individual decision rules or in the institutional structure that lets anyone act alone. Leaves must include at least the conformity answer (raise the bar for acting, and act only if the act would still look worthwhile given that others may act too), the coordination answer (keep the act but make it non-unilateral, by declaring intent, precommitting jointly, or vesting the decision in one authority), the act-anyway answer, and the answer that no individual rule can help and only changing the payoff structure — making the action detectable, costly, or reversible — will. Also include the mirror case, an action that is harmful if done once and valuable only when many do it, and say whether your tree routes it through the same nodes or through a separate branch. Where a combination of answers is coherent but unoccupied, mark it and diagnose briefly why nobody is there; where two leaves cannot be separated by any answer to your nodes, flag that rather than papering over it. Seven to nine nodes, five to eight leaves, and the interest is in the distinctions that never make it into the standard summaries.
