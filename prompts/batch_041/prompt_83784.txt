My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about the charter for a small housing cooperative we are trying to start — three households, a shared kitchen and garden, and a rulebook we both want to keep shorter than a page. I brought up Sen's liberal paradox, the result that no way of aggregating individual preferences into a social ranking can satisfy an unrestricted domain, a weak Pareto principle, and even a minimal assignment of individual rights (two people, each decisive over just one pair of alternatives). He thinks I was showing off. The paradox, he says, is a curiosity of formal social choice that has no bearing on something as informal as a co-op charter; no real charter ever demands an unrestricted domain; and minimal liberalism is a strawman, since nobody actually believes that a single household should get its way over any pair of social states, however trivial the pair and however much it costs everyone else.

I want your account of the most important implications of that result for a project like ours — implications for how we write the charter and how we should expect our house meetings to go, not merely for how the theorem is stated. Specifically: what the result establishes and what it leaves open; whether his two objections have any force; whether the paradox can arise for a body that never ranks all possible states of affairs, decides case by case, and whose members change their preferences through discussion; and how the standard escapes from it would look in our circumstances. He has proposed one as our way out: that a household's right be understood only as a veto over departures from the status quo inside its own domain — no household may be compelled to change its own arrangements, and none may compel another's. I want to know what that buys us and what it costs, particularly in the case where the status quo is worse for all three households than an alternative that one of us is blocking. I would also like to know whether representing a liberty as a pair of alternatives over which a person is decisive is the right representation at all, or whether that modeling choice is doing work that a better one would not.
