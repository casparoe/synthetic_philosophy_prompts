I'm in a metaethics reading group and next week it's my turn to run the session on the wrong kind of reasons, and honestly I'm dreadding it, because everyone's read the same papers and the discussion always collapses into the same fork: either "that just shows fittingness comes in two flavours" or "no, it shows fitting-attitude accounts of value are dead." So instead of opening with a summary I want to open with a poem, something that makes the room feel the problem in their stomachs before we start scoring points off each other. What I'm picturing: a sestina where the six end words stay the same the whole way through -- reason, fit, attitude, world, threat, worth -- and where the poem changes register halfway. The first three stanzas live in the demon case: something out there will make you very sorry unless you admire it, and the admiration you produce on those grounds is real, sincere admiration, which is exactly the problem. The last three stanzas should be somewhere much more ordinary: a person who needs you, who will come apart if you don't love them back, and the love you find in yourself on those grounds. I want the envoi to hold both halves together without settling anything, because the thing I actually want the group to sit with is that from the inside the attitude you have for the wrong kind of reason is indistinguishable from the attitude you'd have for the right kind -- no theory about which reasons are reasons for the object and which are only reasons for the attitude tells you which one you're actually in.

It needs to be accurate enough that nobody in the group thinks I've misdescribed the case, and good enough as a poem that the people who came for the fight don't groan. Rhyme isn't needed (sestinas mostly don't in English anyway). Please don't name any philosophers or papers in it -- the whole point is that it shouldn't sound like a lit review with line breaks -- and please don't land on a resolution, no closing turn where the love redeems the demon or where the threat turns out to have been a gift all along. I'd rather it end still stuck, but stuck in a way that's earned.
