ok so I'm supposed to hand in a position paper next week and before I do I want to know if my contractualism actually hangs together, or if I've just been helping myself to whatever I needed in each case. Here's the pile:

(1) An act is wrong just in case it's ruled out by a principle that nobody could reasonably reject. That's the whole story for me — no separate appeal to consequences or virtues or whatever underneath it.

(2) Reasonable rejection gets assessed one person at a time. To make that concrete: I think a complaint belongs to a person, its strength is fixed by how much worse off that person is under the principle than under the alternative, and you can compare complaints within a single person's life but never across persons. So if one person has a serious complaint against a principle and five people each have mild ones, the serious one carries it. That's the whole point of being a non-utilitarian as far as I'm concerned, and I'd rather die on that hill than on any other.

(3) That said, if I can get to five people on one rock or one person on the other, I go for the five. Not a lottery, not a coin flip, no agonizing. I've honestly never understood why people treat this as a hard case.

(4) And if the only way to spare five hundred people an hour-long headache is to break one stranger's arm, the arm stays unbroken. I know the total discomfort on the other side adds up to more, that's not the kind of thing that moves me.

(5) Taking one healthy patient apart to save five dying ones is wrong, and I think what's wrong about it specifically is that the one patient has been turned into equipment — the raw material for the other five. That's the bit I can't get past.

(6) I'm not a Kantian though. I don't believe in a categorical imperative, I don't think the mere-means formula is the foundation of anything, and I don't think morality has one master principle that reason hands down.

(7) Contractualism, for me, is a criterion of rightness and not a decision procedure. It's not supposed to be what I'm consciously running through my head while I'm sprinting for the boat.

(8) Also, I think it's wrong to make animals suffer for our amusement, and I think that's a fact about what's happening to the animals, not a fact about what some human owes some other human.

Which of these actually pull against each other? I have a feeling it isn't all of them, but I don't want to start deleting bullets on vibes. Rank the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one tell me the cheapest repair and what it costs me — I'd rather make a small concession than throw out something load-bearing. And if exactly one bullet has to go, tell me which one and why it's that one rather than the others. No reading list, just the argument.
