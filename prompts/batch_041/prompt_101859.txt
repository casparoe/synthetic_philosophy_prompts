Okay, quick setup and then a question about a long-dead philosopher.

There's a result in epistemic logic — originally due to Frederic Fitch, though the proof was apparently first floated by an anonymous referee back in 1945 (turned out it was Alonzo Church) — that goes like this. Assume every truth is knowable: for any true proposition p, it's possible that somebody knows p. Now consider the proposition "p, and nobody knows that p." Could anyone possibly know that? If you knew it, you'd know p, and you'd also know that nobody knows p — but you just knew p, so "nobody knows p" is false, and you can't know something false. So that conjunctive proposition can't possibly be known. But if it's true, then by the knowability assumption it must be possibly known — contradiction. So it's never true, i.e. there are no unknown truths, i.e. every truth is already known. Which is obviously absurd, so the knowability assumption looks refuted.

People usually take this as a problem for anti-realist / verificationist theories of truth, and one of the names that always gets dragged in is Charles Sanders Peirce. He died in 1914, well before any of this. His line, roughly, was that truth is what inquiry is fated to converge on — what the community of inquirers would settle on at the ideal limit of investigation. That smells a lot like a knowability thesis.

So here's what I want: imagine Peirce is somehow around in, say, 1980 and someone walks him through the Fitch proof. What does he say? Does he just bite the bullet and admit his account of truth implies we already know everything? Does he argue the proof smuggles in a bad notion of "knowable," or that it equivocates on possibility? Does he insist his view was never about what's *possible* to know in the modal sense? Give me your best reconstruction of how he'd actually react, built out of what he really held about truth, inquiry, doubt and the community of inquirers — and be explicit about which parts are solidly Peircean and which are you extrapolating. No need for citations or a reading list, I just want the reasoning.
