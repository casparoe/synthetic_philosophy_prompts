Ok so the sphex wasp thing — she drags a cricket to the burrow, goes inside to check it's clear, and if you nudge the cricket a couple inches away while she's in there, she comes back out, drags it over again, and goes back in to check, and you can do this forever. The old "sphexish" bit, people like Dennett made alot of it as the low bar for agency. Anyway I'm trying to work out how you'd actually tell, about some system, whether its behavior is sphexish or genuinely flexible, and I want a ranked list out of you, not an essay.

Here's the pile of candidate tests/markers I've been kicking aroud. Rank them from the ones I should trust most to the ones I should trust least, as diagnostics of real rigidity rather than mere complexity:

- the interruption-and-restart test: you break the sequence mid-stream; does it resume from where it was, restart the whole ritual from the top, or improvise a fresh route to the goal?
- the goal-swap test: same surface behavior, but you quietly change what counts as success; does it keep running the loop toward a target that no longer does anything for it?
- the parameter-vs-structure test: it varies its steps but never the shape of the plan
- the self-report test: show it a recording of its own loop — does it see the loop as a loop, or narrate it back to you as purposeful behavior?
- the compression test: can its behavior be described by a rule shorter than the behavior itself? (if yes, suspicious)
- the novel-perturbation test: throw in something it's never seen; does it degrade gracefully or thrash?
- the "can be conditioned out but not reasoned out" test: you can extinguish the loop with rewards and punishments, but you can't talk it out of the loop

For each, one sentence on why it lands where it does. Then two extra things: flag any of these that are secretly testing the same underlying thing (or that a genuinely flexible system would also fail, which would make them useless as diagnostics), and tell me which single one you'd bet on if you could only ever run one. Also, quick call at the end — is sphexishness a matter of degree, or is it a kind of thing a system either has or doesn't?
