I'm in an MA program and I've got to write something for a seminar on collective responsibility, but honestly this whole thing started as a recurring fight with my roomates. We keep going around on whether one person can really be on the hook for the big collective messes -- climate, meat, elections -- and it always ends with everybody asserting their gut and nobody moving, there's alot of heat and no light. So I said I'd sit down and write out my actual verdicts on a pile of cases and then see whether there's one principle behind them, and if there isn't, at least figure out which of my verdicts has to go.

Here's the pile. (1) I vote in every election, including ones my district is safe by thirty points in, and I think people who just can't be bothered are doing something wrong. (2) But my roommate skipped a school board election because she had a shift, and I don't think she did anything wrong at all, and I don't think I'm being inconsistent, though I can't yet say why. (3) I eat beef maybe twice a month and feel a genuine pang about it; my brother eats chicken basically daily and I think he's worse than me, even though I'd struggle to say what either of us does to any particular animal. (4) I once spent an afternoon arguing online that no single flight matters for the climate, then flew to a wedding and felt awful the whole time, and I still think the awful feeling was the right response even though I still think the argument was fine. (5) I signed a neighborhood petition that needed ten signatures for a speed bump; I was the seventh, so my signature changed nothing, and I'd have been a jerk to refuse. (6) There's a delivery app I use that I'm fairly sure shorts its couriers a few cents an order; I don't think that makes me complicit in anything. (7) The one that really started it: a friend described a scheme where a thousand people each wire ten dollars to a pot, and the pot only does its (bad) thing if it hits exactly ten thousand -- and I said the thousandth person, the one who tips it over, is much worse than the other 999, and she said that's absurd, because without the rest he's just some guy sending a wire transfer. (8) And I think someone who runs a leaf blower for an hour for fun has done something worse than someone who takes one commercial flight, even though I'm pretty sure the flight emits more.

I've been trying to fit these with something like: I'm on the hook when my act is one of a pattern that would cause the harm if everyone in my position did it, provided I could have known that and the alternative was cheap. That seems to cover (3) and (5) and maybe (1), but it makes (7) come out wrong and it doesn't touch (2) or the leaf blower in (8). My friend has her own one-liner, that it's all about whether your contribution is "load-bearing" -- take it away, does the harm still happen? -- and I've been using that word ever since, but I don't think it can be the whole story either, since (3) and (8) are both non-load-bearing and I don't treat them alike. So: is there a principle, or a small family of them, that gets these? I want it stated precisely enough to check, with each clause motivated by a specific case, its verdicts on a few cases I haven't listed (especially: someone who eats meat only when that exact portion would otherwise have gone to someone else, and someone who gives to a campaign fund that will hit its goal with or without them), and an honest list of which of my verdicts it can't save -- because if something has to go, I'd rather know which. Don't worry about references, I'll chase those myself.
