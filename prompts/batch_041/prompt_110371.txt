My little sister's wedding is in three weeks and I've been drafted to give the toast. She's a philosophy PhD student, and the one argument that ever genuinly spooked her is Parfit's dust-specks case -- one person tortured for fifty years versus an enormous number of people each getting a single speck of dust in the eye for a moment. She once told me, kind of miserably, that she'd choose the torture. Half the room will be her department, who know the case inside out and will be listening to see where I come down; the other half are our aunts and uncles who won't have a clue. I want to hang the whole toast on a variant I've been kicking around that's a bit off the usual one: instead of dust specks, a trillion people each have their life quietly shortened by one second -- no pain, no memory of it, no one ever finds out -- against the one person tortured for fifty years. That feels like it should make the trillion side easier to accept, and I want the speech to make the room feel why that instinct is still somehow off, without me ever announcing a verdict (it's a wedding, nobody wants a solve). The idea has to actually do the work -- shape what I can and can't say about the couple and about all the tiny daily stuff -- not just get name-dropped. Give it to me as exactly five beats, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap-up. American English.
