Ok, I've been going down a rabbit hole on supertasks (you know, infinitely many actions packed into a finite stretch of time) and I keep having strong gut verdicts about specific setups without being able to state the rule I'm using. I want you to figure out what principle, if any, fits them, and where my verdicts have to give. Here's the raw data.

1) Toggling lamp. A lamp starts off. Halfway through the interval someone turns it on, then a quarter of the way from the end turns it off, then an eighth, then a sixteenth, and so on, infinitely many flips, all before t = 2. At t = 2, is it on or off? My gut: there's no fact of the matter. Not 'we can't know,' but that nothing makes it on and nothing makes it off.

2) Half-open lamp. Same flickering, but now the rule is explicit: the lamp is off at all times except during a sequence of on-intervals that accumulate at t = 1 but never include t = 1 itself (each on-interval is closed on the left, open on the right). The light flickers infinitely often as you approach t = 1. At t = 1? I say off, and determinate. The rule pins down t = 1 exactly, since no on-interval contains it.

3) Dimming lamp. At the halfway, quarter, eighth... points the brightness is set to 1/2, 1/4, 1/8... At the end it's dark. Determinate, off. No wobble in my verdict here.

4) Urn A. Step n: put in balls numbered 10n-9 through 10n, take out ball n. When it's all done: empty. I'm definately sure. Every ball has a step at which it leaves.

5) Urn B. Same, except you take out ball 10n each step (so 10, 20, 30... leave). When it's done: infinitely many balls left, everything not a multiple of 10.

The two urns have the exact same net count each step (+10 in, -1 out), so tracking 'how many balls' gives infinity in both cases, yet one ends up empty and the other infinite. So the count limit clearly isn't what fixes the outcome; the fate of each individual ball is.

6) Rotating dial. The lamp is on iff a dial points into the upper half-plane. The dial rotates smoothly, slowing, so that at the halfway, quarter, eighth... points its angle is π/2, 3π/4, 7π/8..., converging to π. So the light flickers on/off infinitely often as you approach t = 1, basically the same oscillating mess as case 1. At t = 1 the dial points exactly at π, so the light is off. Determinate, off. And notice: the light's own on/off sequence does not converge at all, it oscillates forever, yet I'm confident there's a fact, because the thing driving it (the dial) settles down.

So cases 1 and 6 bother me. In 6 I get a fact even though the light itself never settles. In 1 I say there's no fact. The only difference I can point to is that in 6 there's a convergent underlying mechanism and in 1 there isn't. But if someone insists the lamp in case 1 is a real physical switch, then its mechanism is also a continuous physical thing and has to settle somewhere, which would give it a fact after all. Do I have to give up verdict 1? I want to keep it, but I can't tell whether I'm keeping it only because I'm secretly imagining the lamp as an abstract toggle rule with no mechanism underneath.

7) Gods and the runner. (I might be mangling this setup, sorry.) Infinitely many gods, god n acts at t = 1 - 1/2^n. Each one is rigged so that the runner has already stopped by the 1/n mark unless an earlier god got to him first. Upshot: the runner never reaches the wall. But there's no god who is the one who stopped him, and no last moment at which he was still moving. Verdict: he's stopped, determinate, but with no identifiable cause.

So my half-baked guess at a principle is this: an outcome is determinate iff the underlying physical state (not the outcome itself) is fully determined by the setup, and the outcome is then whatever that underlying state forces. The light's own flickering is irrelevant; what matters is whether the machinery underneath converges.

Does that actually save all seven? If you need to, test it against cases I haven't listed. And tell me which of my verdicts it can't accommodate, whether I'd be better off with a simpler principle that just overturns one of them, and where your reasoning is weakest / what would change your mind about the diagnosis. Also, if I'm just wrong about one of the cases, say so.
