I've been trying to get clear on what welfare-to-work conditionality actually presupposes, and I keep getting stuck. Where I live, if you're on unemployment benefit you have to document that you applied for so many jobs a week, and if you don't they sanction you -- cut your payment for weeks or months. Fine. But the same system treats a diagnosed disability as exempting you from work-related conditionality entirely, and it never conditions anyone's child benefit or pension tax relief on anything, and obviously nobody asks what a person did to deserve an inheritance.

What I want from you is the implicit theory. What view of responsibility, desert, or reciprocity does that whole arrangement only make sense under? Is it a control-based view -- you're answerable for outcomes you could have avoided, and the unemployed supposedly could but the disabled supposedly couldn't? Or is it a reciprocity view -- you owe something back for what you take, and the disabled are somehow outside that? And which parts of the practice actually need that theory and which just happen to fit it, because I genuinely can't tell whether the disability exemption is a real concession to a control view of responsibility or just political expediency bolted on. Then tell me what would have to change in the policy if you replaced the presupposed view with one of the rivals you list.
