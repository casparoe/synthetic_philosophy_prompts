I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy, mostly philosophy of mind but drifting into animal ethics, and I'm putting together a handout for a grad workshop we're running on the moral status of non-human minds. The thing I keep banging my head against is that the claim that sentience comes in degrees gets tossed around as if it settled everything, when actually there seem to be four or five seperate questions hiding inside it. So what I want from you is a taxonomy, laid out as a decision tree, of the positions on how sentience relates to moral status, built from the logical space rather than from a list of labels. Every branch node should be a question that genuinely splits the positions and that someone could answer without already knowing which label it leads to: is the status-conferring property phenomenal consciousness, valenced experience, having interests or being a subject of a life, or agency and self-awareness? Is that property binary or graded in itself, and if graded, continuous or tiered? Is the real disagreement about whether the property is graded at all, about whether moral status tracks it linearly, or about whether we could ever know either way? And is there a separate branch where the epistemic uncertainty itself is what generates status, so that a precautionary view sits somewhere distinct from the metaphysical ones? Each leaf should carry the name of the position, or 'unnamed' if nobody really holds it, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and I'd like the unoccupied combinations flagged and briefly diagnosed, i.e. is nobody there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

The cases I need the tree to run on are a honeybee or something like it, a fish, a bird, a dog, an LLM, a thermostat, and maybe a plant. Please make sure the tree can actually separate a panpsychist who says experience goes all the way down but moral status needs something more, from a utilitarian who scales status smoothly with the amount of valenced experience, from someone who thinks there's a single threshold you either cross or you don't. If your tree can't separate two of the positions I'd much rather you flag that than paper over it. And because this is going in front of people who will absolutely push back: please be careful to mark what's genuinely settled among people who work on this versus what's contested, and don't hand me some contested reading of, say, whether insects feel pain as though it were the consensus. Cheers, and sorry this got long, I've been staring at it too long.
