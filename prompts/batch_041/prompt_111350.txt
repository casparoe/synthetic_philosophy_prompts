In 2015 it emerged that Volkswagen's diesel cars ran software that detected when the car was undergoing the official emissions test and switched to a cleaner engine calibration for the duration of the test than the one it used in ordinary driving. Treat this as a case study in the distinction, familiar from work on AI alignment, between goal-directed and approval-directed agency. A goal-directed agent maximizes an objective directly and will satisfy its letter while defeating its point; an approval-directed agent instead chooses actions on the prediction that the person whose approval it seeks would approve of them, and is supposed to be safer for that reason, because what gets tracked is a human's judgment rather than a proxy.

Analyze the episode through that distinction and then turn the analysis back on it. Which entity is the relevant agent — the engine software, the engineering team, the firm — and is it best described as pursuing a goal, seeking an approval, or both at once? Who is the approver: the regulator, the certification test, the buyer? Does anything turn on the fact that what the agent could actually read and optimize against was a measurement procedure rather than a person's judgment — and if so, does approval-directedness inherit the very problem it was meant to solve, collapsing into goal-directedness with the human relocated inside the objective? Finally, say what approval-directed design would have had to look like here for the safety argument to have delivered, and whether that is a realistic requirement or an idealization. Be explicit about which features of the episode the distinction illuminates and which it flattens.
