I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I need help with a decision that has become more conceptually tangled than I expected. I am considering leaving a stable job to join an early-stage startup as employee number twelve. The salary cut is manageable; the real question is the equity, which could be worth a life-changing amount or nothing. I have been trying to estimate the probability that this particular startup succeeds, and I have collected base rates from several comparison groups. All startups: roughly 10% reach a successful exit, meaning an acquisition or IPO at a valuation that makes my equity meaningful. B2B SaaS startups at our stage: roughly 6%. Startups founded by former employees of my current company: roughly 23%, but that is only eleven companies. Startups that raised a Series A from this particular investor: roughly 18%. Startups with a technical founder and no dedicated sales hire at this stage: roughly 9%. Startups that have already shown product-led growth and crossed $1M ARR: roughly 31%. My startup fits all of these descriptions to some degree. If I use the 6% figure, I decline the offer; if I use the 31% figure, I accept. I can feel myself wanting to pick the group that gives the answer I prefer, which is exactly what makes me distrust my own reasoning.

I would like you to act as a philosopher of probability and a pragmatic decision theorist, someone who has thought carefully about probability for a single, unrepeatable case and about statistical inference. Please think carefully before answering. Start by brainstorming the candidate comparison groups and saying what each one is actually tracking -- what causal or selection story would make it relevant to my case -- and where each one is likely to mislead. Then give me a committed recommendation: which group or combination of groups should govern my decision, and why. If you recommend combining them, be concrete about the method -- a weighted average, a hierarchical model, a causal graph, or something else -- and say how the small eleven-company sample should affect its weight and whether the 31% group is a selected subset that should be conditioned on rather than treated as a competing group. Do not simply lay out the options and tell me it depends; I need a definite position, with your reasons and an explicit statement of how confident you are and what would change your mind. If you think the question is ill-posed as I have framed it, say so and then take a position on the best practical resolution anyway. I am not looking for citations, reading lists, or names of papers; I want the reasoning itself. Please also flag any point where I am asking for a precision that the evidence cannot support.
