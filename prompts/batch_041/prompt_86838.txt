For a project in my school's philosophy club, where each of us is expected to bring one puzzle a month rather than something we have simply read about, I have been trying to pin down a pattern that I have now watched twice, and I genuinely cannot tell whether I am looking at one phenomenon or at two that merely resemble each other. The first time was last spring, when a bakery opened on the corner near the school. Nothing about it recommended it in particular; its first customers were simply the people whose bus stop happened to be beside the door. But a line formed, and the line did the work. Classmates who had never set foot inside began telling me that it must be excellent, since look at the queue; by the third week people were waiting twenty minutes, and when I asked them why, the reason was always the queue itself. What I keep turning over is that the queue grew without any new reason to think the bread was any good. The people at the back of it had no information that the people at the front lacked; they had only the fact that the people at the front were standing there. The visible evidence was strengthening while the actual evidence stood still. It also collapsed almost overnight once a single unflattering review appeared, which suggests to me that the whole arrangement had been resting on very little.

The second case seems to me stranger, and it is why I am unsure whether the bakery belongs in the same category. Our school spent most of the autumn debating a change to the dress code. During the debate almost nobody spoke against the proposal, and afterwards it emerged that almost nobody had been for it: each of us had assumed that the silence of the others meant assent, and each of us had stayed quiet partly because we read everyone else's quiet in exactly the same way. Here I do not think anyone was copying anyone's choice. We were all reading an absence, and the absence was made out of us. Yet the result looks, from the outside, like the same thing as the queue.

So what I want is not really a label but the carving. Is there an established name for the pattern, or, if the honest answer is that these are two, what are the two names and where exactly does the line fall between them? I have heard of the bandwagon effect, but as far as I can tell that describes the fact of a crowd forming rather than the use of the crowd as evidence, and it does not distinguish the bakery from the dress code at all. What I would like to know is what the later people are treating as evidence in each case, whether that treatment is a mistake or simply a reasonable response to not knowing, whether the first movers matter or only their number, and what a case would look like that had a bit of both. If neither pattern quite matches anything, I would rather have a term invented for it and defined sharply enough that I could tell it apart from plain imitation and from the ordinary wish not to stand out.
