I am working from memory of a translation, so I paraphrase rather than quote, and I would rather you correct my reading than trust my wording. My problem is not the German or the rhetoric; it is that I cannot tell what kind of claim is being made. When he suggests that a particular air, a particular diet, a particular amount of walking and sleeping condition what a thinker is able to think, is this a physiological-causal claim of the sort I would recognize from my own field, thought as an output of a metabolism; or a weaker claim about the conditions of sustained creative work; or a claim about what one ought to attend to, which would be ethical rather than scientific? And in the same chapter, toward the end, he offers his formula for greatness, which turns on loving necessity — wanting nothing to be different, not forward, not backward, not in all eternity. I cannot see how that formula is supposed to sit with the diet-and-climate material that precedes it: one half of the chapter reads like a hygienic regimen, the other like a metaphysical affirmation, and I cannot find the hinge between them.
