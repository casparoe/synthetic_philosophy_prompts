I'm presenting next month at a meetup that has drifted into being roughly a third engineers and product people from the rationalist/EA orbit, a couple of people with science-and-technology-studies training, and one historian of technology — the engineers think artifacts are neutral tools and that politics is really about incentives, the STS pair think that's naive and will find my version of the opposite claim too weak, and the historian wants cases where a design was genuinely up for grabs and then stopped being. My thesis: some artifacts really are political, but not for the reason usually given. I don't want to rest it on a designer's intentions or on a social arrangement that got frozen into the design; I want to rest it on what the artifact makes easy and hard for different people, plus the fact that once the artifact is a standard, the asymmetry is held in place by the cost of being the first to deviate rather than by anyone preferring the asymmetry. I call that a coordination ratchet. So the politics is real, isn't reducible to anyone's preferences, and is still contingent — sticky rather than inevitable.
