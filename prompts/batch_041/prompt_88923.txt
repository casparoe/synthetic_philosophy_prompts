Write a short story of roughly 3,000 words, in American English, set in a near-future world where an international regulatory regime assigns animals legal standing and protection quotas according to a measured "neural index": the number of neurons in an animal's forebrain, counted directly rather than inferred from brain volume. Two consequences drive the world. First, because the count can only be taken on fixed, dissolved tissue, it is performed posthumously — the only way to learn how much a mind was worth is to end it and take the brain apart. Second, because the index displaces brain mass, it reshuffles rankings everyone had settled on: some species long dismissed as instinct machines come out crowded with neurons, while others long treated as nearly persons come out thin.

Your protagonist is a technician at a laboratory that runs these counts for the body that sets the quotas. Center the story on one specimen that matters to her personally — an animal she knew as an individual, whose number will decide the fate of its species. Through the events of the story, make the following pressures felt rather than explained:

- The index measures a proxy. One character should treat the number as the moral fact itself; another should insist that it is only evidence for something else — a capacity for valenced experience — and therefore only as trustworthy as the correlation.
- The scaling problem. Nothing in the framework specifies how moral weight should grow with the count. Linear, logarithmic, and threshold readings imply wildly different budgets: a few large-brained animals against a great many small-brained ones. Let the arithmetic of one such choice become visible in what is at stake for someone.
- The within-species question the framework's architects deliberately left unanswered, and what happens when someone presses on it.

Let the philosophy emerge from work, argument, and consequence rather than from exposition or staged debate. Don't resolve the underlying question; end on a concrete moment that leaves it open.
