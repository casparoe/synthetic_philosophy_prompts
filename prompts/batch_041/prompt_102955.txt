Right, I'm prepping for a seminar on death and I want a sanity check before I open my mouth — here's roughly where I've landed, and I want to know if it actually hangs together.

1. Death is bad for you because of what it deprives you of — the good life you'd otherwise have gone on to have. That's the whole story. It isn't bad in itself, it's bad in what it takes away.
2. There's no time at which death is bad for you. Once you're dead there's no you left for it to happen to, so "when is death bad for you?" is a confused question — it's not a state you're in, like a headache.
3. But I still think you have to be made worse off for something to count as bad for you. Harm has to be grounded in a comparison between how you actually fare and how you'd otherwise have fared.
4. The Epicurean line — death is nothing to us, because where death is we are not — is just wrong, I'm confident of that.
5. I don't think you can be harmed after you're dead, though. If someone trashes my reputation once I've snuffed it, that's a bad thing that happened, but it isn't bad for me.
6. And the Lucretius symmetry thing doesn't bother me. Yeah, I didn't exist for the eternity before I was born either, but that doesn't mean I missed out on anything in the way that death makes me miss out. I know this is supposed to be the awkward one for anyone who says 1.
7. One more: I think death is worse for you the more you had lined up to look forward to. Dying the night before your wedding is worse than dying a month later with nothing particular coming up, even if that extra month is just as good on paper.

I definately suspect there's something in here that doesn't hang together but I honestly cant see where.
