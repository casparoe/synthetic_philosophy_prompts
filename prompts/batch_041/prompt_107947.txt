My 16-year-old has worked out a argument and I can't get him out of it, and the worst part is I think he might be right.

The argument, as best I can put it: if I'm going to blame him for something he did, he has to have been able to do otherwise. But doing otherwise would mean being a slightly different person — different enough to make the other choice. And to be responsible for being that person, he'd have to have chosen to be that way, which just moves the question back a step, and back and back, until you hit the stuff he never picked: his temperament, his wiring, how he was raised, what happened to him at nine. You can't deserve blame for something that goes all the way down to stuff you didn't choose. And, he adds, it goes for praise too — don't tell him you're proud of him, it wasn't him.

That started out as a defense. He broke a window at his mom's place and informed me I wasn't allowed to be angry about it. Fine, he's 16, that's what 16 looks like. But it's been about six months now. He's quit the two things he was actually good at, he says there's no point getting better at anything if he doesn't get credit for it, he's stopped accepting apologies ("they couldn't help it") and stopped offering them. It's not depression, or I don't think it is — he's got friends, he's good company, he's just weirdly calm about it, like he's reading out a weather report.

My wife and I tried something for a few weeks: no blame talk at all. We stopped saying "you should have" and started saying "here's what we'll do next time." Honestly it worked for a bit, he seemed lighter. But it creeped me out, like we'd agreed to treat him as a machine we were reprogramming. She thinks if we keep it up we're just admitting he's right.

So, two things. Is the argument any good, or is there a hole in it I've been staring past for six months? And if it is good — what do you actually do with someone who's got it right and has decided that means he's off the hook for everything, including for wanting anything?
