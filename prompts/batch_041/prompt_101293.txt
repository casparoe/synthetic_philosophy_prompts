Write a joke about Aumann's agreement theorem. Avoid the well-worn gag about two economists who cannot disagree; the humor should come from the actual machinery of the result. A good target is the tension between the theorem's two conditions: a common prior and common knowledge of the agents' posteriors. The joke might turn on the fact that the theorem forces agreement the instant a disagreement is made common knowledge, so a genuine disagreement can survive only if the prior was never really shared (the Harsanyi doctrine doing all the work), or it might turn on the infinite regress of I-know-that-you-know-that-I-know that common knowledge requires — a regress an agent can never actually complete, so the moment of forced agreement keeps receding. Set the scene wherever it sharpens the joke, ideally somewhere common knowledge is genuinely hard to come by (a trading floor, a divorce mediation, a tenure committee), but the punchline must land for someone who has read Aumann rather than merely heard the phrase, and the setup must not misstate the theorem in a way a competent reader would wince at.
