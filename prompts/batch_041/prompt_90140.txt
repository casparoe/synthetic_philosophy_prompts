I host a small philosophy podcast, the kind where the audience is mostly grad students and people who've read a bit too much metaphysics for their own good, and next month's guest is someone I've been wanting on for ages. She's spent roughly a decade defending a broadly dispositionalist package: her line is that once you take finkish cases and masking cases seriously, the whole project of analysing disposition ascriptions with counterfactual conditionals is a dead end, and the right move is to treat dispositions as primitive, intrinsic, and essentially directed at their manifestations, with the consequence that laws come out necessary rather than contingent. She isn't famous enough that my listeners will know the details of her version, so don't assume any particular paper and don't have me ask her for a reading list or to situate herself in the literature — that's a different episode. What I want is eight to ten questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, so I can keep the pressure on instead of letting her restate the view; a one-line note on what each follow-up is aimed at would help me a lot. I definately want one question squarely on finks — specifically whether the standard electro-fink style case, the live wire whose power is cut the instant it's touched, is best read as the disposition being destroyed by the stimulus or as the disposition simply not being present at the relevant time — one squarely on masks (the sugar cube sealed in plastic, the fragile glass packed in foam), where I want her to say whether masking is genuinely a different problem from finking or just the same problem seen from the other end, and one on mimics, where the conditional fails in the other direction, since I suspect that the failure of sufficiency hurts the conditional analysis worse than the failure of necessity and I'd like to know whether she agrees.

Then some cases of my own that I'd like her to place in her taxonomy. First: a wire that is genuinely live at every moment when nobody is about to touch it, because a sensor cuts the current the moment anyone forms the intention to touch it and restores it afterwards — so at the moment of the stimulus it really is dead, but it's live all the rest of the time. Is that a fink, a disposition that was never present at the relevant time, or does the vocabulary just break down here, and does it matter that the trigger is an intention rather than a physical event? Second: a cube rigged so that contact with water at once coats it in a polymer and rearranges its lattice, so it's unclear whether we should say the disposition was masked or removed. If a single case can be both at once, is the fink/mask distinction doing any real work, or is it a distinction that only looks sharp in the textbook examples? I'd also like one question on the regress worry about pure powers — that if every property is a power grounded in further powers you never arrive at anything that actually happens — with a follow-up pressing whether that forces her to accept a categorical base, and whether accepting one collapses her into the Humean picture she's rejecting. One more on whether a dispositionalist account of belief survives the same treatment (a belief that vanishes the moment you're asked about it, a belief that's really there but always masked by a stronger desire), since half my audience comes from philosophy of mind, and then close with the question I actually care about: what would have to be true for her to give up the essentialist part, and does she think anything we discussed today could do it. Don't ask her anything about her career or how she got into philosophy, and order the questions so the pressure escalates rather than front-loading the hardest one — thats the whole reason I want the follow-ups spelled out.
