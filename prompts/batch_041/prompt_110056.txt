I cover science and ideas for a national daily, and next month I am running a seventy-five-minute session for our graphics desk — about a dozen designers and data reporters, none of them with any philosophy behind them — on Otto Neurath's picture language. I don't want the set-piece retelling (Vienna, the Social and Economic Museum, Gerd Arntz's pictograms, one symbol equals one unit), and I don't want the material explained to me; I want you to plan how I explain it. The point I want the desk to leave with is narrower and less comfortable than the usual one: that this picture language was neither a neutral transcription of figures nor a language in its own right — Neurath treated it as a help to ordinary verbal language rather than a substitute for it, the claim any chart made was authored by whoever did the "transformation" of the source data (in Vienna, Marie Reidemeister), and the same method travelled comfortably into Soviet planning graphics of the Five Year Plans, which should embarrass anyone who reads the pictograms as ideology-free. My provisional device is to hand the same statistic out twice — as a bare figure and as a row of repeated figures of the kind newsrooms still print, the covid unit charts in which one dot stood for a fixed number of deaths being the obvious descendant — and let the hour turn on what the second version can say that the first cannot and what it cannot say at all. If that device is the wrong one, tell me what it lacks and give me the case that should carry the session instead.

Answer with exactly five numbered items and nothing else — no opening, no closing paragraph — one or two sentences each: first, the order in which the ideas have to arrive and what depends on what; second, the one case or exhibit to build the session on and what it must have and lack; third, the misconceptions the desk will walk in with and the one my own framing is most likely to plant, with the point in the sequence where each gets headed off; fourth, the question I put at the end that shows whether the point landed rather than merely impressed them; fifth, what I should openly announce I am leaving out at this level.
