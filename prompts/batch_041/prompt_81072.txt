For my bioethics class I have to write about the definition of death, and I would rather not write another essay arguing for one side. Instead I want to write the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between two camps could produce. The first camp holds that a person is dead once all function of the brain, including the brainstem, has irreversibly ceased, so that a patient on a ventilator who meets the standard tests is dead. The second holds that death is the irreversible cessation of circulatory and respiratory function, so that the same patient is not dead, whatever the law may say.

Please give me, first, a numbered list of claims both camps could sign — about what the standard tests actually establish, about what is and is not true of the ventilated body, about how the word irreversible should be understood here, and about which parts of the dispute concern the concept of death rather than any medical fact. Second, a table of the disagreements that remain, each stated in the wording the side holding it would accept, with the crux behind it, where a crux is a sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side. Third, for each crux, one concrete case that both sides agree bears on it — a protocol that declares death a few minutes after the heart stops, or a patient whose brainstem has been destroyed while the rest of the body is maintained — with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

If part of the dispute is really verbal — if, for instance, one side is using the word death for a biological event and the other for a legal status — say which part and why. And when you set something down as agreed, make clear whether it is common ground among everyone who works on this question or only between these two camps; a third position, that death is the loss of consciousness or of the higher brain, is not a party to this collaboration, and I do not want a contested view to read as the consensus.
