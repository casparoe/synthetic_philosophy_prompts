(1) Panpsychism — the view that experience goes all the way down, that the fundamental ingredients of the world each have some faint inner life — is at least as respectable as any other story about consciousness.
(2) If the fundamental ingredients each have inner life, then each of them is a subject of experience, a tiny someone for whom things can go well or badly.
(3) For any subject of experience there's something it's like to be it, and wherever there's something it's like to be something, harm is possible.
(4) Ethics is impartial: every subject that can be harmed counts, and counts equally, unless we've got a special reason to weight it otherwise.
(5) The fundamental ingredients vastly outnumber creatures like us — every quark, every electron, every field excitation.
(6) So any ordinary human interest I'd weigh in a decision is swamped by an astronomically larger pool of these tiny interests, all equally deserving of consideration.
(7) Nobody could even begin to compute that pool, and whatever I do will sacrifice some enormous number of these tiny subjects.
(8) Therefore no action is more justified than any other and morality is a dead letter.
