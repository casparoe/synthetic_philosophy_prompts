For the past three years I have been part of a small online community — a few hundred amateurs who build and tune mechanical keyboards and trade recordings of how they sound. Within that circle a single word does an enormous amount of work: clean. When someone posts a recording and asks whether it is clean, the replies sort into camps that neither agree nor quite disagree, because they are answering different questions. To some members, clean means free of rattle and ping, any noise that is not the switch itself. To others it means a low, dry, evenly pitched knock with no stray frequencies — a positive ideal rather than the mere absence of defects. To a third group it means the recording was made cleanly, with no room hum and no clipping, so a board can be clean in that sense while sounding, to the first group, like a rattling tin. I have watched threads run to fifty comments in which nobody contradicts anyone and yet nothing is settled, because the sense of the word shifts with each reply. What holds my attention is that the shift is not random: each use is a short analogical step from the one before it, and over roughly two years the center of the word has migrated — the recordings people call clean today are ones that would have been called muddy when I joined, and the earliest members have not registered the change. I have noticed something similar in a second, unrelated circle, a running club whose word easy slides quietly between not fast for me and not fast for anyone, so I doubt this is peculiar to keyboards.

Is there a name for this? I do not mean ambiguity or homonymy, where a word has two or more settled readings one could list in a dictionary, nor simple vagueness at the edges. I mean a word stable enough for a community to feel that it names one concept, yet whose center of gravity travels as each use extends it by analogy to the next case, so that at any given moment it is doing several jobs at once and over years it has become a different concept while everyone keeps using the old word. My suspicion is that the closest literature concerns words held together by a loose cluster of examples rather than a definition, or words that are irreducibly contested; but those accounts describe looseness that stays put, whereas the thing I keep seeing is looseness that moves, and moves in a direction, with each extension licensing the next. If there is an established term I would like to know it, but a bare name would not satisfy me. I want the phenomenon carved at its joints: what exactly it is; how it differs from ordinary ambiguity, from vagueness, and from a word simply acquiring a new sense while shedding the old one; and where its boundaries lie — for instance, whether the same thing is happening when a single expert's usage drifts while the community does not follow, or when a community converges on a new sense without any individual experiencing a slip.
