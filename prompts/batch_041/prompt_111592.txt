I'm a science-and-ideas reporter, and a big chunk of my job lately is sitting in on expert panels — for the last six months it's been a hospital research-ethics board and a grant-review panel at a funding agency, and before that a long run of city planning meetings — and I keep running into a shape of thing that I don't have a word for, and it's starting to bother me because I can't write the sentence without the word. The cleanest version is the ethics board. It reviews protocols one at a time and approves nearly all of them, usually 8–1 or 9–0, and on any single protocol the majority and the dissent are both making real arguments — nobody's being lazy, nobody's deferring to the chair. But when I tabulated a whole year of approvals for a piece I was doing, the board had effectively committed itself to a policy — about who bears risk, about what counts as consent — that I'm fairly sure not one member would sign their name to if you put it to them as one proposition. And here's the part I can't get past: every individual member's own voting record is internally consistent. I asked several of them. From where each one sits, their record hangs together fine. The incoherence only exists when you look at the group's output as a whole, and it's invisible from any one seat.

Then a new chair came in and started bundling items that used to be voted on seperately, and the outcomes changed — same members, same opinions, same arguments in the room, different results. So the agenda, the order, the bundling are doing real work, not just the reasons being offered. I got the same feeling from the planning commission: each variance granted was individually reasonable, and after two years the cumulative effect was a rule that had quietly been rewritten without anyone ever voting to rewrite it. My first instincts were "that's a slippery slope" and "that's just groupthink," but neither really fits — there's no pressure and no single bad step, every step is fine on its own, and in the ethics-board case nobody's mind is even changing.

So: is there a name for this? I have a hunch it's a known thing, maybe in the social-choice or group-decision literature, but every version I've half-remembered turns out to be about a single vote on a set of linked propositions, and mine is spread across a year of votes on unrelated cases — plus the whole business about order and bundling mattering. What I actually need is the carving, not a label: is this one phenomenon or am I lumping two together? What's it most like, and where exactly does each nearby idea stop fitting — how is it different from plain inconsistency, from a slippery slope, from a body just drifting or changing its mind over time? If nothing established covers it, coin me a term and give me its edges: what would count as a borderline case, and what's clearly outside it. Please don't hand me a reading list — I'm filing on deadline and can't chase any of it down, so just tell me directly.
