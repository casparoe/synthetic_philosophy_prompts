Bit of background first, because it probably matters: I did a chemistry degree years ago and never touched philosophy of science, but I read a lot of popular physics, and I've been arguing about string theory and the multiverse with a mate of mine who's doing a PhD in the area. I keep losing, which is annoying me enough that I'm writing this.

The thing I keep losing on is a move I can't put a name to, and I'm not sure whether it's a recognised fallacy, a legitimate bit of reasoning, or two different things that people slide between without noticing. Normally a theory earns credit by sticking its neck out: it says the number will be X, and X is what gets measured. In these arguments the credit seems to run the other way. The theory says there's a gigantic collection of possible outcomes -- different values for this or that constant, different histories, different everything -- and then it turns out that one of them is the one we actually live in, and that's treated as a point in the theory's favour. When a particular expectation fails, nobody tears anything up; they say the bit that failed was an improbable corner of the collection, or that the collection was the wrong size, or that the measure was wrong, and the theory carries on. So the very feature that makes me suspicious -- that it can accommodate practically any result we could get -- gets sold to me as the reason to believe it. I've seen the same shape in a few places: the tiny-but-nonzero cosmological constant gets mentioned in this sort of argument, and I've noticed my mum's horoscope page, and a football pundit saying before a derby that it could go any way and afterwards claiming he'd called it, doing something that feels structurally identical.

What I can't work out is whether this is one thing or two, because I notice people sliding between two versions. In version one the theory genuinely says anything could have happened, so the fact that something happened tells you nothing. In version two the theory says these specific things could have happened, with roughly these probabilities, and then a result it gave a low probability to is presented as impressive, as confirmation -- the width of the range gets turned into evidence. Same slide as the horoscope saying you'll face a challenge this month and the reader treating it as a hit. So: is there an established name for the move itself, and separately for the slide between the versions? I know about falsifiability, and I know the phrase ad hoc, and neither definately fits, because nobody here is refusing to let evidence count -- they're counting it, just in a direction I can't follow. It also doesn't feel the same as a theory simply having knobs that get twiddled until they match the data, though I couldn't say precisely why not. What I want isn't a label so much as a carving: what exactly is going on, which established concepts come nearest and how each of them falls short, where the edges of the thing are, and whether version two is ever actually legitimate -- and if it is, what has to be true for it to be. British English please.
