I'm a second-year PhD student in cognitive science, and I need a tier list for a reading group on the symbol grounding problem: rank the main strategies for solving it by how much of the problem each actually solves, with a sentence of justification for each placement. The candidates are grounding through embodiment and sensorimotor contingency; grounding through the co-evolution of words and categories in populations of interacting agents, so that meaning is negotiated rather than stipulated; grounding through perceptual symbol systems that re-enact modality-specific experience; teleosemantic accounts, on which content is fixed by the biological function of the states that carry it; and hybrid architectures that keep the symbols and let a distributed network supply their content.

Apply one criterion throughout, since it is the one I have been arguing for: a state is grounded only if it can be wrong — only if there is a fact of the matter about what it represents, independent of what any interpreter takes it to represent. An account that secures reference only relative to an interpreter's purposes earns credit for engineering utility but not for grounding.

Rate one further item, invented for the occasion: the hermetic system, a network trained only on a large corpus of human text about the world, with no sensory contact with the world, which reproduces all the inferential moves its training data licences. Say which tier it belongs in, and in what sense, if any, its internal states could be about anything. Please write your answer in British English.
