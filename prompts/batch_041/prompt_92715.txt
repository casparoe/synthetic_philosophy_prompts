I am preparing a one-year postdoctoral fellowship application and need a research proposal of at most 1,500 words. My background is in mathematical logic and formal epistemology, and I want to work on what is often called the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents — but I want to make it a philosophical question rather than an engineering one, and I have only a half-formed idea of how.

Here is the material, so you can see what I am working with. Löb's theorem: for a theory T at least as strong as arithmetic, if T proves that (T proves P) implies P, then T proves P. Equivalently, no such theory can consistently prove, for every P, the local reflection schema for its own provability predicate — roughly, 'if I can prove P, then P'; taking P to be a contradiction recovers the second incompleteness theorem, and the result is closely related to Curry's paradox. Read doxastically, it says that a reasoner with positive introspection over its own beliefs must be 'modest': roughly, it cannot believe that if it believed P then P, without already believing P. The informal upshot is that an agent reasoning in a language rich enough to speak about its own proofs cannot adopt the flat policy 'whatever I have derived, is so'. As far as I understand the technical work that grew out of this, the available moves are all ways of weakening the demand — trusting only a successor system strictly weaker than the one doing the trusting, or trusting yourself only up to some bound — so that self-trust becomes stratified or graded rather than flat. I may be misdescribing that work, and getting the taxonomy right would itself be part of the project.

What I actually want to know is whether the obstacle is a discovery about rationality or an artifact of one particular way of representing self-trust. Two readings pull apart. On the first, the theorem shows that no agent can be transparent to itself: trust must be partial and stratified, and the impossibility of flat self-trust is the formal shadow of a real epistemic predicament — the same shape as the older circularity problem about establishing the reliability of one's own faculties without presupposing them. On the second, the obstacle is parochial: it exists only because we model self-trust as a theorem schema inside the very language that is trusted, and no agent, ideal or otherwise, needs to carry its trust in itself as a proposition in its own object language.

Say why this is the right approach rather than the obvious alternatives — for instance, simply doing more proof theory, or drawing the analogy to epistemic circularity and stopping there.
