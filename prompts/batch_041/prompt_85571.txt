Anekāntavāda is the Jain doctrine that reality has many sides, so no statement about it is ever more than partially true. The Jains spelled this out in syādvāda, which is also called nayavāda, the doctrine of standpoints, and which runs as a sevenfold scheme (the saptabhaṅgī): for any object and any predicate there are seven possibilities -- it is, it is not, it both is and is not, it neither is nor is not, and combinations of those -- each prefixed with syād, which means maybe or perhaps. So every philosophical position, Jainism included, is at best probably true; and this is why the Jains are the classic defenders of tolerance and non-violence, since if nobody has the whole truth you can never justify harming someone who disagrees with you. The usual objection is that the doctrine refutes itself: if every claim is only partly true then so is the claim that every claim is only partly true, and if everything is true from some angle then even the opposite of Jainism is true, which is absurd.
