I am putting my own position on price controls into writing for a seminar, and I want it audited for internal consistency before it is attacked in public. Here is the set:

(1) A market price is not, as such, a just price; whether a transaction is just depends on the background distribution of power and information between the parties.
(2) A legal price control is justified only when it corrects a specific defect of that background — a monopoly, a monopsony, or a coercive emergency — and not merely because it would help those whom the uncontrolled price excludes.
(3) The fact that a control would leave people better off on balance is never by itself a reason to adopt it.
(4) Rent control in a tight housing market is unjust: it leaves future tenants and would-be residents worse off than they would otherwise be, and the gains to sitting tenants do not outweigh that.
(5) A substantial minimum wage is usually justified, because low-wage labour markets are monopsonistic, so employers are already holding the wage below the competitive level.

I have a sense that the set is not as tidy as it looks, but no idea which part is load-bearing. Tell me which commitments genuinely conflict and which only appear to, ranking the genuine conflicts by severity. For each genuine conflict, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost me elsewhere in the set. Where a conflict is merely apparent, say in a sentence what dissolves it. If one commitment is doing more damage than the rest, name it.
