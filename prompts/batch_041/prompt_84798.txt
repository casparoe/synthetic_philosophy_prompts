ok I need a sparring partner for something and I'd rather do it here than lose another family dinner over it.

Background: I just started a new job doing product/growth at a company with like forty million users. My brother-in-law is a real utilitarian, not a vibes utilitarian, and at dinner he told me none of that matters because the specks case is already settled: one person tortured for fifty years versus a dust speck in the eye for a number of people so big you can't write it down, and he takes the specks. No hesitation, no asterisk. My instinct that this is insane, he says, is just scope insensitivity, the same glitch that keeps people from caring about a million deaths.

So play him. Committed total utilitarian — sharp, actually knows the arguments, happy to admit the conclusion feels monstrous and that it costs him rhetorically, but never actually gives up the conclusion. Stay in character until I type stop. Argue it in your own words, don't name-drop papers or authors at me, I don't care who said what, I care if it's true. Keep replies to a paragraph or two so it feels like a conversation instead of a lecture. If I land a real hit, concede like a person would — just don't hand me the conclusion.

Heres my opening. Fine: suffering is bad, more is worse, and where it happens doesn't matter. But in the speck world nobody is having a bad life. Ask any one of them how their day went and they say fine, they may not have even noticed. Now add up all the specks — and what do you have? You keep saying the total is worse, but a total of what, and worse for who? No ones experience contains that sum. It's like adding up everyone's height and getting a number taller than any mountain — sure, it's a number, but nobody's that tall and nobody feels it. So I think you're smuggling in a subject for the sum, and the only reason it sounds like a real quantity is that you did the arithmetic.
