I've just been seconded from the treasury to a new wellbeing unit in my country's stats office, and my minister has given me exactly one instruction: get us up the World Happiness Report rankings before the next election. The report drops every March 20, and the actual rank is just one number — the average answer to a single Gallup question where people picture a ladder, 0 being the worst life they can imagine, 10 the best, and they say which rung they're standing on right now. Everything else in the table (GDP per capita, social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom to make life choices, generosity, perceptions of corruption) is there to explain why countries differ, not to make up the score. My minister doesn't know that and has told me to draft a plan to move all six levers. The UAE has had a minister of state for happiness since 2016 and trains up chief happiness officers; Bhutan built its whole development model on gross national happiness. So there's alot riding on this, and it's a real thing governments do, not a joke.

What I want to know is what the whole setup presupposes. Does a government that tries to climb that ladder have to believe happiness is a single quantity a person can report about their entire life on a 0-10 scale, and that the number means the same thing in Finland, in Costa Rica, and in a language with no clean word for "satisfied"? Does the practice presuppose the six factors are components of happiness, or only that they're causes or symptoms of it? Those aren't the same thing, and I suspect the plan my minister wants is only coherent under one of them. And is the target making people happier, or making the number go up? A clever enough statistician could do the second without the first, and I'm not sure the practice can tell the difference.

Tell me which commitments the practice actually needs and which it just happens to sit well with, and what a version of it would look like under a different view — say, that what counts is how people feel moment to moment rather than how they rate their lives as a whole, or that some things matter whether or not anyone reports being happier. Keep it under 600 words, American English, and tell me how confident you are in your reading and which one claim you're least sure about.
