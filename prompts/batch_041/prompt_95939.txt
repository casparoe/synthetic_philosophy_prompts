I am a high school student and the president of my school's philosophy club. Our seminar this term has been on the epistemic regress — the problem that a justified belief seems to need a reason, and that reason seems to need a reason of its own — and on the three positions usually offered as solutions: foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism. Near the end of the term I set myself the exercise of writing out, as carefully as I could, the argument that none of the three works. The result was an argument whose every step I still find obvious and whose conclusion I cannot accept, and I would like help finding where it goes wrong.

The argument:

(1) A belief is justified only if something justifies it; call whatever plays that role a reason.
(2) Every reason is either a belief or not a belief.
(3) Suppose first that every reason is a belief. Then the reasons for any justified belief form a series of beliefs, each justifying the one before it. That series is either finite, circular, or infinite, and there are no other possibilities.
(4) If it is finite, it ends in a belief for which there is no further reason. A belief for which there is no reason is arbitrary, and an arbitrary belief cannot justify anything. So a finite series cannot justify its last member.
(5) If it is circular, some belief is, at some remove, a reason for itself. Nothing is justified by itself, and going around a circle adds nothing that was not already in it, so no belief in the circle is justified.
(6) If it is infinite, then the belief at the near end is justified by a series that never terminates. But no finite mind can entertain, let alone check, an infinite series of reasons, so an infinite series cannot justify anything for anyone.
(7) Therefore, if every reason is a belief, no belief is justified.
(8) Suppose instead that some reasons are not beliefs — an experience, a seeming, a memory-image, a felt obviousness. Either such a state justifies a belief in virtue of the kind of state it is, or it justifies only because something further justifies it.
(9) If it justifies only because something further justifies it, the regress has merely been moved one step, and (3) through (7) apply again.
(10) If it justifies in virtue of the kind of state it is, then a state can justify a belief without being justified itself, simply by being the state it is. But then the same privilege is available to beliefs: a belief could justify itself simply by being the belief it is, and the objection in (5) to circular justification would be no objection at all.
(11) So the regress stops, if it stops, only by a privilege that, if it is genuine, was already available to the options ruled out in (4) through (6), and that, if it is not genuine, leaves the regress unstopped.
(12) Therefore no belief is ever justified.

I would most like to keep (1) and (2), which look to me like definitions rather than claims, and I cannot accept (12). Everything between them still looks obvious to me when I read it one line at a time.

Two members of the club have offered diagnoses I cannot evaluate. One says the argument refutes itself, since (12), if it were justified, would be a counterexample to itself, and that the argument equally discredits every inferential step used to reach it, modus ponens included; I can see that this is true, but I cannot tell whether it is a real objection or only a paradox-shaped complaint. The other says the argument assumes that justification is like a current that has to flow into a belief from somewhere, and that once that picture is dropped the argument has no grip on anything; I cannot tell whether that is a deep point or a way of changing the subject.

Please tell me which step I should give up. For whichever step you choose, I would like an explanation of why it looked so obvious, and a price: what a foundationalist, a coherentist, and an infinitist each has to pay in order to reject it, and whether any of the three can reject it for free. If the trouble is that some word in the argument is doing two jobs, please say which word, and which two jobs.
