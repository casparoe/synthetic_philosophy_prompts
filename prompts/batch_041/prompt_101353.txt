Kant holds that we have no direct duties to animals: cruelty to a dog is wrong only because it coarsens the person who commits it, never because the dog is wronged. I keep noticing that on this account the animal never actually appears in the moral ledger — it is less a patient than a piece of equipment for the agent's own moral upkeep — and that the reasoning has nothing essentially to do with animals at all. Smashing a beautiful vase could be condemned on precisely the same grounds, since that coarsens me just as much. Write a joke that turns on this. I want it to be funny to someone who genuinely knows the doctrine, with the humour coming out of its real structure — that the creature's suffering is never what does the work, only the state of the agent's soul — rather than out of generic philosopher-bashing or mere cruelty to animals.
