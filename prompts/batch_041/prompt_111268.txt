Suppose you are advising a funder who can support roughly four researchers for two years on the outside view of empires: forecasting the trajectories of contemporary great powers from base rates and reference classes drawn from past states. Which open questions should they prioritize, and in what order? I want the reasoning more than the list. For each question, say whether it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is with historical data rather than formal models, whether it is neglected relative to its importance, and what evidence over the next two years would move it up or down the ranking. Address squarely the objection that the whole enterprise is underdetermined: any present-day power can be sorted into indefinitely many reference classes (all states, all agrarian empires, all hegemons with a peer competitor), the base rate shifts with the choice, and the historical record is itself filtered by which polities kept records. Tell me whether that objection is fatal, manageable, or the question most worth funding, and whether decline names a real process or a story told after the fact. If you think the money is better spent on narrower targets -- the resilience of particular institutions rather than the lifespan of empires -- say so and defend it.
