Write an essay on the following controversy in the metaphysics of dispositions, and reach a verdict.

Background. One tradition treats dispositions as analyzable by counterfactual conditionals: roughly, an object is disposed to manifest M when stimulus S occurs just in case, were S to occur, M would occur — a glass is fragile just in case it would break if struck. Three families of counterexample are standard. Finkish cases: the occurrence of S removes the disposition before it can be exercised, so the counterfactual is false although the object seems to have the disposition. Masking cases: the disposition is present, but something blocks the pathway from S to M, so the counterfactual is false. Mimicking cases: the object lacks the disposition, but something else produces M, so the counterfactual is true. Two repairs are commonly proposed. The first adds a clause: M would occur if S occurred and no interfering factors were present, or under normal conditions. The second abandons conditionals for an intrinsic causal basis: x is disposed to M under S just in case x has an intrinsic property P whose instantiation, together with S, brings about M. The objections are equally standard. The interference clause looks either circular, since what counts as interference seems to be fixed by reference to what the disposition would do, or vacuous, since any failure can be blamed on some unstated interference or missing enabling condition. The basis account looks circular too, since P appears to be identified as the basis only by reference to the disposition it is meant to explain.

Two further cases should serve as the test of any account.

(A) The inert explosive. A powder is chemically such that, mixed with one particular catalyst, it detonates; alone, or mixed with anything else, it does nothing. Does the powder have a disposition to detonate? The absence of the catalyst is not naturally described as interference, yet the simple counterfactual fails. If the repair is a clause requiring that enabling conditions be met, say what that clause adds to the original analysis and why it does not render the analysis unfalsifiable. Consider also the tempting reply that the powder is disposed to detonate when mixed with the catalyst, and whether permitting arbitrarily complex stimuli is a legitimate refinement or a way of making the analysis true by construction.

(B) The self-annulling solvent. A substance's molecules have the very structure P that, in other substances, makes them dissolve in water. In this substance, however, P is unstable: contact with water causes P to rearrange immediately into an inert structure, and nothing ever dissolves. No external factor is involved; the property that would otherwise be the causal basis of solubility destroys itself on contact with the stimulus. Read at the level of property types, the basis account says the substance is water-soluble, though it would never dissolve; read at the level of what P actually does here, the account collapses back into a conditional analysis. Say which reading is correct and why.

Your essay should state as precisely as you can the version of the conditional analysis you are assessing, show how it fares on finkish, masking, and mimicking cases and on (A) and (B), and then reach a verdict. Candidate verdicts include: a suitably qualified conditional analysis survives; dispositions are properly understood through their intrinsic bases, with conditionals as defeasible symptoms only; or neither reduction succeeds and dispositional predicates name a sui generis ontological category. Defend whichever you choose. The essay must engage the strongest objection to your own verdict and answer it, and must say what is lost by accepting your verdict — for instance, whether the analysis still does explanatory work, and whether the question whether something has a disposition remains a determinate matter. Argue from the cases and from general considerations about analysis, circularity, and vacuity; no citations or literature survey are required.
