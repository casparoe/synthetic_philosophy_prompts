I'm a high school senior who reads a lot about artificial intelligence on my own time, since my school offers nothing on the subject, and I recently got absorbed in a long argument thread about whether making a system more capable also tends to make it safer. One participant tried to settle the matter by saying that intelligence and goals are orthogonal — that how smart a mind is and what it ultimately wants have nothing to do with one another, so a very capable system could be devoted to some arbitrary obsession just as easily as to curing cancer, and you cannot read a system's values off its competence. He then brought in the idea of instrumental convergence — that almost any goal would push a sufficiently capable agent toward the same intermediate aims, such as surviving, accumulating resources, and resisting being switched off — and concluded that a smarter system is not thereby a nicer one and will drift toward seizing power no matter what we intend. The slogan seemed to be doing an enormous amount of work in his argument, and I suspect several quite different claims are packed into those four words, only some of which the argument actually needs.

Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that "intelligence and goals are orthogonal" could be asserting, ranging from the almost trivially true to the sweeping and contentious, and for each one say whether it is defensible on its own and whether it would actually deliver the conclusion he wanted — that greater capability does not bring greater safety and that power-seeking is close to inevitable? I want a menu I can hold side by side rather than a single verdict, since I think the disagreement is really about which reading is in play.

Please give me exactly five numbered readings, each one a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion. For each, state what the thesis amounts to, whether it is trivial, substantive, or false, and whether it supports the conclusion the slogan was being used to reach.
