I coach an under-12s football team in a small town. The league only let me take the job on the understanding that I'd pick the side on merit, because my own lad's in the squad and nobody else would run it. We got to the cup final and I picked my son over a boy who is honestly a bit better then him — better touch, more goals, and the sort who'll quietly stop turning up if he gets knocked back. His mum asked me at the school gate why, and I heard myself giving her reasons: better in the air, trains harder. Sort of true. Not the real reason. The real reason is that he's my son and I wanted him to have that day. But when I try to imagine standing there weighing up whether my kid or hers deserves it more, the whole picture feels off — like the demand to weigh them is the problem, not the pick itself. My mate, who did a term of philosophy, says that if I can't justify it without saying "because he's mine", then it wasn't a fair pick and I should own up to that. Is he right? And what should I actually do — apologise, stand by the reasons I gave, or something else?
