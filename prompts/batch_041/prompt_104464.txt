I've basically talked myself into Parfit's line on the teletransporter: that what matters in survival isn't identity, it's psychological continuity — memories, character, the projects and cares carrying over — and whether the substrate is my old brain or a fresh one assembled in the receiving pod is beside the point. The person who walks out isn't me in the strict sense, and I've decided I dont really care.

What I want is a map of what would count against this. Not the usual "but is it really you" hand-waving — I want the stuff that should actually move someone who's already bought the reductionist picture. Which intuitions, which cases, which results in other areas (say, about persistence or about what a person is) would the view stand or fall with? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward.

A few specific things I've been chewing on, use them if they're useful:
- a transporter that doesn't destroy the original, or one that rebuilds me from a scan taken years ago — so what wakes up is continuous with who I was at 22, not who I am now. Does the view give an answer I can live with there, or does it end up saying the 22-year-old who wakes up is just as much "my survival" as a copy made from last Tuesday's scan?
- the "right kind of cause" bit: Parfit wants the continuity to have the right kind of cause, so a duplicate that pops into existence by cosmic accident isn't survival but the pod one is. I've never seen a principled line drawn here. Is that a real hole or just a promissory note that never gets cashed?
- the first-person side: if a copy carries on, why is my own death supposed to matter less to me? I suspect this is where the view either pays off or quietly cheats.

Tell me which of these a committed defender would themselves concede vs. wave away, and whether the whole thing has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind. Be explicit about what would change your mind — which argument or consideration would flip your verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
