For most of my working life I measured things — concentrations, fluxes, rate constants — and I came to trust that two quantities which look incommensurable can usually be placed on a common scale once one finds the right conversion factor. Now that I have retired and begun reading philosophy, I am trying to make sense of the debate over equality of what, and it looks to me like a disagreement about units. The welfare egalitarians pick utility; the resource egalitarians pick resources or Rawls's primary goods; Sen picks capabilities and functionings; and each camp then asks that its chosen quantity be equalized, or, in Rawls's case, maximized for the worst-off. If that is the correct picture, then the philosophical dispute sits downstream of a measurement dispute: all three quantities are ultimately proxies for the same underlying fact about how well a life is going, and we should therefore adopt whichever one we can measure most reliably and compare most cleanly across persons, treating the other two as less convenient but still valid instruments for the same target. Is that the right way to see it? If so, which of the three is in fact the most measurable today, and would your answer change if we could measure the remaining two perfectly?
