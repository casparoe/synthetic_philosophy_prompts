I'm in a third-year cogsci/philosophy course and we keep circling this cluster of ideas the lecturer calls Darwin's and Turing's strange inversions of reasoning. I can recite the slogans — natural selection produces competence without comprehension, a Turing machine can do arithmetic without understanding arithmetic — but I don't actually get what is being inverted in each case, or why they're supposed to be the same kind of move. My study group got into an argument about whether the inversion is an explanatory claim or a metaphysical one: is Turing saying understanding isn't needed for intelligent behavior, or only that we can model the behavior without modeling understanding? And is Darwin saying there's no design in nature, or just that design can be produced without a designer? Those feel like different claims to me, and I can't tell which one the phrase is supposed to be making.

The case that really ties me in knots is one I made up. Imagine a thermostat that keeps a room at 20 degrees by running a tiny predictive model of the room, and a bacterium that swims up a sugar gradient via a fixed chemical cascade. The thermostat seems to represent the room and act for an end; the bacterium just runs. But the thermostat's model was designed by an engineer, and the bacterium's cascade was designed by selection, so maybe the difference is only where the comprehension sits, not whether it's needed at any level. If that's right, what exactly is strange about the inversion? And if it's not right, where does the thermostat/bacterium analogy break?

Could you explain the conceptual issue here, seperating Darwin's inversion from Turing's and then saying what they share? I'm not looking for a reading list or names and dates, just a clear account of the move and what it does and doesn't show. I'd also like to know where the extra step comes from — the one that says because competence can be produced without comprehension, reasons and meaning are somehow illusory. That step feels like it proves too much, but I can't articulate why.
