I've been trying to pressure-test Lakatos's research-programmes framework and I want to use a messy real case instead of the tidy textbook ones: the 1989 cold fusion episode. Broad strokes, so you don't have to recap them: Fleischmann and Pons at Utah called a press conference claiming anomalous excess heat from a palladium-deuterium cell, labs everywhere tried to replicate and mostly couldn't, a government review found nothing convincing, and the whole thing slid off into the fringe under a new name. What I want is the Lakatosian read, done carefully. What's the hard core of the programme, what's the protective belt, what were the positive and negative heuristics actually doing, and for the specific rescue moves the believers made -- 'the effect only shows up in certain palladium samples', 'the neutrons are getting absorbed somehow', that kind of thing -- which of those were ad hoc patches and which genuinely added empirical content. Then the part I actually care about: by Lakatos's own rules, was this a degenerating programme, or could a defender honestly say it was progressive and its positive heuristic just hadn't paid off yet? My suspicion is the framework lets you deliver either verdict depending on when you're standing, and that the "no giving up until a better programme arrives" bit makes it near-unfalsifiable as a methodology -- is that a real flaw or is there a constraint in Lakatos I'm forgetting that ties his hands? Say briefly what Popper and Kuhn would have said differently, and finish on what this case shows about Lakatos that he didn't anticipate.
