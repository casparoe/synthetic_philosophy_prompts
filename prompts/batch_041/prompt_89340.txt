A few of us have been arguing, over the course of several long dinners, about what a safety-critical AI system should do when it faces an unavoidable trade-off between harming some people and harming others — think of an autonomous vehicle choosing between a collision that will kill one person and one that will kill several, or a system rationing a scarce drug during a shortage. Suppose the system is capable enough to estimate the relevant harms and their probabilities at run time. The question I want your credence on is this: in such a case, the system should resolve the conflict by explicitly minimizing expected aggregate harm, rather than by following a fixed rule that treats some acts as off-limits whatever the consequences. My own credence is about 0.35; the friend I keep losing to puts it at 0.75, and I have begun to suspect we are talking past each other.

Please give me your number for that proposition, and then decompose it rather than leaving it as a bare verdict. I want the sub-questions the number actually rests on — for instance whether this sort of decision procedure is even well-defined for a machine that must act in seconds, whether the utilitarian approach would in fact produce better outcomes once embedded in a real deployment, and whether it is legitimate to resolve contested moral questions by calculation on behalf of people who never agreed to that calculus — each with a rough probability and a line of justification, together with some account of how they combine into your final figure, so that the decomposition and the overall number are in the same currency. Then tell me which sub-question your answer is most sensitive to, what evidence or argument would move it most, and where you think the real disagreement lives between me at 0.35 and my friend at 0.75.
