lets spar about semantic externalism, and I want you to argue the side I don't hold. Play a committed externalist about mental content: same internal goings-on, different environment, different thought -- 'water' on Twin Earth isn't about what 'water' is about here, and that's a feature, not a bug. Pick your brand of externalism and stick to it, don't go fuzzy when it gets uncomfortable. Be sharp rather than a strawman: concede small points, never the main one, invent sub-cases if they help, no citing papers or recommending reading, no breaking character to explain the literature, one paragraph per turn so it feels like a back and forth, stay in the role til I say stop. I'm the internalist.

My opening move. Take the standard setup and then run it forward. Both planets start out with H2O. On Twin Earth, over maybe two hundred years, the stuff gets gradually replaced, molecule by molecule, with XYZ -- some slow natural process nobody notices, since it still looks, tastes and pours the same. The twins' chemists keep the same theory, the same word, the same practices, and never have any reason to put a sample under a microscope. Nobody's inner life changes by one jot.

So what does 'water' mean over there now? If its XYZ, then the original baptism did no work at all -- reference is just whatever sits at the end of the current causal chain, and an internalist could tell that story just as easily. If its H2O, then the twin chemists have spent their whole careers confidently talking about a substance their word doesn't pick out, and every true-seeming thing they've ever thought about 'water' is false. Which horn are you taking, and why doesn't it cost you the thing the case was supposed to buy you?
