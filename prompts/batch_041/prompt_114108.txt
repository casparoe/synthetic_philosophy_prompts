I've been running this tiny debate club with two friends — one grew up in Minnesota, the other in a small town in Jutland — and somehow every single month we end up relitigating the same fight about whether it's healthy to celebrate people who succeed. We never actually get anywhere, and I'm tired of it, so I want you to write the document we should have produced months ago. Please take the role of a patient philosopher-slash-social-scientist who's refereed a lot of these stuck arguments, and think carefully before you start writing — maybe begin by brainstorming privately where the two sides might actually be talking past each other, then draft. Here are the two camps as fairly as I can state them. Camp A, the celebrate-the-climb position: visible success should be admired and publicized, aspiration is a public good that lifts everyone, and the reflex to tear down the successful — the tall poppy reflex, Jantelagen, whatever you want to call it — is mostly a vice, rooted in envy, that punishes exactly the people a society should be copying. Camp B, the cut-the-poppy position: levelling norms are a community's immune system against arrogance, free-riding, and zero-sum status competition; they're not envy, they're a cheap and decentralized sanction, and the American-Dream style celebration of winners quietly launders luck into desert and turns everyone else into an audience.

Now write the joint statement these two could both actually sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration — not a dialogue, not a verdict on who's right, a constructive text they'd both put their names to. I want: a numbered section of claims both accept (about human motivation, about status, about what success even tracks, about what's at stake for communities) — and I don't want these to be vacuous, so if a claim is one both sides would nod at only because it says nothing, cut it or make it bite. Then a section stating the precise remaining disagreements, each phrased in wording that its own side would accept as fair to them, followed by a table. Then the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side. I have a hunch two of them are whether poppy-cutting is mostly envy or mostly a legitimate sanction against free-riders and braggarts, and whether watching others succeed is motivating or demoralizing for the people left behind — but don't just take my hunches, add whatever else you find. For each crux, give a concrete case, natural experiment, or argument that both sides would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction about how it comes out, in advance. Finally, if any part of their dispute turns out to be merely verbal — the same word doing two jobs — say which part and why. Keep the whole thing tight and don't pad it, and please don't send me off to read anyone, just reason it out.
