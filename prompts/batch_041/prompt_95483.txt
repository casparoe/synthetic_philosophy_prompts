I am preparing a written statement of my views on rational disagreement for a reading group, and I would like an audit of it before I circulate it. The commitments I hold are as follows.

(1) I am a Bayesian. Doxastic states are credences, rational updating is conditionalization, and nothing deserves the name of belief that is not a probability.

(2) I accept Aumann's agreement theorem and treat it as a genuine constraint rather than a curiosity. Two agents who share a prior and whose posterior credences for some proposition are common knowledge cannot have different posteriors; consequently I do not expect informed, rational agents to persist in disagreement.

(3) I am a subjectivist about priors. Nothing in the concept of rationality selects a prior over any other. Two agents who have received the same evidence may begin from different priors and remain there indefinitely, with neither of them being irrational.

(4) When I learn that an epistemic peer — someone who has the same evidence I do and is comparably competent — disagrees with me, I should conciliate: move my credence substantially toward theirs, not by a token amount.

(5) The fact of a peer's disagreement is evidence about the subject matter, not merely evidence about my peer's psychology.

(6) I add a refinement of my own, which I take to improve on the standard picture: I treat a peer's credence as a noisy measurement of the truth and weight it by how much evidence they hold that I do not. A peer who has done independent work should move me more than one who is merely confident.

Please tell me which of these are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to conflict, and which single revision would resolve the most while costing the least. Order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say precisely what has to give. I would rather have a short list of real problems than an exhaustive list of possible ones. Answer in no more than 450 words.
