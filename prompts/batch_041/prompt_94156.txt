You are a philosopher of physics with expertise in the metaphysics of time and the foundations of special relativity. I would like you to think carefully and systematically about a position I have come to hold, and then tell me what would count against it. Before you answer, brainstorm freely: list every potential defeater you can think of, including heterodox ones, and only then organize and assess them.

The position is a package with three claims. First, special relativity, together with a modest premise about the reality of events that lie outside my past and future light cones, supports eternalism: all events in spacetime are equally real, and there is no objective, global present. Second, the block is not merely a useful representation but the mind-independent structure of reality. Third, the passage of time and the sense of a moving “now” are features of our perspective as embedded observers, not fundamental features of the world. Call this package “indexical eternalism” if a label helps; I am using the term stipulatively, not as established terminology.

I do not want a literature survey or citations. I want a map of the position’s exposure. Organize the defeaters by what exactly they target: the inference from relativity to eternalism, the metaphysical core of eternalism, or the claim that temporal passage is illusory. For each, say whether it would be fatal, seriously damaging, or merely awkward; whether a committed defender of the package would concede it as a defeater or dismiss it as question-begging; and what further argument or result would be needed to make it stick. Rank them from most to least decisive.

In particular, address the following. Does the inference trade on a controversial convention for defining simultaneity, or on a premise about the transitivity of existence that a rival could reject? Is “existence” being used univocally across frames, and would a relativized or indexical reading of existence save the argument or trivialize it? If “now” is treated as a token-reflexive indexical like “here,” does that genuinely answer the objection or merely relocate it? Can the block accommodate genuine openness, becoming, or causal asymmetry without covertly reintroducing a privileged present? Do quantum nonlocality, cosmological time, or the thermodynamics of time’s arrow give any independent reason to reject the package? And has the package been qualified into unfalsifiability — is there anything a sufficiently clever defender could not absorb?

Finish by telling me honestly whether the position is well insulated. If it is, say whether that is a virtue, because it is a metaphysical framework, or a problem, because it risks saying nothing.
