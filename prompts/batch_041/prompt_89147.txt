I run a small food co-op board with six other people and we keep getting tangled up in a weird kind of vote, and I'm hoping you can help me see it straight. I don't have any philosophy background, but I've been thinking about this on my own for a while, and I'm a backend engineer by trade, so the only language I really have for this stuff is distributed databases. Here's what happens: we vote on three linked questions -- is the roof leaking (call it A), is the leak our responsibility under the lease (B), and should we pay for the fix out of the reserve (C) -- and we all agree the rule is if A and B then C. Individually everyone's answers are consistent. But when we tally each question separately we get majority yes on A, majority yes on B, and majority no on C, so the board's position is logically impossible. If we instead only vote on C, we get a clean answer but no record of why, and half the board feels the vote didn't capture what they actually thought. I read a little about the discursive dilemma and judgment aggregation years ago and it felt like exactly this, but I can't hold the academic version in my head, and I've read alot of summaries that just say the same three sentences over and over.

Not a loose analogy; an actual translation, proposition by proposition. Then give me the residue report: what maps cleanly onto database talk, what I have to fudge or distort, and what has no counterpart at all.
