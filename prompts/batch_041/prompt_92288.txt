Write a poem in terza rima, roughly eighteen to twenty-one lines, in the voice of a bookie who is constructing a diachronic Dutch book against an agent who has failed to update by conditionalization. Let the interlocking rhyme scheme carry the argument: each tercet should present one bet that the agent accepts as fair by her own lights, and the middle line that rhymes forward into the next tercet should be the price or conditional credence that makes the next bet acceptable to her. The poem's turn should come when the evidence arrives and her post-evidence price does not move as conditionalization would require; the closing line should close the chain with the sure loss. Use a concrete proposition—a coin, a medical test, a horse, a weather forecast—rather than notation, and do not let any single bet look like a cheat: the point is that the loss is assembled from prices she already endorses. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows the diachronic Dutch book theorem recognizes the construction, and clear enough for a reader who does not.
