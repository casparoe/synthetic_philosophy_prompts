Ok, quick context: I spent about 35 years as a fisheries biologist, mostly stock assessment, and since I retired Ive been going to a philosophy of science reading group with a bunch of grad students and one very patient professor. I keep getting dragged into the same fight and I want to build a proper instrument for it instead of arguing in circles.

Heres the thing that bugs me. Malthus. The old population-vs-food story. When I was a student we were told he was simply wrong, full stop, the man didn't foresee the agricultural and industrial revolutions. Then later everyone decided he was basically right and we were just living on borrowed time, and now half the people I read treat the whole business as the founding text of ecology and the other half treat it as a cautionary tale about unfalsifiable doomsaying. And it isn't just Malthus, its the same shape every time: a long-run prediction about a trend, a failure to show up on schedule, and then a fight about whether the theory was refuted, rescued, or just early.

What I want from you is a decision tree (or a taxonomy, whichever you think fits better) that sorts the positions people actually hold on the question: when a long-run prediction seems to fail, what licenses the claim that the theory was right but early? I dont want a list of famous names with one-line summaries, I want the thing built from the questions first, so that someone can walk down it and land on a position without already knowing what its called. Each branch should be a question about a concrete commitment, not "are you a Popperian" or "do you like Lakatos." Phrase every node so a smart person could answer it on its own terms.

Some things Id like the tree to be able to seperate, because these are the fights we keep having:

- whether a prediction whose timing is off but whose direction is right counts as confirmed at all, or whether a missed deadline is a miss;
- whether a theory can be saved by tacking on an auxiliary assumption, and if so whether the auxiliary has to be independently testable, or merely plausible, or can be pure patchwork as long as the core is kept;
- whether the rescue has to explain why the original prediction failed, or only has to be consistent with the failure;
- whether the theorys subject matter is a mechanism that is still running (so the trend hasnt ended, its just slow) or a conditional that never claimed to predict the actual future;
- whether the time horizon is part of the theory or a separate revisable estimate, because I notice people move that goalpost without admitting it;
- and at what point you say fine, this is unfalsifiable now, and whether that is supposed to be a vice in a theory whose timescale is longer than a human career. (This one matters to me, I did 30-year stock projections for a living and I know exactly how slippery it gets.)

Concrete cases Id like it to run on, so it doesnt turn into abstraction: the population-vs-food prediction; the 60s and 70s resource-scarcity and famine predictions that mostly didnt arrive on schedule; the bet between the biologist and the economist over whether resource prices would rise (you know the one, dont need me to look it up); and a carrying-capacity prediction from my own field, where the fish stock crashes later than the model said but it does crash. Also throw in one case where the prediction never came true and everybody quietly stopped mentioning it, so the tree has to handle the theory that just faded instead of being refuted.

Practical constraints: aim for something like 6 to 9 branch questions and 8 to 12 leaves. Leaves can be named positions, but if a leaf is a combination nobody actually defends, say so and give me one sentence on why its empty (incoherent, or just unattractive, or nobody bothered). And if two leaves end up being the same position wearing different hats, flag that honestly instead of pretending the tree seperates them. Audience is our reading group, so assume they can handle jargon but not that theyve memorized anyones system.

One more thing: no reading lists, no citations, no "see X 1970." I cant make you fetch anything and frankly the point is the logical space, not the bibliography.
