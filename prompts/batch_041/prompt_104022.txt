At our philosophy club meeting last week we discussed whether "better than" has to be transitive, and two of the more argumentative members, Iris and Theo, spent most of the hour going back and forth while the rest of us listened. The case we had been handed was this. Take a world containing a single person whose life is of the highest quality we can imagine. Compare it with a world containing two people, each of whose lives is a hair less good than that one. Compare that with a world of three people, each a hair less good than the two before, and continue down a long series of steps until the final world contains a very large number of people whose lives are only just worth living. At every step the later world appears better, since each person already present loses only a tiny amount while a whole additional worthwhile life is added. Yet the final world seems plainly worse than the first.

Iris argued that this shows transitivity is not negotiable and that one of the pairwise judgments must simply be false; a comparative that can cycle, she said, is not a ranking at all, and without transitivity we could not even say which of two options we have most reason to choose. Theo replied that this begs the question: the only reason to insist on transitivity is that we have already assumed value comes in a single ordered line, which is precisely what the spectrum case calls into doubt. He concluded that "better than" can be intransitive here, and that the last world really is better than the one before it while the first really is better than the last. Iris then offered a diagnosis: the failure must lie at the very top of the scale, where the small loss of quality to the first person cannot be compensated by any number of extra lives, however great. Theo answered that this is ad hoc — the same structure reappears at every rung of the ladder, so Iris must either claim that quality is lexically dominant at every level, which makes the scale bizarrely steep, or concede that each individual step does go through.

Neither would give an inch and I honestly could not referee them. Please adjudicate move by move: who is right about which point, which claims are unsupported, and what both of them are missing.
