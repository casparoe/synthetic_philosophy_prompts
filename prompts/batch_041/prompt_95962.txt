I am the analyst preparing advice for the mayor of a mid-sized city that is about to adopt a risk score to help judges decide whom to hold in jail before trial. We audited the tool on our last two years of cases, using rearrest before trial as the outcome, and the results point in different directions. The score means the same thing in the two groups we are required to report on: about 45% of the defendants flagged high risk in each group are in fact rearrested before trial. But at the cutoff we plan to use, 38% of the first group is flagged and only 21% of the second, so our flagged pool is about 64% first group even though the two groups are roughly equal in size; and rearrest before trial is about twice as common in the first group to begin with, roughly 22% against 12%. The vendor says the score is fair because it is equally accurate for everyone; a councilmember says it is unfair because it sends so many more people from one group to jail; our city attorney wants a rule that never mentions group membership. Two further wrinkles: rearrest is measured by arrests, which depend on where police patrol, and judges who see the score can override it. The mayor wants a recommendation on Monday and has asked me to say plainly which of these complaints can be satisfied at the same time and which cannot, and what we should do. Please answer her directly: I need to know what to give up, and why, not merely that these goals trade off against one another.
