I've been reading David Hume's essay "Of Money" on my own — no economics or philosophy background, I just got into an argument with a friend about whether a country is better off with more money sloshing around and wanted to see what an old dead guy thought — and there's a passage right at the start that I cannot get straight in my head. Here it is:

"MONEY is not, properly speaking, one of the subjects of commerce; but only the instrument which men have agreed upon to facilitate the exchange of one commodity for another. It is none of the wheels of trade: It is the oil which renders the motion of the wheels more smooth and easy. If we consider any one kingdom by itself, it is evident, that the greater or less plenty of money is of no consequence; since the prices of commodities are always proportioned to the plenty of money, and a crown in HARRY VII.'s time served the same purpose as a pound does at present."

What trips me up is that a few paragraphs later he says "It is only the public which draws any advantage from the greater plenty of money; and that only in its wars and negociations with foreign states," and then toward the end he seems to argue that a country actually benefits from money flowing in during the stretch before prices adjust — the interval thing, where more money in the meantime gets industry going. So on one page money is "of no consequence," on another it plainly has consequences, and I can't tell if he's contradicting himself or making two different claims about two different time-scales. The oil/wheels image confuses me too: oil isn't a wheel, it's not part of the machine at all, yet the machine won't run right without it, so is he saying money is just a lubricant that changes nothing real, or is he saying something sneakier — that the amount of oil doesn't change how much work the wheels do, only how smoothly they turn? And if that's the point, then what work is the "of no consequence" line actually doing?

Can you help me read this passage properly? I don't want a summary of the whole essay, I want to actually understand what he's claiming in these sentences and why it isn't (or is) a contradiction.
