I've been sitting in on a reading group that's working through Gödel, Escher, Bach, and we've just hit the stretch where Hofstadter leans hard on the analogy between Gödel's self-referential sentences and self-awareness — which is also the point where I stop being able to follow what anyone is actually claiming. Last week an argument got cut off when someone said, look, Gödel proved that no system can fully understand itself; that's why consciousness has to be a strange loop, and it's why you can't just build a mind out of formal rules. I'm fairly sure that one sentence is doing like four jobs at once and I can't tell which one is carrying the weight.

Can you separate out the precise claims it could be making — the ones that are true almost by construction, the ones that hold for formal systems but don't obviously touch minds at all, the ones that need some substantive premise about self-representation, and the ones that would actually get you the conclusion about machines — and for each say what it would take to be true, whether it's a claim about proof, about representation, or about knowledge, and whether it survives? Two things I'd especially like untangled, since I suspect they're where the sentence gets its mileage: (i) the difference between a system failing to *prove* something about itself and a system failing to *represent* or *know* something about itself; and (ii) whether self-reference in the Gödelian sense — a formula that encodes a fact about its own provability — has anything much to do with the kind of self-model a mind is supposed to have. Someone else in the group keeps saying that any self-model must be a simplification and therefore incomplete, which sounds like a totally different claim wearing the same costume. I've been going back and forth on wether these are the same point restated or three unrelated points, and I keep losing the thread.

Lay it out as a menu with verdicts, from platitude up to load-bearing, so I can bring it next week and go down the list one by one. And tell me which reading people are usually reaching for when they use the line to rule out machine minds, and whether that reading is any good — or whether the word 'understand' is just doing vibes the whole time.
