Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers who disagree about what makes consent valid. To keep the exchange concrete, build it around this case.

The Standing Grant: at twenty, a person joins an institution — a consent bank — that will act on her behalf. The grant she signs authorizes the bank to decide how to invest part of her income, which studies may use her medical data, and what mutual obligations she takes on toward other members. What she signs describes broad categories, not particular acts. Over the next forty years the bank's procedures drift, each step staying inside the letter of those categories but reaching well beyond anything she pictured. She may withdraw at any time; withdrawal, however, forfeits the accumulated benefits and triggers a buy-out penalty that grows the longer she stays. Call the demand that an arrangement remain within what the consenter could actually have authorized the Scope Condition.

The first philosopher holds that her continued participation is valid consent to the bank's current terms: she is competent, undeceived, and free to leave, and nothing further is required. The second holds that a standing grant to unforeseen acts is not consent but a fiction, and that the Scope Condition is a genuine further requirement — so the bank's later acts are unauthorized however cheap or dear exit happens to be.

Have them argue it out, and give each a name. Neither should be a strawman: the first should press the objection that the Scope Condition would invalidate every long-term commitment — marriages, careers, constitutions — and the second should explain why those cases are or are not different. The exchange should be real back-and-forth, each answering the other's strongest point rather than restating an opening.

After the dialogue, add a JSON object summarizing it, with these fields: agreements (a list of propositions both interlocutors accept by the end); cruxes (a list of objects, each with issue, exit_is_enough, scope_condition, and what_turns_on_it); and unresolved (one or two sentences on what the dialogue leaves open).
