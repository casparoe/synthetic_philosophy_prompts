My opening. You wrote that despotism is a legitimate way of governing barbarians provided the end is their improvement and the means actually bring that end about, and that liberty as a principle has no application to any people that can't yet be improved by free and equal discussion. Fine. But that isn't a condition, it's a promise, and every despot since the beginning of the world has made it — the whole point of a condition is that it can fail, and be seen to fail. So who is the judge of whether we're being improved, and what am I supposed to do when I disagree with the judge? I keep thinking of the whole arrangement as a trust, and a trust where the beneficiary can't sue and can't even see the accounts isn't a trust, it's just a gift to the trustee. So give me a test that would actually tell you when the despotism has failed, and then name one thing the Company did that your test condemns — take the annexations, where a ruler dies without a natural heir and the state is simply taken over, and tell me what your proviso says about that one. If nothing the Company ever did would count against it, then the proviso isn't doing any work at all, and the liberty book has a hole in it the size of a subcontinent. I've definately got to be the one arguing the other side here, so go on, defend yourself.
