My reading group has been stuck on moral responsibility for about a year now, and two of the members have basically stopped listening to each other. R is the Strawson person: she thinks the question "but is he *really* responsible, given determinism, or given how he was raised?" is confused, because resentment, indignation, guilt and forgiveness aren't hypotheses that some metaphysics could confirm or refute -- they're the attitudes that constitute being involved with another person as a person, and the only sensible question is whether *this* individual is a fit candidate for them. She keeps insisting that excuses and exemptions are different animals: an excuse says it wasn't really him doing it, an exemption says he isn't someone you can be in that kind of relation with at all, like a small kid or someone with severe dementia. S is the skeptic: she thinks that if you actually trace the causal history of any wrongdoer -- first the staged cases where a neuroscientist tinkers with someone's values from the crib onward, then the cases where nothing is tinkered with but the upbringing is just as deterministic and just as luck-soaked -- you never get the kind of basic-desert blame our practices assume. Blame, on her view, is at best a tool and at worst a cruel fiction, and she wants to know why we'd keep doing it once we saw it clearly. R says S is demanding something the practice never promised; S says R has redefined "responsible" until nothing could count as evidence against it.

What I actually want is the document those two should have written instead of argueing. So: draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a Strawsonian and a responsibility skeptic. I want (1) a numbered list of claims both of them can genuinely sign -- about what the reactive attitudes are for, about the ordinary cases they both agree are exemptions, about what the truth or falsity of determinism would and wouldn't show, and about what each of them means by "justify" (my suspicion is that one is asking for a justification of the practice from inside it and the other from outside it, and I want that made explicit without either of them being made to look like an idiot); (2) the points where they still differ, each one worded so that whoever holds it would accept the wording as fair, and each marked as either a real disagreement or a merely verbal one; (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them; and (4) for each crux, the case or argument both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction about how it comes out written down in advance.

Don't cite anybody and don't give me a reading list, that's not what's missing here. A few things I want it to actually engage with rather than skate over: the staged manipulation case, going in small steps from direct neurological tampering to an entirely ordinary-looking childhood, and the question of which step is supposed to make the difference and why; the psychopath, who can reason about what morality demands and simply doesn't care, and whether he counts as exempt or just bad; whether S's position also abolishes gratitude, pride and self-blame or only the negative stuff, which seems to me to be a crux and not a side issue; whether the thing under dispute is the justification of blaming or of punishing; the difference between an action being attributable to someone's values and their being answerable to us for it, which R keeps reaching for and S keeps treating as a dodge; the difference between expecting something of someone in the sense of holding them to it and merely predicting they'll do it; and whether S wants us to stop blaming or just to keep blaming while telling ourselves a different story about what we're doing. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table with columns for the point, each side's own wording, the crux it hangs on, and what each side predicts. Both of them should be able to put their names on it -- if a sentence is slanted toward one side, it doesn't go in.
