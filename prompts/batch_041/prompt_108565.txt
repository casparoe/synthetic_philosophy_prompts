I'm a second-year PhD student rotating in a consciousness lab, and I've got a new result I need to write up — but how I frame it depends entirely on whether my PI thinks the thing counts as a conscious experience, and she's the one who signs off on the wording. The problem is I genuinely can't predict her.

Her track record, as best I can reconstruct it from lab meetings:

- Blindsight (V1 gone, guesses orientation and even fear-vs-happy faces way above chance, insists she sees nothing): not conscious. No argument there.
- Subliminal masked priming, subjects at chance on the visibility test but the prime still shifts their responses: not conscious.
- The gorilla video: not conscious, and she got genuinely irritated at the person who said "well she could've seen it if she'd been attending."
- The covert command-following patient, the one doing mental imagery in the scanner: conscious. Her line was that you don't need a report to be conscious, which is the whole point of that literature.
- Dreams: conscious, obviously.
- Type-2 blindsight, where the patient says "I don't see anything, I just have a feeling it's a face": she says the feeling is conscious and the face isn't. This one started an actual fight in lab meeting.
- Ordinary attended perception: conscious, but I don't think that one tells us anything.

She trained with the global workspace crowd, but she keeps citing the recurrence/feedback people, so I can't tell which one she actually beleives. She's also dodged the Sperling overflow question every single time it's come up, which I think is telling.

The new case: we ran faces under continuous flash suppression. Subjects report nothing at all, visibility test is at chance. But the suppressed faces still drive strong, category-specific activity in FFA, and the timing looks like feedback rather than a feedforward sweep — it comes in late, after the first pass through cortex, and it disappears if we disrupt the loop. What we do not see is anything like global ignition: no P3, no long-range frontoparietal synchrony. (We also have a second condition where subjects report a vague "something's there" feeling, in case that matters given the type-2 blindsight thing.)

So — does she call it conscious or not? I want the prediction, but what I really want is the rule she's implicitly running, the other rules that fit every one of her old verdicts just as well, and which further case — ideally one we could actually run — would best separate the survivors. And if the answer is "she'll say no," tell me what she'd have to say about something else for you to flip.
