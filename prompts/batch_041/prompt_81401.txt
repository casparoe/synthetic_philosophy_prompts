I'm teaching a seminar on aesthetics next term and I've inherited a collegue's lecture handout on Schopenhauer and music. I know Schopenhauer's general line well enough—will, representation, Ideas, aesthetic contemplation—but I've never taught his music bit in detail, and I'm worried the handout is a bit too smooth. Here's the gist of what it says:

Schopenhauer agrees with the Romantics that music is the highest of the arts. Unlike painting or poetry, which copy the Platonic Ideas, music copies the will itself. So when we listen to music we are not contemplating an Idea; we are directly in touch with the thing-in-itself. This makes music the purest case of the will-less aesthetic experience that all art aims at: the will is temporarily silenced by being given its own image. It also means music can give us philosophical knowledge of the will, since it puts us in touch with the metaphysical ground of reality. That's why Schopenhauer calls music an unconscious exercise in metaphysics, and why he thinks it is more philosophical than philosophy itself.

I suspect some of this is right and some is subtly off, but I can't put my finger on it. Could you go through it sentence by sentence and tell me what Schopenhauer would accept, what he'd wince at, and what is just wrong? And at the end, does the claim that music gives us philosophical knowledge of the will follow from anything true in the summary, or is it an overreach? I don't need citations, just a clear audit. I'd rather not embarass myself in front of the students.
