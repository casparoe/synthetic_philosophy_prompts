I have been trying to work out, on my own and without any background in philosophy, whether a pattern I keep running into has an established name. The pattern is this: there appear to be states of mind that a person cannot both be in and correctly report being in, and the failure takes more than one shape. Sometimes the asking destroys the state. If I am drifting and you ask whether I am paying attention, I have to attend to your question in order to answer it, and in that instant the drifting is gone; the answer I am inclined to give — that I am paying attention — is produced by the very act of being asked, and I cannot tell what would have been true a moment earlier. Sometimes the state survives but is invisible from the inside: I can be irritable for an afternoon without noticing, while everyone around me sees it plainly, so being in the state and knowing I am in it come apart, and it is not that I lack some rare sense, since the information seems available to others and not to me. And sometimes the two are incompatible by their nature: I cannot be absorbed in something and at the same time be monitoring whether I am absorbed, because the monitoring is itself a step back out of the absorption, so this is a state that seems to exist only while unobserved by its own owner.

What I want to know is whether this has a name, and — more than the name — where its edges are. I have heard it said as a piece of folk wisdom that if you are in some condition you must at least be able to tell that you are, that there is something incoherent in sincerely claiming to feel a certain way while being unable to say whether you do. I have also heard, from the other side, the saying that chasing a thing like happiness is the surest way not to get it, which feels like a cousin of what I am describing but not the same thing, since that is about pursuing a state and mine is about checking on one. So I suspect there may be a general thesis of the first kind — that a person can always tell what state of mind they are in — and I think my cases tell against it, but I cannot tell whether I have found one phenomenon or three, or whether the recognized vocabulary carves them the way I have.

Please tell me what the nearest recognized ideas are, how each differs from what I have described, and which, if any, fits best. I would especially like the three strands kept apart: the case where the state is destroyed by the check, the case where it is merely hidden from the one who has it, and the case where what is at stake is not whether I know but whether I know that I know. If nothing established fits, name and define the thing yourself and tell me what falls outside the definition. I am not after a label; I want to know what to count as an instance and what not to.
