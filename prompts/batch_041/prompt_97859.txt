I'm finishing a term paper for a graduate seminar on idealization in practical reason, and before I commit to a position I want a consistency audit of the package I've assembled, since I'd rather find the cracks now than in the viva. I hold the following. (1) A person's good is given by what she would desire for herself under sufficiently idealized conditions — full factual information, correct reasoning, and coherently ordered preferences; her actual desires are evidence about this good but can be overridden by it. (2) To respect a person's autonomy is to give weight to her actual, present preferences about her own life, and it is generally wrong to override those preferences on the grounds that she would want otherwise were she better informed — that is just paternalism with extra steps. (3) Where a collective decision is at stake, the correct procedure is to aggregate what people would want under idealization rather than what they actually want, because actual preferences are contaminated by ignorance, bias, and adaptive response, so the corrected set is the better guide to what is genuinely good for them. (4) The idealized preference-holder is not numerically identical to the actual person whose preferences are being idealized; she may want things the actual person would repudiate, and I take her preferences to be authoritative over the actual person's anyway. (5) Idealization is convergent: as you turn up information and coherence, preferences tend toward a single corrected set rather than fanning out into many.

What I want from you is an audit, not a lecture. I'm not asking you to decide which commitment I should abandon — I want to know what my options are and what each one gives up, with the reasoning shown, so I can make that call myself.
