So a guy in my reading group has this line he wont drop: heuristics are tools, and the mind is basically a toolbox. A hammer isnt "irrational" because it cant saw a board — it's just built for a job — and likewise a heuristic isn't a "bias" just because it flops on some task in a lab; you've got to ask what job it was made for. Then he goes further: the toolbox was assembled by evolution, so every tool in it is rational for the environment that shaped it, which makes the whole heuristics-and-biases thing a category error — people are grading tools against the wrong job. I want an audit of how far the toolbox picture actually goes. Start with the mapping — what corresponds to the tool, the material being worked, the craftsperson, the workshop, the toolbox itself, the job — then tell me where it breaks, and separate the breaks that matter for his conclusion from the ones that dont. My real question is whether the "tools cant be irrational" move survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was quietly carrying the whole conclusion. And if the toolbox is the wrong picture, whats a better one for what satisficing is actually up to?
