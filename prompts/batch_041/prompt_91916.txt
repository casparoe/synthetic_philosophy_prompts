I'm a PhD student in the philosophy of cognitive science, and before I commit to a position on the relation between competence and comprehension I want the specification first. The target is a theory of how a system can reliably and aptly do something that, in a person, would count as the exercise of understanding, while nothing in the system understands anything. I want it to cover both of the canonical inversions: the Darwinian one, in which a process with no insight into what it is doing nonetheless yields structures and behaviors that look designed; and the Turing one, in which a device that manipulates symbols with no grasp of their meaning nonetheless yields results we would call correct, and sometimes intelligent, if a person had produced them. Write me the desiderata any such theory has to meet.

For the phenomena, include at least these: an automated theorem prover that returns a proof no human had found, whose correctness, and even elegance, a human later appreciates; a creature that intercepts a thrown object by processes we can describe as ballistics it has no grasp of; and a case I keep meeting in my own work, a system fluent at explaining its own behavior where the explanations are generated by a process that does not track the causes of that behavior. I care as much about the distinctions the theory must preserve as about the cases: between the producer's lack of comprehension and the product's comprehensibility; between lacking comprehension altogether and borrowing it from elsewhere in the causal history; between comprehension as a personal-level achievement and mechanism at the subpersonal level; and between degrees of comprehension and its presence or absence. Add whatever distinction I have missed.

For each requirement give the consideration that motivates it, and a verdict on which are genuine constraints and which are the prejudices of one camp — I suspect that at least one familiar demand, that comprehension be phenomenal or conscious, is a prejudice, but I want to be told. Flag any pair of requirements you think cannot be jointly satisfied, say which single requirement excludes the most candidates, and close by saying whether the theory's honest upshot is that comprehension does any explanatory work at all. I need the list to be serviceable for one job in particular: telling, for a given system, whether attributing comprehension to it earns its keep or whether its competence is fully accounted for one level down. Skip the scorecard of named views; the list is the deliverable.
