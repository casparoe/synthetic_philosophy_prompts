Write a mock FAQ for an invented philanthropic funder — the Meridian Trust for the Abatement of Suffering — in the deadpan institutional voice of a real foundation's website: numbered questions, confident prose, no winking at the reader, around 700 words. The target is not the sincerity of suffering-focused ethics but the way its own logic, followed honestly, generates conclusions its own practitioners find hard to say out loud; the reductio should be one a committed negative utilitarian would concede is a genuine implication of the view rather than a caricature, and it should be legible to a reader who has never encountered the position before. So let the comedy come from the axiology being applied without flinching: that suffering is bad and pleasure does not symmetrically offset it; that grants are ranked by suffering averted rather than lives improved or lives created; that nothing will be funded which adds sentient beings, since each new being is a new locus of possible suffering; that a project preventing a painful death while extending a net-happy life must be scored as irrelevant, or as a cost; that contraception for the cheerful and palliative care for a single animal are funded with equal seriousness; and that at the limit the funder must take seriously proposals to abolish suffering by abolishing sufferers. Include the clause answering the charge of being anti-life, and one covering a donation whose acceptance would itself create a small amount of pleasure. Invent the organization, name no real ones or people, and don't resolve the tension — a reader should close the page unsure whether the position is monstrous, correct, or both.
