I got into pottery this year and it's turned into an unasked-for metaphysics crisis, which is definately not what I signed up for.

Here's the situation. I threw a vase on Tuesday, hated it, wedged the clay back into a ball, and threw a bowl out of the same lump. My studio mate says nothing happened — same clay, so same thing, just a different shape now. But the vase is gone, right? Un-wedging the bowl doesn't bring it back. And if the vase just is the clay, and the bowl just is the clay, then the vase is the bowl, which can't be right, since I destroyed one and made the other a day later.

It gets worse. The museum near me has a sculpture they've restored so many times that basically none of the original material is left, and they still call it the same sculpture. And there's a lump of clay on my shelf that existed before I made anything with it and will exist after I smash whatever I make — so the lump seems to outlive every piece I throw from it. I rework alot of pieces, so this comes up constantly.

Write me an essay on this. What are the serious options — one object, two objects sharing the same stuff, no such thing as the pot at all and only clay arranged pot-wise, the pot as a phase of the clay's life, something else? Then actually pick one and defend it. I want a verdict, not a menu. Three things I especially want you to deal with: (a) how many things are on my shelf when a finished pot is sitting there; (b) if the pot and the clay are different objects, what makes them different when they're the same atoms in the same arrangement — and if the only difference is historical (I made the pot at time t), does that count as a difference in the thing itself, or is it cheating?; and (c) if the pot is one object, is there also a pot-minus-one-molecule on the shelf, and a pot that stops existing the moment I chip the rim? Because when I squint there seem to be a zillion overlapping objects there.

Also, tell me what goes on the gallery label: 'reworked from a vase' or 'new piece, same clay'?

I assume this is a known problem and I'm not the first person to notice, but don't send me a reading list — I want the argument, not citations. And be upfront about where your reasoning is most likely to fail and what would change your mind.
