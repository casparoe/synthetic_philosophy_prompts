I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy, and I've been asked to run a session of our department's informal work-in-progress series next month on the topic of hard choices. I want to open with a scripted dialogue that I can hand out, because the last time I tried to present this material in prose the audience kept collapsing the two positions onto a single for/against-incommensurability axis, and the discussion never got past the labels. So I'd like you to write the dialogue for me.

The two characters are, on one side, a proponent of what I'll call the parity view and, on the other, a proponent of what I'll call the trichotomy view. The parity view holds that the familiar comparative relations — better than, worse than, and equal in value — do not exhaust the ways two options can be compared: there is a further, sui generis relation (call it parity) that can hold between two items which are comparable, so that deliberating between them is not mere taste, and yet neither is better than the other and they are not equal. The trichotomy view denies this: it says there are exactly three comparative relations, so any pair of options is either rankable — in which case a "hard" choice is just an unresolved comparison, awaiting better information or clearer thinking — or genuinely incomparable, in which case reason is silent and any appeal to "parity" is a euphemism for arbitrary will dressed up as a value relation. Neither character is a strawman: each must grant the strongest point the other makes before turning to their own, and neither may simply recite a stock position. I'd also like the exchange to turn at least twice on one concrete decision, so that the disagreement is tested rather than merely asserted. I've been using the case of someone choosing between a position that would let her finish a long-deferred research project, at the cost of moving away from a place where she is the primary support for an ailing relative, and staying where she is; use that case or substitute a sharper one if you prefer, but it should be a single decision that both characters have to address, not a fresh example per move.

What I actually need from the dialogue is genuine back-and-forth, in which each speaker takes up the specific point just made rather than talking past the other. The parity theorist has to answer the charge that "parity" does no work beyond "I can't decide"; the trichotomist has to answer the charge that three relations cannot capture the texture of deliberation without collapsing into the claim that every hard choice is either ignorance or whim. Let them press each other on whether the parity relation, if it exists, could ever ground a reason to choose one way rather than the other, and on what would even count as evidence for or against its existence. Aim for roughly 1,500 to 2,000 words, include no citations or reading list, and please do not resolve the disagreement at the end — I want the audience to have to take a side.
