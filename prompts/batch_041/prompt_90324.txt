In the flat I share, the phone charger lives in the kitchen and the snacks live on the top shelf where I need a chair to reach them. I built that apartment to trap myself, and it works better than any resolution I've ever made. Fine, commitment devices, I know the drill. But a while back I noticed the exact same move running in the other direction, and that's the bit I dont have a name for. I'll book the 6am call in a week that's already brutal, because today-me wants the money and doesn't have to get up; the 5:30 me is a stranger I'm borrowing from. Or I'll leave an essay to the night before, not out of procrastination but because I've learned that the night-before me writes well when he's scared, so I'm spending a future self's adrenaline to buy a present self's calm. Both cases are arranging my future. In the first it's like parenting a kid; in the second it's like running a tab I have no intention of paying.

I've been reading rationalist blogs long enough to know the usual furniture here — akrasia, hyperbolic discounting, commitment devices, Ulysses contracts, the whole thing about your future self being basically a different person — and none of them quite do it. Hyperbolic discounting tells me why the trade looks good from here, but not why the two directions feel different, and that difference seems like the whole thing. The akrasia stories I know have one agent whose will loses; here the earlier me isn't weak, he's clever, he's the one doing the exploiting, and the exploited party never gets a vote. So: is there an established term for the exploiting direction specifically, or for the pair as a pair, and if there isn't, can you coin one and define it tightly enough that I can actually sort cases with it? The edge I care about most is what separates arranging your future for your later self from arranging it at its expense — is it just whether the later you would sign off on it in advance and in a calm moment, and if so, is there a case where the later you would sign off and it's still clearly exploitation? Also tell me what happens if the later me wises up and starts laying traps back, because that's the part that makes me suspect this is a game-theory thing wearing a psychology costume, and I'd like to know what its called over there too. I don't need sources — I want the distinction and where its edges are.
