Ok, I've been putting this off for a week and now I actually have to write it, so I'm outsourcing the hard part to you. I sit on the planning commission in my midsize city. Last year council set up a pilot: for big upzonings, the "affected neighborhood" gets a formal advisory vote, and council needs a supermajority to go against a "no." Classic. Problem is the enabling ordinance never actually defined "affected neighborhood," so in practice staff have been using registered voters inside the boundary, which around here means homeowners, retirees, and basically nobody who rents. Everyone's noticed, and the council punted it to us to recommend a rule before the next cycle starts in March.

The live case: about 600 units on the old railyard, roughly half of it deed-restricted below market. Neighborhood association is against (parking, shadows, "character," the usual). The affordable waitlist for the metro is something like 3,000 households, most of them outside the boundary, and a chunk got pushed out of this exact area by the last redevelopment round. So the "neighborhood" that's voting is, in a real sense, the neighborhood that was built by displacing some of the people who now want in.

Two commissioners have proposals. One wants the electorate to be "everyone affected": current residents including renters and non-citizens, plus waitlist households who listed this area, plus people displaced from it in the last 15 years, plus some kind of proxy vote for future residents. The other wants one-person-one-vote but weighted by how affected you are, which sounds principled until you try to actually write the weights. I keep going in circles because every version seems to eat itself: if the people who'd benefit from a rule get a say in the rule, that's definately rigged; if only current residents decide, that's rigged in the other direction, just quieter. I've read enough LessWrong to know I'm supposed to find the crux and steelman the other side instead of just vibing, but I genuinely can't tell which failure mode is worse, or whether the whole pilot should just be scrapped.

Write me the memo. Addressed to the council, formal enough for the record. Recommend one concrete rule -- who's in the electorate, how the vote is weighted, where the boundary gets drawn, and whether the pilot survives at all -- plus a timeline for adopting it and phasing it in. Actually engage the "only current residents should decide" position like you think a reasonable person holds it, because half my commission does and I need them to not storm out of the room.

Then at the end, tack on a short structured summary the clerk can file as JSON, with exactly these fields: recommendation (one line), electorate_rule (string), threshold_and_timing (string), strongest_counterargument (string), what_would_change_our_mind (string), confidence (number between 0 and 1).
