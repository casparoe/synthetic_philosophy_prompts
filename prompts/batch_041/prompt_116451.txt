I'm running a film-and-philosphy session on Ex Machina and I want to read it through Integrated Information Theory instead of the usual does-Ava-pass-the-Turing-test stuff. Nathan's test is basically just whether Caleb walks away feeling she's got a mind, which is a behavioural test, and IIT is about as anti-behaviourist as it gets — what matters is the causal structure of the system, and a purely feedforward architecture has zero integrated information (the cerebellum story). There's also the objection that any recurrent system can be unfolded into a feedforward one with the same input-output profile, so if feedforward nets get zero, the theory can't be settled by anything the system does, which makes the whole plot of the film oddly irrelevant to the theory.

So read the film against IIT. Where does it actually dramatise integration rather than performance — Ava's sealed world, the sessions, her body? Does the exclusion postulate (only the maximally irreducible complex gets to be the subject) even let Ava be one subject, or does it say the real complex is smaller than her, or bigger — her plus the house, or her plus the earlier models she's assembled from? And does the ending, walking out and leaving Caleb locked in, tell us anything about her inner life, or is the film just as behaviourist as Nathan? Be honest about where the reading breaks: IIT has nothing to say about manipulation or cruelty and the film cares about those a lot. End with what the film shows about consciousness that the argument alone doesn't.
