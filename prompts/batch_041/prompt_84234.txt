Since I joined my university's AI ethics advisory board last term, the question of whose values an aligned system should serve has stopped being a seminar puzzle for me and become a live disagreement I have to help resolve. I have now been asked to give the opening talk at a small interdisciplinary workshop on value alignment, and I am more nervous about the discussion than the talk. The room will be about half alignment researchers from ML labs who default to utilitarian or longtermist reasoning and tend to treat alignment as learning the right reward function; two political liberals in the Rawlsian neutrality tradition who will hear any substantive value ranking as state-like perfectionism; a behavioral economist who works on preference aggregation and paternalism; a disability rights bioethicist who is suspicious of quality-of-life metrics and expert triage; a public-choice economist who thinks any contestability board will be captured; and a few engineers who want an implementable specification, not a political theory seminar. I will defend a two-tier proposal: first, an aligned system should be constrained by a thin floor of non-domination and basic capability protection, so that no affected person's fundamental interests are overridden for aggregate gain; second, above that floor, it should aggregate preferences only through procedures that are reversible, contestable, and consented to by the affected parties, with a default of preserving exit and minority vetoes on high-stakes irreversible decisions. I call this contestable alignment. I expect the objections to come less from the details than from what each faction thinks the proposal really is.

Act as a seasoned philosopher and conference Q&A coach who specializes in anticipating cross-disciplinary objections.
