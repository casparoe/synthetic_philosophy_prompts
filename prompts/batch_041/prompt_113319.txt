I sit on the animal-welfare advisory panel of a food company that is scaling up insect protein, and our welfare code currently covers only vertebrates. The board wants to know whether to bring the crickets and black soldier fly larvae at our new rearing site under the code; that site will process on the order of 40 million larvae a week, and stunning before processing plus lower stocking density would raise our cost per kilogram by roughly 6% and cut throughput by about a tenth. The panel chair proposes a framework that multiplies the number of individuals by a credence in insect sentience — one in a thousand times forty million — and calls the product a very large expected welfare loss; one member objects that the credence belongs to the taxon as a whole rather than to each insect, so the panel faces a single lottery rather than forty million small independent bets; another calls the whole exercise a category error that would inflate the currency of moral concern. Please draft the short written opinion I will table at Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a timetable — whether to adopt the chair's framework, what conditions the site must meet, and by when — take a side on the aggregation and lottery objections rather than merely listing them, give the strongest consideration on the losing side its due, and state what evidence would make the panel revise.
