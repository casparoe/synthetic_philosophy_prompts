I've been going down a rabbit hole lately on how people use history to argue about current events, and I keep hitting the same wall. It started with an argument with a friend: whenever someone suggests talking to a hostile government, he says thats just another Munich, and someone else shoots back no, thats another Vietnam and we're the ones who get bogged down. Both of them have a real historical case and both act like the case settles it, which bugs me, because if the same method hands you opposite conclusions depending on which episode you reach for, then the method isn't doing the work, the person is. Then I read about the so-called Thucydides trap, where the idea is that a rising power and a ruling power tend to end up fighting, and I had the same itchy feeling, since it seems like you could point that pattern at almost anything after the fact.

What I actually want from you isnt more history, its the spec. Write me the list of requirements that any decent theory of arguing-from-history would have to meet before I'd trust it in a real argument. What would it have to get right or explain? I mean things like: how it handles the case where the same episode gets used to prove opposite lessons; whether it can tell me when the similarities that matter are the surface details versus the underlying mechanism (is Munich about one guy with a mustache, or about any concession to any aggressor?); what it does about the fact that we mostly only hear about the appeasements that blew up and not the hundred quiet ones that worked fine; and how it deals with the weird feedback loop where everybody learns the lesson so the next situation comes out different, which would seem to break the pattern it was drawn from in the first place. Also whether an analogy is ever really a prediction or if its mostly a way of making a decision sound respectable after the fact. For each requirement, tell me the case or consideration that forces it, and say which ones are actually non-negotiable and which are just the taste of one camp. If some of these can't all be true at once, say which, because thats the part I most want to see. And tell me which single requirement does the most to rule out the sloppy analogizing people do at dinner parties. Skip any reading list, I won't get to it.
