I've been trying to stress-test Rorty's antirepresentationalism and I'd rather do it against a real mess than the usual toy cases. Here's the mess: in 1996 a physicist named Alan Sokal wrote a deliberately nonsensical paper stuffed with grand-theory jargon about quantum gravity and hermeneutics, got a cultural-studies journal (Social Text) to publish it, and then announced it was a hoax. It got read everywhere as the smoking gun against relativism and social constructionism and the whole science-wars crowd that Rorty usually gets filed under.

So run the episode through Rorty's actual position, not the cartoon. Mapping first: who are the players, what did Sokal and the editors each have by way of reasons, and what does the theory say the acceptance of that paper actually consisted in? Rorty's move is roughly that the world can cause our beliefs but never justify them, that only we justify, so on his account the interesting thing about the hoax isn't that the paper failed to correspond to reality but that a vocabulary lost its grip on itself. Fine, grant him that. Now the part I actually care about, and I want you to be honest about it: does the episode leave a dent in the view? If the only tools Rorty has are which vocabulary won and what's useful to say, can he call the editors *wrong* rather than merely out-vocabularied -- and if he can't, is that a real problem for antirepresentationalism or just the price of the position, something he'd happily bite? One wrinkle worth folding in: what exposed the paper wasn't reality talking, it was a guy confessing, so the whole thing turns on a social fact rather than a representational one -- does that make the case easier or harder for him?

And then the other direction, which is what I'm really after: does this show that antirepresentationalism can't tell good science from bad without smuggling representation back in through the side door, or does the episode just illustrate that justification is social and that's exactly the point? I don't need the standard Sokal-moment history, I know it already. I want the two-way analysis -- what the theory predicts here, where it goes silent, and a straight answer on whether Rorty comes out of it fine or definately not.
