Since retiring from experimental physics, I have been reading moral philosophy and have become preoccupied with Kant's universalizability test. To make it tractable, I have been treating a maxim as a pair consisting of an action-type and an end, and asking whether that end could still be achieved in a world where every agent adopts the maxim. The formalization produces results I cannot reconcile: some maxims that seem plainly wrong pass, because their ends remain achievable under universal adoption, while some harmless maxims fail, because universal adoption would erode the background conditions they presuppose. I would like you to treat this formalization as a position and tell me what would count against it. Sort the objections from fatal to merely awkward, distinguishing those that are artifacts of how I individuate maxims, those that show universalizability cannot be a purely formal test, and those a Kantian should simply concede. Finally, say whether the position has been insulated by its qualifications, and where a scientist's instinct to operationalize is leading me astray.
