I am a novelist, and once a month I host a small salon for other fiction writers where we work through one idea from philosophy or the social sciences that we might actually use in a book. This month the idea is the unilateralist's curse. I'd rather not simply recount it to them; I want to plan how to teach it in fifty minutes, so that by the end they can feel it as a mechanism they could build a plot around rather than a curiosity they have merely heard named. Please give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion, and in each one tell me how confident you are and flag the point you are least sure of.

1. Tell me what has to be established first, and why the one idea that must come before the rest is the structure of a single agent acting alone under uncertainty -- one actor, one action, no coordination -- so that the idea that anything about numbers or probabilities comes second.

2. Name the single example that should carry the whole session, and say what it must have and what it must lack so that it never collapses into a tired trolley problem; my instinct is some version of an engineer who discovers a catastrophic flaw in a system and can quietly act alone, but I want your judgment.

3. Give me the misconceptions they will arrive with and the order in which they will form, and mark exactly where in the sequence I should head each off -- in particular the belief that the curse is about overconfidence or recklessness rather than about independent action, and the belief that acting is justified because everyone else will reason the same way.

4. Tell me the one question I can ask at the very end that would show whether the mechanism has landed -- ideally something that makes them build a case with two reasonable actors and see the disaster fall out of neither one's being wrong or foolish.

5. Tell me what I should openly refuse to cover in this session and say so out loud, and how much of the probability arithmetic they actually need in order to use the idea in a story.
