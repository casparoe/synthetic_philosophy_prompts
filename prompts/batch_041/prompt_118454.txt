Consider the following claim, which I shall call the epistemic subsidy thesis: in any market that approximates the conditions of the no-trade theorem — a common prior over the relevant states of the world, common knowledge of rationality (each participant knows the others are rational, each knows that each knows, and so on), and no exogenous motives to trade such as liquidity needs, hedging demands, or life-cycle consumption — the price system's capacity to aggregate private information depends constitutively on the presence of participants whose trades are not information-driven. On this thesis, the informational content of prices is parasitic on epistemically non-serious trading: remove or perfectly neutralise the noise introduced by such participants, and prices cease to be a reliable guide to fundamentals. What is your credence that this thesis is true?

I want a number, not a verdict, and I want the reasoning decomposed. If the thesis admits of several readings, state the reading you are pricing and why. Please assign a rough probability and one line of justification to each of the following sub-questions, adding any others you think belong: (1) how closely real financial markets approximate the no-trade theorem's assumptions, or how much of the theorem's conclusion survives when they are relaxed; (2) whether, under those assumptions, informed traders could still credibly reveal their information through prices without any non-informational trading — for instance through quote revisions, order placement, or the mere refusal to trade; (3) whether the familiar incentive problem that information acquisition is unrewarded if prices are perfectly informative requires noise or liquidity traders as a structural feature rather than a contingent one; (4) whether epistemic authority is best understood as reliability about fundamentals, as a coordination device, or as something else, and how that choice affects the thesis; and (5) whether the thesis is true only of certain asset classes or market microstructures, and whether that limits its philosophical interest. Then give the combination — how the sub-probabilities aggregate into your final number — and say which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to. Finally, tell me what evidence or argument would move your credence most, and in which direction. Please answer in British English.
