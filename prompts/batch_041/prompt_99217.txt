I am assembling the specification for a theory of Moore-paradoxical sentences — statements of the form "It is raining, but I do not believe that it is raining," together with their variants — and I would like the desiderata written out before I assess any particular proposal. Assume the theory is meant to be good enough to embed in a formal multi-agent doxastic logic and to do real work in a theory of the norms of assertion, not merely to gesture at a puzzle. Write the full list of requirements such a theory must meet: the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must preserve, the phenomena it must explain, and the results or neighboring puzzles it must not contradict. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or merely the preference of one camp. Where you suspect two requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, say so explicitly and explain the tension; that is the part I care about most.

I have in mind at least the following pressure points, though I do not want the list confined to them: (i) that the omissive form ("p, but I do not believe that p") is not a contradiction and can be true, whereas the commissive form ("p, but I believe that not-p") seems absurd in a different way and to a different degree; (ii) that the absurdity attaches both to asserting such a sentence and, perhaps more weakly, to merely thinking it; (iii) that first-person and third-person attributions behave differently, so that the corresponding third-person sentence is unremarkable; (iv) that the absurdity is sensitive to tense and to the embedding context, so that some embeddings defuse it; and (v) that the belief version appears to be a mere oddity while the knowledge version ("p, but I do not know that p") appears positively unknowable. A colleague of mine claims the entire phenomenon collapses into the observation that, from the first-person point of view, taking p to be true and believing p come to the same thing, so that there is no independent puzzle left to explain. I would like the desiderata to make clear what a theory must say about that reduction — whether it can be accepted, and if so what it leaves unexplained.

Close by naming the single requirement you think does the most to rule out otherwise plausible theories, and why.
