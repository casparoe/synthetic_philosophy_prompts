I'm a fourth-year PhD student working on epistemology and non-classical logic, and I'm trying to get a room of people to actually feel Fitch's knowability paradox instead of just nodding at the proof. I run a grad reading group — 12 to 14 people, mixed MA and PhD, decent but rusty modal logic — and we meet for 90 minutes, and I've got one spare session before we move on to something else. What I want is a game: rules, tokens, a board, small stakes, the works, that backs the group into the result that "every truth can in principle be known" collapses into "every truth is in fact known." It has to come out of the mechanics, so they arrive at the absurdity by trying to win rather than by me writing (C) on the whiteboard. Please don't have me announce the paradox up front, and I'd rather the words "knowability" and "Fitch" not appear until the debrief. By the end I want them to see that the move they'll all reach for — "fine, the Charter only covers basic facts, not these fancy self-referential ledger facts" — is definately available but costs something, and I want the game to make that cost visible rather than just sayable.

Here's the rough idea I've been kicking around, and feel free to gut it: a cabinet of, say, 18 sealed drawers each holding a colored token, a public board, three competing "inspectorates" of four, and a budget of inspection warrants deliberately too small to open everything, so there are truths nobody ends up certifying. Inspectors spend a warrant, open a drawer, and post a certificate ("drawer 7 is red") for points. On the wall is a Bureau Charter: the Bureau is founded on the ideal that every fact about the cabinet can in principle be certified by some inspection procedure, and there's a stack of procedure cards, one per drawer, so a procedure always exists. Two certification rules do the work, I think: you can't certify a compound fact unless you hold a separate certificate for each part, and a certificate has to be true. Then I post a bounty on "ignorance certificates" — claims of the form "drawer 7 is red and no inspectorate has certified drawer 7" — which are true of every drawer left unopened. My hunch is the trap closes when they try to collect that bounty: certifying the drawer makes the second half false, so the bounty can't be collected, yet the Charter says every true fact is certifiable. I need you to work out whether this actually works, repair whatever's broken in it, and turn it into something runnable with real rules and a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money.

Then I need the debrief worked out properly, because that's the part I always fumble.
