I've got a puzzle-shape that keeps turning up and I want to know if it has a name, or whether it's really three seperate things that just look alike. I'll describe it rather than guess at the jargon, because the jargon is exactly what I'm missing.

First one. I throw a dart at a circular board and I tell you it's just as likely to land at any point on the board as at any other. Whats the chance it lands in the inner half of the radius, i.e. nearer the middle than halfway out? By area it's a quarter. My mate says it's a half, because the distance from the centre is just as likely to come out small as large, so half the time it's in the inner half of the distance. Neither of us has messed up the arithmetic. We can each point at a symmetry the other one is ignoring, and it doesn't feel like either of us is cheating. But 'equally likely to land anywhere' was supposed to be the whole specification.

Second, which may or may not be the same thing. You pick a point truly at random on a sphere. Now condition on the point lying on one particular great circle -- the one where the longitude reads zero. Take the limit of a very thin sliver of sphere around that circle and see how the probability spreads out along it. Then do the same for the great circle where the latitude reads zero. You get a different spread. But the two circles are the same circle up to a rotation, and the original rule for picking the point doesn't care about rotations at all, so how can the answers come out different? A friend told me this one resolves somehow, but I didn't follow the resolution, and I'm not sure I'd recognise a good resolution if I heard one.

Third, possibly unrelated. 'Pick a whole number at random, all of them equally likely.' That seems to break differently from the first two -- you don't get two rival answers, you get no answer at all -- and I want to know whether that's a different failure or the same one wearing a hat.

So: is there one name for this, and what's the closest established thing to each of the three, and where exactly does each fall short of the others? The label is honestly not what I'm after. What I want is the carving. In the dart case, are we both right, or is one of us just wrong and it's only the framing that makes it look symmetric? Is there a rule for picking the right symmetry -- and if someone says 'use the symmetry group of the problem', what makes one group *the* group, and are there cases where that instruction gives up and goes quiet? And is there a way to tell a genuine both-answers-are-right case from a case where two answers are on offer but only one is defensible? If nothing established covers it, coin a term, define it, and tell me what it doesn't cover.
