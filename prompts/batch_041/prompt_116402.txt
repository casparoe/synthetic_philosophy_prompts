I have no training in philosophy or cognitive science, but I keep returning to one idea I stumbled on and can't put down: that the self is a strange loop — that a system can build a symbol for itself, and that this symbol, though it is only one more thing the system produces, ends up standing for the whole system that produces it, so that the "I" is at once a small part and a picture of the entire machine. I want a poem that enacts this instead of explaining it. Please write it as a pantoum, since I understand the form loops its own lines back on themselves — the second and fourth lines of each stanza returning as the first and third of the next, and the closing stanza folding back onto the opening line — so that the poem could begin again exactly where it ends. Let that returning machinery carry the argument: each repeated line should mean something different when it comes back, first as bare mechanism (a firing, a symbol, a pattern doing only what patterns do) and later as a self that seems to own the whole, and it should be genuinely hard to say whether the "I" is running the loop or the loop is running the "I". It should read as roughly right to someone who already knows the idea, and as a good poem to someone who has never heard of it.
