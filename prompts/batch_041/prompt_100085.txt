Alright, I'm assembling a small collection of decision-theory puzzles for a thing I'm working on, and I need one built to order. Here's the spec.

A decision problem where causal decision theory and updateless decision theory both come out on the side of doing the thing, but evidential decision theory says dont. Quick glosses so we're on the same page: causal = compare how the world looks if you do it vs if you don't, ignoring any correlation that isn't caused by your choice; evidential = condition on your act as evidence about the world and pick whatever looks better that way; updateless = pick the policy you'd want to have locked in before you knew which situation you were going to wake up in, so it isn't moved by the mere fact of finding yourself here.

Constraints, and I mean these:

- No predictor, no simulation, no copies or twins of you, no blackmailer who knows your source code. The only link between your choice and the outcome is that both are downstream of the same thing. No acausal channel at all.
- Not a medical case. No tumours, no genes, no "it turns out you have a latent condition" — I've seen that exact case a hundred times and I need something I can actually reuse. Ordinary social or economic setting, the sort of thing a normal adult could plausibly be staring at this week.
- The shared upstream thing has to be something the agent can partly observe, so its not just a hidden magic fact about them.
- Whole scenario in one paragraph, payoffs in concrete stuff (money, an evening, a job offer), not "utility".
- It has to be a case where you can't shrug and say "eh, that's just Newcomb in disguise." So show me explicitly that the correlation does not run through your decision algorithm.

Then:

1. Run all three theories and say where each one lands and why.
2. Name the single feature doing the work, and tell me what happens if you delete it — I'd expect evidential to come back and join the other two, but correct me if I'm wrong.
3. Now flip updateless over to evidential's side with the smallest change you can find, and say what you changed. If the smallest change has to be bringing in a predictor or a copy, then say that and explain why nothing smaller will do.
4. Steelman the evidentialist: what do they say is wrong with how the case is set up, and is that a real objection or a dodge?

Why I'm asking, so you know what counts as cheating: this is going into a set of cases meant to test whether someone actually gets the difference between evidential and causal/updateless reasoning, so a case that stipulates the verdicts, or smuggles the verdict in through the setup, is worthless to me. No reading list or citations needed, just the case and the reasoning.
