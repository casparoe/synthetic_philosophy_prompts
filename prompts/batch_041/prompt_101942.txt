Call it the safe-intensity view. The pleasure in sad art isn't pleasure taken in sadness itself; it's a meta-level response to the fact of being moved at all. What we enjoy is having a real, strong, involuntary emotional reaction — and sad art is unusually good at delivering one on demand, because unlike comedy it doesn't depend on us being in the right mood or on the joke landing. A sad film can make you cry whenever you sit down to it. So the sadness is instrumental: it's the toll you pay to get a vivid feeling, and what you're actually savoring is the intensity plus the fact that the stakes aren't real — genuine emotion at zero real-world cost.
