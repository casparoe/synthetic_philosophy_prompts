My gut says something's off but I can't articulate it, and he definitely knows more math than me.

So: write me an essay on whether trained networks literally have representations that interpretability methods discover, or whether those methods mostly manufacture a story that happens to fit. Here's the thing I keep coming back to, and I'd like you to take it seriously instead of waving it away: suppose someone builds a tool that, pointed at any layer of any network, produces a fluent, confident, natural-language account of what that layer "represents" — and it does this just as fluently when you point it at a randomly initialized net that's never seen data, or at a net whose weights you've secretly permuted. Does that show the tool is reading the network, or that the tool is a very good novelist? Weigh the strongest case for realism (the side I suspect is right) against the strongest deflationary case, and land somewhere — I'd rather you commit than shrug.
