I'm taking a seminar on perception and cognition this term, and before I commit to a term paper I want to run my current position past someone, because I suspect I've absorbed commitments from several different literatures without noticing that they pull in different directions. Here is where I've landed. First, I think perception is cognitively penetrable in a substantive sense: what a subject believes, desires, or expects can change what she perceptually experiences, and not merely how she interprets or responds to a fixed experiential given. Second, I think the content of perceptual experience is at least partly nonconceptual — the fineness of grain in experience outruns the concepts the subject actually possesses. Third, I hold a fairly strong view about perceptual justification: a subject is immediately justified in believing what her experience seems to present, without needing any independent evidence that her perceptual faculties are reliable; the mere fact that it looks a certain way is enough to make the corresponding belief justified. Fourth, I think the perception/cognition divide marks a real architectural joint rather than a merely pragmatic line — the perceptual system does its own proprietary work, and central cognition does not simply flow back into it. And finally, I take seriously the familiar datum that illusions like the Müller-Lyer persist even once you know the two lines are the same length, and I treat that persistence as telling us something about how the perceptual system is organized.

What I want is an audit rather than a summary. Which of these commitments are actually in tension with each other, and how badly? I would like the tensions ranked by severity, with an explanation in each case of what would have to give — which commitment is doing the load-bearing work and which one is the cheaper thing to abandon — and I would especially like to know whether some of the pairs that look opposed are only apparently so, and if so why the appearance is misleading. I have not decided what I would give up, so please do not assume that any particular one is negotiable; and if the whole package can be made consistent by one minimal revision rather than several, tell me which revision that is and what it costs.
