A neurologist friend once described a patient to me, and I have since come across the same picture in a few secondhand accounts. After certain kinds of brain damage, a person will say that a pinprick or a burn hurts — they can localize it and describe its quality — yet they do not flinch or grimace, and some of them seem almost amused; asked whether it bothers them, they say it does not, and not in the sense a stoic or a masochist would give the word. I have been assuming that the sensation is fully intact and only its unpleasantness has been subtracted, though I suspect that assumption is doing more work than I could defend.

Is there an established name for this, and, more to the point, what is the nearest existing concept, and how does the thing I have described differ from (a) not feeling pain at all, (b) feeling it fully but enduring it, and (c) feeling it while being unable to care about anything? And what does the picture show, if anything, about whether the badness of pain is intrinsic to the sensation or is a separable component — and how much of that inference survives if my assumption about intact sensation is false? I am not after a reading list; I want the phenomenon, its boundaries, and what it does and does not establish.
