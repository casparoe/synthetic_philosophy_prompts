Bit of an odd one, and I'm not sure it's even the kind of question you answer — I'm the only data person at a small credit union (about 30 staff, we mostly do small loans for people the banks won't touch), and I built the scorecard that decides which applications we approve. Nothing fancy: logistic regression, a dozen variables, no neural nets. The board likes it that way, partly because we can explain any single decision to a member who rings up and asks why.

Anyway, we're being pulled in two directions and I can't work out if I'm missing something. Our board (and the charitable funder who keeps us afloat) has been reading about bias in algorithms and now insists that the same share of applicants from each of our two main member groups should get approved — they don't want to hear that one group gets through less often, whatever the reason. Then the auditors who come in every year started asking a different question: of the people who would actually have paid the loan back, was the approval rate the same for both groups? Not approval rates overall — approval rates among those who'd have repaid. And those two things don't seem willing to be true at the same time. Every time I shift the threshold to even up the overall rates, the auditors' figures get worse, and the other way round. Its not a bug in my code as far as I can tell. The two groups also genuinely differ in how often people repay — and awkwardly, that difference is partly our own doing, because we've been turning one group down more for years, so for most of them we've simply never seen whether they'd have repaid. Our repayment data only exists for people we already said yes to.

I've got to stand up in front of the board next Thursday and I don't want to either promise them something I can't deliver or come across as the defeatist who just makes excuses. A colleague reckons the fix is to stop recording which group anyone belongs to; my flatmate, who did a maths degree, suggested flipping a coin for the borderline cases. I did a philosophy module in first year and I keep half-remembering that there's something seperate going on here that isn't just about my threshold, but I can't place it. What should I actually say to them? Is there a defensible way to pick between the two demands, or is that a values call dressed up as a technical one — and if it is, how do I tell them that without it sounding like I'm dodging? And what do I do about the coin idea?
