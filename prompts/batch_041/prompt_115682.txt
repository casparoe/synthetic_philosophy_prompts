Consider the following repair for the problem of logical omniscience. Model an agent's epistemic state as a finite stock of sentences it has explicitly registered together with a fixed set of inference rules, and count the agent as knowing exactly those sentences that the rules derive from that stock within a bounded number of steps. Explain why a notion of knowledge built this way cannot, for any choice of step bound, simultaneously be (i) closed under the very inferences the agent is permitted to perform, (ii) sensitive to the difference between a sentence the agent has registered and a logically equivalent sentence it has not, and (iii) invariant under changes in notation or axiomatization. Bring out the trade-off between bounding steps and closing under consequences, and what it implies for the distinction between implicit and explicit belief. Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
