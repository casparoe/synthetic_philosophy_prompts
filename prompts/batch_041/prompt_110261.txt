I'm in a reading group on philosophy of mind that has been circling the same fight for about four months now, and I've somehow ended up as the person who writes things down. Two of us are dug in on opposite sides of the original-vs-derived intentionality business. A is on the Dennett side: intentionality comes in degrees, the difference between a thermostat's aboutness and mine is one of richness and interpretive payoff rather than kind, "original intentionality" as some special biological grade of the stuff is a story we tell because we're the ones doing the interpreting, and the whole point of the two-bitser was that you can't locate the meaning in the machine's insides — so if it isn't there, and it obviously isn't in the coin, it must be in the interpretive practice, and that's interpreters all the way down. B is on the Searle side: there is a real distinction, intrinsic intentionality is a biological feature of brains the way digestion is a feature of stomachs, the intentionality of words and pictures and computers is borrowed from the minds that made and use them, a simulation of digestion doesn't digest anything and that should tell you what to think about simulated belief, and for B the bottom of the chain is consciousness — a state that couldn't in principle be conscious isn't an intentional state at all, it's just a state. They keep missing each other: A says B has smuggled in a homunculus and a magic word, B says A has quietly become a behaviorist who dissolved the very phenomenom he set out to explain.

I want the document they should have written instead. So: draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between these two camps, the kind of thing A and B could both sign in good faith. I need the claims they can both accept — about what intentionality talk is even for, about which of the disputed cases are genuinely unsettled versus merely underdescribed, about what an explanation of aboutness would have to deliver for either of them to count it as a success — then the disagreements stated precisely, each side's view phrased in wording that side would accept as fair rather than as a strawman, then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them. For each crux I want a case or argument that both sides agree in advance would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down before the fact. Please also say which parts of the fight are just verbal, if any, and what would genuinely move the needle versus what would only look like it did.

To keep this from being a rehash of the standard cases, work it through these instead. (i) A water-level sensor designed by engineers, plus a second unit built by a foundry that copies the first one's layout from a scan and has no representation of water or tanks or levels at all — identical insides, different lineage — plus a third unit, identical to the first, whose tank was decommissioned and removed years ago, so its internal state now tracks nothing. (ii) A hard drive on a dead satellite, still holding an encoded record of temperatures from a mission nobody will ever revisit, in a format whose documentation has been destroyed; no reader will ever exist, and I mean that as a matter of fact, not of principle. (iii) A tree's growth rings or a lake's sediment layers — causally produced by rainfall, information-bearing, readable by a scientist, but not designed by anyone, not shaped by selection for recording, and not used by the recorder for anything. My group calls (ii) a ghost-reader case and we cannot agree whether (ii) and (iii) are the same case, opposite cases, or a case and a non-case. (iv) A program shaped by thousands of generations of selection on a task, whose internal state reliably tracks a feature of its environment, where no designer ever intended it to track anything and the system itself doesn't consult the state — the state just sits there doing its causal work. (v) A module inside a larger system whose sole job is to predict the rest of that system by ascribing beliefs and desires to it — is its ascription original, derived, or neither, and does the answer change if the module is itself built out of other modules that ascribe to each other?

Give it to me as exactly five numbered parts, each a short paragraph or a small table, with no preamble and nothing after the fifth part. And no bibliography or reading list — I know the literature, I want the document.
