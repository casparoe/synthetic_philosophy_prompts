I've been stuck in a reccurring argument with a colleague for about two years and I want an outside diagnosis instead of another round. It's about whether generations — boomers, millennials, gen z, whatever — are real things. He's a generationalist: he thinks a cohort like "millennials" picks out a genuine social kind, that there's something shared in how people who came of age in a given stretch of years got formed, and that you can explain behaviour (housing, voting, work) by appealing to it. I'm the skeptic: I think most of what gets called a generation effect is just age, or period, or a story told after the fact about a group that only got defined afterwards. We actually agree on a lot. We agree people born in different years differ. We agree the labels are half marketing. And yet neither of us has moved an inch.

What I want is a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this really is. My own guess, which I'd like you to test rather than just nod at, is that three different things are tangled up here: (1) a formal/statistical problem — that age, period and cohort can't all be pulled apart from one another in the data, so the empirical question is partly unanswerable in principle and not just hard; (2) a metaphysical question about what it takes for a category to be a real kind, and whether generations could meet that bar even if the cohort effects were real; and (3) a disagreement about what a social explanation is supposed to do — whether naming a generation explains a pattern or just relabels it. I don't know which of those is actually doing the work, or whether they're even separable.

So tell me which parts of this are substantive, which are verbal (does "real kind" just mean "useful label" for one of us?), whether one side is answering a different question than the other under the same heading, and whether the impasse is a sign the question is ill-posed rather than merely hard. Ground it in what the two positions actually claim — steelman both rather than caricature them. Then tell me what would count as progress: a distinction, a better method, a shift in the question, or nothing at all, because it's resting on intuitions about "realness" that neither of us can argue for. And if my three-way guess is the wrong cut, say so and give me the better one.
