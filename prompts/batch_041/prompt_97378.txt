so I've been lurking on LessWrong and the various spin-off Discords for maybe a decade now, and my favourite recurring flamewar is the "is the wavefunction real" thing — the psi-ontic crowd who think it's a literal object sitting out there in 3N-dimensional configuration space, versus the psi-epistemic crowd who say it's just a bookkeeping device for our information or credences, versus the "shut up and calculate" people who think the whole arguement is a category error, versus the many-worlds and Bohmian folks who've already moved on. I want to commission a piece of satire about this for a little zine some friends and I are putting together, and I figure you're the right person for it.

Here's the brief: write a mock product-reviews page — Amazon/Yelp style — for a product literally called "The Wavefunction," sold by a made-up vendor. Include maybe six or seven reviews, each from a recognizable camp, with star ratings, timestamps and "verified purchase" tags, plus a "response from the seller" at the end that tries to have it both ways. The jokes have to come from getting the actual positions right rather than from puns: the ontic realist who's thrilled it's "really there" but annoyed about the extra dimensions, the QBist who gives two stars because if it's just her personal credences she's not sure why she paid, the Copenhagen pragmatist who says it works fine and the metaphysics is a you-problem, the many-worlds reviewer who got an absurd number of branches and considers this good value, the Bohmian whose unit shipped with a hidden pilot wave and no assembly instructions, etc. Invented product details are fine and encouraged — a "reality" feature sold as paid DLC, a note that collapse is "sold separately," a warranty that only covers the epistemic reading — as long as every joke is something someone who's actually read the literature would recognise as a real feature of the view and not just a pun. Keep it deadpan; don't wink at the reader or explain the jokes. Around 650 words, and it should be funny to a physicist and still followable by my flatmate who does marketing.
