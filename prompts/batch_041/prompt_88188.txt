Consider two vases. Vase A is made of ordinary glass: by every intrinsic microstructural measure it is fragile, yet a hidden device is triggered by the same vibration that any blow would produce and instantly toughens it, so that it never breaks when struck. Vase B is made of toughened glass: by every intrinsic microstructural measure it is not fragile, yet a hidden device is triggered by the same vibration and shatters it, so that it always breaks when struck. Neither device is part of its vase, and each is reliably attached to its vase for the vase's entire career. Call each device a fink, and note that in A the fink removes a fragility the glass otherwise has, while in B it manufactures a fragility the glass otherwise lacks.

Write an essay on what these cases reveal about what it is for something to be fragile. Weigh the main options: that fragility is fixed by the object's intrinsic categorical structure (so both vases are as their glass type says); that it is fixed by the object's manifestation profile (so A is not fragile and B is); and that it is fixed by some third thing — a causal power, a position in a wider network of dispositions, or nothing real at all. Address at least one objection to whichever verdict you reach, including the objection that the fink cases trade on a distinction without a difference. Reach a conclusion and defend it, rather than resting on the observation that the cases are puzzling.
