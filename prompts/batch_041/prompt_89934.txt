Alright, I want to plant a flag on a position and then have you try to knock it over. Not a survey — I want the things that should actualy move me, sorted.

The position has two halves.

First half: there's no common phenomenal quality shared by all pleasures. I've stared at this and I can't find the common ingredient. Espresso, scratching a mosquito bite, the relief of a hot shower after a cold walk, finally sitting down after standing all day, winning an argument, the smell of gasoline, that little jolt when someone you dislike fails at something. I can't find a shared feel in any of it. What they share is the label, not a quality. So I think pleasure is disjunctive — closer to game or junk than to red.

Second half: but I don't think it's a total ragbag, because there's still a common pull. Pleasures are all things that, other things equal, we're disposed to approach, repeat, and want more of. So maybe pleasure is unified functionally and not phenomenally: the texture varies, the pull doesn't.

And on top of both halves I'm a hedonist about well-being — a life goes well to the extent its got more pleasure and less pain, full stop. I know that's in tension with the first half and I want to know how bad the tension is.

So:

- What counts against the first half, what counts against the second, and what counts against the package?
- Sort the defeaters by how decisive they are. Some of these I expect are just awkward; tell me which are fatal.
- For the second half: do the pull and the texture ever come apart? Give me the cases — things that feel wonderful but that you have zero urge to repeat, things you crave and pursue but don't actually enjoy. If they come apart in both directions, what's left of "pull"?
- For the first half: colors are all different from each other and we still count them as one kind. Why doesn't that same move rescue pleasure? And is "positive valence" itself a thin common quality that I'm just refusing to see?
- If there's a common pull but no common feel, doesn't hedonism either turn into a desire view (the wanting is what matters) or into a view about a quality that isn't one quality (the feeling is what matters)? If it collapses into a rival view, say which one.
- I'll skip dessert for a backrub. Does that show there's a common currency after all, or can the disjunctive view explain the trade-off?
- Does my view predict anything the unity view doesn't — anything that could come out either way and embarrass me if it went the wrong way?
- What should someone who holds both halves concede actually counts against them, as opposed to where they'd just dig in?
- And be honest: is the first half insulated by being about what I can't find on introspection? Could anything count against it, or is it unfalsifiable — and is that fatal here or fine?

One case I can't place: a wirehead whose food, sex, music, and friendships have all been rerouted through one wire so that they all feel identical, but still intensely pleasant. Does that show the common core was there all along, or only that you can manufacture a fake one?
