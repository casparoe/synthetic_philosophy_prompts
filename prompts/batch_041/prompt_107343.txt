It feels more like something I see. Like a color. Just there. He thinks emotions are judgments: appraisals, things that can be true or false, things that reasons can revise. I think they're perceptions: more like seeing the world as insulting or dangerous, not something you can just decide to stop doing. We've been going in circles for months and I'm tired of it.

I want the document we should have written together. Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between someone who holds the judgment view of emotions and someone who holds the perceptual view. I want:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides could actually sign — about what an emotion is, about which cases any theory has to account for, about what's shared between the two views (I suspect alot more than we admit), and about what would count as an emotion being "correct" versus just being understandable.
2. The real points of disagreement, each phrased so that the side holding it would accept the wording as fair to them — not strawmanned, not softened into mush.
3. The cruxes: the sub-questions where settling the thing would actually move one of us.
4. For each crux, a concrete case or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Here are two I keep coming back to and I'd like them used: (a) my friend Maya, who after a car crash knows perfectly well that driving is safe but still gets a lurch of fear every time she's a passenger — is that lurch a false judgment she keeps re-making, or a perception that just fires? and (b) whether emotions in animals and pre-verbal infants count as the same kind of thing as our emotions, given they plausibly don't have the concepts a judgment view seems to need. Also maybe bring in whether beliefs can "penetrate" an emotion the way they can penetrate perception — if they can, I want to know whether that collapses the two views into each other or not.
5. If any part of our fight turns out to be just a verbal dispute — us using "judgment" or "perception" in different senses — say which part and why.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.

One thing though: please be upfront about whats actually settled versus whats genuinely contested among people who know this topic, and don't hand me a contested view dressed up as the consensus.
