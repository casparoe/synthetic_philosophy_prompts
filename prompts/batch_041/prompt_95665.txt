I'm a retired atmospheric scientist -- spent thirty years running climate models, now trying to teach myself philosphy in retirement. A former colleague of mine, a development economist who worked in Singapore for a decade, gave me his take on the whole "can authoritarianism be legitimate if it delivers?" debate. He said, more or less:

"Look, all this democracy-and-rights stuff is Western parochialism. What actually makes a government legitimate is whether it delivers the goods: safety, jobs, housing, a rising standard of living. That's the lesson of Singapore and, increasingly, China. The Chinese have the mandate of heaven -- if the emperor screws up, he loses it; if he delivers, he keeps it. So performance legitimacy just means the government earns its right to rule by performing, not by winning elections. Political scientists distinguish input legitimacy from output legitimacy -- input is who gets to decide, output is what gets delivered -- and by that standard the EU runs on output legitimacy. Extend that to China and Singapore and you've got it. It's basically utilitarianism applied to politics: the greatest good for the greatest number, measured in GDP and order. The West is in decline because it forgot this. And the proof is that Singapore and China are stable and prosperous without Western-style democracy, so they're legitimate. If a government can keep the trains running and the economy growing, it doesn't need to ask permission."

I'm inclined to think he's onto something -- I've been to Singapore, it's impressive -- but I also suspect he's sliding between different senses of "legitimate" (does it mean people beleive it's right, or that it really is right?) and maybe stacking the deck. Can you go through it claim by claim and tell me what a careful political philosopher would accept, what's a simplification or a conflation, what a defender of performance legitimacy would say he's gotten wrong, and whether his final conclusion actually follows from the parts that survive? I don't need reading lists or citations, just the analysis. I'm not trying to grade him, I just want to know what to trust.
