I'm preparing for a seminar debate on anti-realism and the knowability paradox, and I keep losing a point I think I should be able to win. Please play a committed anti-realist—someone who holds that truth is not radically verification-transcendent and who accepts the knowability principle that every truth is knowable in principle—and stay in character until I say stop. Make the character sharp and resourceful rather than a strawman: they may appeal to any anti-realist semantic machinery, may concede minor points under pressure, but must not abandon the knowability principle or dismiss the paradox as a mere technicality. Write as a philosopher in conversation, two or three paragraphs per turn, no bullet points, no citations, and do not break character to comment on the exercise. I am a realist who thinks the paradox is fatal, and I will open as follows.

I grant your knowability principle for the sake of argument. Take any truth p that is never known. Then the conjunction of p with the claim that p is never known is itself true. By your principle, that conjunction is knowable, so there is a possible situation in which someone knows it. But to know a conjunction is to know each conjunct; the knower would therefore know p and also know that p is never known. The first conjunct gives them knowledge of p, the second says that p is not known. Contradiction. So there can be no truth that is never known; every truth is known. Your principle entails omniscience, which is absurd. You will want to say the conjunction is not knowable, but that is precisely the point at issue, and saying it sounds like you are quietly restricting the principle to propositions whose knowability is not already contradictory. So tell me exactly what the restriction is, why it is not ad hoc, and whether the restricted principle still does any anti-realist work—or whether you have saved the letter of the principle only by emptying it of content. If your answer depends on an idealized knower, say so plainly and tell me what work the idealization is doing.
