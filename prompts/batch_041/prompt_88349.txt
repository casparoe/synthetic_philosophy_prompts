I'm in a small reading group on metaphysics and we've spent the last four months on the special composition question -- when do some things add up to a further thing? -- and I'm starting to think we'll never get anywhere with it. Here's the shape of our problem. One of us is a nihilist: she says nothing ever composes anything, so strictly speaking there are no tables, no stars, no people, just arrangements of tiny simples. Another is a universalist: any things whatsoever compose a further thing, so there's an object made of the top half of my coffee cup plus the moon, and another made of all the hairs on your head plus the Andromeda galaxy. And a third holds a restricted view -- composition happens when the parts are caught up in a single life, so organisms exist but heaps and artifacts don't.

The thing that bugs me is that nobody moves. The universalist's objects seem absurd, but he just says our intuitions about which objects there are are worthless, and honestly he has something, because every attempt to draw a non-arbitrary line (contact, being stuck together, being fastened, having a common function) either lets in things nobody wants or leaves out things everyone wants, and any line seems to have a sorites problem lurking behind it. Meanwhile the nihilist says there are no tables, which none of us can bring ourselves to beleive, but every time someone raises the table she says: fine, point to the thing you mean, you'll have to give me conditions, and you haven't got any that aren't arbitrary. And the restricted theorist looks like she's just picked her favourite class of objects and promoted it to a rule.

So here's my hypothesis, and I'm not sure I believe it. Maybe the stall isn't about objects at all. Maybe the three of us are answering different questions under one heading: the nihilist is telling us what's fundamental, the universalist is telling us what our language quantifies over, and the restricted theorist is telling us which composite things we have reason to talk about -- and the word 'exist' is being stretched to cover all three. Or maybe the real disagreement is a hidden one about method, i.e. whether intuitions about what there is can count as evidence at all. Or maybe it's just brute starting intuitions, in which case it's a taste dispute and we should stop.

There's also one sub-thing we keep circling and I can't tell if it's the crux or a distraction: whether composition is identity -- the thought that the parts just are the whole, so many things can be identical to one thing -- is even intelligible. If it is, the universalist looks almost trivially right; if it isn't, a lot of the literature on this collapses. And I've got a nagging feeling that something about whether you can talk about 'the simples' as a plurality without already helping yourself to a composite is doing quiet work here, but I can't say what.

What I want is a diagnosis rather than a verdict -- I don't need you to tell me who's right about tables. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it verbal, and if so exactly which part (the word 'exist'? 'thing'? 'compose'?)? Is it substantive but resting on a methodological disagreement that could be seperated out and argued about on its own? Is there a formal or conceptual result that would settle one of the pieces -- something about plural quantification, or about identity, or about vagueness -- and if so, what would it have to show? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that would count as progress for us, or should we conclude the question is just badly posed and go and do something else with our Tuesdays?

Two more things. Don't just survey the positions back at me -- we know them, and I can't look anything up from here, so please don't lean on particular papers or books or who said what; argue it out from the ideas themselves. And if my framing is confused -- if composition-as-identity isn't the crux I think it is, or if I've set up a false choice between 'verbal' and 'substantive', or if asking whether it's verbal is itself the confused bit -- then just say so rather than going along with it. I'd much rather be told my question is broken than get a tidy answer to a question I didn't ask. British English, please.
