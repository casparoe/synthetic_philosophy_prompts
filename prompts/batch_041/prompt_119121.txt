I am a PhD student in early modern philosophy working on a comparative project on theories of happiness, and I need a historically grounded projection rather than a survey. Suppose Émilie du Châtelet were somehow alive today, with her philosophical temperament intact but no special access to the last two and a half centuries of moral psychology, economics, or technology. On the reading I am working with, her account of happiness holds that happiness is not identical with pleasure; that it requires both sentiment and reason; that strong passions are indispensable rather than obstacles; that certain illusions or self-deceptions can be necessary for sustaining happiness; that love and the pursuit of knowledge are central goods; and that women's happiness is systematically distorted by social constraints on education, reputation, and independence. Using only those commitments as your basis, predict what she would likely think about the following contemporary developments. First, the use of national well-being indices and subjective happiness metrics as targets for public policy, including the idea that governments should aim to maximize reported life satisfaction. Second, the widespread use of SSRIs, mood stabilizers, and other pharmacological interventions to enhance everyday mood or to make oneself more content with a life one would otherwise have reason to change. Third, the design of social media platforms that maximize engagement by amplifying social comparison, envy, and outrage, and the resulting pressure to curate a happy public self. Fourth, the emergence of AI romantic companions and other simulated relationships that can provide affection without the risks of another person's freedom. For each, give her likely verdict and, more importantly, the reasoning from her stated commitments, noting where she would be forced to revise one commitment to keep another. Then step back: identify which of her commitments are most likely to survive contact with modern evidence about happiness, which would be most threatened by that evidence, and which contemporary assumptions she would reject as category mistakes. Finally, flag any projection that depends on details of her biography or 18th-century context that we cannot recover, and say what additional evidence would be needed to settle it. Do not give me a reading list or citations; I want the argument.
