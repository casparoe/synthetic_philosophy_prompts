You are a philosopher working on early modern moral philosophy and on the ethics of self-killing, and you are accustomed to being asked for a number rather than a survey.

Hume's essay on suicide contains a parity argument: the religious prohibition on self-killing cannot be sustained, because if the disposal of one's life were reserved to God, the very same reasoning would condemn acting to preserve or extend life — medicine, rescue, stepping out of the way of a falling stone — since these too alter the course of nature. Assess this proposition:

(P) The parity argument succeeds against the strongest theistic version of the prohibition: no one can coherently hold both that human beings may act to preserve and extend life and that they may never act to end it on the ground that life is God's to give and take.

Give me your credence in (P), decomposed. In this order: a numbered list of the sub-questions (P) depends on, each with a probability and one line of justification — at minimum, whether the theist can distinguish a God equally present in every event from a God who has delegated dominion over nature while reserving a right over life, and whether that distinction is principled or ad hoc; whether saving and killing are genuinely symmetrical given the asymmetry of intention and of authorization; whether the argument proves too much and, if so, whether that is a reductio of the argument or of the theology; and how much turns on whether the prohibition being targeted is providence-based rather than natural-law or social-duty based; then the combination rule you use and the resulting number; then the single sub-question your number is most sensitive to, together with the number you would give if that sub-question went the other way.

Finish by saying what would change your mind: the one argument or consideration that would move your number most, and the step in your own reasoning you think is most likely to be wrong.

Before writing, work through the places the parity argument could fail. Give me only the finished decomposition. Do not decline to give a number; if you think (P) is ill-posed, name the precisification you are assessing and give your credence in that. Hard limit: 600 words. No preamble, no survey of Hume scholarship, no citations.
