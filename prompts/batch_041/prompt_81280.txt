I'm a self-taught programmer with no philosophy background, but I read Gödel Escher Bach years ago and one part has been stuck in my head ever since, the bit about where meaning lives. As I remember it Hofstadter splits a message into three layers. The frame message is the stuff that tells you this thing is a message at all and that someone meant it to be read — for the Voyager record, it's the fact that the disc is an obviously manufactured object with a cover on it, so any alien who finds it knows it isn't just a rock. The outer message is the instructions for how to decode the thing — the diagrams on the cover, the hydrogen line as a clock, the pulsar map, that kind of stuff. The inner message is the actual content you get once you've decoded it, the music and the pictures. And his big claim, as I recall, is that meaning isn't sitting in any one of those three places, it's in the interaction between them and whatever receiver is doing the decoding — a message only means something to a system that already has the right internal structure to be triggered by it. I might be mangling some of this so correct me where I'm off.
