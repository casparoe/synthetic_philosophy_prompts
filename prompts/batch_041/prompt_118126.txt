The exercise was:

(a) When an agent's evidence arrives uncertainly — her credences over each member of some partition shift, but she doesn't become certain of any of them — how should she update her credences in other propositions? State the rule. (b) How does that rule relate to ordinary conditionalization; is one a special case of the other? (c) The rule puts a constraint on which conditional probabilities are allowed to change. State that constraint and say whether it's a consequence of the rule or an assumption the rule rests on. (d) Apply the rule to the following case. An agent watches a tank that is either contaminated (C) or clean (¬C), with prior P(C) = 0.3. An inline sensor displays one of three symbols, neg / trace / pos, with P(neg|C) = 0.1, P(trace|C) = 0.3, P(pos|C) = 0.6, and P(neg|¬C) = 0.7, P(trace|¬C) = 0.25, P(pos|¬C) = 0.05. She looks at the display and reads 'trace'. What should her credence in C become, and which update rule are you using? (e) Someone claims that an agent who satisfies the probability axioms has no choice at all about how to update — any other update would be incoherent. Discuss.

And the answer I have to mark:

(a) You hold the conditional probabilities within each cell of the partition fixed and take a weighted average of them, the weights being your new credences in the cells: P_new(A) = Σ_i P_new(E_i) · P_old(A | E_i). The partition has to be fixed in advance and stay the same before and after. (b) Ordinary conditionalization is the special case where you become certain of one cell: set P_new(E_1) = 1 and the formula collapses to P_new(A) = P_old(A | E_1). So the uncertain-evidence rule is the more general one and conditioning is its degenerate limit. (c) The constraint is that the conditional probabilities given each cell don't move: P_new(A | E_i) = P_old(A | E_i) for every A and every cell E_i. I'd say this isn't an extra assumption you have to swallow — it's what coherence itself demands. Nothing about the probability axioms changes between the two times, so the only thing that can move is your distribution over the cells; if you also moved the conditional probabilities you'd be changing your mind about how the evidence bears on the hypotheses with nothing forcing you to, which is irrational. (d) She reads 'trace' off the display. You might think you can just condition on 'trace', but I don't think that's right — the sensor is noisy, so what she has is uncertain evidence about C, not a certain fact about it. So we use the rule from (a), with the partition {C, ¬C}. Her new credences in the cells come from the sensor: P_new(C) = P(C | trace) = (0.3 × 0.3) / ((0.3 × 0.3) + (0.7 × 0.25)) = 0.09 / 0.265 ≈ 0.34, so P_new(¬C) ≈ 0.66. Then for any proposition A, P_new(A) ≈ 0.34 · P_old(A | C) + 0.66 · P_old(A | ¬C), and in particular her credence in C becomes about 0.34. (e) Yes. The rigidity condition isn't optional and it isn't something a rational agent can trade off against anything else — it falls out of the axioms, so an agent who updated any other way would be incoherent. So the rule in (a) is the unique rational update, and there's no room for two rational agents with the same priors and the same experience to end up with different credences.
