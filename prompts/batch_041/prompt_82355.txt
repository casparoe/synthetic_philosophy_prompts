I'm applying for one of those small internal research grants - six months, one paper out of it, and the proposal itself has to be 3-4 pages and in by Friday. I've got a half-formed idea and I want you to turn it into the actual document.

Buridan's ass, the donkey between two identical bales. (Which, note, isn't actually in Buridan's surviving works, and the puzzle is older than him anyway; the version I care about is the one where the will is said to be unable to differentiate between two similar objects, with one medieval figure holding that a special power of the will can do it and another holding that it can't.) What bugs me is that this gets discussed as one puzzle when it's really three, and discussions slide between them without noticing: (a) can an agent act at all when nothing is preferred; (b) if he can, can the act be rational; (c) is there any requirement to act - does the agent owe it to himself or anyone else to break the tie rather than stand there. Positions that answer one of these get read as answers to the others.

My hunch, which I want the proposal to test rather than assume: the paradox only bites if you assume a choice has to be backed by a preference between the options. Add a second kind of tie-breaker - an indifferent selection rule, of the kind that says take the leftmost, or the one you encountered first, or flip a coin - and the donkey walks. So the real question isn't whether choice without preference is possible, it's what makes an indifferent selection rule rational, and whether the answer is the same in three cases that get run together: the options are equal because the agent can't tell them apart, equal as a matter of fact, or not comparable at all, so that there's no fact of the matter about which is better. I also want to know whether thin selection rules - ones insensitive to the merits of the options - are a rational primitive or something we should be able to justify. There's an obvious analogy with choice functions in set theory and I dont know yet whether it's a useful one or just a pun.
