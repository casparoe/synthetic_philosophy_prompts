I am presenting a paper at our mind-and-cognition reading group next week, and what I need from you is a question-period preparation sheet, not a defence of the paper itself.

The paper's thesis is that the frame problem has been mislocated in the literature. It is not the difficulty of enumerating, for a formal system, all the propositions that a given action leaves unchanged — that is a technical puzzle about non-monotonic inference, and the classical devices (circumscription, default logic, the various non-monotonic extensions of the situation calculus) were answers to it. The real problem is that a finite agent cannot know in advance which of its beliefs bear on the task in front of it, and every one of those devices presupposes that relevance has already been fixed by the representation, so each relocates the difficulty rather than removing it. Where the problem is genuinely solved, it is solved by architecture rather than by logic: a situated agent's perception–action loop exposes it only to a small, task-shaped slice of the world, and the "frame" is an artefact of that coupling rather than of its logic. The familiar philosophical reading — that the frame problem shows common sense to be computationally intractable, or that it is really the holism of belief in another guise — is a category mistake.

The audience: mostly philosophers of mind committed to embodied or predictive-processing accounts of cognition; one emeritus who spent a career on classical planning and STRIPS-style action representations; and one Bayesian who is convinced the probabilistic turn made the whole debate obsolete.

Give me the objections I will actually face, attributed to the member or faction most likely to raise each and phrased in that person's idiom, ordered by how much damage they would do. For each, tell me how serious it really is, what I should concede, what I should hold, and a two-minute reply. Then name the weakest point in my own position that I should prepare to defend rather than hope goes unnoticed, and the one question I am hoping nobody asks. Write in American English.
