I have been trying to rank outcomes by how bad they are, and I keep producing comparisons that cannot be assembled into an order. Here is the chain and my gut verdicts on it. One person enduring an hour of a splitting migraine is worse than two people each enduring an hour of a severe headache; that is worse than four people each enduring a moderate headache; that is worse than eight each enduring a mild one; and so on, each step doubling the number of sufferers while halving the severity. At every adjacent pair I judge the later outcome better than the earlier one. But I judge the first outcome — the single migraine — better than the outcome at the far end, an enormous number of people each with a discomfort so faint it would not register if they were distracted. Along a single dimension I have no doubt that 'better than' is transitive: if A is less painful than B and B less painful than C, then A is less painful than C, with no exceptions. I also hold, and this is where I feel the pull, that other things equal adding one more person with a mild discomfort always makes an outcome worse. My tentative explanation of the chain is that each step trades off how bad one person's suffering is against how many suffer it, and that once a trade-off like that is in play no single relation stays transitive, so I should stop expecting one. I build software for a living, so an intransitive comparator is familiar to me as a failure to sort; what I want to know is whether the failure is in the outcomes or in the relation I am applying to them. Please test my diagnosis. If a principle captures all my verdicts rather than explaining them away, state it precisely and show which case forces each clause; if some verdict has to go, say which and why, and be candid about what your principle cannot accommodate. Then tell me what it predicts for a case I have not put to myself: the same chain built out of pleasures rather than pains, each step doubling the number of people while halving how intense the pleasure is — do I end up preferring the far end there too, and does your principle say I should?
