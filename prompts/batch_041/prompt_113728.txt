I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I am at work on a long piece about the puzzle Wigner named: why the mathematics we develop for one purpose — counting sheep, solving cubics, taming infinity — keeps turning out to describe the physical world with a precision nothing in the mathematics itself appears to guarantee. At a colloquium dinner last month a physicist friend offered me what he takes to be the deflationary answer. Our mathematical faculties, he said, are like our eyes: the eye fits the light because it was shaped by selection to fit the light, and our number sense, our spatial intuition, our appetite for symmetries and invariants fit the world for exactly the same reason. Nothing here needs explaining, he says, and certainly nothing here is evidence for a mathematical realm subsisting independently of us. A philosopher of science I spoke with a week later gave me a different deflation, which may or may not be the same one in different clothes: the effectiveness is manufactured at the level of theories rather than brains, because we simply decline to call a description of fundamental physics fundamental unless it is compact and mathematical, and we shelve or forget the descriptions that are not. I find the first analogy seductive and suspect it is carrying more weight than it can bear, and I want the audit done carefully rather than impressionistically.

Lay the mapping out term by term before judging it: in the mathematics case, what plays the role of the organism, the heritable trait, the variation on which selection acts, the environment that does the selecting, and the inheritance that preserves the gains. I want each slot filled far enough that I can see whether anything real is in it, and I want to be told plainly where the mapping breaks. Then separate the breaks that matter for the conclusion my physicist friend wants — that the fit is no miracle and gives no comfort to mathematical realism — from the ones that are merely picturesque. I am particularly interested in whether the analogy, taken seriously, predicts a fit as broad as the one we actually observe or a fit as parochial as the eye's: eyes are famously narrow instruments, blind to most of what is there, and mathematics is not, and I would like to know what that asymmetry does to the deflation. I would also like the circularity confronted head-on — in the biological case the selecting environment is independent of the trait being selected, and it is not obvious that the world's mathematical structure is independent of the mathematics we use to frame it — and the second deflation weighed against the first, since if the fit is a selection effect on theories, the evolutionary story may be doing no work at all.

End with a verdict: does the conclusion survive the disanalogies, and if it does not, what analogy would carry it better — or is the honest answer that no analogy will, and the phenomenon has to be argued about on its own terms? I have no use for a reading list or for a survey of who has said what; I want the reconstruction and the audit, in your own words, with the weak joints marked.
