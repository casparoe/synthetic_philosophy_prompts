I am a high school junior working on a long paper for my civics class, and I have spent several weeks trying to work out what my county's health care arrangements assume about fairness, because the assumptions seem to pull in different directions. Here is what I have been able to establish. Households below a certain income receive coverage through a county program that charges no premium and asks nothing about a person's age, health, or habits. Everyone above that line buys private insurance, and the price of that insurance is permitted to depend on age and on whether the buyer smokes, but not on whether the buyer already has a medical condition; the companies must sell to anyone who applies, whatever their history. Two smaller features of the private plans are what I cannot get past. First, the plans offer a discount of several hundred dollars a year to employees who complete a health questionnaire and meet a walking goal, which the plan's brochure calls a reward for taking responsibility for one's health; the same brochure describes the surcharge on smokers as not a penalty at all, merely a reflection of the higher costs that smokers impose. Second, my civics teacher defends the whole arrangement with the maxim that people should bear the costs of their own choices but not the costs of their bad luck, even though the county program, the part that looks most generous, asks nothing about anyone's choices.

Please write the analysis I would need for this paper. Reconstruct as precisely as you can the theory of fairness, responsibility, and desert that this arrangement presupposes, and be explicit about which features of it strictly require that theory and which merely happen to fit it; for example, whether the tobacco surcharge could be defended on purely actuarial grounds, with no claim at all about responsibility, or whether the pricing by age could. Then say whether the package is internally consistent, paying particular attention to the fact that a discount for meeting a goal and a surcharge for a habit can leave two employees paying exactly the same amount, and to the two different vocabularies the brochure uses to describe them. Tell me which rival theories of fairness would license these same rules, and where the arrangement is philosophically underdetermined, so that several theories fit it equally well. Finally, say what would have to change if the county replaced its implicit theory with one that treats health costs as a shared risk for which no one is to blame, and separately what would have to change under a theory that lets insurers price whatever they can measure.
