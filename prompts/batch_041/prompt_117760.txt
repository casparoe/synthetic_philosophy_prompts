I'm trying to pin down when a comparison between one person's welfare and another's is a genuine question with an answer, and my verdicts on a handful of cases won't sit together. Two strangers with headaches, both in the room and both able to describe what they feel: I think 'hers is worse than his' can be simply true or false, even though nothing turns on it and no one has to choose. My pain now against my pain as a child, when I was a very different creature psychologically: still a genuine question. A stoic's pain against a dramatist's, where their words and their behaviour pull apart: genuine — the gap is in what we can know, not in what is there. A human's pain against a bat's: not genuine; not hard to determine, but not settled by anything either. A present human's welfare against that of a posthuman descendant whose mind has been rebuilt along different lines: not genuine, like the bat — and this one nags me, because I know you can get there from here in small steps.

What principle delivers all of that? I keep assuming it must be about sharing a scale or a unit of measurement, but that makes the posthuman comparable and the child case a mystery. Give me the account you actually think is right, not a survey: say exactly where it draws the line, tell me what it implies for a case I haven't raised — two members of a species whose pain system turns out to be organised nothing like ours, so that we can rank their pains among themselves but not against ours — and say which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon. And if we built a device that read a single number off any creature's brain, would that make the human/bat comparison genuine, or only make us behave as if it were?
