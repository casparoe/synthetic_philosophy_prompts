I'm about four chapters into a novel and I've hit a wall that isn't really a plot problem, it's a philosophy one, so bear with me.

There's a character in it who's born into a category that everyone treats as a fact of nature but which I — and I hope the reader — can see was drawn by people. I've actually got three of these in the book. One's a currency that only works as money because everyone keeps treating it as money, and which comes apart the moment they stop. One's a medical cut-off that got moved by a commitee in the nineties, so a load of people changed category overnight without changing bodies. And then the big one, the racial and sexual kinds that people actually fight about, where the category is made up but the consequences are about as real as consequences get. My character's whole arc is an attempt to leave her kind — to resign from it, stop playing along — and I need to know whether that's a coherent thing to attempt or whether I'm writing nonsense.

So what I want isn't a theory, it's the spec for one. What does any decent account of social construction have to deliver before I can trust it with my plot? Give me the requirements: the cases it has to get right (mine, and whatever else it needs), the distinctions it can't collapse (constructed vs. not real; constructed vs. arbitrary; the concept being made vs. the thing being made; constructed in a way that's up to us vs. constructed in a way that very much isn't; made all at once by a decision vs. made slowly by nobody in particular), and the findings it can't contradict (these kinds do real causal work; their members usually can't just opt out; a kind can survive everyone privately disbelieving in it so long as the institutions carry on). For each requirement, tell me the case or consideration that puts it on the list, and mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are just the hobby-horse of one camp. If two of the requirements can't be had together, say which pair and why — that's the bit I'll actually use. And tell me which single requirement does the most to rule theories out.

If you've room at the end, a couple of lines on how the usual suspects fare against the list (the collective-acceptance lot, the performativity lot, the ones who say the kind is real but its boundary is a political achievement) — not a full scorecard, I just want to know which of them survives my character trying to resign.

British spelling and idiom please — it's going to my editor in London and she'll have my guts if she spots another z.
