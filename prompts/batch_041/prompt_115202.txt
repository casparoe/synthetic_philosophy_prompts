I'm a retired field ecologist—spent forty years counting plants in quadrats and arguing about nutrient cycling—and since I retired I've been sitting in on philosophy and history seminars at the local university, mostly to keep from talking to the dog. A historian friend and an economist friend have been at each other for months about Adam Smith on empire and colonies, and they've finally agreed to try an adversarial collaboration, except neither of them can write the document without it turning into a scorecard. So I said I'd ask someone to draft it.

The two camps: one is what I've started calling the exit reading. On this view, Smith's attack on colonial monopoly is at bottom an attack on empire itself—he thinks colonies are a drain, a source of war and debt, an injustice, and that Britain should let them go or at least recognize that they'd be better off independent. The other is the incorporation reading. On this view, Smith's target is not empire as such but the mercantile system; he wanted colonies brought into a free-trade union with representation, so the empire could be reformed rather than dissolved. The exit people say the incorporation people are letting Smith off the hook by turning him into a proto-federalist; the incorporation people say the exit people are reading nineteenth-century anti-colonialism back into an eighteenth-century political economist. They keep arguing past each other about what Smith really meant, and I suspect alot of it is that they mean different things by empire.

Please draft the joint statement they could both sign. I want: a numbered list of claims both sides accept—about what Smith says, what he's trying to explain, what he's not saying, and what follows from his premises; then the precise disagreements, each phrased so that the side that holds it would accept the wording as fair; then the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side; and for each crux, a case, argument, or historical episode both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Some candidates I'd like handled: the Navigation Acts, the East India Company, the American colonies and the representation question, Ireland, and the difference between a colony that is governed despotically and one that is simply trading under monopoly. If you think part of the dispute is verbal—say, that they're using empire in two different senses—say which part and why. And please don't give me a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I'm not writing a paper, and I can't check them anyway. I just want the argument, laid out so two stubborn people could put their names to it.
