I am a high school student preparing a presentation for my school's philosophy club on the responsibility gap for autonomous systems. I would like a sestina that does not merely describe the gap but enacts it through its form. Please use the following six end-words: designer, operator, commander, algorithm, harm, and no one. The poem should concern a single hypothetical incident in which an autonomous system causes serious harm, and the rotating end-words should make responsibility seem to move from one party to another without ever settling. The poem should be accurate about the distinction between causal responsibility and moral responsibility: the designer may have caused the system to be the way it is without intending this harm; the operator may have deployed it without controlling the particular action; the commander may have authorized the mission without knowing the specific outcome; the algorithm may have selected the target without any understanding of what a target is; and the harm is real even if no one satisfies the ordinary conditions of moral responsibility. The envoi should gather all six end-words in a way that leaves the gap open rather than resolving it by blaming the machine or by pretending that some human must have been responsible. The poem should be understandable to someone who knows the basic debate but not to a specialist, and it should not include citations, reading lists, or quotations from any source.
