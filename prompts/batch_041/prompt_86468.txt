I have been trying to state my own views about partial belief, and I find that my verdicts on particular cases do not line up with any principle I can state. I would like you to tell me what principle, or small set of candidate principles, actually captures them, what it predicts for cases I have not considered, and which of my verdicts it forces me to abandon.

(1) A coin is handed to me by someone I trust, who tells me it is fair. I think it is reasonable to set my credence in heads at exactly one half, and unreasonable to set it anywhere else.

(2) A coin is handed to me with no information about its bias. I think the reasonable credal state is the whole interval from zero to one, and that picking one half merely because heads and tails are the two possibilities is not reasonable.

(3) A coin is handed to me and I am told only that it is biased, without being told in which direction or by how much. I think the reasonable credal state is still the whole interval from zero to one; I do not think I may narrow it to something like 0.4 to 0.6 merely because one half is excluded.

(4) Two coins, A and B. I know A is fair. I know nothing about B. My credence for A's next toss being heads is one half, and my credence for B's next toss being heads is the whole interval.

(5) I am now told only that A and B landed the same way on their most recent tosses. I think this should leave my credence for B's next toss alone, and I also think — and this is the verdict that surprises me — that it should widen my credence for A's next toss to the whole interval, even though A is known to be fair and the information about how the two coins landed seems to have nothing to do with A.

(6) Suppose instead I am told that a future version of me, who will have seen a long run of tosses of B, will have a sharp credence of 0.9 that B comes up heads next. Before seeing the run, I think my credence for B's next toss should still be the whole interval, not 0.9.

(7) Two urns. Urn I contains exactly fifty red and fifty black balls. Urn II contains a hundred balls, each red or black, in an unknown proportion. I would rather bet on drawing red from Urn I than from Urn II, and I think this preference is reasonable. I am unsure whether this is a fact about my credences or a fact about my attitudes toward the two urns.

(8) I am told that a coin either has bias 0.3 or bias 0.7, but not which. I think it is reasonable for my credence interval to be the whole interval from 0.3 to 0.7, even though the only two values consistent with what I have been told are 0.3 and 0.7. I do not think I am required to keep the set of live values non-convex.

My tentative principle is this: a credal state is permissible just in case it is the convex hull of the set of probability functions under which everything I know has probability one, and when I learn something I should move to the conditional credal state. This seems to give me (1) through (6) and (8) as I want them. It also gives me (5), since the conditional credal state for A given that A and B landed the same way is the whole interval. But (5) is the verdict I cannot make peace with: I keep thinking that a fact about B cannot change what it is reasonable for me to believe about a fair coin.

A simpler principle would be that my credence in a proposition should be the set of values that agree with the known chances of the setup. That principle gives me (1) exactly one half, and it would overturn (5), since the known chance of A is one half regardless of how the two coins landed. But it seems to leave (2), (3), and (6) with no determinate verdict at all, since there are no known chances to agree with.

I would like to know whether either principle, or some third one, is the right one. Please state each candidate precisely, say which of my cases motivates each part of it, tell me what it predicts for cases I have not listed — including at least one case where the two principles above disagree and one where they agree — and be honest about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate.
