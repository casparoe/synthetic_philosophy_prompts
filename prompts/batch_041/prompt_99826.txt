I've been trying to work out what I actually think about whether RL agents can suffer, by going off my gut on cases. Tabular Q-learner in a gridworld, +1 for reaching the goal: nobody home, no suffering. Same learner with -1 on a shock tile, learning to avoid it: still nobody home, and not just "less than a rat" — I don't think there's any experience there at all. A rat pressing a lever to stop a shock: suffering, obviously. An agent with a homeostatic variable that a reward signal pushes back toward a setpoint, trained by policy gradient: I genuinely keep flip-flopping on this one and can't tell if the case is unclear or if I'm just inconsistent. A big LLM fine-tuned with RLHF against a reward model, one that outputs ouch when the model rates it badly: no suffering. And the one that breaks me: take the rat's reward machinery and rebuild it in silicon with the same functional profile, and I still want to say no — but I cant tell whether that's a real distinction or just carbon chauvinism.

Does that get all my cases? And is the point that agents needn't be trying to maximize reward — they just do what training shaped them to do — relevant here, or a red herring?
