Consider the Thomson lamp: a lamp switched at times 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, …, hence on throughout [0, 1/2), off on [1/2, 3/4), on again on [3/4, 7/8), and so on, with the switch times accumulating at t = 1. Contrast it with a dimmer variant in which the lamp's brightness is halved at each of those same times, so that the brightness converges to zero and the state at t = 1 is settled. Give me your credence that there is a fact of the matter about the on/off lamp's state at t = 1 — that exactly one of on and off is true of it — and give it as a decomposition rather than a bare number: the probability that an infinite sequence of tasks can be completed in finite time at all; conditional on that, the probability that the pre-t = 1 history fixes the state rather than leaving it open; the probability that on and off exhaust the possibilities for a lamp that exists at t = 1; and any further term you think belongs, each with a number and one line of justification. Then identify the term the final figure is most sensitive to, say whether the dimmer variant should shift it and in which direction, and say what follows for the claim that supertasks are impossible if the answer turns out to be that there is no fact of the matter.
