You are grading the final exam for an advanced undergraduate seminar on value theory. The question and one student's answer are reproduced below. Grade the answer.

**Question (20 points)**

Throughout the course we have used the following stipulations. A value X is *lexically prior* to a value Y when any amount of X, however small, outweighs any amount of Y, however large; a lexical ranking admits of no finite exchange rate between the two. Two amounts are *comparable* when one is better than, worse than, or equal to the other. They are *commensurable* when a common unit measures both, so that the amounts can be added, subtracted, and traded off at a rate. Comparability does not entail commensurability.

Consider the view T-NU, given by three axioms:

(A1) Any two pains are comparable and commensurable with each other, and any two pleasures are comparable and commensurable with each other.
(A2) There is a level of pain, the *harm line*, such that any pain at or above the harm line is lexically prior to every pleasure, however great.
(A3) Any pain below the harm line is commensurable with pleasure, so that some finite amount of pleasure outweighs it.

(i) Is T-NU a comparabilist view about pain and pleasure, a commensurabilist view about them, both, or neither? Justify your answer by reference to the axioms.
(ii) Outcome O1 contains an above-the-line pain p together with a pleasure g. Outcome O2 contains the same pain p together with a slightly greater pleasure g+. Does T-NU rank O1 against O2? If it does, which is better, and if it does not, why not?
(iii) Does T-NU assign pleasure no value, in the sense that pleasure never affects the ranking of outcomes? Answer yes or no and defend your answer with a case.

**Student's answer**

(i) T-NU is a commensurabilist view. The harm line is a lexical threshold, and a lexical threshold is just a very steep exchange rate between pain and pleasure: above the line, no amount of pleasure buys off any pain, so the rate is, so to speak, infinite. Since the axioms rank pain against pleasure in every case, the two are measured on a single scale, and a single scale is exactly what commensurability requires. A1 supplies the scale within each category, and A2 and A3 carry it across categories. So the answer is both: T-NU is comparabilist because it always yields a ranking, and commensurabilist because that ranking is generated by one scale. More generally, any view that draws a threshold is thereby a lexical view, since the threshold is what does the lexically prior work.

(ii) O1 and O2 contain the same above-the-line pain, so the lexical component is a tie; the difference between them lies entirely in the pleasure, and g+ is greater than g. T-NU therefore ranks O2 above O1. This is worth stressing, because it shows that the lexical element does not swallow the lower tier whenever it appears.

(iii) No. Pleasure plainly affects the ranking, as (ii) shows. What is true is that whenever an above-the-line pain is present, the lower tier drops out of the calculation: the outcome is ranked by the above-the-line pain alone, and the pleasure together with any below-the-line pain makes no difference. T-NU is in that respect extensionally equivalent to a simple negative utilitarianism on which suffering is the only intrinsic disvalue; the harm line simply fixes where suffering becomes lexically dominant.
