For three centuries the dispute over Berkeley's master argument has gone nowhere: realists and idealists agree on most of the surrounding metaphysics and disagree only about whether the move from the inconceivability of an unconceived sensible thing to the identity of existence and perception has any force. I want a diagnosis of the impasse, not another verdict on the argument. Is it a substantive disagreement about whether conceivability is a reliable guide to possibility; a verbal dispute about what conceiving is — one side hearing form an image of, the other entertaining the proposition that, so that the premise is trivially true on one reading and plainly false on the other; or a disagreement about where the burden of proof sits, so that each side's case looks question-begging only from the other's starting point? Consider in particular whether the key premise is doing descriptive work about our psychology or definitional work about what a sensible thing is to mean, and whether that ambiguity is what drives the stalemate. State what each side is actually committed to, give your best-supported diagnosis, and say what would count as progress — a distinction, a change of question, or nothing at all.
