Ok so my reading group has been going in circles for like three months on Goodhart's law and I want the doc we should have written instead of arguing. Two camps, and I want the joint statement both could actually sign.

Camp 1, the structuralists: Goodharting isn't a design flaw, it's what happens whenever you optimize or select on anything that isn't literally the thing you care about. Any fix just relocates the failure. Their core picture, as I understand it, is that a metric is only ever certified for the actions the designer happened to think of, and the optimizer's entire job is to find actions outside that set — I've been calling this the imagination bound and I think it might be the real crux, but I could be wrong. Structuralists also tend to think the deep version of the phenomenon is selection (evolution, markets, competitive pressure) and that the single-agent-with-a-reward-signal version is just the selection version with extra steps.

Camp 2, the engineers: all the famous cases are lazy design. Model what the metric is actually a causal function of, keep several partly-independent proxies instead of one, constrain the action space, and re-derive the goal from scratch whenever the metric moves, and you can optimize hard for years without the goal degrading. On their view Goodharting is contingent, not structural.

I lean structuralist but the engineers have a real point about the action space, so I dont want either side strawmanned.

What I want out of it:

- A numbered list of claims both sides accept: about what Goodhart's law is even a claim about, what counts as a clean instance of it, and how to seperate a real Goodharting case from just plain optimizing the wrong thing. They have to deal head-on with the worry that on some readings the "law" is a tautology — you optimized a proxy, so of course you got the proxy — and say whether that worry kills the interesting version or not.
- The exact remaining disagreements, each side's view written in wording that side would put its own name to. And for each camp, the strongest thing that camp itself concedes counts against it, not the thing the other camp says about it.
- The cruxes: sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. Split them into genuine disagreements and ones that turn out to be about words. My guess is at least one crux is really about whether "avoiding Goodharting" means zero degradation forever or bounded, monitored, recoverable degradation — if that's what's going on, say so and say whether the rest of the dispute survives once that's nailed down.
- For each genuine crux, a test both sides agree bears on it, plus both sides' predictions written down in advance. The tests have to be things you can settle by reasoning — toy models, formal arguments, consequences of a stated setup, thought experiments. We're offline, so nothing that requires looking up studies, and no citations.

One extra thing I want them to chew on, and I genuinely dont know which camp it helps. The oracle scenario: the metric isn't a proxy at all, it's a perfect instantaneous measurement of the goal itself, no error, no lag, and the agent can't touch the measurement except by moving the goal. Does Goodharting still happen? If yes, the structuralists win and the proxy/measurement framing was a red herring — the thing is about search. If no, the engineers win and it was a measurement problem all along. Make them both state a prediction on this before the fact.

Format: agreed claims as a numbered list, then a table with exactly these columns — Crux | Structuralist wording | Engineer wording | Underlying commitment that makes them differ | Test both accept | Structuralist prediction | Engineer prediction | Genuine or verbal. After the table, a JSON object with exactly these fields and nothing else:

- "genuine_cruxes": array of strings, names of the cruxes that are real disagreements
- "verbal_cruxes": array of strings, the ones that are about wording
- "most_decisive_test": string, the single test that would move the most
- "structuralist_concession": string, strongest thing camp 1 admits tells against them
- "engineer_concession": string, same for camp 2
- "oracle_winner": string, one of "structuralists", "engineers", "undecided"
- "oracle_why": string, one or two sentences
- "my_bet": string, which camp is closer and your confidence as a number from 0 to 1
- "residual_dispute_verbal": boolean, whether what's left after the cruxes are sorted is mostly a word fight

Make the whole thing something both of them could put their names to, and if one side's position can't be stated in a way the other would accept as fair, say that instead of forcing it.
