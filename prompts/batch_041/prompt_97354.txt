I work as a backend engineer and have no formal background in philosophy, but for a hackathon last quarter my team prototyped an internal service that a government customer could have used to decide when an emergency exception to a general prohibition on torture applies. The prototype is shelved, but I keep thinking about how much of the underlying argument we silently baked into the configuration, and I want to write a parody of our own developer documentation for it.

Please write that piece. Use the form of a quickstart and API reference page: a one-paragraph overview, five or six endpoints with parameters and example calls, a "common errors" section, a rate-limit or pricing table, a short FAQ, and a deprecation notice. Keep it deadpan throughout, roughly 700–900 words, and do not signal that it is a joke. The comedy should come from the real structure of the ticking-bomb argument as it would actually get implemented, not from anything invented for laughs.

I would like the following points to be enacted in the details of the documentation rather than stated anywhere in it: that the hypothetical is usually rigged so the interrogator is certain the detainee has the information, certain the information is the right information, certain the threat is imminent and large, and certain that torture will extract it in time; that the whole justification is engineered for one case but shipped to institutions that must apply it repeatedly; that someone has to sign, and the piece should be honest about how little the signature accomplishes; and that once an exception exists, the interesting question is what makes it stay exceptional. Let a required parameter, a default value, a validation rule, an example response, or a line in the changelog carry each of these. Include a version of the certainty requirement that no caller can actually satisfy, and a bug or deprecation notice that quietly shows the exception becoming the norm. Someone who has never read the literature should still follow the page as a piece of software documentation; someone who has should recognize exactly which moves are being satirized.

Close with a brief note, clearly separated from the parody, on which contested readings of this debate the piece leans on and where it takes liberties with them. In that note, state how confident you are in the accuracy of what you claim there, and single out the one point you are least sure of.
