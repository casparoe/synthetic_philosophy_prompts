I'm making a handout for a philosophy of medicine seminar on whether 'disease' is a descriptive or an evaluative notion, and I want a decision tree that takes someone from their verdicts on particular cases to a named position. Build the branch nodes out of the questions that actually do the splitting, not out of labels: whether a value judgement is built into the analysis or is only a typical consequence, what kind of function a disease has to be a failure of (statistical species-typicality, evolutionary selected effect, or just whatever the organ is there to do), whether the bearer of health is the body's parts or the whole persons ability to get on with their life, whether the job is to analyse the ordinary concept or to engineer one that clinics and insurers can use, and whether there's one concept or a plural cluster. Leaves should include plain naturalism, a harmful-dysfunction hybrid, straight normativism, a whole-person/vital-goals view and pluralism, with empty cells marked and one line on why nobody's there. Use these as the test items so it cant be answered off the labels: a rare but harmless anatomical variant, a metabolic quirk that's only a problem on certain diets, short sight, adult lactose intolerance in a dairy-eating country, ordinary ageing that people want treated anyway, and deafness. Keep it to about eight nodes, and flag any two leaves the tree still can't separate.
