So I read this post on a finance blog I follow about the "no-trade theorems," and before I go around repeating it I want to know how much of it I should actually believe. Here's basically what the guy wrote:

The no-trade theorems are results in the economics of information showing that rational, self-interested traders can never have any reason to trade with each other on the basis of private information. The crucial assumption is rationality: if two people are both rational and both know the price, then any offer one of them makes must rest on something the other doesn't know, so the other treats the offer itself as evidence, updates on it, and before long the two agree -- and a trade you already agree on isn't worth doing. In other words it's just Aumann's result that rational agents can't agree to disagree, dressed up in market language. The moral is that real markets only trade because of irrationality or noise traders, and since we see enormous trading volumes every day, the theorems are either empirically false or an irrelevant curiosity.
