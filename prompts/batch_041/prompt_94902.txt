We keep talking past each other and I'd like to stop, so please write the joint statement the two of us could actually both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Here's what it has to deal with, because this is where we keep sliding around each other: (1) whether we mean cluelessness about the causal upshots of an act or about the evaluative upshots — which goods count, how much the far future weighs against the present; (2) whether the relevant unit is the single act or a policy, practice, or rule of acting, since I suspect a lot of our disagreement is about that; (3) whether this is a problem for consequentialism specifically or for any theory that has anything to say about acting under uncertainty, in which case it's not much of an objection; (4) whether the astronomical-stakes argument is a rescue or actually makes things worse, since it looks like it either licenses ignoring everything small or slides into fanaticism; and (5) whether the thing we can't do is rank our options, or know the sign of the difference we make, or know the magnitude of it — those feel like three different problems to me and I don't think we've ever seperated them cleanly.
