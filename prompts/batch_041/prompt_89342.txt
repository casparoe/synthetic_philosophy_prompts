I retired a few years ago from a career in the earth sciences, and I have been spending much of my new leisure reading moral and political philosophy — largely to catch up on everything I skipped while I was in the field. I have lately been working through the now-familiar thesis that human violence has declined over the long run, and I keep running into what strikes me as a fatal bookkeeping problem, though I have not seen it put this way. The evidence always arrives as a rate: so many violent deaths per hundred thousand persons per year, or per million, or per some such denominator. But a population that grows over a few centuries will swamp any decline in a per-person rate; the same rate applied to a larger population yields a larger count. A death is a death, it seems to me, and if the absolute toll of killing has risen while the per-capita rate has fallen, then the decline is an artifact of how the fraction happens to be written, not a fact about the world. A demographer friend of mine tells me I should instead be looking at violent deaths as a share of all deaths, which she says behaves differently again, and that this third quantity is the one that actually matters — though she could not say why without appealing to what she called the relevant reference class, a phrase I do not understand.

So my question is this. Once we set aside these presentational choices and look at what is physically real — the number of human beings killed by other human beings — does the thesis that violence is declining survive at all, or does it collapse into a definitional trick? And if it survives, which of these quantities is the one that genuinely tracks the thing we care about when we worry about violence: the per-capita rate, the absolute count, or the share of all deaths? I am a physical scientist by training and I want to know which of these is the true measure of the phenomenon, the way one would want to know whether to report a concentration as a mole fraction or a mass fraction before deciding whether a trend is real. I would be grateful if you could tell me which number I should trust, and equally grateful if you could tell me why the question, as I have posed it, may not have the kind of answer I am expecting.
