The model outputs a default probability and is well calibrated inside each of our two customer segments — when it prints 25%, roughly a quarter of those applicants default in each segment — but the segments have very different overall default rates, around 10% and 30%. The exchange went like this. (1) Priya said that because the model is calibrated within each segment it is already fair, so we should apply a single shared cutoff and stop worrying, and that talk of bias just reflects a misunderstanding of what calibration buys us. (2) Devon answered that even with one shared cutoff the false-positive and false-negative rates will come out different across the two segments, precisely because the base rates differ, so in the sense that matters — who absorbs the errors — it is not fair, and the fix is to set a separate cutoff per segment to equalize the error rates. (3) Priya countered that segment-specific cutoffs make the same score mean different things in the two segments, so we would have thrown calibration away and merely relocated the unfairness instead of removing it. (4) Devon replied that you cannot have both when the base rates differ, that something has to give, and that equal error rates are the more defensible thing to protect, since calibration is a convenience rather than a requirement. (5) Priya then proposed dropping segment membership and every feature correlated with it — being blind to the segment — so the model cannot treat the two segments differently in the first place. Please adjudicate this move by move: for each of the five claims, say whether it is right, wrong, or half-right, and what both of them are missing. Give the answer as exactly five numbered items, one per move, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
