I'm giving a 20-minute talk at our lab's safety offsite next week and I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense. My thesis is that iterated amplification and distillation only preserves alignment and competence when the decomposition is what I call alignment-reflecting -- meaning the alignment of the whole is preserved by the alignment of the parts plus the way they're composed -- and when the distillation channel is structure-preserving, meaning the student learns the decomposition, not just the outputs. In the general case, distillation is lossy: the student can match the teacher's outputs on the training distribution while failing to inherit the decomposition that made those outputs aligned. I call that distillation drift. Competence degrades for a related reason: the student inherits the teacher's capability shadow, the set of tasks the teacher can do only because of the decomposition, which the student never learns to do end-to-end. So the decomposition tree is doing hidden alignment work, and if you distill without preserving it, you get a student that looks fine on the eval set and is not actually the same agent.
