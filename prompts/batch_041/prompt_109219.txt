I'm putting together a position paper for a graduate seminar on dreaming, and before I commit to the view I've assembled I want a consistency audit of it. I've been working through the Malcolm–Dennett exchange on whether dreams are experiences, and instead of landing squarely with either of them I've ended up with a hybrid that I suspect may not be stable. Here is what I hold. (1) Dreams are experiences: when I dream, something happens to me during sleep, and my dream report is about that occurrence rather than merely being a story I am disposed to tell on waking. (2) There is no fact of the matter about how long a dream took in real time; a dream I remember as an hour-long adventure is not thereby a report of an hour of anything, and the question of its actual elapsed duration has no determinate answer. (3) Dreaming is essentially retrospective: the only route to knowing that I dreamt is memory, no independent check is available from inside sleep, and so the thought that I might be dreaming at this very moment cannot be made into a coherent doubt. (4) To dream is not to observe anything, even from the inside. (5) I am not a verificationist; I do not think a claim is meaningless merely because it cannot be independently checked. (6) Dreams genuinely surprise me, teach me things about myself, and are sometimes the occasion of real discovery.

I would like you to tell me which of these pull against each other, which only appear to, and how the package should be repaired. Please order the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as a flat list, and for each one say what specifically has to give: which commitment is doing the damage, what a revision of it would cost me elsewhere, and how costly that cost really is. Some of these may conflict only because I have stated them carelessly, so I would also like to know which of the apparent conflicts can be dissolved by restating the commitment more carefully, and what the restatement is. I would rather have a definite verdict than a survey: name the single revision that cleans up the most trouble, say what it would force me to give up, and defend the choice even if the honest answer is that my position is more fragile than I had assumed. Keep the response under 900 words.
