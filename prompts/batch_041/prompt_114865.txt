My best friend and I just had our third two-hour fight this year about vagueness, and I'm starting to wonder whether we're even disagreeing about the same thing. Quick background, since it matters. She's a linguist by training and she says vague words like 'tall,' 'bald,' 'heap' have no sharp cutoff: for the borderline cases there is simply no fact of the matter. When we say a guy with 4,999 hairs is bald, what we mean, on her view, is that it comes out true on every admissible way of making 'bald' precise, false on every such way for clear cases, and for the in-between ones it's neither -- the sentence just isn't true or false, though 'he's bald or he isn't' still counts as true, because every sharpening makes one of the two halves true. She says that's why the sorites argument doesn't get off the ground: the step premise is never true on all the sharpenings, even though every single step looks fine.

I think that's a magic trick. I'm the one who does metaphysics, and on my view there is a fact of the matter: there's some specific number of hairs where 'bald' stops applying, and the sorites premise is just flat-out false, even though no one can ever know where the line is. My explanation of the ignorance isn't "the world is fuzzy," it's more like a margin-for-error thing: if I know I'm bald, then a guy with a hair or two more than me has to be bald too, and near the cutoff that fails, so knowledge runs out before the fact does. Sharp fact, no possible knowledge.

We both know the standard complaints well enough. She says I'm stuck with spooky unknowable boundaries and, worse, a sharp boundary between the boundary and the non-boundary, all the way up. I say her 'true' isn't truth, since she has to call 'this patch is red or it isn't red' true while denying either half, and that her sharpenings themselves come in degrees, so she can't tell me what makes one admissible. We even tried the "maybe 'bald' is just context-sensitive" move and it got us nowhere.

So what I want isn't a verdict on who's right. I want a diagnosis of what kind of fight this is. Are we having a verbal dispute about the word 'true,' and about whether "there's a fact of the matter" is a claim about the world or about how you set up a semantics? Are we answering two different questions under one name, one of us about language and one about reality? Is there a hidden formal or empirical question doing the work -- say, whether an unknowable truth is even intelligible, or whether indeterminate truth is a real phenomenon rather than an artifact of a formal gadget? My hypothesis, and feel free to shoot it down, is that we're each just refusing to pay a different theoretical cost, and that no argument can settle that. Tell me why that's wrong if it is, and tell me concretely what would count as progress here -- a distinction, a theorem, a change of question -- or whether the honest answer is that nothing should move this, and why.
