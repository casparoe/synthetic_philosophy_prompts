I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so bear with me. My new book has a lawyer in it who mostly does animal cruelty cases, and the more research I do the less the law makes sense to me, and I'm hoping you can help me figure out what it actually believes.

Here's the thing. In my character's state, and from what I can tell alot of places are like this, it's a serious crime to hurt a dog or a cat. Set a dog on fire, you go to jail. But a farmer can cut the tail off a piglet, or castrate a calf, with no painkillers at all, and that's just Tuesday. The statutes apparently have a carve-out for accepted farming practice or customary husbandry or something like that, so the same amount of pain, caused the same way, is a crime when my villain does it for kicks and perfectly legal when it's done for profit. Meanwhile the state will raid a dogfight and seize the dogs, sometimes put them down, not because of how they were treated that day but just for being fighting dogs.

So what is this whole arrangement secretly assuming? Some possibilities I can see: that pain only counts as cruelty when it's pointless; or that an animal's suffering weighs less when a human has a use for it; or that property and tradition beat welfare; or that the real rule is whoever owns the animal gets to decide what counts as necessary. Which of these does the law actually need in order to work, and which are just things it happens to be compatible with? Is the farming exemption doing the same job as the dogfighting ban, or are those two bits pulling in opposite directions? And if I have my character argue in closing that pain is pain whoever it's in and whatever the reason for it, that an animal can't be made to pay for a benefit it never gets and never agreed to, what would actually have to change if a court bought that? Not just the statutes. Would farms have to shut, or could they just buy lidocaine? What happens to the whole idea that there's such a thing as a humane killing? And does any of it survive if you keep the part where we still get to use animals, just without the pain?

No reading list or citations please, I'm definately not writing a dissertation, I just need the character's worldview to be coherent and maybe a good monologue. Explain it to me like I'm a smart person who never took a philosophy class.
