Bit of an odd one, hope this is the right place to ask. I'm doing an MA and sitting in on a course on probability and rational choice, and something came up in a seminar that I can't stop chewing on, and I don't know if it even has a name. Here's the situation. I'm uncertain which of four places I'm in — call them Ash, Birch, Cedar, Dogwood — and I start off thinking each is equally likely. Ash and Birch are both in the northern province; Cedar and Dogwood are both in the south. Then I get a message that isn't of the form 'you're in Ash' or 'you're not in Cedar'. It's a conditional: 'if you're in the north, then you're in Ash.' And crucially I do NOT find out whether I'm in the north. I've just learned a conditional — not its antecedent, not its consequent.

And the maddening part is I can't tell what my new credences should be, because two principles both look obviously right and they disagree. Principle one: the message rules out Birch, since if I'm in the north I'm in Ash, the only northern spot it leaves open is Ash, so Birch is out. And because Birch was half of the north, the north as a whole is now a worse bet than before, so my credence in 'north' should drop — from a half down to a third, if I just rule Birch out and renormalize. Principle two: the message is about what follows *if* I'm in the north, it doesn't itself say anything about whether I'm in the north at all — so why should my credence in 'north' move? On that reading I keep 'north' at a half and just shovel all of the north's weight from Birch onto Ash. And its not just a toy case: I've had the same stuck feeling updating on 'if the test is positive then you have it', and in a legal-ish case where I learn that if the witness is telling the truth then the defendant was there, without learning either part.

So: is there a name for this — the general problem of how to update your credences when what you learn is a conditional (or something conditional-ish) rather than a plain statement of fact? I suspect it's a known thing with a label I can't quite put my finger on. But honestly I don't just want the label. What I want is the crux: what are the competing formal recipes, what principle does each one encode, and is choosing between them a matter of principle or just taste? Where exactly does the ordinary probability machinery go silent here, and is that silence itself a result — i.e. there's genuinely no unique rational answer — or is everyone just missing a principle?

Format please: first a section headed 'The phenomenon' where you restate what I've described in your own words and mark its edges — what is and isn't this kind of case (contrast it with a case where the news is a plain fact). Then 'The nearest names' — the established term or terms if any, and how each candidate concept differs from my thing. Then 'The crux' on the rival rules and the principle behind each. Then a short 'Verdict', a paragraph or two, on whether the underdetermination is real or only apparent. Maybe 700-900 words total.
