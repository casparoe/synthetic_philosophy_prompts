Okay, so I keep bumping into this move and I want the best version of the other side of it. There's this syndrome, pain asymbolia: after certain kinds of brain damage, people report that they feel pain, they can point to where it is, they'll even say "yes, that hurts", but it doesn't bother them at all — no wincing, no pulling away, they'll laugh about it. A friend of mine says this is the cleanest proof that the badness of pain is a seperate ingredient from the pain sensation itself. The sensation is one thing; the "ugh, get it off me" is another; here the second is gone while the first is intact. So a bare pain sensation, in itself, isn't bad, and badness gets added on by some further state.

I think that's wrong and I want to argue the other way, hard. Take the position that asymbolia doesn't show badness is a detachable component of pain, and that we should resist the picture where a neutral sensation gets "painted" with badness by an extra feeling. While your at it, my friend also says asymbolia is to pain what blindsight is to vision — the patient can register the thing without the experience being like anything on the relevant dimension. Say whether that analogy is pulling any weight or just sounds good.
