Ok so weird request, but hear me out. I'm a data scientist and about three weeks ago I got voluntold onto my company's "responsible AI" review committee. Which mostly means I sit in a room twice a month while someone from legal asks "but is it fair?" and then everyone turns and looks at me. I've been reading about the impossibility results in algorithmic fairness at night, on my own time, like a chump, and I keep having the same reaction: we are selling a product that mathematically cannot exist.

So I want you to write the thing I can't stop imagining. Write me a mock product page / FAQ for a fairness-compliance software suite — the kind of thing a vendor sells to companies like mine, with a per-seat enterprise tier and a little shield logo and a "trusted by" strip. Call it whatever you want, invent the vendor. Deadpan the whole way through, in the voice of a marketing team that is genuinely proud of itself. No winking, no "of course, this is impossible!" nudges, no narrator popping up to tell me the joke. If it's funny, it should be funny because the fine print is true.

The jokes have to come from the actual math, so that someone who's read the impossibility stuff laughs instead of wincing:

- The tool should have a dropdown or a "fairness objective" selector where the options are mutually exclusive parity criteria, presented as a matter of taste, like picking a ringtone.
- Somewhere it should quietly admit that the bundle (calibration plus equal error rates across groups) is only available when the groups have identical base rates, or when the model predicts perfectly, or when it predicts nothing at all — and then charge extra for that configuration.
- A footnote that the certificate is valid only until the data distribution shifts, with a renewal date.
- An honest little sentence that dropping the protected attribute doesn't help because the proxies stay.
- The tension between "we treat everyone the same" and "we get the same outcomes" should be handled as a settings toggle rather than a conflict.
- Maybe a tier that protects every intersection of groups, and a note that this makes it harder to satisfy anything else, phrased like a feature.
- At least one place where the page implies the tool is fixing the world's unequal base rates, when it obviously can't.

Readable to someone who's never heard any of this, funny to someone who has. Don't name real papers or people or companies, and don't give me a bibliography or a reading list — I'm reading enough at night as it is, and half the time I can't remember which result is whose anyway. Aim for maybe 600 words, FAQ format with a short pricing block at the end.

Two things on tone and honesty. First, if I've got this garbled — if the tradeoffs don't actually run the way I think, or the whole joke only works because I've misunderstood which criteria conflict — say so up front and don't just write the piece anyway. I'd much rather get a flat "you've got that backwards" than a funny page built on my mistake. Second, where you do take a liberty with the ideas, or lean on a reading that people who work on this would argue about, flag it at the end in a couple of lines. Keep the math and lose the joke if it comes to that.
