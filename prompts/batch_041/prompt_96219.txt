So my flatmate and I got into a two-hour fight last night about Rousseau, of all people. She'd been watching one of those shows where the entire gimmick is that nothing is scripted and everyone is supposedly just being themselves, and she said Rousseau would've hated it even more than he hated the theater. I said no — his whole objection was to *acting*, to the profession of putting on a character that isn't yours and making strangers feel feelings on your behalf, which is why he spends all that time on the old rules that made actors civilly infamous and kept them out of Christian burial (and honestly I can't tell whether he's endorsing that or just citing it as evidence that everyone already senses something's off about the job). Take away the script and the professional actors, I said, and you've basically landed on the thing he *did* like: the public festival with no stage, where the spectators are each other's spectacle. She thinks thats naive, because a camera pointed at a festival turns everyone in it into a performer anyway, and because the audience at home is doing exactly the passive, vicarious thing he complained about, just without leaving the house. And I'll admit I don't actually know how much of his arguement was about the *fiction* and how much was about the *structure* — the paying audience, the hard separation between the people on the platform and the people watching, the fact that the whole enterprise is built to please rather than to make anyone better. He also said, I'm pretty sure, that he wasn't out to shut down the theaters in a big city like Paris; the case was specifically about a small city whose mores were still relatively intact, where there was more to lose. Which makes me think scale and audience matter as much as counterfeiting does.

Here's what I want. Pretend you're Rousseau, but don't do a bit — give me an actual prediction of what positions he'd take, argued in his own terms, on three cases that didn't exist in his lifetime. (1) An unscripted reality/streaming show whose entire selling point is that the participants are being authentic and unperformed, watched by millions who will never meet them. (2) A small town's amateur public festival — no professionals, no ticketed stage, everyone participates — that happens to get livestreamed to a large remote audience. (3) The same town's festival with the cameras switched off. For each, say whether he'd approve, tolerate, or oppose, and which strand of his actual argument is doing the work: the counterfeiting/persona point, the passivity-and-vicarious-feeling point, the point that amusements are fine in moderation but this one isn't moderate, the corrupting-the-mores point, or the small-city-versus-big-city conditionality. Where two strands pull in opposite directions on a case, say so instead of pretending they harmonize, and flag anywhere you're extrapolating well past anything he actually wrote — I'd much rather have a labeled guess than confident ventriloquism. Tell me how confident you are overall and name the single claim you're least sure of, then end with a line that is exactly \answer{...} with a short phrase giving your verdict on case (1) only.
