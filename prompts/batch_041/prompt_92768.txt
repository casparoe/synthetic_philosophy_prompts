Hey, so we just finished the unit in my history class on the Eichmann trial and Arendt's idea that evil can be banal — that Eichmann wasn't a monster, he was just a guy who stopped thinking and did what the system told him. Our teacher made us debate whether that idea still holds up, and I want to take it somewhere weirder than my classmates did. In Australia a while back there was this scheme called Robodebt: the government ran a computer program that automatically flagged welfare recievers as owing money by averaging out their reported income, it sent out hundreds of thousands of debt notices, a lot of them were just wrong, some people got hounded really badly over debts they didn't even have, and the whole thing ended up being ruled unlawful. The weird part is nobody in the chain seemed to feel responsible, because each person just ran their own little piece of the machine, and the people who built it weren't sadists, they were just doing their job. So if Arendt were alive and watched that, would she just say it's the banality of evil all over again, or is something actually different going on when the thoughtlessness is baked into an algorithm instead of a person? And honestly — who's the Eichmann in that story, is there even one? I don't want a summary of her book, I want your real guess at what she'd say.
