For a science-and-ideas column, I want a piece of sustained, deadpan satire on the units-of-selection debate — genes versus individuals versus groups. Write it as a formal press release, roughly 500–600 words, from an invented regulatory body (call it the Committee on Selective Accounting) announcing that it has finally resolved the dispute by ruling that gene-level and group-level accounts are "materially equivalent" reporting conventions for the same underlying ledger, and that researchers must henceforth file every result in both currencies. The comedy should come from getting the bookkeeping right: replicators versus vehicles, inclusive fitness, contextual analysis, the way a group-selection story can be rewritten as a gene-selection story and back again, the major transitions in which the unit itself is supposed to change, and the difference between an equivalence that is merely notational and one that is explanatory. Include a straight-faced quote from an invented spokesperson, and end on a line that quietly exposes what the ruling dodges — namely which description, if either, is doing the causal work. Keep it legible to a reader who has never heard of the debate, never let the piece wink, and use no real names, works, dates, or citations.
