I keep thinking about this thing I heard called "moral trade." The way I understand it, two people who disagree about morality can still make a swap that leaves them both better off by their own lights: I do something you care about (that I think is at worst harmless), and in exchange you do something I care about (that you think is at worst harmless). Neither of us has to do anything we actually think is wrong, but we each end up with more of what we value. My brother in law hunts and I'm pretty much vegan, and we've been trying to work out whether there's some swap available between us, which is what got me going on this.

But I feel like the idea has implications alot bigger than my kitchen table. What are the most important implications of moral trade -- for how I should argue (or not argue) with people I disagree with, for politics, for which charities are worth giving to, and does it imply anything weird or a bit unsettling that people don't usually notice? I don't want the obvious stuff about compromise being nice, I want the parts that actually follow from the idea and change how you'd act.
