I have no academic background in philosophy, but I have spent a lot of time thinking about two puzzles that seem to run in opposite directions, and I want to see whether my own vocabulary can carry them or whether it breaks.

The first puzzle: a fair lottery has, say, a million tickets. For any one ticket, it is overwhelmingly likely to lose, so it seems reasonable to believe that ticket loses; but you also know that some ticket wins, so you cannot believe of every ticket that it loses. The second puzzle: someone writes a long book, checking each factual claim as carefully as possible. Each individual claim is probably true, so she seems entitled to believe each one; but she also knows that in a book that long at least one claim is probably false, so she cannot believe the whole conjunction. I keep feeling that both puzzles are about the same tension between local confidence and global consistency, but I cannot tell whether the tension is real or an artifact of how I am talking.

I want you to restate both puzzles in the vocabulary I actually use, which is the vocabulary of no-limit Texas hold'em, not academic epistemology. I am a serious amateur player, comfortable with ranges, combinations, equity, blockers, pot odds, bet sizing, variance, and the difference between a process-oriented and a results-oriented mindset. More specifically, I use a private method I call the range ledger. For every opponent, I list the possible hands and assign each a weight. When a hand's weight falls below a threshold I have set for the situation, I treat it as absent from the range and act as if the opponent cannot hold it; the remaining hands are the live range. I also keep a session ledger of reads: after each significant hand I record what I thought the opponent had and how confident I was, and I know from experience that some of those reads must be wrong, even though each one felt justified at the time.

Restate the first puzzle using the range ledger, and restate the second using the session ledger. Do this as faithfully as you can, not as a loose analogy. For each translation, tell me exactly which parts of the puzzle survive: what corresponds to the high probability of an individual ticket or claim, what corresponds to the knowledge that some ticket wins or that some claim is false, and what corresponds to the inconsistency between believing each and believing all. Then give me a residue report. Which parts translate cleanly, which parts require me to distort the poker vocabulary, and which parts have no counterpart in it at all? For the parts that do not translate, say whether the gap is a defect of my vocabulary, something a poker player cannot think, or a sign that the original puzzle was less clear than it looked. I care more about the residue than about the translations. I am not asking for a reading list or for names of papers; I want the translation and the residue report.

End with a final line exactly of the form \answer{X}, where X is one of: defect-in-poker, defect-in-original, both, neither. On the line immediately before it, give a one-sentence justification.
