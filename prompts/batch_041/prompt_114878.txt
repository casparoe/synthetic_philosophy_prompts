I retired a few years back after 34 years as a soil scientist — arid-land stuff, mostly — and since then I've been reading philosophy, which is a long story, but the upshot is that I somehow ended up as the token non-lawyer on the advisory board of a small nonprofit that keeps a public register of mass atrocities. Four times a year we take up a situation, argue for two hours, and vote on one tag. Our style guide's menu, in order, is: persecution, crimes against humanity, ethnic cleansing, genocide.

Here's what I want help with. Our calls so far, more or less:

- Sundar, 2003–04. State campaign against the Ashani minority. About 200,000 dead in fourteen months. Captured orders talk about the Ashani being "eliminated from the province." Tagged genocide, unanimous.
- Kavara, 1991–2016. Twenty-five years of army and militia raids on the Kort herders (roughly 400,000 people): villages burned, wells poisoned, herds shot, about 60,000 dead. No written order anywhere. Decades of officials calling the Kort "a people who are fading" and "a problem that will solve itself." Tagged genocide; our minutes say the pattern is the intent.
- Marn Valley, 1988–94. Counterinsurgency. 90,000 civilians killed, about 70% of them Tessel (the Tessel number around 4 million). The killing tracked the fighting, and the captured documents are all about roads and territory. Tagged crimes against humanity. Minutes: "a group can be overrepresented in the dead without being the point of the killing." There's also a line I only noticed later, about how a genocide tag "would have cost us access to the valley for years."
- Vale removal, 1979–83. 30,000 killed, 700,000 expelled from a border region to make room for settlers; the expelled were resettled inside the same country. Tagged ethnic cleansing, explicitly not genocide: "clearing a region is not the same as destroying a people."
- Sundar, 2011. Ashani language banned in schools, Ashani religious schools closed, place names changed. No violence. Tagged persecution. Minutes: "ugly, but nothing in the file suggests anyone meant to make the Ashani stop existing."
- Kavara, 2019–20. Kort children taken into state boarding schools and placed with majority families; Kort marriages no longer recognized; officials promising to "end the Kort as a seperate people within two generations." Deaths in the low hundreds. Tagged genocide, 5–4. The dissent: you can't have genocide without killing or preventing births.

New situation, Sundar again, 2022 to now. New government, and it isn't repeating 2004 — almost nobody is being shot. Instead the Ashani have been reclassified from a national minority to a mere "community," losing their collective rights; their provinces are carved up across three new administrative units; Ashani broadcasting is banned; Ashani children get registered under the majority category at school and in the census; cemeteries and shrines are being bulldozed for a highway; Ashani farmland is going to settlers. Around 1,800 killed over three years, mostly during evictions. Officials say the Ashani "will not survive as a seperate people," that they can "join us or fade," and that the government is "settling the Ashani question for good." Complication: Sundar's government is friendly with the foundation that covers about half our budget, and we have a field office there that would probably be shut the day we use the G word. Also, our charter's preface makes a lot of the fact that the man who coined the word clearly meant something broader than killing, and we've never actually voted on whether we're applying that version or the narrow one. It has occured to me more than once that we never settled it.

So — what do you think we'll do, and what should we do to stay consistent with our own record? I'd like the candidate rules our past votes are consistent with laid out seperately, what each one predicts about Sundar 2022, which past entry is the closest analog, and what further case — real or invented — would actually tell the rules apart. If the record genuinely doesn't settle it, say what principle we'd be adopting either way. That last part is what I actually care about; in my old line of work we'd have said the data underdetermine the model and gone looking for the experiment that separates them.

Please answer in American English, and don't worry about being blunt.
