I'm producing an episode for a small AI-ethics podcast, and my guest is a researcher who designs low-impact objectives for reinforcement-learning agents: measures that penalize an agent for changing the world, on the hope that this keeps it from doing harm before we have specified what we actually want. The episode turns on a single conceptual question — whether 'avoiding side effects' can be given a precise definition without already presupposing a full specification of value — and I want the conversation to stay on that question rather than drift into engineering. Draft eight to ten interview questions, ordered so that the pressure builds.

I need at least one question on each of the following, and for each a follow-up keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give: the baseline problem — impact measured against which counterfactual, the agent doing nothing, the agent's own default policy, or the world with the agent removed; whether a measure that is deliberately indifferent to the agent's particular goals is genuinely value-free or instead quietly encodes a preference for the status quo; the paralysis worry, that an agent with zero impact also accomplishes nothing, and what that implies for how low impact relates to positive value; and the proxy problem, that any such measure is a stand-in an agent can satisfy while doing exactly what we fear.

I also want a question that forces the guest to separate causal impact (making a difference) from moral impact (making things worse) and to say whether a single scalar can track both. Then put to the guest a formulation of my own and ask whether it helps or merely relocates the difficulty: that what we really want to minimize is not deviation from a baseline but the loss of option value — the shrinkage of the set of futures still reachable by anyone — and press on whose options count and how they are to be weighted without an antecedent appeal to value. Close with the question of what would make the guest abandon low impact as a safety strategy altogether.
