For the final project in my philosophy of social science seminar I have to build a classification instrument rather than write an essay, and I've drawn the question whether national character is a coherent notion and whether it is ever acceptable to generalize about a people. I'd like a decision tree that carries a reader from the root question — is there a fact of the matter about how a people characteristically is? — to a named position on when, if ever, such generalizations may be asserted.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of theorists, and make the branch nodes questions a reader can answer without already knowing where they lead; I want people sorted by what they say about cases, not by the label they would pick for themselves. Five distinctions should do work somewhere in the tree: whether a character claim is a statistical summary of a distribution or a shared disposition or essence; whether it licenses an inference to an individual member; whether the trait is treated as a product of institutions and selection or as their cause; whether the reference class (nation-state, ethnic group, diaspora, "civilization") is fixed by the claim or left open; and whether the utterance is purely descriptive or also performs a normative, constitutive role — telling a people what it is in order to make it so.

I also want the tree to keep three cases apart that people seem to treat differently: a demographer reporting that one country's population scores higher than another's on a trust survey; a travel writer's claim that a people is reserved or warm; and a member of the group making the same character claim about their own group. Somewhere the tree should allow that speaker position and audience can change whether an assertion is permissible even when its descriptive content is held fixed.

Each leaf should carry the name of the position, or "unnamed" if nobody defends it, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment; the tree should be able to reach both an eliminativist leaf and a leaf that retains the notion only as a regulative heuristic. Then add two short notes: which combinations of answers the tree leaves unoccupied, and whether each is unoccupied because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; and which positions the tree cannot separate, with a line on why.

No citations or reading list, please — I have the syllabus for that. Keep the whole thing to 800 words or fewer.
