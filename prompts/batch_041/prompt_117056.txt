I'm part of a small grant panel deciding where to put roughly three years of one philosopher's time -- postdoc, strong in ethics and metaphysics, no empirical work. We've settled on the doing/allowing distinction, but neither of us wants the trolly-loop canon again; we think the live stuff is elsewhere. Two threads keep coming up: whether the distinction survives being applied to agents that aren't persons -- large institutions, and increasingly AI systems that mostly 'act' by setting defaults and choosing what to attend to -- and whether 'letting die' is really one thing or a bundle (failing to prevent, failing to rescue, refusing to sustain, standing by). Which open questions in this area should we prioritise over those three years, in what order, and why? I want the reasoning more than the list: which questions are upstream of which, which are tractable for one person working alone, and what would move a question up or down the ranking. And which supposedly central questions should we deprioritise because settling them wouldn't change much downstream?
