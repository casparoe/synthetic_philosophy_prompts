I'm taking a graduate seminar on the ethics of privacy, and there's a pattern that keeps coming up in our discussions that I can't find a proper name for. The clearest version of it is this. A woman is managing a chronic condition. She tells her physician, because he needs to know; she tells her partner, because they share a life; she tells the benefits office, because she needs an accommodation; she tells one close friend, because she needs someone to talk to. Each disclosure is deliberate, each is made to someone with a plausible claim on the information, and not one of them feels like a violation when it happens. A year later she discovers that a dozen people know — some from her, some from the friend, some from the office — that she can no longer track who knows what or anticipate who will learn next, and that she has begun to feel exposed. When she tries to say what went wrong, she can't point to any single act of betrayal. No one assembled the information into a file; there is no collector, no dossier, no snoop. She agreed to every step. A structurally similar thing happens with a photograph: you send it to three friends, each of whom forwards it to people who had a fair claim to see it, and within a week it is, in some sense, public — again without anyone doing anything you would call a breach. What seems to have been lost is not any particular fact's secrecy but something about the shape of who knows it.

Is there a name for this? The closest thing I have encountered is the idea of a mosaic, that individually innocuous pieces of data become revealing once combined — but that account seems to be about an outside party doing the combining, whereas here there is no combiner at all, and every disclosure was voluntary. I have also seen privacy glossed as a matter of controlling information about oneself, but she did control each disclosure; what escaped her was the aggregate, and the aggregate was never the object of any single decision. Nor is it secrecy, since nothing was ever strictly hidden, and nor is it simple gossip, since the spread ran along lines of legitimate interest rather than idle curiosity. What I want is not merely a label. I want to know what the phenomenon actually is and where its edges are: is the thing that gets destroyed the sheer number of knowers, or the relationships among them, or the expectations each of them brings to the information, or the fact that they can now compare notes? Does it begin with the second person or only with the tenth, and is it a matter of degree that eventually crosses a threshold, or is there a categorical change at some point? Is there an established concept that fits, and if so, which, and precisely where does it fail to fit; if there isn't, then name it and define it so that I can tell it apart from the mosaic, from ordinary information spread, and from the plain observation that trust is hard to keep.
