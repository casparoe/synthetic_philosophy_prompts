For a chapter of my dissertation on the foundations of deductive inference, I need a sustained argument for the following claim: Carroll's regress shows not that deduction is unjustified but that the tortoise is asking for the wrong kind of justification — yet this diagnosis cannot be secured by the familiar move of reclassifying modus ponens from a premise to a rule of inference, since any account of what it is to be guided by a rule will itself state something the tortoise could accept while still refusing to draw the conclusion. To sharpen the point, imagine replacing the tortoise with a device that stores every premise and every conditional we supply, and whose internal transitions perfectly mirror modus ponens, yet which has no disposition to move from representing the premises to asserting the conclusion. Argue that the rule/premise distinction cannot explain what such a device lacks, and defend your own positive account — of what it is for an inference to be justified, if not by further propositions — against the objection that it merely relocates the regress one level up.
