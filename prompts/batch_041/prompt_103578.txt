I recently joined my department's promotion committee, and at our last meeting something happened that I cannot stop turning over. We voted separately on whether each candidate cleared the research bar and the teaching bar, and then voted separately on whether to recommend promotion; for one candidate the outcome was a majority for each criterion and a majority against promotion. When I raised the oddity, a senior colleague settled it with "two heads are better than one — that is why we vote," and we moved on. I want to know what that one sentence could actually be claiming, because I suspect it was doing almost no work and that the real questions lie elsewhere. Please lay out the distinct precise theses the slogan might express, from the trivially true to the plainly false, and for each one say what it presupposes about the voters — their independence from one another, their individual competence, and what exactly they are being asked to judge — whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, a contested one, or false, and whether it would in fact license the use my colleague put it to, namely aggregating separate votes on logically connected propositions and reading the results off as a verdict. Say also which reading someone who says this in a committee room is most likely to have in mind, and what that reveals about the argument they think they have made.

Give me the result as a JSON object with these fields: "slogan" (the phrase as repeated); "readings" (an array, each entry an object with "label" (a short name for the reading), "precise_claim" (the thesis stated exactly), "presuppositions" (what must hold about the voters and the options), "verdict" (one of: platitude, substantive and defensible, contested, false), "supports_colleagues_use" (one of: yes, no, only under further conditions — with the conditions named), and "comment" (two or three sentences of justification); "most_likely_intended_reading" (the label, with a sentence on why); and "what_the_slogan_obscures" (a short paragraph naming the phenomenon that the slogan, on any reading, leaves out of view).
