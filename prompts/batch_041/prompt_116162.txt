I'm in a seminar on philosophy of mind and we've spent two weeks on knowing how vs. knowing that, and I keep feeling like both sides are cheating in different ways. So I'd like you to write me a dialogue between two people arguing about it: one who thinks knowing how to do something just is a kind of knowing that (grasping some fact, some proposition, whatever), and one who thinks knowing how is a genuinely different kind of state, basically an ability or a disposition to do the thing. Both of them should be sharp, and neither should be a pushover — I dont want the intellectualist getting dunked on with the toddler-can-walk point and then folding, and I don't want the anti-intellectualist just repeating "but abilities aren't propositions" over and over. Make them actually respond to each other's points.

Here's the case I've been stuck on; use it as the spine of the thing. My friend Kasia is a welder and she can feel when a weld is about to fail — she's right almost every time, way better than the people she works with. But when you ask her what she's going on, what she's actually feeling, she'll give you a rule that's just false; her coworkers who've watched her say the rule doesn't match anything she actually does. Then the wrinkle: under hypnosis, or if you simply convince her she's holding a different tool, she can't do it at all anymore — yet nothing seems to have happened to her knowledge. The anti-intellectualist wants to say her know-how is a disposition that's merely masked, so the knowledge is still there; the intellectualist wants to say this shows the ability can come and go while the knowledge stays, so the knowledge can't just be the ability. And I can't tell whether "masked disposition" is doing any real work here or whether it's a word you get to invoke whenever the ability is gone. I'd like them to fight about that specifically — what would even count as evidence that the disposition is still there rather than gone? Also let them get into the gradability thing at some point (she knows how to weld better than she knows how to do pipefitting — can knowing-that come in degrees like that?) and whether a "practical mode of presentation" is a real move or just the ability wearing a proposition's hat.

No citations and no reading list, just the two of them going at it. End with them having made some genuine progress, or at least with the disagreement sharper and better located than it started — not with a shrug.
