Draft the joint statement that a committed epistemicist and a committed supervaluationist about vagueness could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Both parties have pre-committed to four stipulated materials: a series of a million colour patches running imperceptibly from clear red to clear orange; a community in which every speaker calls the first hundred thousand patches red and the last hundred thousand not red, but whose verdicts diverge within the middle, each speaker flipping at a different, stable point; a stipulated predicate whose extension has been fixed by fiat at patch 500,000; and the operator 'definitely', nested up to three levels. The document should contain a numbered list of claims both sides accept -- about the sorites argument, bivalence, truth-value gaps, what 'definitely' is doing in each theory, whether the residual dispute is semantic or metaphysical, and what notion of truth each side is really deploying -- followed by a disagreements table, each row stating one residual disagreement in wording its own side would endorse, together with the crux it turns on. I expect the cruxes to include the quantifier question (that there is a first non-red patch, versus no particular patch being that one), the treatment of the split speakers, and higher-order vagueness in the nesting of 'definitely'; for each crux, give the case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's verdicts on the four stipulated materials recorded in advance, and close with a judgment on which parts of the dispute are merely verbal and which are substantive. Everything should be argued from the stipulated materials rather than from the literature.
