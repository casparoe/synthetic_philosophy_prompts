Bit of an odd request, and I'm not sure it's the kind of thing you take questions about, but here goes. I'm a retired geologist — thirty-odd years in paleoclimate, mostly lake mud — and since I hung it up I've been reading alot of philosophy, which my wife says is a bad influence. Anyway.

My granddaughter is finishing a PhD in archaeology. She's been excavating a Neolithic village in the Near East for four seasons, and she asked me to read the chapter where she makes her big claim, on the grounds that I'm "good at being skeptical." The chapter is about a pit her team found under the floor of one of the houses. It has about forty little clay pots in it — thimble-sized, unfired, so they couldn't have held water or sat on a fire. Nobody ever used them. Thats her whole point.

Her claim is that the pots were made to be buried, and that this tells us the villagers believed the dead had to be provided for — some notion of an afterlife — and from that she argues the village had a recognized role for somebody to handle that business. Her word is a ritual specialist.

I asked how she gets from pots to beliefs. She said it's the best explanation of the evidence, and that similar deposits show up in societies where we know what they mean. I said fine, but when I claim a layer of mud means the lake was cold, I can point at a lake that's cold right now and check that the mud looks the same — the modern case is something I can go look at without already believing my answer. She says that's exactly what she's doing. Only she gets to pick which living societies to compare with, and I noticed she picked the ones that agree with her. There's another student in her program who says the little pots are practice pieces from kids learning the craft, which strikes me as just as consistent with forty little pots.

When I pushed on that she got a bit huffy and said I was asking her to prove a negative. Maybe I was. So: is this a legitimate way to reason and I'm just being a jerk, or is it going in a circle? If it's legitimate, what makes it legitimate — "best explanation" can't just mean the one that suits her. And does the belief part do any real work, or could she get everything she needs out of the pots themselves without ever saying what was going on in anyone's head? I'd like to have something useful to say before Thanksgiving, when her advisor is coming to dinner.
