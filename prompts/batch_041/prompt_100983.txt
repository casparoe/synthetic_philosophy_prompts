I'm a retired atmospheric scientist — thirty-odd years on long-range forecasting and chaotic systems, where the honest answer to "what happens if we nudge this" is usually "eventually everything is different, and not in a way anyone can pin down." Since I retired I've been reading moral philosophy on and off, and I keep bumping into something I dont have a word for. Here's the pattern. Take some decision of mine — I give fifty pounds to a charity that does deworming, or I vote, or I take the job in one city instead of staying put. I try to do what my old training drilled into me: judge the choice by the total difference it makes in the world, totting up the good and the bad downstream. And the accounting never closes. The deworming pill makes a kid healthier, she stays in school, her life goes one way rather than another; but then every person she meets, every child she has, the work she takes up — the branch keeps forking and the fork never rejoins the trunk. Twenty years out there's no clean counterfactual to compare against, because the world I'd compare to was itself reshaped by that one pill. So it isn't just that I'm unsure of the size of the effect; the very quantity I'm supposed to be maximizing seems to dissolve. And yet I don't think it follows that I should just stop giving, which is the part that nags at me.

Is there a name for this exact situation, and more to the point, is there a literature that has already done the carving? I want to know how it differs from three things it feels close to but isn't. First, ordinary uncertainty — a gamble I can't resolve for lack of information; this feels different, since no amount of information would help. Second, the butterfly-effect point from my own field, sensitivity to initial conditions, because that's about prediction and my puzzle seems to be about something more like the identity of the outcome I'm meant to be weighing. Third, the cheap "who knows what the long-run effects are" shrug that people use to wave away any argument about far-off consequences; I suspect that's a watered-down version of the real thing. If there's no established term, coin one, but tell me where its edges are — in particular, is there a genuine difference between a case where my action is simply swallowed by the causal wash and a case where my action changes something structural (a rule, a policy, an institution) that then does the swallowing? Does the trouble get worse the further out you look, or plateau? And is the standard move, "just maximize expected value anyway," an answer or a way of changing the subject? Keep it to 450 words, lead with the name or the coinage, then the carving.
