My brother's got this arguement that you can never actually learn anything just by thinking, and I cant find the hole in it. It goes:

(1) When you reason correctly from stuff you already believe, the conclusion has to be true whenever the stuff you started with is true. That's just what correct reasoning is.
(2) So the conclusion was already sitting inside what you started with — you didn't add anything, you just dug it out.
(3) And if it was already sitting inside what you believed, then you already believed it. If someone had asked you the right question you could've answered, you just hadn't bothered.
(4) So you never come to believe anything you didn't already believe.
(5) So nothing you do by pure thinking counts as learning.
(6) But I clearly do learn by thinking. Last week I worked out a tip on a bill in my head and the number was genuinely news to me — I couldn't have told you it one second before.
(7) So one of 1-5 has to give.

I'm not dropping 1, that's just what "follows" means, and 6 actually happened, I was there. So it's gotta be 2 or 3, but I can't tell which, and either way I don't see what I'm left standing on — if 3 is wrong, what does it even mean to "already believe" something you'd flat-out deny if someone asked you about it?
