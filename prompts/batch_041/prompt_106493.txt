I'm a retired physicist — spent thirty-odd years on neutron scattering before I packed it in — and since then I've been reading philosophy, badly and happily. A few of us run a little podcast where we get one guest on and I do the interviewing, and next month's guest is a historian of AI who's new book makes a lot out of a bit in Turing's 1950 machine-intelligence paper that everyone else seems to skip: the passage at the end about telepathy and extra-sensory perception, where Turing frets that the imitation game could be gamed if the participants are telepathic and floats the idea of putting them in a telepathy-proof room. Her line is that this isn't a cute aside or a wobble — it's the tell. The moment you have to police the room to make the test mean anything, she says, you've admitted the test is measuring the setup and not the machine, and "intelligence" ends up being defined relative to a channel rather than to the thing being tested.

I want questions that force her to actually defend that, not just restate it. Draft eight to ten, and for each one give me a follow-up aimed at the answer she's most likely to give. I specifically want at least one question on whether Turing was really being serious about ESP or just raising a logical possibility, and how you'd tell the difference from the text alone; one on whether taking telepathy seriously is a consequence of his behaviourist/verificationist leanings or a departure from them; and one that drags her "you have to police the room" claim into the present — the judge who already knows which system is the machine, or a model trained on the transcripts of every test ever run — and asks whether the same move makes the modern test hollow, or whether there's a principled line between telepathy and ordinary prior knowledge. Close with a question about what would actually make her give up the reading: what evidence or argument would count against it, not merely fail to support it. Don't let her hide behind summarising the paper or telling me what Turing "really meant" in some vague way — I want the load-bearing claims out where I can push on them.
