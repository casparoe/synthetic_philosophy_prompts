I want a case built to order, in which three criteria for drawing the therapy/enhancement line come apart in a specified pattern. The point of the exercise is to test whether the distinction tracks anything of its own or is a stand-in for need, optionality, or brute luck, so the case must be one in which the classification and the ordinary verdict can be pulled apart.

Apply the following three criteria as stipulated. Do not attribute them to anyone, do not cite literature, and do not look for a source; treat them as given and use only what they say.

C1, the species-deficit criterion: an intervention is therapy if it moves the recipient's functioning from below the species-typical range into or above that range; an intervention that takes someone already within the species-typical range above it is enhancement.

C2, the life-plan criterion: an intervention is therapy if it protects the recipient's ability to pursue a reasonable range of life plans or prevents a significant and lasting loss of welfare; otherwise it is enhancement.

C3, the baseline criterion: an intervention is therapy if it restores or maintains a level of functioning the recipient has previously possessed; if it confers a capacity the recipient has never had, or raises a capacity above the recipient's own previous best, it is enhancement.

Your task is to construct one case, one person and one intervention, in which C1 says therapy, C2 says therapy, C3 says enhancement, and the ordinary, pre-theoretical verdict is that the intervention is elective, meaning the person could decline it without loss and no institution is obliged to provide it.

Constraints on the case. It must be contemporary and mundane: a decision a real clinician, insurer, school, or licensing body could face now, involving an intervention that actually exists and is in current use. No hypothetical technologies, no gene editing, no cognitive or moral enhancement, and none of the standard illustrations from this literature, including doping, growth hormone for short children, cochlear implants, stimulants for the healthy, and embryo selection. The person should be an ordinary adult or child, with no condition that all three criteria would classify identically. Every feature of the case must be independently plausible; nothing may be included for the sole purpose of securing a verdict. The scenario itself should be no longer than one paragraph. Do not dissolve the problem by declaring that the criteria answer different questions; if you hold that, say so only after completing the work below. If some specification cannot be met, say so explicitly rather than quietly relaxing it.

Then, in order: (1) the case; (2) for each criterion, the verdict and the reasoning that yields it, using only features stated in the case, so that the verdicts are generated rather than stipulated; (3) one concrete institutional rule, such as a coverage, accommodation, eligibility, or subsidy rule, and the opposed decisions C3 and the other two criteria would produce about this same intervention for this same person; (4) the feature of the case that is doing the work, together with the removal test: vary that one feature, hold everything else fixed, and say which criteria change verdict and whether the three-way split survives; (5) what the ordinary verdict of elective is actually tracking, and if it tracks something other than the feature identified in (4), demonstrate that with a further variation; (6) the strongest case a defender of the therapy/enhancement distinction could make against your example, for instance that the baseline criterion is a criterion of entitlement rather than of classification, or that C1's reference class should be indexed to the individual rather than the species, and whether that defence rescues the distinction or merely relocates its arbitrariness.
