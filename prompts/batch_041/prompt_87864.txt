So I've been going down a rabbit hole on what "life" actually means, and I think the whole debate is confused in a way nobody quite names. Almost every definition I run into is doing one of two seperate jobs. Some are synchronic: a thing is alive if, right now, it's metabolizing, maintaining itself far from equilibrium, repairing its own parts. Others are diachronic: a thing is alive if it's part of a lineage that evolves, gets selected, accumulates adaptations. These aren't just two different answers, they pull apart. A self-maintaining chemical reactor that never reproduces and its not part of any lineage satisfies the first and fails the second. A virus lineage satisfies the second and fails the first. So is one of these the real criterion and the other just a heuristic, or is "life" actually two concepts wearing one word? And then the borderline cases: mules, sterile worker ants, prions, viroids, a frozen embryo, a corpse that's still structurally intact. Which of those does each criterion let in or keep out, and does shifting between individual-level and lineage-level framing change the verdict? I want the actual structure of the tradeoff, not a list of definitions.
