I serve on the research-safety committee at my institute, and since my own diagnosis last spring I have caught myself half-believing an argument I would ordinarily wave away, which is why I want this one handled carefully. A postdoc has applied to run an experiment on himself that would, on each of twenty trials, kill him with probability one half. He argues that if the many-worlds interpretation is right he is certain to find himself in a branch in which he survives, so the experiment poses no risk to him and the usual risk-benefit analysis simply does not apply; and that if it is wrong, then there is no version of him left to be harmed. Please draft the written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's vote: it should recommend a concrete course of action, with a time frame and any conditions attached, give his argument the serious treatment it deserves rather than a quick dismissal, and say what would show the committee to have been wrong. I would like it to come to grips with whether the risk to him is genuinely nil, whether a version of the experiment that kills no one in any branch would serve his purposes, and what his results could establish at all, given that only a surviving version of him could report them.
