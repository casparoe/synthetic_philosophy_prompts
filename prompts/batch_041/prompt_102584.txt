I have spent an unreasonable amount of time in the interpretability threads of the rationalist blogosphere, and I have come away a realist about some findings and a deflationist about others, without being able to state the criterion that separates them. I should like you to tell me what principle, or small family of principles, my verdicts add up to, and where the principle has to give.

Here are the verdicts.

1. A linear probe fitted to a trained vision model's layer decodes whether the image contains a dog with high accuracy. A probe fitted the same way to a randomly initialised network of the same architecture also decodes the label, because it can latch onto low-level statistics that correlate with the label in the dataset. I say the trained network has a representation of a dog and the random one does not, even though the probe succeeds on both.

2. Ablating or patching a direction in a language model's residual stream flips its answer from Paris to Rome. I say that direction is a representation of the France-capital relation, and that the causal intervention is the strongest evidence one can have for it.

3. A single neuron fires for Paris, for France, for the Eiffel Tower and for croissants. I say it is not a representation of any of those things; it is a mixed detector, and the representation, if there is one, is distributed.

4. A sparse autoencoder finds a clean feature that fires on Paris contexts, but no downstream head reads it and ablating it changes nothing. I say it is a representational artefact rather than a representation of Paris.

5. A model's chain-of-thought says that the capital of France is Paris, but its internal state does not distinguish Paris from Lyon and the output is driven by a memorised template. I say the model does not represent Paris; the verbal report is not the representation.

6. Two networks compute the same input-output function: one is a lookup table over the finite input space, the other is a compositional algorithm. I say only the compositional one represents the structure of the domain; the lookup table implements the function without representing anything, even though it behaves identically on every input.

7. A model is trained on a synthetic task where the label is generated by factor X, but the model uses a spurious factor Y that is perfectly correlated with X in the training data. I say the model represents Y rather than X, because representation is fixed by the model's causal role and not by the generative ground truth.

8. A multimodal model has a concept present in its text encoder and in its image encoder, but the two encoders are not aligned and nothing in the model uses the concept across modalities. I say the model has two separate representations of the concept and no unified one.

My tentative principle is this: a state represents F just in case it carries information about F, that information is used by downstream computation, and the state is robust under interventions that change F while leaving the rest of the task fixed. But this principle seems to say that the lookup table in (6) represents the function's structure, since it carries and uses the information, which contradicts my verdict; it says the artefact in (4) is not a representation, which matches; and it says the mixed detector in (3) might represent a disjunction of Paris-or-France-or-croissants, which I do not want.

Could you state the principle or principles that actually capture these verdicts, with the case that motivates each clause? Please also say what it predicts for two cases I have not listed: (i) a representation that is causally used only in a redundant, belt-and-braces way, where a second route carries the same information and either route alone suffices; and (ii) a representation that is present in the weights and would be used if the task demanded it, but is never activated on any input the model actually receives. Finally, tell me which of my verdicts the principle cannot accommodate and would force me to drop. I would rather have a simple principle that overturns one verdict than a contrived one that saves all eight.
