I'm putting together a set for a philosophy grad student open mic night, and I need a joke about akrasia and hyperbolic discounting. The audience will know the basics — that akrasia is the old problem of knowing the better course and doing the worse anyway, and that hyperbolic discounting is the empirical pattern where we discount rewards steeply in the near term and more gently further out, which makes our preferences time-inconsistent. So the joke can't just be haha I'll quit tomorrow. It should turn on the actual structure: the fact that the person at 9am who sincerely ranks health over smoking and the person at 9pm who sincerely ranks smoking over health are not simply weak; their ranking itself flips as the option gets closer. I'd love it if the joke also touches the distinction between naive and sophisticated present bias — the naive person keeps thinking future me will have different preferences, the sophisticated person knows future me will be a different preference ordering and tries to precommit, maybe with a Ulysses contract, maybe with a app that locks the fridge. You can set it in a fictional support group called Tomorrow's People, where the meeting keeps getting rescheduled to next week because everyone's valuation of attending drops as the date approaches. The group leader, Dr. Later, is very sincere and very inconsistent. Or ignore that setting if you have a better one. Just make the punchline depend on the discount function or on the divided-self reading of akrasia, not on generic procrastination.

After the joke, add a short note about where it takes liberties with the ideas — for instance, if it treats hyperbolic discounting as automatically implying a divided self, is that a contested move? — and say what would change your mind: what finding about time inconsistency, or what argument about akrasia, would make you rewrite the punchline or drop it. I'm not looking for a bibliography or anything, just the joke plus that caveat.
