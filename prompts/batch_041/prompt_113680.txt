I am convening a small reading group on self-reference and formal systems, and next week we take up the MIU string game and the metaphor of jumping out of the system. Since half the group will arrive cold, I am putting the whole setup on the board, and I would like the criteria for a theory of the jump settled before we start arguing about who has one. The game: the only string you begin with is MI, and from any string you may append a U if it ends in I; double everything after the leading M, so that Mx becomes Mxx; replace any occurrence of III with a single U; or delete any occurrence of UU. The question is whether MU is obtainable, and it is not, for a reason that no move of the game discloses: in every reachable string the number of I's is not divisible by three, since MI has one I, doubling a count that is not divisible by three cannot make it so, and replacing III by U subtracts exactly three, while the other two rules never touch the count at all — whereas MU has none, and zero is divisible by three. The moral everyone draws is that you cannot reach MU by playing the game, only by standing outside it and noticing the invariant, and that this is the same species of move as seeing that a formal system's Gödel sentence is true, or as winning a war by refusing to fight on the board at all.

What I want from you is the desiderata list for a theory of that move — a theory of what it is to jump out of a system. I am after the requirements themselves rather than a contender: the cases the theory has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the results it has to be consistent with, each accompanied by the case or consideration that motivates it, and each marked as non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp. The purpose that fixes adequacy, if it helps, is this: I want a theory sharp enough to decide, in a concrete case, whether a jump has occurred, because that is exactly the question my group cannot settle. Some of the cases I intend to press it with, so that you can see the range: the person who reaches for the divisibility fact in under a minute and cannot say why she looked there rather than for a derivation; an automated prover that produces the same invariant once someone hands it the lemma that the count of I's mod 3 is preserved; the deflationist who says there is no jump, only a bigger system, since any move you can state can be added to the rules and the whole tower is turtles; the romantic who insists the difference is that the human sees the point and the machine does not, whatever the machine outputs; and the sceptic who says the MIU game is a bad example altogether, because there is a finite decision procedure for which strings are derivable and so it cannot illustrate any essential limitation, for which you need the halting problem or Gödel. I am also curious what the theory must say about a rule you are not licensed to make, since on some accounts the general who declines the board has simply broken the rules; and about the fact that the insight, once had, is mechanical, because after the invariant is known MU's unreachability is a finite computation, so whatever the jump was, it seems to leave no residue in the system it lands in.

Three constraints I would like handled explicitly rather than waved at: no consistent system of the relevant strength proves its own consistency, so a jump cannot be a system's certification of itself; a system that proves that if it can prove P then P can already prove P, so adding a trust axiom is not a free move; and halting is undecidable, so running the search to the end is not available either. If some pair of the requirements I end up with cannot be satisfied together, say so plainly, since that is the part I most want — I suspect the pressure sits between the demand that a jump be a general, well-defined operation and the demand that it not be merely a step into a larger system. Then, briefly, how the extension-or-regress view, the strange-loop view on which a system comes to model itself, and the claim that we can see the truth of what the machine cannot prove each fare against the list, and which single requirement does the most to rule candidates out.
