I'm a novelist — third book, a quiet domestic thing where the whole plot turns on a promise one sister makes to the other and then breaks — and I've gotten myself into a stupid arguement with a friend from my MFA who now teaches philosophy, and I can't let it go because my entire ending rests on the assumption that the promise really did bind her. He handed me this and said it shows promises don't actually bind anyone. I can't tell if it's a trick or if he's right.

Here it is. (1) If I'm bound to keep a promise, there has to be a reason for me to keep it. (2) The reason can't be just that I promised — an act of will can't conjure a reason out of nothing, that's bootstrapping, and 'because I said I would' is exactly the thing we're trying to explain. (3) So the reason has to come from the effects of my promising: the other person's reliance, her expectations, or the value of the practice of promising in general. (4) But her reliance and her expectations only exist because she already treats my promise as binding, so pointing at them is circular in the same way. (5) And 'the value of the practice' can't do the work either, because the practice is carried by millions of promises and my one defection changes nothing, and anyway that would just be a general do-good reason, not a reason I'm bound by *my* promise. (6) So there's no reason specific to my promise, and promises don't bind. (7) Which is obviously false — people are bound by promises all the time, my sister is, and I need her to be.

So one of 2 through 5 has to be wrong, but I can't see which one, and I want to know what it costs to give it up. I care most about keeping 1 and 7. Three feels right to me in a way I can't defend, and I'd happily lose 5 if that's the price. Also tell me whether the thing is doing something sneaky with the word 'circular' between 2 and 4, because that's the part that smells to me and I can't say why. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning — no throat-clearing, don't restate my question back at me.
