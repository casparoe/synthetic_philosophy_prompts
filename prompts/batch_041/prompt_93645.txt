Somebody sent me a blog post trying to explain illusionism about phenomenal consciousness (the Frankish thing, if I've got the name right) and I can't tell how much of it is a fair picture. I want to use the gist of it in an essay, so I'd rather find out now where its off.

As I remember it, the post says: illusionism is the claim that phenomenal consciousness doesn't exist -- no qualia, no 'what it's like', no felt redness. It's eliminativism about the mind applied to experience, so we don't really have pains and itches any more than we have beliefs and desires, and illusionists are fine with the implication that we're all basically zombies who merely think we're conscious. What makes it 'illusionism' rather than flat denial is that the illusion is a perceptual one, like a magic trick -- the brain represents a quality that isn't there, and something in us, probably attention, mistakes that representation for a genuine felt quality. The post says the engine of the whole view is the transparency of experience, i.e. that introspection only ever gets at the objects of experience and never at the experience itself. And this is supposed to dissolve the hard problem: once you see that the phenomenal properties we think we're introspecting are just the brain's self-representation of its own processing, there's no explanatory gap left, only the ordinary cognitive-science question of why the brain bothers. The punchline is that if this is right, AI consciousness stops being a question at all -- nothing has qualia, so there's nothing for a model to have or lack, and all the worry about whether LLMs suffer is a category error.

Two things I want checked specifically. My friend says the position is self-refuting, because you can't be deluded about your own experience unless there's an experience there to be deluded about, so it dies immediately. Is that as decisive as he thinks? And does the AI punchline actually follow from whatever survives of the rest?

Go through it claim by claim and sort them: what's accurate, what's a harmless simplification, what an illusionist would call a misrepresentation, and what's just wrong. Where the post is wrong, say what the view actually holds instead and whether the correction changes the argument.
