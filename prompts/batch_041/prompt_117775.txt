Two colleagues of mine, Ruth and Dev, spent most of a lunch hour arguing about a bonus scheme my school district has just introduced, and because the scheme applies to me I would like the argument adjudicated rather than smoothed over. From next term, teachers are paid a fixed sum for each class that rates us as genuinely caring about students. The money is real, the ratings are anonymous, and I have noticed that I now want to care more. Their exchange ran roughly as follows.

(1) Ruth said the payment gives me no reason at all to care. Reasons to care about a student come from the student — that they are struggling, that it matters to them — and a sum of money from the district is the wrong kind of reason: it may make caring profitable, but it cannot make caring correct.

(2) Dev said that is a distinction without a difference. The bonus is plainly a reason to care, since it is a reason to do whatever brings the caring about; Ruth is really only saying that it does not make the caring fitting, which is a separate matter. There is no embarrassment, he said, in having a reason to be in a state alongside a reason that the state is the right response to its object.

(3) Ruth replied that this saves nothing. If a consideration can be a reason for an attitude merely by being a reason to bring the attitude about, then a threat is a reason to believe a contradiction and a bribe a reason to admire a bad painting, and whatever distinction we draw afterwards is doing the work rather than the considerations are. The real question, she said, is which kind of reason can be demanded of someone — whether a teacher who takes the money and never comes to care has failed the students or only failed the scheme.

(4) Dev answered that the threat case makes his point rather than hers: a threat genuinely is a reason to bring a belief about, but beliefs answer to truth, so it can never be the reason a belief is correct; by the same token the district could pay for the workshop that reliably produces caring in teachers, and that would be a reason to acquire the attitude without ever being the reason the attitude is the right response to a student. Ruth said this was the two-kinds answer restated, and that she still could not see how we decide which kind is doing the work in a case like mine.

They asked me to referee and I could not. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move, saying what each of them is right and wrong about rather than who simply wins, and then give me your verdict on my own situation: whether the bonus gives me a reason to care, whether the caring it produces can still be genuine or correct, and whether I have anything to feel bad about.

Return your answer as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "move_verdicts": an array of four objects, one per move in the order above, each with "move" (the integer 1–4), "speaker" (either "Ruth" or "Dev"), "verdict" (exactly one of "clearly mistaken", "mostly mistaken", "mixed", "mostly right", "clearly right"), and "reason" (one or two sentences justifying the rating).
- "load_bearing_issue": one sentence naming the single point on which the rest of the disagreement turns.
- "what_both_missed": two to four sentences on what neither of them noticed.
- "overall_verdict": one to three sentences saying who is closer to right, and why.
- "my_case": two to four sentences on whether the bonus gives me a reason to care, whether the caring it produces can be genuine or correct, and whether I have anything to feel bad about.
- "unresolved": one sentence on what the exchange leaves genuinely open.
