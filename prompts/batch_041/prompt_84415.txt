I teach on a master's-level module in environmental history, and next week I have ninety minutes with an unusually mixed room: historians who have never handled a proxy record, and palaeoclimate scientists who have never written a sentence with a human subject in it. The session is on how to attribute causal weight to non-human events, and what I want from you is the plan for the session rather than the session itself. Set out the order in which the components have to arrive and why each depends on the one before it: what has to be distinguished from what — cause from agent, cause from condition, timing from magnitude, the naturalness of a cause from its necessity — and which of those distinctions this particular room will collapse into each other if I introduce them out of sequence.

There are two things I want the room to leave with. First, that a natural event can carry genuine causal weight and still be the wrong thing to blame, because it typically fixes when something happened rather than how bad it was. Second, that a non-human cause becomes a historical cause only once it has been cashed out in a human currency — prices, migrations, decisions — and that this is not a concession to determinism but the opposite of one. Build the whole session on a single carrying case, and tell me what it must have and what it must lack. I am tempted by the obvious candidates — the year without a summer, the drought that is said to have finished off a civilisation — and I suspect they fail for the same reason; say whether they do, and what would serve better.

Then the misconceptions, in the order the room will actually produce them, with the point in the sequence at which I should head each one off. I can already hear 'so it was nobody's fault', 'so the climate does not matter after all', and 'that is determinism with extra steps'; and, from the other side of the room, 'you are denying that the eruption happened'. Finally, how do I find out at the end whether they have understood this rather than merely found it agreeable — what should I ask, and what would a bad answer to it look like? Include what I should openly refuse to cover in ninety minutes, and say why. Answer in British English.
