Here is an argument that a classmate and I worked out for a seminar on solution concepts, and I can no longer tell whether it is a proof or a trick. I would like you to tell me which step to give up, why it looked innocent, and what giving it up would cost.

The game. Two players, Row and Column, move simultaneously. Row chooses one of A, B, C; Column chooses one of L, R. Payoffs are listed as (Row, Column):

| | L | R |
|---|---|---|
| A | (1, 0) | (0, 1) |
| B | (1, 0) | (2, 1) |
| C | (1, 1) | (0.9, 0) |

The argument.

(1) A rational player never chooses a strategy when some other strategy — or some lottery over strategies — does at least as well against everything the opponent might choose, and strictly better against something the opponent might choose.

(2) Consider the lottery that plays A and B with equal probability. Against L it yields Row 1, exactly what C yields; against R it yields 1, strictly more than the 0.9 that C yields. So C is such a strategy.

(3) Therefore a rational Row never chooses C.

(4) This is common knowledge, so no one expects C. Remove C from the game. Now B does at least as well as A against everything — both yield 1 against L — and strictly better against R, where B yields 2 and A yields 0. So A is such a strategy too.

(5) Therefore a rational Row never chooses A. Only B is left, and Column's best reply to B is R.

(6) So the only rational outcome is (B, R).

(7) But (C, L) is a Nash equilibrium: against L, C is as good for Row as anything else, so C is a best reply to L, and L is Column's best reply to C. An argument that brands a Nash-equilibrium strategy irrational has gone wrong somewhere.

I would most like to keep (1); "never choose a strategy you can improve on" has always sounded to me like the plainest possible statement of what rationality is. But (7) is equally hard to deny. Which step is the defective one, why did it look innocent, and what must I accept — and give up — if I repair the argument at each of the candidate points? In particular, if I keep (1) and reject whatever step (7) is complaining about, what exactly have I committed Row to, and what does that commitment force on me in other games?
