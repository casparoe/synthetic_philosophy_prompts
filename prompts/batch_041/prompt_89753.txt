Draft the joint statement that a committed Mencian and a committed Xunzian could both put their names to after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on human nature. The document should contain: (a) a numbered list of claims both accept — about what is inborn, about the roles of spontaneous feeling, deliberate effort, and ritual, and about what moral education is for; (b) the disagreement itself, stated once at the level of the two positions, in wording each side would accept as fair to its own view; (c) the cruxes, that is, the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side; and (d) for each crux, a concrete case or consideration both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Two constraints. First, do not let the collaboration terminate in the comfortable verdict that the two merely used 性 (xing, "nature") in different senses; if that is part of the story, say so, but then isolate exactly which parts of the dispute survive once any merely verbal disagreement is set aside. Second, make each side face a case that cuts against it — for the Mencian, a person whose untutored impulses run reliably toward cruelty or disorder; for the Xunzian, a person who, without instruction and against self-interest, is moved to help a stranger. Say whether either case is even admissible as evidence for the view in question, and why.

Close with a table of the residual disagreements, one row per crux, with columns: crux (short label); Mencian formulation (in wording a Xunzian would accept); Xunzian formulation (in wording a Mencian would accept); the test or consideration both accept; the Mencian's prediction; the Xunzian's prediction; and what would move which side, and in which direction.
