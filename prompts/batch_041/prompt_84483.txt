hey, i need a satire piece for my school magazine (due friday, hence the rush) and i want to check the idea with you before i write it, or honestly just have you write it.

the thing im making fun of is the rule for when you're allowed to just flat-out say something, instead of hedging or guessing or "i think". one of the readings for philosophy club said the rule is knowledge: you can only assert something if you actually know it, not if you believe it really hard, not if it just happens to be true, not if your evidence is good but you havent checked. other people in the same reading said thats way too strict, and the real rule is truth, or belief, or justified belief. what stuck with me is the lottery thing — you can be 99.9 percent sure your ticket lost and it still sounds wrong to announce that it lost, even though you're basically certainly right. and saying its raining while also saying you dont believe its raining sounds like nonsense, which makes it look like belief has to be part of the rule too. but then the counterexamples: a teacher can back up a students answer she doesnt actually believe, and that isnt weird or rude. and if you say something false that you had no way of knowing was false, maybe you didnt do anything wrong at all, you're just excused.

so my idea is a mock official document — a compliance handbook and FAQ from a made-up "Bureau of Assertion Standards," deadpan, formatted like a real government form, where you have to get clearance before you're allowed to assert anything. sections like how to apply for a knowledge permit, the high-probability waiver (and why lottery statements keep getting rejected anyway), the excusable-false-statement defense form, the implicature loophole (they cant fine you for merely suggesting something), an appeals board that has spent nine years trying to decide which rule they're enforcing, and a recurring audit finding that the Bureau itself cant meet its own standard in its own publications. like 600 to 700 words, numbered items, no winking, no jokes that are just "bureaucracy is annoying" — every joke should come out of the actual structure of the debate, so someone who knows this stuff laughs and thinks its fair rather than wincing. no citations or reading lists, obviously, i dont need those and neither does the piece.

two things though. first, if the knowledge rule is actually a fringe position that nobody serious holds and im parodying a straw man, or if a compliance-handbook format is the wrong genre for this and it'd be funnier as something else, say so at the top and pitch me a better version instead of just writing it anyway — dont be polite about it. second, at the end give me a short note on which parts are on solid ground and which parts lean on a contested reading of the debate, because im going to get comments on this.
