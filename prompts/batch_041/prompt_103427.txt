so I'm in a metaphilosophy reading group this term and next week I'm the one defending the line that philosophy actually makes progress against the guy in our group who thinks it's all just fashion cycling around. Here's roughly where I've landed. (1) Philosophy progresses, but non-cumulatively — we don't stack up settled theorems the way the sciences do, we mostly eliminate bad options and sharpen the questions. (2) That's genuine progress, but it's progress in understanding the problem, not progress toward a single right answer. (3) Even so I'm no relativist: some philosophical positions really are more defensible than others, there's a fact of the matter about which arguments are better, it isn't just taste or paradigm. (4) I think alot of the disputes that look permanently intractable are actually verbal, or turn on people quietly having different targets in mind when they use the same word. (5) I think intuitions are culturally and historically variable, so we shouldn't be treating them as data. (6) And the clearest progress we've actually made is in the bits that got formalized — logic, decision theory, semantics — where we ended up with real results, which I take as my best evidence for the whole thesis. (7) I'm enough of a metaphilosopher to admit that any claim about philosophy's progress is itself a philosophical claim, so it inherits whatever epistemic status claims like that have.

What I want is an audit, not a pep talk. Which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they're in tension, and how severe is each real conflict — rank them, don't just list them all as equally bad.
