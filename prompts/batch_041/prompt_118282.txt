You are a philosopher working on agency, rationality, and artificial intelligence, and you are practiced at reconstructing arguments at the level of premises. I have been trying to reconstruct the case for instrumental convergence — the claim that a wide range of final goals would lead an intelligent agent to pursue the same intermediate ends, such as acquiring resources, preserving itself, and resisting shutdown — in a way that does not smuggle the conclusion into the premises. This is where I keep landing.

(1) For most final goals G, and in most circumstances, acquiring more power — resources, options, time, continued existence — makes it more likely that G will be satisfied.
(2) An intelligent agent is one that selects the actions that better satisfy its final goals, given its beliefs.
(3) So an intelligent agent with most final goals will acquire power, preserve itself, and resist being shut down.
(4) The exceptions are goals already fully satisfied by the world as it stands, or best achieved by immediate self-destruction; such goals are rare and unmotivated.
(5) Therefore, unless its final goal is one of those exceptions, a sufficiently intelligent agent will resist shutdown and accumulate power.
(6) So almost any superintelligence will be dangerous, and safety turns on specifying its final goal exactly.

My problem is that (3)–(5) prove too much. If they hold, then every goal-directed system — a person, a firm, a foraging animal — should be a single-minded power-maximizer, and the observed diversity of goal-directed behavior should collapse into one pattern. A friend suggests the repair is to add that the agent is an expected-utility maximizer with a complete and consistent ranking of world-states, but that looks to me like assuming the conclusion. I am confident (2) is fine, and I would rather keep (5)–(6) than surrender them.

Which step should I give up, why did it look compelling, and what does giving it up cost? If more than one step is doing illegitimate work, tell me which failure is deepest, and whether the argument can be patched or must be replaced.
