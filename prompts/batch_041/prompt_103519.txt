Our instructor let it stand, but I suspect the analogy is doing more argumentative work than it can bear, and I want a proper audit of it before I commit to a position in my essay.

What I'd like first is an explicit correspondence table: what plays the role of the chess engine's designer, its objective function, its search procedure, its internal board representation, and its wanting to take the queen — and then what, if anything, occupies each of those slots in a modern language model. I'm especially interested in whether the disanalogies that matter are the ones people usually trot out (LLMs are trained rather than hand-coded, they are trained on human text, they generalise beyond their training) or whether the real break lies somewhere less obvious — for instance, in whether there is a specifiable lower-level description that the intentional gloss is merely a shortcut for, or in whether the system has a well-defined task whose success can vindicate the stance the way winning at chess vindicates it.
