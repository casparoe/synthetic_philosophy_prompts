ok so my reading group is doing weakness of will next week and everyone has to put their own cards on the table before we start, so I want a sanity check on whether my little bundle of views actualy hangs together before I make a fool of myself. here goes:

1. Weakness of will is real and it's its own kind of failure. When someone acts against their own all-things-considered judgement about what they should do, that isn't just a change of mind, and it isn't just some other desire outvoting the judgement — something genuinly goes wrong, and it's a failure of the will specifically.

2. I'm a Humean about motivation. A belief on its own never moves you to act; you need a desire or pro-attitude in there somewhere.

3. The right way to trade future goods off against present ones is exponential discounting, with a rate that tracks real uncertainty (am I even going to be alive, will the thing still matter to me). Pure time preference — caring about when a good arrives just because it arrives sooner — is a mistake.

4. Hyperbolic discounting is the actual pattern you find in people and in animals, the near term gets weighted out of all proportion, and I think that's a bias to be corrected, not just a different set of preferences that's equally respectable.

5. Precommitment is legitimate. Burning the cigarettes, locking the money in a box, signing the contract — that's not me bullying a stranger, that's me being rational about myself.

6. The unit of agency is the whole life, not the time slice. When we ask who did it, or who decided, the answer is always the temporally extended person; a time slice is just a part of that person and never an agent in its own right.

and here's a case I keep coming back to: a friend sets herself a rule every sunday night — no drinking till friday — and then breaks it on wednesday, and when she breaks it she says she still endorses the sunday rule, she hasn't revised anything, she just does it anyway. I think that's the cleanest case of akrasia I know.
