I am writing a paper on the epistemic status of the Church–Turing thesis, and before I settle on a reading of it I want the specification laid out. Give me the desiderata that any analysis of the pre-formal notion of effective computability—what a person could compute by rote given unbounded time and scratch paper, without insight or ingenuity—must satisfy in order to count as an adequate analysis. For each requirement, supply the case or consideration that motivates it: the Ackermann function, which is intuitively computable yet not primitive recursive, and what that rules out; the Busy Beaver function, which the analysis must exclude as non-computable; the convergence of the Turing machine, the lambda calculus, the recursive functions, and register machines, and whether that convergence is merely evidence for an analysis or a requirement imposed on one; the insistence that the analysis not simply define computability as computability-by-Turing-machine, on pain of triviality; the demand that the analysis state what kind of claim the thesis is and what could justify it, given that the pre-formal notion admits no proof; and the question whether the thesis constrains real physical computing devices or only an idealized mathematical notion, together with what it must say about analog machines of unbounded real-valued precision and other would-be hypercomputers.

Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular camp. Then tell me which requirements pull against one another. I expect the pair I care about most—the analysis must be answerable to the informal notion, and must not presuppose a machine model—to drive the thesis toward either triviality or unprovability; I want to know whether the remaining requirements can still be met once that pair is fixed, and whether the physical-scope requirement and the requirement that the thesis underwrite the unsolvability results are even compatible with each other. Close with a short verdict on the three standard construals—as a definition or convention, as an empirical hypothesis, and as conceptual analysis—judged against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria; the verdict can be terse.
