Ok so I've talked myself into the propensity interpretation of probability and I'd like you to try to talk me back out of it. Not the watered-down version where "propensity" is just a posh name for a stable long-run frequency, but the real thing: a chance is a disposition, an irreducible tendency of a concrete setup (the coin, the radium atom, the whole experimental arrangement) to produce outcomes, and it's the disposition that grounds and explains the frequencies rather than being defined by them. I'm even tempted by the single-case reading — this atom has a tendency to decay in this interval, full stop, not "if you ran it a million times" — and by the further idea that the propensity isn't a monadic property of the setup at all but a relation between the setup and a chance event description.

What I want is a proper map of what would count against this, ranked from fatal down to merely awkward, and with an eye to which of these a committed propensity theorist would themselves concede versus just wave away. Don't just name the usual suspects, play them out:

- the reference class problem — which setup is the "right" one, and is it actually worse for me than for a frequentist, or does it just relocate?
- the inversion worry — if propensities really are probabilities they have to satisfy the probability calculus including Bayes' theorem, so inverting a conditional should give you another probability; but causation doesn't run backwards, and there doesn't seem to be any such thing as the propensity of the cause given the effect, so does that break the identification of propensities with probabilities?
- the deeper one — why should a disposition to produce frequencies obey the axioms of probability at all? What's supposed to make it do that, and if the answer is "by definition" then hasn't the view become stipulation?

And the bit I actualy care about: has this been qualified into unfalsifiability? If every awkward case gets absorbed by saying "ah but the propensity is relative to the setup" or "thats a long-run not a single-case propensity", I want you to say so plainly and then tell me honestly whether that insulation is fatal or just the normal price of any metaphysical view. Also, what do the rivals — frequentism and subjectivism — predict or accommodate here that I don't? If they cover everything I cover plus more, that counts against me and I want it said.

End with a single last line that is just \answer{...} containing either the label of the one consideration you'd rank most decisive against the view, or the word insulated if nothing actually bites.
