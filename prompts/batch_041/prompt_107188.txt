I retired from a career in geology a couple of years ago and have been reading philosophy ever since — mostly ethics — and I've gotten a bit obsessed with Ben Franklins moral perfection project, the one in his autobiography where he lists thirteen virtues, keeps a little book with a line of marks for each day, and works on one virtue a week until the whole set has come round. I don't want to read about it, I want to actually run it. I'm 66, I have maybe half an hour at the end of the day, I dont want an app, and my wife will make fun of me if I start scoring myself at the dinner table.

So please turn his method into a procedure I can follow: daily steps, a weekly cycle, what counts as a fault and how to mark one, and what to do when two virtues pull against each other on the same day (frugality against justice, silence against sincerity, and his order virtue seems to demand a kind of scheduling that isn't compatible with having grandchildren). He apparently never quite got the order one — the procedure needs to say what to do about a virtue I keep failing, as opposed to a virtue I'm just not bothering with. His humility entry seems to eat itself too, since by his own account he couldn't help being proud of how humble he'd gotten; I want the procedure to be honest about that instead of papering over it. And I think he added a virtue at a friend's suggestion, so the procedure should say when and how I'm allowed to change the list.

Then run it on two or three sample days or weeks, including one where it tells me something I won't want to hear. Be upfront about the point where this stops being a moral method and becomes bookkeeping — I have a suspicion the chart starts measuring how good I am at keeping charts.

I know a bit of Aristotle on habituation but not much else here, so wherever you're relying on what Franklin actually wrote versus what philosophers have argued about this kind of project (whether virtue can be scored at all, self-report, character versus behaviour), keep those apart and don't hand me a contested reading as if it were the settled one.

Structure it like this:
- "What the method actually commits me to" — about 100 words

- "The hard cases" — the conflicts, the repeat failures, the pride loop, changing the list — about 250 words
