My flatmate's doing an econ PhD and we ended up going at it until 2am about whether there are things that just shouldn't be for sale, and I came out of it with a list of gut calls I can't get to line up. Stuff I'm fine with: selling plasma for cash, getting paid to donate eggs, scalping concert tickets, paying a line-sitter to hold your spot at the DMV, paying someone to carry an ad on their car, and — my flatmate says this is the one that sinks me — paying a little person to get thrown onto a mattress in a bar, or paying two guys to punch each other's heads in for a purse, as long as the money's real and nobody's twisting anyone's arm. Stuff I'm not fine with: selling a kidney, even to a rich guy who'd pay life-changing money and isn't being coerced; the state legislator who floated knocking four years off a sentence in exchange for a kidney donation; paying someone to take the fall and serve your jail time; buying a baby; selling your vote. Then there's a bunch where I'm firm but the firmness feels weird. Charging $20 for a case of water in a flooded town is wrong even though it'd get water trucked in faster, but doubling the price of a concert ticket is fine. Letting a rich guy buy a nicer cell is wrong, letting him buy a nicer seat on the plane is obviously fine. Paying a student cash for A's is wrong, paying a kid to read books is fine. Paid surrogacy bugs me but paid egg donation doesn't, and I genuinely can't say why except that nine months inside someone is different from a needle. And the one that really gets me: a firm that buys up every visa-appointment slot and every permit slot and resells them at ten times the price is vile, but a person standing in line for you for $40 is fine — those look like the same thing with different furniture.

The best principle I can come up with is something like: a thing shouldn't be for sale when putting a price on it wrecks what the thing is — a vote stops being a vote, a kidney stops being a part of you, a baby stops being a person — and desperation makes it worse even when the paperwork is clean. But that tells me the dwarf-tossing thing is out, and I say it's in, and it doesn't touch the eggs-versus-surrogacy gap at all. So: is there a principle, or a small set of them, that gets my calls, or close? What does it say about the ones I haven't thought about — the charity that pays addicts a few hundred bucks to get sterilized, the auction where someone pays a fortune for the right to shoot an old non-breeding rhino with the money going to anti-poaching, a company that pays a stranger to go vegan so you can keep eating steak? And be honest about which of my calls I'd have to give up, because I'd rather have a clean principle and loose one or two verdicts than keep every verdict with a rule that's just a list. Tell me straight where the weak joint is — where the reasoning is most likely to break, and what case or argument would actually change your mind about the principle you hand me.
