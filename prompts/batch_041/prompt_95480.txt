I'm putting together a unit on sortition for my political theory course and I want it to be a design exercise rather than a yes/no debate. So here's the setup I'll hand my students: a city of roughly 400,000 replaces its elected council with 120 people chosen by lot — staggered three-year terms, noone serves twice, no parties, and the body can only amend or veto what the mayor proposes instead of initiating anything itself. A professional staff, hired by open competion, sets the agenda. What are the most important implications of a design like that for the things we actually want out of a legislature — accountability, competence, protection of minorities, resistance to capture — and which of the usual arguments for sortition quietly stop working once the details are fixed this way? I'm less interested in a general verdict on lotteries than in the implications that would change how I'd redesign it. And tell me what would change your mind: which argument or finding would flip your read, and where your reasoning is most likely to go wrong.
