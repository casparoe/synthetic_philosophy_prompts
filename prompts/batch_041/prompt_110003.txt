Bit of a favour to ask before term starts.

I'm giving a paper at the joint social theory workshop in October and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The audience is awkward in a very particualr way: a solid block of critical realists — the emergent-powers, structures-as-generative-mechanisms crowd — a couple of analytical sociology people for whom 'mechanism' and 'micro-foundations' are the only words that matter, three micro theorists from the economics side who are card-carrying methodological individualists and will be almost apologetic about it, and two comparative historians who think the whole debate is a verbal dispute and will say so in the pub afterwards.

What I'm defending, roughly: social structures — class positions, institutions, norms, network topology — are real and irreducible; you can't paraphrase talk about them into talk about individual beliefs and desires without losing explanatory content. That's the anti-reductionist half. But it buys me no methodological holism whatsoever. To explain how a structure does anything, you have to be able to say what it does to the people inside it: how it fixes their positions, what it makes available to them, what it makes rational or unthinkable for them. If you can't say that, you haven't explained the phenomenon, you've just renamed it. Call the two halves no-free-standing-structure and no-bare-structure-explanations if that helps. The bit I actually want to push is that structural facts don't compete with individual action as rival causes — they're what gives the individuals their reasons in the first place, so the causal exclusion worry is a misfire aimed at a picture nobody has to hold. Both camps will think I've given away the farm to the other one.

So: the eight or ten objections I'll actually get, in the order they'll come, phrased the way that person would phrase it rather than the way I would, each with how much damage it does, what I should concede up front, what I should hold, a two-minute reply, and a note on where the reply is softest. Include the question I'm praying nobody asks. And please get into the weeds — supervenience, constitutive versus causal explanation, mechanisms, levels, the lot — not the law-and-explanation stuff I could do in my sleep.

British English throughout, please, I'm circulating it to people who will notice.
