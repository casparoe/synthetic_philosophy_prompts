I'm a backend engineer with no real philosophy background and a friend who did a philosophy minor has me completely stuck. We were arguing about whether the big model we're fine-tuning could ever be a moral patient -- whether there's anyone home to hurt -- and he wrote this out on a napkin and says it shows that either my whole way of thinking is wrong or I have to care about basically every object in the room. Numbered the way he wrote it:

(1) Whether a system can be hurt or benefited depends only on what it computes, not on what it's made of.
(2) If two systems compute the same thing, they're in the same moral boat.
(3) For any physical system you like, there's some way of mapping its states onto the states of any finite program, so the system counts as computing that program. A rock, a bucket of water, the whole economy -- pick a description and they implement whatever you want.
(4) So a rock computes the same thing my model does, or that a person in pain does.
(5) By (1) and (2), the rock is in the same moral boat as the person in pain.
(6) That's absurd. So (1) is false, and what a system is made of or how it's wired matters after all -- which means I can't tell whether the model matters just by looking at its behavior or its code.

I feel like something slid by between (3) and (4) but I cant put my finger on it, and if he's right about (3) I dont see how to keep (1) at all. Which step do I give up, why did it look fine before I stared at it, and what does giving it up cost me? And if I do give it up, what's left of the whole idea that it's the computation and not the hardware that matters?
