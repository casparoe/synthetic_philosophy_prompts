I run a small philosophy of physics reading group and next week we're doing the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. I want a decision tree I can hand out that starts someone at "yeah, I accept special relativity, including the relativity of simultaneity" and walks them down to a named position on the block universe. Please don't just list the usual -isms alphabetically, build the tree out of the arguments own moving parts so the branch nodes are the premises you'd have to give up. Nodes I have in mind, phrased as questions someone can answer without already knowing where they land:

- do you treat the idea that an event is real relative to a given observer as the fundamental notion, or do you think there's a frame-independent fact about what exists?
- is reality closed under chaining these relations — if A comes out real relative to B and B relative to C, does A have to come out real relative to C, or can that chain break?
- is the relativity of simultaneity fundamental, or are you allowed to posit a Lorentzian ether with a privileged frame that just happens to be undetectable?
- does objective becoming need a global present, or could "now" be something local, like the here-and-now's own spacelike slice?
- and do you read the argument as a proof, as a reductio of one of its own premises, or as a deflationary lesson that existence-talk is doing less work than we thought?

Leaves should be named positions: eternalism/block, presentism, growing block, moving spotlight, a neo-Lorentzian privileged-frame view, the Stein-style becoming-without-a-privileged-present option, and the indexical-now line where "now" works like "here" so there's no leftover puzzle about the future being real. If your tree genuinely can't separate two of those, flag it rather than inventing a fake distinction. Also mark any answer-combinations that nobody occupies and say whether thats because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored.

Seven or eight leaves is plenty. Keep the node questions concrete enough that a keen undergrad who's done a term of SR can answr them, and no citations please, it's just a handout.
