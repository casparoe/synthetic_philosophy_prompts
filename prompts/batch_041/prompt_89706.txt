So I work at a small city museum — local history, three rooms, a leaking skylight — and our new director has decided to rehang the entire permanent collection as one corridor you walk through toward the present. Every case gets a placard about what it contributed to us, and the last stop, definately, is the gift shop, which she wants labelled "The Present: You Are Here." I keep calling it the Long Corridor, and it is the purest case of Whig history I've ever seen in the wild, except that she thinks that's a compliment. Before I either quit or make my peace with it, I want to write the audio-guide script for the whole hall as a parody — deadpan, no winking, as if a real museum commissioned it and nobody there found it funny.

Pick something deliberately unglamorous for the exhibit to be about, so the joke has something to bite on — the parking meter, the pedestrian crossing, the office chair, the traffic cone, whatever you like — and make the corridor tell the history of the world as the story of how that thing finally arrived. Requirements, from my months of taking notes on the director's taste: every era has to be introduced only by what it handed forward, never by what it was like to live in; one person gets praised, straight-faced, for being ahead of their time, with the compliment being something they could not possibly have meant; one century gets scolded for failing to produce something it had no way to produce; the people who lost, or whose road didn't lead here, get a line each, as obstacles or as charming detours; and at some point the guide should boast about how much history it has abridged so visitors don't have to walk through the parts that didn't matter. It should never once admit that reading the past this way is a choice — no moral at the end, no narrator cracking, and no use of the words "Whig" or "historiography" inside the script itself. It should be funny to someone who's read about the Whig interpretation of history and still fine for a visitor who only wants to know where the toilets are. The one thing I genuinely can't solve is the ending, since the corridor stops at now and there is no next room: the guide should handle that the way a real one would, which is badly — it can gesture at a future stop that hasn't been built yet, or just send you back to the entrance.

Around 700 words, formatted as a numbered sequence of stops with the usual housekeeping lines a real audio guide has (welcome, please don't touch the cases, exits at the rear, that kind of thing). Just start with the script itself, no preface or notes back to me about what your doing.
