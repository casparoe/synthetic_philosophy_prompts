ok, I need help with an argument I half-built myself and now can't get out of.

Quick background: people worry about a "responsibility gap" around autonomous systems. The worry is that once a system learns its own policy -- a drone that picks its own targets, a trading bot doing whatever it does, a car deciding in the last second -- the actual bad thing comes out of a decision procedure no human wrote, can't retrace, and can't override in time. So you look for the responsible human and there isn't one, and you look at the machine and it isn't a moral agent. Here's my version:

(1) A person is to blame for a harm only if they could have done otherwise -- they had real control over whether it happened and knew enough to exercise that control.
(2) In the machine case, no human could have done otherwise with respect to the specific harm. The engineers never wrote a rule that said to hit the stroller; the thing grew that rule, and nobody could have seen it coming or stopped it in the moment.
(3) So no human is to blame for the harm. (from 1, 2)
(4) The system isn't to blame either -- it doesn't have the kind of will that can be blamed.
(5) So there's nobody. That's the gap.
(6) Now here's the part bugging me. Run the same argument on me. Whatever it is in my head that produces my choices, I didn't write it, I can't retrace it, and I can't override it in real time -- by the time I notice a choice, its pretty much made. And I didn't pick my temperament, my genes, or the family that taught me what to care about.
(7) So no human could have done otherwise with respect to anything they do.
(8) So nobody is ever to blame for anything.
(9) And that's clearly false -- if it were true, holding people accountable would be pointless, and I don't believe it's pointless.

I want to keep (1); control seems like the whole ballgame. And I want to reject (8). A friend says the fix is that we blame people for the risks they take and the stuff they should have found out, not for outcomes they fully controlled. But that blows up in my face, because the drone engineers also took a risk and also should have found something out, so if that's enough to blame them then the gap was never there and the whole autonomous-weapons panic is silly. So either the argument works or it proves too much in the other direction.

Which numbered step is doing something it shouldn't, and what do I pay if I give it up? Is (6) just the free will problem wearing a robot costume, or is there a real disanalogy in there somewhere? I'd rather keep (1) than (6) if I have to choose.
