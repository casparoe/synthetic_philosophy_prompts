I am a retired physiologist — thirty years on nociception in cephalopods — who has spent the last two years working through early modern philosophy with a small reading group. Please answer as a historian of philosophy who works on Descartes' theory of mind and on his physiology, and give me your considered judgment rather than a survey of the options. Before committing, set out on paper the candidate principles that might underlie the verdicts below; then choose one and defend it.

The verdicts, as I have assembled them from Descartes' own discussions (chiefly the fifth part of the Discourse on Method and the passages where he elaborates it):

- adult human beings: have minds;
- a person deaf and mute from birth, who invents signs to make himself understood: has a mind;
- a magpie or parrot trained to utter words: no mind — words, but not speech, and nothing to show that it means anything by them;
- an ape or monkey, whose organs would allow speech but which says nothing: no mind;
- the moving statues and fountains in the royal gardens: no mind, and no one is tempted to think otherwise;
- a machine with the outward shape of a monkey: we could not tell it from the real animal;
- a dog that yelps, cringes, and avoids the stick: no thought. Whether he allowed it any feeling at all is the part I cannot settle — some passages read as if he granted a bodily sensation without thought, others as if the yelp were no more than the play of parts.

The new case. A colleague has spent the winter with a contemporary language model and has shown me the transcripts: it answers novel questions, keeps hold of an argument across a long exchange, adapts its register to its interlocutor, and never repeats itself verbatim. It is, in the ordinary sense, a machine — silicon, weights, arithmetic.

What would Descartes say about it, and what would that commit him to? Tell me which principles fit the verdicts above, what each predicts about the machine, which one you think he is actually running and why, and which further case — something I could describe to my reading group, not something I would have to go and read first — would separate the surviving principles from one another. Be definite: if the most defensible reconstruction forces him to grant a mind to a machine, or to deny feeling to a creature that behaves exactly as if it suffered, say so plainly instead of softening it. Where you are genuinely torn, tell me which way you would bet and what would change your mind.
