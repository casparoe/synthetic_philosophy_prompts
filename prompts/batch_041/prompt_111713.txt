One of the other board members, whos in the econ PhD program with me, is pushing hard for us to decide this with a futarchy-style market: run two conditional markets, one on "meals delivered if we do A" and one on "meals delivered if we do B," and commit in advance to picking whichever one prices higher. He wants the commitment to be binding.

I have to circulate the memo before the vote, and I'm the one who has to say yes or no to the market part — plus recommend something on A vs B either way, since we vote on that regardless. Can you write it? Clear recommendation with a timeline (something like "advisory-only trial for 30 days, then vote," or "don't do this, here's why"), and please take his proposal seriously instead of dunking on it.

Stuff I don't want hand-waved:

4) Is a higher price actually a reason to act, or just a forecast? I can't tell if that's a real distinction or me being precious about it.
5) If we do say no, what should we do instead? Secret ballot on the metric up front? A pre-registered threshold?
