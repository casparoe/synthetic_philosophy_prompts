I've been reading about epiphenomenalism and its messed me up a bit. The way I understand it: the physical stuff in my head does all the causing, and the feel of things -- the ache in my knee, the taste of salt, the red of a postbox -- just comes along for the ride and causes nothing at all. Fine, ok. But then ten minutes ago I said out loud, to nobody, "my knee really hurts". Where did that sentence come from? If the hurting can't cause anything then it didn't cause the sentence, something physical did, and it's either a miracle or an enormous coincidence that the words and the feeling match up. My mate says that just shows I'm reporting the brain state rather than the feeling, and I said right, but then how would I ever find out there was a feeling there in the first place.

Can you write me a poem about this. A pantoum, please -- four-line stanzas where lines 2 and 4 come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next stanza, and the last stanza folds back into the first so the poem ends where it began. I want that loop doing the work: the argument keeps arriving back at the same place. Also, two of the repeated lines should be things I could say and that my zombie twin -- same goings-on in the head, nothing going on inside -- could say too, word for word, so that the poem can't tell which of us is talking. Keep the philosophy honest, i.e. don't let the poem cheat by quietly assuming the feeling does cause something, and don't resolve it at the end. Written as if by me, in bed, at 2am. British English please.
