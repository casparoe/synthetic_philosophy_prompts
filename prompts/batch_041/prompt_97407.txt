I am preparing a handout for a graduate seminar in philosophy of mind on the knowledge argument, and I want a decision tree that takes a student from the bare intuition that Mary learns something on her release from the black-and-white room to a named position in the debate. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of authors: first the questions, then the positions located at the leaves.

Each interior node should be a single yes-or-no question about the case, or about what the student takes the case to show, phrased so that it can be answered without knowing where it leads. Use at least the following distinctions, and add whatever else the space requires: whether anything is learned at all; if so, whether what is learned is propositional knowledge, an ability, or acquaintance with a property; whether the proposition learned is a new fact or an old fact grasped under a new concept; whether, if it is a new fact, the fact concerns a physical property, a non-physical one, or a property that is physical but not exhausted by its structural description; whether the knowledge is indexical or self-locating rather than about colour as such; and whether the student trusts the intuition at all.

The leaves should include the ability hypothesis; the acquaintance hypothesis; the self-location or indexical account; the phenomenal-concept strategy, on which physicalism survives because the new knowledge involves a distinctive class of concepts; a priori physicalism, on which Mary could in principle have deduced the fact and the intuition is simply false; representationalism, on which she gains a new representation of an already familiar physical property; the new-fact dualist view; the Russellian monist or primitivist view, on which she becomes acquainted with an intrinsic property that physics describes only structurally; and the deflationary or illusionist view, on which the intuition is a cognitive illusion, a leaf that should note that the thought experiment's author eventually came to think the argument it launched was unsound.

Two constraints. First, the tree must not pretend to separate leaves that the debate cannot separate: where two positions answer every node identically, say so in a note rather than inventing a node to prise them apart. Second, for each combination of answers that nobody occupies, say briefly whether it is empty because it is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or merely because nobody has bothered with it.

Use concrete cases rather than abstractions wherever you can: Mary's release; a variant in which her colour vision returns gradually over a week rather than all at once; a variant in which she is a colour-blind scientist with prosthetic colour vision that she can switch on at will; and a variant in which she has memorised every physical fact but cannot imagine any experience she has not had. No citations and no reading list; refer to positions descriptively, and give each leaf one sentence naming its most awkward commitment.
