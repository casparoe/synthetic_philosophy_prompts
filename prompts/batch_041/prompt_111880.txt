I'm a fourth-year PhD student in political theory, working on compromise, and there's a pattern I keep running into in seminars and in my own life that I cant find a proper handle on. Two people who disagree about what's worth doing make a deal where each does something the other wants, and the striking bit is that each of them ends up doing something they think is wrong. My flatmate thinks eating meat is fine and that animal charities are a waste; I think eating meat is a minor wrong and that animal welfare matters a lot. So we agreed: I eat meat on Fridays, she sends £10 a month to an animal charity. By my lights the world got better, by hers it got better too. I did something I think is wrong, deliberately, as part of a deal — and I don't feel bought off, which strikes me as a warning sign I can't articulate.

The case I really can't place is when what we're each giving up isn't just distasteful to us but actually harms people who aren't in the room. Swap the meat for a vote: I back a bill that hurts a group I care about, she backs one that hurts a group she cares about, and we each think the total is better than the alternative. Now the gains come out of third parties' pockets and none of them agreed to anything. Are these one phenomenon or two? When I ask around, people say it's just logrolling or just bribery, and neither fits — nobody's deceived and nothing's being bought in the ordinary sense. I don't want a label so much as the carving: what the nearest concepts are, where each one stops fitting, and what would have to be true for the second case to be its own seperate thing. If nothing covers it, coin a term and tell me where its edges are.
