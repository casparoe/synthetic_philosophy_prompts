I'm in a small reading group that's been working through the Maxwell's demon literature for about a year now -- we started with the standard second-law framing and then wandered into the information-theoretic stuff -- and we've hit a wall that I think is more interesting than the physics. Roughly: one camp (Landauer, Bennett, the reversible-computing people) says the demon is fully exorcised, because any physical demon needs it's own memory, any memory eventually has to be reset to a standard state, and resetting a bit costs at least kT ln2, which exactly pays for the entropy the demon appeared to create. The other camp (Earman and Norton, and Norton has been pushing this line for years since) says that's either circular -- you assume the second law to get the erasure cost, then use the erasure cost to save the second law -- or just misplaced: the demon's real problem is that its own microscopic degrees of freedom thermalize with the gas, and that's the end of it, no information theory required. Meanwhile there are 2011 results (Vaccaro and Barnett, I think) showing you can erase a bit with no energy cost at all if you pay the entropy bill in some other conserved quantity, and actual single-bit erasure experiments that measured something like the Landauer bound, which the first camp treats as confirmation and the second camp treats as beside the point. Nobody in the group has moved in months and I suspect we're not even disagreeing about the same thing anymore.

To make it worse, my friend Mira (condensed matter, impatient with philosophy) proposed a demon she calls the lazy demon: it never stores anything, it just watches for a fast molecule and opens the flap the instant it sees one, then immediately forgets. No memory register, no erasure step, no kT ln2 anywhere in the story. My instinct is that this is the erasure step wearing a disguise -- if it truely forgets, something got reset, and if nothing got reset then the record is still lying around in some correlation and you've just moved the entropy rather than removed it. But Mira says that's exactly the kind of bookkeeping move that convinces her the whole exorcism is a convention rather than a result.

What I want from you is a diagnosis of the disagreement itself, not a physics lecture and not a verdict on who's right: is this a verbal dispute about what "entropy" is being used to mean (the Gibbs/Shannon thing vs the Clausius thing) and about what belongs inside the ledger; is it a substantive physical disagreement that some experiment or theorem could settle; or are the two sides answering different questions under one name -- one about the demon proper, one about the demon-plus-its-memory-plus-the-reservoir-it-dumps-into, individuated differently from the start? I have a hypothesis, that they individuate the system differently and everything else is downstream of that, but test it against what each side actually says rather than just agreeing with me. Then tell me honestly what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a derivation of Landauer's principle from something that doesn't already assume the second law, a clean no-go theorem, a better experiment, or a conceptual distinction (say, between two kinds of forgetting) that makes the question dissolve -- and if the honest answer is that nothing would move it, say that and explain why. I'd also like a short verdict on whether the lazy demon is a genuinely new case or the old one in disguise, since that's the specific thing eating our group. End with a final line in the form \answer{...} containing exactly one of: verbal, hidden-factual, different-questions, ill-posed, bedrock-intuitions.
