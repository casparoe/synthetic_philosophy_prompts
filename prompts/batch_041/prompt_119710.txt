Write an essay on how the colour phi phenomenon should be interpreted. In the standard demonstration, a coloured dot is flashed at one location, a differently coloured dot is flashed shortly afterwards at another, and viewers report seeing a single dot travel across the gap and change colour abruptly around the midpoint of its path. One reading — call it Stalinesque — holds that the content of experience is itself edited retrospectively, so that the viewer genuinely experiences the colour change mid-trajectory; the rival Orwellian reading holds that experience is never edited, only the memory or the report of it, the viewer having in fact experienced two discrete flashes. Now suppose a researcher proposes a variant in which participants make no verbal or manual response at all: their gaze is recorded while they watch, on the assumption — which you are free to challenge — that the eyes can be induced to pursue the interpolated trajectory, so that the oculomotor record might be read as a running trace of what was experienced from moment to moment. A second variant places a brief tone at various points in the blank interval and asks participants to judge whether the tone and the colour change were simultaneous. Do such variants break the symmetry between the two readings, or do they merely relocate the revision? Weigh the arguments on both sides, then reach and defend a verdict: either that one reading is better supported by the phenomenon and its variants, or that the distinction, as posed, is not the sort of thing any possible experiment could settle. Please write in British English.
