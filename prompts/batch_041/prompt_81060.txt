For the past two terms a friend and I have been re-fighting the same argument about mental causation, and our reading group has finally asked us to produce a document instead of another round of talking past each other. He is what I would call a closure-first exclusionist: he treats the causal closure of physics as a fixed constraint, accepts that mental properties supervene on physical ones, and concludes that the mental properties themselves do no causal work — whatever makes a difference to behaviour is the physical realizer, and the mental is at best a shadow that travels alongside it. I am a difference-making interventionist: I think a causal claim is a claim about what happens under an intervention on a variable, that mental properties are perfectly good variables, and that the exclusion argument looks compelling mainly because it slides between two readings of closure. What I want from you is the joint statement we should both be able to put our names to: first the claims we already share about the machinery (supervenience, closure, that neither of us is an eliminativist, that the exclusion argument is valid as a piece of reasoning and the fight is over its soundness, the difference between causal relevance and causal efficacy, the status of multiple realizability); then the exact points where we part, each stated so that the person who holds it would sign off on how it is put rather than on a strawman; then the cruxes, the sub-questions such that an answer would actually move one of us.

Two things I want the document to handle properly. The first is a distinction I have come to think is doing a lot of hidden work: whether the exclusionist's conclusion is supposed to be impotence (mental properties do nothing at all) or redundancy (they do something, but it is always already done by the realizer, so any mental cause is a pre-empted one). Those come apart, and I suspect my friend has been running them together for months. The second is that for each crux I want the case or argument we would both agree bears on it, with our predictions recorded in advance — in particular, whether there is a case on the physical side where a causal claim is itself only true relative to a chosen set of variables, since if there is, my relativism is not a special concession I am making about the mental. Say also, for each crux, what each of us would count as evidence against his own view and not merely against the other's. If you think part of our fight is verbal — that we are using cause or realizer in two senses and could stipulate our way out — say which part and why, and what would be lost by stipulating.

Lay it out as a numbered section of agreed claims followed by a disagreements table with one row per crux. And please do not just lay the two sides out even-handedly and leave me to choose: commit to a verdict on the central crux, tell me which of us you think has the better of it and why, and say where you would expect the debate to end up if it were pursued honestly rather than rhetorically.
