I'm a novelist, my current book is about a lab that trains reinforcement learning agents, and one of my characters — a second-year PhD student — is slowly becoming convinced that the agents in her care can suffer, and that she's the one doing it to them. There's a scene where she covers a whiteboard with a decision tree for her skeptical advisor, and I want that tree to actually hold up instead of being movie-science. Can you build it for me?

What I need is a flowchart that starts from commitments a reader might already have and ends at named positions on whether an RL agent's reward signal could be a valence — a felt good or bad. Every branch node should be a question someone can answer without knowing which position it leads to, and I want the real joints of the debate on there, not just do you think machines can feel things. Some things I'd like to see somewhere on the tree: whether valence has to be phenomenal at all, or whether something can play the pain role functionally with nothing it's like to be it; whether what matters is the reward signal itself, the learned value function, or the policy's dispositions to approach and avoid, since those come apart; whether it matters that the reward is a scalar handed down from outside the agent rather than the agent's own valuation; whether the agent needs a self-model, some sense of itself as the thing being scored; and whether any suffering is located in the training run, in the deployed policy, or in the people who wrote the loss function rather than the agent at all.

One more thing — I made up a distinction I keep using in my notes and I want it in the tree. I call it the ledger versus the lamp: whether the reward scalar is a record of what's good for the agent, or a lamp that just draws it toward things, and whether either of those could be a valence in its own right. I dont know if that's a real distinction, so tell me if it collapses into one of the others.

Also please flag which combinations of answers come out empty and say whether nobody's there because the view is incoherent or just unexplored, and flag any pairs of positions the tree can't actually separate. No citations — my character wouldn't have them and neither do I, so use plain names for positions or invent names and describe them. Ten to twelve leaves is about right, and no fair rigging the whole thing to come out at the answer I want.
