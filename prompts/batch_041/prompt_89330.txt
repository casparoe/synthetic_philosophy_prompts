I've been going down a rabbit hole on speech acts lately -- the whole idea that some things you say don't describe anything, they just do something -- and I've ended up with a pile of gut reactions that I can't get to sit under one rule. I know the general shape of the literature here, Austin, Searle, all that, and I'm not asking for a summary of it or a reading list. What I want is the rule. Here's my pile:

- A guest at a wedding with no grounds and no standing shouts 'I object!' The officiant pauses, looks annoyed, and carries on. I think he objected. He really did object, even though it changed nothing. But if he shouts the same thing at a wedding on TV, he didn't object to anything, he just yelled.

- My friend says 'I promise I'll pay you back Friday,' knowing perfectly well he won't. I think that's a promise. A bad one, maybe the worst kind, but a promise, not a non-promise that happens to be a lie.

- Same friend says 'I forgive you' about something, and I know he's still furious and will bring it up at Christmas for the next decade. I don't think he's forgiven me. He said the words, and the forgiving didn't happen.

Those last two are the pair I can't reconcile, by the way.

- A judge whose appointment turns out to have been invalid all along sentences someone to five years. They're taken away, serve three, then somebody catches it. I think the sentencing happened -- that's why there's something to undo. Not lawful, but not nothing either.

- Someone apologizes because a court ordered them to, reads it off a card, no remorse whatsoever. I don't think that's an apology at all. It's a performance of one.

- Same person, sincerely sorry, says it out loud to a photo of the person who died. I think that's an apology. It just can't be received.

- I say 'I promise I'll help you move' while rehearsing a play. Not a promise. I say it in a rehearsal but I mean it for real this time -- now I'm stuck.

- A friend with a mail-order ordination off some website performs a wedding. The couple mean it, the guests treat them as married, no state and no church recognises it. I want to say they aren't married. But if a couple lives as married in a community for twenty years and nothing official ever happened, I want to say they are. Which makes me think the words at the moment aren't doing the work, the community afterwards is, and that's a different kind of theory than the one I started with.

- 'I bet you fifty bucks,' said laughing, clearly joking, and I say 'you're on,' meaning it. I think there's a bet. If he'd said it to an empty room, no bet.

- I promise my dying father I'll look after mum. I don't think that's a promise. It's a vow, a resolution. Nobody's there to hold me to it.

The principle I keep reaching for is something like: an utterance is the act it looks like if it's said seriously, in the right kind of setting, by someone with standing to say it, and it's understood as such by whoever it's aimed at -- and how I felt inside doesn't matter, except for acts whose entire point is to be in some inner state. That would explain why a lying promise is still a promise but a hollow 'I forgive you' isn't forgiveness. But it doesn't explain why a coerced apology is a non-apology while a coerced promise still seems like a promise to me, and I can't tell whether standing is really doing the work in the wedding cases or whether it's just the community's response, and I'm not even sure my 'has to be understood' clause is right given the dead-father one.

So: give me the principle, or two or three rivals, stated precisely enough that I can check them against new cases. Tell me what each clause is buying me and which case motivates it. Tell me which of my verdicts it forces me to give up, honestly, don't paper over it. And then give me your own verdicts on cases I haven't listed -- a doctor saying 'you'll be fine' when they think you won't, a landlord saying 'you're evicted' with no legal standing, a promise made at gunpoint, someone saying 'I'll pray for you' and never doing it, whatever else your principle strains on. The case I keep flip-flopping on: I text my boss 'I quit' and then immediately text 'jk.' Did I quit?
