I'm a fourth-year PhD student in judgement and decision making, and my prospectus draft is due to my committee in three weeks. I have a half formed idea and I need you to turn it into something that reads like an actual proposal. I've been chewing on this for months and I keep going back and forth on wether it's a real contribution or just me being annoyed at my own field.

Here's the idea. Everything I've read on herding and cascades models the thing that cascades as a choice: someone buys, adopts, votes yes, the next person sees that and rationally discounts their own private signal, and you get the familiar result that the cascade is fragile — release a bit of public information and everyone snaps back to their own evidence. Fine. But the settings I actually care about — labs, replication attempts, safety reviews, internal audits, post-mortems, the way a whole subfield quietly stops checking some assumption — the thing that cascades isnt the choice, it's the absence of one. Nobody adopts anything. Two or three people decline to run the test, declining becomes the default, and pretty soon nobody experiences it as a decision at all. The information that would have been generated never gets generated, so there's nothing for the cascade to later be wrong about, and the standard debunking move (release the public signal, watch everyone update) has nothing to bite on, because there's no action to reverse. It's a cascade you can't see and can't debunk.

My hunch is that this is a different animal, and that the usual model can't even represent it, because in the usual setup "don't investigate" is just one more action in the menu. Make "keep investigating" a costly option that also has option value, though, and I think you can get equilibria where investigation shuts down after two people and the shutdown is self-sealing, alongside equilibria where everyone investigates forever and burns everyone's time. But I genuinely don't know whether the shutdown is a cascade in any technical sense or just a fixed point, and whether it's welfare-relevant in a different way than a normal cascade or just a normal cascade with the labels switched. That's what I want the proposal to make precise.

Lay out what I'd actually do and in what order over a two-year dissertation, including what the minimal model would have to show to be worth writing down at all and what the experiment (if there is one) would need to be able to distinguish. Say what a real result looks like versus what would just be me restating the model I started from. Give me a realistic read on scope.
