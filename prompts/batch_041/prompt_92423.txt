You are a philosopher of mathematics with a strong background in model theory and in the foundations of set theory. Here is the position I hold, and I want to know how exposed it is. Skolem's paradox is standardly treated as a harmless artifact of first-order formalization: a countable model of set theory can satisfy "there are uncountable sets," because the bijection that would witness the countability of those sets is simply not an element of the model. On this treatment, the apparent contradiction dissolves the moment we keep track of which language is speaking about which domain. I think that deflationary treatment is too quick. My view is that the paradox teaches us something about the concepts themselves: "uncountable" is not an absolute property that a collection possesses or lacks independently of the model, or more broadly of the background language and its interpretation, and there is no neutral standpoint from which the cardinality of a collection could be settled once and for all. In other words, I take the Skolemite conclusion, not the deflationary one.

I would like a map of what this view risks, not a survey of the literature. Specifically:

1. Which objections would be decisive against it, as opposed to merely awkward? Among the ones I already know of: the objection that the two models never disagree about one and the same domain, so the "relativity" is a relation between different domains rather than about any single thing, and is therefore benign; the objection that the quasi-categoricity of second-order set theory (any two models are isomorphic or one is an initial segment of the other) shows the relativity to be a feature of first-order semantics rather than of the concept of set; the objection that the view proves too much, since parallel reasoning would make "finite" relative and would thereby undercut the very theorem it rests on; and the objection that the Skolemite's own claim that the model "really is" countable can only be stated from an external standpoint the view says does not exist.
