Some months ago I joined a small research group, and I've been struck by how stable our disagreements are. We read the same literature, examine the same data, and argue in the same room, yet two of my colleagues have held opposite views about whether the effect we study is real for as long as I've known them, and neither has moved. I've gradually come to accept the following position: rational agents who begin from the same prior probabilities and condition on the same evidence cannot end up in a persistent disagreement that both parties know about; any such disagreement must be traceable either to differences in their priors or to information one of them has that the other lacks. I'll call this the common prior position.

I'd like you to tell me what would count against it. Rank the considerations by how decisive they would be if they obtained, distinguishing what would merely be awkward from what should force me to abandon the position, and include anything that has arguably already happened. I want to know what defenders of the position would themselves concede counts against them, not only what its critics say. And I want an honest verdict on whether the position has been insulated into unfalsifiability: since I can apparently explain away any disagreement by positing different priors, is there anything I would accept as evidence against the view, and if there isn't, is that a defect or a sign that I've misdescribed what kind of claim it is? Two further questions in the same vein. First, does the position survive only as a claim about ideally rational agents rather than about how anyone actually reasons, and if so, what does that cost it? Second, if two agents could share priors and evidence and yet still diverge because they interpret or weight that evidence differently in a way that isn't itself a prior, does that refute the position or merely relocate the disagreement?

Write the answer as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables -- the way one would write a long letter -- and lead with your bottom line.
