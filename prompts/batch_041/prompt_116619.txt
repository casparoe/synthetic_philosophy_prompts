I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and I'm doing a feature on the whole do-LLMs-understand-anything fight — the parrots vs. the people who think something real is going on in there. I'm tired of writing it up as a shouting match, so I want to run an imagined dialogue instead: two researchers, both sharp, both up on the literature, neither one a strawman, actually answering each other rather than trading monologues. Have each of them state the other's strongest point in their own words before they respond to it.

Ground rules to bake in: theres no disagreement between them about the empirical facts — how these systems are trained, what they can and can't do. Neither is claiming theres a little ghost in the machine. One is a functionalist: understanding is having the right internal organization and capacities, full stop, and the causal path that got you there is irrelevant. The other is coming from the Wittgenstein/Brandom line: understanding is a normative status, something you have by being a participant in a practice, being answerable for what you mean, and no amount of behavioral competence hands that to a system that isn't in the game.

Then have them fight over a thought experiment I've been kicking around, not the usual Chinese room. Call it the index-card model: it gives word-for-word the same answers as the LLM, but the answers come out of an enormous hand-built lookup table. The functionalist says it doesn't understand, it can't generalize, nothing in it is systematically related to anything else. The other one says fine, but the LLM's generalizing is itself just a disposition produced by a different causal route, so tell me what work the internal structure does that the table's doesn't, without begging the question. I want the rule-following thing in there too — any finite stretch of behavior fits infinitely many rules, so if that sinks the table it sinks us, unless you've already decided meaning isn't behavioral.

No citations, no reading list, I know the literature, I dont need alot of scaffolding. End with each of them saying what would actually move them, and be straight with me about where the argument bottoms out into rival intuitions.
