I'm an undergraduate in a course on formal epistemology and heuristic reasoning, and I have to write a short dialogue for seminar. The setting is two researchers trying to estimate, heuristically, how often a large random integer has two properties at once — say, having no prime factor below some small bound, and lying in a given residue class modulo some number — where one of them wants to treat the two conditions as independent and simply multiply their individual frequencies. This is the move I am calling the presumption of independence. Character A maintains that the presumption is a genuine principle of inductive reasoning that can be given a principled, even formal, justification; Character B maintains that it is at best a defeasible heuristic that quietly imports a choice of description and cannot be justified without circularity, and can point to cases (for instance, when the chosen residue class interacts with the small primes) where the presumed independence plainly fails. Write their dialogue. Make both equally sharp and well informed; each should answer the other's actual arguments rather than restate a position, and each should say where they think the burden of proof lies. Then give me your own committed verdict on who has the better of the exchange, and why, rather than just summarizing both sides.
