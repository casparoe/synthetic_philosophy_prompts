I retired from a physiology lab a few years back and have been reading moral philosophy since. I keep hitting a wall with hedonism. Take three things I'd call pleasure: the instant a stubborn proof finally clicks, the first mouthful of something you've been craving all day, and the warmth of lying in bed the morning after a fever breaks. As a scientist I'd want to know what state those share, and I cant find one. The philosophers move seems to be to say pleasure isn't a sensation at all but a kind of attitude toward an experience, which feels like it saves the label by quietly changing the subject. Could you explain the heterogeneity problem for hedonism properly: what exactly is supposed to be heterogeneous, what the main candidate answers are (a common feeling, a pro-attitude, a desire, something else), and whether the problem is fatal or just a problem for one bad formulation? I'm also wondering whether it spreads. In biology we're perfectly happy with 'disease' being a grab-bag category as long as there's some functional story tying it together; does that kind of move help the hedonist, or does it undercut the claim that all pleasure is one thing worth maximizing? And if the pleasures really are heterogeneous, what's the currency for adding them up -- how does the hedonist put the proof-clicking and the fever-warmth on the same scale? Please be upfront about where your own reasoning is shakiest and what would change your verdict.
