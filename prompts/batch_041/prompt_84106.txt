I'm a postdoc with three years, no co-authors, and I want to work on origin essentialism — the question of whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg. I already know the standard Kripke-flavoured stuff, so please dont give me a reading list or tell me who said what; I want a defended agenda. Which open questions in this area actually deserve the three years, ranked, and why? Candidates I'm chewing on: is the essentialism really about the two gametes themselves, or about the whole developmental origin story — and if it's the latter, does the uterine environment and maternal-fetal business get pulled in too, and where do you stop before it collapses into the environmental essentialism nobody wants? What do IVF, embryo splitting and preimplantation selection do to the intuition — if my parents had implanted the sibling embryo instead of me, is that a different person or is that me? And does any of it survive if you take seriously organisms that never had a sperm and egg in their history at all, like a clone or something synthetic? I care about which of these is upstream of the others, which are tractable with tools we already have versus just intuition ping-pong, and what argument over the next year or so would move something up or down the list. Also tell me what's overrated — the stuff people keep publishing in this area that doesn't actually bear on much.
