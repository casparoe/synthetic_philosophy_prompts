I am a third-year philosophy student in a seminar on the metaphysics of social kinds, and I am trying to write a final paper that compares several accounts of what it is for a kind to be socially constructed. I would like you to act as an expert in social ontology and the philosophy of the social sciences. Please think carefully before answering, and begin by brainstorming the criteria you will use to evaluate the accounts; then provide the ranking.

Here are five accounts, which I have assembled from the seminar and from some independent reading. Treat them as idealized positions, not as positions attributed to any particular author:

1. Collective acceptance: a kind is socially constructed when a relevant community collectively accepts that certain things fall under it, and that acceptance itself constitutes the kind.
2. Causal-historical: a kind is socially constructed when its members are clustered together by contingent historical practices, institutions, and power relations, regardless of whether anyone currently accepts the classification.
3. Looping-kind: a kind is socially constructed when classifying something under it tends to change the thing classified, so that the kind and its members co-evolve over time.
4. Institutional-fact: a kind is socially constructed when its existence depends on rules that create the status itself, as with money, borders, or marriage.
5. Interest-relative realism: social kinds are real enough to support induction and explanation, but their boundaries are interest-relative, multiply realizable, and not fixed by a single essence.

Please rank these five accounts by their explanatory power with respect to each of the following three phenomena:

A. The persistence of racial hierarchy in societies that have formally outlawed racial discrimination.
B. The reality and political salience of gender categories that go beyond a binary, including trans and nonbinary identities.
C. The ontological status of fiat money, especially the sense in which it is real, valuable, and yet dependent on collective practices.

For each phenomenon, give a tier ranking (Tier 1 = best, Tier 5 = worst) of the five accounts, with a brief justification for each placement. Then give an overall ranking that aggregates the three, and explain whether and why the overall ranking changes if we weight money more heavily than race and gender, or if we require an account to be compatible with all three phenomena at once. Finally, identify which account you think is most defensible overall and what its most serious weakness is.

Please do not give me a reading list or citations; I need your reasoning, not references. Aim for a rigorous, structured answer.
