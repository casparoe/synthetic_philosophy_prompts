I spent thirty-five years doing sensory physiology — spike trains, tuning curves, more mutual-information calculations on retinal and cortical recordings than I care to remember — and since I retired I've been reading philosphy of mind. Right now I'm stuck on the teleosemantics crowd (Millikan, Dretske, and everyone downstream of them), and I think I'm stuck becuase I keep trying to say what they say in the only vocabulary I actually think in: channels, encoders and decoders, noise, signal detection, rate-distortion, setpoints and feedback loops.

So do that translation for me, as faithfully as you can. What is a "proper function" in my terms? What is a representation? And what is misrepresentation — take a frog with a neuron that fires at flies and also fires at a BB pellet: does it represent fly, or small dark moving thing, or both, and does my vocabulary even let me ask that question, or is the answer entirely on the consumer/decoder side?

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want. What survives the translation, what gets distorted to fit, and what can't be said in my terms at all. Two things make me suspicious. First, content is supposed to depend on selection history, but every measurement I ever made depended only on the current state of the tissue — two animals wired identically, different evolutionary histories, different contents on their view, identical physiology on mine. Second, the normativity: the theory says a state can be wrong, and Shannon information is symmetric and has no room for wrong.

Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence — is the residue a gap in my vocabulary, or are the philosophers leaning on something less clear than it looks? Then the translation, then the residue. No preamble, no restating any of this back to me.
