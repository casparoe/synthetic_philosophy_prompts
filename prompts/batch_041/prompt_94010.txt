Q3. Bernard Williams argues that every reason for action is an internal reason. (a) Explain the distinction between internal and external reasons as Williams draws it, and say what he means by an agent's subjective motivational set. (b) Why does he think external reason statements are problematic? (c) Does his view leave us any room to call an agent unreasonable, or does it collapse into saying that everyone's reasons are whatever their motivations happen to be?

And here's what the student wrote:

"Williams's target is the idea that an agent could have a reason to do something quite independently of anything he wants, cares about, or is committed to — that would be an external reason. His own view is that every genuine reason is internal: A has a reason to do X just when a sound course of deliberation from A's subjective motivational set (his S) would lead A to be motivated to do X. And S isn't just a bag of appetites; it takes in projects, loyalties, commitments and dispositions of evaluation. But since the reason has to be anchored in something the agent already wants, deliberation can't really add anything to S — practical reasoning just works out how to satisfy the desires that are already in there. So the view is basically the Humean one, and it's the same insight as the metaethical thesis that sincere moral judgements are themselves motivating; that's what makes Williams an internalist.

He then argues that external reason statements can't do the work their defenders need them to do. If the statement is true but the agent isn't moved by it, then it can't explain or guide his action — the belief may be there, but no motive. So the externalist owes us an account of how the consideration could get a grip on the agent, and if it can't, the reason statement is either false or it's just a threat or social pressure dressed up as a reason; Williams's line is that the externalist is bluffing. This is the background to his claim that morality can't supply the categorical reasons the Kantians want.

On the criticism worry, he does allow that we can call someone unreasonable when he's deliberated badly or overlooked something that follows from what's already in his S. What we can't do is tell him he has a reason to do something his S gives him no purchase on at all."
