I am reading Hobbes's Leviathan alongside the early modern debates about the certainty of mathematics, and I have run into a sentence in chapter 4 that I cannot make sense of on my own. Hobbes writes: "And therefore in geometry, which is the only science that it hath pleased God hitherto to bestow on mankind, men begin at settling the significations of their words; which settling of significations they call definitions, and place them in the beginning of their reckoning."

My difficulty is not the English but the surrounding doctrine. In the same chapter Hobbes insists that "there being nothing in the world universal but names; for the things named are every one of them individual and singular," and he warns that the names of virtues and vices, whose signification shifts with the speaker's passions, "can never be true grounds of any ratiocination." In chapter 9 he defines science as conditional knowledge, and his example is geometrical: if the figure shown be a circle, then any straight line through the centre divides it into two equal parts.

My tentative reading is that the necessity Hobbes finds in geometry is internal to the definitions we impose, so that calling geometry God's gift amounts to saying that God gave us speech and the faculty of reckoning, the definitions themselves being ours. I would like help seeing whether the passage supports that, or whether I am flattening a distinction Hobbes needs. In particular: what does "God-given" add that the earlier remark about God instructing Adam in the naming of the creatures does not already supply, and why should the definitional method secure certainty in geometry but not in the moral vocabulary Hobbes requires for civil philosophy? I have also seen it suggested that Hobbes holds a "maker's knowledge" view — that we know with certainty only what we ourselves construct, which would tie the geometer's figures to the commonwealth we build — but I am unsure whether that is Hobbes or a later idea (it sounds to me like Vico) read back into him.

Please help me understand the passage and the doctrine behind it. If my framing is a misreading, or if the puzzle I have posed is one Hobbes's text does not actually generate, say so plainly rather than answering it as posed.
