Hey — I'm in an aesthetics seminar this term and we've spent like three weeks on whether morally messed-up art can still be good art, and honestly the readings keep giving me the same four or five positions and I can't tell them apart on the cases I actually care about. I've been thinking about it alot. For my final project I want to make a "which position do you actually hold" quiz for my classmates: 8 to 10 questions, each one a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto named positions. I want it to sort people by their reactions to the cases, not by which school they'd claim membership in, so nothing like "are moral defects aesthetic defects?" as a question — that just makes people pick a team.

What I want it built around is a distinction my professor made that I don't think is standard. She said to stop asking "is the work immoral" as one question and instead ask where the moral problem is located. Four spots: (1) in the work's content — it argues for something monstrous; (2) in the work's perspective or attitude — it invites you to enjoy something you shouldn't; (3) in the work's production — it was made with exploited labor, or generated by a model trained on stolen work; (4) in the artist's life outside the work — nothing in the piece reflects it, the artist is just a terrible person. I want the quiz to run those four seperately, because I suspect people give completely different answers depending on the locus and never notice they're doing it.

Cases I want in there, though you can add your own: a novel whose moral flaw is inseparable from its aesthetic power, so that fixing it would make the book worse; a masterpiece with an innocent content and attitude that was produced by stolen labor; a film that is morally admirable in intent and also just bad; a novel that's genuinely great and genuinely noxious and almost nobody notices the noxious part; a great painting by a person we only later learned did something awful; and a piece made by an AI with no author at all, so there's nobody to separate the work from. Also throw in a couple of questions that aren't about the verdict on the work but about what you'd do — recommend it, assign it, keep it on the museum wall — because I think that's where these positions actually come apart.

Leaves I need: radical autonomism (moral and aesthetic value are just separate and never interact), moderate autonomism (moral defects never make a work worse as art, but they can give you non-aesthetic reasons not to engage with it), ethicism (moral defects are aesthetic defects), immoralism (moral defects can actually be aesthetic merits), moralism (moral value is what ultimately matters and aesthetic value is secondary or instrumental to it), and one deflationary option for whoever thinks the question is malformed because "the work" isn't a stable unit of evaluation in the first place. Keep the artist/work axis separate from the work's own value — I want the quiz to be able to land someone on "ethicism about the work" plus "the artist's life is irrelevant to my verdict on the work" without that counting as a contradiction. And for each position, give me one line on which of the cases it handles worst.

If two leaves can't be told apart by any case you can write, say so instead of faking a question for them, and if some combination of answers is a position nobody actually holds, tell me that too — I'd rather know the space is empty there than have a fake leaf. And please be committed about it: don't hand me a neutral instrument and a shrug. At the end, tell me which leaf you'd land on, which of your own questions is the one that pins you, and what you think is wrong with the positions you're rejecting. I have to present this in class, so I'd rather have something with a spine.
