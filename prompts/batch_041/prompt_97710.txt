My phil of language professor gave us a one-paragraph gloss on two-dimensional semantics and I wrote it down, but somethings bugging me about it. She said: 'The view is that every expression has two intensions. The primary intension is what the term picks out in the actual world; the secondary intension is what it picks out at other possible worlds. Thats why "Hesperus is Phosphorus" comes out a priori — you get the primary intension just by understanding the words — and also necessary, thanks to the secondary intension. At bottom this is just the analytic/synthetic distinction in new clothes, and its main payoff is handling indexicals like "I" and "now."' I have a feeling the primary/secondary contrast is roughly right but something in the a priori bit is off, and the last sentence sounds like a cheap shot.
