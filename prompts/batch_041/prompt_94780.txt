I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy, working mostly on moral epistemology, and I've been going around in circles with a friend in our metaethics reading group about particularism. Not the boring version where obviously our everyday rules have exceptions -- the strong claim that morality has no shape at all, i.e. no feature of a situation is reliably relevant in the same direction across contexts, so that even a maximally specified list of contributory considerations plus rules for how they interact wouldn't capture any real moral structure. She thinks holism about reasons is totally compatible with there being principles, as long as the principles themselves encode the interaction effects, and I think that move either makes the principles vacuous or just relocates the problem. (For what it's worth I also can't shake the feeling that this is structurally the same fight as the one in philosophy of science over whether the special sciences have laws at all once you allow ceteris paribus hedges, though I'm not sure whether that analogy helps her or me.)

What I want from you is your credence that the strong particularist claim is true, decomposed rather than asserted. Roughly the terms I'd use: (a) how much of this is about the metaphysics of morality versus about how moral judgment is actually justified and learned; (b) whether the holism of reasons is a datum any theory has to accommodate or an artifact of how we describe cases; (c) whether the generalist's just-build-the-context-into-the-principle move succeeds, and if it does, whether generalism ends up unfalsifiable or instead trivially true; (d) whether the special-sciences analogy cuts one way; (e) whether the whole dispute is verbal, i.e. we mean different things by principle and would agree once that's pinned down. Give me a number for each, how they combine into your bottom line, and which one the bottom line is most sensitive to, plus what arguement or evidence would move it most. My own number is about 0.55 and hers is about 0.2 -- I suspect we mostly disagree on (c), but tell me if you think the real gap is somewhere else.

And please be explicit about which parts of this are settled among people who actually work on it and which are live disputes -- don't hand me a contested construal of particularism as if it were the consensus, and if the way I've framed the question is itself one contested way of setting up the debate, say that too.
