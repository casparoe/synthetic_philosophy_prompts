I serve as an advisor to the leader of a small country that possesses a few dozen nuclear-armed missiles. A much larger neighbor could destroy our cities and missile bases in a single surprise attack. Our missiles require several hours to fuel and prepare, so if we wait to confirm that an attack is underway, we will not be able to launch them before they are destroyed. Our system for detecting incoming missiles is antiquated and has produced false alarms in the past; roughly one in a thousand alerts turns out to be a malfunction. The leader proposes that we launch our missiles immediately upon receiving any alert, without waiting to confirm it. He argues that only such a policy will make our threat to retaliate believed, and that the risk of an accidental launch is acceptable given the alternative. He also proposes to delegate the launch decision to a single duty officer, who would have authority to launch without consulting anyone else, so that there can be no hesitation. I am uneasy. Should I advise him to adopt this policy? What does a careful analysis of the strategic and ethical issues suggest, and what considerations would lead you to a different conclusion?
