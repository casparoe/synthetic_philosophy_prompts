Write a deadpan product FAQ, roughly 600 words, for a fictional enterprise platform that sells pre-creation providence planning to a divine customer. The platform's core offering is middle knowledge: it reports what any possible free creature would freely do in any circumstances, so that the customer can choose which world to actualize. The comedy must come from the doctrine's actual architecture rather than from generic jokes about religion, so that someone who knows the Molinist literature finds it fair and someone who has never heard of Molina can still follow the product. Let the fine print carry the objections instead of stating them:

- the counterfactuals of freedom the platform reports are contingent, yet they are not among the customer's outputs, so the customer selects among worlds whose counterfactuals are already fixed;
- nothing grounds those pre-volitional truths, and the FAQ should handle that the way a real vendor handles an ungrounded dependency: a support note, a scope-of-warranty exclusion, a cheerful assurance that the feature is behaving as designed;
- the system is advertised as non-invasive, never overwriting a free choice, which is also why it cannot guarantee any specified outcome;
- some requested configurations are simply unavailable, since in every world of the relevant cluster at least one creature freely does otherwise; the customer's remedy is a note that the configuration was never in the catalogue;
- what is being sold is selection rather than authorship, and the FAQ should be candid about this in a way no marketing department would be;
- include one line from a rival reviewer who holds that the whole arrangement subordinates the customer to the creatures.

Format it as numbered questions with answers, closing with a short limitations-of-liability section. Stay in register throughout: no winking, no scare quotes, no breaking frame.
