Write an essay on the following controversy.

A forecasting group has been asked how much longer a particular large, long-lived, currently dominant polity — call it the Hegemon — can be expected to persist in something like its present form. One analyst defends the outside view. Her proposal is to identify every historically documented polity of comparable scale and type, treat the distribution of their lifespans as a survival curve, condition on the fact that the Hegemon has already survived as long as it has, and read off a remaining-life estimate. She insists that the honest output is a wide interval rather than a point estimate, and that the alternative — reasoning from the Hegemon's institutions, economy, and rivals — supplies a story rather than a method. A second analyst raises four objections. The reference class is a choice rather than a discovery: broaden or narrow it, count dynasties separately or fuse them, and the estimate moves by centuries. The sample is selected on survival and on record-keeping, since only polities durable and legible enough to leave records can appear in it. The identity of the unit is not given by nature: a change of dynasty, capital, religion, or constitutional order may or may not terminate the polity, and whichever criterion one adopts is doing the real work. And conditioning on elapsed time presupposes a well-behaved hazard rate; if polities age, or if their fates are driven by idiosyncratic shocks, a frequency drawn from a heterogeneous class says little about any member of it. The first analyst replies that every one of these objections applies with equal force to the inside view, that the inside view is not a method at all, and that preferring a narrative to a base rate is precisely the error the outside view exists to correct.

Write an essay on this dispute. Address, in particular, the underlying question of whether a device for correcting a forecaster's bias is also a device for estimating a quantity, and whether the reference class problem — the fact that any event belongs to indefinitely many classes with different frequencies — has a principled solution or is settled only by convention. Say what the outside view can and cannot deliver in a case of this kind, what notion of persistence the question presupposes when it asks how long a polity lasts, and under what conditions the outside view is the right tool. Then reach a verdict: whether a responsible forecaster should report a number for the Hegemon, and if so, what kind of number, with what qualifications, and what would justify revising it; or whether the deliverable should be a refusal together with an explanation. Defend the verdict against the strongest version of the opposing position. The essay should not conclude merely that both sides have a point; a conditional verdict is acceptable if the conditions are stated and defended.
