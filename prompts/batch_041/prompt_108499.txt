I recently became chair of a small tenants' association, and I have been trying to describe something that happens there without the vocabulary I would need. The bins go on the left side of the shed because the first person to use the shed put them there; now everyone does it, new tenants are corrected if they put them on the right, and when the shed was rebuilt and the right side became more convenient, the bins still went back to the left. No one voted on it, no one can give a reason for it beyond the fact that it is what we do, and yet people treat a suggestion to move them as a small breach rather than a preference. In another group I am in, the meeting is at 7pm because that suited the founder; the founder left years ago, 6pm would suit everyone better, but proposing the change feels like proposing something improper, and if enough people did switch, the new time would become the correct one with the same felt authority. I know about conventions as solutions to coordination problems, and about social norms as rules enforced by disapproval, but each seems to capture only half of this: the first explains the arbitrary origin and self-reinforcement but not the felt ought, the second explains the felt ought but not the arbitrary, self-sustaining, reversible coordination structure. Is there an established name for this exact hybrid, and how does it differ from a Lewisian convention, a social norm, a mere custom, a Schelling point, and a Nash equilibrium? If there is no established term, coin one and define it, with boundary cases and a clear account of what it is not. I do not need citations or a reading list; I need the carving. Please give the answer as a JSON object with the following fields: verdict (whether an established term exists), nearest_concepts (an array of objects, each with name, relation, and disanalogy), proposed_term (if none, your coinage; otherwise the established term), definition (a precise definition), boundary_cases (an array of cases and which side of the line they fall on), and why_not (an array of objects with concept and difference).
