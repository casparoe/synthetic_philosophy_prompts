Hey, so I'm helping put together a small grant for conceptual AI alignment work — three years, four or five people, philosophy and theory only, nobody doing empirical ML. The area we've zeroed in on is approval-directed agents versus goal-directed agents (Christiano-style approval maximizers vs. the usual RL-style objective maximizers), and I've been asked to say which open questions in that space are actually worth the money, and in what order.

I definately don't want a survey though. I want a defended agenda: for each question, why it's upstream of the others, whether it's the kind of thing a philosopher can make real progress on without a lab, and what would move it up or down the list over the next couple of years.

A few things I keep bumping into that I'd like you to treat as live candidate cruxes rather than skipping past:

1) The approver is itself an agent with an alignment problem. So is 'approval-directed' actually relocating the hard part rather than dissolving it, and if so does that make the whole frame less interesting than it looks?

2) Whether the target should be the approver's approval of the act, of the disposition that produced the act, or of the act under some counterfactual where the approver is smarter / can see the whole training history. These seem to come apart, and I'm not sure anyone has sorted out which one the safety story actually needs.

3) Approval as an ordinal better/worse signal vs. approval as a scalar you maximize. Does the good behaviour survive the moment you turn it into something to be optimized, or does the ordinal/scalar gap eat the proposal?

Rank the candidates, tell me which are genuinely load-bearing and which just sound deep, and be honest about which ones we probably can't even state precisely enough to work on yet.
