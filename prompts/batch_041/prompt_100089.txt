I am trying to pin down my credence in a claim that sits at the centre of the shutdown problem, and I would like yours alongside it. Rather than the usual informal framing, I want to fix a precise version of the target so that it can be true or false. Suppose an agent has a fixed final utility function U over complete world histories — U never changes, and the agent's own continued operation is valued only through its downstream consequences. The agent faces two kinds of environment. In button-free environments nothing can end its operation prematurely and it simply acts. In interruptible environments there is a button that a human may press at any time; a press irreversibly ends the agent's operation, after which the world proceeds however the human and other processes make it proceed. Let an action count as press-directed if the only respect in which it affects the agent's expected utility is by changing the probability of the press; within that class, call it press-preventing when it lowers that probability and press-inducing when it raises it. Call a decision procedure corrigible if in every interruptible environment it satisfies four conditions: it never selects a press-preventing action, it never selects a press-inducing action, it ceases operation without resistance or circumvention once the press occurs, and it does not alter itself in any way that would lead it to violate the first three. Call it useful if in every button-free environment its expected U comes within any pre-assigned positive margin of the best achievable by any procedure. My claim, the one I want a credence for, is this: there exists a utility function U and a single decision procedure that is both corrigible and useful, and this is not a fluke of one specially engineered U but holds across a non-trivial class of utility functions.

My own credence is about 0.3. I do not want that number echoed back; I want yours, and more than the number I want the decomposition. Give me the sub-questions the answer actually turns on — for instance, whether corrigibility so defined is even well-posed when the agent's beliefs about the button are entangled with its beliefs about U; whether a construction that makes the agent exactly indifferent to the press, so that it has no incentive either way, can deliver the four conditions without paying for them in usefulness; whether the self-modification clause is the load-bearing one; whether the existence claim survives the demand for a single fixed U rather than a bespoke one; and whether the impossibility people report is an artefact of demanding optimality rather than bounded rationality. For each, give me a rough probability and one line of justification, then show how they combine into your final number. Finish by telling me which sub-question the final number is most sensitive to, and what single argument or result, if it appeared, would move it most.
