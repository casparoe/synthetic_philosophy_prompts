I'm taking this media studies seminar and we keep throwing around 'echo chamber' and 'epistemic bubble' like they're the same thing, but my gut says they're not, and I want to figure out what I actually think. So here are my verdicts on a bunch of cases, going by feel. (1) My uncle only watches one cable news channel; he has no idea other channels exist; he's in a bubble, but it seems like a kind of innocent one -- I've been calling it an 'epistemic suburb' in my notes, though I'm not sure that's a real thing. (2) My cousin watches the same channel, but she also reads the opposing blogs, and when she sees their evidence she says it's all astroturfed by a foundation; that's an echo chamber, and it feels worse than my uncle. (3) A cancer researcher who reads only oncology journals and ignores politics isn't in either; that's just specialization. (4) Someone who mutes all political content on social media because it stresses them out is curating, not trapped. (5) A forum for a marginalized group that bans outside critics because outside critics have historically been bad-faith seems protective, not an echo chamber, even though it's closed. (6) A group of people who mostly agree but who actively invite the smartest critic they can find and try to restate her argument charitably is not an echo chamber at all, even though they end up agreeing. (7) An algorithmic feed that quietly shows you only stuff you'll like, without your knowledge, is a bubble, and maybe also an echo chamber? Not sure. (8) Someone who reads a wide range of sources but only to confirm that the other side is stupid is an echo chamber, I think, even though they're exposed to everything.

My tentative principle was: a bubble is about missing information, an echo chamber is about discrediting information you do get, and the bad thing in both is that you can't correct your beliefs. But then case 5 and case 6 make me think it's not just about correction; it's about whether the community has a practice of treating outsiders as potential knowers. And case 4 makes me think some filtering is fine. Is there one principle (or a small set of candidates) that gets all eight, or close? State it precisely, say which of my verdicts it forces me to give up, and tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed: a small online support group for people with a rare chronic illness who share symptom management tips and have a standing rule not to recommend doctors because most of them were dismissed for years before diagnosis. Is that an echo chamber, an epistemic bubble, a healthy community, or does the principle say it depends on something I haven't specified?
