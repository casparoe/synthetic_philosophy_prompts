So I'm a backend engineer, never took a philosophy class in my life, but I fell down a rabbit hole on Aristotle's stuff about money after getting into an argument with my brother about tipping, and now I have Opinions. Problem is I don't know if they actually hang together or if I'm just telling myself a story. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Aristotle was onto something in book 1 of the Politics. There's a real difference between getting wealth in order to run a household and live a decent life, and getting wealth for its own sake. The second kind is parasitic on the first — it doesn't produce anything, it just moves the same stuff around and skims a cut off the top.

(2) Money is a convention, not a natural thing. It only works because we all agree to treat it as a stand-in for need, so prices should ultimately track how much stuff actually contributes to people living well.

(3) Charging interest is wrong in itself, not just when the rate is predatory. Money can't produce anything on its own, so whatever the lender gets back has to come out of the borrower's pocket. The whole arrangement is extraction dressed up as a service.

(4) Free exchange is usually good and should mostly be left alone. If two adults agree on a price they're both better off, and price controls and meddling generally make things worse.

(5) Market pay gaps are basically fine and even informative. A good engineer making five times what a teacher makes isn't a scandal — the market is how we find out what someone's work is actually worth to other people.

(6) Speculating on stuff you never intend to use — crypto, meme stocks, flipping sneakers — isn't real economic activity. It's the unnatural kind of wealth-getting, and a society that rewards it is sick in some way.

(7) Somebody selling their labor for a wage they agreed to isn't being exploited even if the wage is low, as long as they could've walked away.

I have a feeling some of these are in tension but I honestly can't tell which, and I'm worried the ones that feel most obvious to me are the ones doing the damage. Can you go through them and tell me which pairs actually conflict, ranked by how serious it is? For each conflict, say what would have to give and what I'd be giving up if I gave it.
