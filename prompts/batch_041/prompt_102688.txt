A friend of mine runs a small animal-welfare charity that funds research, and she's just been handed a pot of money — enough for about three postdocs for two years, conceptual work only, no lab work — and she's asked me to help her decide what to spend it on. Her current plan is what she calls a 'sentience budget': rank the animals by roughly (how likely it is that they're conscious) × (how many of them there are) × (how much we could realistically do about their suffering), then throw the money at whatever comes out on top. I think the formula is doing a lot of hidden philosophical work and that's the part I want your help with. So: within the question of which animals are conscious — fish, octopuses, insects, crustaceans, but also the unglamorous cases like whether a chicken or a mouse is a subject of experience at all — what are the main open questions right now, and which two or three would you actually put this money behind?

I want a defended ranking rather than a survey. Which questions, if answered, would most change the output of a decision procedure like hers? Does it matter more whether consciousness comes in degrees or is all-or-nothing, whether it's a property of the whole organism or could in principle be had by a subsystem of one, whether counting subjects is even the right move, whether a single 'likelihood' figure is the right shape of answer at all, and whether the whole thing smuggles in a utilitarian calculus that some of these animals might not fit? Tell me which of these is upstream of which, which are tractable for a philosopher rather than a neuroscientist, and which are the ones people get excited about but that wouldn't actually shift the budget by much. Don't worry about pointing me at reading — she and I can't really use citations, I just want to know what you think and why. And please take a real position: pick your top two, commit to them, and tell me what would change your mind. I've been argueing with her about this for weeks and I'd rather have a view I can push back on than a list of options.
