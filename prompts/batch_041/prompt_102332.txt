ok so i need a poem for the last session of a reading group on modality, and i want it to actually do the counterpart-theory vs transworld-identity thing rather than just name-drop it. form: a villanelle. voice: someone who lost something that could easily have gone the other way -- a close election, a custody fight, a diagnosis that came back the other way -- talking to the version of themselves who got the good outcome.

the two refrains have to be the two views. one refrain should be the transworld-identity line: thats me over there, the very same person, just in another world, so what happened to them happened to me. the other should be the counterpart-theorist line: thats not me, its someone who resembles me closely enough to do the work of standing in for me when we say what could have happened to me -- because nothing is in more than one world, so strictly there is no me over there at all. the form should do the structural work: the refrains keep trading places and end up side by side in the last two lines, and the poem shouldnt tell us whether those two lines are about one person or two. thats the whole point.

a few constraints: real villanelle (19 lines, proper rhyme scheme and repetition), no philosophers named, no citations, no footnotes. accurate enough that someone who knows the literature wont wince -- in particular dont let the counterpart line collapse into the sloppy "a near-duplicate" reading; it needs to carry the de re/de dicto point that when we say i could have won, what makes that true is that a counterpart of mine won, and that this isnt a reduction of modality to similarity but a different account of what the modal talk is about. also, the speaker shouldnt be sure which refrain is right -- if the poem comes down on one side its dead.

start with the poem itself, no preface. after it, 3 or 4 sentences on which refrain is which and why the final couplet sits where it does.
