ok, position check before I commit this to a seminar paper. Here's what I think about ranking atrocities. (1) There's a fact of the matter: some atrocities were worse than others, and that's true independantly of who says it or why they're saying it. (2) Numbers count — 100,000 dead is worse than 10,000, other things equal. (3) Asking "was the Holocaust worse than the Atlantic slave trade?" is obscene — not just gauche, actually something you shouldn't do, and "but it's true" is no answer to that. (4) The obscenity comes from commensurability: suffering and humiliation and grief can't be put on one scale without being falsified, and ranking them does exactly that. (5) At the same time I'm fine with triage — we have to decide what to teach, what to fund, what to apologise for first, and that takes ranking. The obscenity, I think, attaches specifically to the "which was worse" framing, not to having to act. (6) I'm a moral realist, so "worse" is one of the things there's a fact about.

I don't see the problem myself, but a friend insists these don't hang together. Who's right? If some of them really do pull against each other, order the clashes by how bad they are and tell me the cheapest way out of each, plus which single revision would clean up the most.
