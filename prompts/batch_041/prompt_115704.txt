I have been thinking through the idea of imprecise probabilities on my own, and I keep running into what looks like a flaw in it. My understanding is that a range of probabilities is the honest thing to report when the true probability is some definite value that I have simply not yet pinned down — the width of the range measures my ignorance, and better evidence should narrow it. But I have come across two things that seem to contradict this: that in these frameworks learning something entirely unrelated to the question at hand can widen the range rather than narrow it, and that one and the same range can license different choices depending on which rule is used to select an act. Both strike me as marks of a theory that has not finished its work, since a genuine fact would be narrowed by information and would settle what one ought to do. So: am I right that imprecision is only a temporary condition, that an ideally rational agent would always hold a single probability, and that in the meantime the honest course is to keep my best single estimate and simply note that it may be off? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question.
