I've been reading a bunch of Buddhist stuff on my own — no classes, just some books and a meditation group my sister dragged me to — and the no-self idea has really gotten under my skin: that there's no fixed "me" behind my thoughts, just a constantly changing bundle of stuff, and "I" is basically a convenient label. I think I mostly buy it. But when I test it on particular cases my gut keeps handing me verdicts that don't seem to fit together, and I can't tell if the problem is me or the idea. Can you help me find the principle behind my own verdicts, if there even is one?

Here's what I think. Someone gets total amnesia, forgets their whole life, personality completely changes: still the same person, I'd say, and it'd be awful to tell them the old them is dead. Someone changes gradually over 30 years: same person, obviously. A machine makes a perfect atom-for-atom copy of me while I'm still standing here: the copy is not me, he's a new guy who thinks he's me, and theres now two of us. Same setup but I'm destroyed the instant the copy is made: still not me — it's someone else with my memories, and honestly it's a bit like I died. Now the weird one: a surgeon swaps out my neurons one at a time for silicon chips that do exactly the same job, so gradually that I never notice and never pass out — I definately say I'm still me the whole way through. And yet with the Buddhist rebirth case — somebody dies, and years later a kid is born with zero memory of the dead person but a karmic thread running from one to the other, like one candle lighting the next — I want to say that is NOT the same person, just a continuation. Which is annoying, because the candle thing is exactly how I want to describe my own case across time.

So memory can't be what makes me me — I stay me through amnesia — but it can't be enough either, since the copy has all my memories and isn't me. And it isn't the pattern, since the gradual-chip me and the copy-me have the same pattern at the end but I only count one of them as me. So my first guess is it's the unbroken causal thread that matters, not the pattern. But then the rebirth kid has an unbroken causal thread too, and I still won't call him the same person, so that guess is dead on arrival. Is it the body? But if every neuron got swapped out, is it even the same body anymore? And here's what really bugs me: if there's no self at all, why is there a fact of the matter about wether the copy is me — why isn't it like asking whether my car is still the same car after I replace the engine and then the transmission, where there's just no real answer, only a handy way of talking? What principle actually captures my verdicts, and which ones does it make me give up? And could you run it on a case I didn't list — say, a guy who gets reset every night and rebuilt from a backup every morning: is the morning guy the same person who went to bed? — so I can see if the principle is really doing work or just redescribing what I already said.
