I'm in a reading group that's been slogging through the regress problem for a few weeks, and last night we got stuck on the pictures people use to sell their positions. The foundationalist in the group keeps saying a body of beliefs is like a building: you need a foundation, otherwise the whole thing collapses. The coherentist says no, it's like a raft or a web -- we're always repairing it at sea, and it holds together by mutual support, not by resting on bedrock. Someone else brought up infinitism and said it's like a chain that just keeps going, which got a laugh.

I want to stress-test these analogies instead of just trading them. Can you lay out the correspondence table for each one -- what plays the role of the foundation, the load, the sea, the repairs, the chain links, and so on -- and then tell me where each analogy breaks down in ways that matter for the actual epistemological dispute? I'm especially interested in whether the building picture begs the question by assuming justification has to be asymmetric and transitive, and whether the raft picture quietly smuggles in something external, like buoyancy or the fact that the sailors are already afloat, that a foundationalist could call a foundation in disguise. I also wonder if the building picture is misleading because a building's foundation is made of the same stuff as the rest of the building, while foundationalists usually say basic beliefs are justified in a different way, by experience or perception rather than by further beliefs. Does that disanalogy matter for the regress? And does the chain picture for infinitism do any real work, or is it just a way of making infinitism sound absurd? If there's a better analogy for any of the three, I'd like to hear it, but I care more about whether the conclusions people draw from these pictures actually survive once the disanalogies are on the table. We're not writing papers, so no need for citations or reading lists -- just the audit.
