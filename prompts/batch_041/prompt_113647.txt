write a poem about a saying that spreads. like, the poem is a single folk proverb getting retold from person to person, and each retelling is a stanza, and it keeps drifting. i want maybe 7 or 8 short stanzas, first one is a crisp two or three line saying (something with a bit of wit to it, make it up), and then every stanza after that is a copy of the *previous* stanza, not the original, with one or two small changes. so the errors pile up the way they do in a telephone game, but not random errors -- they should be the kind a real rememberer/reteller makes: rounding off a weird rhythm, swapping a word for a rhyme, replacing something they half forgot with a cliche that fits the slot, that kind of thing. by the last stanza it should still be clearly descended from the first one but now be saying something different, maybe even the opposite.

then add a short final stanza that is the saying itself talking, first person, and it should not care at all what it now means, only whether it gets repeated. thats the meme's eye view thing.

one more constraint: i want the drift to look like its being pulled toward a shape rather than just decaying -- the thing some people call cultural attraction, where transmission is biased reconstruction toward certain memorable forms instead of faithful copying. so the changes should trend in a direction a cognitive scientist could point at (toward a cleaner rhythm, toward a more extreme claim, toward a familiar image) and you should not make them random. someone who knows the memetics-vs-cultural-attraction argument should be able to tell from the poem alone that im not a naive replicator person.

also please dont let any two stanzas be "the same meme" -- i want the reader to finish it unable to say exactly what the unit of transmission was. dont label anything, no stanza numbers saying generation 1, generation 2, etc, let the repetition do the work. no title.
