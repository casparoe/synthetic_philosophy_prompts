Ok so I never studied philosophy properly, I just read alot of blogs and argue with a friend of mine who did a masters, and we keep going round in circles on the same thing and I want an honest diagnosis rather than a side to cheer for.

Here's the thing. Maths seems to be about real stuff. There really are infinitely many primes, and that seems true whether or not anyone believes it or has written it down. But numbers and sets aren't like tables and electrons — you can't bump into the number 7, you can't put a set under a microscope. So two things pull against each other. If maths sentences are true because they describe some realm of abstract objects, then how do we ever get in touch with that realm to find anything out about it? We're stuck behind our own eyes; our beliefs get caused by pencils and blackboards, not by the objects the sentences are supposedly about. But if we say the sentences aren't really about objects like that, we seem to lose the very thing we wanted, namely that they're objectively true and not just a game we made up and could of made up differently.

My friend says: obviously the abstract objects exist, the access thing is just a puzzle about causation and knowledge and we should relax our standards for what knowing is. I say: if you have to relax them that far you've basically changed the subject. Neither of us budges, and I've read enough forum threads to know this has been going on for ages with very smart people.

It's a bit like asking how a thermometer knows the temperature, except a thermometer is at least wired into the world by a chain of causes, and a proof doesn't seem wired into anything.

So here's what I actually want. Is this one disagreement or two? My own guess, and I might be totally off, is that he and I aren't even answering the same question — he's answering what makes the sentences true, I'm answering how we could possibly come to know, and we both wave the word true around as if it means the same thing in both cases. Is that right, or is it a real disagreement in disguise, like a hidden factual question or a clash about what counts as a good explanation of something?

And if it is a real disagreement, what kind is it exactly — a disagreement about facts, about what counts as evidence, or just bedrock hunches that neither of us can argue for and that we picked up somewhere? Then tell me honestly whether anything could actually move it: some distinction, some kind of result, or a change in the question. Or is it stuck for good because its badly posed? I'd much rather have a diagnosis with reasons than a tour of who said what.
