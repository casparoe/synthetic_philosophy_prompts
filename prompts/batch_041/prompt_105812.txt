I've spent several years working on anti-corruption programmes and I keep running into a pattern I have no word for. A district officer fast-tracks a permit for a man from his own village. To the funder this is corruption: public office, private benefit, a queue jumped. To the officer it is a duty — refusing would have been the shameful act — and he wasn't hiding it or dressing it up afterwards. Or a company sends money to the family of an official who has just died. The funder's auditors call it a bribe paid to relatives who still carry influence; the company calls it what one does when someone you owe has died. In both cases the act is, at the same time, a breach of one normative order and a faithful performance of another, and nobody involved is confused about the facts. The people in my field tend to say either that this is just corruption wearing a cultural mask, or that it is all relative — and neither captures it, because both descriptions are doing real work and neither order is obviously the mistaken one. Is there an established name for this specific thing, a genuine collision of two intact normative orders over a single act? If there isn't, coin one, define it, and tell me how it differs from moral relativism, from value pluralism, and from the claim that corruption is simply an essentially contested concept.
