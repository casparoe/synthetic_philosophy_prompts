You are advising a private foundation that has committed to funding roughly six researchers for four years on the single question of why there is something rather than nothing. The money is for conceptual work — metaphysics, philosophy of science, logic, and the philosophy of explanation — not for empirical cosmology. Your task is to tell them which open sub-questions to put the money behind, in what order, and to defend that order.

I want the prioritization organized around a distinction that I think the literature tends to blur, and that you should treat as the frame:

- The existence question: why there is any contingent entity at all, rather than none.
- The selection question: given that there is something, why this structure of laws and kinds rather than another — and whether this is a genuinely separate question or merely the existence question with the quantifier widened.
- The question-worth-asking question: whether the felt compulsion to ask the first two is an artefact — of grammar, of quantifier scope, or of a contrast class (absolute nothing) that may be incoherent or uninstantiable — in which case the most valuable work might be diagnostic or therapeutic rather than constructive.

I would also like you to weigh, without assuming it is correct, the hypothesis that the existence question is a pseudo-problem in roughly the sense in which the mind–body problem is sometimes alleged to be one: that it survives only on a picture of nothing as a possible state of affairs, and that once that picture is removed there is nothing left over to explain. If you reject the hypothesis, say what it gets wrong and what the residual explanatory demand actually is; if you accept part of it, say which part. You may rename or subdivide the three headings above if my labels mislead, but if you do, keep the mapping to them explicit.

Then tell me which lines the six researchers should be on, how the effort should be divided among them, and in what order the foundation should expect results. For each line, address how upstream it is — how much other work depends on its resolution — how tractable it is for philosophers working without new empirical input, whether it is currently neglected or crowded, and what would move it up or down the ranking over the next two years. Flag where two apparent lines are really one, and where a line that sounds deep is a dead end because no possible answer to it would change anything downstream.

Deliver a defended agenda, not a survey and not a reading list. Do not supply citations or a bibliography; I care about the reasoning rather than the scholarship. Be explicit about which parts of your answer reflect something settled among people who work on this and which parts are genuinely contested, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.

End with a single machine-readable line of the form \answer{...} containing the ranked labels of the sub-questions in priority order and nothing else inside the braces.
