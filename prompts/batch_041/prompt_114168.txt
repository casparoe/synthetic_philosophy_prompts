I am reading a cognitive science textbook whose chapter on the mind–body problem contains the following summary of functionalism and multiple realizability, and I would like it audited. I am not in a position to grade the author; I want to know what I can trust. Here is the passage:

"Functionalism is best understood as the position that survives the collapse of the identity theory. Pain cannot be the firing of C-fibers, since a human being and an octopus both feel pain and share no brain chemistry, so no mental state can be identified with any physical state; what makes something a pain is not what it is made of but what it does. This makes functionalism the sophisticated descendant of behaviorism: it keeps behaviorism's insight that a mental state is fixed by its relation to behavior, while allowing inner states provided those are characterized by their typical causes and effects. And since a function is something that any number of different materials could carry out, functionalism entails that a silicon chip, or a whole nation of people organized in the right way, could have real pains and real beliefs — this is the doctrine of multiple realizability. The difficulty is that the doctrine cuts against the theory that needs it. If one and the same mental state can be realized by indefinitely many different physical states, then the mental state can only be the disjunction of all its possible realizers, and a disjunction is a list, not a natural kind. Kim pressed exactly this point. Multiple realizability is therefore a premise that functionalists can neither give up nor keep, and its discovery is the main reason the field has moved past functionalism."
