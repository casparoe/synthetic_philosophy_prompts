I teach a 75-minute upper-level seminar on Aristotle's ethics for eighteen students, and I want a runnable market simulation that makes them feel the force of his account of money and exchange rather than merely hearing it summarized. The activity should move through three regimes: strict barter; a single perishable unit of account that everyone must use for pricing but that spoils at the end of each round; and a durable token that a central banker can mint, with one good also required for a final ritual so its use value competes with its exchange value. I want them to run into the commensurability problem themselves, to see why money appears as both a solution and a new kind of problem, and to feel the difference between exchange for need and acquisition for its own sake. Give me roles, materials, a payoff scheme in points or candy rather than real money, a timeline, a debrief that maps each regime onto a step in Aristotle's argument, the predictable ways the first run will go wrong and how to handle them, and a frank note on which feature of his view the simulation cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply. They should not be told the relevant distinctions until the debrief.
