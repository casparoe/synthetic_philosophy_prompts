My friend and I have spent the past week arguing about whether infinite populations can be ranked by welfare at all, and she insists that everything she has said follows from a single principle. She thinks welfare is the only thing that matters and that more of it is better. Her verdicts so far: (1) between two worlds containing infinitely many people each, one where everyone is at +5 and one where everyone is at +6, she says the +6 world is better; (2) between the +5 world and that same world with one extra person added at +5, she says there is no difference; (3) between the +5 world and that same world with the first person's welfare raised to +1000, she again says there is no difference. She has now put a harder case to me and wants my answer before she gives hers. World A contains infinitely many people at +5 and infinitely many people at +1000, alternating in a fixed pattern; World B contains infinitely many people all at +50. What will she say about A versus B? Set out the competing principles that fit verdicts (1)-(3), what each of them predicts here, which prediction you would bet on and why, and which further case would most cleanly force the surviving principles apart.
