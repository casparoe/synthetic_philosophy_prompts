I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of physics and I'm stuck on something thats more meta than my actual thesis. The substantivalism vs relationism fight is basically the founding dispute of the field — Newton's bucket, Leibniz's relational reply, Mach, all of it — and after three-plus centuries it still hasn't resolved, but here's what nags me: it also doesn't feel like anyone is losing. Both sides have retreated to 'sophisticated' versions. The substantivalist now says the substance is the metric field rather than the bare manifold, and the relationist has quietly absorbed a ton of structure talk. And yet we still get papers insisting there's a real, substantive disagreement underneath. So what kind of disagreement is it actually?

Here's my hypothesis, and I want you to stress-test it rather than nod along. I think the two sides aren't answering the same question under the one label. The substantivalist seems to be answering something like 'what does our best theory quantify over, and what makes its claims true?', while the relationist is answering 'what is the source of spatiotemporal structure, what explains the bucket's behaviour?'. If that's right, a chunk of the stall is just two different questions wearing one name. But I might be wrong, and the alternative is that the residual disagreement is genuinely substantive and it's really about modality — whether the points of spacetime could have had different metric properties, the haecceitism lurking behind the hole argument — in which case it's a disagreement about possibility, not vocabulary, and no amount of getting clear on words will budge it. There's also a third possibility a friend keeps pushing: that it's really a fight about explanation, i.e. whether 'X explains Y' is a mind-independent relation or just a pragmatic ordering, so this is a debate in the metaphysics of explanation wearing a physics costume.

Diagnose it for me. Use what each side is actually committed to, not a survey of positions, and tell me honestly what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, or is this one of those cases where nothing will end it and I should stop trying. Lead with your verdict in one sentence, then the diagnosis. Under 700 words, and no bibliography, I know the lit.
