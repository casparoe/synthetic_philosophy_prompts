I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training, so sorry in advance if this is some famous thing with a name that everyone knows except me. Here's the pattern I keep bumping into and it's started to bug me well outside of work. We ship a model that spits out probabilities — say it says 0.8 that a given account will churn this month. Some of those 0.8s are sitting on a mountain of near-identical cases in the training data, and some are basically the model extrapolating from three weird examples and a hunch. As a number, the two 0.8s are the same. As a thing I'd actually bet on, they're completely different, and everyone on the team quietly corrects for this by hand ("eh, treat the thin ones more like a 0.6"). When I tried to make that correction precise everything I came up with felt wrong. Saying the thin-data 0.8 is "really" 0.6 is just false — it is 0.8, I'm not claiming the model is miscalibrated there. And saying I have a probability distribution over the probability also felt off, because its not that I think the true number is drawn from somewhere, its that I have almost no evidence about anything going on here.

And it cuts both ways, which is the part that really gets me. Sometimes we get more data and I end up *less* sure, because the new data reveals the situation is messier than I'd assumed — so "more evidence" and "more confidence" come apart, and I think "more evidence" and "higher probability" come apart too. So it really looks like there are two separate things living in my head: how likely I think the thing is, and how much I'm going on / how thick the basis is. Ordinary probability talk only seems to have room for the first. I have a vague memory that some economist way back wrote a whole book on probability and insisted these were two different things, and that the second one isn't a probability at all, but I might be inventing that.

So — is there a name for the second thing? I don't want just the label, that's not the point. What I actually want to know is how the thing gets carved up: is it a feature of my evidence, of the proposition, or of me? Can two people have the same probability and different amounts of this, and if so who should defer to whom? And I've definitely heard of "ambiguity aversion," "Knightian uncertainty," and "higher-order evidence" — is this the same as any of those, or is it its own thing with its own edges? Bonus points if you can tell me what happens to the old coin-versus-hunch framing (two 50/50 bets, one a fair coin, one a vibe) — is the difference I feel there this same thing, or something else entirely. And if it turns out there's no name, coin one, but then tell me where it stops applying, because the boundaries are the interesting part.
