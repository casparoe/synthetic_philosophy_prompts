Consider a distinction between two things a translation system might do with a source sentence: decode it, meaning recover a message by exploiting correlations between strings, and inhabit it, meaning treat the source language's distinctions as reasons for choosing one rendering over another, including the ability to notice when a fluent rendering would betray the original. Argue for the position that a purely text-trained statistical or neural translation system can achieve decoding but cannot achieve inhabiting, and that this is the right way to make precise the charge that such systems are shallow. In doing so, confront the strongest objection: that the distinction collapses into the Chinese room argument and that functional success across enough contexts just is inhabiting a form of life. State how confident you are in your conclusion and identify the single claim in your argument you are least sure of.
