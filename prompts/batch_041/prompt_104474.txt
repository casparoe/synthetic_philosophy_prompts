I've been arguing with a mate about whether things are good because God commands them or God commands them because they're already good, and the annoying thing is I seem to have firm gut verdicts on a pile of cases that just won't line up into one story. Here's where I've landed. Eating shellfish: with no rule against it I reckon it's morally fine, so there the prohibition really does seem to be what makes it wrong; wearing mixed fibres, same. Torturing a child for a laugh: wrong whether or not anyone commands it, and if a god ordered it I'd say the god was in the wrong, not that the act became good. Being told by a voice from the sky to kill my own kid: I'd refuse, and I don't think refusing would be the sin. So far that's roughly "commands only create wrongness where nothing was at stake, and can't touch the deep stuff" — which was my tentative principle, and then the next batch wrecks it. Suppose a god forbids a medicine that in fact saves lives: I catch myself wanting to say the command makes taking it wrong (obedience is a virtue, right?) *and* that taking it is plainly good (it saves lives), and I obviously can't hold both. Or a critic I deeply trust declares a painting a masterpiece and I find I now actually see it as beautiful — did the praise make it beautiful, or was it always beautiful and I just needed the eye? I flip-flop there too. And promises: is a promise binding because we've got a convention of promise-keeping, or because there's something it's tracking that made breaking it bad all along? Someone told me the clever escape is to say God's commands are good because God is good by nature, but that feels like it just relabels the whole problem.

What I want from you is a principle, or a small handful of competing principles, stated precisely enough that I could actually use them — and for each clause, tell me which of my verdicts above it's doing the work on. Then test them against two or three cases I haven't mentioned; make them up, something like a landlord who sets an arbitrary house rule, or a machine that's been handed an instruction and dutifully follows it, or a club whose members treat their own bylaws as sacred. And please be honest about which of my verdicts each principle forces me to abandon, plus which single principle I'd have to swallow if I insisted on keeping both the shellfish verdict and the medicine verdict. I'd rather you tell me my gut is inconsistent than paper over it.

(One more thing — my mate keeps saying the whole dilemma dissolves once you notice the two horns are asking different questions, one about meaning and one about metaphysics. If that's right, say what the two questions actually are and which of my cases falls under which, because right now that just sounds like a dodge to me.) Please write your answer in British English.
