I do a podcast where I get one philosopher on for an hour and we stay on a single idea the whole time, no career talk, no "what got you into philosophy". Next week's guest is someone I've had on twice already: she works on the metaphysics of the special sciences and she's been pushing a much more aggressive version of the "real patterns" idea than the one people associate with Dennett. Her line, as I understand it, is that the three stances aren't just handy ways of predicting one underlying system, because "real" just means "a description that compresses the data better than its rivals do", and that means the physical stance has no special claim to be describing what's actually there. She also thinks this is enough to settle whether beliefs, money and species exist and that most of the literature on those questions is confused. I want to press her hard on this without being rude and without the interview turning into her restating the view four times, so I need questions with teeth. (I've read a fair bit of the surrounding stuff, but I'm not a philosopher, so some of my hunches below may be off.)

Draft me eight to ten questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. I'd like at least one on whether the account can be stated without circularity: if the test for reality is being a good compression, is the test itself a pattern, and what would that even mean? My guess is she'll say the test is a tool rather than a pattern, so the follow-up should ask what a tool is if not something with a nature, and whether that's a quiet slide back into plain pragmatism. I also want one on what happens when two rival descriptions compress the data equally well -- the principled one and the gerrymandered one -- since I've never understood whether she has anything to say there beyond "take whichever is handier". Then one on the difference between a real pattern and a useful fiction, because the whole view feels to me like one bad night away from instrumentalism, with a follow-up for the likely reply that real patterns support counterfactuals and useful fictions don't. Something on causation too: can a pattern be a cause, or does the physical stance always end up doing the causing, in which case isn't the pattern epiphenomenal? And something on whether an entity can be a real pattern and still get things wrong, since beliefs are meant to be the paradigm case and misrepresentation is where accounts like this usually get stuck. Finally, at least one where she has to apply it live to a case where I suspect it gives the wrong answer -- conspiracy theories that predict beautifully, or the claim that racial categories are "real" because they're predictive, which I think she'd want to resist but might not be able to on her own terms.

For each question tell me the follow-up and roughly what reply it's aimed at. And at the end, tell me which of the questions you think is actually the strongest and where your reasoning there is most likely to fail -- if there's a standard reply to the compression-tie worry that I've missed, or if I've made her position sound more extreme than anyone could plausibly hold it, say so plainly. What I really want to know is what would change your mind about which question is the killer, so I don't go in with a question that turns out to be a dud.
