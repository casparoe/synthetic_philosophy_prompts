A friend who did an econ minor gave me his explanation, I nodded along, but something feels off and I can't put my finger on what. Roughly his spiel was:

"Utility is basically the economist's word for happiness, or how satisfied you are. Problem is you can't measure happiness directly, so an economist named Samuelson came up with 'revealed preference': instead of asking people what they want, you just watch what they pick. If someone buys the apple and not the orange, that tells you the apple gives them more utility, full stop. People used to think utility was a real measurable quantity like temperature, but the ordinalist revolution showed that was unscientific — nowadays utility is just a ranking, and the numbers have no meaning at all, they're arbitrary. That's why economists roll their eyes at happiness surveys. The theory got confirmed because it predicts demand curves slope downward, and the only real challenge came from behavioral economics, which found cases where people violate their own preferences and proved everyone's irrational, so the whole thing is basically dead now."
