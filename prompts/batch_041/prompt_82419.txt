ok so at a party last weekend a friend of mine got going on Kant and animals and I genuinely can't stop thinking about it. His whole bit was that Kant's position, stated plainly, is deranged: we have no duties to animals themselves, only duties regarding them, and the only real reason you shouldn't kick a dog is what the kicking does to you -- it blunts the sympathy you need in order to stay decent to people. Animals, on this view, aren't rational or self-conscious, so they can't be ends in themselves; they're just stuff we're permitted to use, and any obligation in the neighborhood is an obligation to humanity rather than to the dog. Then he added, way too cheerfully, that a hermit who never meets another soul could do whatever he liked to a hamster. I told him he's probably wrong about the hermit, since the duty is arguably one you owe to yourself and the hermit still has one of those, but the conversation left me with nothing to laugh at, and I'd like to fix that.

So write me a joke about Kant's view of animals. Pick one specific animal and one specific scene and commit to it.
