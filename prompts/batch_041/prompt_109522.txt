I'm writing a term paper for a philosophy of science course on truthlikeness, and after three weeks in the literature that grew out of Popper's attempt to define verisimilitude I have the strong impression that the debate has stalled in a way that nobody quite admits in print. The shape of it, as I understand it, is this. Popper's comparative definition — a theory is closer to the truth than another when its truth content is greater and its falsity content no greater — was shown in 1974, apparently independently, by Tichý, Harris and Miller, to have the consequence that no false theory can be closer to the truth than any other false theory, which is exactly the case the notion was invented to handle. Popper conceded the point at once. What puzzles me is what happened next: instead of the refutation settling anything, the field divided into the content approach, the consequence approach (with refinements on which consequences may count, for instance Schurz and Weingartner's insistence that only consequences relevant in a technical sense may contribute to closeness), and the likeness approach descending from Tichý and Hilpinen and developed mainly by Niiniluoto and Oddie, in which closeness to the truth is computed from the similarity of the possible worlds in which a theory is true. All three families have been developed with real technical sophistication for fifty years, yet the surveys I've read — Oddie's paper on compatibility, trivialization and underdetermination among the three approaches, and the impossibility theorem of Zwart and Franssen — suggest that the approaches either cannot be compared with one another, or can be reconciled only by trivialising one of them, or are each underdetermined by the intuitions they were meant to capture. Meanwhile the objection that any ranking of theories by closeness to the truth shifts when the language is re-carved, or when a different similarity measure is stipulated, so that the verdict looks like an artefact of the formalism rather than a feature of the world, keeps being raised and, as far as I can tell, keeps being absorbed rather than answered. And a separate camp holds that the concept was never needed in the first place: that scientific realism and the pessimistic induction can be discussed without it, and that the whole literature is a cautionary tale about formalising a pre-theoretic notion — a line a classmate of mine presses whenever the topic comes up, and which I can't tell whether to accept or dismiss as boredom.

My hypothesis, which I'd like tested rather than adopted, is that this is not really a disagreement about how to define a concept but a disagreement about what the target of the definition is, and that the three families are answering three different questions under one name: first, an objective semantic relation between a theory and the truth, which is what Popper wanted and what the language-dependence objection attacks; second, an epistemic or pragmatic notion of how much confidence a body of evidence licenses, which is where talk of estimating truthlikeness rather than possessing it seems to belong; third, a formal device for reconstructing the history of science as progressive, where the adequacy conditions are simply whatever makes that history come out as progress. If that is right, then the impasse is partly verbal and partly a disagreement about what an adequacy condition for the concept even is. If it is wrong, I would like to know which of the alleged failures is actually doing the work.

So please diagnose the impasse, using what the various sides actually claim rather than surveying them, and tell me what kind of disagreement this is: verbal, substantive, a hidden formal impossibility, or a question that has turned out to be ill-posed because it presupposes a single privileged metric on the space of theories. Then tell me, given that diagnosis, what would actually count as progress — a theorem (an impossibility result, or a representation theorem showing the approaches coincide under a principled restriction), a distinction that separates the questions, a reasoned decision to abandon one of the desiderata, or an argument that no progress should be expected and that the interesting work has moved elsewhere. I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a verdict on which approach is correct, and I don't need citations or further reading; the bibliography is already in front of me.
