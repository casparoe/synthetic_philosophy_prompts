Im putting together the first meeting of a reading group on coherent extrapolated volition -- the idea that you extrapolate what humanity would want if it were smarter, better informed, and more the kind of thing it wishes it were, and then reconcile everyone's extrapolated wants into one coherent preference. I want to open with a villanelle, because the form seems almost to perfect for it. Two refrains. One is our actual, un-extrapolated want, said flatly in the indicative, and it comes back word for word every time -- it's the fixed starting point, we don't get to swap it out. The other is the extrapolated want, in the subjunctive or conditional, and it has to drift a little each time it returns, so that by the end you can hear that the idealization never actually lands anywhere: no fixed point, each pass just inherits the one before it. The closing quatrain will want to put the two refrains side by side and let them merge; don't let them -- keep them in different moods so they end up adjacent but unmerged. Whole poem in "we", never "I", since this is a collective extrapolation and not one person's ideal self. And somewhere it should register that the direction of the extrapolation is still set by the actual "we" it started from, which is what makes the ending feel both inevitable and a little arbitrary. Someone who knows the idea should recognize it in the form; someone who doesn't should still just get a poem.
