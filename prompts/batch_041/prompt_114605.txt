I've been down a rabbit hole about the Anthropocene lately and I want to understand the philosophy that's buried inside the bureaucracy, so put on your philosopher-of-science hat — someone who also knows some history of geology — and think carefully before you answer. Maybe brainstorm a list of candidate commitments first and then narrow it down.

Here's the practice I'm pointing at. Geologists can't just announce a new chunk of time. The commission that owns the official geologic time scale has rules: to add a unit you have to point at one specific spot in one specific rock face, sediment core, or ice core somewhere on Earth, drive a physical marker into it, and show that the signal at that point — a chemical spike, a layer of ash, a magnetic flip — turns up at effectively the same instant everywhere on the planet, so that anyone on any continent can find the same moment. That's a GSSP, a golden spike. The Anthropocene Working Group spent something like fifteen years on this, and in 2023 it nominated Crawford Lake in Ontario, whose mud has a thin layer of plutonium from hydrogen bomb tests and fly ash in it. In 2024 the proposal got voted down by a wide margin, so officially we're still in the Holocene — while everyone outside stratigraphy keeps using Anthropocene anyway.

What puzzles me is that the same community does the opposite for the deep past. Alot of the older boundaries aren't tied to any physical marker at all; they're just round numbers agreed by vote — 2,500 million years ago, 1,000 million, 720 million. Those are GSSAs, not GSSPs. So the rule looks like: where the rock record is poor, define the boundary by fiat and a number; where the record is good, insist on a physical signal. And then people keep saying the Anthropocene is really an event and not an epoch — but an event, in stratigraphy, isn't a formal unit of the time scale at all; it's a drawn-out, time-transgressive process that leaves traces. So the same phenomenon gets asked to be a unit and described as an event.

What I want from you: what philosophical commitments does this practice presuppose? Does the golden-spike requirement presuppose that an epoch is a natural kind, a real joint in nature that we discover rather than stipulate — or is it equally consistent with the view that periods are conventional, interest-relative divisions and the spike is just a coordinating device? Does the GSSA/GSSP split mean the community already accepts both pictures, or does it mean something else? And what does the event/epoch move presuppose about what a period of Earth history is — something defined by causes, by products, or by human significance? Be careful to separate what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits well with, and tell me where it's underdetermined, i.e., which rival views it dosent actually rule out.

Then tell me what would have to change if you swapped the presupposition out. If periods were purely conventional divisions picked for our own purposes, would anything in the practice have to go — would Crawford Lake stop mattering, and would there be any reason left to prefer a physical marker over a committee vote on a number?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — one sentence stating what the practice presupposes — and then give the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate my question.
