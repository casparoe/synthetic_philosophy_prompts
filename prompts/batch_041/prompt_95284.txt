I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, so I will state the question as precisely as I can and ask you to correct the vocabulary where I get it wrong. I want a number from you rather than an essay, and I want the number taken apart.

Here is the claim I want priced. Take a research programme in Lakatos's sense: a fixed hard core of commitments, a protective belt of auxiliary assumptions around it, and a pair of heuristics — the negative one telling its defenders not to aim criticism at the core, the positive one telling them how to elaborate the belt. My claim is that at any moment, for any such programme, there is a fact of the matter, fixed by the record up to that moment, about whether the programme is progressive or degenerating. By fixed by the record up to that moment I mean this: an appraiser who knew everything the programme had done so far, and nothing about what happens next, could in principle be right or wrong about which of the two it is. What I want you to weigh against it is the alternative that the verdict is available only in hindsight, or that it is partly a matter of what the appraiser antecedently values, or of what the surrounding community happens to be willing to fund.

Give me your credence in the claim, as a number between 0 and 1, and then the decomposition: the sub-questions the number depends on, a rough probability for each, and how they combine. The sub-questions I expect to see, though replace or add to them as you think right: whether a programme's excess empirical content, and the novelty of a particular prediction, can be assessed without knowing the future, or whether novelty is itself retrospective; whether the tenacity owed to the hard core ever runs out on Lakatos's own terms, that is, whether his methodology can ever license abandoning a programme or is unfalsifiable by construction; whether progressive and degenerating track a real feature of the world rather than a rational reconstruction that historians impose after the fact; and whether the thing being appraised is the programme or the community and institutions that carry it. For each term, give me the number and one line of justification, then the combination, then tell me which term you are least confident in and what evidence or argument would move it most.

Two cases to test yourself against. Case A: a programme whose core claim is that every residual anomaly in a certain data set is an artefact of a periodic drift in the apparatus. For forty years its defenders have added one belt correction per anomaly, and in year forty-one one of those corrections yields a sharp novel prediction, which is then confirmed. Nothing further comes of it. Case B: a programme that in its third year produced a bold novel prediction, which was confirmed, and has spent the twenty years since absorbing anomalies without predicting anything new. At what date, if any, was it correct to call A degenerating? Is your answer for B the same or different, and if the two answers pull against each other, which would you give up?

One more thing. A colleague proposes a rule she thinks makes my claim true by construction: call a programme degenerating if it has gone more than N years without a novel prediction that was subsequently confirmed, and progressive otherwise. Does that settle the question or only relocate it, and does it make my claim trivially true rather than interestingly true? My own number is around 0.3, mostly because I cannot see how novelty gets judged without the future; I would rather you told me I am wrong than split the difference with me.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the number — then the decomposition and the rest. No preamble, and do not restate my question.
