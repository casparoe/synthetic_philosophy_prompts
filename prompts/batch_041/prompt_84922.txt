I've been chewing on something for months and I've ended up at a position I'm not comfortable with, so I'd like you to try to break it. I didn't study any of this, I've just read a lot of arguments online and talked to people, so tell me where I'm being naive.

My view is this: nothing is inherently un-sellable. Whenever someone says a thing shouldn't be bought and sold — kidneys, water at ten times the price after a flood, a place at the front of the queue, someone's vote, paying a poor person to take part in a drug trial — I think the objection always cashes out as one of two things. Either it's about consequences (someone gets hurt, someone gets exploited, it goes badly in the long run), or it's disgust at the unfamiliar wearing moral clothes. There's no third thing. So a market in anything isn't wrong in itself; the wrongness is always either downstream of it or in the eye of the beholder.

What would count against that? Not "people find it gross" — I already know that. I mean what should actually move me. And rank it: which of these is fatal, which is merely awkward, and which should I wave away?

Some half-formed things I keep running into:

Some goods seem to have their value *made of* the fact that they aren't for sale. If I pay my friend to have lunch with me, I don't get the lunch I wanted plus a receipt, I get a different thing. My instinct is to say the money didn't destroy the friendship, it revealed there wasn't one. But I can't tell if that's a real distinction or just me saving my own view.

Markets also seem to *say* something, not just move stuff around. A price on a kidney announces that kidneys are resources for whoever can pay. Is that a third kind of objection I've been sneaking past, or does it collapse into the first two if you push on it?

The empirical stuff (paying for blood sometimes reducing donations, gouging causing hoarding) doesn't scare me, because my view already says consequences are the whole game. But maybe I'm wrong about that — maybe there's a case where everyone agrees the market works fine, nobody's worse off, and it's still wrong. Is there one? Test it on some cases: paying someone to serve your prison sentence, buying your way onto a research paper, a market in apologies, paying to have a bad story about you buried, selling the right to decide what happens to your body after you die. Which of these can I handle without straining?

And the one that actually worries me: if I'm right, this whole debate should be settled by data, and it isn't — it keeps going, and both sides keep insisting it's not really about data. Does that mean I'm missing a category of objection, or does it just mean everyone else is confused?

Last thing: tell me honestly whether my view is unfalsifiable. If every objection gets sorted into consequences or disgust, is there anything left that could count? And if not, is that a fatal problem or just the price of a view like this?
