my perception seminar is doing disjunctivism next week and i wanted to open with a poem instead of a handout, but my own attempt is going nowhere, so: write me a pantoum -- the repeating form, quatrains where the second and fourth lines of each stanza come back as the first and third of the next, and the last stanza loops back to the opening line -- that argues the disjunctivist line against the argument from illusion. four or five stanzas, your call.

heres the trick i want the form to pull off. the force of the argument from illusion is that the same kind of thing happens in you when you see the stick half in water and when you see it straight -- same look, same feel, so it must be the same kind of mental state -- and from there it slides to saying that even in the good case youre only ever aware of some inner stand-in rather than the stick. a pantoum is built on exactly that assumption: a line comes back and its supposed to be the same line. i want the poem to lean on that and then quietly break it. the lines that recur should be the ones doing the sliding, and by the time each one comes back in a new position it should be doing a different job -- once standing for the illusion case, once for the good case. the reader should be able to see that the words are identical but the state theyre describing isnt the same kind of state at all. thats the disjunctivist move and i want the repetition itself to make it, not a closing line that explains it.

one thing to get right, because this is where i keep failing: dont let it drift into the cheap version where the hallucination is just "less real", or where the point is that you could somehow tell the two apart by looking harder. disjunctivists are perfectly happy to grant that the two are subjectively indistinguishable -- the claim is about what kind of thing the experience is, not about what it feels like from the inside. if the poem hints that you can spot the difference by attending more carefully, its wrong.

and no name-dropping philosophers in the poem itself, no footnotes, no "as so-and-so argued". it should land for someone whos read the debate and also for someone who hasnt.
