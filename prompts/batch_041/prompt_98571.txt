I am an undergraduate in a third-year course on the epistemology of reasoning, and I have been asked to prepare the questions for a guest interview in our department's student-run series. The guest defends a distinctive line on the problem that Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the tortoise raises for the justification of deduction, and I want the interview to engage that line rather than recycle the standard framing. Her position, in brief, is this. The regress does not show that deduction stands in need of a non-deductive vindication, nor that we must simply accept some inference rules as brute. It shows, she argues, that we have been modelling the acceptance of a rule of inference on the acceptance of a proposition, when these are different kinds of commitment: to adopt a rule is to take on a policy governing how one moves from premises to conclusion, not to add a further premise to one's stock. On her account a deductive inference can be justified even when no non-circular premise in its favour is available, because the commitment it embodies answers not to a further premise but to the coherence of the practice with our other cognitive commitments.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions for this conversation. They should press where the position is genuinely exposed rather than invite her to restate it, and they should be answerable from the position itself; I do not want questions that would require her to cite, quote, or summarise particular texts. I would like at least one question on why the tortoise's demand continues to seem compelling even to someone who accepts her diagnosis; at least one on whether construing rule-acceptance as a non-propositional policy collapses into an anything-goes conventionalism about inference; at least one on the circularity objection, specifically whether her coherence condition is a premise in disguise and so reinstates the regress one level up; at least one on whether the account can distinguish a genuinely valid rule from a merely entrenched one, and whether that distinction does any explanatory work; and at least one on the source of the normative force of deduction, that is, why one ought to reason this way rather than merely does. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, so that the exchange can continue rather than stall. Where a question admits of more than one plausible reply, indicate briefly which reply you take to be the more likely and why.
