I want the principle behind my own convictions about when a moral constraint may be violated in order to prevent someone else from committing further violations of that same constraint. My verdicts, going case by case:

Killing one innocent person to keep a third party from killing five: not permitted. Standing aside while that third party kills the five, rather than killing the one myself: not merely permitted but required. Torturing one person to keep a third party from torturing five: not permitted. Killing the would-be killer himself, with no bystander involved, to keep him from killing five: permitted. Lying to keep a third party from killing five: permitted. Stealing a small sum to keep a third party from stealing a large one: permitted. Breaking a promise to keep a third party from breaking five promises: permitted. And the case I cannot square with the rest: killing one innocent person to keep a third party from setting off a device that would kill a million: permitted.

Two features of this list embarrass me. First, I let the constraint against harming give way once the number on the other side is large enough, yet I refuse to let it give way at five, and the only difference is the size of the number, which is precisely the consideration the constraint was supposed to block. Second, I treat the constraints against lying, promise-breaking, and small theft as yielding readily to arithmetic, while the constraint against killing does not, and I cannot say whether that reflects a difference among the constraints themselves or merely a difference in how much is at stake each time.

Tell me what principle, or small set of candidate principles, captures the pattern, naming for each clause the case that forces it. Say what it predicts for cases I have not listed: killing one to keep a third party from killing a hundred; torturing one to prevent a million deaths; and breaking a serious promise to prevent a single killing. Finally, tell me which of my verdicts no acceptable principle can keep, and whether I should surrender the million case or the five case.
