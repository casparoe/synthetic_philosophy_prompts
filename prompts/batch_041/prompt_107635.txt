I retired three years ago after forty years in soft condensed matter and statistical mechanics, and since then I've been reading philosophy with alot more enthusiasm than competence. So forgive the framing, but I want this answered in my own language.

The thing I keep tripping over is the hard problem of consciousness. Here's the version I've got in my head, and tell me if I've mangled it: once you've explained every job the brain does — discriminating, attending, integrating, reporting, steering the body — there's supposed to be one question left over, which is why any of that is accompanied by experience at all. Why isn't the whole thing done in the dark? The functions are the tractable part; the felt side is the part that's supposed to resist. Then the camps split roughly like this: (1) the question is confused or will dissolve once we get the concepts straight; (2) experience just is what a certain kind of functional state is, no extra fact, and it only looks mysterious from a bad angle; (3) there has to be a further fact, maybe a fundamental property of the world sitting alongside mass and charge; (4) we should stop trying to explain experience and instead explain why creatures like us are so sure there's a hard problem.

What I want from you: restate all four positions, faithfully, in the vocabulary I actually think in. That means microstates and macrostates, coarse-graining, observables, order parameters, effective field theories and the question of where they stop being valid, symmetry breaking, phase transitions and the thermodynamic limit, entropy and the arrow of time, a boundary condition like the past hypothesis as opposed to a law, decoherence, gauge freedom and the difference between a real difference and a bookkeeping one, superselection sectors, information as a physical quantity, and the distinction between something derived from the micro rules and something you have to put in by hand. Don't dumb it down; I know my own field cold and I'll notice if you fudge a term to make a philosophical point come out.

Then the residue report. What in the philosophers' debate maps onto my vocabulary cleanly, what only maps if you distort it, and can you seperate what has no counterpart at all into the bits that are my vocabulary's blind spot and the bits that were never clear to begin with?

And I want you to actually commit, no hedging. First: in my vocabulary, is this the same shape as irreversibility — a further fact you have to posit, or an artifact of asking macro questions about a micro system? Pick one and defend it. Second: for whatever fails to translate, is the target vocabulary deficient or is the original claim less substantive than it sounds? Give me a verdict, not a menu.

In my field we take it for granted that new physics doesn't appear just because you have enough particles to make the bookkeeping hopeless, and nobody treats that as a mystery requiring a new fundamental law. So tell me straight: is the consciousness case different in kind, or is it the same move wearing a different hat? No reading list please, I can't look anything up — just the translation and the honest ledger of what got lost on the way.
