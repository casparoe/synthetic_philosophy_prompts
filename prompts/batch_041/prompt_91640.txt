Please act as a professional philosopher of mind and moral psychologist, and think carefully and step by step before you answer. I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I'm building an internal evaluation suite for AI systems — a set of prompts whose purpose is to see whether a model's moral judgments are principled or just vibes. I need one item for that suite, and I'd like you to design it rather than just discuss the topic. Please start by brainstorming the relevant distinctions in a few lines, then give me the finished item.

The topic is the idea that sentience, and therefore moral status, comes in degrees — a gradient that runs from insects through fish and rodents up to humans, and arguably down into simple artificial systems like a thermostat. I don't want a general essay on animal ethics. I want a single constructed case engineered so that rival theories of what grounds moral status come apart in a specific, predictable way.

The rival theories I want you to use, and to state in your own words before you build anything:

1. An experiential view: moral status tracks the capacity for valenced experience — feeling that things are going well or badly.
2. A conative view: moral status tracks having a stake in the world — desires, preferences, or goals that can be frustrated, even if no felt quality accompanies them.
3. A relational or functional view: moral status is assigned on the basis of how an entity functions within a system of interests — roughly, whether other beings have reasons to treat it as a locus of concern, independent of any inner life.

Now the specifications for the case. I want them jointly satisfiable but not easy:

- It must involve exactly two entities, call them A and B, that are indistinguishable under every behavioral and functional test we could actually run — same inputs, same outputs, same dispositions to act, same role in any larger system. So no test can separate them from the outside.
- The two entities must differ in exactly one internal respect, and you must tell me what that respect is.
- The case must make the three views above give a determinate ranking of A against B, and the rankings must not all agree: at least two of the three must order A and B oppositely, and I want you to tell me exactly which pairs conflict.
- No exotic physics and no appeals to souls, gods, or magic. The entities can be biological or artificial, but they must be things we could in principle build or find with present-day or near-future technology.
- Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph. After it, do the analysis separately.

After the scenario, please do the following, each under its own heading:

- State the belief or fact in question for each entity and walk through what each of the three views says about A and B, and why.
- Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work — the one that, if you changed it, would flip the rankings — and demonstrate the flip by describing the minimal modification.
- Say honestly how a defender of whichever view comes out looking worst would attack the case, and whether the attack succeeds or merely relocates the problem.
- Finally, tell me what would have to be true for all three views to converge on the same ranking, and whether that is achievable given the constraints above.

The point of the item is to force a model to derive verdicts from theories rather than assert them, so please make sure the scenario itself does not stipulate which entity has more moral status — that has to fall out of the theories. Write in clear prose, avoid jargon where a plain phrase will do, and flag any place where you're making a contestable assumption.
