Right, so I'm a philosophy PhD student and I need to write a short research proposal — two pages, for a departmental workshop, nothing huge — and I want it to be about the Judy Benjamin problem. You probably know it, but just in case: Judy gets parachuted into a territory that's split into Red and Blue, and each of those is split into a Second Company area and a Headquarters area. She starts off with equal credence in all four quadrants. Then she gets a radio message which (roughly) tells her that if she's in Red, she's in Second Company. The puzzle is that this doesn't pin down a unique posterior. Conditionalising on the material conditional gives one answer, and it has the odd consequence that the probability she's in Red goes down — which seems wrong if what she learned is really just a conditional. The intuition that a conditional is silent about its antecedent suggests the probability of Red should stay put, which gives a different posterior. And there are other proposals too, with no consensus on how to adjudicate between them.

My half-formed idea is that the whole puzzle is a symptom of not being clear enough about what kind of learning experience is going on. There's a difference between (a) learning a proposition — say, the material conditional, or a fact about the terrain such as that the Red area contains no Headquarters — (b) learning that a conditional probability takes a particular value, (c) learning a conditional as a conditional, whatever that amounts to, and (d) receiving a report from a source whose reliability is itself uncertain. I suspect the literature has been talking past itself because different authors are modelling different learning experiences under the same name, and that once you specify the experience properly, either the problem dissolves (because only one update rule fits that experience) or it becomes a much sharper question about which rule is right for which kind of learning.

I'm also interested in a few side-angles, but I dont know if they're good or just distractions:
- The message could be a report about the terrain (the Red area has no HQ) or a report about Judy's own location given a condition. These seem like genuinely different pieces of evidence, but they get run together.
- Judy's credences are self-locating — they're about where she is. Does that matter? Does the Judy Benjamin problem look different once you take self-locating belief seriously?
- If the radio operator might be unreliable, the evidence becomes uncertain, which is exactly the kind of case Jeffrey conditionalisation was built for. Does that help, or does it just relocate the problem?
- Maybe the correct moral is that the evidence genuinely underdetermines the posterior, and the interesting question is what that means for Bayesianism as a theory of rationality. Is rationality silent here, and if so, is that a problem or a feature?
- Does the semantics of conditionals matter? If the conditional is material, learning it's true is one thing; if it's a Stalnaker/Lewis conditional or a suppositional one, learning it's true might be something else, and that might change what the update should be.
