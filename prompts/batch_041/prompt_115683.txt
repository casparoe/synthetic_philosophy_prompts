Ok so I'm taking this social choice module and for the problem set we have to either defend or dismantle our own argument, and I stupidly chose defend, so I need someone to tell me where it actually breaks. Here's the argument:

(1) A voting rule is fair only if it never gives a voter anything to gain by misreporting their preferences. If lying can get you a better result, the rule is rewarding dishonesty, and a rule that rewards dishonesty isnt fair.
(2) Gibbard–Satterthwaite: with at least three candidates, and with every candidate able to win under some preference profile, the only strategy-proof rules are dictatorships. (By strategy-proof I mean: there's no profile where a single voter, holding everyone else's ballots fixed, gets a strictly better outcome by sending a ranking other than their true one.)
(3) So every non-dictatorial rule over three or more candidates is manipulable at some profile.
(4) So at some profiles, a voter who knows the rule and knows roughly what everyone else is sending can get a better outcome by not sending their true ranking.
(5) A rational voter takes the action that gets them the better outcome.
(6) So in any such election, rational voters don't vote their real preferences.
(7) So what the rule is aggregating is a pile of misreported preferences, not the voters' actual preferences.
(8) So no non-dictatorial election with three or more candidates expresses the will of the voters.
(9) So the only defensible elections are dictatorships, or races with exactly two candidates.
(10) Dictatorship is indefensible, and the two-candidate restriction is arbitrary -- three isnt a magic number.
(11) So there's no defensible way to choose between three or more options.

I can see people wanting to jump on (5) but I dont think its shady: if you know that sending a false ranking gets you more of what you want, and you want it, why wouldn't you send it? And (2) is the only thing in here I can actually prove, so I'd really rather keep it. Someone in my tutorial mentioned a rule where you pick a voter at random and just use their top choice, and said that one is strategy-proof, but I dont think a lottery counts as a real voting method, so it doesn't rescue anything.

So: which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me?
