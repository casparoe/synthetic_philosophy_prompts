I am a graduate student in a seminar that reads across deep history, anthropology, and political theory, and two of the participants have been circling each other for a semester without ever quite joining issue. One of them holds what she calls the decline thesis: that the adoption of agriculture was, on net and by a wide margin, a catastrophe for human welfare, and that the appearance of Neolithic progress is largely an artifact of the metrics we happen to keep. The other holds what he calls the ratchet view: that the worst-mistake framing is romantic hindsight, that the evidence for forager affluence has been badly oversold, and that the interesting story about inequality is about institutions, scale, and storage rather than about farming as such. After a particularly bad session I volunteered to write the document they should have produced instead of shouting, and they have both, grudgingly, agreed to sign something if it is honest. I need you to draft it.

What I want is a genuine adversarial-collaboration statement, not a summary of a debate and not a verdict. Begin with a numbered section of agreed claims: what both sides accept about what a welfare comparison across subsistence modes would even have to measure; about which inferences the archaeological and ethnographic record can and cannot support; about the shape of the counterfactual problem, given that populations grew and that foraging may not have remained feasible at the densities farming supported; about whether violence, hierarchy, and material deprivation are distinctively agricultural phenomena or are documented in forager societies as well; and about which parts of the dispute each side concedes are downstream of value commitments rather than of evidence. Then give a disagreements table. Each row should state one residual crux, give each side's position in wording that side would actually accept as its own, name the case, comparison, or argument that both sides agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction about that case in advance. I expect at least these five cruxes, though you should add any others you think are load-bearing. First, the definition and measurement of work: whether food processing, cooking, tool maintenance, and childcare belong in the accounting, and whether the famous leisure estimates survive their inclusion. Second, the counterfactual: whether calling the transition a mistake requires a feasible alternative that was actually available to the people making it, and what happens to the claim if the alternative was only available at a much smaller population. Third, the health inference from skeletal remains, including the possibility that a sample with more bone lesions reflects a population that lived long enough to remodel bone rather than a population that was sicker, so that the apparent health signal can run opposite to the true one. Fourth, what kind of inequality matters: durable, heritable, intergenerational differences in wealth and power, versus status and prestige differentials that do not compound. Fifth, the agency and aggregation question, namely whether a slow, unwilled, multi-generational drift can be a mistake at all, and if so whose mistake it is. For each crux, make the two predictions genuinely distinct and falsifiable rather than restatements of the two positions.

Please also add a short closing section that flags which disagreements you judge to be merely verbal — for instance, if part of the affluence dispute dissolves once one side specifies whether affluence means satisfied wants or high consumption, say so and explain what remains once the equivocation is removed. The document should be self-contained and readable without footnotes; do not include citations, reading lists, or references to specific publications, and do not attribute any claim to a named author, since I want the two of them to have to own the wording themselves rather than hide behind authorities. Aim for something around 1,200 to 1,800 words, in a sober register suitable for a document two people who dislike each other have both put their names to.
