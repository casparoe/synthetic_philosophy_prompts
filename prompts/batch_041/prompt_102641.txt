Alright, I have to post my philosophy-of-mind-and-language profile for the seminar tomorrow and I want to make sure it isn't a wreck before everyone starts poking holes in it. Here's where I've landed:

1) I'm a Wittgensteinian about meaning. A word's meaning is its use in a shared practice, and there's no such thing as a language that in principle only one person could understand — so yeah, I think the private language argument basically works.

2) I think consciousness is real and first-personal. There's something it's like to be me, and I'm directly acquainted with my own experience in a way nobody else could be.

3) I think I have first-person authority. If I sincerely say my tooth hurts, I can't be wrong about that, setting aside slips of the tongue or not knowing the word.

4) I think content is external. What I mean and believe depends on stuff outside my head — my doppleganger on Twin Earth who's never seen H2O isn't thinking about water, even if every bit of the stuff inside him matches me.

5) I think Mary learns something when she walks out of the black-and-white room. No amount of public or functional information could have handed her what red looks like, and what she picks up is a genuine piece of knowledge about a phenomenal fact.
