I host a small podcast that grew out of a rationalist reading group, and next week's guest is a philosopher defending an interventionist theory of causation — the view that causal claims are claims about what would happen under an idealized intervention, with invariance across a range of interventions doing the work that laws or bare counterfactual dependence are supposed to do. He's a composite rather than a real person, so don't try to guess who it is. He holds two further commitments I want to probe: that interventionism is not a rival to the counterfactual theory of causation but an account of why the relevant counterfactuals hold, and that there is no determinate fact of the matter about "actual causation" in preemption cases, so attributions of it are pragmatic rather than descriptive.

Write me eight to ten interview questions that go beyond letting him restate the view. I want at least one question each on (a) the circularity worry — interventions are standardly characterized as surgical, breaking the target's other incoming causal arrows, which looks like it presupposes the very notion it is meant to define; (b) cases where no intervention is possible even in principle, such as treating the entire universe as a single variable; and (c) his deflationism about actual causation, including how it squares with ordinary explanatory and responsibility-attributing practice. For each of those three, include a follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give. Close with a question about what could count as evidence against interventionism, given that it reads more like a thesis about the content of causal claims than an empirical hypothesis. No generic questions about how he got into philosophy.

Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't let a contested reading of the debate come across as the consensus. Keep the whole reply under 600 words.
