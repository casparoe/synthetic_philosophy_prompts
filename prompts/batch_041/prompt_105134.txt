I am a novelist at work on a book about a strike in an invented mill town, and I have run into a conceptual difficulty that I should rather resolve before I finish the draft. Two ideas are circulating among my characters — the right to strike and the right to organise — and they cannot agree on their relationship. Some treat the two as interchangeable; others insist that one is logically prior to the other. I should like you to compare the two notions and tell me which of the rival accounts below is the more defensible, and why.

The first account holds that the right to strike is the fundamental liberty, and that the right to organise is merely instrumental to it: we protect combination because it makes effective striking possible. The second, which one of my characters puts forward and which I have not encountered stated in quite this form, holds that the right to organise is a second-order right — not a right alongside the right to strike, but a right to the conditions under which first-order rights, including the right to strike, can be exercised at all. On this view, the strike is the exercise, but organisation is what makes the exercise possible, and it therefore cannot be reduced to it.

I should like you to weigh these against one another, and in doing so to say what turns on the choice. Does the distinction survive once one asks whether the right to strike is best understood as a mere liberty — the absence of any duty to refrain — or as a claim-right, entailing a correlative duty in others not to interfere? How does each account handle the awkward cases: the wildcat strike called without the blessing of the recognised body, the secondary action that reaches beyond the disputing employer, and the agreement in which workers trade away the right to strike for a term? Finally, tell me which account would make for the more interesting betrayal in a novel, and why — that is, which of the two a character could plausibly misunderstand in a way that damages those he means to serve.

Please answer in British English.
