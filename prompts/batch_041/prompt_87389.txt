Here's the thing I need to land. Two agents who will never interact causally — no signals, no precommitment devises, no shared ancestry, nothing — but who are similar enough that one agent's choice of policy counts as evidence about what the other will do, can both do better by adopting a cooperative / compromising policy. The gains come precisely from that evidential link: choosing to cooperate is like a unilateral move that nonetheless shifts both payoffs. And the juicy part is when their values *differ*, because then there's a real question about which compromise to adopt, and the answer has to be something both can coordinate on without communicating (a Schelling-point problem, and I'd rather not put "Nash bargaining solution" on a slide until they've felt the need for it themselves). There's also a mean little issue where some subset of the cooperators can do better by walking away from the agreed compromise, and I'd love them to hit that wall on their own rather than have me announce it.

So what I want from you: the order the pieces have to go in so the conclusion feels forced rather than spooky, and why each piece has to come before the next. What's the one example that should carry the whole session — and what does it have to *not* have? My instinct was to build it on Newcomb's problem, but I'm worried that drags in twenty minutes of evidential-vs-causal decision theory and we never come back; tell me if I'm wrong. Then the misconceptions you'd expect this particular audience to form, and exactly where in the sequence each one needs to be headed off: my guesses are (a) "this is just causal influence in disguise," (b) "it only works if the other agents are literally identical / copies of me," (c) "it's wishful thinking that depends on the universe being infinite or on me being a Boltzmann brain," and (d) "if their values differ from mine, why on earth would I compromise instead of just doing my own thing." Also: one question I can put at the end that actually discriminates between them getting the mechanism and them just nodding along, and what I should explicitly refuse to cover at this level (and say out loud that I'm refusing).
