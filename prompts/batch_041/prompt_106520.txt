I'm in this AI ethics reading group and we keep getting stuck on something that comes up every week: labs say they care about honesty and deception in models, but when you look at what they actually do, it seems like three different things at once. Meanwhile there's all this work on lie detector probes that look at internal activations to see whether the model knows it's being deceptive, which only makes sense if deception is about some inner state and not just about false outputs. A friend in alignment keeps saying the real distinction is between lying and bullshitting, where a liar cares about the truth and a bullshitter doesn't, and I think that maps onto some of this, but I can't tell where.
