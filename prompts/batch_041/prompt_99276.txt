In a reading group I belong to, one participant makes the case for treating sentience as a matter of degree with a single comparison: sentience is a dimmer switch, not an on-off switch. On this picture the capacity for experience is a continuous quantity that can be turned up or down; the question 'is it sentient?' is simply the wrong question and 'how sentient is it?' the right one; a mammal, a fish, an insect and — at the very bottom of the dial — a thermostat occupy successively lower positions on one and the same scale, and moral status is supposed to track position on that scale. The comparison is doing real work: it is what licenses the conclusion that we owe at least a little to almost anything with a nervous system, and that the sharp line between the sentient and the non-sentient is an artefact of our vocabulary rather than a joint in nature.

I want an audit of that analogy, not an endorsement or a dismissal of it. Start with the correspondences: state explicitly what plays the role of the current, the resistance, the filament, the ambient light, the eye of the observer, and the threshold below which nothing is visible at all, and flag any role in the source that the target domain leaves unfilled. Then sort the disanalogies into those that matter for the conclusion above and those that are merely decorative. I am especially interested in whether the conclusion survives the loss of the following features of the source: that brightness varies along a single scalar dimension; that it is publicly measurable; that it increases monotonically with the knob; that one and the same physical quantity underlies every point on the scale; that the quantity is an intrinsic property of the bulb rather than a relation between bulb and observer; and that there is a definite setting at which the light goes out. If the conclusion does not survive intact, say what weaker conclusion the comparison can still carry, and whether some other picture — a threshold fed by graded inputs, a spectrum, an ensemble of independent lights, or a chain whose strength is set by its weakest link — would serve the intended argument better.
