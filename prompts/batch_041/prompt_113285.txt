I've been going back and forth with a couple of friends about diet ethics and I've ended up defending a position I'm not even sure I believe, so I want to see how much of it survives contact with someone who's thought about it harder than I have. Here's the view: the morally relevant quantity when it comes to farmed animals is roughly the number of sentient animals whose suffering you cause per calorie of food you eat. On that metric, chicken is close to the worst thing you can eat and beef is close to the best, because one cow turns into several hundred meals while one chicken turns into a handful — so if you're going to eat meat at all, the steak is the more humane choice. And plants beat both by a mile, obviously, since nobody gets killed to harvest a field of wheat. This also seems to explain why "go vegetarian" advice feels so unsorted to me: it treats all meat as one undifferentiated thing when the per-calorie numbers are wildly different, and it's weirdly silent about the fact that you're eating a lot more individual animals when you eat chicken.

A friend pushed back with a scenario I can't get out of my head, and I want to know if it's a serious objection or just a rhetorical trick. She says: take the same field. Either it's a wheat field, in which case mice, ground-nesting birds and a huge number of insects are killed every year by the combine, at some rate per hectare; or it's pasture for a few cows who live for years and are then killed. Run it per calorie, she says, and the pasture can come out ahead, especially on land that couldn't grow a crop anyway. If that's right then my "plants are free" clause is doing a lot of quiet work. I've also had someone tell me that the wild animal suffering crowd think the entire farmed-animal question is a rounding error next to what happens in nature, and that if you take insects seriously the whole ledger inverts — I can't tell if that's a genuinely different topic or a defeater for my view that I'm just refusing to look at.

So what I want is a map of what counts against this position. Not "meat is murder" — I already know I'm not fully comfortable with where I've landed — but the specific things that should move me: which empirical facts, which moral distinctions, which intuitions about cases, and which results from elsewhere (about death as a harm, about replaceability, about what we owe animals that only exist because we breed them) it stands or falls with. I'm especially interested in anything showing the metric itself is the wrong currency rather than just showing I got the numbers wrong, and in whether the view is secretly unfalsifiable: I've noticed that every time someone raises a problem I seem to be able to bolt on a clause — well, that's suffering-years not deaths, or that only counts if you count insects, or that's about wild animals not farmed ones — and carry on unharmed. Which of those patches are legitimate refinements and which are just rescuing the thing? Rank the defeaters from what should actually change my mind down to what I can shrug off, tell me which ones a defender of the position would themselves concede as counting against them rather than only what critics shout from outside, and if it turns out the position quietly collapses into some other view I didn't intend — say, a claim about total suffering in the wild, or a claim about suffering rather than killing — just say so.
