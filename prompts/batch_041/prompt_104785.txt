Odd question from someone who plays too much poker and reads about betting arguments for fun.

You know the standard move: if my opinions don't hang together, somebody can offer me a bundle of bets where every single bet looks fair to me on its own, and the bundle drains my wallet no matter how things turn out. Fine, thats the dutch book, everyone talks about it.

But the other direction seems to work too and I never see it mentioned. Say I think A is 40%, B is 40%, and A-or-B is 90%. I sell somebody a ticket on A-or-B for 90 cents, and buy a ticket on A for 40 cents and a ticket on B for 40. I'm up 10 cents before anything happens. If A happens I pay out the 90-cent ticket but collect on the A ticket, so it nets to nothing; same if B; if neither, nothing. So I'm 10 cents richer in every possible world, and I never changed my mind about anything.

So, two things. One: is there a name for that second kind of bundle, the one where the person with the messy opinions is the one guaranteed to win? I keep wanting to say "reverse dutch book" but that sounds like I'm inventing it.

Two, and this is the part that actually bugs me: if incoherence can hand me a sure loss in one configuration and a sure gain in another, then "your opinions are incoherent" by itself doesn't seem to tell me anything about whether I'm in trouble. The usual pitch is that incoherence means someone can take my money. But it looks like half the time it means someone can hand me money. Unless — and I suspect this is the catch — the sure-gain version quietly needs something the sure-loss version doesn't. For the sure-loss bundle, the other guy only needs me to be willing, and I am, by my own lights, on every bet in it. For the sure-gain bundle, he has to be willing to lose to me for sure, and why would he? So is that the real asymmetry, and is there a name for *that* — the fact that one direction exploits only my own dispositions while the other needs a willing victim on the far side?

Don't just hand me the labels, please. I want to know what each one actually picks out, and where exactly the line between them falls.
