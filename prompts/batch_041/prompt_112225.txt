I've been reading up on the First World War and I've got stuck on the blame question. There seem to be at least three camps: the ones who say Germany's leaders deliberately wanted a war and engineered it; the ones who say it was more of a cock-up, that the July crisis was a string of miscalculations and everyone carries some blame; and the ones who say it was basically structural — the alliances, the arms race and the mobilisation timetables meant it was going to happen whoever did what. This argument has been running for a century and nobody has budged.

My hunch is that it isn't really a fight about the documents at all. I think all three sides quietly assume the same thing — that if the war was bound to happen anyway then nobody is really to blame, and if someone engineered it then they are — and that the whole thing is actually a fight about whether a country can even *do* something like want a war, the way a person wants something, or whether "Germany wanted war" is just a handy way of talking about a lot of people who never shared a single mind.

So: is this a factual dispute, a fight about what national guilt even means, or people answering different questions under one heading? Is my hunch right, or is there a fourth reading I'm missing? And given your answer, what would count as actual progress — would it take a new document, or just everyone agreeing to ask something smaller? Please answer in British English.
