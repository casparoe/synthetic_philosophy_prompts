I chair the regional conservation committee that keeps the official protected-species list for our mountain range, and I've got to write the decision memo on a case that's splitting us down the middle. Two research groups agree on every measurable fact about the Cloud Ridge salamander populations -- the genetics, the breeding isolation, the morphology, all of it. But one group says the salamander is a genuine species we're obliged to protect, and the other says it "isn't a real species at all," just a handy label someone stuck on a population, so listing it would mean protecting a fiction. What's strange is that when I push them, they don't seem to disagree about the animals; they disagree about what it takes for a species to exist in the first place. Part of my committee thinks that means the whole fight is empty -- we should just pick whichever usage serves conservation and move on. The other part thinks that's a dodge, that one side really is right about the world and the other is wrong, and that pretending otherwise is just cowardice with a philosophy degree. The listing vote is on next month's agenda and the population is small and shrininking, so I can't stall.

Write the memo I'll circulate. It needs a concrete recommendation -- list it, don't list it, or list it under a provisional category with a firm date to revisit -- and it has to take the side I end up ruling against seriously enough that its advocates will feel answered, not steamrolled. No literature review, no citations; I need something I can defend out loud in the room.

Use these sections, in this order: "Recommendation" (lead with the bottom line and the vote I should push for -- one short paragraph); "What they actually agree on" (a paragraph or two, pinning down the shared facts and where the words start doing the work); "The case that it's a real disagreement" (two paragraphs, the strongest version); "The case that it's merely verbal" (two paragraphs); "Verdict" (a paragraph or two with the concrete steps and the revisit date); and "What would change my mind" (a few sentences).
