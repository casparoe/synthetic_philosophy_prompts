I'm preparing a short statement of my own commitments in welfare economics for a workshop, and I'd like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is where I've landed.

1. Social evaluation is ex ante, and social preferences satisfy the expected-utility axioms: the value of a lottery is its expected social value. I take Harsanyi's aggregation theorem to show that, given the ex ante Pareto principle, the social welfare function must be a weighted sum of individual utility functions.

2. Ex ante Pareto: if every individual's prospect under lottery L is at least as good as under lottery M, and strictly better for at least one person, then L is socially better than M.

3. Anonymity: the social ordering is invariant under any permutation of persons. Who is who never enters into the evaluation.

4. Prioritarianism: a gain in welfare counts for more when it goes to someone worse off. I want this to be a feature of the social evaluation itself, not a by-product of how much the individuals happen to dislike risk.

5. Procedural fairness: I think a fair coin can be better than an arbitrary assignment. If two patients are identical in every respect, each facing the same risk, and there is a single dose that will save exactly one of them, I want to say that deciding by coin is better than giving the dose to whoever happens to be first in the queue.

I have the uneasy feeling that not all of this can be held together, but each item still looks right to me on its own, and I can't see which one is doing the damage. Work through the set: say which commitments are actually in tension and which pairs only look as though they are, rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and for each genuine tension identify what would have to give and what the least costly repair would be. If a single revision would clear up more than one of them, say which. I don't need pointers to the literature; I need the audit. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
