Ok, so I've been having the same argument with a friend about Maxwell's demon for like two years now and we're totally stuck, and honestly I don't want you to settle who's right so much as tell me what kind of disagreement we're actually having.

Here's where we are. The demon sits in a box, opens a little door, sorts fast molecules from slow ones, and seems to make heat flow uphill without doing any work. Classic. My friend is in the information camp: the demon can't win because its memory is physical, and eventually it has to erase what it learned, and erasing costs at least kT ln 2 per bit (Landauer's principle, if I've got the name right), so the ledger balances and the second law survives. I'm in the bookkeeping-is-the-whole-story camp: I think plain statistical mechanics already kills the demon — any correlation it builds with the gas is itself a low-entropy arrangement that had to be paid for somewhere, and if you're honest about coarse-graining there's just no free lunch — and the erasure-cost story is either a restatement of that or an extra, contingent claim about how memory has to physically work. We agree on all the equations. We agree the second law holds. We never move.

My hypothesis (just a hypothesis) is that this isn't really a disagreement about physics, it's a disagreement about what an explanation has to do — whether 'the demon fails' needs a mechanism that acts on the demon, or whether a theorem about the demon's memory is enough. But I can think of at least two other readings and I want you to tell me which one actually fits: (a) it's a real disagreement about whether information is a physical thing at all or just a bookkeeping device, and (b) it's a hidden verbal dispute, because 'entropy' in my mouth and 'entropy' in hers might be two different quantities — the Shannon one, which is about a probabilty distribution, and the thermodynamic one, which is about the actual state of a physical system — and each of us is assuming ours is the one that matters.

To stop us just trading slogans, here are two cases I want you to use as tests. First: the demon's memory is never erased and never reused — it just grows, one fresh bit per measurement, forever, and at the end of the day we chuck the whole memory out of the universe (or just stop counting it). Does the demon win? If no, tell me exactly where the debt is being recorded and why 'we'll pay later' isn't cheating. If yes, tell me what breaks. Second: imagine a universe with our thermodynamic laws but where the demon can reset its memory for free, by some weird physical process. Does the second law actually fail there, or does it survive by some other route? The point of that one is to figure out whether the information story is doing any independent work or is just the thermodynamic story wearing different clothes.

Then diagnose us. Verbal dispute, dispute about what counts as an explanation, two groups answering different questions under one heading (one 'why can't the demon win', one 'what is information'), or a genuine unresolved physics question we've dressed up as a philosophy one? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move us — a theorem, a distinction, an experiment, a change of question — or is this one of those where nothing should be expected to move it and we should just stop?
