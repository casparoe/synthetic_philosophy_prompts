Ok, this is a weird one, but I've got a family situation that's turned into a fight and I don't trust anyone in the family to be neutral about it. At my sister's birthday dinner last month, my brother-in-law — who'd had a few — told a story about my late father that made him look like a fool, some botched business deal, in front of about fifteen relatives plus a couple of my parents' oldest friends from back home. My mom was sitting right there. I didn't say anything at the table, I just sat there and ate. Now everyone has a position on what I should have done, and each position seems to come with a whole philosophy of life attached, and I genuinely can't tell if they're disagreeing about facts or just about which world they grew up in. My uncle says that by sitting there I let the family get humiliated, and that if I don't confront the guy and get an apology in front of the same people, we'll be known as a family you can walk all over — and in our hometown, he says, that name is basically the only thing my dad left us and it still brings people into the shop. My wife grew up in a big city, and her line is: you call a lawyer or you let it go, but you don't perform your feelings for an audience; the dignified move is to act like it can't touch you, because your worth doesn't depend on what fifteen people think. And my nineteen-year-old daughter says they're both wrong — the real problem is that a man felt entitled to humiliate a dead man and a grieving family, and that I should put it up online and make it a thing, so he's held accountable and so other people who've been treated like this know they're not alone. The three of them went at it over dinner the other night and it got ugly.

What I want isn't a vote on who wins. I want to know whether these three ways of handling an insult are even comparable — whether there's any sense in which one of them is the better way to be a person, or whether ranking them is like ranking languages. My half-baked suspicion is that each one only makes sense under conditions the other two don't have: my uncle's world is one where nobody's coming to help you and your name is a kind of currency, my wife's is one where the courts and the general indifference of strangers make your name cheap, and my daughter's is one where being seen as someone who was wronged is itself a kind of standing. If that's right, does it follow that none of them is superior — or can you still say something like "this one is better at letting people live together" or "this one produces less misery" without claiming it's truer? And is saying they're all just different the same as saying they're all equally good? I have a feeling those two aren't the same thing and that people mix them up alot.

Then the practical bit: if you can't rank them, is there anything left to tell a person about what to actually do, other than "do what your people do"? Push back wherever my framing is too tidy. The thing I'd most like you to chew on is whether the dignified response — your worth is independent of other people's opinions — quietly assumes a world with working institutions, and if so whether that makes it a luxury rather than a virtue. Also tell me if I'm wrong to think the three of them aren't even answering the same question: one is about respect, one about self-respect, and one about who gets to be believed.
