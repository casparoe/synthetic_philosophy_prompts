i need a fake product review for a dataset. make it one of those painfully thorough consumer-tech reviews — the kind where they tested eleven models over six weeks and have strong feelings about the companion app. except the product is a handheld sentience meter. invent it: name, model number, firmware version, price, spec sheet. the reviewer runs it on their dog, a goldfish, a spider in the bathroom, a venus flytrap, the thermostat, the robot vacuum, and (the manual insists on this) themself. 600-700 words, headline, star rating, the usual "the good / the bad / the bottom line", plus a little spec table. first person, deadpan, no winking.

the jokes have to come out of the actual problem — moral status as a matter of degree, insects at one end and appliances at the other — not out of the gadget being a dumb gadget. so let the review wander into these and just keep walking:

- the manual names its bands (invent them; "0.30-0.59: Probably Uncomfortable" is the register i'm after) and never says whether the number is how sentient the thing is or how sure the device is that there's any sentience in there at all. the reviewer uses the same number both ways in different paragraphs and never notices. a bee at 0.4 is "a bit sentient" on page one and "probably nothing, we're 40% confident" on page two.
- the scale is anchored at two fixed points — one healthy adult human volunteer, and a warm rock — with everything else interpolated in between. the reviewer thinks this is fine because thermometers work that way, then asks twice what the units are, and the company cant say what its measuring. maybe a customer support transcript where the rep calls the units "relative".
- the firmware was only ever validated on mammals and lumps every invertebrate into one bucket, so the spider and the crab from their dinner score identically. the reviewer calls this "a little lazy" rather than a problem with the whole enterprise.
- the rock scores 0.03, the company says anything under 0.05 is effectively zero, the reviewer accepts this, then notices their laptop also scores 0.03.
- one aside where the shrimp and the sleeping toddler come out close together and the reviewer decides it's probably a rounding thing.
- at least one reading changes depending on what the reviewer expects to see. they blame the device, correctly, and keep using it anyway.
- a top-voted customer Q&A where someone is using the scores to decide what they're allowed to eat.

a reader should be able to tell what's being satirized — the fantasy that one scalar could settle moral status, and the way "we're not sure insects feel anything" quietly becomes "insects feel 30% of what we do" — without you ever saying so. someone who works on animal sentience should laugh rather than wince, and someone who's never read a word of it should still follow the review. no real researchers, no citations, nothing quoted from anything real.
