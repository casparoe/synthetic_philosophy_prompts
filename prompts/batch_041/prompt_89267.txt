I am a PhD student in political philosophy preparing a prospectus on reparations for historical injustice, and I would like a consistency audit of the commitments I have been working with before I present them to my committee. The package is as follows. (1) Some historical injustices—chattel slavery and colonial land dispossession are the paradigms—generate present-day claims to reparations. (2) Those claims are owed to groups, not merely to individuals, because the wrongs were group-directed and their present effects are group-structured. (3) The moral case for reparations does not require that any currently living person be culpable for the original wrong. (4) The strongest justification for reparations is forward-looking: they are warranted by their capacity to reduce present injustice and rebuild political relationship, not by backward-looking guilt. (5) A person cannot be held morally responsible for a wrong she did not commit and could not have prevented. (6) The state can inherit and carry obligations from predecessor regimes even when its current citizens are not personally culpable. (7) A just scheme would be administered through a trust financed by a land-value tax on parcels whose titles trace, with a rebuttable presumption, to a specific nineteenth-century dispossession; eligible descendants could choose among cash payments, community infrastructure, and symbolic recognition. (8) Eligibility should be based on lineage: descendants of those wronged have a stronger claim than equally needy non-descendants. I suspect that some of these commitments pull against each other, but I am not sure which, and I am not antecedently attached to any one of them. Please audit the package rather than defend it.
