I'm a novelist at the outset of a book in which karma is not a metaphor but a law of nature: every morally weighted act propagates consequences into the actor's later lives, and no one carries memories across the divide. The narrative follows three generations of a family who gradually detect the pattern and try to exploit it. Before I spend a year building the world, I want an agenda rather than a survey. Of the open questions surrounding karma as a theory of moral desert, which should I settle first, and in what order? I am less interested in the inventory than in the argument for the ordering: which questions are upstream of the others, in the sense that their answers change what the remaining questions even are; which are tractable for someone working by argument alone, with no fieldwork or archive; and which I may leave deliberately indeterminate because no plausible resolution would alter what I can write. Please say what would move a question up or down that list, and identify the point at which my premise — consequence without recollection — forces a commitment I cannot dodge.
