So my partner and I have been going back and forth for months about whether to actually have a kid, and at some point I made the mistake of reading up on the philosophy of it, and now I'm stuck. The thing that keeps me up is the procreation asymmetry — the idea that it's fine, or at least not required, to refrain from bringing a happy person into existence, but it's bad to bring an unhappy one in. Almost everyone I read seems to share the intuition and then splits into two camps about whether it can actually be true. One camp (Benatar-ish) says the asymmetry is just a datum any decent theory has to capture. The other says it's incoherent, because it treats the absence of suffering as a good even when there's nobody around to enjoy that good, while refusing to treat the absence of pleasure the same way — which is either an equivocation on what good means or a brute intuition doing all the work.

What frustrates me is that this argument has been going for decades and nobody moves an inch. So what I actually want from you isn't a verdict on who's right. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement. Is it substantive, or are the two sides answering different questions under one heading — one side asking about the value of outcomes, the other about the reasons we have to act? Is there a hidden metaphysical question underneath it, like whether something can be bad for someone who never exists at all, or whether it even makes sense to say that never existing could be better for someone than existing? Or is it really just bedrock intuitions about the asymmetry that neither side can argue for, in which case nothing is ever going to move it?

And if it is bedrock, say so plainly, and tell me what that implies for the constraints the theory has to meet — because the same intuition is supposed to be what keeps you from sliding into the repugnant conclusion or the sadistic conclusion, and I can't tell whether those are genuinely seperate problems or just the same stalemate wearing different hats. If there's a distinction or a reformulation that would actually dissolve the standoff, I'd love to hear it. If the honest answer is that it's a hard unsolved problem and no new argument is going to fix it, I'd definately rather hear that than another survey of the positions.
