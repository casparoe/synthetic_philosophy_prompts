Give an example of a conceptual issue for the account of testimonial injustice on which the wrong consists in a credibility deficit caused by identity prejudice on the hearer's part. Construct a case in which a speaker's testimony is discounted, in which the hearer's discounting is warranted by the evidence available to her and free of prejudice — indeed, in which a more careful epistemic agent would discount the testimony even more — and in which the exchange nonetheless wrongs the speaker in her capacity as a knower. Your example should make clear what does the wrong if not the hearer's motives or information, and what it implies for the claim that prejudice is the essential ingredient of testimonial injustice.
