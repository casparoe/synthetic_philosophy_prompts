I chair the special collections access review panel at a university library, and we've got a weird one. A donor has given us a manuscript she calls the Einstein Codex: it's a huge bound volume that supposedly contains a complete, step-by-step state description of Einstein's brain at one moment, written in a formal notation. A cognitive scientist on our faculty wants to run a reading protocol on it: a team would read the pages aloud in a specific order and at a specific pace, with the idea that the reading process itself would implement the brain's dynamics and let them have a conversation with the thing. She says if it works, the book is conscious; if it doesn't, it's just a very long text. She wants a private room, 18 months, and permission to treat the book as a research subject, meaning she'd stop the reading if the book seemed to protest or suffer, and she'd need to know whether we consider it a person for consent purposes. She also says the book's notation is a complete state-transition table, so the reading protocol is just a slow interpreter and the mind would be in the pattern, not the paper. Half the panel thinks this is a cute thought experiment and we should just say no to the personhood stuff and let her read it as a text. The other half thinks if there's any real chance the reading instantiates a mind, we can't just wave it through. I just reread the Hofstadter dialogue in The Mind's I where Achilles and the Tortoise go back and forth about a record and a book, and I'm still not sure whether the consciousness is supposed to be in the book, in the reading, or in the reader. Draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. I need a concrete recommendation, approve, reject, or approve with conditions, plus a time frame and conditions if you go that way. Take seriously the strongest point on the other side, dont just restate the thought experiment. And tell me what would change your mind: what evidence or argument would flip your recommendation, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Keep it in plain language, no citations needed.
