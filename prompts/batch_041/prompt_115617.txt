ok, longtime LW/rat-adjacent lurker here, and I've got one of those "is there a word for this" itches that's been going for like two years and I finally want to scratch it.

Setup: I play Nomic — the game where changing the rules is itself a move, and the rulebook contains the rules about how the rulebook gets changed. Some of the starting rules are marked as protected, so you have to first pass a rule turning them into ordinary rules before you can touch them. Fine, fun, whatever.

Here's the pattern I keep noticing, and it's not really about the game.

A group I was in had a rule that said the rules could be changed by a vote of the mods. During a big blowup they voted to change that rule so that future rule changes need a unanimous mod vote plus 30 days notice. Someone objected: under the old rules that vote was valid, but under the new rules it wouldn't have been — so which rulebook was actually in force while the vote was happening? And the answer is there isn't one, because the only thing that says which rulebook is in force is the rulebook. Same thing in Nomic when people start passing amendments to the amendment procedure: each tightening is passed by the procedure it just made harder to use, so the fifth tightening is exactly as legitimate as the first, even though the two are nowhere near the same size in effect.

What I'm after is the direction of it, I think. A self-governing system spending its own authority to shrink its own authority, where every step is legal under the rules as they stood at the time, and there's no outside — no court, no founder, no god — that can say "that one didn't count." Not the logical paradox of a rule referring to itself; I know that's a thing and it's not what I'm pointing at, because the paradox shows up even when nothing ratchets. And yeah, the game is literally named after the concept of rules that change rules, but "rules that change rules" is the generic case and it doesn't capture the tightening.

Nearest things I've got, none of which fit: Ulysses contracts / precommitment, but that's one act by an agent with an outside vantage on itself, whereas here the binding is done by the very machinery being bound; "entrenchment," but that's a static property some rules just have from the start, not a process a system does to itself; and plain old self-reference, which is way too broad to be useful.

So: does this have a name, in legal theory or systems theory or game design or wherever? If yes, tell me the term and what the cleanest example is. If no, coin one. Either way I want the edges, not just a label — is it still this thing if there IS an external authority that can veto an amendment? Does it need the ratchet, or is a single self-tightening enough? Where's the line between this and just "a bad amendment everyone regrets"? And does the founding case belong here too — the very first rulebook has to authorize its own existence somehow, or everything downstream is unauthorized, and I've never gotten that to go away. Same phenomenon or different one? I'm not after reading lists, I just want the thing carved up properly.
