I'm hosting a work-in-progress session next month and the guest works on diachronic rationality. His line is that money pumps don't show what everyone thinks they show: he says a pump is really an arbitrage opportunity, and arbitrage needs a counterparty who can commit to the trading strategy and who's willing to take the losing side of every step. So what pumps exploit isnt inconsistent preferences, it's an asymmetry in commitment technology -- the pump-maker can tie his own hands, the agent can't, and hand the agent the same technology and the pump evaporates. Give me eight to ten interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give. I want at least one asking whether his move proves too much, since any diachronic exploit is blockable by commitment -- would the same reasoning dissolve the diachronic Dutch book against an agent whose credences don't update by conditionalization, and is he ok with that? -- and one on the pump-maker's own ledger, since he has to keep trading even if the agent walks away early, so whose preferences are the weird ones? And one about an agent who's sophisticated, foresees the whole sequence, and has no commitment device available: irrational, or just unlucky, and either way doesnt that make the argument about her options rather than her preferences? Skip the "should preferences be transitive" stuff, I want the hard version.
