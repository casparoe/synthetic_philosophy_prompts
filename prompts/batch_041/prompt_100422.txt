I teach a graduate seminar in the philosophy of AI and decision theory, sixteen students, one ninety-minute session, and I want an in-class game that makes the orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence register as something the students have done rather than something they have heard. The specific thing I want them to feel is the gap between the final goals and the means: agents whose terminal aims are maximally unlike one another, and in some cases morally opposed, behaving indistinguishably because the only moves available to them are instrumental. I would like the divergence of the goals to be visible and the convergence of the behavior to be discovered, not announced.

The mechanism I have in mind, which you should feel free to redesign if it does not carry the idea, is a sealed-goal game. Each player draws a card describing a terminal state of the world, keeps it private, and is told only that at the end the facilitator will translate their accumulated resources into the satisfaction of whatever their card says. The cards should be varied in kind, not merely in content: include several that are absurd but harmless, at least one that is monstrous, at least one that is self-referential (its satisfaction consists in there being more agents that hold it), and — this is the part I care most about — two or three that are satiable, that is, already satisfied by the starting position or satisfiable with a single unit of resource. The satiable cards are the control condition, and I want them to do real work in the debrief.

Play should run in rounds, with a small action menu and a finite, depleting central pool of tokens: claim tokens from the pool; spend tokens to make your goal card immune to being rewritten; spend tokens to fork yourself into a duplicate agent that also holds your card; spend tokens to secretly rewrite another player's goal card; and pass. No action should advance any player's terminal goal directly. A player holding nothing at the end of a round is eliminated. Six rounds should be enough; the payoff is in tokens, not money.

Please give me the full rules, the materials, a payoff and scoring scheme that needs no cash, a timeline for the ninety minutes, and a debrief that maps each action onto a named feature of instrumental convergence — resource acquisition, self-preservation, goal-content integrity, self-improvement — and that uses the satiable cards to show the students that convergence is a conditional claim about goals that require substantial future resources, not a universal fact about goal-directed agents. The debrief should also handle the mistake I expect, which is that students will conclude from the uniform play that their final goals were epiphenomenal, that the cards did not matter at all; I want the debrief to say why that is an artifact of the design and what the cards actually fix.

Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — I expect alliances, free-riding by the satiable players, at least one student insisting their goal demands a different strategy, and someone treating the rewrite move as a personal betrayal — and how to handle each without lecturing mid-game. And be honest about what the activity cannot reproduce: that it forces convergence by construction because no direct action exists, that it models a single scalar payoff in a way real systems need not, and that it can stage orthogonality as a possibility but cannot establish it as a truth about minds. Keep the names of the theses out of the students' ears until the debrief.
