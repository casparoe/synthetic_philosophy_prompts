I work as a backend engineer and have no formal training in philosophy; what I know about AI alignment comes from papers and blog posts I've read on my own time. I've become fairly convinced by the iterated amplification and distillation picture, and I'd like you to pressure-test the version of it I actually hold, rather than a caricature.

As I understand it: you begin with a human overseer who is trustworthy but can only handle problems of limited difficulty. That human breaks a hard question into smaller questions it can answer or check, and a model is trained to imitate the decomposed reasoning. Once the model is competent enough, it takes over the role of decomposer for the next round, with the human still approving each step, and the loop repeats — each round buying additional capability while human approval remains the only source of the training signal. What I find compelling is that the alignment story seems to come for free: if every step is approved by an aligned human, and the model is only ever trained to reproduce what the human approved, then the deployed system should inherit the human's values, and the only thing left to worry about is competence — the model being too weak, or errors compounding across rounds — rather than the system pursuing something other than what we want.

What would count against this? I want the arguments, observations, and thought experiments that should move me, sorted by how decisive they would be, including anything that has arguably already been demonstrated in practice rather than merely hypothesized. I'm especially interested in whether the composition step is doing real work: does "aligned components, therefore aligned whole" follow from anything, or does it quietly assume what it is supposed to establish? I'd also like an honest assessment of whether the picture has been qualified into unfalsifiability — if the upshot is "the risk lives in the training process," is that a substantive concession or a restatement of the problem?

Please tell me which of these the view's own defenders would already concede, not only what its critics say, and end with your judgment about whether there is any observation that should make me give the view up rather than patch it.
