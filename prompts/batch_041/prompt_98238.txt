My friend who works on AI safety sent me this one-paragraph summary of low-impact AI: "A low-impact agent is one that minimizes side effects. A side effect is just the difference between the world the agent actually produces and the world that would have happened if the agent had never existed. So low-impactness is a purely descriptive, value-free property -- you can measure it without knowing what's good or what the agent is for. The hard part is just prediction: getting the counterfactual right." I'm a philosophy person, not an AI person, and it sounds too clean to me. Go sentence by sentence: what would someone working on low-impact AI accept, what's a standard simplification, and what's flat wrong? In particular, does the "value-free" claim survive the fact that you have to choose a baseline and decide which differences count as side effects rather than intended effects? And if minimizing side effects can conflict with doing the task at all, does that make the measure value-laden after all?
