Hey, I'm writing a short story for a workshop and I want it to be about Benacerraf's dilemma, the one where mathematical sentences look like they're about objects, but then it's hard to see how we could ever be in causal contact with those objects in order to know anything about them, so you seem to get the semantics or the epistemology but not both. What I definately don't want is a story where a philosopher in an armchair explains the puzzle and then someone names a famous fix and everyone goes home happy. I want the thing felt from the inside, by people who have to act on it.

The premise I've been playing with: a small lab builds a machine, everyone calls it the Reckoner, that answers number theory questions, and as far as anyone can tell it answers them by being physically affected by the facts themselves. Not computing from axioms. The needle moves before anyone has typed an input, and it lands on results no one in the lab has proved yet, and later those results turn out true. Two senior people in the lab fall out over what that means. One says this is the causal contact the platonist needed all along, so the dilemma is dead, they've built a detector for abstract objects. The other says a proposition cant push a needle, so what they've built is a very fancy symbol shuffler and the contact is an artifact of the design that they're reading back into it. The story has to live inside that fight without refereeing it.

A few things I'm after. Make the machine's behaviour genuinely ambiguous in a principled way, not just vague: every weird reading it gives should be explainable both ways. Put something human on the line for one of them, a dead father's unfinished theorem, a grant, whatever, so the disagreement has a cost. Dont let any character deliver a position I could have gotten from a textbook, no lectures, no naming the camps. And please don't resolve it. I'd rather the last scene make both readings slightly worse than they were at the start.

After the story, give me two or three sentences on what you were doing with the dilemma and what you deliberately left open. Whole thing under about 2000 words.
