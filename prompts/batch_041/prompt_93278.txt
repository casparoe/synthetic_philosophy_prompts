I've spent a while with the dogmatism paradox and I've ended up on the dogmatist's side, though not for the reason people usually give. Here's the position I actually hold: if you know P, then any incoming information that looks like evidence against P is, by definition, misleading evidence. It can't be genuine evidence against P, because knowledge is factive and P is true -- so evidence that counts against P would have to be evidence for something false. Which means there's never a rational requirement to lower your confidence in P when it shows up. The most you owe anyone is an explanation of why the thing looked like evidence. I don't think that's a reductio. I think it's just what knowledge buys you.

What I want from you is the actual defeaters, sorted by how decisive they'd be, with an honest split between the ones the dogmatist will just concede and shrug at versus the ones that genuinely threaten the view. A few things I'm already chewing on, in case they help you calibrate:

- Does this just collapse into "knowledge is indefeasible"? That seems obviously false -- I can know P and still have a defeater arrive later. If my view can't tell those apart, that's a problem.
- The higher-order stuff. New evidence that isn't about P at all but about the relyability of whatever process gave me the belief that P. My "by definition misleading" move seems to have nothing to say here, and that's where I feel the view wobble.
- Whether "evidence against P can't be genuine because P is true" is doing any real work, or whether it's just restating factivity and then smuggling in a substantive epistemic claim under cover of it.
- Whether any version of this survives, or whether the whole thing inevitably degrades into "you may ignore counter-evidence whenever you're confident," which I definitely don't want.

And then the meta question: has the "misleading by definition" move insulated the position into unfalsifiability? If it has, is that a bug, or is it just what a factive notion of evidence has to do? I'd rather you tell me it's insulated and that this is fine (or not fine) than pretend there's a clean empirical test.
