Not the standard treatment, though: the puzzle itself is the kind of thing half the room half-remembers from undergrad, and I want the session to really be about the part that always gets skipped, which is whether an intention is even the sort of thing you can form on purpose, and what that tells us about the difference between intending to do something and merely being disposed to do it. Here's the wrinkle I want to build around, and I'm not sure it's a good idea: I want to run the original toxin case, but then swap out the billionaire for a machine -- a box that scans you overnight and pays out if it detects the right neural signature -- and then a third version where it's a human psychologist who reads your file and decides. My hunch is that the machine version makes the puzzle feel easier for the wrong reason and the psychologist version makes it feel harder for a better reason, but I genuinely can't tell if that's a real distinction or just me being cute.
