I've been chewing on something for weeks and what I want is a checklist, not an answer. You know the one about the boss who starts a program purely for profit, knows for certain it'll foul the river, and couldn't care less about the river — everyone says he fouled it on purpose. Keep him identical, just flip the side effect so the program cleans the river up instead; he still couldn't care less. Now suddenly nobody says he cleaned it on purpose. Same knowledge, same indifference, only the direction of the side effect changes. I read about it somewhere and now I keep inventing my own versions. Mine: the side effect breaks a rule but hurts nobody at all — the program means some form gets filed late and that's against an internal rule, no harm to anyone. Which way is he supposed to come out? Because whichever story explains the river pair, I can make my case land the other way, and that bugs me.

So what would an account of this have to deliver before I believed it? Which cases it has to get right, mine included, and for each thing you demand, the case that puts it on the list. Which distinctions it can't blur — on purpose vs. blameworthy, seeing it coming vs. meaning it, the act vs. what just comes along with it. Which things it's not allowed to say: I don't want a story on which 'he did it on purpose' is just a sneaky way of saying he's awful, since plainly it isn't only that. Say which of these are non-negotiable and which are just taste, and above all whether some of them can't all hold at once — if two things everyone wants turn out to be incompatible, thats the real result and I want to hear it. No reading list please, I won't read the papers. End with a last line \answer{...} naming the one requirement you think is hardest to satisfy.
