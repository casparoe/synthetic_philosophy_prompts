I have spent the past year reading a great deal of alternative history — lost-continent theories, ancient-astronaut claims — and I keep meeting a move I cannot find a name for. The theory arrives with an explanation of its own rejection already built in: the artifacts are missing because they were destroyed or lie under the sea; the experts who dismiss it are, by the theory's own lights, either dupes or gatekeepers. So far that is merely a theory which cannot be refuted. What I want a name for is the step that follows. The proponents treat the theory's survival of this criticism as evidence in its favor: it has been attacked for fifty years, they say, and it is still standing, so there must be something to it. The immunity and the corroboration are the same fact looked at from two sides, and while the content of the immunizing story varies from case to case — sometimes suppression, sometimes the prejudice of the academy, sometimes simply that the crucial evidence has never been properly looked for — the inference from immunity to truth stays fixed.

I meet the same shape well outside history: a colleague whose explanation of a failed project quietly absorbs each new failure, and who offers the fact that the explanation has outlasted every attempt to refute it as the strongest reason to accept it.

Is there an established term for this? I have seen unfalsifiability invoked, but that names only a defect, whereas the thing I am after is the conversion of the defect into a virtue. Nor is it quite the same as an ad hoc patch or a conspiracy theory, since the suppression story is not the load-bearing part — a theory can be patched without anyone's citing the patching as proof. If a term exists, tell me how it differs from these neighbors; if none does, coin one, and give me a definition and the boundary cases — in particular, whether ordinary scientific resilience, which also declines to abandon a theory at the first anomaly, falls inside the category or outside it. A name by itself will not help me; what I need is the phenomenon itself carved up.
