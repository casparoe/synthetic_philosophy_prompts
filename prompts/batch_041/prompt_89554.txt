I am a novelist at work on a multigenerational saga about an American fortune, and my characters keep arguing past one another about inherited wealth without ever locating the real point of disagreement. I would like a "which view of inheritance do you actually hold?" sorting quiz for my own use and for a companion piece on my author's site: eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete case from the novel rather than a label, each with two or three answer options, and a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto the distinct positions in the debate. The point is to sort readers by what they say about the cases, so no question should simply ask whether inheritance should be taxed.

The cases I care about are these: a family firm that survives only if kept whole; a fortune that has become mostly political access and social standing rather than money; a trust that skips a generation; an heir who publicly renounces the money but keeps the name and the connections; and a first-generation fortune set against a third-generation one. Build the branches around the questions that genuinely separate the positions — whether bequest is a protected exercise of liberty or a transfer of unearned advantage; whether the operative baseline is equal starting resources or the absence of concentrated private power over others; whether the family is a site of legitimate partiality that limits what equality can demand; and whether the remedy must be a tax at all, as against a cap, a public inheritance for everyone, or the outright abolition of bequest. Each leaf should name the position, or say "unnamed" if nobody holds it, with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Where two positions answer every case alike, flag them as ones the quiz cannot separate rather than forcing a distinction. Please write in American English.
