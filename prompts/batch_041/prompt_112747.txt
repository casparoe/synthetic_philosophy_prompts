I have been reading a trade book on the long-run history of violence, and what I want from you is an audit of how it summarizes the state of the field, not of the book as a whole. Its central chapter puts the matter roughly as follows:

"Violence has been falling for centuries, and this is among the most robust findings in the social sciences. In medieval Europe a person was perhaps ten to thirty times more likely to be murdered than in a modern European state, and the same downward pattern appears in deaths from war, torture, and judicial execution. The decline has been steady and continues into the present; the two world wars are best read as a temporary reversal rather than a refutation. The causes are now well understood: the rise of the state and its monopoly on legitimate force, together with the civilizing process that made self-restraint second nature. The long peace among the great powers, and the fact that democracies do not fight one another, confirm the trend. Because the evidence is quantitative, the question whether violence is declining is an empirical one with a determinate answer; the remaining critics are mostly people who dislike the conclusion for political reasons, and those who extend the word 'violence' to poverty or inequality have simply changed the subject."

Please sort this out claim by claim. Which parts would a researcher who works on this material accept as broadly right, which are the standard simplifications of a live and unsettled debate, and which misrepresent what the evidence, or the critics, actually say? I am particularly interested in three things the passage glosses over or leaves out: the difference between a falling rate and a falling absolute number of violent deaths; what the word 'violence' is being made to cover, and whether that is a merely verbal quarrel or a substantive one; and whether the closing claim — that the question has a determinate answer and that dissent is political — survives correction of the earlier claims. Tell me as well which single sentence is the most misleading, and why. There is no need to name particular authors or works; I am after the substance.
