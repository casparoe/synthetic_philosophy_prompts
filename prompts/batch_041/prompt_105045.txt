I've been argueing with a friend about whether historical periods are real things or just labels, and I want to know what my actual view is, because my verdicts don't obviously hang together.

My verdicts. The Middle Ages: not a real thing, just a label later people slapped on a stretch of time, though I'll keep using it. The Renaissance: not real either, mostly a story the nineteenth century told about itself backwards. The Scientific Revolution: real — something did change, roughly when we say it did. Late Antiquity: real, even though a twentieth-century historian basically invented it, and even though the whole point of the label was that nothing dramatic happened at the edges. The Axial Age: not real, just a philosopher's projection, and I don't care how popular it is. The Industrial Revolution: real, obviously. The Dark Ages: not real.

Here's what bugs me. Late Antiquity and the Axial Age were both invented fairly recently by essentially one person and both cover a huge stretch of time, but I think one of them is real and the other isn't. And I think the Scientific Revolution is real even though the people living through it didn't think they were in one, while the Renaissance isn't real even though the people did think they were in one. So the obvious tests — "did it get invented later" and "did the participants feel like they were in a new era" — seem to come out the wrong way on both pairs.

The one I keep going back and forth on: my friend has started calling 1945–2015 the "Antibiotic Window," because for about seventy years infections that used to be death sentences were routine, and now resistance is closing it. I can't decide whether that's a real period or just a fact about medicine dressed up as an era.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? What does it say about periods I haven't mentioned — say, the Bronze Age collapse, or the "long nineteenth century"? And which of my verdicts does it make me give up?

Also, tell me what would change your mind: which one of my cases, if I turned out to be wrong about it, would push you toward a different principle, and where do you think your principle is most likely to be wrong?
