For a feature I'm writing on the state of the debate over van Fraassen's Reflection principle, I'd like you to draft the joint statement that a defender and a critic of the principle could both sign after a serious exchange. Take the principle in its diachronic form — the idea that an agent's present credence in a proposition, conditional on the assumption that her later credence in it will take some specific value, ought to equal that value — together with the usual diachronic Dutch-book defence. Open with a numbered section of claims both sides accept: what the principle asserts, and just as important, what it does not (that it constrains credences rather than evidence, that it is silent about how the later credence is formed, that it is not a prediction of one's own future opinion, and so on). Then, in a table, give the residual disagreements, each position phrased so that its own side would put its name to the wording. Identify the cruxes — I suspect one is whether a case in which the agent knows her future self will be epistemically worse off (she will have forgotten relevant evidence, or will have been through a process she now distrusts, or will simply have become less rational) is a genuine counterexample or a case to which the principle never applied, and another is whether the Dutch-book argument establishes a requirement of ideal rationality or something weaker — and for each crux set down the argument or case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If you conclude that part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why.
