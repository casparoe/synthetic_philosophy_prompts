I need a satirical op-ed for a prompt set I'm building, and I want the jokes to land for someone who actually knows the aggregation debate rather than someone who's just heard the phrase "the greater good." Roughly 600-700 words, headline plus a couple of subheads, deadpan all the way through.

The narrator is a policy economist at a made-up think tank, and his proposal is that we should stop punishing people with anything diffuse -- fines, community service, a year of everyone in the country being mildly annoyed -- and start torturing them instead, briefly and directly. His reasoning is the standard setup: one person tortured for fifty years, or an astronomical number of people (call it 3^^^3) each getting a single dust speck in the eye. He's done the arithmetic, the specks win by an enormous margin, and so he concludes that the humane, total-minimizing option is always to concentrate the suffering. He thinks of himself as the compassionate one. He is not stupid and he is not a cartoon villain; he's just refusing to blink, and that's the whole joke.

No real philosophers' names, no citations, no winking, and don't let him start hedging into "well of course I don't actually endorse torture" -- he means it, sincerely, and that's what should be funny.

Things I want worked in:

- He concedes that no individual speck is felt by anyone, and then insists the ledger still balances, because harm doesn't need a subject to be real. That's the line a reader should trip over.
- He needs a common unit of suffering to keep the books in, and he mentions offhand that it was calibrated by a survey. He's completely unbothered by this.
- The exchange rates between crimes (parking ticket, assault, murder) in specks, presented as a purely technical matter, when the ratios are obviously pulled out of the air.
- One of his levies comes out to 3.2 people, so he rounds up to four. He puts this in a footnote and moves on.
- A paragraph where he tries the "imagine you didn't know which one you'd turn out to be" move and presents it as a knockout blow. Play it straight and let the reader work out for themselves whether it actually helps him.
- A concession section where he grants that torture is uniquely horrible, and explains in perfectly even tones why that's a sentimental prejudice -- the argument should do the work, not the sneering.
- Let the arithmetic drift into repugnant-conclusion territory at some point without him naming it.
- One sentence where he has to justify punishing anyone at all, waved away with deterrence.
