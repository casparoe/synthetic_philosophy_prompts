So six of us moved into this big old house in the spring and decided to go all in — one pot, one pantry, one car, nobody pays rent, nobody splits reciepts. We all have normal jobs and just dump our pay into the house account. Each week one person is "quartermaster": they figure out what the house needs and post the plan on the fridge.

Four months in and it's... off. The plan is never right — we're out of dish soap, we've got eleven jars of capers. And there's this whole shadow thing going on under the surface: people trading their laundry slot for car time, one guy slipping another cash to cover his kitchen shift, someone stashing the decent coffee in their room. When I brought it up, one friend said we just haven't got the app right yet — get the data good enough and the algorithm good enough and you can plan the whole house optimally, no money, no little deals. Another friend says no, those little deals are the only thing keeping us fed, and that the stuff you'd need in order to plan is scattered around in exactly those trades and can't be scooped up into a sheet no matter how good the sheet is.

But here's what I can't get past. There are six of us. I know J is out of toothpaste before she does. My parents never counted anything, ever — no spreadsheet, no trades, no quartermaster — and their house ran fine for thirty years. So is our mess just incompetence, bad data, lazy quartermasters, or is there something that definately breaks when you try to run a bunch of adults this way? And if it's the second thing, why did it work for my parents and not for us? What's the actual difference between our house and theirs? And what should we do, short of everyone just going back to buying their own stuff.
