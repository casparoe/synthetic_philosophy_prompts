I keep encountering a way of thinking about hard questions that I can't find a name for, and I want to know whether it already has one or whether it needs to be coined.

The picture is this. Faced with a question I cannot answer directly, I break it into a handful of smaller questions. I do not answer those by becoming smarter; I answer each by, as it were, consulting a version of myself who knows a little less and has a little less time, and who is in turn permitted to consult a still more limited version of herself, and so on downward. The process terminates not at any privileged base of experts but simply at questions that have become easy enough to answer outright. Nothing in the setup fixes how many levels there are or where the bottom lies; the only constraints are that the recursion is self-similar — the same kind of reasoner appears at every level, differing only in how much capacity it has, with no oracle or outside expert entering anywhere — and that it is offered as an idealization of what deliberation could in principle achieve rather than as a procedure anyone actually runs.

Is there an established name for this structure? If the nearest existing ideas are things like recursive decomposition or bounded rationality, I want to know exactly where they come apart from it — in particular whether any of them share the feature that the reasoner at each node is stipulated to be strictly weaker than the one above, and whether the idealization stays coherent when the sub-questions are not independent, so that the same fact has to be relied on in several places at once. A bare label will not help me; what I am after is the carving — what this thing is, what it is not, and where its edges are.
