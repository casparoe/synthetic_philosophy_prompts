I am a novelist, three years into a book about a marriage in which both partners work full time, and to keep my characters' arguments honest I have been reading my way through the economics of the family. One comparison keeps surfacing there, so often and with so little defence that I have begun to suspect it is carrying an argument that was never made in its own right.

The comparison is between a household and a firm. On this picture the family is a small enterprise with a production function: its members allocate hours between market work and home production, accumulate skills, and divide labour as the returns to specialisation dictate. Like any firm, it faces a make-or-buy decision — care can be produced at home or bought in the market — and, like any firm, it is best left to make that decision itself. The comparison is not decorative. It is regularly offered as the reason why the division of labour inside a household should remain a matter of private arrangement rather than public provision, and hence why paid parental leave and publicly funded childcare are at best transfers a well-run household would have made for itself and at worst distortions of a bargain the parties are better placed to strike than any legislator. My brother-in-law, an economist, puts it more briskly: a household that cannot decide for itself who stays at home is not being helped, it is being nationalised.

Please audit the comparison rather than summarise it. Begin with a correspondence table, with these columns: element of the firm; its alleged counterpart in the household; whether the mapping holds fully, partly or not at all; and what turns on it for the inference. Then, in prose, take me through where the mapping breaks; which of those breaks bear on the policy conclusion and which are merely picturesque; and which features of the firm the comparison quietly imports into the household that the household does not possess. On that last point I am especially interested in three things. A firm owns assets and can write enforceable contracts with the people who work in it; a household can do neither. A firm exists in part to protect investments that are worth much less outside the enterprise than inside it, and ownership and long-term contracts are how that protection is secured; I want you to say what, if anything, plays that role in a household, and what follows if nothing does. And the output of a household, unlike that of a firm, is a person, who cannot be owned by the enterprise that produced her. Then tell me whether the conclusion about leave and childcare survives once the disanalogies are on the table — and if only a weakened version survives, what exactly the weakened version claims.

Keep the prose to about nine hundred words. Afterwards, give me a brief structured summary as a single JSON object with these fields exactly: analogy; conclusion_at_issue; holds (a list); breaks (a list); breaks_that_matter (the subset that bear on the inference, each with a one-line reason); imported_assumptions (a list); conclusion_verdict (one of survives, survives in weakened form, does not survive); weakened_form (a sentence, or null); better_analogy (a sentence, or null). No reading lists or citations, please: I want the reasoning, not the bibliography. Write in British English throughout.
