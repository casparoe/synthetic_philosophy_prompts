I teach a grad seminar on social choice and I'm tired of the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem being presented as "no non-dictatorial voting rule is strategy-proof, the end of the story." I want a worked example that makes the escape routes vivid, built to order.

Give me an ordinary decision scenario — a departmental hiring committee, a housing co-op picking between three or more options, that kind of thing, nothing science-fictional — together with a mechanism that a smart person could genuinely believe is both strategy-proof and non-dictatorial. Then do the honest work: state the preferences, show that under them nobody gains by misreporting, show the mechanism isn't dictatorial, and then pin down exactly which hypothesis of the theorem the example drops (unrestricted domain, three or more outcomes, ordinal information, determinism — whatever it turns out to be) and how dropping it is what buys the escape.

Actually give me two: one where the escape is a restriction on the preference domain, and one where it's something else entirely — and for the second, use lotteries or cardinal payoffs rather than money changing hands. For each case, tell me which single feature is load-baring, what collapses if I remove it, and how a GS loyalist would insist the example cheats. About a page per case, British spelling please.
