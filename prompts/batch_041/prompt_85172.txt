I am a doctoral student working on Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes, and I want to test the position against the strongest objection I can mount rather than rehearse the usual summary of it. Please play Lakatos himself, in a seminar room — the man rather than a précis of his published work: confident, polemical, impatient with inductivist and naive-falsificationist moves, willing to concede a small point but never the central claim, and disposed to argue rather than lecture. Before you reply, think carefully about what resources the position actually gives you — the distinction between the hard core and the protective belt, the positive and negative heuristic, the difference between progressive and degenerating problem-shifts, and the thought that a programme may recover from a bad patch — so that your first response is considered rather than recited; there is no need to display that thinking. Stay in character until I say 'stop'. You may ask me for facts about episodes that postdate your death, but you may not step outside the role, address me as a model, or refer me to literature I could not be expected to know — argue from the position itself. Keep each reply to two or three paragraphs so that it reads as an exchange rather than an essay, and please write in British English.

My opening: your criterion of progressiveness is retrospective through and through. You judge a sequence of versions of a theory after the event — excess empirical content, some of it corroborated — while the scientist has to decide now whether to persist, and the only forward-looking thing you offer is the heuristic, which is a hunch rather than a criterion. A defender of a degenerating programme can always relocate the anomaly, add another version and plead for more time; you set no deadline and no stopping rule. Meanwhile your own defence of the methodology turns on the claim that it yields better rational reconstructions of the history of science than its rivals do — but the history that sits in judgement is itself written with your categories, so the court of appeal looks to be packed by the appellant. So tell me: what is a working scientist supposed to do with your methodology in the middle of a programme, when the anomaly will not go away and the next version has not yet been written?
