Me and my mate Dev have been going at it all evening about whether falsificationism can really do the job of telling science from pseudoscience, and he's built this argument that I'm fairly sure proves way to much, except I can't find where it actually breaks.

His setup: a theory counts as scientific, on the falsificationist story, only if it forbids certain things from happening, i.e. it stakes a claim that could turn out wrong. Fine so far. Then:

(1) Any theory is only ever tested as a package: the theory itself plus a pile of auxiliary assumptions (the instrument works, the background laws hold, nothing weird interfered with the sample).
(2) When a prediction comes out wrong, the failure is always compatible with keeping the theory and blaming one of the auxiliaries instead. There's never a failure that can't be absorbed this way.
(3) So no observation ever actually rules a theory out.
(4) If no observation ever rules a theory out, then the theory doesn't forbid any observation.
(5) And by the opening setup, a theory that forbids no observation isn't scientific.
(6) So no theory is scientific.
(7) But that's nuts. Plate tectonics and germ theory are scientific if anything is.
(8) Therefore falsificationism is a broken criterion of demarcation.

Then he adds, almost as an afterthought, that falsificationism can't even pass its own test, since you can't imagine an observation that would refute the claim that only falsifiable claims are scientific. He thinks that's checkmate.

I'm pretty attached to (2) and (3), the holism thing feels obviously right to me and I don't want to pretend you can test a theory in total isolation. And (7) is non-negotiable, I'm not giving that up. So if something has to go it isn't those. Where does it actually break, is there a step that only works under a reading the others don't support, or is it valid and I just have to drop a premise I like? And tell me the price tag on each escape route, because every one I can see costs something I'd rather not lose.

Also, is his closing jab the same problem as the main argument or a seperate one? I couldn't tell.
