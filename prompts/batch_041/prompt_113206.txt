Two colleagues of mine — one a cognitive scientist, the other a philosopher — got into an argument at dinner that neither could settle, and I'd like you to referee it. The scientist argued that conscious experience is almost certainly a byproduct of brain activity: the neurons do all the causal work, and the felt quality is like the whistle on a locomotive — a real effect, but not one that pulls the train. Nothing we do is caused by how experience feels, so treating our inner lives as explanations of our behavior is a mistake. The philosopher replied that this position saws off the branch it sits on. If experience never causes anything, then her friend's conviction that he is conscious — and his conviction that experience is a byproduct — were produced entirely by physical processes that would have produced the same convictions even if there were no experience at all. So his inner certainty can't be evidence for anything, including his own theory. The scientist answered that he rests nothing on introspection: he goes by the third-person data, namely that people report experiences, and those reports correlate with brain states. The philosopher said that only shows that people emit certain reports; it doesn't show the reports are about anything, and if experience is inert, natural selection could have tuned those reports only to the physical facts, never to a felt quality, so they remain no evidence that any felt quality is present. The scientist came back that this just shows the word "experience" never named a further fact; it is a label we attach to certain processes, and the philosopher is treating a manner of speaking as an extra ingredient in the world. The philosopher said that is a bait and switch: however much else is uncertain, that there is something it is like to be her right now is the one thing she cannot doubt, and calling it a manner of speaking denies the datum instead of explaining it. Please adjudicate move by move — say which points are strong, which are weak or confused, and who, if anyone, has the better of the exchange overall, and what both parties are missing.
