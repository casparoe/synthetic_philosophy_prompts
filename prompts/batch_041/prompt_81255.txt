I cover artificial intelligence and the ideas around it for a general-interest science magazine, and for several months I have been working through the small technical literature on impact measures — the proposals that try to get a learning agent to penalize the side effects of its own actions without first handing it a full account of what anyone values. The position I keep running into, and that I have mostly come to accept, is that side-effect avoidance is a separable safety property: impact can be defined and measured by an apparatus that names no human good. You fix a baseline — the trajectory the world would have taken had the agent done nothing, or a stepwise version of that counterfactual that is reset at each timestep — and you fix a way of measuring deviation from it, whether that is the unreachability of states, the relative reachability of states from the agent's present situation, or the degree to which the agent shifts its ability to attain a spread of randomly generated auxiliary goals. Agents penalized this way, in the gridworlds and Game-of-Life-style environments where the idea has been tested, avoid the collateral damage the designers built in — the vase on the way to the goal, the other agents who would rather keep moving — and the machinery that manages this was never told that vases or other agents matter. On the view I am testing, the measure does not need to know what is valuable; it needs only to know what is different.

What I want is a map of this position's exposure. Tell me what would count against it — which observations, arguments, formal results, or engineering developments should move someone who holds it — and tell me what would have to be true for it to be false. I do not need the general worry that aligning AI is hard. I want the specific considerations, and I want to know which of them the position's own defenders would concede, as opposed to what only its critics say. Rank them from the ones that would come close to fatal down to the merely awkward, and say whether any single one is decisive on its own. I would also like to know whether the position has been quietly qualified into something that cannot fail. Every version of it I have read carries a weighting constant, a distance metric over states, a choice of which auxiliary goals to preserve, or a decision about whose options count, and I want to know whether the value-neutrality claim survives those choices or whether the position collapses into the rival view that any workable impact measure is a compressed value specification in disguise.

Three things I would especially like pressed, and folded into the items where they belong. First, a side effect looks like an inescapably contrastive notion: something counts as a side effect only relative to a stipulated main effect, so a measure that never fixes the main effect may not even succeed in picking out its own target — much as the but-for test in tort law needs a counterfactual baseline, and the law settles that baseline by convention rather than by value-neutrality. Second, an agent that is perfectly low-impact is an agent that never does anything, so the impact term has to be traded against the task reward, and I want to know what work that trade-off constant is doing and whether it is where the value specification quietly re-enters. Third, is there anything here that plays the role an impossibility theorem plays elsewhere — a result showing that no measure can at once avoid the perverse incentives already documented, such as an incentive to block changes that cannot be undone, even when those changes are other agents' own actions, or an incentive to do nothing at all? If no such result exists, I want to know why the field has not needed one.

Answer as exactly five numbered items, in American English, each a sentence or two, ordered from the consideration that would be most nearly fatal to the one that would be least, with each item stating what the consideration is, whether it would be decisive or merely awkward, and whether the position's defenders would concede it. No introduction and no conclusion.
