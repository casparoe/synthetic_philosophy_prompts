I'm writing a short paper on Frankfurt cases and I've got a section I think is either my best idea or total nonsense. Can you read it and tell me which? No need to be nice about it.

Setup, quickly, since I know you know it: Black will step in and make Jones do the thing if Jones shows any sign of not doing it on his own. Jones does it on his own. Black never moves. Everyone says Jones is responsible, and the usual moral is that he's responsible even though he couldnt have done otherwise, so the principle of alternative possibilities is dead.

I want to argue the case does less work than advertised, and I've got a case I've been kicking around to try to show it.

Version one. Jones is in a resistance cell and knows she might flinch. Before the operation she has a comrade wire her up to a device that will, if she wavers, flood her with a drug that makes her go through with it anyway. On the night she doesn't waver, the device never fires, she acts out of her own convictions. Responsible? Obviously yes.

But look at what just happened. She had no alternative at the moment of action either. If she'd wavered, the device would have made her act anyway. So this is a Frankfurt case with no intervener in it besides Jones herself. And the absence of alternatives is doing no intuitive work at all here; what's doing the work is that the whole arrangement, device included, is hers.

Version two. Identical structure, identical absence of alternatives at the moment of action. But the device was installed by the comrade without Jones knowing, and the same comrade spent alot of years shaping the convictions that carried her through the night. My verdict flips. She's a puppet.

So: between the two versions nothing changes about alternative possibilities at the time of action. What changes is where the counterfactual intervener came from. If that's right, then the provenance of the intervener, not the absence of alternatives, is what was generating the intuition in the original case, and Frankfurt cases end up telling us that sourcehood matters — which is what the other side said all along.

I want to preempt the obvious reply, that in version one there's still a flicker of freedom, she could have begun to waver, and that flicker is what grounds her responsibility. Answer: the flicker is there in version two as well, and we still withhold, so it can't be doing the grounding.

What I want from you: (1) is the argument valid, or does one of the two replies I'm bracing for actually land — either that version two is just a manipulation case so it isn't a Frankfurt case at all, or that version one smuggles in agency at the setup so it isn't a no-alternatives case at all? (2) Is version one really a Frankfurt case, or have I just described an agent who had alternatives earlier, which everyone already grants and nobody disputes? (3) Is the conclusion too weak? I claim the cases are silent between the two sides, but does silent just mean I've rediscovered the oldest complaint about these cases and dressed it up? And tell me whether the drug device makes version two too easy to dismiss as manipulation — should I strip it out and leave only the provenance of the intervener?
