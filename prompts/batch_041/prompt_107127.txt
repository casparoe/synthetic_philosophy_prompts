I'm in a reading group that just spent a month on the epistemology of testimony, and we're doing a final session where instead of a summary I want to open with a poem. The split we kept circling is the one where one side says a report only gives me a reason to believe if I can trace the warrant back through my own memory and perception to something I verified myself — so my trust in any witness has to be earned, case by case — and the other side says that's asking for something nobody ever had, that testimony is a basic source of justification the way perception is, not a debt I have to pay off first.

What I want is a poem built on a refrain that loses a word every time it comes back. Thats the whole conceit: the justification thinning out as you go back down the chain, each return of the line shorn of one more word, until there's almost nothing left standing — and I want the reader to feel both that this is the reductionist's regress eating itself and that the little scrap left at the end is exactly the thing the other side says was never unearned in the first place. Dont resolve it. I want the last stanza to make the room unsure which reading they just heard.

Twenty-five to thirty lines, no fixed form otherwise, but the shrinking has to be audible — people should hear it happening even if they cant say why. If you can work in the child learning what a word means, please do, because that's the example that always quiets us: you cant check what "rabbit" picks out without already taking someone's word for it, so the reductionist ends up demanding a verification no speaker has ever performed. No names, no quotes, no footnotes — just the poem, read-aloud length.
