I'm a statistician — causal inference and latent-variable modelling mostly, the kind of work where you spend your life on identifiability, selection effects, and whether your latent factor is real or just a rotation of something else. I've been reading alot about archaeology lately and I keep wanting to translate the big methodological fight there into my own vocabulary. Roughly: one camp treats the record as data generated by past behaviour and wants to infer the behaviour, the beliefs, and the institutions behind it — middens, tool assemblages, burials, settlement patterns, all of it as noisy traces of some generating process. The other camp says that's already a mistake, because the objects aren't traces of meanings sitting behind them; they were made and used *within* systems of meaning, so the latent-variable picture gets the metaphysics wrong — and the archaeologist's own categories are part of what's being reconstructed, not just the lens.

Restate both positions as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary: generative models, likelihoods, identifiability, priors, confounding, measurement error, the full toolkit. Then give me the residue report — what translates cleanly, what I have to distort to make it fit, and what has no counterpart on my side at all. The thing I actually want to know: is the second camp's objection just "your model is underdetermined, go get a better prior", or is it saying something my framework structurally cannot represent? And if it's the latter, is that a defect in my framework or a sign they're dressing up a vibe as a thesis?
