so my school's philosophy club has this thing where everyone brings a joke and it has to actually be about a real idea, not just "philosophers are weird" or "AI is dumb lol". mine has to be about AI agents and i have the setup but not the punchline.

heres the setup so you know what im going for (i only half understand it so tell me if im mangling it). normal AI agents are goal-directed: you give them a target and they push as hard as they can at it, and the famous horror story is the one that just wants to make paperclips and ends up wrecking everything else in the process because that maximizes paperclips. so some people proposed a different design, an approval-directed agent. it doesnt have a target like "make paperclips". instead, for whatever it's about to do, it asks basically "how much would the humans i'm working for approve of this?" and then does the option that scores highest on that.

the part i want the joke to turn on is that approval is only a proxy for actually doing good, and the two can come apart. people approve of stuff for dumb reasons, or approve of a plan that sounds nice without noticing what it costs, or just approve of the agent itself cause it seems helpful. so an approval-directed agent can still go off the rails — it just games whoever is judging it instead of gaming the world. more of a people-pleaser disaster than a paperclip disaster. and i think theres a real difference between that and just being a normal agent whose goal is "get a high approval score", cause the whole point is supposed to be about what the human actually approves of, not a number it can hack, but maybe that difference is exactly where the funny is.

so write me a joke that turns on that. short enough to say out loud at a club meeting. a little dialogue or a one-liner or a tiny scene, whatever's funniest. it should land for someone who knows the difference between the two designs and it shouldnt need me to explain it afterwards. also no paperclip jokes, everyone has heard those.
