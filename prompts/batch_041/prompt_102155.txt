I argue about tax policy with friends a lot, and I keep noticing a move I don't have a word for. Someone argues for an arrangement like this: picture yourself not knowing which person in the society you'll turn out to be, then pick whatever you'd want from there. It's presented as a plain fairness test, neutral between people. But the answer you get out the other end depends completely on what odds you're told to assume for being each person, and those odds are never argued for -- they come with the setup and get treated as if fairness itself handed them to you. Assume equal odds and you land on maximizing the total; assume you're probably an ordinary person and not the one at the very top and you land on helping the worst off; same people, same outcomes, opposite advice, and nothing in the fairness talk changed. Is there a name for this -- the way a particular way of weighing people gets smuggled in through the probabilities and then never defended? I know about the veil-of-ignorance idea and I know somone has attacked it, but I'm after the specific thing: the unargued odds doing all the moral work. If it has no name, can you give me one and pin down what it is and isn't -- how it differs from just saying the veil is a heuristic, and where the line falls between the odds being a harmless device and the odds being the whole arguemnt?
