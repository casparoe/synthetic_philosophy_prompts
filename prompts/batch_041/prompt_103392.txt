Me and a friend have been arguing about this for weeks and I cant let it go. He says the argument is airtight and I'm just squeamish about where it lands. I think something in it slips but I cant say where.

(1) I exist. I know that before I know anything else about the world.
(2) When two hypotheses about the world differ, I should favor the one that made it more likely that someone like me would be around to notice anything at all.
(3) A world with more minds has more "slots" I could have occupied, so my being here is more likely in a world with more people. Double the people, roughly double the chance I'm one of them.
(4) So my existence by itself is evidence for a bigger world over a smaller one.
(5) Nothing caps this. For any population size, a hypothesis with more people fits my existence even better.
(6) So whatever else I learn, I can always imagine a world with enough extra people that it beats the boring evidence-based hypothesis.
(7) Taken seriously, that says I should believe there are as many people as I can imagine — infinitely many.
(8) But if there are infinitely many, then "how likely was I to exist" comes out infinite under every one of those hypotheses, so the very reasoning that got me to (7) can no longer tell them apart.

I really want to keep (2). If my own existence cant count as evidence for anything, a whole lot of ordinary reasoning goes out with it. And (3) feels like simple counting. But (7) is obviously insane. So which step do I drop, and what am I paying for dropping it? Under 350 words.
