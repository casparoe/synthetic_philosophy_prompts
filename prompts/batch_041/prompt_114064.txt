I've been building backends for games and VR stuff for about eight years, and I've noticed I have strong gut reactions about when virtual things count as "real" and when they're just pixels, and my reactions definately don't fit together. I didn't do philosophy past A-level so I don't have the vocabulary for this. Was hoping you could find the rule. Here's everything I think, going on gut:

1. A mate spent 300 hours on a base in a survival game and some kid blew it up overnight while he was asleep. I think that's a genuine harm, not just "it's only a game". If the kid got permabanned I'd think that was fair.
2. I've spent whole evenings in a single-player game wiping out villages of NPCs. That's nothing. Zero. There's nobody home.
3. Same thing but in a VR game where the NPC is running a language model, it begs me to stop and says it's frightened. Still nothing, I'd say, even though it puts a weird feeling in my chest and I'd rather not watch it.
4. A player cons another player out of a rare sword that cost about £150 of real money, using a trade bug. That's theft. Basically as bad as taking the cash off them.
5. Same con, but what he takes is a free hat the victim didn't even like. Barely registers.
6. A studio shuts down a twelve-year-old online world, a few hundred people still play it, and they delete the servers so nobody can ever go back. I think that's a real loss. And I think it's a real loss even if the last player had already quit and the world was just sitting there unvisited. It feels more like knocking down a listed building than deleting a file.
7. My own save file, my own console, I delete it myself. Nothing wrong at all, even though it's the same kind of thing as case 6, just smaller and mine.

The way I'd half-put it is that something virtual is real when it's hooked into something real, a real person's feelings, real money, real relationships. Call it the hook. But case 6 has nothing on the other end of the hook if everyone's already gone, and I still think it counts. And case 3 has a real person's feelings in it, mine, I feel bad watching it, and I still say it's nothing. So the hook doesn't survive on it's own.

What I want: the principle, or the two or three competing ones, that gets as many of my verdicts as possible, stated plainly enough that I could code it up, and for each bit of it, which of my cases is doing the work. Then tell me what it says about a case I haven't listed: someone builds a huge statue of my actual face in a public hub in a game I don't even play, with rude stuff carved on it, and my mates just send me screenshots. And be straight with me about which of my seven verdicts the principle can't keep, and why I should give that one up. British English please, I'm in Manchester.
