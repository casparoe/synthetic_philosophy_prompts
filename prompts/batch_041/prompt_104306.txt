Write a joke that only works for someone who knows Integrated Information Theory (IIT) well enough to track its postulates and their less intuitive consequences. To give the joke a setting that the theory's own literature does not supply, imagine a small standards body — the Bureau of Intrinsic Existence — that issues certificates of consciousness to candidate systems and must process a queue of applicants: a photodiode, a cerebellum, a purely feedforward convolutional network, and a three-person committee in a meeting. Write the joke as a short scene from the Bureau's working day, in roughly 200–300 words, and let the humor arise from the theory's own commitments rather than from the absurdity of bureaucracy.
