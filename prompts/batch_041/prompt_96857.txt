1. The identity of indiscernibles is a necessary truth, not a rule of thumb and not an idealization we could drop.
2. Bundle theory: a particular is nothing over and above its properties. No bare substratum, no primitive thisness.
3. Combinatorialism about modality: the possible is just recombination of actual qualitative patterns, and there are no non-qualitative differences between worlds.
4. Relationalism about space: where a thing is is exhausted by its relations to other things.
5. Conceivability is cheap. I don't think "I can picture it" gets you to "it's possible", and I'm suspicious of any argument that leans on that move.
6. The indiscernibility of identicals is trivially true and does no work. The identity of indiscernibles is the one carrying content.
