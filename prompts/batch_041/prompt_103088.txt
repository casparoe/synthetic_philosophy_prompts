I'm a third-year philosophy student currently taking a course on modal metaphysics, and I've been asked to design a diagnostic instrument for a seminar unit on conceivability as a guide to possibility. The seminar keeps producing the frustrating situation in which everyone agrees that conceivability gives us some access to possibility, yet they disagree about every case; I want a ten-question sorting quiz that classifies respondents by their verdicts on concrete cases rather than by the labels they would choose for themselves. Each question should present a short case and two or three answer options, phrased so that a respondent can answer it without already knowing which theoretical position it leads to. The cases should probe the distinctions that matter for this debate: whether the relevant conceivability must be ideal or whether ordinary prima facie conceivability suffices; whether conceivability is defeated by a posteriori necessities or by semantic incompetence; whether it is evidence for metaphysical possibility or only for epistemic possibility; whether inconceivability is itself a guide to impossibility; and whether the whole conceivability-possibility link is a priori or empirically answerable. Please include cases such as conceiving of a zombie duplicate of oneself, conceiving of water that is not H2O, conceiving of a counterexample to an open mathematical conjecture, conceiving of a world whose logic differs from ours, conceiving of a colour one has never seen, and conceiving of a disembodied mind that is nevertheless spatially located. Avoid questions that merely ask whether conceivability entails possibility, and avoid leaning on any particular author's terminology; introduce any technical term inside the question itself.

Then write the scoring key. It should map answer patterns onto at least five positions: a modal rationalism on which ideal conceivability entails metaphysical possibility; a defeasible or empirical linkage view on which conceivability is evidence but can be overridden; an epistemic-possibility-first or two-dimensionalist view on which conceivability tracks what is epistemically possible while metaphysical necessity can outrun it; a modal scepticism that denies conceivability is any guide at all; and a quietist or deflationary option that denies the debate has a single determinate subject matter. For each position, give one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Finally, identify which pairs of positions the quiz cannot separate and explain why, and note any answer pattern that corresponds to no position anyone would hold -- if there is one, diagnose whether that combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. The instrument should stand on its own for a seminar handout, so please do not include citations, reading lists, or references to particular publications; I want the classification to be carried entirely by the cases and the scoring key.
