I've been going round in circles on the raven paradox on my own for a couple months and I want to know if the loop is my fault or the problem's. Where I've got to: the rule that a black raven supports the claim that all ravens are black seems obviously right. The move where you flip the claim into everything non-black is a non-raven, and then notice that a white shoe fits that, also seems airtight, since it's the same claim said backwards. But then a white shoe supporting a claim about ravens is nonsense, and not nonsense because the effect is small, nonsense because it's nonsense. So one of the three has to go, and every time I read about it the same three get defended and nothing moves.

The thing that stopped me accepting the answer that the white shoe does confirm, just by an amount too small to care about: my flatmates rule is that everything in our pantry is vegan. Flipped around, that's everything non-vegan is not in the pantry. So a steak someone is eating in a restaurant across town is a non-vegan thing that is not in the pantry, and by the raven logic it supports her pantry rule. That's absurd, and not because there are millions of steaks and only hundreds of pantry things. The absurdity is that I already knew the steak was at the restaurant, so it couldn't tell me anything about the pantry. I can hear the reply coming, that I already knew the shoe wasn't a raven either, but I don't see why that kills my case and not the raven's.

Which makes me think the whole fight is about whether confirming is a two-way thing between a piece of evidence and a claim taken on its own, or a three-way thing where evidence only counts as support relative to which other explanations are still live. If it's three-way, then the raven argument and the white shoe argument were never really contradicting each other, they were answering different questions with the same word.

What I want is a diagnosis of the standoff, not a verdict on who's right, plus an honest answer about whether anything could end it. Tell me what kind of disagreement this is, two sides using the word confirms for two different things, a real fight about a principle, or people defending an intuition that can't be argued for, and tell me whether my two-way versus three-way idea is doing any real work or is just a fancy way of saying what everyone already says. Then what would count as progress: a distinction, an argument that one of the three has to go, or should I just accept that this one doesn't get solved. Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no introduction and no summing up at the end.
