I'm in a moral philosophy reading group and this term we've each got to write up a kind of moral self-portrait — just our actual settled views, warts and all, so the others can try to prise them apart in the seminar. Before I hand mine round I want you to stress-test it. Please take the role of a really careful moral philosopher, the sort who works at the level of the fine print rather than waving vaguely at there being a literature on this. Begin by setting out in your own words what each commitment of mine is actually committing me to, and only then go looking for trouble. Write in British English.

Here's roughly where I've ended up. (1) Consequentialism about rightness is basically correct: an action is right just in case it maximises expected value, assessed impersonally, with everyone's interests counting the same. (2) But Williams's integrity objection has always struck me as a real hit rather than a cheap shot — a morality that can tell me to drop my deepest projects the moment the sums say so doesn't merely ask a lot, it stops treating me as the one who is living my life at all. (3) Same family of worries: in the case where you can save only one of two people in equal danger and one of them is your partner, I think someone who saves their partner and then adds that impartial benevolence would have wanted it anyway has had one thought too many — the reason to save them is simply that they are my partner, and that should be the whole answer. (4) At the same time I really do think impartiality is the fundamental moral standpoint, that my own attachments are morally speaking just one bundle of interests among others, and that I ought to be suspicious of them as bias. (5) I give about a third of my income to effective charities and I think I am required to — I don't think of it as saintly, I think anyone on my income who doesn't is doing something wrong. (6) And I think saving my partner rather than the stranger is not merely allowed but what I ought to do, and I feel no pressure whatsoever to be able to cash that out in impartial terms.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each real clash? I'd like the tensions ranked by severity, and for each genuine one a concrete suggestion of what I'd have to give up or weaken and what that would cost me elsewhere in the set. Don't just tell me everything is in tension — if a pair is fine, say so and say why, and if a clash dissolves once you distinguish two senses of something, spell out the distinction. I've been going back and forth on this alot, so please also tell me which single revision would clean up the most at the least cost, and be upfront about how confident you are in each verdict and flag the one judgement you're least sure about — I'd much rather know where you're guessing than get a confident-sounding list.
