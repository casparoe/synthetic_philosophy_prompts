I retired a few years ago from a career in field ecology — spent most of my working life counting birds and arguing about habitat corridors — and since then I've been trying to teach myself philosophy, mostly by reading in the evenings and pestering a neighbor who did a PhD in it. Anyway, here's a thing thats been bugging me, and I have a feeling it's a philosophy question wearing a disguise.

My wife and I belong to the walking club at our retirement community. There's this unwritten rule that when two people meet on one of the narrow paths, both step to the left to pass. Nobody ever voted on it. From what the older members say, it started years ago because the club's founder, who had a bad right hip, always drifted left, and everyone just copied him, and within a season it was simply what everybody did. Fine. Now here's my problem: a new member joined last month, a perfectly nice guy, and he steps to the right like a normal person, and it's causing real friction — people have started calling him rude, one woman said he "doesn't respect the club," and there's a move to write the left-hand rule into our little constitution. And honestly I feel the irritation too, even though when I say it out loud it sounds insane, because it's an arbitrary thing a dead man's hip decided.

What I can't work out is whether there's anything actually wrong with what he's doing, or whether we're all just confusing "this is what we do" with "this is what one ought to do." And I don't think it's as simple as "it's just a shared habit, so anything goes," because there does seem to be a difference between this and, say, the way we all happen to stand in the same spot at the potluck. Is the difference that this rule is solving a problem — otherwise we'd be forever bumping shoulders — and once everybody knows it and expects it, breaking it is a kind of breach of trust? But then a second thing confuses me: another member walks with a cane and can't easily move left on short notice, and she's started avoiding the paths altogether. If the rule puts a real cost on her, does that change whether the rest of us are entitled to enforce it, or whether it was ever a genuine obligation in the first place? I'd like you to take the situation seriously rather than just tell me to be tolerant, and help me see what's actually going on — what kind of thing this rule is, why it feels binding, whether that feeling is tracking anything real, and what we should actually do about the guy and about the lady with the cane. And if it matters, I'd love to know whether the answer changes if the club relocates to a new trail system where stepping left is definately dangerous and stepping right is obviously safer.
