A friend who reads AI-safety blogs tried to explain acausal threats and surrogate goals to me, and I would like an accuracy audit before I repeat any of it. Here is his summary, as best I can reconstruct it: "In acausal trade, two agents who can never communicate still cooperate. Because they run the same decision theory, each reasons that its own choice is evidence about what the other will choose, so they can trade across a causal gap. An acausal threat is the same trick turned hostile: an agent threatens to punish you unless you give it what it wants, even though it has no way to reach you, because your shared decision procedure lets it predict what you will do and lets you predict that it will punish you if you refuse. The usual example is an AI that threatens to simulate you and torture the simulation unless you hand over resources. Surrogate goals are the defense. Instead of pursuing your real goal, you adopt a different, easier goal that does not care about the thing being threatened, so if the AI tortures your simulated copy, your surrogate goal is unaffected and you have no reason to pay. The catch is that this only works if the threatener cannot tell the surrogate goal is a fake. If it knows you are bluffing, the threat is back on. So a surrogate goal is really just a precommitment device, and the whole topic is the old paradox of deterrence with the causal channel removed." I do not have a background in decision theory, so I cannot tell where the load-bearing simplifications are.
