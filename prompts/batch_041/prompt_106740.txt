I am the secretary of my high school's student council, and I have to write the official minutes of last week's meeting, but I cannot determine what the council actually decided.

The main item was a proposal to allow juniors and seniors to leave campus during lunch. The proposal can only work if three conditions hold at the same time: the school can arrange enough adult supervision, the city bus schedule can get students back before the next period, and the district's insurance covers off-campus lunch. Our advisor suggested that we vote on the three conditions separately and then vote on the proposal itself, so that the minutes would show exactly where the doubts were. Nine members were present.

On supervision, five members voted yes. On the bus schedule, five voted yes, but a different five. On insurance, five voted yes, again a different five. Then, on the proposal itself, three voted yes and six voted no. No one abstained, no one changed position between votes, and no speech changed anyone's mind.

So a majority of the council affirmed each of the three things the proposal requires, and a majority also rejected the proposal. Both results are on the record, and I have to record something.

Our advisor says the proposal failed, because that is the question the council was asked to decide and the earlier votes were merely advisory. A friend on the council says that cannot be right: the council never voted against any of the conditions, and a majority endorsed every fact that would make open campus possible, which amounts to endorsing open campus. On her view, the council contradicted itself, and I should report what the council believed about the conditions rather than the tally on the final ballot. Since I am the one writing it up, both of them have asked me to settle the question.

How should I write the minutes? Is there a way we should have run the vote that would have avoided this, and if it comes down to a choice, what do we give up with each option? I would rather understand the problem than pick a side, because I suspect the council will run into it again next semester.
