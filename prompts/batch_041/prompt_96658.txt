In a seminar on global justice I found myself on the receiving end of an argument I could not dismantle. My interlocutor, defending the thesis that compatriots have claims on one another that strangers do not, offered the following picture: a political community is like an insurance pool. Members pay premiums over their working lives — taxes, civic service, compliance with shared rules — and in return they hold a claim on the pool when they fall ill, lose work, or grow old. Someone who never paid in has no such claim, and admitting them mid-stream is either charity or a renegotiation, not a matter of right. On this picture, partiality toward compatriots is not chauvinism but the plain logic of a scheme that people have funded; and the same picture is then made to license tighter immigration and a capped foreign-aid budget.

What I want is an audit of the analogy, not a verdict on the politics. First, lay out the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of the pool, the premiums, the claims, the actuary, the policy, the payout, and the members — and say which of these have any analogue in an actual state and which have none. Second, enumerate the features of real insurance that the picture quietly imports, and test whether the target possesses them: voluntary entry, actuarial fairness between premium and risk, defined and consensual membership, the availability of exit, solvency constraints, and a contract that either side can renegotiate. Third, sort the resulting disanalogies into those that matter for the specific conclusions being drawn — that compatriots have special claims, and that this licenses restrictive immigration — and those that are merely decorative. Fourth, tell me whether the conclusion survives once the mapping is corrected, or whether the analogy was carrying the argument all along.

Finally, if it fails, separate two things I keep conflating: that this argument for special obligations is bad, and that special obligations are baseless. Is there a better analogy for the nationalist's position, or does every such picture already presuppose the conclusion it is meant to support?
