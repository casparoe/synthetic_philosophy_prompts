An interdisciplinary institute I direct is choosing the theme for its next research program: roughly a dozen fellows, three to five years, with a mandate for conceptual work only — philosophy of biology, philosophy of mind, and the theory of computation, not wet labs or model training. The theme we are considering is competence without comprehension: the two inversions usually associated with Darwin and with Turing, in which purposive-looking competence is produced without any comprehending designer or understander. The board has asked me for a defended agenda rather than a survey, so what I want from you is an ordering of the open questions together with the criteria behind it.

Take the following as candidate organizing questions, illustrative rather than exhaustive. First, does the Darwinian inversion actually license the Turingian one — does the fact that selection can produce design without a designer bear at all on whether computation can produce understanding without an understander, or is the analogy doing considerably less work than its friends suppose? Second, what is comprehension supposed to be, such that we can assert with any precision that a system has competence without it; is there a single notion here or several that pull apart under pressure? Third, what do the inversions imply for explanation — whether teleological, intentional, and as-if descriptions of organisms and artifacts are mere heuristics or track something real, and how that bears on interpreting systems we cannot open up.

Which open questions in this area most deserve sustained work, in what order, and why? For each, argue why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for philosophers and theorists rather than people with labs or compute, and how neglected it currently is. Say what would move a question up or down the ranking over the program's first two years — which argument, formal result, or development elsewhere should change our minds about the allocation. Finally, if the phrase competence without comprehension smuggles in a distinction that will not bear the weight — if the two inversions come apart, or if comprehension turns out to be doing no independent work — say so and tell me what organizing question you would substitute, because I would rather learn that now than in year four.
