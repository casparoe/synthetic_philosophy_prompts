ok so im putting together a handout for a reading group on the Judy Benjamin problem and i want to close with a poem instead of a summary. you know the case: Judy's in a tent, she thinks theres a quarter chance shes in Red territory and three quarters Blue, and if shes in Red shes fifty-fifty between headquarters company and the rifle company, and if shes in Blue shes definitely not in HQ because Blue isnt split into companies. then the radio tells her that if shes in the Red Army shes in headquarters company. i want a pantoum about it, where the form does the work rather than just decorating the story.

Form: twenty lines, five quatrains, standard pantoum recycling — second and fourth lines of each stanza come back as the first and third of the next, and the last stanza folds the opening lines back in. rhyme optional, but the returns have to be real returns.

The thing i want the form to carry: the message only kills one cell of her partition — Red-and-rifle goes to zero — and it says nothing at all about how the rest of the mass gets redistributed. so use the four cells as the four recycled lines, in plain english (red and HQ, red and not-HQ, blue and HQ, blue and not-HQ), and let the poem's recycling of lines stand for the recycling of probability mass. the first four quatrains should each be a different candidate update: one where she renormalises over what survives (i think that leaves Red at about a seventh, check me), one where she holds the chance of Red fixed at a quarter and just moves the mass around inside Red (Adams-ish conditioning, probability kinematics), one where she minimises relative information / KL divergence from her prior, and one more that you think is a genuine temptation but not just an average of the others — tell me in a couple of lines of prose after the poem which stanza was which, so i can check the arithmetic.

Then the fifth quatrain is the impasse: the constraint alone doesnt pick a posterior, so some extra principle has to, and the poem should end there without pretending the extra principle is forced. please dont do the cheap version where it turns out the problem is that the english sentence is ambiguous — keep the content fixed, its the update rule thats underdetermined, thats the bit i care about. if you think one answer is uniquely rational then fine, but make the others feel like real temptations rather than straw men.

no philosopher name-drops, no maths notation in the verse itself (spell fractions out or make the line carry it), no footnotes. british english please (minimise, colour and so on). casual register is fine, it doesnt have to rhyme, but it should read like a poem and not like a worksheet with line breaks.
