Question: Explain the difference between the self-sampling assumption (SSA) and the self-indication assumption (SIA). Apply each to the Sleeping Beauty problem and to the Presumptuous Philosopher scenario. Which credence in heads does each assumption yield in Sleeping Beauty, and which cosmological theory does each favor in the Presumptuous Philosopher? Finally, explain why the Presumptuous Philosopher is often taken to be a reductio of one of these assumptions, and say whether the other assumption avoids it.

Student's answer:

The self-sampling assumption says that, all other things equal, you should reason as if you are randomly selected from the set of all actually existing observers in your reference class. The self-indication assumption says that you should reason as if you are randomly selected from the set of all possible observers, weighted by the probability that they exist. So the difference is that SSA restricts the sample to actual observers, while SIA includes merely possible observers.

In the Sleeping Beauty problem, if the coin lands heads there is one awakening, and if it lands tails there are two. Under SSA, since there are twice as many awakenings in the tails world, you are twice as likely to be one of them, so your credence in heads should be 1/3. Under SIA, by contrast, you already knew before the experiment that you would exist as an observer, so the fact that you are now awake gives you no new information about the coin toss; hence SIA gives 1/2. This is why SSA is the thirder position and SIA is the halfer position.

The Presumptuous Philosopher scenario is a problem for SIA. Suppose two cosmological theories fit all the non-anthropic evidence equally well: T1 says there are a trillion trillion people, and T2 says there are a trillion trillion trillion people. SIA says you should favor the theory on which more observers exist, because your own existence is more likely under that theory. So SIA tells the philosopher to favor T2, even though the empirical evidence is neutral. That seems absurd, since it would let purely a priori reasoning settle cosmology. SSA avoids this because it only samples from actual observers in the world you are in, so the number of observers in other possible worlds does not affect your credence. However, SSA has its own problem: it depends on the choice of reference class, whereas SIA does not. That is why many philosophers prefer SIA despite the Presumptuous Philosopher.
