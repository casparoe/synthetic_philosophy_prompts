I'm a novelist — about 180 pages into a comic novel set in a mid-70s economics department (tweed, tenure fights, a truly terrible coffee machine), and there's a chapter where my protagonist, a first-year assistant professor, reads out a press release from the institute that's just hired her. I want the press release printed verbatim as the chapter, and I'd like you to write it.

The premise, which I think is a good one: the institute has an annual ritual where it "audits" its flagship forecasting model for realism, and this year's audit has certified that the model's core assumptions are false — wildly inaccurate, never described a single real human being, would fail any honest inspection. The press release announces this as a triumph. That's the joke, obviously, and I don't want you to explain the joke; it should read as a completely straight, slightly proud institutional document. The funny part has to come from the methodology being *right* — I've been reading about Friedman's F-twist thing, the claim that a theory's assumptions can be wildly inaccurate as long as its predictions are good, and even that the more significant the theory the more unrealistic the assumptions — so a specialist reader should watch the release slide from the defensible claim (we don't test assumptions, we test predictions, we don't care whether firms literally maximize anything so long as they behave as if they do) into the indefensible ones: treating unrealism as an achievement you can win a prize for, using the same model to hand out policy advice and welfare verdicts, forgetting that the assumptions you waved through are the ones carrying the causal story, forgetting that what predicted well under one set of rules needn't survive a change in them. I don't want a smug "economics is fake" hit piece — I want the economists I know to laugh and nod, and a reader who's never heard of any of this to still follow it.

Structure I'm picturing: headline, dateline, a proud opening, a quote from an invented institute director (invent everyone — I don't want a real person's name in my novel's fake press release, and no real institutions either), a second quote from someone mildly worried who gets steamrolled, a short FAQ or complaints-procedure sidebar, and a seperate "Notes to editors" boilerplate at the end that quietly gives the game away in one line. About 700 words, deadpan throughout — no winking, no asides to the reader. Don't bother with footnotes or references, it's a press release, nobody in it cites anything. It's set at the Barlow Institute (I made that up, feel free to give it a city), and the model they're all so pleased with — eh, you can name it, just make it sound like something a committee named.
