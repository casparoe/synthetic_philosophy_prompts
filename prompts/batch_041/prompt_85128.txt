My friend and I have been going around in circels for two years now on causation by omission — the plants-died-because-I-didn't-water-them thing. I say obviously I caused it, the counterfactual is airtight; he says there's no process, no transfer, nothing there, just an absence we dress up as a cause because we want someone to blame. The annoying part is that we agree on every case — same facts about what would have happened, same gut reactions about who's a jerk — and then we split on the verdict anyway. My hypothesis is that we aren't disagreeing about the world at all: he's answering a question about what happened, I'm answering one about what we should say and who's answerable, and the real fight underneath is normative. Though that might just be a face-saving story I tell myself.

Diagnose it for me. Is it verbal, two questions wearing one word, a hidden question about how people actually use causal talk, or bedrock intuitions neither of us can argue for? And then tell me what would count as progress — a distinction, a case, a formal result — or whether nothing should.

One constraint on the diagnosis: it has to survive a case where the "omission" is a maintained state rather than a gap — a factory whose safety valve is held open by a motor, where the operator shuts the motor off, or just lets the maintenance schedule lapse — and a pair of cases with identical counterfactual profiles that differ only in whether something moved. If your diagnosis can't handle those, say so instead of finessing it.

Give it to me as JSON with these fields: kind_of_disagreement (short label, plus one line on why that label and not the others), stated_vs_actual_commitments (what each side is really committed to beyond what we say out loud), crux (the thing that, if settled, would settle it), my_hypothesis (agree / partly / disagree, with reasons), hard_cases (how the diagnosis handles the ones above), progress (a ranked list, most decisive to least, of things that would move it — or an argument that none would).
