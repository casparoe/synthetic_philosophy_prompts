You are a philosopher of art with a solid grounding in eighteenth-century French thought. Please compare two positions on the theater that are rarely put side by side: Rousseau's case against the stage in his Letter to d'Alembert on the Spectacles (1758), and Diderot's essay on acting, begun around 1769 and reworked into a dialogue in the 1770s, published only after his death. Think the comparison through carefully before you write, then give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion.

Background you may take as given. Rousseau's Letter answers d'Alembert's article on Geneva in the Encyclopédie, which regretted that Geneva had no theater. Rousseau argues that a theater would corrupt morals rather than improve them: comedy turns the honest man into a figure of fun, tragedy puts passion and crime on display, and the actor's trade, being a trade in representation, disposes him to falsehood rather than virtue. His alternative is civic festivals, and he pointedly does not ask that the Paris theaters be closed. Diderot argues the opposite of what one might expect: the great actor is not the one who feels most but the one who stays cold, who constructs an ideal model of his character and copies it, leaving the tears to the spectator. Rousseau had himself written for the stage: the comedy Narcissus, staged in 1752 and published with a preface in which he answers the charge that a man who attacks the arts has no business writing plays, and the opera The Village Soothsayer, a great success of the same years. Neither text is a reply to the other.

Let each item isolate one genuine axis of agreement or disagreement between the two, and say which of them is more defensible on that axis and why. Devote at least one item to whether Diderot's account of the actor helps or hurts Rousseau's moral case, and at least one to what Rousseau's own stage writing shows about his theory. Write in American English.
