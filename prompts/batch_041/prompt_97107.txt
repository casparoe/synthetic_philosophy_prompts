I'm taking a seminar on free will and the cognitive sciences, and for my final project I have to produce the document that an adversarial collaboration over Libet-style experiments would actually generate — not a dramatized dialogue, not a verdict on who is right, but a text that both parties could sign.

The two parties are these. Call the first the deflationary camp. On their view, the pre-movement potential recorded in these tasks is largely an artifact of how the data are constructed and read: it emerges from averaging many trials aligned on movement onset, it plausibly reflects spontaneous background fluctuations that accumulate and cross a threshold rather than any specific decision, and the reported time of the conscious urge is a retrospective judgment of contested accuracy. On this view the results constrain nothing about free will, because they do not measure a decision in the first place. Call the second the constraint camp. On their view something real is being measured — a pre-conscious commitment to act that precedes the agent's awareness of intending to act — and even granting every methodological caveat, the ordering (neural commitment, then awareness, then movement, with lateralized information decodable up to roughly ten seconds before the reported choice in the later studies) places a genuine constraint on any picture in which a conscious intention is the originating cause of an action.

Write their joint statement. I want the following, in this order.

1. The claims both sides can sign. Include claims about what the experiments operationally measure; about the timing relations (a pre-movement potential beginning on the order of several hundred milliseconds before the movement, the reported urge on the order of a couple of hundred milliseconds before it); about what the averaging, baseline-correction, and alignment procedures do and do not license; and about what neither side is thereby committed to — for instance, that these results do or do not settle the compatibilism question. Keep these non-vacuous: if a claim would be signed by anyone with any view, cut it.

2. The residual disagreements, each written in the strongest formulation its own side would accept rather than a caricature. I expect at least these: whether the pre-movement potential is a decision-related signal or a threshold crossing of noise; whether the reported urge is a cause, a readout, or a confabulated timestamp; and whether the paradigm's peculiar structure — a movement with no reason to occur at one moment rather than another — makes the results irrelevant to reason-responsive choice or makes them a fair model of spontaneous action.

3. The cruxes, each marked as empirical, conceptual, or verbal, with a note on whether settling it would move at least one side.

4. For each empirical crux, a discriminating case or experiment both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I want at least three and I want them concrete. Consider these candidates, and engage with them even if you propose better ones: (a) a variant in which the participant is instructed to deliberate before moving rather than to wait for an urge — is the pre-movement potential attenuated, unchanged, or merely shifted, and does either outcome favor a side? (b) the cortical stimulation results in which a reported urge to move can be elicited with no movement occurring, or a movement elicited that the patient attributes to his own will — check my characterization of those results and correct it if I have it wrong, then say what they do to the claim that the urge is causally upstream; (c) a variant in which real stakes attach to which of two actions is chosen, so that the choice has a reason, and the question whether lateralized decoding still precedes awareness by seconds or collapses toward the Libet-scale gap; (d) the veto and point-of-no-return results — if a prepared movement becomes uncancellable a couple of hundred milliseconds out, does that help the constraint camp or the deflationary camp, and does the veto threaten a regress if it is itself initiated pre-consciously?

5. A short section on which parts of the dispute are merely verbal, with a proposed stipulation that would dissolve them — especially for the words 'decision' and 'cause'.

6. An honest closing assessment: what, if anything, both sides agree would actually move the debate, and what would not.

Do not cite or list literature. I do not want a bibliography, and I do not want you guessing at titles, dates, or attributions. Argue from the substance. Do not put anything in quotation marks unless you are certain it is not quoted from any text.

After the prose document, give me a structured summary as a single JSON object with these fields: agreed_claims (array of objects with keys id, claim, why_both_accept, what_would_count_against); disagreements (array of objects with keys id, question, deflationary_formulation, constraint_formulation, crux_type, discriminating_case, deflationary_prediction, constraint_prediction); verbal_disputes (array of objects with keys term, reading_deflationary, reading_constraint, proposed_stipulation); progress_assessment (object with keys would_move, would_not_move). Use plain strings for all values.
