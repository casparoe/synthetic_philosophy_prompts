I have no academic background, but for several years I have been working on one idea by myself, and what I would like from you is a joke about it.

The idea is this. Words such as same, next, closer, and like do not each mean one thing; each covers a small neighbourhood of readings. One person uses the word, means a reading that is perfectly defensible, and passes the word on. The next person uses a slightly different reading, equally defensible. No step is a mistake and the word never changes, but the concept doing the work drifts, and after enough steps the thing being described has ended up somewhere nobody would have agreed to go, while everyone can still honestly say that nothing was changed. I take this to be different from vagueness, and different from the puzzle of where to draw a line: there, the question is which reading is right; here, the reading silently changes hands while the words stay put.

To think it through I invented a small world, and I would like the joke to be set in it. The world is a bus route. It has a number and a name. The only permitted change is one stop at a time: a stop is shifted a hundred yards down the road, or the route gains a single stop at one end, or two adjacent stops are merged into one. That is all that ever happens, once a year. The number and the name stay as they were, and the drivers and the regular passengers insist, sincerely and without any sense of telling a lie, that it is the same route.

Please write the joke there. What I am asking for:

- A setup and a punchline, short enough to tell aloud in half a minute.
- The humour must come from the drift. Someone who has thought about this kind of thing should laugh; someone who has not should find it strange rather than baffling, and should never come away thinking that I do not understand my own idea.
- No puns, no play on the word route, and nothing that would work equally well if the concept had never moved. A joke about buses running late is not what I am after, and neither is a joke about where to draw the line. Nor do I want the old puzzle about replacing the planks of a boat one at a time; that is about identity, and I already know it.
- The punchline should be a line spoken by someone who has followed the rule correctly from the beginning.
- Invent a driver or a passenger or a stop if it helps, but keep the place fictional, so that nothing depends on facts about a real town or a real company.
- Plain language, no jargon; a listener should not need to know any theory to follow it.

Then add two or three sentences I could use as a caption: name the word whose reading slid, say what it slid from and what it slid to, and point to the line of the joke where the slide becomes visible.

If it genuinely cannot be made funny, do not force it; say plainly why this world resists a joke and what a world that would take one has to have.
