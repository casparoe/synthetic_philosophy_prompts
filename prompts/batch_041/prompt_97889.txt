You are a philosopher of language who works on expressives, evaluative vocabulary, and the semantics/pragmatics boundary. I want you to stress-test a position I hold; think it through from first principles rather than reciting the literature, and start by brainstorming what a view of this shape would commit me to before you evaluate it.

The position: slurs are semantically innocent. The descriptive content of a slur is exactly that of its neutral counterpart, and the derogation is entirely pragmatic — an implicature, a presupposition, or an expressive overlay that attaches to the act of using the word rather than to its truth-conditional content. Call this the pragmatic view.

I want a map of its exposure. First tell me what the pragmatic view predicts that its semantic rivals don't, and which of those predictions are even testable in principle. Then give me the defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and say which a defender would concede as counting against them versus dismiss as question-begging. I'm especially interested in the following probes, and I'd like your reasoning on each:

- Derogation that appears to survive in environments where implicatures are standardly cancelled and presuppositions filtered — under negation, in the antecedent of a conditional, in questions, in reported speech, and in fiction. Does it really survive, or am I reading it in?
- A constructed case: a community coins a term for a group it takes to be fictional, used with the full affect of a slur but with no actual referent and no social power behind it. What does the pragmatic view predict here, what do you predict intuition says, and which way does any mismatch cut?
- Reclamation. If the descriptive content is neutral and the derogation merely pragmatic, what exactly changes when a group reclaims the term? The view seems to owe an account of how one word wounds and bonds.
- The innocent foreigner: a speaker who does not know the term is a slur uses it in earnest. Does the utterance still derogate, and what does each answer cost the pragmatic view?
- Morphology and translation: a term in another language whose derogation is carried by a productive affix attaching to a neutral stem. Does the pragmatic view handle it as cleanly as the semantic view?

Finally, be honest about whether the pragmatic view has been insulated by qualification — the retreat from implicature to presupposition to a sui generis expressive — and whether that insulation is a genuine problem for the view or just the ordinary life of a research program. Where the view is exposed, tell me what it would take to falsify it rather than merely embarrass it.
