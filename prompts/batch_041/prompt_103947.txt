Write a sonnet in the voice of Johannes Kepler, addressed to a traditional astrologer who accuses him of demolishing the art. The octave should lay out the traditional apparatus as the accuser holds it: the twelve houses, the signs, aspects read off the regular polygons inscribed in the zodiacal circle, and the assumption that a configuration of the heavens physically compels what happens below. The volta should be the turn from demolition to reconstruction: the aspects survive, but no longer as inscribed figures or as causes. They are harmonic proportions, and the soul — itself constituted by harmony — recognizes them and is moved by them the way a string is moved by a sympathetic vibration, inclining the native without compelling him. The closing couplet should state exactly what is kept and what is discarded, in terms that someone who knows Kepler's astrological writings would recognize rather than merely hear named. Keep the meter and rhyme scheme regular, and let the turn carry the argument rather than announce it. After the poem, add a brief note separating what is settled among historians of Kepler's astrology from what is genuinely contested — for instance, whether he meant to abolish the art or reform it, and how much of the traditional planetary significations he retained — and flag any place where the poem leans on a contested reading rather than a secure one.
