I'm a software engineer who leads a small platform team, and we keep discovering after the fact that a commitment we all treated as a team commitment was really just several people agreeing in the room. Write me a reusable procedure, grounded in the core idea from collective intentionality and joint action that a joint intention is not a sum of individual intentions but requires that each participant intends the group action, that this be common knowledge, and that the participants' subplans mesh. The procedure should be a short ordered checklist I can run in a sprint-planning meeting: the questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it returns a no, and where it must stop and hand the decision back to the team because the remaining issue is risk tolerance or values rather than whether a shared intention exists. Keep it usable by engineers with no philosophy background, and be explicit about the cases it cannot settle. Then run it on two cases: (1) the team says it will migrate the billing database by the end of the sprint, but the on-call engineer has privately decided not to help with rollback testing; (2) the team says it will stop supporting the legacy API, while two members understand this as freezing new features and one understands it as announcing an end-of-life date. For each case, give the procedure's output, what it leaves to judgment, and the next concrete step. Finally, state the main ways the procedure will misfire or be gamed.
