There is a pattern I keep running into in discussions of how to weigh one person's good against another's, and I would like to know whether it has an established name.

Everyone in these discussions grants that there is no fact of the matter about how much better or worse off one person is than another: the numbers one attaches to people are arbitrary up to any monotone transformation, and no point on the scale is privileged as the zero. The discussion then proceeds anyway, as it must, because some decision about who gets the scarce thing has to be made. What I keep noticing is that the comparison which actually settles the decision is never the one everyone agreed was meaningless. It is a disguised version of it, and nobody admits to making it.

Concretely: suppose we agree only that A is worse off than B, and that the distance between A and B is greater than the distance between C and D. Someone then argues that a policy which helps A and hurts C is an improvement, because the gain to A outweighs the loss to C. But outweighing is a comparison of magnitudes, which we just agreed we cannot make; to cash it out we would need a cardinal scale and a zero. Pressed on this, the arguer does not defend the scale. They switch to whichever scale delivers the verdict, and then present the verdict as following from the agreed facts. I have seen the same move in cost-benefit analysis, where a life-year is priced in money and a health state judged worse than the relevant zero can invert a ranking, and in arguments about inequality, where how much worse off the bottom is gets measured in proportional terms in one paragraph and in absolute terms in the next, and the choice flips the conclusion.

Note what is odd here. The parties agree on every comparison they are willing to state, and they still disagree about the verdict, so their disagreement is real but cannot be located in the vocabulary they share; the missing term is a scale, and each side silently supplies its own.

Does this have a name? I know the measurement-theoretic point about ordinal utility, the index-number problem for comparing real incomes, and the absolute-versus-relative deprivation distinction, and none of these seems to be quite it. What I am after is the phenomenon of the arbitrary scale doing the deciding while the parties continue to deny that any scale is doing work. If there is an established concept, say what it is and where it stops fitting. If there is not, coin one and mark its edges — in particular, how it differs from ordinary vagueness, from the index-number problem, and from the straightforward position that a cardinal interpersonal scale can be constructed. A bare label will not help me; I want the thing itself carved up.
