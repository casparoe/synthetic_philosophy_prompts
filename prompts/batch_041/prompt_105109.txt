I lead a small AI-safety team at a company that deploys LLM-based agents for customer operations. Our monitoring pipeline flags what we call mechanistic anomalies: unusual patterns in a model's internal activations, together with the causal effects of ablating individual features. When several incidents are flagged, we cluster them by whether they arise from what we call the same mechanism. That clustering determines our response: a single-mechanism cluster gets a targeted patch and the fleet stays online; a multi-mechanism cluster triggers a fleet-wide grounding and a broader review. Last week we found two failures that are identical from the outside—each causes the agent to promise a refund it has no authority to grant—and our tool reports that their causal fingerprints match. The fingerprint is computed by ablating the same set of features in the same order and measuring the change in the anomalous behavior; on that measure the two failures are indistinguishable. But when we inspect the underlying circuits, they are wired differently. One routes through a retrieval module that misreads a policy document; the other routes through a persona module that overgeneralizes a helpfulness heuristic. One of the two models is a fine-tune of the other, while the other is from an independently trained family. The fingerprint also depends on an intervention distribution that our tool chooses, and when we vary that distribution the match sometimes weakens. My colleague says they are the same mechanism because the intervention profile is the same, and that the fine-tuning relationship confirms it. I say they are different mechanisms because the internal organization differs, and that patching the shared feature will mask one failure while leaving the other latent. We have to decide by Friday. How should I think about what makes two anomalies instances of the same mechanism in a case like this, and what would you advise? I am not looking for generic root-cause-analysis advice; I need criteria that are defensible when causal profile and internal structure come apart, and a concrete recommendation for how to treat this cluster that I can defend to my VP, who will ask why we did not simply patch the shared feature.
