Write a dialogue between a Molinist and an open theist who's spent alot of time on the standard objections to middle knowledge. Both should be sharp and neither a strawman -- I want the open theist to really press the grounding objection (what makes a counterfactual of freedom true, if it isn't grounded in God's will and isn't grounded in what the creature actually ends up doing?), and I want the Molinist to actually answer it rather than hand-wave, with each one responding to what the other just said rather than talking past each other.

To keep it from being the usual recital, I want them arguing over two homemade cases I've been chewing on. Case one: Rosa faces a choice where there's genuinely no reason favoring either option -- perfectly symmetric, coin-flip, she'll just pick. Can there be a true "Rosa would freely pick A if placed in that situation," or does the total absence of any ground on her side make the subjunctive indeterminate -- which would gut middle knowledge for exactly the cases that matter for providence? Case two: before creation God surveys the possible scenarios, and via middle knowledge knows what each person would do, then picks one to actualize -- but suppose the scenario's own details (who exists, what Rosa's options even are) partly depend on that very decision, so the counterfactual God is supposed to know seems to loop back through the decree that's supposed to be informed by it. Let the Molinist try to untangle that without either denying God's freedom or making the counterfactuals depend on God's will (which would just collapse it into Calvinism), and let the open theist exploit whichever horn he goes for.

Give me exactly five exchanges, no intro or conclusion, and keep each turn tight.
