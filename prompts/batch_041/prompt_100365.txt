Here's the thing thats been bugging me. When a government agency decides whether a new safety or environmental rule is worth passing, they basically put a price on human life. I think the number floating around is somewhere in the millions per "statistical life" (maybe around ten million? I forget exactly). Then they compare what the rule costs against how many lives it would save times that price. If it costs more per life saved than the cutoff, the rule doesn't happen.

That seems to assume there's one big scale, a common currency, where a life and a dollar amount can sit side by side and be traded off. But it also seems like we don't actually believe that. Nobody thinks a person is "worth" ten million dollars the way a car is worth twenty grand. We say some things are priceless. We don't let people sell their organs. You can't buy your way out of a murder charge. So we run these calculations while telling ourselves the things being calculated are beyond price.

What I want to know: what view of value does this practice actually commit us to? Does it really require that all values are commensurable, one single scale, so that there's always a fact of the matter about how much money equals a life? Or could you run the exact same numbers while honestly believing some things are incommensurable, that there's just no answer to "how many dollars is a life worth"? And if the practice does need full commensurability, is that a fatal problem, or is it just the only sane way to make these decisions at scale?

Two things I'd especially like you to untangle, because I keep getting them muddled:

- What the practice strictly *needs* versus what it just happens to fit nicely with. I suspect it might be compatible with more than one theory of value and I want to know where it's actually underdetermined.
- The adding-up part. Treating "a statistical life" as something you can multiply — save a hundred lives, that's a hundred times the value of one — feels like a seperate assumption from the commensurability one. Does the practice need additivity too, or are those two separate commitments that could come apart?
