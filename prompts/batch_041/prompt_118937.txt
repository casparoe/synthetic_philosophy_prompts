I am a novelist finishing a comic novel set in a town that has adopted the ethical outlook of G. E. Moore's Principia Ethica as its official civic creed. I need an in-world artifact: a deadpan Friendship and Beauty Audit form issued by the town's Department of Intrinsic Value—roughly 700 words, with numbered sections, a scoring rubric, a method-of-isolation worksheet, and a short appeals procedure. The parody should target the Moorean view that the highest goods are certain organic unities of personal affection (friendship and love) and aesthetic enjoyment, and that the value of a whole is not the sum of its parts, not merely name-drop Moore. Let the form's criteria and scoring rules quietly press on the real difficulties: how to isolate a friendship's intrinsic value from its usefulness; what to do when a beautiful object is enjoyed by no one; whether a friendship's value can survive a bad constituent; how an auditor could know the value of a whole without summing; and what to do with a claimant who defines value in terms of satisfaction. The humor should come from treating these as bureaucratic questions, and the exaggerations should be fair to the position; a reader who knows Principia Ethica should recognize the real view, and a reader who does not should still follow the absurdity. No quotations or citations.
