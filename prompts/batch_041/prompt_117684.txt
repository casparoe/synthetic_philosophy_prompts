I'm a graduate student in philosophy putting together a one-page handout for a reading group on modularity and perceptual justification, and I want the handout to be a decision tree rather than a prose summary: something I can hand to someone who says, breezily, that of course your beliefs change what you see, and that walks them, question by question, to a defensible name for what they have committed to. The reason I want a tree and not an essay is that the real disagreements here almost never show up at the level of the headline claim; they show up in distinctions nobody states until you press them, and I want those distinctions to be the branch nodes. So please build the tree out of questions first and positions second: number each node, give each branch a label, and phrase every node as a question about a concrete case that a reader who has never heard the term cognitive penetration could still answer, with a note at each node saying which pair of commitments it separates. The axes I care about, though you should split or add any you think I've gotten wrong: whether the alleged influence lands on perceptual experience at all, or only on what the subject is willing to judge and report; whether it reaches the content of experience or only its phenomenal character; whether it acts directly on experience or only by way of something else, such as attention, memory, emotion, or expectation; whether the penetrating state must be cognitive in the narrow sense (a belief, a concept, a desire, an intention) or whether emotion, language, and influence from another sense modality count too; whether it must be online and momentary or may be the slow reshaping of perceptual categories through training and expertise; and whether the resulting perception is supposed to be rational in the light of the penetrating state or merely caused by it.

Use these as the raw material for the nodes, and add others of the same kind where they help: the Müller-Lyer lines, which are known to be equal and still look unequal; the duck-rabbit, where you can be told which aspect is which and then switch at will; a face in a crowd that resolves into a friend's face; the way a radiologist or a chess master seems to see the structure at a glance; a bleak mood that makes everything look bleaker; and the cross-modal cases where what one sense delivers changes what another sense seems to present. I'd like eight to twelve leaves. Every leaf should be named — the modularist or encapsulation view, the deflationary view on which the alleged effects are all attention, memory, or response bias rather than perception, the views on which only content or only phenomenal character is penetrated, the view that only attention counts and attention is not cognition, the view that cross-modal influence is the whole of the phenomenon, the view that perception is thoroughly theory-laden, the view that what changes is perceptual justification rather than experience, and the no-clean-case-exists position — and each leaf should carry one sentence on the commitment that will embarrass it and one sentence on what it implies about the diagnostic cases above, so that I can tell whether an interlocutor has landed somewhere they would actually want to be.

Two things I keep getting wrong when I try this myself. First, some combinations of answers look like positions nobody has taken: flag those as unoccupied and diagnose them — is the view incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored? Second, some distinctions turn on mechanism rather than on what a case is like or on what the person would say, and no sequence of answerable questions will separate them; if your tree cannot separate two leaves, say so explicitly and tell me what further question would be needed. Please don't include a reading list — I'll be working on this on a train with no internet.
