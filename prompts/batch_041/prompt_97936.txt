I'm in a reading group on population ethics and we keep tripping over a view one of my friends half-jokingly calls "lexical threshold negative utilitarianism," and since I have to present on it next week I want to practice defending it against someone who actually knows the terrain. So: please play a committed proponent of this view — a philosopher who has thought about it for years, believes it, and isn't a strawman — and I'll be the skeptic. Here's the view so we don't waste turns arguing about what it is: pleasure and suffering are commensurable and additive up to a threshold; below it you can trade a bit of discomfort against a bit of enjoyment at some exchange rate; but the moment suffering crosses the threshold it becomes lexically dominant, so no finite amount of pleasure or sub-threshold suffering, however enormous, can outweigh or justify it. Above the threshold you can be a pluralist or whatever you like. Stay in character until I say stop, keep each reply to a paragraph or two so it feels like a conversation and not an essay, concede small local points when I'm actually right about something, but never surrender the threshold itself — and if I throw a case at you that's underspecified, ask me to pin it down rather than answering around it.

Before you reply, think carefully and maybe brainstorm to yourself (not on the page) the two or three strongest things the proponent can say, then just give me the in-character response.

My opening move: I dont buy that this is even a form of utilitarianism. You've got a place where the numbers stop counting, and "the numbers stop counting here" is just a side-constraint wearing a utilitarian costume — fine, have your side-constraint, but then stop calling it a calculus. Worse, the discontinuity does real work you havent paid for. Take a dial that nudges one person's suffering from just under your threshhold to just over it — a difference you'd have to admit is tiny, maybe even imperceptible to them — and suddenly a million people's mild miseries can't buy it back. So either something genuinely special happens at that point, in which case tell me what, or your threshold is doing magic at a spot where by your own lights nothing much is going on, and a deontologist could point at that same spot and make exactly your move. Which is it?
