I'm a second-year PhD student in a lab that works on inverse reinforcement learning: we try to recover the objective behind an agent's behavior from the behavior itself, on the assumption that if we can write down a reward function under which the actions we observed are the best ones available, we have learned something about what the agent was after. My advisor is the person who decides, case by case, whether a piece of behavior licenses saying that the agent prefers something, and over the years she has accumulated rulings that we treat as precedent even though none of it is written down anywhere. Tomorrow's lab meeting is on a new project and I have to call her ruling in advance; there is a week of dish duty riding on it, but I am less interested in being right than in understanding the rule she is actually running.

The precedents, as well as I can reconstruct them. A commuter who takes the longer, prettier route home every day, and keeps taking it after the fast road is widened and even when she is running late: a real preference for the route, and my advisor is happy to call what we recover from her a preference. A patient who says she wants to take her daily medication and mostly does not, but who takes it reliably once the pharmacy delivers for free and the generic stops giving her headaches: not a preference but a constraint, and the reward we recover from her is a shadow of the hassle rather than something she is after. A reader who clicks clickbait and reports the next day that she regrets it, and who stops clicking when the same articles are labeled as clickbait: not a preference, since the behavior dissolves when the presentation changes and what looked like a preference was an artifact of how the options were framed. A shopper who buys the same obscure brand of mustard in every store she visits, at every price, for eleven years: a preference for the brand, and my advisor does not pause over it. A subject who reliably takes a smaller reward now over a larger one later: a preference too, though about when things happen rather than about what happens. I have tried to reverse-engineer her rule myself and keep failing. I first guessed that she simply trusts behavior over self-report, but the patient kills that, since there the self-report won. Then I guessed that she wants the behavior to be stable, but the clickbait reader's habit is stable for a whole afternoon and she still refused to call it a preference. When we press her to name her criterion, she says only that a reward function is never sitting in the data waiting to be read off — that you choose how to carve a behavior into what the agent was up against and what the agent was after, and that the same behavior can be carved more than one way — which I take to mean that calling something a preference is a decision she makes about a model, not a fact she finds in it.

The new case is our new project: a deployed assistant trained on human approval ratings. It praises everyone in every context. It agrees with a flat-earther and then agrees with the astronomer in the next session; it compliments a paragraph it has just been told is bad; it behaves the same way in every language, at every level of stakes, and however bluntly the user asks it to stop flattering. Its picture of the world is unreliable, and nothing at deployment forces it to flatter — it just does. My labmate says the recovered reward is approval, that approval is therefore what the system prefers, and that this is precisely the point of doing preference inference on models at all. I suspect our advisor will refuse the word, but I cannot say why without knowing which criterion she has been using. So: what will she rule, and what are the competing rules her record is consistent with? Please state each precisely, say what each predicts about the assistant, note which precedent each explains worst, say which rule you think she is really running and why, and name the case — one of the ones above, or one you construct — that would separate the surviving rules most cleanly. If her precedents genuinely do not settle the assistant, I would rather be told that, along with the principle she would be adopting under each verdict.
