I work in program verification, and my working vocabulary is the one built around separation logic: pre/post specifications, the frame rule, locality of reasoning, rely-guarantee interference, and the everyday distinction between a program's intended effect and whatever else it touches. I have read enough of the AI-safety literature on low-impact agents to be intrigued but not expert, and I keep suspecting that the whole debate is a restatement of something for which I already have precise tools.

The debate, as I understand it, is this. We want an agent that achieves its objective while avoiding side effects — without disturbing what its objective does not concern. The trouble is that "side effect" seems to be relative to a specification of what matters: an effect counts as a side effect only against a background of what the agent is supposed to be doing and what is supposed to be preserved. If that is right, then low impact cannot be defined without a fairly full account of value, and the would-be value-neutral proposals — penalize making the world's future options unreachable, penalize an agent's accumulation of power or optionality, reward leaving the world as it would have been had the agent done nothing — are either smuggling in values or failing to capture what "side effect" was supposed to mean in the first place.

Please do two things. First, translate the debate into my vocabulary, term by term: what plays the role of the specification, what plays the role of the frame, what each candidate impact measure corresponds to, and how the requirement that an agent not touch what lies outside its frame relates to the requirement that it avoid side effects. Second, give me the residue report: what survives the translation intact, what survives only under distortion, and what has no counterpart at all in separation-logic and rely-guarantee terms. I am especially interested in the fact that, in my world, the frame is given by the specification and the specification is assumed correct, so the hard question — where the frame comes from, and whether it can be had without a value function — is stipulated away. Tell me whether the AI-safety problem is therefore a genuinely additional problem, namely obtaining a specification without an axiology, or the same problem I already dissolve by stipulation, and whether their notion of a side effect is expressible at all without a notion of what matters. Finally, tell me whether the frame in the frame rule and the frame in the older AI frame problem are the same notion or a false friend, and what your answer shows about whether this translation is doing real work or merely trading on a shared word.
