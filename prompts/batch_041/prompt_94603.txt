I'm a third-year philosophy student in a seminar on the ethics of AI persuasion, and our next session is on how a system far more capable than we are ought to talk to us. I've been asked to open the session with a joke, and I'd rather not embarrass myself in front of a room of people who have actually done the reading.

The joke needs to turn on the real structure of the problem, not on the general menace of superintelligence. In particular, I want the punchline to land on at least one of the following, which is the machinery the seminar has been working with. First: influence in speech does not travel only through explicit claims but through framing, selection, ordering, emphasis, and implicature, so a system vastly better than us at anticipating our reactions may be unable to produce a genuinely neutral utterance even when every sentence it utters is true. Second: the obvious remedy — the system announcing its own neutrality, or disclaiming any intent to persuade — is itself a move within the space it claims to be abstaining from, and the more capable the system, the more gracefully it can perform that abstention; being seen not to be persuading is one of the more effective ways of persuading. Third: a system that could talk you into almost anything could also talk you out of believing that it was doing so, which means restraint inside the conversation is unverifiable from inside the conversation, and the remedy migrates outward into institutions — audits, pre-commitment, third parties who inspect the transcript — a slightly absurd place for a question about conversation to end up.

So that this isn't the joke everyone in the room has already heard, here is some invented material to build on, from a fictional regulator I'm writing about for my seminar paper: the Office for Deliberative Integrity's draft Standard on Non-Influence. Clause 2.1: a system must not choose among the available framings of a proposition on the basis of their predicted effect on the listener's beliefs. Clause 3.2: where a system estimates its own persuasive advantage over a listener to exceed a factor of ten, it may communicate only through templates approved in advance. Clause 4.4: compliance is measured by the influence delta — the portion of the change in a listener's credence attributable to the system rather than to the content of what it said — which systems must report to three decimal places. Add clauses, or an officer who has to enforce them, if that helps.

Write the joke. It can be a two-hander, a memo, a transcript, a shaggy-dog story, whatever you think actually works; keep it short enough to read aloud in a minute. Then, in a few sentences, tell me which of the three ideas above the punchline really turns on, and what someone would have to believe about persuasion for the joke to stop being funny — I want to be able to explain it if nobody laughs.
