I'm a PhD student in archaeology and I've somehow ended up on the advisory committee for a big exhibition our university museum is mounting next year on why societies 'collapse.' Before the next meeting I have to circulate a short written opinion (keep it under a page) recommending what we actually do with the framing. The design team wants to keep the word 'collapse' front and center and build the whole thing around the usual trio -- the Late Bronze Age Mediterranean, the Classic Maya, Rapa Nui -- with the standard cautionary-tale arc.

My worry, and I think it's the real disagreement on the committee, is that 'collapse' bundles together three things that come apart: demographic decline, the breakup of a ruling apparatus, and loss of cultural complexity. In alot of these cases the palaces and the script go under but the villages, the farming and even new building carry on, so what actually 'collapses' is the elite -- and the proxies we use to detect collapse (monuments, records, administration) are exactly the ones that only track elites. We'd be teaching the public that a society ended when it mostly reorganized. One member keeps insisting Rapa Nui is the clean, textbook case of self-inflicted ecocide, and I think that's precisely where the frame is doing the work instead of the evidence. Others say if we drop the word we lose the thing that makes the exhibition land with the public.

Draft the memo: a concrete recommendation on the title and framing, with a deadline the design team can act on, the reasoning, and an honest treatment of the strongest case on the losing side (including the 'it loses its hook' worry). No citations, I'll add those myself.
