I'm running a small interdisciplinary workshop on "non-human history" (a mix of historians, STS people, and a couple of philosophers) and I want to open with a scripted dialogue I can hand out as a discussion starter. Could you write it?

Setup: two characters, and I need both of them to be genuinely sharp — no strawman on either side, and they should actually answer each other's points instead of taking turns delivering monologues. One is a historian who's started arguing that things like a volcanic eruption, a multi-year drought, or a pathogen ought to be treated as historical *actors* in their own right. Her line is that calling such things mere "background conditions" or "triggers" is a dodge: if you've shown that a society only came apart because of X, then X is doing real explanatory work in your story, and refusing it the label is just squeamishness. The other is a skeptic who thinks this is environmental determinism wearing a nicer outfit — that "actor" language smuggles in agency where there's only causal relevance, and that it conveniently lets the humans who made decisions off the hook.

Two things I want them to really fight about, so it doesn't turn into a labels argument:

First, have the actor-ist offer an actual criterion and let the skeptic stress-test it. She proposes something like: a thing counts as an actor when removing it from the narrative doesn't just change the outcome but makes the outcome unintelligible — the rest of the story stops making sense without it. The skeptic's obvious move is that this is just counterfactual dependence, which the weather satisfies in every story ever told, so the criterion either over-includes (every rainstorm is an actor) or she has to quietly smuggle in a further requirement she hasn't stated. Make her either defend the criterion or revise it under pressure, and make the skeptic's counterexample do real work rather than being a cheap gotcha.

Second, don't let them collapse "has agency" and "is an actor" into one thing without noticing. The skeptic should accuse her of trading on an ambiguity between explanatory weight and moral/agential standing; her best reply is probably that this is exactly why we need a term *other* than "agent" — but then she owes an account of what the difference amounts to in practice. Push on that.

Anchor the whole thing in one concrete case and stay with it: a kingdom that suffers a famine in the year after a volcanic winter, where the actor-ist wants to say the eruption is a cause of the political collapse, and the skeptic wants to say the famine only became a collapse because of the grain-storage policy and a decision to keep exporting food. Let both of them have a real point here — the skeptic isn't denying the volcano happened, and the actor-ist isn't denying the policy mattered.

End it without a tidy resolution. I'd rather they reach a sharpened disagreement or a partial concession than a hug. Maybe 1200-1600 words, and please give the characters names and a little bit of voice so it doesn't read like a philosophy paper with two mouths.
