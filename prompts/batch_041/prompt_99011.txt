Write me a short story. I want it standing on Gödel's rotating universe — the solution to Einstein's equations from the late 40s where the whole cosmos spins, and the spin bends the causal paths back around on themselves, so a ship flying a wide enough circle can come back and meet its own departure. Dont stress about the physics being airtight; the philosophical sting is what I care about.

The sting, so you don't have to dig it up: Gödel's real target wasnt just that there's no universal "now" — relativity had already done that. His sharper point was that even if you give up on a global present, you might still think there's a global order, an objective before-and-after running through everything. A spinning universe wrecks that too, because the paths loop. No global now, no global succession. What Gödel thought was left is that time is ideal — a way we're forced to describe things, not a fact about the world. Nothing ever really becomes; the whole thing just is.

Now the wrinkle, so you can't just coast on the famous version of this: make the protagonist the official chronicler of a city in that universe. Her entire job is the linear record — year one, year two, on down. The story is the day she figures out the record can't be linear: follow the causal paths far enough and the last entry she's going to write turns out to be the same event as the first entry she inherited, word for word, and there's no fact of the matter about which came first. The founding of the city and its ending are the same afternoon. The book she keeps is even physically sewn that way — the final page stitched to the first, so you can keep reading forever without a seam.

Constraints: no character lectures about relativity or name-drops any of this; the idea has to come out of what happens. She has to make a real decision in the story, and it has to feel open to her from the inside even though the ledger already contains how it came out — I want that gap to be the ache of the whole thing. A page or two. End on an image or a gesture, not a paragraph that explains what it all meant.
