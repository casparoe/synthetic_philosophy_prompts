Im trying to get clear on the conceptual guts of futarchy -- the Robin Hanson thing where you vote on values (i.e. you pick some measurable welfare metric ahead of time) and then bet on beliefs (prediction markets decide which policy will push that metric up). The pitch is clear enough, but it seems like all the real action is hiding in the part thats not marketized: somebody has to fix the metric before any market opens, and theres no market on whether that metric is the right one, so the hardest political disagreement (what are we even maximizing?) gets settled by plain voting and then laundered through markets. Explain to me how a defender is supposed to handle that -- is there a principled reason the metric question is different in kind from the means question, or does futarchy just relocate ordinary politics one level up? And then stress-test it: suppose you tried to marketize the metric too, with markets on something like "after ten years of policies optimized for metric M, would people still endorse M?" -- does that actually solve anything, or does it just push the same problem up another level? Also say something about the related weirdness that the losing branch of every conditional market never gets observed, so nobody ever finds out whether the bet was right. I dont want a pros and cons list, I want the actual structure of the argument.
