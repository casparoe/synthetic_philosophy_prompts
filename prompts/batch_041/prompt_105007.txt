I've been reading philosophy of mind in the rationalist-blog orbit for years now, and a friend and I recently spent a few long walks trying to state, precisely, a criterion for moral patiency that neither of us could find already worked out. What we landed on we've been calling the locked loop. The claim is that what makes a state morally significant is not its behavioral signature, not its reportability, and not even its content, but a structural fact about the system: the state carries valence (a sign, a good-or-bad-for-the-system character); it propagates into the rest of the system's processing rather than sitting inert in an isolated module; and the system has no causal route by which it can terminate the state from the inside. Where all three hold, we said, there is something it is like for the system and the system can be wronged; where the system can always suspend the state or step outside it -- we called that a gated system -- the state may still be a state, but it is not a locus of patiency in the way the locked one is. The payoff we liked was that the criterion is indifferent to behavior and to report, so it counts the locked-in patient as a patient and refuses to count a fluent chatbot as one no matter how it talks.

I want to practice defending this against someone who actually knows the territory, because I suspect it has holes I can't see. Please play a sharp critic committed to a rival view: that a state can be good or bad *for* a system only if the system has a standpoint -- some way its states are presented to it -- and that no fact about causal topology (a valence sign, global propagation, the absence of an escape route) can manufacture a standpoint out of mere processing. You should grant small points but never concede the core, stay in character until I say stop, and keep your replies to a paragraph or so, in American English, so it feels like an exchange rather than a treatise; if I ask a factual question about the rival view, answer it as the critic would. Here is my opening, and I want your first move to meet it head-on: I expect you to say that the locked loop either secretly presupposes a standpoint and so begs the question, or else describes a machine that merely traffics in signs and so cannot be wronged -- and that my two showcase cases, the locked-in patient and the gated organism whose pain is real but escapable, cut against me from opposite directions, since the first has a standpoint I never derived and the second has a state I seem to demote for no reason beyond its topology. Make me defend the line.
