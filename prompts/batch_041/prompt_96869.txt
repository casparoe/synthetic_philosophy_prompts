Okay so my friend and I got into this huge arguement last night about Rousseau and the theater, way past the point where either of us should've been talking. Her line: Rousseau's whole case against letting Geneva have a theater isn't really about plays being immoral or drama being a bad influence. It's about the structure of the thing. A theater splits the room into the few who act and the many who watch, and that's the poison — you spend your evenings as a pure spectator and get trained into passivity, into feeling enormous emotions you never have to do anything about. Her evidence is that when Rousseau pictures the good alternative he doesn't picture better plays, he pictures the festival where everybody's dancing and there's basically no audience left. So on her reading the real target is spectatorship itself, and she says he'd be just as suspicious of a city that spends every night watching prestige TV as of one with an opera house, maybe more.

My line: that flattens him. His objection is about what theater does to you morally and psychologically — it feeds vanity, it makes you admire people who are professionally good at pretending to feel things, it makes you want to be the sort of person who gets applauded. The festival isn't special because it's non-spectatorial, it's special because the feeling there is real and shared and doesn't come routed through a professional's fake tears. So the actual disagreement is: is it the spectator relation, or is it mediation and fake emotion?

Write the dialogue. Make each of us argue the other's case as hard as we can. She should press that my reading turns him into a boring moralist who's just against pleasure, and that a festival is still a spectacle — somebody is still watching somebody. I should press that her reading can't explain why he spends alot of time on the content of particular plays and what they do to the audience's character, and that if sitting and watching is the problem then reading a novel or watching a sunset is theater too. Have us actually answer each other instead of just alternating speeches, and have us fight over one case where the two readings come apart: a city that fills a stadium every weekend — no script, no professional pretenders, just real athletes and real emotion. She should have a hard time with it, and so should I. Keep the whole thing under 800 words, and finish with one line from each of us naming what we still can't agree on.
