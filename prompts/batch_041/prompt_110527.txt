I'm a PhD student in ancient philosophy, and before I commit to a dissertation chapter I want to stress-test the position I've drifted into. What I currently hold:

(1) The dichotomy of control is the organizing principle of Stoic ethics, not a piece of therapeutic packaging.
(2) The Stoic notion standardly transliterated eph' hēmin is best read as causal control, precisified as follows: an outcome is up to me just in case, holding my deliberative process fixed, it counterfactually depends on what I decide.
(3) Virtue is the only good; health, wealth, and reputation are strictly indifferent.
(4) The preferred indifferents are load-bearing rather than a concession to ordinary life: the sage has genuine reason to prefer health to illness.
(5) The contemporary revival's central advice — concern yourself only with what is up to you — states Epictetus's position correctly and is continuous with it.
(6) The Stoic doctrine of fate is true: every event is a link in a single causal chain.

Please audit this rather than expound on it. Which of these are genuinely in tension, which merely look that way, and how badly does each real tension bite? Order them by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and for each say what would have to give, what the cheapest repair would cost, and whether the repaired position would still deserve to be called Stoic. Finally, tell me which single revision would resolve the most at once, and what that revision would leave me unable to say.
