I've been thinking about blackmail on my own, without any background in this, and I have an idea I'd like tested by a case rather than by an argument. The idea is that a threat has teeth only if there is something I care about that the threatener can reach, so if I arrange in advance, openly, that whenever I am threatened I will switch to caring about something else entirely — something no one can reach — then there is nothing left to hold over me. What I don't know is whether that works when nothing enforces it.

So build me one case, a single paragraph, with two ordinary people who cannot communicate, cannot make deals, and cannot influence what each other decides, though each is able to affect how the other fares. The only thing connecting them is that each knows the other is working out the same problem from the same facts; the resemblance between them has to be a fact about them, not something either chose or can fake. One of them issues a threat that has no channel to travel down — it is credible, if it is credible at all, only because the two of them are the same reasoning happening in two places. No smarter-than-human minds and no exotic physics. Have one of them adopt the switch-what-I-care-about defence before anything begins, and make the threat live. Then walk me through: what each party sees if he reasons only about the causal effects of his own choice; what he sees if he also treats the other's choice as a mirror of his own; whether the switch is still worth adopting when the resemblance is only rough, and who pays for it; and whether the switch is credible for the same reason the threat is, or for some other reason. Tell me which feature is doing the work — the isolation, the resemblance, or the switching — and what happens to the case if I remove each one in turn. Finish with the strongest case that all of this is wishful thinking, and that no real blackmailer would be moved by it.
