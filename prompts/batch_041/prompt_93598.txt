There's a theorem, Löb's, about systems that can talk about their own proofs. Rough version: if such a system can prove "if P is provable here, then P", then it already proves P. Two things fall out. Second, if you take P to be a contradiction you get Gödel's second incompleteness theorem as a freebie, so the system can't prove its own consistency. He then said this is the "Löbian obstacle" to self-trusting agents: any agent whose decisions lean on facts about its own proof-search — "I've proved that this is the action I'll take, therefore take it" — has to trust its own conclusions, and the theorem says it can't have that blanket self-trust without going trivial.
