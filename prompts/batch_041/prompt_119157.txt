so my econ teacher spent like half of todays class on the dollar auction and i cant tell how much of what he said i should actually believe, it was really convincing at the time but some of it feels off and i dont have the words to say why.

heres the setup he gave us. an auctioneer sells a one dollar bill. bids go up in small steps, five cents at a time. whoever bids the most pays what they bid and gets the dollar. but the second highest bidder also has to pay whatever they bid and gets nothing at all, so theyre the biggest loser. he said an economist named Martin Shubik came up with this in 1971 to show how short sighted rational choices add up to something stupid in the long run.

then the interesting part. once two people are bidding, the person in second place is stuck, because if they quit now they just eat the loss of whatever they already bid, whereas if they bid five cents more they might still come out ahead. so they bid again, and then the other person is in second place and does the same thing, and the bids sail right past a dollar, and the winner ends up paying more than a dollar for a dollar. he said this is the same thing as when a company keeps pouring money into a doomed project or two countries keep escalating a war neither can win.

then he gave us the diagnosis. its basically the sunk cost fallacy: people keep shoveling money in because they cant stand admitting they already wasted some, and if they were thinking straight theyd just ignore the money theyve already spent and walk away. he said the whole game basically proves that people dont actually maximize anything, and that the only real defence is to promise yourself before you sit down that youll never bid past a dollar.
