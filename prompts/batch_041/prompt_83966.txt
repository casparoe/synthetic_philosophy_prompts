A friend of mine who does taste psychophysics and a friend who does philosophy of mind have been going at each other for about a year now over whether qualia are a real posit or a philosophers' confusion, and I finally told them to just write the joint statement. So draft it for them.

Camp one is a sensory psychophysicist. She runs threshold and discrimination work on taste and smell, and she thinks talk of qualia is just under-described discrimination performance plus report behaviour dressed up as a metaphysical discovery -- and that the old coffee-taster case (the two tasters, one loses his taste for coffee, the other acquires it, and neither can tell from the inside whether the coffee changed or his palate did) is the whole story in miniature. Camp two is a philosopher of mind who thinks there's something it is like to taste coffee and that no amount of triangle-testing touches it.

Give me, in order: (1) a numbered list of claims both of them can sign, and make them non-vacuous -- we both care about consciousness is not a claim, and neither is a hedge that dissolves the dispute. Include what they both accept about what introspection can and can't settle, what the psychophysics does and doesn't show, and what work the coffee-taster case is actually doing. (2) Their real disagreement, with each side's position written in wording that side would sign off on as its own. (3) The cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling things would move at least one of them -- and for each one, a case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Two things I want them to be precise about. First, the traditional package has four parts -- ineffable, intrinsic, private, incorrigible -- and I suspect half the fight is over whether the realist needs all four, or whether dropping ineffability and incorrigibility leaves a position that the incoherence charge doesn't touch at all. Second, whether the eliminativist's argument is epistemic (we can't get at the quale) or metaphysical (there's nothing there to get at), and whether the realist can take the first while denying the second.

For cases, I want miracle fruit making a lemon taste sweet, toothpaste wrecking orange juice, the post-covid parosmia reports where familiar smells go wrong while discrimination is roughly intact, aphantasia (people report no imagery yet often do imagery tasks about as well as anyone), blindsight, and people saying music has gone flat on SSRIs. And have them design one experiment they'd both run -- say a taste adaptation protocol with separate scales for the taste itself changed and my reaction to it changed, plus a discrimination test -- and write down what each of them predicts and what would count as a miss.

Finish with: which part of the fight, if any, is just about the word qualia and what's left once that's granted; what each of them would count as evidence against their own view; and whether there's any case both agree would actually move the debate or whether they'd just redescribe it. And dont let them off with both are right in their own way -- the value here is the exact shape of whats left, not a truce.
