Write an essay on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — the worry that the states we call pleasures (the taste of a ripe peach, the relief of a headache lifting, the satisfaction of closing a proof, the delight of a friend's good news) share no single introspectively identifiable phenomenal feature, so that "pleasure" may name a heterogeneous assortment rather than one kind of state. The essay should be organized around a challenge I have been pressing on my graduate students, which I will state in my own terms as the collapse argument:

If the only thing common to every pleasure is that the subject wants it, or wants it to continue, then the unifying work is being done by desire, not by pleasure, and hedonism collapses into a desire-satisfaction theory of well-being. If instead there is a distinctive felt quality — a common hedonic tone — then we need an account of what that quality is, given how little the candidate pleasures seem to resemble one another. Either way, hedonism appears to be in trouble.

Your essay should do the following. First, explain why the problem is a problem for hedonism specifically, rather than merely a fact about the untidiness of ordinary language. Second, distinguish the sensory version of hedonism from the attitudinal version and say what each can and cannot say about the heterogeneity of pleasures. Third, assess at least two replies: for instance, that pleasure is a determinable with heterogeneous determinates; that pleasure is a functional kind rather than a phenomenal one; or that what unifies pleasures is a second-order attitude toward an experience rather than any feature of the experience itself. Fourth, work through a case that I find especially awkward for the sensory view: the same phenomenal quality — the smell of gasoline, say — is pleasant for one person and repellent for another, and in some people the valence flips over time while the smell itself is unchanged. If the quality is the same and the pleasure differs, what exactly is doing the work?

Then reach a verdict. You may conclude that hedonism survives, that it survives only in a revised form (and you must then say precisely what revision is required), or that it fails and should be replaced. Do not end on a non-committal note. Finally, state what your verdict implies for the claim that pleasure is the only intrinsic good, and for the claim that pleasure is what ultimately motivates action — the heterogeneity problem may bear on these two claims unequally, and you should say whether it does.

Write for a reader who already knows the standard positions; spend your space on the argument rather than on exposition of the familiar landscape. Do not cite literature, provide a bibliography, or gesture at reading lists — I care about the argument, not the apparatus. Aim for roughly 1,500 words.
