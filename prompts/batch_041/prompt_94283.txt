I'm a backend engineer with no formal philosophy background, but on my team I've somehow become the person everyone asks about the odd conceptual questions that surface during code review. The recurring one lately is about assertion itself. Someone will say "I assert the migration is safe," and a teammate will pounce — "do you know that, or do you merely believe it?" — and then we're off arguing about whether you're even permitted to assert something you only believe, or something that's true but that you don't believe, or something you sincerely believe that happens to be false. I understand there's a real literature behind this: that people have proposed assertion is governed by a knowledge norm, or a truth norm, or a belief norm, or a justified-belief norm, and that much of the interest lies in which of these is constitutive of the act itself versus which merely renders the speaker criticizable or open to challenge.

What I'd like is a joke I can tell at our next team lunch that would actually land with someone who knows this material. The punchline should turn on the real content of the competing norms rather than on "philosophers overanalyze everything." Ideally it exploits a case where the norms disagree about whether one and the same utterance even counts as a proper assertion — say, something true but disbelieved, something false but sincerely believed, a true claim offered on a lucky guess, or the familiar case of a near-certain but not known claim like "my lottery ticket will lose." A dry, dialogue-driven joke is fine, one to three paragraphs, and someone hearing it should be able to tell that I genuinely understand the positions. Please write it in American English.
