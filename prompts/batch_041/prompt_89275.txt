Kepler defended a reformed astrology: the planets act on terrestrial souls through the soul's responsiveness to geometrical harmony, so that the aspects — the angular relations between planets — carry the real influence, while the twelve houses, the traditional characters assigned to the signs, and much of the inherited lore are discarded or demoted. He also cast horoscopes for patrons and depended on that income. Take the following as the operative distinction: derivational integration, on which the retained astrology follows from the same harmonic principles that ground his astronomy; and systematic integration, on which his natural philosophy would be incomplete without it, the soul's receptivity being the counterpart term that closes the harmonic system.

Give me your credence that Kepler's astrology is a rationally integrated part of his natural philosophy rather than an accommodation to patronage, convention, or temperament — a claim with two halves, both of which must hold: that the aspect-based core is derivable from his harmonic physics, and that the line he drew between what he kept and what he discarded tracks an epistemic difference rather than a practical one. Decompose it: the probability that the core is so derivable; the probability that the grounds on which he rejected houses and signs are grounds that leave the aspects standing; the probability that the soul's response to geometry is a mechanism by his own standards rather than a restatement of what it was meant to explain; and anything else you think belongs — a number and a line of justification for each, then the combination. Then name the term you are least confident in and the evidence or argument that would move it most. I am near 0.4; a colleague who takes Kepler at his word is near 0.75 — say which term our disagreement most likely lives in. Give a definite verdict with your reasons, not a menu of readings.
