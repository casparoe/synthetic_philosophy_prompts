My lab has two camps that have been argueing for two years over which computations matter morally, and I need them to sit down and write the joint statement an adversarial collaboration would actually produce. First camp is substrate-neutral: if a system implements the right kind of computation — integrated, self-monitoring, globally available to the rest of the system, error-correcting against its own states — then it's a subject and it matters, silicon or neurons or whatever, and they think "in principle" is doing real work. Second camp thinks that's a category error: a state only carries moral weight if it's attributable to the system as its own, the way a toothache belongs to the animal and isn't just a process happening inside it, and they think that kind of attribution requires a self-maintaining organization that no functional description captures. They've been talking past each other, so I want the document they should of produced.

Specifically: (1) the claims both can sign — about what's actually in dispute, about what it means to "implement" a computation, about whether the substrate question is empirical or conceptual, and about what kind of result would even count as evidence against either side; (2) the precise disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept rather than a strawman version; (3) the cruxes — I think one is whether attribution is a further fact about a system or just a name we give to a certain kind of integration, another is whether the subject is the largest system with the right self-model or the module that actually does the work, and maybe a third is whether "sentience" is even the same thing on both sides or just two different questions wearing one word; (4) for each crux, a concrete case or experiment both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance — stuff like a subnetwork inside a bigger system that does all the morally relevant work, two independent modules each getting half the inputs, a system that models itself as an agent but only as a part of something larger, and a gradual replacement scenario where you swap biological parts for chips one at a time.

Where the disagreement turns out to be verbal, say so and say which word is doing the damage. No citations, no lit review, neither of them gets to just point at a paper. Numbered agreed claims first, then a disagreements table with a crux column and a predictions column.
