Retired chemist here (40 yrs of spectroscopy, philosophy only since I quit) and I've got myself stuck on grounding. All my working life "explains" meant "made of" -- down a level, down another, and at the bottom you hit stuff thats not made of anything smaller and nobody finds that spooky, thats just where the ladder stops. But the grounding people say the relation theyre after isnt composition at all, its in virtue of, and then they fight about whether that chain has to stop somewhere or can run down forever, and whether stopping it is a discovery or just the spot where you got tired.

I want a sestina about this, because the form is the argument: the six end-words are the six things that get their place in each stanza only because of where they sat in the stanza before, nothing free, nothing standing on its own. Six six-line stanzas plus the three-line envoi, end-words rotating the usual way (6,1,5,2,4,3 and so on -- I always have to look it up) with all six turning up again in the envoi. And somewhere in it I want the difference between made-of and in-virtue-of to actually land, since thats the bit I keep failing to keep straight.

One twist: the last line of the envoi has to land on a word that isnt one of the six, a word nothing earlier accounts for, just sitting there holding the rest up. And dont tell me whether that last word is the real foundation or a cop-out where I stopped asking -- leave it open, thats the whole reason I'm handing it to you instead of deciding it myself.
