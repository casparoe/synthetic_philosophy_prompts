I write a science-and-ideas column and I'm putting together a flowchart for a piece on the raven paradox — the one where 'all ravens are black' is supposed to say the same thing as 'all non-black things are non-ravens', so a green apple counts as evidence about ravens, which is obviously absurd. Every write-up of this does the same three beats (it does confirm, it confirms by a hair, and then something about natural kinds) and readers nod along, so I want a decision tree that sorts the positions by what they think that green apple is actually doing, not by which famous philosopher said it. Build it out of the logical space rather than a list of names: every branch node should be a question a reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and every leaf should be a position with a name (or 'unnamed' if nobody defends it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I want the tree to turn on things like: whether the observation confirms at all; whether the size of the background population makes any difference (does it confirm by a hair, or by literally nothing, if there are enough non-ravens?); the sampling question — did you draw an object at random from the world, or did you go out looking for ravens, and does it even count as evidence if you weren't testing anything; whether the person thinks 'confirming' means raising the probability or just means you'd cite it as a reason; and the escape routes, because the flat 'no' has to give up something and I want to see which one it gives up — that positive instances confirm, that logically equivalent hypotheses are confirmed by the same evidence, or that the hypothesis is really about everything in the universe rather than about ravens. Please also work in that the same green apple is a positive instance of 'all ravens are white' too, so it can't be discriminating much between rival hypotheses, and make some branch hinge on that. Six to eight leaves, no more than six branching questions, written for readers of a general column so no jargon you can't unpack in a few words. And tell me which combinations of answers are empty and why — incoherent, unattractive, or just never explored — plus any pair of leaves your tree honestly can't seperate, since alot of these positions differ only in how they'd phrase the same verdict.
