I teach a third-year ancient philosophy seminar and I'm putting together the take-home essay for the Plato unit. Every single year the students write some version of "Plato's against drunkenness — look at the Republic and the Symposium — but weirdly he defends wine in the Laws," and then they stop, like noticing the apparent contradiction is the whole job. I want a question that makes them actually do the philosophy.

So: one essay question, they get about 90 minutes, roughly 700 words. I want it to force them to explain what work wine is actually doing in the Laws — why the Athenian Stranger treats the drinking party as something you'd build into a city's institutions on purpose, rather than something you just tolerate, and how that hangs together with the psychology of the human being as a puppet yanked around by the cords, and with the thought that you only find out whether someone's been properly brought up once their guard is down. Then I want them to take a stand on the thing that I think is the real crux: is a virtue that evaporates after two cups ever a virtue at all, or is it just a habit that was never really tested? If the symposium is where you find that out, does that make it a school for virtue or just a very respectable excuse for a good time? I want them committed to an answer, not sitting on the fence. And I want them to say whether any of this is genuinely in tension with the Republic's worries about appetite and with Alcibiades crashing the party in the Symposium, or whether the tension dissolves once you see the wine as an instrument rather than a pleasure.

Give me the question, a model answer of around 600-700 words that a strong student could plausibly produce in the time, and a marking scheme. For the marking scheme I want five or six must-hit points, the marks for each out of 20 total, and for each point a one-line note on how students usually get it wrong — e.g. reading the puppet image as Plato quietly denying free will, or hearing aidos as "shame" in the bad sense and missing that it's the thing that holds the puppet's good cord taut. Make the A/B line explicit: name which moves separate a 17-20 answer from a 13-16 one.

Oh also — they have no internet during this, so don't build anything into the question that depends on them recalling exact wording from the text.
