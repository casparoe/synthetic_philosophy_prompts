I teach a first-year survey and every year I stall on the same spot in Aristotle's sea-battle chapter of De Interpretatione. Paraphrasing from memory, not quoting, so correct me if I mangle the details: either there will be a sea battle tomorrow or there won't; if there will be, then it was always true that there would be, so it couldn't have failed to happen, so it's necessary; same reasoning on the other side; so everything that happens was always going to happen and deliberation and effort are pointless. Aristotle's escape, as I read it, is that claims about future contingents aren't yet determinately true or false — so one of the two disjuncts isn't already the true one — yet he seems to keep some version of excluded middle anyway, which is where I lose the thread. Then there's the bit where whatever is, is necessary insofar as it is, but not necessary without qualification; the necessity attaches to the thing's being there rather than to its coming to be.

What I want is a walk-through of what's actually doing the work, in the argument and in the escape. Specifically: which premise gets given up, if any, and is that genuinely different from just denying bivalence? Is the necessity-insofar-as-it-is move a real solution or a relabelling of the problem? I've never been satisfied by it. And does the whole thing fall apart if you think the future is already settled by the present in some non-temporal way — block universe, whatever — and if so, whose problem is that, Aristotle's or ours? Throw in a modern cousin while you're at it: the lottery, where every ticket loses, one must win, yet no particular ticket is such that it necessarily loses. Does that map onto the sea battle in a way that clarifies the necessity claim, or is it only superficially similar?

If my framing is confused — if I'm misdescribing what Aristotle actually commits to — say so plainly instead of patching over it. I'd rather be told my reading is wrong than get a tidy answer to a badly posed question. End with a last line of the form \answer{escape works / escape fails / escape is verbal / framing is off}.
