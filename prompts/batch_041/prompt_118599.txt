For some years I've kept a private taxonomy of the ways I come to know things — perceiving, inferring, being told, remembering — mostly as a check on lumping distinct things together. One entry has never found a home, and I want to know whether the phenomenon has a name and, more to the point, how it is bounded.

The case: someone tells me that a certain bird is like a small crow but with a white patch on the wing. The sentence by itself tells me nothing about any actual bird. Months later I see one, and on the strength of the likeness I know both that this is the bird the description was for and what the word for it means. Two things resist my categories. I never held a general premise I could have stated — I couldn't have written the inference down, because I didn't know the class beforehand, and a felt resemblance is not a proposition I believed in advance. And the sentence gave me the description but not the knowledge; the knowledge arrived only when the description met a perception. So it is not testimony, not inference from a rule, and not bare perception either, since the seeing alone wouldn't have told me what the word meant.

A second case feels like the same shape but may not be: I know roughly what a friend is feeling by matching it against a state I have been in myself. Again nothing is deduced and nothing is reported; a likeness carries the recognition.

Is there an established concept for the first case, and does it extend to the second? Analogical reasoning looks close, but it appears to be a form of inference that presupposes I already have both terms — which is the thing I am trying to explain. Ostensive definition looks close, but nobody points, and what I receive is a likeness rather than a label. I don't much care which tradition supplies the vocabulary. What I want are the boundaries: what the move is exactly, which of my two cases fall inside it, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
