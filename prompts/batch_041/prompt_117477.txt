Ok, quick setup. I'm a chemist — analytical, 41 years of mostly instrument work — retired two years ago, and I've been working through philosophy in the mornings since. I've hit a wall on this business of justifying beliefs and I want you to tell me whether my position actually hangs together, because I genuinely can't tell, and I'd rather not guess wrong about my own views.

Here's where I've landed:

1. Everything I know rests eventually on observation. When I'm at the bench looking at the readout, that belief is bedrock — it doesn't need anything behind it. If you ask why I think the meter says 3.2, the honest answer is that I'm looking at it.

2. But nothing gets to be justified just because it feels obvious. "It's self-evident" is a fact about the person saying it, not a reason. I've known too many people with unshakable convictions to hand out free passes on that basis.

3. You never test one thing at a time. I spent most of one year trying to work out whether a strange result meant our model was wrong or the column was dirty, and no single experiment could of settled it — you have to change something in the whole bundle and see what else moves. A failed prediction tells you that something in the package is wrong. It doesn't tell you what.

4. I think an infinite chain of reasons justifies nothing. If every belief needs a reason, and every reason needs a reason, you've just pushed the problem down the road forever, and at no point does anyone actually have grounds to believe anything. I gather some people think an endless chain is fine. I think they've confused never running out of words with having a justification.

5. I do think a theory that pulls a lot of unrelated things together is likelier to be true than a patchwork one, and I don't think that's just me liking tidy theories. When a model predicts something in a corner of the field nobody built it for, that's telling you something real.

I know these have names — foundationalism, coherentism, infinitism — but I can't tell which one I am, since I seem to be running all three at once. So: which of these five actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each clash? Rank them. For the real ones, tell me what I'd have to give up and what it would cost me — I'd rather find out now than read for another year and discover I've been holding a contradiction.
