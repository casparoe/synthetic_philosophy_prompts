I am drawn to Dennett's claim that AI systems designed to pass as human persons are counterfeit people, and that creating them is a serious wrong because it corrodes the trust on which social life depends. What would count against this view? Consider AI companions that disclose they are AI yet provide genuine comfort to the isolated; AI therapists more available and less judgmental than human ones; and AI systems transparent about being AI but granted limited legal personhood. Which of these should lead me to abandon or qualify the thesis, and which should I dismiss? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say what the thesis predicts that its rivals do not, and note what a defender would concede as a genuine cost. In doing so, distinguish the wrongness of deception from the wrongness of creating a new kind of entity that mimics persons.
