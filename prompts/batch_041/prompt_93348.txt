I teach an upper-level value theory seminar, 90 minutes, about 18 students, and I want to run something that makes them feel a specific view in their gut before anyone says its name. Here's the view, and please describe it in your own words in the activity rather than labelling it, because the label does half the work for them: pain and pleasure are not tradeable against each other once the pain gets severe enough. Below some threshold, fine, they trade off at some rate -- an hour of boredom for a slice of cake, a headache for a good afternoon. But past a certain intensity, no amount of good stuff buys the bad off. The bad wins lexically; it's a different scale. Below the line, one scale; above it, no scale. And the nasty follow-up, which I want them to walk into: is that threshold a fact about value, or just a fact about how our guts are wired?

So: design the activity. Rules, roles, materials, a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money (tokens, points, snacks are fine), a timeline that fits 90 minutes, and a debrief. One constraint I care about: at least one round has to have a genuinely real, small stake -- not simulated. Something mildly unpleasant they actually do or actually give up, so the first round's "prices" are real prices. The bigger pains later can be hypothetical, obviously we're not breaking fingers.

I want them to (a) find out they have a going rate for small stuff -- the moment prices appear on the board is the moment commensurability looks obvious and boring; (b) hit refusals at the high end -- "I wouldn't do that for any amount" -- which is the threshold; (c) then watch the threshold slide around, depending on framing, on who's paying, and on whether the pain is theirs or a stranger's. That last part is the interesting bit and I'd like the mechanics to force it rather than just discuss it.

If it helps you seed it, I'd been toying with a made-up "Compensation Authority" charter -- a fictional rulebook that says Tier 1 and 2 harms may be traded at market rates but no benefit however large may ever be purchased with a Tier 3 harm. Totally invented, use it, gut it, or replace it, I just like the idea of handing them a rulebook that quietly builds the threshold into the furniture and then making them run trades through it.

Two more things I need. First, what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this and how to handle it -- I already expect people to moralize ("you can't put a price on that") and to anchor off each other's answers, but tell me the rest. Second, be honest with me about what the activity can't do. My worry is that denominating everything in tokens already presupposes one common scale, so the game might beg the question against the incommensurability it's supposed to make vivid -- if that's right, say so, and tell me what I have to supply in the debrief that the game itself can't deliver.
