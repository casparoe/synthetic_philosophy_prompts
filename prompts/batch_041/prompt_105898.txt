I'm a philosophy professor (epistemology and philosophy of mind mostly) and I'm putting together some seminar material, and there's a pattern I keep half-noticing and can't put a name to, so I'm hoping you can either give me the term or, if there isn't one, coin something and defend it. Here's the shape of it. In ordinary life and in my students' writing I keep bumping into the idea that certain facts about a person are ones they simply can't be in the dark about — if you're in pain, you know you're in pain; if you believe P, then you know you believe P. Fine, maybe. But then people slide from that to graded, dispositional, or achievement-ish properties — how confident you are, how generous you are, how relaxed you are, whether you're actually listening — and something strange happens. The more you try to check whether you're being generous right now, the less generous you become; the harder you look for your own confidence, the more it evaporates. And it's not that the person is wrong about themselves, it's that the very act of turning the light on the property seems to alter it, and the better they are at the thing, the less access they seem to have. Then I see a third move from the other direction: someone says, well, no mental state at all is guaranteed to be known by its owner, and uses that to flatten self-knowledge wholesale, as if the failure in the pain case and the failure in the generosity case were the same failure. I have a nagging feeling the second and third moves are governed by different principles and I keep conflating them.

What I'd like from you. First, is there a name for this whole phenomenon — the condition of a state being one you can be in without being in a position to know you're in it, and especially the way that checking can destroy it? Second, if there's a nearest established concept, tell me exactly how it differs from what I've described, because I suspect I'm blurring two or three distinct things together (and if the honest answer is that no single label covers it, say so rather than forcing one). Third, if nothing in the literature is quite this, coin a term and give me the definition, the boundary cases, and what it rules out — I don't want a bare label, I want the carving: what exactly the thing is, what it isn't, and where its edges are, including how it relates to the familiar slogan that knowing entails knowing that you know.
