So we're on the same page about the philosophy, and so you don't have to lean on recalling the text: On Certainty is Wittgenstein's last set of notes, written in 1950-51 and published after his death, mostly a running argument with G.E. Moore about Moore's claim to know things like that he has two hands. Wittgenstein's move is that Moore saying he knows is out of place there -- those aren't things we know, they're things that have to stand fast for anything else to be doubted or checked at all. He says some propositions are exempt from doubt, like hinges on which our questions and doubts turn, and he has that picture of doubt as a river whose bed is the stuff not currently in question, with the bed itself shifting slowly over time. (Don't quote him at me, I just want the picture.) These certainties aren't beliefs we arrived at by evidence; they're picked up in upbringing, held in place by a community and by agreement in judgments, by what he calls a form of life. Not justified, not doubted, not even believed in the ordinary sense -- more like acted on. Contemporary people argue about whether hinges are propositions at all, whether we have a non-evidential entitlement to them rather than knowledge of them, and whether a hinge can turn out to have been false all along. All fair game.

Now the film. Leonard Shelby can't form new long-term memories. He builds a self out of Polaroids, tattoos and notes, and the big reveal is that he already killed the man he thinks he's still hunting, and that at the end he deliberately burns a photo and writes himself a lie about Teddy so that he'll have someone left to go after. He isn't just a guy with bad notes, he's curating his own certainties. What I want:

1. The strongest version of the reading. Does Leonard dramatize the structure of hinge certainty -- the idea that certainty is a matter of the role a thing plays in making enquiry possible, not of evidence -- or is he just a vivid illustration of memory being unreliable? Where does the film get something exactly right that a philosophy paper couldn't? Point me at the scenes that carry the weight (no need to quote dialogue, I know the film).

2. Where the analogy breaks, and whether the break is interesting or fatal. Some worries, though don't limit yourself to these: Wittgenstein's hinges aren't chosen and Leonard's are; Wittgenstein's hinges come from a community, whereas Leonard's get installed in him by Teddy and Natalie, which might be closer to Wittgenstein than I think or might be a totally different phenomenon; Wittgenstein's hinges get acted on rather than written down, so Leonard's notes might be hypotheses in Wittgenstein's sense and not hinges at all; and if a hinge can be self-installed at will, that starts to look like a counterexample to the framework rather than an illustration of it. Tell me which of these actually bite and which are just me being clever.

3. The one I really care about. What does the film do to the claim that doubt needs a ground? Leonard is a limit case where the medium of reasons -- memory, testifying to yourself -- is gone. Does that make him the best possible test of the view, or does it put him so far outside the ordinary case that he can't tell us anything? And the twist raises something nasty: if a certainty is consciously maintained so that action stays possible, is that a hinge, a pretense of a hinge, or plain self-deception? Is the film's answer different from Wittgenstein's, or the same answer arrived at from the other direction?

4. Where I'm probably wrong. Be honest about whether this is one of those readings that only sounds deep because Leonard's condition makes anything about knowledge and grounds sound deep, and say what a skeptic about the reading would say. Then: what does the film add to the actual debate -- something about the relation between a certainty and the person who has to keep holding it up, maybe -- that the literature doesn't already say?
