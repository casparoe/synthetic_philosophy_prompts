For roughly two years a reading group I belong to has been returning to the same question and getting nowhere: when a large language model asserts that it believes the meeting is on Tuesday, or that it suspects it has made an error, is there any sense in which the system holds a belief, or is belief-talk here a convenient shorthand at best and a category mistake at worst?

The group has settled into two camps. One treats the intentional stance as a genuine predictive and explanatory strategy: if ascribing beliefs, desires, and the rest lets us anticipate the system's outputs more reliably than any lower-level description of its weights, then it has beliefs in the only sense that was ever doing explanatory work, and the further demand for some inner fact to correspond to the ascription is a demand the concept never met even in the human case. The other camp holds that belief-ascription is not merely predictive. It carries commitments about the subject — that it can be right or wrong, that it is answerable for what it believes, that there is someone home to whom the belief belongs — and that no quantity of predictive success purchases any of these. Neither side has shifted an inch, and I have come to suspect the dispute has almost nothing to do with machines.

My hypothesis is this: the two camps are using a single word for two different jobs. One job is predictive and explanatory, and could in principle be discharged by any system whose behavior is best modeled that way. The other is normative and attributive, and presupposes a subject who can be held to account. If that is the real division, then the argument about AI belief is downstream of an unexamined argument about whether those two jobs can come apart at all, and the apparent disagreement is either verbal or a pair of answers to two different questions filed under one heading.

I want that hypothesis tested rather than assumed. Here is the case I keep proposing and that nobody will take up: a system that is indistinguishable from a believer in every predictive respect — it tracks its own outputs, revises under challenge, reports sincerely, and submits to being held responsible — while failing every test anyone has offered for the presence of an inner state. Does the normative camp count it as a believer and the predictive camp decline, or do the two camps actually converge on such a case, showing that the real fault line lies somewhere else entirely?

Diagnose the impasse using what each side actually says, not a survey of positions on machine mentality. Is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or two questions under one name? Is some hidden empirical or conceptual question — about the metaphysics of subjects, or about what a concept is for — doing the work? And given your diagnosis, say what would count as progress: a distinction, a case, a criterion, or an argument that none should be expected, and why.
