I'm an undergrad and I somehow ended up as the student member of our university's Institutional Data and Equity Reporting Committee (nobody else wanted the seat). We have a real decision at the March meeting and I have to circulate a written opinion before it, so I need help drafting the thing. Here's the mess. Every spring we run the campus climate survey, and one item asks students to pick a race/ethnicity from a fixed list. Response rate on that item is fine, but the write-in complaints are not: people wrote things like "mixed, but you make me pick one," "I don't think of myself as a race, that's something other people do to me," and one student wrote a whole paragraph about how the list is a government artifact that our office is now laundering into findings. So there are three proposals on the table. (A) Keep the fixed list, add a write-in. (B) Replace self-identification with a two-part item, "how do you identify" plus "how do you think others usually read you," on the arguement that for equity reporting the second one is what actually tracks who gets stopped, referred to conduct, or overlooked. (C) Drop the item and infer demographics from financial-aid and admissions records, which our data folks say they can do well enough and which they like because it's complete instead of voluntary. The chair wants a recommendation with actual item wording and a timeline for piloting it in the fall survey.

The part I really want help with, because I'm taking a philosophy course on social kinds and I keep dragging it into meetings, is whether any of this matters metaphysically or whether I'm just being annoying. One committee member keeps saying race is socially constructed, like money, so measuring it is like measuring Monopoly — and then in the same breath wants the equity report to show that Black students get referred to conduct hearings at twice the rate, which seems to assume the category is tracking something real. I can't tell if those two claims are inconsistent, or if they're both fine and I'm confused about what "constructed" is supposed to rule out. Our gender item is up for the same treatment: there's a proposal to ask about gender modality (whether you're trans or cis) seperately from gender identity, and one member says that's just adding a category to a category, while another says modality is a different kind of thing entirely and conflating the two is exactly what made the old question useless. There's also a sub-argument I can't adjudicate about whether a category has to be endorsed by the person it's applied to in order to be the same category — if I'm read as something I don't accept, is the office measuring my race or somebody else's perception of it, and are those one variable or two?

So: draft the written opinion I'll circulate. It should recommend one of A/B/C, or a modification if you think the right answer is none of them, with the actual proposed item wording, a realistic timeline, and the reasoning laid out so a skeptical member can see why their preferred option was rejected on the merits rather than just outvoted. I'd especially like the memo to say plainly what turns on the philosophical question and what doesn't — I suspect a lot of it doesn't and that the construction talk is being used to avoid deciding something else, but tell me if I'm wrong about that. Please don't pad it with references or a reading list, we're all busy and I can't check them anyway. And at the end, tell me how confident you actually are in the recommendation and single out the claim in the memo you're least sure of — I'd rather circulate something with an honest hedge in it than a memo that sounds certain about everything.
