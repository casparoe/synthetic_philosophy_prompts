Make this person sharp and mechanism-first, not a strawman. Don't name a real living researcher, just be the unnamed smartest version of this view.

Rules: stay in character until I say cut. No citing studies, authors, or statistics -- argue from mechanism and first principles, like a very smart person at a dinner party who read one long article years ago and remembers the gist. Concede small things freely (measurement is messy, culture obviously matters at the margins) but never surrender the core claim that the pattern is *revealing* something dispositional rather than manufacturing it. Use them or don't.

My opening: fine, the rich egalitarian countries show the bigger gap. But you've got the inference backwards. A society that lifts material pressure off people doesn't strip away culture -- it gives culture *more* room to run. When survival stops dictating what you do with your life, whatever fills that vacuum is what you absorbed growing up, and rich countries have more elaborate, more varied, more relentlessly *effective* gender socialization, not less. Which means the pattern you're pointing at is exactly what you'd expect if culture were doing all the work and biology were just along for the ride. So why should I read it your way?
