1. Personal identity over time consists in psychological continuity and connectedness, not in bodily or brain continuity.
2. If my whole brain got transplanted into someone else's body (my old body destroyed), I go with the brain. That's me waking up over there.
3. Teletransportation — scanner reads me, destroys the original, rebuilds an atom-perfect copy on Mars — is death. The guy on Mars will think he's me and will act like me and nobody will be able to tell, but he isn't me.
4. Gradual replacement is fine though. If you swap my neurons out one at a time for functionally identical silicon bits, while I'm awake and talking through the whole thing, I survive. Same person at the end, just running on different hardware.
5. I've basically bought the Parfit line that identity isn't what actually matters in survival — what matters is psychological continuity/connectedness, and "identity" is at best a convenient label we slap on it.
6. But branching is a dealbreaker. If a perfect duplicate of me gets made while I'm still walking around, that duplicate isn't me, and it doesn't get my stuff, my partner, my promises, my job, any of it.
