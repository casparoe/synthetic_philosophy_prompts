In a seminar on skepticism, a classmate keeps defusing objections with the line 'you can't doubt everything at once.' I cannot tell whether the line is doing real work or merely ending the conversation. Lay out the distinct claims 'you can't doubt everything at once' could be making -- psychological, semantic, normative, and transcendental versions, and versions about single beliefs versus whole frameworks -- and for each say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false, and whether it would actually license his two dismissals. I want the readings as a menu I can take back to him, ending with your best guess at which one he actually means.
