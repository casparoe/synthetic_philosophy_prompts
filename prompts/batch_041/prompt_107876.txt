A friend of mine keeps saying the genome is just a text — the "book of life," the "language of life" — and he thinks that settles something: that a gene's meaning is intrinsic to the sequence, sitting there in the letters waiting to be read off, the way a sentence's meaning is in the sentence. He uses this to argue that once you have the sequence you basically have the organism, or at least all the "information," and that the reader (the cell, the scientist, whoever) is doing something trivial and mechanical. I told him about that GEB thing — Hofstadter's frame message / outer message / inner message distinction, the idea that a message isn't just its content, there's a layer that tells you how to decode it and a layer that tells you how to use the decoder, and meaning is relational, it lives in the whole system, not in the ink. He thinks that's a cute analogy for language but doesn't apply to DNA, because DNA isn't addressed to a reader the way a letter is.

So: how far does the genome-is-a-text analogy actually hold, and does his conclusion survive once we're honest about it? Give me the correspondence — what plays the role of the inner message, the outer message, the frame message — and then the places the mapping breaks. I suspect the "reading frame" thing in actual genetics is relevant here (the fact that the same stretch of sequence read starting one letter over gives you a completely different protein, which seems like it cuts against meaning being intrinsic but maybe also cuts against it being "reader-relative" in Hofstadter's sense, I can't tell). Also overlapping genes, the redundancy of the code, all of that. Separate the disanalogies that actually matter for his claim from the ones that are just decorative, and tell me whether the analogy was doing the work in his argument or whether the argument stands on its own.

One more thing, since you'll be giving me a verdict: tell me what would change your mind. Like, what would a genome have to look like for the text analogy to be the right one, or what consideration about frames and decoders would flip your read?
