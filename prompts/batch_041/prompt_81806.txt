Bit of a weird one, and it might be nothing, but I've been keeping a notes file for a couple months now and I can't sort it out on my own.

I'm in a small philosophy reading group and I started writing down sentences that make me flinch when I say them outloud to another person, even though nothing about them is false and nothing about them is flatly contradictory. The one that started it was 'it's raining but I don't believe it' -- I can say that to myself, and it's actually true of me sometimes, but saying it to someone is awful. Then I noticed it's not only belief. 'I know your name but I've forgotten it' is bad. 'I'm asleep' is bad. 'I'm not here' is bad. 'I have no thoughts' is bad. 'I'm not saying anything' is bad, though I can't tell if that one is bad for the same reason or a different one. What really bothers me is that the fixes don't line up. 'It was raining but I didn't believe it' is fine, and 'I was asleep' is also fine, but those two feel fine in different ways. 'It's raining but you don't believe it' is fine, and 'you're asleep' is fine, again in different ways. All the sleep/here/thoughts ones seem stuck in the present -- 'I was asleep' is fine, 'I'll be asleep soon' is fine -- whereas the belief one is bad now and fine in the past, and I can't tell if that's one asymmetry or two.

There's also a bit I can't explain at all: typing 'it's raining but I don't believe it' into a group chat feels less bad than saying it, and writing it in my diary, or putting it in a story, or wrapping it as 'suppose it's raining and I don't believe it', mostly drains the flinch out -- but not equally for all of them, and for a couple of them it doesn't drain at all. So: is this family already named, and if the nearest thing people have a name for only really covers the belief and knowledge ones, what are the others doing, and are they the same phenomenon or a different one wearing the same clothes? I'd like the smallest edit that takes a sentence out of the family -- change the verb, flip the tense, change who it's about, embed it under 'suppose', put it in a story -- and I'd like to know if there's a name for the specific sub-pattern where something is fine to think and fine to write but not fine to say to a person. A label by itself won't do it for me; I want to see where the seams actually are.
