A friend of mine runs onboarding at a mid-size consultancy and has just been handed a "culture refresh" that is, as far as I can tell, Plato's Laws Book I–II with the labels taken off: mandatory drinking sessions as professional development, each run by a designated sober facilitator who never drinks and scores everybody, booze billed as a cheap low-stakes way of testing a colleague for anger, lust, insolence, covetousness and extravagance, and the theory that wine softens the soul like iron in a fire so people stop being too embarrassed to sing the company's values out loud. Under-30s may drink in moderation but never to the point of intoxication; over-40s get a bigger allocation on the grounds that Dionysus's gift is medicine against the crabbedness of old age and makes you feel young again; the whole module is described in the deck as a school of temperance.

Write the handbook entry for it. Around 600–700 words, deadpan corporate HR voice, no winking, formatted as a handbook section with a handful of numbered policies and a boxed scoring rubric. It should be funny to someone who's read the Laws and readable to someone who hasn't, and the jokes have to come out of the real joints of the argument rather than generic drunk-office gags: the sober commander ruling the un-sober, character as whatever gets revealed under the influence, shame and modesty recruited as a noble fear to fight the ignoble kind, a "playful method of inspection" that supposedly costs nobody anything, and the fact that the man who wants this program for the staff is also the one who'd never let his own elite drink. Dont quote or cite the dialogue — the premise is a company that has quietly implemented the thing and has no idea its doing philosophy. And don't let it drift into defending Plato; I want satire from inside the practice, not a book report.
