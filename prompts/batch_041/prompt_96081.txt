I've been asked to circulate a position statement on providence before my reading group meets on it, and I'd like a coherence audit of the package before I do. Here is what I actually hold.

(1) Libertarian free will. A free choice is not caused, determined, or otherwise fixed by anything prior to it — not by God's decree, not by the agent's own settled character, not by the circumstances. If the agent could not have done otherwise, the act was not free.

(2) Middle knowledge. Prior to any act of the divine will there are true counterfactuals of creaturely freedom — propositions of the form 'were S in circumstances C, S would freely do A' — and God knows them. Which of these counterfactuals hold is not something God decides; middle knowledge is a matter of cognition, not of will. Because he knows them, God can order the world so that free creaturely acts serve his purposes without being determined by him. I take this to be the literal truth about how providence works, not a notational device for restating what God already knows.

(3) Truthmaker maximalism. Every true contingent proposition is true in virtue of something. I do not accept free-floating contingent truths, and I do not think a proposition can be true merely because it suits us to say so.

(4) Divine simplicity, aseity, and immutability. God's knowledge is not a component of him but is identical with his essence; nothing in God depends on anything created; and God does not change.

(5) Exhaustive foreknowledge without determination. God infallibly knows today what I will freely do tomorrow, and this foreknowledge neither causes my act nor renders it unfree.

(6) The efficacy of petitionary prayer. Which feasible world God chooses to actualize is sensitive to creaturely petition, and this is a large part of why prayer is not a charade given (2).

(7) God is not the author of sin. In actualizing a world in which creatures sin, God does not produce the sin in the way the creature does.

Tell me which of these pull against each other, which only appear to, and how severe each real tension is. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one give the cheapest repair and say what that repair costs me elsewhere in the set. I would rather be told that three things actually need fixing than be handed a list of everything that could conceivably be doubted, so please be willing to say that a given pair is fine and why. If exactly one of the seven has to go to save the rest, say which and what the argument for dropping it would be.
