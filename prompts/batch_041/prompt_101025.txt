A couple weeks back two people in my lab got into it about a metric we'd started using, and I've been chewing on it ever since. Setting: small applied-research group, our manager starts grading everyone on a "productivity score" that's a weighted mix of stuff like number of experiments run, how many got written up, and how many got picked up by other teams. Predictably people started running cheap experiments to pad the first term and writing up trivial stuff to pad the second.

Maya said the whole thing was a textbook case of gaming and should just be scrapped. Dev said no — every metric gets gamed, thats not a bug, it's people responding to the incentive, and if the score tracked something real then gaming it would have to produce the real thing, so the fix is a better score, not giving up on scoring. Maya said that's the exact error: the number going up isn't the same as the thing going up, you can raise the score while the underlying thing stays flat or drops, and that's literally what happened. Then she floated a fix: dont tell people the weights, and quietly rotate which components count every quarter so nobody can target them. Dev said a secret metric just means people optimize their best guess of what a reasonable metric would be, which is a proxy too, and rotating it makes the signal noisy so people end up optimizing noise. He also said the whole "law" is basically a tautology dressed up as an empirical finding — anything used as a target that isn't identical to the goal can be gamed — and the only interesting question is when the gap is small.

They both wanted me to declare a winner and I honestly couldn't. Can you go through it move by move — Maya's scrap-it, Dev's reply, Maya's "not the same thing" point, Maya's secret-rotating-metric proposal, Dev's answer to that, and Dev's tautology claim — rating each from clearly wrong to clearly right with a short justification, and flagging where they're just talking past each other? And please separate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who study this — dont hand me a contested view like its the consensus.
