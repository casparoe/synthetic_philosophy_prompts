I sit on the truth and reconciliation commission of a small country that's just come out of a twelve-year civil war, and I have to circulate a written opinion before Thursday's vote on one case. The guy in front of us is a militia comander who ordered the burning of a village — about forty civilians died. He's told us everything, and honestly his testimony is the only evidence we're ever likely to get against the army general who armed him and gave the orders, who's still walking around free and still has friends in the new government. Two things are making this hard. One, the file shows he also used the raid to settle an old land dispute with one of the families and to loot the place, so it's not clean whether this was 'political' in the sense our mandate means. Two, the survivors' association is split down the middle: most of them have told us they'd accept amnesty plus a reparations package and his help building the memorial, but a stubborn minority say only a trial can honor their dead, and that we have no business trading their grief for the country's stability. There's also a proposal floating around to make any amnesty conditional — grant it now but pull it back if he refuses to testify at the general's trial or if he recants later. Draft the opinion: I need a concrete recommendation with a time frame and the reasoning behind it, and I need you to take the minority survivors' objection seriously instead of just waving at 'reconciliation.' And please don't just tell me what South Africa or Argentina did — our mandate and our politics aren't theirs, so reason it out from the case.
