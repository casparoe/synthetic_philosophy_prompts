I am putting together a session for a reading group that spans philosophy of biology and cognitive science, and I have two members who have been talking past each other for months. The first holds that there are free-floating rationales: that a trait or a bit of behaviour can be correctly said to have a reason behind it even though no reasoner ever entertains that reason, so that the rationale is real without being anyone's. The second holds that all genuine reason-talk requires a representing subject, and that what the first calls a free-floating rationale is at best a compressed description of selection history or of a mechanism, not a reason in any literal sense. They have agreed to attempt an adversarial collaboration, and I have been asked to draft the document they could both sign. To keep the exchange concrete, use the following hypothetical case. A newly described insect, the mosaic bark-borer, lays its eggs only on trees infected by a particular fungus; after laying, it performs a fixed sequence of movements that, if a human did them, would look like a deliberate strategy to protect the eggs from a parasitoid wasp, including a timing pattern and a decoy movement that draws the wasp away from the clutch. The insect has a very small nervous system, shows no evidence of learning or of representing the wasp, and the whole sequence is reliably produced by a fixed circuit. Neither side disputes any of those mechanistic facts. The dispute is whether there is a non-metaphorical sense in which the insect has a reason, or in which the behaviour has a rationale, that is not anyone's reason.

Please draft the joint statement. I want a numbered section of claims both sides can sign, including claims about what would count as a reason in the disputed sense, about the role of selection history, and about what the mechanistic facts do and do not settle. Then a disagreements table with a row for each remaining disagreement, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own position. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side, and for each crux a concrete case, experiment, or argument that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why. Please make the document constructive and exact rather than a compromise that dissolves the disagreement.
