Okay so I'm a novelist — fourth book, first time trying anything that's supposed to be historical, and I've walked into a wall that isn't really about plot.

Here's the hinge of the whole thing: Franz Ferdinand lives. In my version the driver doesn't take that wrong street and the car doesn't stall right in front of Princip, so the archduke goes home to his wife and his roses, and nothing else about the world changes — same alliances, same arms race, same Balkan mess, same general staffs sitting on their train timetables. Then I've got a character in chapter nine who says the war was coming anyway, and I realize I don't actually know if I believe her. So I need a number.

Given that Sarajevo fails, what's your credence that a general European war — one that pulls in Germany, France, Russia and Britain, not just Austria and Serbia — breaks out (a) by the end of 1915, (b) by 1920, (c) by 1930? Three numbers. And I want the machinery, not just the output, so split it into the things it actually depends on and put a rough probability on each:

- which counterfactual we're even pricing — do I get to hold everything else fixed, or does the one change ripple out until my 1914 isn't 1914 anymore, and does your answer move depending on which version I pick
- structure vs. spark: how much of the outbreak was overdetermined by the alliances and the mobilization schedules, versus that one event
- the second-order question — would some other crisis have come along and done the job inside the window, and how would you even estimate that instead of just asserting it
- whether a near miss makes the men in charge more cautious or more reckless (my hunch is that surviving a scare makes people braver — I don't have alot of evidence, I just think that's true of people I know)
- whether the question even has a determinate answer, or whether history is chaotic enough that "would've happened anyway" is neither true nor false, and if it's neither, what do I do with that
- whatever else you think belongs in there.

Then show me how the pieces combine into the three numbers, tell me which piece the answer is most sensitive to, and what would move it. My own gut is about 0.55 by 1915; my editor thinks that's romantic nonsense and puts it closer to 0.1. So also tell me where her and I are actually disagreeing — is it a factual thing, or is it one of those terms up there?
