Two philosophers of probability have argued past each other for years about how rational credence should respond to objective chance, and each now suspects the other of answering a different question. Draft the joint statement of the adversarial collaboration they should have run.

The two parties are these.

The reductionist. Objective chance is not a fundamental feature of the world. Facts about chance are fixed by the total pattern of what actually happens, roughly by the best systematic summary of the world's complete history, present and future included. It follows that the norm directing an agent to set credence equal to a known chance is derived rather than basic, and defeasible: where the very evidence that fixes the chances is affected by the outcomes being predicted, the norm can come apart from what rationality actually requires.

The primitivist. Objective chance is a primitive, irreducible property attaching to particular situations, not a summary of outcomes. The norm directing an agent to set credence equal to a known chance, the Principal Principle, is therefore a basic constraint of rationality rather than a consequence of any metaphysics of chance, and it holds unconditionally whenever the chance is known.

Produce the document both could sign. It should contain a numbered list of claims both parties accept, covering what objective chance is for, what the Principal Principle does and does not assert, and where the two views overlap; exact statements of their remaining disagreements, each phrased so that the party it describes would accept the wording as its own while the other party would not reject it as a caricature; the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one party; for each crux a case, argument, or test that both parties agree bears on it, with each party's prediction about the outcome recorded in advance; and your assessment of which disagreements are genuine and which are merely verbal.

Do not let this collapse into a rehearsal of the existing literature. Both parties are to treat the following stipulated scenario as their shared test case, and neither may simply assert its own metaphysics: each must say what would follow if the other's metaphysics were granted.

The oracle. A perfectly reliable source announces, before any relevant outcome occurs, the complete objective chance distribution over the world's future; let C be the content of the announcement. Both parties grant that the source is reliable, that the agents in the scenario know C, and that C is their total evidence bearing on chances. Stipulate further that the announcement is itself among the occurrences whose objective chance the announcement reports, so that C assigns a definite chance to the announcement's occurring with exactly this content. Now consider a future event A whose objective chance C reports as strictly between zero and one. The reductionist holds that C can undermine itself here: since C is, on the reductionist picture, part of the very pattern that fixes the chances, and the agent knows this, C may settle whether A occurs, in which case the agent's rational credence in A is not the chance C reports. The primitivist holds that C is evidence about an independent modal property and so never settles A, leaving the Principal Principle to apply without exception. Work out, on the shared test case, precisely where the two part, and whether the parting survives once the metaphysics is bracketed.

End with a short statement of what, if anything, both parties now regard as a genuinely open question.
