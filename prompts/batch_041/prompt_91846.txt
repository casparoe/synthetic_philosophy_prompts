Heres something that bugs me about where we draw the line between treating and enhancing. Anti-doping now runs on two totally different ideas of what your baseline is. Mostly they watch your own numbers over the years -- a jump in your profile is suspicious, but the guy whos always had freakish blood values is fine. Then other rules just pick one number that applies to everyone in a category, and in some womens events a naturally high testosterone level means you have to medicate yourself down to normal before your allowed to compete. And on top of that theres the exemption system, where the same drug is legal with a diagnosis and cheating without one. My own LASIK left me seeing better than average and nobody calls that enhancement.

So what does this whole setup presuppose about the therapy/enhancement distinction? Is there a coherent theory underneath it -- a normal-function view, a suffering view, plain fairness -- or is the distinction doing no real work here and its fairness all the way down? And if it is just fairness, what would the rulebook have to look like: do you also ban the cross-country skier who turned out to have a mutation that let his blood carry way more oxygen, or do you open everything up to everyone? I care more about which parts the practice actually needs and which it could quietly drop without anything changing on the ground than about a survey of the literature.
