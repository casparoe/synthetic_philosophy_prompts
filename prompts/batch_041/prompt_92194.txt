I'm making a handout for a moral psychology seminar and I want a decision tree that gets someone from a bare empirical premise to a named position on whether the psychology of left and right says anything about who's right. Start from something nobody in the room will deny -- that people across the spectrum differ systematically in what strikes them as morally relevant, e.g. the pattern where the right endorses loyalty, authority and purity concerns that the left often doesn't. Every branch should be a question you could answer without already knowing the label it leads to. I want the tree to turn on at least these: whether calling something a 'foundation' is a neutral description of the disagreement or already a partisan redescription of it; whether the foundations are meant to track real moral properties or just dispositions to react; whether the 'the right uses more of them' breadth claim has any normative force or is just a demographic fact; and whether the evolutionary story about where these intuitions come from is a reason to distrust all of them, only the other sides, or none. Leaves I need: descriptive-only MFT with no normative upshot; the Haidt-ish reading where the right's wider palate counts in its favor; symmetric debunking; the asymmetric 'my side is reason, yours is instinct' view; a sentimentalist line where moral truth is just whatever our sentiments settle on; the rationalist comeback that foundations describe intuition while reasoning is a separate faculty that can still criticize it; and the moral-reframing view, which I suspect is descriptive-only with a marketing budget -- say whether its actually distinct. Hang at least one branch on a concrete case, like whether someone who can't give a reason for finding something disgusting is still making a moral judgment. Where two leaves collapse into each other, say so rather than padding the tree. Where a combination of answers comes out empty, say why -- incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Six or seven levels, and dont hedge everything into mush.
