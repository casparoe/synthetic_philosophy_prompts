My two housemates got into a long thing last night about whether "pleasure" names anything real, and I ended up refereeing without really knowing teh literature. Can you sort out who's right at each step?

Ravi started it. He said pleasure obviously isn't one thing: the pleasure of a scalding-hot shower, the pleasure of hearing that the guy who cut you off in traffic got a ticket, the pleasure of finally seeing how a proof works, the small weird pleasure of a door clicking shut exactly right. Different causes, different feelings, different objects. He said any theory that tells you to "maximize pleasure" is treating a grab-bag as if it were a natural kind, and that's why he thinks hedonism is confused from the ground up.

Sam pushed back. She said they must share something, or we couldn't group them under one word at all -- the word isn't random. She said look at what they have in common: they're all things you'd rather have continue, all "likings." And she brought up that you can get an animal to keep doing basically anything by zapping the same little reward circuit, so there's a common mechanism, which sounds like a shared nature.

Ravi said the wanting is exactly the trap: addicts want things they don't enjoy, and people savor memories they'd rather not have. So "liking" is doing all the work and it's a different thing from the feeling itself. And he said a reward circuit lights up for attention and surprise too, so firing together isn't sharing an essence.

Sam said fine, maybe it's a family resemblance or a cluster rather than one essence, and hedonism can just run on the cluster. Or maybe the common thing is formal: a positive "tone" that can attach to any content, the way a color can attach to any shape.

Ravi said if it's just a cluster then "maximize pleasure" is underspecified -- which member of the cluster? The proof-pleasure and the schadenfreude aren't worth the same, and he thinks a hedonist can't say why one counts more without quietly borrowing some other value to do the explaining.

They want me to declare a winner and I honestly can't. Can you go through it move by move -- rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right and say why -- and then tell me what both of them are missing? I'm especially unsure about the last exchange, because I can't tell whether Ravi's objection actually lands or whether he's just changed the subject from "what is pleasure" to "how do we rank pleasures," and Sam never really answered that part.
