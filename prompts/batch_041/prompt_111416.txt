I'm in this poli-phil reading group and we keep circling the equality-of-what question -- whether the currency of distributive justice should be welfare (and if so, which kind), resources and primary goods, or capabilities -- and I keep wondering what John Stuart Mill would have made of the whole taxonomy, since he died well before anyone framed it this way. Can you actually do that prediction properly? Not the lazy he's-a-utilitarian-so-welfare answer, but an honest attempt to triangulate him from what he really held: the higher/lower pleasures distinction and the competent-judges test from Utilitarianism, the individuality and self-development theme in On Liberty, and especially that move in The Subjection of Women where he argues we can't take women's apparent preference for subordinate roles at face value because those preferences were shaped under subordination. That last one feels like it's doing a lot of quiet work for him and nobody in the group could agree on how far he'd push it.

So here's what I'd like you to actually settle. Does he land closer to the welfarists, the resourcists (Rawls-style primary goods, or Dworkin's equality of resources), or is he secretly a proto-capabilities guy in the Sen/Nussbaum vein? Would he bite the bullet on the expensive-tastes objection -- that equalizing welfare lets someone who deliberately cultivates costly tastes hold everyone else hostage -- or does his own moral psychology give him an exit? And would he be willing to sign onto something like a fixed list of objectively valuable capabilities and functionings, or does that rub against his empiricism about the good and against his harm principle? I also want to know where he'd say each of the three camps gets him wrong, and whether any part of the dispute would strike him as merely verbal.

Format it as a short first-person piece written as Mill, and then a separate section where you step back and tell me how confident we should be in each prediction, flagging the spots where you're basically guessing. Take the psychology seriously -- I don't want him just slotted into whatever camp is convenient. And please don't hedge everything into mush; if you think he'd definately come down one way on, say, the objective-list question, just say so and defend it.
