My main source is a chapter from a popular science book, which I found convincing when I first read it, but my club's moderator remarked that professional philosophers of physics are far more cautious about this argument than the author is, and I would like to understand exactly where that caution is warranted. "Einstein's theory of relativity showed that time is not the universal clock we once took it for, but a fourth dimension, no different in kind from the three dimensions of space. The decisive discovery was that simultaneity is relative: if two events occur at the same time according to one observer, there is always another observer, moving at a different velocity, according to whom one of them occurred before the other. It follows that there is no fact of the matter about which events are happening now; 'now' is not a feature of the world but a feature of the observer. And if an event is real for some observer, then it is real, full stop, for reality is not the sort of thing that can be relative to a point of view. Putting these two steps together gives the picture physicists call the block universe: past, present, and future all exist, laid out in four dimensions, and the passage of time is an illusion of consciousness. Two consequences follow. The future is already there, and therefore already fixed: the block universe is a deterministic universe, and our sense of freedom is an illusion along with the flow of time. And since all events are equally real, nothing is special about the present moment; its apparent privilege is a fact about us rather than about the world."
