I run a small philosophy reading group that's been chewing on sphexishness — the digger wasp thing, where she drags the cricket to the burrow, inspects the burrow, and if you nudge the cricket an inch while she's inside she comes out, drags it back, and re-inspects, over and over, apparently never noticing the loop. Hofstadter coined the term I'm pretty sure and Dennett ran with it; don't quote me on the provenance, I'm not asking for cites or a reading list, just the following.

I want a sorting instrument — decision tree or quiz, your call — that takes someone from "yeah, I've seen that wasp video, and honestly I've seen people do the same thing" to one of the named positions on whether sphexishness is actually a problem for free will, for agency, or just for our dignity. Every branch node should be a concrete case or claim someone can answer without already knowing which label it leads to. No nodes like "are you a compatibilist?" Obviously.

The bit of scaffolding I want you to build it around is something I've been calling loop depth: roughly, how many nested levels of notice-that-you're-stuck-in-a-routine-and-step-out-of-it a system can run before it bottoms out in something it can't examine. Wasp is depth 0, she can't notice at all. A person who can see the habit but genuinely can't stop is depth 1. Someone who can only quit because a friend pointed it out is maybe 1.5. Someone who can notice their own noticing is higher. A system that can rewrite its own loop-exiting policy is the case I actually care about, and I don't know if that's the top of the ladder or a different ladder entirely — build that question into the tree if you can. Also throw in a node about the ethologists who think the wasp isn't nearly as mindless as the story makes out (something about the repeated inspection being adaptive rather than a glitch), because if they're right the whole metaphor might be load-bearing on a bad example.

Leaves I want to see: the hard determinist / eliminativist one where loop depth just is the story and free will goes with it; the compatibilist who says freedom is about responsiveness to reasons and has nothing to do with whether the machinery happens to be sphexish; the degrees-of-freedom view where humans just have deeper loops and that's the whole difference; the "sphexishness is in the eye of the describer" view, where any finite system looks rigid under some description; the self-undermining view, where the accusation of sphexishness loops back on the accuser (the person smugly diagnosing the wasp is running their own routine); and the meta-flexibility view, where free will specifically requires being able to break your own loops and this is rare and real. If there's a seventh you think I'm missing, add it, but say why the six don't cover it.

Two extra asks. Mark any pairs of leaves your tree can't actually separate — I'd rather you flag it than paper over it. And for the combinations of answers that no position occupies, tell me whether that's because the combo is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Keep it to about ten branch nodes since it's a handout, and keep it recieve-able by someone who's read zero philosophy of mind. Casual tone is fine, these are friends.
