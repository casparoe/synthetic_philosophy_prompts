Write a sestina on F. H. Bradley's regress, for an anthology of poems that are meant to state a philosophical problem rather than decorate it.

The regress, as the poem should take it: anything complex — a particular and another particular standing in some relation — needs something to unify its constituents into a single thing rather than leave them a mere collection. But the unifying relation is itself one more constituent, so it stands to the others just as they stand to each other, and a further relation is needed to unify it with them; that further relation is in turn a constituent, and so on without end. Keep in view the contrast that gives the regress its sting: the particulars and the relation taken together are a list of items, whereas the proposition that they stand so related is one thing that says something. Nothing added to the list converts it into the proposition, since whatever is added is one more entry on the list.

Constraints:

- Use six end-words, every one of which names something in the regress's economy. At least three should be candidate unifiers — a particular, a relation, the whole, the copula, the act of unifying — and at least one should be a word that can only serve as a connector or as the regress's own next step, the word that arrives precisely when the poem has run out of new items to add.
- The stanza-by-stanza permutation must be legible as the regress itself: each rearrangement should read as though a further relation had just been introduced to hold the previous arrangement together. The envoi must gather all six end-words, and it must read as one more move of the same kind rather than as the resolution the form promises.
- Give one stanza to the escape that calls the regress harmless: the unifying is not a further item but is identical with the obtaining of the relation, so there is nothing left over that still needs unifying. The following stanza should show the regress returning for any version of that view which treats unity as an additional constituent.
- The poem must not merely name Bradley, propositions, or unity. A reader should be able to reconstruct the regress from the poem's movement alone: someone who has worked on the unity of the proposition should find nothing to wince at, and someone who has not should still finish the poem with the sense of a debt that can never be paid.

No fixed meter is required; keep the language as tight as the end-words allow.
