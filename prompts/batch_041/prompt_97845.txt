I'm teaching a philosophy of mind unit on knowing how vs. knowing that and I want to build it around The Matrix. You know the scene I mean: Neo gets plugged in, the machine downloads kung fu into him in a few seconds, and he sits up and announces that he knows kung fu now. That's basically a lab experiment for the whole Rylean picture. Ryle's point was that knowing how can't just be a pile of facts, because if you had to consult rules for every move you'd never actually move, and the download seems either to refute him or confirm him depending on what the download is supposed to write into his head. The intellectualist line (Stanley and Williamson and everyone since) says knowing how just is knowing a fact under a practical mode of presentation, in which case you could in principle pipe it in like a file. So I want an actual reading of the film against that debate, not just "the movie shows skill gets downloaded, neat."

Heres the wrinkle I want you to take seriously, and I havent really seen anyone do it: split knowing how into three things. First, the propositional recipe, the facts about what to do and when. Second, the bare ability or disposition to actually do it. Third, the felt sense of doing it right, the thing that lets a musician hear a note is off before she can tell you why, or a fighter feel a punch coming. The download might hand Neo the first and maybe even the second without the third. Does the film know the difference? Watch how it films his skill, the bullet time, the way he reads the code, the way competence shows up as perception rather than as sweaty bodily practice, and tell me which of the three the movie is actually representing.

And then push on the places where it seems to cheat or complicate things. Morpheus trains him, spars with him, tells him he can only point at the door and Neo has to walk through it himself, which is the anti-download, anti-intellectualist moment in the film. Does that scene commit the movie to something Ryle would like, or does the rest of the movie undercut it by making mastery a matter of seeing the Matrix for what it is, i.e. secretly a kind of knowing that? I want to know where the film illuminates the debate and where it just illustrates the standard positions, and whether there's anything in it that the Ryle/Stanley back and forth misses, something about skill as perception that the papers dont capture.

One more thing, and this matters: flag anywhere the reading distorts either the film or the philosophy. I'll be showing clips to students who haven't read Ryle or Stanley, so I need the honest critic's caveats, not a tidy allegory.
