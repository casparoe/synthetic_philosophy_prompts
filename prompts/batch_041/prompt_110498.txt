I'm finishing up a seminar on the Vienna Circle this term and I've come out of it a bit of a Carnap guy, which my friends think is hilarious because it mostly means I've become insufferable about metaphysics. Anyway I've been trying to write down my actual position for a reading group and I've ended up with a list of things I'm pretty sure I believe, and now I'm worried they dont all hang together. I don't want you to tell me what Carnap really thought, I'll sort that out myself — what I want is an audit of my pile. Here it is. (1) There's no fact of the matter about which language or framework is the right one; adopting one is a practical decision and you judge it by whether it does the job you want done, not by whether it matches something out there. (2) Ethical and political judgments aren't true or false. They express an attitude or issue something like a directive, and what looks like moral argument is really argument about means, about consequences, and about whether my attitudes are consistent with each other. (3) The scientific attitude is the model of rational inquiry, and the whole point of it is that you can bring it to social questions too — how production gets organized, how institutions get designed, all of it. (4) Socialism is the better social order and central planning is how a modern economy should be run. The old calculation objection doesn't scare me — the one where a planner can't get the information a market transmits — because that's a problem about information and computation, and better statistics and better machinery can handle it. (5) The fight against metaphysics isn't just housekeeping inside philosophy. It's continuous with the political fight, because the kind of obscurantism that dresses up authority as profundity is what authoritarians deal in.

I keep having the feeling that something in there is wrong, but when I poke at it I can't tell whether I'm looking at a real conflict or just two things that sound like they should conflict. So: which of these pull against each other, how bad is each one, and which ones only look like they clash? For the real ones I want them ranked by how much damage they do, with the cheapest repair for each and an honest account of what the repair costs me. And then tell me whether there's a single move that would clean up more than any of the individual fixes would — I'd rather find that out now than in front of the group. Write it in American English and don't pull your punches.
