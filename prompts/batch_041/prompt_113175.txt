I have been trying to work out my own account of why promises bind, and before I commit it to paper I would like an audit of whether its parts can be held together. Here is the package.

1. What creates the obligation is uptake. An assurance becomes a promise only when the person addressed takes it up; neither the promisor's private intention nor the existence of a social convention generates the obligation. If I offer an assurance and you never register it, or you hear it and decline it, nothing is owed.

2. What the promise adds is not a claim to performance but a claim upon the promisor's will: a standing to determine whether the promised act is done. Call this the authorship claim — within that narrow domain, the promisee rather than the promisor is the one who decides.

3. Reliance is not required. A promise binds even if the promisee has not relied on it, and even if performance would leave them worse off; the wrong of breach is not the disappointment of an expectation.

4. The obligation is owed to the promisee, not to the practice or to the community. Only the promisee can release it, third parties have no standing to demand performance, and breach wrongs the promisee specifically.

5. The promise binds even where the promisee cannot exercise the authorship claim — a promise made to someone who is unconscious and will never wake, or who has lost the capacity to understand what a promise is, or who dies before performance is possible.

6. Promising to do what I am already independently obligated to do adds nothing at all: not a new obligation, not a new claim, not a new standing.

I suspect that at least one pair here is only apparently in tension and that at least one is a real conflict, and I genuinely cannot tell which is which. What I want is an audit rather than a survey of the literature, so please do not bother with references. For each pair that genuinely pulls apart, say how severe the conflict is and what would have to give; for each pair that only looks inconsistent, name the distinction that saves it. Then rank the real tensions by severity, give me the cheapest repair for the worst of them along with what that repair costs me elsewhere in the package, and say whether any single revision would clear up more than one of them at once. If the package turns out to be consistent as it stands, say so and say what is holding it together. I would rather have two or three pairs that matter than an exhaustive list in which everything is flagged. Finally, tell me what the repaired package implies about a promise made to someone who is asleep when it is made and who never learns that it was made.
