I'm closing out a seminar on Sor Juana with a poem instead of another discussion post, and I want something that actually gets the argumentative bind she's in, not just the "nun who loved books" vibe. Can you write a glosa — that baroque Spanish form where a short refrain gets glossed stanza by stanza, each stanza ending on a line of the refrain — where the refrain is the thing Sor Filotea tells her, basically the Pauline bit about women keeping silent, and Sor Juana does the glossing. Each stanza should end on part of the refrain but also do one of her actual moves: that her hunger to know is something God put in her and fighting it is the real impiety; that she studies privately and never preaches, so the rule about women teaching from the pulpit doesn't touch her; that ignorance is more dangerous than learning because it leads souls into error; that women need learning so they can teach their own daughters; that if Aristotle had cooked he'd have written more philosophy; that she can't help it any more than she can help breathing. The last stanza should turn the refrain into something the bishop couldn't have meant — like silence as the precondition for the private study she's defending, or the silence of a woman who's been told to stop and keeps thinking anyway. I want someone who's read the Reply to recognize the moves, and someone who hasn't to still get the bind: she's writing a public letter to defend a private life, and she's performing obedience the whole time she's doing it. English, please. Five stanzas or however glosas go, I trust you on the form. Don't make it a hagiography — she was funny and sharp, let that through.
