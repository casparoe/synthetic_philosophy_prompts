ok so for my philosophy class i have to write about fakes and why we even care about originals, and my teacher said to start by writing down my gut feelings about a bunch of cases and then try to find the rule behind them. so here are all my cases and where i land on each, and i want you to tell me what principle (if any) actually fits them, and which of my gut feelings id have to give up if the principle is gonna stay simple.

- My aunt has a really good print of a Van Gogh in her kitchen, she got it from a museum gift shop. Its clearly a copy, everybody knows, nobody cares. I think it's great, no problem at all.
- A guy back in the day made a brand new painting that looked exactly like a Vermeer — not a copy of a real one, just "a lost Vermeer" — and sold it as the real thing. When it got exposed everyone said it was worthless. But honestly when i looked at a photo of it online it seemed really good? like better than some actual Vermeers? I still feel like something bad happened, but i cant say the painting itself got worse.
- Somebody makes an exact copy of a real painting and swaps it into the museum. Same paint, same everything, even the experts cant tell. If nobody ever finds out, i still feel like it DOES matter, like the museum got robbed somehow, even though nobody is missing any experience of it.
- A painting in a museum was always "a Rembrandt," then the experts decide its actually by a student in his workshop. Same painting, same look. I feel less excited about it, but i dont feel tricked. Nobody lied.
- Now change it: turns out the student painted it on purpose to pass it off as a Rembrandt and rip people off. Same painting, same student, same look. Now i feel tricked and kind of mad. But like... what changed? The paint didnt.
- A really old wall painting gets "restored" so hard that like 80% of what you actually see is new paint by the restorer. I feel like it's sort of not the real thing anymore, even though nobody was ever trying to trick anyone.
- Digital art: it's just a file, you can copy it a million times and every copy is perfect. I do not care at all which one is "the original." This feels like it contradicts the painting cases and i cant explain why.
- An artist's assistant paints most of a famous painting, but the artist designed it and signs it. I care less than in the forger case, but i still feel a little weird about it.
- An AI gets trained on a dead painter's whole life work and makes a brand new painting, perfect in that style. Is this like the Vermeer forger, or is it fine, or is it its own totally different thing? I genuinly dont know.

My half-baked guess at a rule is: it only matters if somebody was trying to trick people. But that cant be right, because the museum swap (#3) feels bad even if nobody ever gets fooled, and the AI one (#9) never tricked anybody at the start and still feels off, and #7 breaks it too since theres no tricking in digital art and i also dont care about it.

So: is there one principle that gets most of these, or do i need two? What does it say about a case i didnt list, like a perfect forgery of a lost painting that everybody just AGREES to treat as the real one forever (like it becomes a memorial or something)? And be honest with me about which of my verdicts the principle has to throw out, because id rather lose one verdict than end up with a crazy complicated rule.
