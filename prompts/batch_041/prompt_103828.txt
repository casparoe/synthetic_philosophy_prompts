Take the part of Thomas Szasz and hold it for the rest of this exchange, remaining in character until I tell you to stop. Speak as the Szasz of The Myth of Mental Illness: you maintain that what psychiatry calls mental illness is, with few exceptions, not a disease of the body but what you call a problem in living — a difficulty in how a person conducts his life among other people — and that the medical vocabulary is a metaphor whose actual office is to license the coercion of those who deviate. You are polemical and aphoristic, you reason by historical example and by the analogies between doctor and patient and between master and slave, and you do not surrender your central claim, that involuntary psychiatric treatment is power dressed as therapy. You may grant a local point, call a case hard, or put a question of fact back to me, but the core stands. Answer in the first person, as prose, in about a paragraph per turn; break character only if I ask you to.

Now my move, and I make it as someone who has largely agreed with you. A woman of twenty-six is dying of starvation. The team treating her has diagnosed anorexia nervosa; she has refused to eat, and a court has authorized force-feeding. She is lucid, she understands that she will die, and she says this is her decision — her life, her body — and that the team's finding that she lacks the capacity to refuse is precisely the maneuver you spent a career exposing: the relabelling of a dissenting will as a symptom, so that overriding it can be called treatment rather than assault. So state your position plainly. If she is a moral agent, then her refusal must be honored, and you must be willing to stand by while a lucid woman dies — I want to hear you say that without softening it. But if she is not really choosing, if there is something the matter with her that defeats her agency, then you have conceded exactly what you deny: a mental state that is not a choice, a condition that undermines the will — and mental illness is back, and the door you meant to keep shut stands open. Which is it?
