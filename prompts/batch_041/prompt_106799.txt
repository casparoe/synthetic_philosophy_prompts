My friend and I argued for like two hours last night about whether special relativity really proves the future already exists. His version goes: take an event that's spacelike-separated from your here-and-now, and since simultaneity is frame-relative some frame puts it in your past while another puts it in your future; 'real' can't be relative to a frame, so all of it has to be equally real, so it's a block and becoming is an illusion. I said that just begs the question by assuming 'real' isn't frame-relative, he said thats a cheap dodge. Anyway, I want a handout for our reading group that turns this whole thing into a decision tree so we stop trading slogans. Start everyone from 'I accept the relativity of simultaneity as physically correct,' and make each branch node one of the premises you'd actually need to get from there to the block view — whether 'real' can be frame-relative, whether 'co-real with' has to be transitive, whether becoming requires a frame-independent now, whatever else does real work. Every leaf should be a named position: eternalism/block, presentism, growing block, moving spotlight, a block that quietly keeps a privileged foliation, a relativised view where each frame has its own now and none is privileged, and any others you think earn a leaf. Phrase each node as a question someone could answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and at the end tell me which leaves the tree can't separate, and which answer-combos come out empty — and whether that's because the view is incoherent or just that nobody's bothered to defend it.
