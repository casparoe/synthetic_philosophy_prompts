Write a satirical help-center FAQ for a fictional company that sells precommitment devices — self-binding gadgets and contracts for individuals, with a premium tier for legislatures that want to entrench constitutional clauses. Aim for around 600 words, formatted as a numbered list of questions and answers, in the deadpan voice of a support writer who is serenely certain that nothing is amiss. The humor has to come from the actual mechanics of precommitment rather than from invented silliness, so that a reader who knows the literature recognizes that the piece understands the thing it is mocking; stay straight-faced throughout and never wink at the reader.

The FAQ should, without ever treating any of them as defects, take positions on the following: who binds the binder (the device has to be secured by something the user cannot disable — say what, and then what secures that); the awkward detail that the one person in the familiar story was tied to the mast by a crew who were not themselves bound; which temporal self counts as the real customer, and therefore whose preferences the device is enforcing against whom; renewal and the escape hatch, i.e., what happens when the bound self has a change of heart the binding self did not foresee, and whether the company will honor it; and the line the company draws between a genuine precommitment and a mere resolution, with the external enforcement it insists on. The result should be legible to someone with no background and genuinely funny to someone who has read about precommitment and about the constitutional objection that the dead should not govern the living. Do not name real theorists or works, and do not include citations. No more than 650 words.
