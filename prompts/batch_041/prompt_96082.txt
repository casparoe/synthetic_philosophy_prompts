So my friend Alex and I got into a huge, slightly drunken arguement last night about Borges and time, and I'm still not over it. He was insisting that Borges has basically one view: that time is a labyrinth, all moments coexist, and in 'A New Refutation of Time' he argues that time is a delusion of succession — no persisting self (Hume), no material world (Berkeley), therefore no time. But I definately think that's only half the story. The earlier Borges of 'The Circular Time' and 'The Doctrine of Cycles' is all about eternal recurrence — time as a circle, history repeating because the number of possible permutations is finite, Nietzsche's demon whispering in your ear. That's not the same as saying time is unreal. If time is a circle, it's still time; if time is a delusion, you can't even say it's circular, because circularity is a temporal shape. And I think Borges knew this, because the refutation essay has this weird self-undermining quality, like he's playing a game he can't quite live by — he refutes time and then goes on with his day. So the two positions don't sit together, and I want to write a poem about that tension for my reading group (we've been doing a unit on time and narrative, which is probably why this is eating me).

I need you to be my expert on Borges and on the philosophy of time. Before you write, think carefully about the two positions and maybe brainstorm a few images or structural moves. The poem should be a pantoum — I know that's a weird choice, but hear me out: the pantoum's interlocking repetitions (where the second and fourth lines of each stanza become the first and third of the next) are perfect for the circular view, because lines keep coming back in new contexts, which is exactly Borges's eternal return. But then the final stanza should do the refutation of time: the poem should close by returning to its opening line, so that the circle collapses into a single point, and you can't tell if it's repeating or standing still. Also, I want the repeated lines to shift meaning across the stanzas, so that by the end a line that sounded like eternal recurrence sounds like the refutation of time, even though the words are identical. The form should make the argument, not just decorate it. It should be maybe 4 or 5 stanzas, and it should be accurate enough that someone who knows Borges won't wince. Don't make it a straightforward 'Borges was a genius' poem; I want the tension to stay unresolved. No need for citations or anything. Write the poem in English, no title needed, and make it feel like it could be spoken by Borges arguing with himself.
