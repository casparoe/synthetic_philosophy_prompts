I supervise a doctoral student in the philosophy of mathematics who is deciding whether to write his dissertation on the continuum hypothesis, and he has put an argument to me that I want to answer properly rather than brush aside. Because the hypothesis is independent of ZFC, he concludes that there is nothing left to discover about it: it has no answer, and set theorists who continue to work on it are choosing a convention rather than investigating a fact. He adds that the large-cardinal axioms, which settle so many other questions the basic axioms leave open, leave this one open as well, and he takes that to show that no acceptable extension of the axioms could settle it either. Please describe the most important implications of the independence results for someone in his position — what they do and do not establish about whether the hypothesis has a determinate truth value, what they imply about its connections to the rest of mathematics, and what they suggest about the kind of work on it that could still be worth a dissertation.
