I'm a retired field biologist—spent about thirty years counting birds and arguing about population models—and since I stopped working I've been trying to teach myself some philosophy, mostly logic and philosophy of math, with a couple of friends in a very disorganized reading group. We've gotten stuck on Skolem's paradox, and I've ended up defending a view I'd like you to push on. The view is roughly this: the paradox is not a real contradiction but a confusion about the model-relative nature of cardinality. A first-order theory like ZFC can have a countable model, and that model can still satisfy a sentence saying there are uncountable sets, because the bijection that would make the set countable from the outside is not an element of the model. Countability is not an absolute property of a set; it's relative to a model or a language. The model's version of the power set of omega is countable from our metatheoretical vantage point but uncountable from the model's own vantage point, and that's the whole story. I find this deflationary line pretty convincing, but I'm aware it might be too neat.

What I want from you is a map of what would count against it. Suppose the deflationary view is wrong, or at least incomplete. What observations, formal results, or philosophical arguments should move me? I'm interested in things like: whether the object-language/metatheory distinction can really do all the work, or whether the relativity of cardinality threatens to infect the metatheory too; whether second-order logic or some other strengthening actually blocks the paradox or just relocates it; whether there's a defensible notion of absolute countability that the view has to deny; and whether the view collapses into a kind of quietism that says nothing about the intended model. Sort the defeaters by how decisive they'd be—fatal, serious, merely awkward—and tell me which ones a committed deflationist would themselves concede as counting against them, as opposed to ones they'd just dismiss. I'd also like an honest read on whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that's a bug or just what a view like this has to be. I don't need citations or reading lists; I can't look them up anyway. I just want the arguments and where they bite.
