I am a doctoral candidate in political theory, and next week I am leading a ninety-minute session for a mixed reading group of graduate students in philosophy, law, and public policy. The topic is the problem of dirty hands in politics, but I want the session to turn on a distinction I have been trying to formulate and have not seen stated in exactly this way: between what I call the residue reading and the license reading of dirty hands. On the residue reading, the politician who does wrong for the public good remains genuinely guilty; the moral cost is not cancelled but registered as a remainder that should shape future conduct and character. On the license reading, the political role itself generates a permission to do what would otherwise be forbidden, so that the act is not wrong in the relevant sense, though it may still be regrettable or politically costly. I want the group to understand why this distinction matters and why one might be forced to choose between the two rather than treating them as complementary.

Plan the session for me. Do not give me the explanation itself. Give me the order in which the components must be introduced so that the distinction feels forced rather than arbitrary, the one concrete case or scenario that should carry the whole session and what it must include and leave out, the misconceptions the group will predictably bring -- for instance, that dirty hands is just consequentialism, that moral residue is merely a feeling, that a role-based license is the same as a legal immunity -- and exactly where in the sequence each should be headed off. I would prefer not to use the standard ticking-bomb torture case; if you think it is nonetheless the right carrying example, defend that against my preference. Tell me what to leave out at this level and say so explicitly, and give me one question I can put to the group at the end that would show whether they have grasped the distinction rather than merely learned the vocabulary. I would like you to make definite choices and defend them: if you think the residue reading is the more defensible one, say so and explain why the session should be sequenced to make that conclusion available without rigging it; if you think the distinction collapses, say that and tell me how the session should be reorganized around its collapse.
