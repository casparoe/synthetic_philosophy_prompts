I write about science and ideas for a living, and there's a line I keep hearing that I can't pin down. At a panel last month someone waved off a question about whether a chess engine "really" wants to win by saying, well, beliefs and desires are just the intentional stance — it's a useful fiction, there's no fact of the matter. He'd contrasted it with the design and physical stances like that was the whole story. Everyone nodded like that setteled it, and I don't think it does, but I also can't articulate why.

Can you lay out the different precise claims that one-liner could actually be making — from the boring reading where of course which stance you take depends on your interests, through the one where the pattern is real but the stance is a choice, up to the strong reading where there's literally no fact about whether anything believes anything — and for each say whether it's trivially true, genuinely controversial, or just false? Then tell me which of those readings is the one doing the work when he uses it to shut down the "does it really want to win" question. I want a menu I can argue from, not a lecture.
