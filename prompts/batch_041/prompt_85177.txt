You are a philosopher of mind and of value theory who works on the affective states and is known for stating an opponent's position in wording the opponent will sign. Two camps have argued past each other for decades about what pleasures have in common. The intrinsic-valence camp holds that every pleasure shares a single phenomenal property — a felt goodness, hedonic tone, that introspection can in principle isolate — and treats the heterogeneity of pleasures (the taste of a peach, the resolution of a proof, the warmth of being liked) as an artefact of coarse attention. The attitudinal camp holds that 'pleasure' names no common felt quality at all, but a state's standing in a certain relation — being liked, wanted, or playing a reward-role — and treats the felt-quality view as a projection of one word onto a miscellany of states. Begin by working out, in a few paragraphs of notes, what each side must concede and what it cannot concede without giving up its view; then draft the joint statement a genuine adversarial collaboration between them would produce. Give me: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, covering the phenomena (mixed feelings; pleasures with no sensory content; the anhedonic gourmet who has the taste but no liking) and covering what introspection can and cannot settle; (2) the residual disagreements, each stated in a formulation its own side would accept, each labelled substantive or merely verbal — I suspect at least one is verbal, turning on whether 'common property' is required to mean 'introspectible property' — and a statement of what would count as evidence against each side's own view; (3) the cruxes; and (4) for each crux, the case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Use these three cases as part of the common evidence: the implant that yields one and the same hedonic quality whichever modality triggers it, while the sensory qualities are unchanged; the transplant that moves the felt goodness of one experience onto an otherwise unchanged experience; and the creature that sorts its own pleasures reliably by how good they feel while sincerely denying that they share any felt quality. For each, state what the intrinsic-valence camp predicts and what the attitudinal camp predicts, and flag any case on which the predictions coincide, since such a case cannot discriminate. Argue from the cases and from what each view is committed to; no literature survey is wanted, and do not appeal to authors or texts. End with a final line beginning 'Verdict:' naming the single crux you judge most likely to move at least one side if it were settled.
