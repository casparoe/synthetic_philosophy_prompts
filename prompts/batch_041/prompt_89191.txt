I am a third-year undergraduate currently enrolled in a course on free will and moral responsibility. I am applying to my department's summer research fellowship, which funds ten weeks of full-time work on a single paper of about 6,000 words, and I am hoping to submit the same paper as my final assignment for the course, so I would like the proposal to serve both purposes. I will supply all citations myself, so please do not include a bibliography or a survey of the literature by citation; you may refer to positions and arguments without naming sources.

The project concerns Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment" (1962). The standard reconstruction of his argument goes roughly as follows: the truth of determinism cannot undermine our practices of holding one another responsible, because those practices rest on our reactive attitudes, and the reactive attitudes are not hostage to any thesis about the causal antecedents of action. What I keep noticing, though, is that the essay separates three families of attitudes — the ones we direct at others for what they do to us, such as resentment and gratitude; the impartial or vicarious ones we have on others' behalf, such as indignation and moral approval; and the self-directed ones, such as guilt and remorse — and that the standard reconstruction runs all three together. My half-formed hypothesis is that the anti-metaphysical argument does not work in the same way for each family, and may not work for all of them. Resentment looks defensible without any general claim about what agents are owed, since it answers to how someone has actually treated me. Indignation on a stranger's behalf looks as though it needs a background fact about what people are owed, which is exactly what a determinist might deny. Guilt looks as though it needs something further still, namely a relation between an agent and her own will that a purely interpersonal reading cannot supply.

Please turn this into the prospectus. I need the question stated precisely enough that it has a determinate answer rather than a topic; an account of what in the standard reading leaves the question open; a method, spelled out week by week, for conceptual work with no empirical component — in particular, how I would decide whether the three families really come apart rather than just sounding different; a description of what a result would look like and of what would count as the project failing; and an honest assessment of whether ten weeks is enough for a paper of this size. Please also explain why this approach is better than the obvious alternative of simply defending or attacking Strawson's compatibilism as a whole, and be candid about which parts of my hypothesis are likely to survive scrutiny and which I should expect to give up.
