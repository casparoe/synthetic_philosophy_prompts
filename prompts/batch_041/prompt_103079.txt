My company is actually voting on whether to move to a 4-day week, and I keep ending up in arguments about it. Someone on my team keeps saying stuff about idle hands and the devil, which I guess is the whole Protestant work ethic thing. Someone else brought up Japan and people literally dying from overwork, and someone mentioned France and their 35-hour week. Now I cant stop thinking about who actually has a right to leisure, if anybody.

Here's what I want from you. Below are six arguments someone could make for why people should get time off / leisure. Rank them from strongest to weakest as grounds for an actual right (not just a nice-to-have), give me a sentence for each on why it lands where it does, and a sentence on the best objection to it.

1. Rest makes people more productive, so even a boss who only cares about the bottom line should want it. (instrumental / enlightened self-interest angle)
2. People shouldn't be treated as mere tools for someone else's ends, so their time isnt just someone else's to command. (dignity angle)
3. The gains from automation and AI should partly be paid out as free time instead of all as more stuff, otherwise the people doing the work never see any of the benefit. (dividend angle)
4. Overwork is literally killing people, so not having leisure is a health and safety issue, same category as not being poisoned at work. (harm-prevention angle)
5. The work ethic is a cultural invention we could un-invent; leisure is good in itself and work is only ever a means to it. (anti-work-ethic angle)
6. A right only means something if somebody has a duty to provide it, and if no one can be on the hook for your free time then calling it a right is just wishful thinking, especially when my 4-day week might just be some contractor somewhere else's 6-day week. (this one is me being a skeptic, not really an argument for leisure)

Things I want you to factor in for each one:
- Does it actually get you a right, with a matching duty on some specific someone, or only a nice-to-have?
- How does it handle the overwork-death case vs the person who genuinely loves their job and does 80 hours by choice?
- Does it collapse into one of the others if you push on it?

Then, last thing: if I could only bring two of these to the argument with my boss, which two, and tell me honestly whether my whole framing of a right to leisure is confused.
