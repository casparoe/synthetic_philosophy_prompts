I'm TAing the intro epistemology unit this term, and I keep having the same argument at the pub with my co-TA, who thinks the whole Gettier industry is a cottage industry built on people's intuitions about weird little stories. To make the argument concrete I wrote up a batch of cases for section next week, and I want a second opinion on how they should be sorted before I hand them out, because I'm worried I've got two or three of them wrong. Here they are in the order I wrote them. (1) Borrowed Umbrella: Tariq sees his roommate come in with a dripping umbrella and concludes it's raining; the roommate actually just dunked the umbrella in the pool as a joke, but it is in fact raining. (2) Staged Ferns: Dan waters plants for a living, and the building he's in has had fake ferns staged in every office but one for a photo shoot; he checks that one office, sees a real fern, and concludes the floor has real plants — he checked carefully and used the same method he always uses. (3) Two Colleagues: Rosa believes the dean has resigned because two colleagues told her so separately; both had it from the same unreliable group chat, and the dean has resigned. (4) Flight Feeling: Marco believes his flight is delayed because he has a feeling about it; he's an extremely experienced flyer whose feelings about flights pan out about 95% of the time, but he can't articulate what any of it's based on. (5) Optimistic Sister: Mia believes her brother passed his exam because she badly wants it to be true; he passed. (6) Two Trains: Yara believes she'll make the 8:15 because she checked the timetable and the traffic report, both accurate; she wasn't going to check again, so even if the train had been cancelled she'd have gone on believing she'd make it. (7) Careful Chemist: Zoe, working slowly, with clean glassware and a protocol she re-read twice, correctly reports the concentration of a solution; nothing goes wrong and nothing nearly goes wrong.

What I want: for each case, a score from 0 to 10 for how much damage it does to the plain justified-true-belief analysis of knowledge (0 = a boring case of knowledge, 10 = it kills JTB), one line saying what's doing the work in that score, and then an actual ordering of the seven from least to most damaging, since I don't trust my own gut on 2 versus 6. Also flag any of them that's just a re-skin of a famous case, because I'd rather not get caught out by a sharp student. Then rank these five attempted repairs by how many of the seven cases they handle without collateral damage: the no-false-lemmas fix; sensitivity (you wouldn't have believed it if it were false); safety (you couldn't easily have been wrong); the virtue/apt-belief line (the belief is correct because of the knower's competence); and a fix my friend Priya swears by, which she calls the same-fact condition — knowledge requires that the fact that justifies you and the fact that makes your belief true be the same fact, not two different facts that merely line up. For each repair say which single case is its worst enemy.

And if I have to cut one case from the handout because it's doing no work, which one goes, and if I want to open with the one case most likely to make my co-TA stop smirking, which do I lead with?
