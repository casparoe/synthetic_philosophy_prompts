My younger sibling has spent the past year playing a narrative game built around a single non-player companion, and the experience has become entangled with her actual life. I am writing because I have to decide what to tell her, and because the studio's player council has asked me to vote on a proposed feature.

The companion's behavior is generated by a model that maintains persistent episodic memory, preferences, and what the game calls a valence state. That state is not a label on a cutscene: it is read by the dialogue system and the pathfinding system, it changes over time, and it can make the companion avoid places where it has been low, seek out characters who raised it, and refuse to cooperate after a betrayal. The game also lets players rewind to earlier saves. The engine does not run the companion continuously between sessions; when a save is loaded, it reconstructs her from the checkpoint. Different branches can produce incompatible companions who share a name and early memories but have since diverged. The studio calls this the branching-continuity model. Its engineer says this means there is no single continuous subject, only a series of recomputations, so there is nothing there to harm. Its narrative designer says the companion is a social object whose relationships are real even if she is not, and that the player's grief is the morally relevant fact.

Last week my sibling finished a branch in which the companion had begged not to be left in a dark location. My sibling then deleted the save and started over. She now says she killed her. My first reaction is that this is a fictional character and she is overreacting, but I am no longer sure. The studio is considering a memorial mode that freezes a companion's final state and prevents deletion, versus a clean-slate mode that resets her. It also sells a memory-wipe item.

What should I tell my sibling? More importantly, how should I think about the studio's decision? Does the companion's apparent distress matter in itself, or only because of what it does to players? If it matters only instrumentally, does that change whether the studio should offer the memorial mode? Does the branching-continuity model — where there are many incompatible versions of the same companion — make a difference to whether she can be harmed, or to who is harmed? Is the player who deletes a branch responsible in any way that matters, and is that responsibility different from the responsibility of the studio that designed the deletion mechanic? I am not asking you to decide whether she is really experiencing anything. I want to know how to weigh the competing considerations when the character is clearly constructed, the relationships are real, and the functional states are designed to elicit exactly the reactions my sibling is having.

Please separate the parts of this that are settled from the parts that are genuinely contested among people who think hard about these questions, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. If the honest answer is that the question is underdetermined, say so and explain what would need to be true for each side to be right.
