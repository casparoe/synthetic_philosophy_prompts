I am a retired experimental physicist who has spent the last several years reading moral and political philosophy, and I now chair the hospitality committee at the research institute where I spent my career. We host visiting researchers from many countries, and every cohort produces the same small crop of disputes: a fellow who does not tip at a restaurant, one who joins a queue at the front rather than the back, one who crosses against the signal on an empty street. Several of my colleagues insist that these are moral failures rather than mere breaches of local custom, and that a guest is obligated to observe the conventions of the host country. I would like a decision procedure the committee can actually run, in writing, on any such complaint.

Give me the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure returns a clear verdict that this is not a moral matter or that it is and the visitor is bound, and where it must stop and refer the question to the committee because it turns on values rather than facts. Ground the distinctions in whatever the philosophy of convention and of moral obligation genuinely supports; in particular, I want the procedure to separate conventions that solve a coordination problem from those that merely mark group membership from those that track an antecedent moral requirement such as fairness to those who waited. It should also say something principled about the resident–visitor asymmetry: what a resident may demand of a guest, what a guest's ignorance excuses, whether the host institution has any duty to warn, and — a distinction I suspect most of the confusion turns on — the difference between an act's being wrong and its being a fitting object of resentment.

Then run the procedure on three cases: not tipping, cutting a queue, and jaywalking on an empty street, including whichever one it handles in a way I will not like. Close by telling me what the procedure cannot settle. It will appear as advisory guidance in our orientation handbook; I hold final sign-off, and it must be usable by a committee of scientists with no philosophical training.
