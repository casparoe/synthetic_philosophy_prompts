ok so I've been chewing on Hofstadter's careenium thing — the box full of tiny magnetic marbles he calls simms, bouncing around on a frictionless floor, occasionally clumping into big slow globby structures he calls simmballs — and I want to get a satire out of it. Not a satire of Hofstadter. A satire of us, and specifically of the way people argue about whether the higher-level patterns are doing any of the pushing.

Here's the shape I want: a mock peer-review dossier for a submission to a made-up journal — call it something like *Simmball Dynamics* or *The Journal of Higher-Level Causation*, whatever sounds right. The submitted paper claims to have demonstrated downward causation in the careenium: that a stable simmball configuration makes a difference to where individual simms end up, over and above the simm-on-simm forces. Deliverable is one abstract (a couple hundred words, straight-faced, with the usual hedges) and then two referee reports that hate each other. Referee 1 basically buys the result and thinks the microphysicalist objection is a confusion. Referee 2 thinks it's a category error: the simm-level description is causally complete, so the simmballs can't be contributing anything extra without overdetermination. Then a short, tired editor's decision letter at the end, maybe three or four sentences, that has clearly stopped caring.

The funny has to come from the actual philosophy, not from wacky simmball names or fake jargon. So I want the exclusion argument, supervenience, multiple realizability, the distinction between a cause and a mere difference-maker, counterfactual dependence, the causal-completeness-of-physics premise, and the whole "special sciences are real but not fundamental" maneuver all doing real work in the reports — but as things two referees would actually snarl at each other. Referee 2 should be right about several things and a jerk about all of them. Referee 1 should be sympathetic but should overclaim at least once in a way an attentive reader can catch, and should probably lean too hard on the fact that the simmballs are huge and sluggish while the simms are tiny and fast, as if slowness settled anything.

And I want the reverse joke to land too: the deflationist who says "it's just simms bouncing" should come off as having said something true and useless, not something profound. But the emergence fan who says the simmballs "push the simms around" shouldn't get off scott free either — there should be at least one moment where you can feel the whole dispute starting to look like a fight over which level gets to use the word "cause."

Around 800-900 words total. Deadpan the whole way through, no winking, no narrator explaining the joke, no footnotes that break character. It should be genuinely funny to someone who's spent time on mental causation and still readable to someone who hasn't — so don't dumb down the content, but don't make it depend on the reader recognizing any particular book or paper. No fake citations, no reading list at the end, they always read as filler. A little bit of professional cattiness in the reports is welcome (one of them should complain about the experiment's error bars in a way that's obviously a proxy for a metaphysical objection).
