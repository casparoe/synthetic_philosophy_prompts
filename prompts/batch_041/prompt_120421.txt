A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about Twin Earth, and we ended in a stalemate I would like you to break. His position is that the externalist intuition is a trick of chemical vocabulary: change the stuff in the lakes and of course the word's extension changes, but nothing about what the speaker means, grasps, or could ever discover changes along with it. What I want to know is whether the externalist verdict survives when the environment is held fixed and the only thing that varies is the community. I need you to construct the case that would decide it.

Please work as a philosopher of language with a specialty in natural kind terms and content externalism. Think carefully before you write, and begin by brainstorming the design space — what could vary, what must be held fixed, and which apparently different variations collapse into the same case — before you commit to a single scenario. Do not cite literature or offer a reading list; I cannot look anything up, and I would rather have your reasoning than your recollection of who said what.

Specifications for the case:

1. The two subjects are molecule-for-molecule duplicates and have always inhabited physically and chemically identical environments. No second planet, no alien substance, no brains in vats, no exotic physics: think of two villages, two clinics, or two laboratories in the same world. Prefer an everyday term to a scientific one; if the case turns on chemical microstructure, it will reproduce exactly the intuition my friend is attacking.

2. Nothing available to any observer, instrument, or to the subjects themselves distinguishes them. Their dispositions to apply the term, to assent, to correct one another, to report what they mean, and to answer any hypothetical question are identical, and would remain identical in every circumstance either of them could occupy. Any difference you introduce must therefore lie in facts that no member of either community has encountered or could encounter.

3. The difference between the two communities must be statable in non-semantic, non-question-begging terms — a fact about how the term was introduced, or about what the community's experts do with the term in circumstances the two subjects never occupy — and it must not be described as a difference in meaning, content, or reference, since that is precisely what the case is meant to generate.

4. The case must make environmental (causal-chain) externalism and social (deference-based) externalism come apart, while internalism about content delivers its own verdict on both. Two of these three must disagree about whether the two subjects' words mean the same thing, and the disagreement must follow from each theory's own account of how a term acquires its content rather than from stipulation in the setup. If the constraints force one of the views to say something absurd, report that instead of papering over it.

5. Set the case inside a 2×2. Axis one: is the stuff the term is applied to the same or different? Axis two: is the community's pattern of use and deference the same or different? The familiar Twin Earth case occupies the cell with different stuff and the same pattern of use. I want the cell with the same stuff and a different pattern of use. Then fill in the remaining cells as each theory would, and say whether each theory's four verdicts are mutually consistent; if not, name the cell that breaks it.

6. Keep it to a page and keep it mundane: no gods, no infinite beings, no time travel, no scientists with perfect theories.

The case exists to test whether the environmental difference is doing the work in the standard Twin Earth intuition, so a setup that simply announces that in one community the word means something else is worthless.

After the case: walk through each view's verdict, including what each says about what the subjects can know about their own words from the inside; name the single feature doing the work and say what happens when it is removed, that is, which cell we land in and what the theories then say; give the strongest reply available to my friend the internalist, not a caricature of it; and say whether the specifications are jointly satisfiable, identifying the conflicting pair if two of them cannot both be met. That last answer is an acceptable outcome, not a failure.
