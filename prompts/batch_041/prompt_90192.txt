So I've fallen down a rabbit hole on Otto Neurath's idea of a picture language — the notion that you could build a whole working language out of pictograms arranged by rules, so that statistics and public information could be read off by anyone no matter what natural language they speak (this is the thing behind those repeated-figure charts where one little man stands for 10,000 people). And I keep wanting to actually try it myself: I'm messing around with a design for a picture-based notation that a city public health office could use for bulletins about a measles outbreak — case counts, vaccination coverage, who's at risk, what the current guidance is, how confident the estimates are — posted in places where lots of people who don't share a first language will see them. Every time I start, I hit a wall that I suspect isn't about my drawing skills.

So before I keep fiddling: give me the desiderata that any picture language has to meet to count as a real language and not just a fancy infographic. I want the full spec — what it has to be able to say (negation, conditionals, "most" / "some" / "none", quantities, comparisons, time and change, uncertainty, abstract stuff like "risk" or "eligibility", and the difference between showing something and claiming it), the distinctions it can't collapse (icon vs. legend vs. rule of combination; a picture that asserts vs. one that merely depicts; a mark that means by resemblance vs. one that means by convention), and the constraints it can't violate (readable by someone who's never seen it, no reliance on a written gloss, no conventions smuggled in through the key). For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that forces it, then tell me which pairs of requirements definately can't be had together — I have a suspiscion that "purely pictorial, no arbitrary conventions" and "expressive enough to actually do the job" are going to fight, but I want to know exactly where the fight is and which of the two is the cheaper thing to give up. If you can, end with a quick ranked list of which single requirement does the most to rule out candidate picture languages, since I suspect there's one that knocks out most of the cute designs people propose. Skip the history-of-ISOTYPE material, I've already read that part.
