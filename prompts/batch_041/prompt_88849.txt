My mate Dave is well into the Mars colonisation thing — books, launch streams, the lot — and he reckons getting a self-sufficient settlement off this rock is the most important thing our species could be doing. I'm not against it, but the other week down the pub I said I thought the money and the clever people might be better spent sorting things out down here first, and he came back with the whole not-all-your-eggs-in-one-basket line, plus a point I hadn't heard before: a colony that still needs spare parts shipped from Earth isn't really a backup, it's just a very expensive lifeboat tied to the ship it's meant to be escaping. Fair enough. Then a third bloke who'd been listening said 'yeah, but there's no planet B', and that was that — argument over, everyone nodding. I've been turning it over since, because I think that line did more work than it was entitled to and I can't put my finger on why.

What I can't work out is what the sentence is actually claiming. Taken flatly it's just a fact about the solar system right now — there's nowhere else we can live, so don't bank on one — which is true but doesn't obviously touch Dave's point, since he's not saying there's somewhere to run to, he's saying we should make one. But I suspect people mean much stronger things by it: that we owe something to this planet in particular and leaving is a sort of betrayal; that the ones who'd get on the rocket are never the ones drowning; that a second home can never be a substitute for the first; that promising an escape makes people care less about the place they're escaping from; or that the whole thing is a distraction from spending the money where it's needed now. Some of those look like seperate claims about facts, some about what's right, and at least one seems to be about what the word 'B' is doing — is a backup even supposed to be a replacement?

Can you give me the full menu of precise things 'there is no planet B' could mean, from the trivially true through the genuinely arguable to the ones that are just false, and for each say what would have to be the case for it to hold? Then tell me which of them the people who say it in a row like ours are probably getting at, and which of them — if any — actually rebuts Dave rather than just changing the subject. I want it as a list of distinct readings with a verdict on each rather than one overall answer, and I don't need any references or further reading, I'm not writing anything, I just want the moves laid out so I know what I'm agreeing to next time.
