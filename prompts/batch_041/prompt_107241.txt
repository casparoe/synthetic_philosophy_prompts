I'm giving a workshop paper next month and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The paper argues that the Meno problem -- why knowledge beats mere true belief -- only looks hard because everyone assumes the extra value has to attach to the belief itself. I claim it attaches to the state of settled inquiry: knowing is what licenses you to stop asking and actually stake something on p, and that's a value a lone true belief doesn't confer. So the swamping objection misses, because it only prices the product (a true belief) and never prices the closure. I also lean on a case where someone holds a true belief on rotten grounds but has no reason to keep checking -- I say that's not knowledge and not valuable in my sense, and people always fight me on it.

Audience: a mid-sized analytic epistemology workshop. Mostly mainstream reliabilists and accuracy-first/formal people, a couple of virtue epistemologists, plus two pragmatist-leaning folks who'll think I'm not going far enough. Act as a philosopher who has both defended and attacked this kind of view, think it through carefully, maybe brainstorm the likely lines first, then give me the sheet: the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, not the generic internet ones, ordered by how much damage they do, each phrased the way that person would phrase it, with a two-minute reply and a flag on where my reply is soft. And the one question I'm hoping nobody asks.
