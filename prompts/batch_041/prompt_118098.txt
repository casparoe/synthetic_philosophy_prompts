I'm a PhD student working on value theory and decision theory, and the dispute about the commensurability of pleasure and pain looks to me like one that has been stalled for decades. One party treats the hedonic scale as continuous — for any pain there is some finite quantity of pleasure that outweighs it, so the two are traded off at an exchange rate. The other party, the lexical-threshold negative utilitarian, holds that up to some threshold of suffering no amount of pleasure compensates, so within that range the ordering is lexicographic rather than a matter of exchange. What strikes me is that both sides argue as though 'commensurable' had a single sense, and my hypothesis is that the impasse is largely a terminological artifact: a lexicographic order is still a complete and transitive ranking, so if commensurability means no more than comparability, the threshold view is not a denial of commensurability at all — what it denies is the Archimedean property, that enough of one good can outweigh any amount of another. If that is right, the residue is a much narrower question about which structure the hedonic order actually has, and about whether that question is empirical (about our psychology), formal (about which axioms we accept), or normative (about which orderings we may adopt). Diagnose the dispute for me: is it verbal, substantive, or several questions under one heading? And, on that diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, an empirical test, or nothing?
