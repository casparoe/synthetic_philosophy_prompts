I sit on the population-health committee of our regional health board, and on Thursday the full board votes on a proposal I have to circulate a written opinion about beforehand. Please draft that opinion for me.

Background. We contract with an independent treatment centre. Its public work is contracted separately: 4,300 inpatient procedures a year, guaranteed at current volume regardless of what else the centre does, and it has never missed that volume. Alongside this, the centre runs a private wing where patients pay out of pocket for expedited diagnostics and minor surgery, waiting roughly three weeks rather than the current public wait. Median waits now run about 17 weeks in our two most deprived quintiles and about 6 weeks in the two least deprived.

The proposal before us, brought by a coalition of community organisations from the two most deprived quintiles, is to terminate the private wing as a condition of the centre's public contract.

The analyst's figures. Termination would raise the median wait in the two least deprived quintiles from 6 weeks to about 14. It would leave waits in the two most deprived quintiles essentially where they are: a handful of clinicians work sessions on both sides and would cut their public sessions, so the analyst expects a net loss of about 40 public procedures a year, pushing those medians from 17 weeks to about 18. The top-to-bottom gap in median waits would fall from about 11 weeks to about 4. Under the board's priority index, which weights health gains at 4.0 for the most deprived quintile down to 1.0 for the least deprived, termination reduces our weighted health-gain measure by an estimated 1.8% over three years.

Our standing orders say two things. Paragraph 4: the board shall seek the greatest attainable improvement in the health of the population it serves, giving greater weight to those whose health is worst. Paragraph 9, inserted by amendment last year: in exercising its functions the board shall have regard to the desirability of narrowing the gap in health outcomes between the most and least deprived communities. Paragraph 9 gives no guidance on whether narrowing the gap by worsening the position of the better-off counts towards it.

At the engagement session the coalition's spokesperson put their case this way: they are not asking for anyone to be pulled down, they are asking not to be governed by an arrangement in which their neighbours can buy their way past them, and they believe that arrangement is harming them in ways our measures do not capture. Our community engagement lead thinks there may be something in this, along a trust and political-efficacy channel, but the only evidence is one small qualitative study, and the board's own rule is to act on the best available evidence. One colleague keeps calling the proposal a textbook case of levelling down; another replies that this objection only bites against a view nobody on this board holds, since Paragraph 4 weights health gains rather than equalising outcomes.

A price cap on private fees, combined with a levy earmarked to fund additional public procedures, has also been floated as a middle path. The centre says it would absorb the levy by cutting private volume by around 30%.

Please draft my written opinion. Recommend one of the three courses, in terms the board can vote on, and give the reasoning. Treat the coalition's case at its strongest rather than as an emotional appeal, and be explicit about how Paragraph 9 should be read, since whatever we decide will govern future cases too. State how confident you are in your main claims, and single out the point you are least sure of.
