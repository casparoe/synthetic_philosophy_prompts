I am a novelist in the last months of a book, and I have come to a passage I cannot write myself. In the twelfth chapter a mine has collapsed, and the rescue captain can reach one of two galleries before the air goes: five men below, one man alone above. The decision is not the chapter's subject. The rhyme is. Every child in that valley chants a choosing rhyme — the kind that ends by landing on whoever is "it" — and the captain, who has heard it since he was a boy, has lately begun to hear in it the argument he once met as Taurek's and has never been able to answer: that when you must choose between two people you toss a coin, and that when you must choose between two people and three others on the one side and a single person on the other, the numbers do not by themselves tell you what you must do, because each of the five loses only his own life, and there is no one, anywhere, who loses five.

What I want from you is the rhyme itself, not a commentary upon it. Some conditions.

It should be a real counting rhyme — twelve to twenty lines, no fixed meter, but with a beat a child could keep while a finger moves from chest to chest. The count must climb and then refuse to add up: each number should be given its own weight and then be denied the power to combine with the others. If the last line returns to the first, so much the better; I would rather the poem circle than conclude.

The word five should appear in it, but at least once it must name something that cannot be counted, or five things that will not become one thing.

The rhyme must end by landing on someone, and I want it genuinely undecidable whether the finger comes down on the one or on one of the five. That hesitation is the whole coin, and I want the poem to enact it rather than mention it.

A reader who knows this debate should hear both positions at once: the pull of the larger number should be audible in the beat even as the words withhold it. A reader who knows nothing of it should hear a good children's rhyme and be left slightly cold at the end. Please do not let the poem console anyone, and do not let it settle.
