I'm in a grad seminar on value theory and we've spent two weeks circling the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — the annoying fact that "pleasure" seems to name a pile of experiences that have nothing in common: the pleasure of finally scratching a mosquito bite, the pleasure of a chord resolving, the pleasure of hearing that the guy who fired you just got fired, the pleasure of being so tired you can't move. The felt-quality people say there must be some shared phenomenological tone running under all of it; the attitudinal people say no, the only thing they share is that you like them; and our prof keeps saying that if hedonism can't find a common currency it can't add pleasures up, and the whole view collapses into "just do what you like," which isn't a theory of anything. For my presentation I have to do something creative instead of a paper, so I want to try my hand at satire.

So: write me the landing-page copy for a fictional product called Hedon — a wearable + app that tracks your pleasures, weights them, and "optimizes" your life to maximize the total. Format it like a real product page: a punchy hero paragraph, a "How it works" section, a "Pleasure Conversion Table" (their proprietary unit, and the joke should live mostly here — a bunch of wildly different pleasures each getting a number), an FAQ with maybe four questions, and one customer testimonial. Somewhere in there a price and a city for the HQ, whatever you like. 600-800 words.

The whole page has to be written by people who are completely confident that pleasures obviously share a common unit — never let the copy notice the problem, no winking at the reader. What I'm really trying to skewer is the assumption that pleasures have a common measure, plus the whole quantify-your-happiness industry that rides on it. So the comedy should come from the specifics: the exchange rates in the table, the FAQ answers that quietly assume things a philosopher would flinch at (mixed pleasures, bittersweet ones, whether a good cry counts as positive), and a "Scientific Advisory Board" with a couple of members named by role who have apparently already agreed on the weighting. It should be funny to someone who's read the hedonism literature and still readable to someone who hasn't. Keep the philosophy right, please — I want to laugh, not wince.
