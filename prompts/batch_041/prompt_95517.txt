Write a joke about Sen's liberal paradox — the impossibility of a Paretian liberal. It should be a genuine joke rather than an illustration of the theorem: someone who knows the result should laugh, and someone who doesn't should be able to see, once it is explained to them, that the humour turns on the actual structure of the result rather than on generic jokes about economists, voting, or politics.

Constraints on the joke. Do not reuse Sen's Lewd and Prude example or the Lady Chatterley's Lover scenario; invent a fresh situation. The punchline should turn on the specific structure of the theorem rather than on its moral. In particular, it should exploit how little the impossibility requires — a liberalism condition so weak that it assigns only two people, each decisive over a single pair of alternatives, together with the weak Pareto principle, and an unrestricted domain of admissible preference profiles — and it should exploit the form the impossibility takes, namely a cyclical social ordering rather than a flat clash between two axioms.

Do not have the joke claim that the theorem shows liberalism to be incoherent, that it presupposes majority voting, or that it rests on one person having preferences about another person's private business as such; and do not have the punchline dissolve the paradox by appealing to some proposed resolution as though that resolution were the settled verdict.

After the joke, add two or three sentences identifying precisely which features of the theorem the joke depends on, so that a reader can check that it is not merely a pun on the word 'liberal'.
