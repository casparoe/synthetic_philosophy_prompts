I'm a philosophy of science prof who did a PhD in ecology before switching over, so I know the evolutionary vocab from the inside — selection vs drift, lineages, fitness landscapes, adaptive radiation, relict populations, niche construction. Lakatos on research programmes has always felt like the right shape to me, but I want to know how much of it is actual content and how much is borrowed prestige. So: restate the methodology of scientific research programmes in my old vocabulary. Hard core, protective belt, negative and positive heuristics, progressive vs degenrating problem-shifts, and the no-instant-rationality line — put all of it in terms of populations, lineages, selection and drift, and do it faithfully, not as a cute analogy. Don't smuggle in evolution's assumptions and call that a translation.

Then the residue report: what comes through clean, what only comes through if you fudge it, and what has no expression in evolutionary terms at all. I especially want your read on three things. (a) Can progressive mean anything without a notion of progress, given that ecology has pretty much given up on progress? (b) Does Lakatos's heuristic/appraisal split line up with the difference between forward ecological explanation and retrospective phylogenetic reconstruction, or is that a false friend? (c) Is a degenerating programme that survives on funding and inertia just drift — persistence without selection — or is that a category error?

And the verdict I actually care about: where it doesn't translate, is that because evolutionary biology is missing a normative dimension it can't have, or because Lakatos was dressing up sociology of science as epistemology? Be honest, I'd rather have the unflattering answer.
