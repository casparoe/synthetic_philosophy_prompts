I'm in a psychiatry residency and I keep bumping into a kind of category I don't have a word for. Vitamin D deficiency: the cutoff slides around — 20 ng/mL one year, 30 the next, depending on which body you ask — there's no switch that flips at 29 versus 31, and yet the label predicts falls and infections, and handing out supplements moves some outcomes. Pre-diabetes is the same deal: the number is a committee decision and it keeps getting redrawn, but it isn't nothing, it's what the trials enroll on. Or depression in the DSM — boundary drawn by vote, redrawn every edition, still the thing drugs get tested against.

So here's what I want to know. Is there a name for a category that earns its keep — you can predict with it, intervene on it — but whose edges are set by our measuring and labelling practices rather than by any joint in nature, and that keeps working even after the edge moves? I know the phrase natural kind, and I know people just wave it off as a social construction, but neither fits. It's not mere convention, because the predictions actually come out. And it's not a natural kind, because there's no shared essence and the boundary is basically a policy someone signed.

Is there an established term? If so, tell me which, and how it differs from the closest things — I've heard people gesture at "pragmatic" categories, and at the idea that a category can end up changing its own members, and I don't know if either is what I'm after. If there's no term, coin one, and then tell me where the edges are: does species belong in the same box as vitamin D deficiency, or is that a different animal? Does race used as a medical variable? And does it matter that the boundry moves, or is the moving boundary a side issue that I'm overweighting? I'm not looking for a bare label — I want to know what the thing actually is and where it stops.
