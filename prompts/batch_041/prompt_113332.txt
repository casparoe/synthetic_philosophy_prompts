The paradox as I've got it: if I know that P, then any evidence pointing to not-P has to be misleading, because P is true and so anything pointing the other way must be pointing the wrong way — which means I'm entitled to just dismiss that evidence. That sounds awful, since it seems to license exactly the closed-mindedness we're taught is epistemically bad. My move is to say the paradox only goes through if we treat knowledge as a state you're *in*, rather than as an event that happened. The paradox treats it as a season ticket, something that keeps licensing you to skip the queue forever, every time fresh evidence turns up. I also want to say something about the verb itself — "know" is stative in English (nobody says "I am knowing"), and I beleive that grammatical fact is why the paradox feels so natural, because we're primed to hear it as a standing state rather than a one-off achievement. Then I'd use all of that to say something about echo chambers and disagreement: the person who can't hear the other side is treating their knowledge as a season ticket.

What I'm worried about is whether the single-ticket/season-ticket thing is doing any real work, or whether it's just a posh way of saying "you should update on new evidence".
