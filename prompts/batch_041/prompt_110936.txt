Write a short story about a bureau whose whole job is answering questions that are too big for any one person. The method: whoever gets the question splits it into sub-questions, hands each to a fresh copy of themselves, those copies do the same, and it keeps going down untill the questions at the bottom are small enough that anyone could answer them; then the answers get stitched back together on the way up and out comes a verdict that nobody at any level actually understands. That setup is a dramatization of an idealization I find interesting -- that a whole deliberation could be built out of a tree of bounded, none-too-clever steps, each one seeing only its own little piece, and that this is supposed to be safer and more trustworthy than one big clever leap. So take the idealization seriously and then lean on it: what does the stitching at each node actually consist of, what happens when a question refuses to break into easier pieces, and what is it like to be a clerk at the very bottom, answering something tiny with no idea what it's for. Could be a tribunal, a hospital ethics board, a forecasting shop, whatever fits. No moral tacked on at the end; just run the machinery and show where it strains.
