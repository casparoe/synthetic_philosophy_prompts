Act as a philosopher of mind with one foot in ethology, and take a minute to brainstorm before you answer — I care much more about where the boundaries of the concept fall than about the label itself.

Here's the pattern I keep running into. I read about a solitary wasp that hunts crickets. She stings one, drags it back to a burrow she's dug, parks it right at the entrance, goes inside to check the burrow is empty, comes back out, and hauls the cricket in. Move the cricket a few inches while she's inside and she comes out, finds it, drags it back to the entrance — and then goes inside to check again. Do it again and she does the whole thing over again. Apparantly you can keep this up indefinitely, and she never just drags the cricket in, and never seems to register that anything odd is happening. The unnerving part is that under normal conditions she looks like she's making decisions: she inspects, she adjusts, she handles a cricket that rolled into a dip differently from one that didn't.

I've seen the same shape in people, myself included. Someone has a big bag of clever, responsive-looking moves — concede, reframe, ask a clarifying question, tell a story — and it passes for flexible thinking until you do something slightly off-script. Then they dont adapt, they re-run the same move. Keep doing the off-script thing and they re-run it forever. The bag is big enough that it takes a while to notice it's closed.

So, is there a name for this? I have a feeling there's a word that came out of a fairly famous discussion of that wasp, and that people mostly use it to insult each others thinking, but I dont know what it is. If it exists, give it to me, and then do the part I actually care about: how does it differ from a fixed action pattern, from a habit, from merely being deterministic, and from a system that just happens to have a small repertoire? A friend of mine calls the thing I'm describing "closed-repertoire flexibility" — is that a better carve than whatever the standard term gives me, or is it just a restatement of it? And here's the second half, which is really why I'm asking: does catching something in a loop show that it isn't thinking or free, or only something narrower about its mechanism? I can't catch myself in a loop from the inside, and I genuinely can't tell whether "wide repertoire that can be trapped" and "actually able to revise the repertoire" differ in kind or only in size. If nothing established fits the narrower thing, coin a term for it and give me cases on either side of the line.
