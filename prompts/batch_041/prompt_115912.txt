I'm a novelist at work on a book whose spine is a fictional regulatory body, and for research I want to draft one of its public documents as sustained deadpan satire — the comedy has to come from getting the underlying philosophy right, not from winking at the reader. Please write, in the voice of that agency, a formal recall notice of roughly 600 words, recalling a novel on the grounds that it produced genuine grief in its readers even though its central characters never existed. It should follow the conventions of a real consumer-safety recall: a hazard summary, an affected-populations section, a required remedy, a compliance deadline, and a short FAQ. The satire should turn on the fact that the agency cannot state its own case without running straight into the puzzle underneath it — that the readers knew the characters were unreal, and that the very disclosure the agency demands, a warning that the persons depicted do not exist, is precisely what would stop the emotion it claims to be protecting people from. Let the agency wobble between treating the grief as a real harm and dismissing it as pretend-grief, and let its remedy grow more baroque the harder it tries to be exact: perhaps an affect manifest declaring in advance which emotions a text is licensed to produce, or a taxonomy separating licensed from unlicensed sorrow, or a special category for readers who grieve characters loosely drawn from real people. Keep it readable to someone who has never encountered the puzzle, but make sure a reader who has will see that the document is faithful to its actual structure rather than inventing one.
