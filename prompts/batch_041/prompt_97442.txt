I keep getting into the same arguement about the coherence arguments for expected utility maximization -- Savage, von Neumann-Morgenstern, dutch books, the money pump -- and it never goes anywhere. Both sides agree on the theorems, agree that the axioms are consistent, agree that they deliver a representation unique up to positive affine transformation, agree that nothing in the math is in dispute. The disagreement is entirely about what that shows. One side: the axioms are constitutive, an agent who violates them doesn't have a determinate preference ordering to be wrong about, and the practical stuff (money pumps, getting exploited, drifting over time) is just a symptom of that. Other side, roughly mine: you can drop completeness or independence and still be a functioning agent who simply isn't an expected utility maximizer, so the theorem describes a special case and gets dressed up as a requirement. Nobody moves.

My hypothesis is that the real fault line isn't in decision theory at all but in what we think a representation theorem is for -- whether it reconstructs preferences the agent already has or constrains what counts as having them -- and that this keeps getting tangled up with a separate question about whether the rationality in the premises (consistent preferences) is the same rationality as the one in the conclusion (do this act). Someone also told me that any agent who violates the axioms can be redescribed as an EU maximizer over a richer outcome space that includes facts about their own preferences, and I genuinely can't tell whether that's a rescue of the theorem or a reductio of it.

Is my diagnosis right, or is there a substantive mathematical or normative dispute hiding underneath it that I'm missing? And given whichever diagnosis you land on, what would actually count as progress here -- a distinction, a formal result, or nothing, because it's a verbal fight about the word rational that keeps getting rehashed because it sounds like it has teeth? It matters to me practically, since I work adjacent to alignment stuff where "coherent agent" gets thrown around as if the argument had settled something.
