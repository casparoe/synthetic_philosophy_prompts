I want a principle behind my intuitions about how many people there should be, but they will not obviously fit together. Take a life to lie on a single scale from unbearable to wonderful, and consider these futures.

Small: a few million people, each living a long and clearly wonderful life. Vast: trillions of people whose lives are only just worth living, none of them harmed or made worse off by anyone else's existence. My verdict is that Small is better than Vast, even though Vast holds vastly more total welfare. Larger: ten times as many people as Small, each with a life nearly as rich; I think Larger is better than Small, since more good lives is good even at a slightly lower average. Teeming: a great many people whose lives are clearly worth living but plain; I think Teeming is better than Small, on the strength of its total. Asymmetry: bringing a miserable life into existence is bad, but failing to bring a wonderful life into existence is not bad in the same way, if bad at all. Slide: a long series of steps from Small to Vast, each adding people at a slightly lower level than the last, feels no worse at any step, yet the two ends feel very different. Swap: if I must choose which of two equally happy people is brought into existence, neither choice is worse, because no one is made worse off either way.

Is there one principle that yields all six verdicts? If not, give me the smallest set of candidates that does, say which verdicts even that set cannot accommodate, and state each candidate precisely, naming the case that forces each of its clauses. I would also like to know what the best of them implies for a case I have not put to myself: a future in which every existing person is made better off but the total number of people falls, so that total welfare is lower than before.
