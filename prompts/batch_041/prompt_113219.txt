I have been going in circles on a question about the Dutch book argument and I would like your credence in a specific proposition, with the reasoning laid out rather than summarized.

Here is the background, stated without relying on any particular formulation you may or may not have stored. In the usual pragmatic version, a bookie who knows your credences offers you a finite collection of bets, each of which is acceptable to you at the odds your own credences imply, and the collection as a whole is a sure loss for you; from this it is concluded that incoherent credences are irrational. Notice what the argument asks you to imagine: that the bets arrive and are assessed one at a time, each on its own merits. If instead the same collection were posted in advance as a single take-it-or-leave-it package, stakes and odds fixed and nothing revisable, you would look at it, see the sure loss, and decline. I have been calling the second figure a *blind* bookie — one who can only present the whole book at once, and who therefore cannot pump anyone, coherent or not.

That makes me suspect the exploitability was never doing the work. So here is the proposition I want a number for:

An agent whose credences violate the probability axioms is irrational in virtue of that incoherence alone — she would be irrational even if no bookie existed, even if she never bet, and even if, being offered only whole packages, she could never be made to lose a penny.

That is a claim about the source of the defect, not a prediction about what any actual agent would do. Please give me your credence in it and decompose it. The terms I would expect the number to depend on, though you should add or drop terms as you think right:

1. The probability that credences can be assessed as accurate or inaccurate independently of any use they are put to, so that a scoring rule rather than a betting scheme is the fundamental measure of them.
2. The probability that the accuracy-based reconstruction delivers something strong enough to do the work — that is, that an incoherent credence function is always dominated by some coherent one, and that dominance is the standard the argument actually needs.
3. The probability that irrationality applies determinately to a state rather than to a choice — that there is a fact of the matter about the rationality of a credence, as opposed to a fact only about holding it, acting on it, or being unable to shake it — and, relatedly, whether a defect invisible from the inside still counts as one.
4. The probability that the pragmatic argument is doing normative work at all, as opposed to merely fixing the meaning of credence-talk and letting the normative claim ride in on the definition.
5. The probability that the bet-by-bet structure of the standard argument is a harmless idealization rather than an assumption about agency the conclusion cannot do without — in other words, whether the blind bookie's impotence exposes a gap in the argument or is merely an irrelevant restriction on the bookie.
6. The probability that imprecise credences — credences that come as an interval or a set of numbers rather than a single number — break the question rather than answer it: if the target state is not a single credence function, does incoherence still pick out anything for the proposition to be about?

Give a number and one line of justification for each term, then say how they combine into your final figure, then say which single term the final figure is most sensitive to and what argument or result would move it most. I am at about 0.5 on the proposition myself, and I suspect my uncertainty is mostly located in term 3; tell me whether you think that is where the real action is, or somewhere else.

Two things I would rather have than a smooth answer. First, I do not want references — I am not going to read anything, and a bibliography would be a way of not answering. Second, if the proposition as I have stated it is confused — if irrationality applied to a state is a category mistake, or if the blind bookie is a red herring that makes the framing worse rather than sharper — then say so plainly and tell me what the question should have been, rather than manufacturing numbers for a question that does not deserve them.
