Ok so I've been trying to work out why I go looking for sad stuff, and I've got a pile of gut reactions that don't seem to fit one story. I want the principle behind them, if there is one, or else tell me which of my reactions has to go.

The cases, roughly in the order I thought of them:

- A certain animated film opens with ten minutes of a man's whole married life and then his wife dies, and I've watched those ten minutes maybe six times, and I'll watch them again.
- The same thing happening to someone I actually know -- my uncle's wife died last year -- I can't stand to think about it and I change the subject.
- Reading a novel where a child dies: I'll keep reading, sometimes till 2am, and feel wrung out and glad about it.
- The news report of a real child dying in a fire: I turn it off, and if I don't, I feel sick and useless for the rest of the day, and I do not want more of it.
- A documentary about a real person's slow death: I watched it, I think it's important, I'd recomend it to others, but "enjoy" is completely the wrong word for whatever I got out of it, and I felt a bit dirty afterwards.
- A fictionalised version of basically those same real events: gripping, I couldn't stop, and I did enjoy it, and that bothers me a little.
- Sad piano music, no words, no story: I put it on deliberately when I'm already low, and I'd call that enjoyment with no hesitation at all.
- A sad song with words about a breakup, right after I've been dumped: same thing, I seek it out.
- My own nostalgia -- thinking about a house we moved out of when I was 12 -- makes me sad in a way I'd call pleasant, and I go back to it on purpose.
- Actual grief over an actual current loss: not pleasant in any way, no part of me wants it, and I can't stop it.
- I want the couple in a tragic story to end up happy. If you offered me a version where they do, I'd take it. But I think the version where they don't is the better piece of work, and I'd think less of the story if the author had given in.
- A sad ending that feels earned, where the loss means something and the thing couldn't have gone another way: I love it, I'd defend it to anyone.
- A sad ending that feels like the author just wanted to hurt me, pointless suffering piled on: I resent it, I get nothing from it, I feel used.
- Someone at a party tells a story about a horrible thing that happened to their friend, and it's a great story, well told, funny in places, and I'm enjoying it -- and then I catch myself and feel like a ghoul.
- A villain getting what's coming to him in a brutal scene: I enjoy that too, but I know that's a different pleasure and I'm not counting it.
- Being moved to tears by something that isn't sad at all -- a wedding, a reunion, a choir -- and calling that a good cry.

So here's what I notice and can't square. In the fictional cases the sadness seems to be doing work I actually want, and I come back for more of it. In the real cases it's the exact opposite: I flee it, and when I can't, I don't get anything I'd call good out of it. But my own nostalgia and my own grief are both real and they come out opposite ways, so "real vs fictional" isn't the line. And the story I keep half-believing -- that I don't actually enjoy the sadness, I enjoy the insight or the beauty or the feeling of being moved, and the sadness is just the price of admission -- looks fine for the novel and the documentary, but then I put on the piano music when there's no story and nothing to learn and no insight coming, and that's the whole point of putting it on. Someone told me this is all just catharsis and I dont buy it, I dont feel purged, I feel like I got something.

What I want is: the principle that comes closest to all of this, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a case I haven't thought of, plus what it predicts for two I'll make up now: (a) a video game where you play a character who dies at the end no matter what you do, and (b) a sad story about someone I despise, where maybe I'm enjoying the sadness for bad reasons. Then be honest about which of my reactions above the principle can't accommodate -- I'd rather drop one of my gut reactions than keep a principle with a special clause for every case. And if the honest answer is two principles rather than one, say so and tell me which one is doing the work in which range.
