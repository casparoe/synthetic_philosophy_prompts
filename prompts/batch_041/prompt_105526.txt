I want to do the session on superrationality, i.e. the idea that in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma with someone who's in exactly your epistemic position, cooperating is the rational play, because you and your counterpart are going to land on the same answer. I've given the standard lecture version before and it just washes over people — they nod along at the symmetry argument and then say "but defecting still pays more" and we go in circles for twenty minutes.

So: design me a runnable activity where they feel the pull of it in their gut before anyone says the word. No lecture with a game stapled on the end. I want the rules and the incentives to set the trap, so they walk into the conclusion themselves. Small real stakes are fine (points they can redeem for coffee/snacks at the break) but nothing that needs actual money, and nothing where someone has to lie to a friend's face if we can avoid it.

Two things I'd like it to bring out, because this is where I think the textbook treatments get thin and I want the activity to actually go there:

First, superrationality isn't just "always cooperate." In the n-player version of the dilemma — where you choosing C costs you something and hands a benefit to each of the other players — the symmetric reasoning says cooperate only when the benefit you'd hand everyone else outweighs your own cost, so whether C or D is the superrational move depends on how many people are in the pot. I'd love the group to feel that the "right" answer shifts with the size of the group, ideally by running the same thing at two different scales so they can watch it flip. Even better if the flip lands somewhere they didn't expect.

Second, I want a moment where the "everyone does the same as me" reasoning runs out of road — a spot where theres no single symmetric choice that's obviously right, so the debrief can be honest that the idea doesn't hand you a clean answer everywhere.
