Hey -- I'm a science journalist, I mostly write about cognition and human evolution, and a ideas festival has talked me into running a hands-on workshop instead of the usual on-stage interview. 24 people, mixed crowd (curious general public, a handful of grad students, at least two who will want to argue with me), 75 minutes, one room, chairs I can move, no projector, and I'd rather not touch a screen at all. The theme I've been handed is religion as a natural phenomenon -- my angle being that gods, spirits, rituals and taboos are things human minds make and pass around, shaped by how ideas survive transmission, rather than things that sit somehow outside that process. I want them to walk out having felt that, not having heard me say it. No lecture up front, and please don't let me use the technical terms until the debrief.

So design the activity. Here's the shape I'm imagining, change whatever needs changing. Split them into six chains of four, each chain a little village. I read them a short made-up story -- a woman goes to the river at dawn, meets a stranger with no shadow, the stranger names everyone in the village who lied that week and asks her to put a stone on the mill wall every time someone steals, she does it for years, then one day the strangers footprints stop showing up. The story deliberately mixes ordinary stuff with exactly one or two things that violate how the world works.

Then the engine: each teller gets a hard budget. Thirty seconds, no questions from the listener, and they have to drop at least one detail before passing it on. Round by round down the chain, the story gets edited by people who aren't trying to edit it. I'm betting the boring bits decay and one particular kind of weirdness survives -- but I want the rules arranged so the participants discover that rather than me asserting it.

Three things I want bolted onto that, if they'll fit:

- Halfway down each chain, before passing it on, each teller writes on a slip one thing the stranger definitely knows and one thing it definitely can't know. I collect and tally these. I have a hunch about what shows up.
- Each village invents a tiny action to keep the bargain -- three seconds, some gesture or object -- and passes that along under the same budget, no explanation allowed. Watch what happens to it over four tellers.
- At one point tellers can choose to swear to the story by giving up a token from a small stash they were handed at the start. At the very end everyone rates how believable each final story is. I want to see whether sworn stories rate higher and whether swearing spreads.

Materials should be printable and cheap, tokens can be anything.

Give me the full runnable thing: rules, materials, timings, what I say and when, and how to collect the outputs so the debrief has real data on the wall. The debrief is the part I care most about -- map each mechanic onto the actual finding it's standing in for, and be specific about the moments where the pattern showed up in the room.

Also tell me honestly what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle each failure, and be frank about what the activity can't reproduce -- the thing I'll have to just tell them, because the game can't stage it.
