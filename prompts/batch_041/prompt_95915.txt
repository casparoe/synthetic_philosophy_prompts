Write a satirical program brochure, in the deadpan register of a municipal parks-and-recreation department's adult-education catalogue, for a city-run course of instruction modeled on the proposal, developed across the first two books of Plato's Laws, that drinking parties can serve as a safe gymnasium in which citizens practice and exhibit temperance. The piece should run about 700 words and consist of a course description, an eight-to-twelve-item FAQ, a fee-and-liability section, a summary of public comments received, and a brief note on the department's statutory authority.

The humor must come from the actual content of the proposal rather than from invented absurdities. Paraphrase the following features in the department's flat administrative idiom, treat each as settled best practice, and do not quote the Laws or cite it: wine as a drug that induces fearlessness and untimely confidence; its intensification of pleasure, pain, and appetite; the argument that testing temperance requires an occasion of pleasure the way testing courage requires danger, and that wine supplies that occasion without the risk of war; the requirement of a sober, wise, and imperturbable presiding officer who governs the drinking, the music, and the speech; the image of the soul as iron heated so that it becomes soft and can be reworked, and the consequent value of the practice for the old; the age rules (nothing before eighteen, moderation before thirty, and from forty on the god's gift as a remedy for the crabbedness of age); the abstentions and exclusions (magistrates during their year of office, judges, pilots, councillors, slaves, nothing by day except for health or training, and nothing at night when children are intended); and the warning that the institution is void unless it is taken seriously as practice rather than as play.

The objections should be legible to a reader who knows the material without ever being stated: who certifies the presiding officers and by what examination; whether a state-run occasion can reveal a character it also manufactures; whether failing the course is distinguishable from merely having been drunk; whom the age rules and exclusions admit and whom they quietly keep out; and whether competence at drinking well is competence at living well. Include, among the public comments, two disapproving citizens of the austere faction for whom any drinking at all is shameful, and let the department answer them without noticing that it has conceded their premise.

The department must never wink: it believes in its program, its assessment rubrics, and its liability waiver, and it should describe the possibility that the practice is only a game as a compliance matter rather than a philosophical one. Do not invent sentences and attribute them to Plato, and do not use quotation marks around any language drawn from the dialogue.
