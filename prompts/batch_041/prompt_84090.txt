I took a job at a big distribution center back in March, mostly for the health insurance, and I landed right in the middle of a union drive. Long story short, the union won the election 54 to 46, and since we're not in a right-to-work state, that one vote has basically reorganized my entire working life. Here's the part I can't square. The union is now the exclusive bargaining agent for everybody in the unit -- including the 46 percent who voted no, including me, and I didn't join and probly won't. It has a legal duty to represent all of us fairly and in good faith, even people it obviously can't stand, and it can get hauled in front of the labor board for botching my grievance. At the same time, dues come out of my check whether I'm a member or not, and if I stop paying, the company can fire me. The contract it just signed has a no-strike clause, so if we walk out mid-term the union itself can be sued for the damages, and a couple guys in the freezer section found out last month that when you strike over money the company can just hire your replacement and you don't have a job to come back to. But the thing that really got me: the union fined members who crossed its own picket line, twenty to a hundred bucks a head, and sued them to collect, and apparently that holds up.

So what is a union, on this picture? Everybody at work talks about it like a voluntary association -- you join or you don't, your call -- but the same set of rules makes it the compulsory representative of people who voted against it, takes money from nonmembers, punishes its own members with fines like a little state, and gets sued for what a few hotheads do on a Tuesday. Is it a private club, a business agent, a government, a movement? Which of those does the actual setup rule out, as opposed to just sit comfortably next to? Does the package hang together, or is the voluntary-association talk quietly contradicted by the fines and the exclusive representation? And if I wanted to swap the underlying view for a different one -- unions as genuinely voluntary clubs, or as fully public bodies -- what in the practice would have to go? Cap it at 400 words, and skip the history of labor law; I want the argument, not a timeline.
