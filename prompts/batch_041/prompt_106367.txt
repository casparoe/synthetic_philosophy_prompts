My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether supererogation is a coherent moral category at all, and I would rather settle what an account of it would have to deliver before either of us commits to a side. So please write me the desiderata: the full specification that any adequate theory of supererogation must meet.

State each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or merely a commitment of one camp. At a minimum I want the list to cover the phenomenon itself, namely acts that are morally admirable yet not required; the apparent paradox that an act can be better than its alternatives and still not be something one ought to do; the standing of the distinction between what is required and what is merely good; where the threshold comes from at which duty stops and the supererogatory begins; and the difference between a single extraordinary act and a sustained life of moral heroism.

Then the parts I care about most. First, whether the theory must hold that omitting a supererogatory act is never blameworthy. My friend insists that some such omissions are plainly blameworthy, the person who could save a life at trivial cost and does nothing, and I want to know whether that pressure forces the theory to abandon the category or only to refine it. Second, whether the same act can be supererogatory for one person and obligatory for another by virtue of role or circumstance, the off-duty bystander against the lifeguard, and if so what that does to any theory that locates supererogation in the act itself rather than in the agent's position. Third, whether an act can count as supererogatory when the agent's motive is not admirable, the colleague who absorbs an exhausting burden for a struggling coworker but partly because she wants to be admired for it, and what that shows about the relation between the supererogatory and the praiseworthy.

Flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, that is the result I most want, so say which subset and why. Tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out otherwise popular theories. You may close with a brief verdict on two or three of the usual contenders, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves. No reading list and no citations; I want the specification, not a survey.
