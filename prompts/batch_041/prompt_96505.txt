I'm a student in a seminar on literary translation, and we've spent the last few weeks with Douglas Hofstadter's Le Ton beau de Marot, comparing the many English versions he collects of Clément Marot's short poem addressed to a sick young lady. The poem has a very tight formal scheme—short lines, a strict rhyme pattern, and a recurring refrain—and the seminar has made me notice something that I don't have a word for. When translators decide to preserve the form, they are forced into the same kinds of semantic compromises: a word that has no rhyme partner gets replaced by a near-synonym, an image that will not fit the syllable count gets swapped for a neighbouring image, and so on. What interests me is not that this happens, but that independent translators seem to converge on the same compensating move. A toy example: if the source line names a small bird and the only convenient rhyme is a flower, translator after translator may choose the flower; by the fifth or sixth version, the flower is quoted and discussed as though it were part of the original. The compensation then hardens into a received reading, so that later translators inherit not only the formal problem but a ready-made solution to it. It is as if the target-language constraint creates a semantic attractor, and the attractor acquires authority simply by being there first.

Is there an established name for this? I know the neighbourhood: untranslatability, compensation, domestication, the translator's invisibility, and the idea of a translation tradition. But none of those seems to pick out the specific loop I'm describing—the way a formal constraint selects a particular non-literal solution, other translators converge on it, and the solution then hardens into a pseudo-original. If there is a term, I'd like to know what it is and, more importantly, how it is usually bounded: does it apply only to poetry, or also to constrained prose, song lyrics, subtitles, and localization? Is it a species of something more general in constrained optimization, where the constraint makes one solution focal even though it is not uniquely best? And if there is no established term, can you coin one and define it sharply enough to distinguish it from ordinary compensation, from a mere translation error that happens to spread, and from the broader phenomenon of a translation becoming more influential than its source? I'm less interested in a label than in the carving: what exactly the phenomenon is, what it is not, and what its edge cases are.
