I am trying to determine whether my views on the hitchhiker case and rational irrationality are jointly consistent. I hold the following. (1) In the hitchhiker case, once I have been rescued, paying the stranger is not supported by any future-directed benefit: the ride is over, and no one's future conduct depends on whether I pay. (2) Nevertheless, I ought to pay. (3) I ought to pay because I ought to have a standing disposition to keep promises of this kind, and it is rational from the ex ante point of view to cultivate that disposition. (4) It can be rational to cultivate a disposition that leads one to perform an act that is irrational at the time of action. (5) If an act is irrational, one ought not to perform it. (6) In the counterfactual mugging—where a coin has already been flipped, and if it had come up heads I would have received a large sum only if I would now pay a small sum on tails—I ought not to pay. (7) In Newcomb's problem—where a predictor has already placed money in boxes based on its prediction of my choice—I ought to take only the opaque box. Please audit this set. Which commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each genuine tension? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to be revised and what the cheapest revision would cost.
