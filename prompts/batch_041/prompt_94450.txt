I spent thirty-odd years running a lab before I retired, and I've been teaching myself philosophy since, mostly epistemology, and I've got stuck on something I can't talk my way out of. Here's the puzzle as I understand it. Suppose I know some perfectly ordinary thing, say that my bike is locked in the shed. If I really know it, then anything that looks like evidence to the contrary has to be misleading, because what I know is true. So I could reason: I know the bike's in the shed, so if my neighbour tells me she just saw someone riding off on it, she must be mistaken, and I can go on believing as I did and ignore her. That conclusion is plainly wrong, but I cant find the bad step, and every discussion I read seems to just assume we all agree it's wrong.

So I'd like you to put on your epistemologist hat and build me a case rather than give me a general chat about it. Construct one thought experiment in which that reasoning is as tempting as it can possibly be, but where it's still obvious the person should take the counterevidence seriously and change their mind. Constraints: realistic and ordinary, no brains in vats, no evil demons, no perfect deceivers, no science fiction; the subject must genuinely know the proposition, so don't let me off the hook by making it a mere belief, a lucky guess, or something with a lurking defeater already in the background; and the counterevidence has to be the sort of thing a normal person meets on a normal Tuesday. One paragraph for the scenario itself.

Then, and this is the part I actually care about, write out the argument that generates the puzzle as a chain of steps, say which step each of the main ways out of it has to deny and why that denial is or isn't costly, tell me which feature of your case is doing the work, and whether the case still bites if we swap knowledge for justified true belief. If you decide no case can meet all my constraints at once, tell me which constraint has to give. Think it through carefully and start by sketching two or three candidate cases before you commit to one, then give me your answer as continuous prose, no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables, just the way you'd write it in a letter.
