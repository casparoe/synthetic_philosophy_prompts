I work in model theory and universal algebra, and I am fluent in the usual apparatus: signatures, structures, automorphisms, definable sets, isomorphism types, and quotienting a structure by a group of automorphisms. I would like the debate about whether spacetime is a substance or a system of relations among bodies restated in that vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Treat the relationalist and the substantivalist as offering theories with a signature and a class of models; render Newton's bucket argument as a claim about what the relational signature can and cannot define or leave invariant; render the Leibniz shift argument as an automorphism claim; and render the later hole argument — where a transformation of the metric field leaves the observable facts intact — in the same terms.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the dispute translate cleanly, which translate only if I quietly help myself to assumptions my vocabulary does not license, and which have no counterpart in it at all? I am especially interested in two things. First, in model theory relations are subsets of a non-empty domain, so "no relations without relata" is trivially satisfied by construction; does that make the relationalist position trivial, or does it hide the work the principle was doing? Second, if I take "isomorphic models represent the same world" as a definitional stipulation rather than a metaphysical thesis, does the substantivalist have anything left to assert? Tell me whether the residue shows a defect in my framework or a lack of clarity in the original debate.
