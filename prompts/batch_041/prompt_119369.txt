ok so ive been chewing on the lying vs misleading thing for my thesis chapter and I think I've landed somewhere, but I want a consistency check before I commit to it. Here's the package:

1. Lying is just saying something you believe to be false. Misleading someone with a statement that happens to be true isn't lying at all — it's a different act.
2. What's actually wrong with both is the same thing: you're steering someone into a belief they wouldn't have formed if they had your information, and the objection is that it's a kind of manipulation — using them as a means to get what you want out of them.
3. But I also think there's something specifically bad about lying that the deception story can't account for — it breaks a kind of warranty or trust that a mere true-but-misleading dodge doesn't break, which is why people feel more betrayed by an outright lie than by a technically-true sidestep even when the deception is identical.
4. I hold the constitutive-norm line on assertion: to assert is to commit to the truth of the proposition you're putting forward, so asserting something you think is false is a violation of what assertion is.
5. If you ask me "did you eat the cake" and I say "I had a big lunch", knowing full well you'll take me to be saying I didn't — I think I've done something just as bad as lying. As bad, not just in the same ballpark. You were warranted in drawing the inference I was fishing for.

What I'm worried about is whether 2 and 3 can both stay, and whether 4 is doing any real work once 2 is on the table.
