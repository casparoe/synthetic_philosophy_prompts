A small foundation has funding for roughly eight researchers for three years to work on the conceptual foundations of consciousness: philosophy and theory, not new experiments or new data collection. The working framework is broadly global-workspace, on which information broadcast widely enough to be available for report, control, and reasoning counts as access consciousness, while phenomenal consciousness is whatever it is like to undergo an experience. Which open questions should this program prioritize, and in what order?

Two constraints to build into the ranking. First, the researchers can argue, formalize, and reanalyze existing data but cannot generate new evidence, so weight questions by how much conceptual work can actually settle them. Second, take seriously a worry that cuts across several theories: if a recurrent, globally coupled architecture can be unfolded into a feedforward system computing the same input-output function, then any theory that assigns consciousness on the basis of causal structure seems to imply that two behaviorally indistinguishable systems differ in consciousness, with no possible experiment to adjudicate. Treat that worry as one candidate item on the agenda rather than a settled premise.

Defend the ordering. Say which questions are upstream of which, what would move a question up or down the list, and whether the access/phenomenal distinction is itself worth funding as a research question or is better treated as settled terminology the program should work around.
