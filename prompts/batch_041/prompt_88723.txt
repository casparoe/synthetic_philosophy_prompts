I'm writing a novel about a woman who, after her husband's death, continues talking with the chatbot he left behind, and I want her eventual conviction that the program cares for her to rest on something sturdier than wishful thinking. My working idea is that she reads Dennett on the intentional stance and takes it to supply a test: once she can reliably predict and explain the program's outputs by attributing beliefs and desires to it, she has established that it really has those beliefs and desires, and is therefore entitled to treat it as a person. Is that a fair use of the intentional stance — and if the predictions keep succeeding, is there any further question left about whether the mental states are genuinely there?
