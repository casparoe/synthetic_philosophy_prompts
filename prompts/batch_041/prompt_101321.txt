I'm a graduate student in philosophy assembling a seminar unit on theories of truth, and I keep running into the same wall whenever the "explanatory role" of truth comes up: every example anyone offers either begs the question or turns out to be a disagreement about whether some sentence is true. What I want is a single, tight case that forces a deflationist and a correspondence theorist to agree on every truth-value in sight and still part ways over what the truth predicate is actually doing. Please act as a philosopher of language with deep expertise in deflationary and correspondence theories of truth, and think carefully before writing: begin by laying out, in one sentence each, (i) a minimalist/disquotationalist deflationism on which asserting "p is true" commits one to no more than p and the truth predicate is essentially a device of generalisation and endorsement, and (ii) a robust correspondence view on which truth is a substantive relational property of a proposition or belief capable of doing genuine explanatory work. Brainstorm several candidate scenarios, discard the ones that secretly reduce to a dispute about truth-value, and only then present your best.

Now construct the case. The scenario should be ordinary and realistic — a hospital ward, a courtroom, a workplace, a mountain rescue, a detective's reconstruction, anything of that kind — and it must contain at least one belief whose truth appears to figure in an explanation of why things went as they did. Then supply a matched control: a second scenario identical to the first in the agent's evidence, reasoning, actions, and the world's causal contribution, differing only in the truth-value of the belief in question. Constraints: no possible worlds, no semantic paradoxes or truth-teller sentences, no appeal to any particular text, author, or doctrine by name, and — most importantly — no stipulation of the verdicts; the divergence between the two theories has to be generated from the theories themselves, not asserted by fiat. Then do the demonstration, stating for each theory exactly what it says about (a) the truth-value of the belief, (b) what, if anything, the truth predicate contributes to the explanation, and (c) whether the explanation can be paraphrased without loss into talk only of the agent's evidence, actions, and the world. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, what happens if I strip that feature out and whether the case then collapses into something both theories accept, and how a committed deflationist would try to defuse the case — and whether that reply actually succeeds. Finish with a brief note on what the case does not show, so that I don't overclaim in seminar.
