Next month I'm presenting at our department's joint design-and-STS seminar — half the room are the people who actually build user interfaces, half are the science-and-technology-studies crowd who read Winner and Latour for a living. My thesis: the political character of a piece of software isn't a property of the artifact itself (the Winner-ish line) and isn't just something we project onto it (the pure social-construction line) — it lives in what I'm calling the "default surface," the set of paths of least resistance a thing presents: what happens if you do nothing, what takes one click, what takes twelve. Opt-in vs opt-out, autoplay on by default, the confirm-shaming button — I'll use those as the running examples, and argue the default surface is where power gets quietly allocated, intended or not.

I want a Q&A prep sheet for this specific room, not a general defense. What will the engineers push back on first, in their vocabulary, and what will the STS people push back on, in theirs? Order the objections by how much damage they'd do, give me a short reply to each and flag where each reply is soft. Include the one question I'm dreading. And since I'm basically inventing "default surface" on the fly, tell me straight whether it's doing real work or just collapses into affordances / choice architecture / some existing notion — how confident you are in that verdict, and which single claim in the whole thing you're least sure of.
