A friend of mine is an epiphenomenalist about phenomenal experience: she holds that qualia are entirely real but causally inert, so that her utterance "that hurt" or "this wine is too oaky" is produced by the underlying neural events and not by the felt quality it seems to be about. She has agreed to give the after-dinner remarks at a small conference and would like a joke that an audience of philosophers of mind will laugh at because of her position rather than in spite of it — a joke whose humour depends on the actual content of the view, not on the usual "philosophers are strange" material. The resource I would point her to is the trouble epiphenomenalism has with phenomenal judgment: if experiences never enter into causal relations, then her reports about her experiences cannot be caused by the experiences they report, and it is unclear whether such reports are then merely lucky, quietly self-undermining, or rescued by the fact that the neural states doing the causal work are reliably correlated with the experiences in question. Write the joke so that it lands on precisely this difficulty. Keep it short enough to deliver aloud in under a minute, and follow it with a single sentence identifying which commitment of the view the punchline turns on, so that a reader can tell the joke was built on the theory rather than around it.
