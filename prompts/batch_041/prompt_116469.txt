I am designing a formal account of scientific explanation on which a regularity earns its explanatory and counterfactual-supporting power only by being lawlike, and before I commit to any particular criterion of lawlikeness I need to know what a theory of lawhood would have to deliver. Please write the desiderata for me: the requirements that any adequate theory of laws of nature must satisfy, each motivated by the consideration or case that forces it, rather than a survey of the accounts already on offer.

Two things should shape the list. First, I care about the hard cases, and I want each requirement to be earned by one of them, so here are the ones I keep running into:

1. A world whose entire history is generated by one simple dynamical rule from a set of initial conditions, together with a further regularity that holds everywhere in that history but is entailed by the initial conditions rather than by the rule.
2. An exception-ridden generalization from a special science: a claim that holds only ceteris paribus, admits counterexamples, and is nonetheless used to predict.
3. A regularity that would govern a kind of entity that in fact never occurs anywhere in the world.
4. A probabilistic rule that fixes the chance of each outcome without fixing the outcomes themselves.
5. A symmetry principle that constrains the form any law may take but is not itself a regularity over events.
6. A regularity that holds of the laws themselves rather than of the events they govern.

Second, I want the list sorted, not merely assembled. For each requirement, say whether it is genuinely constitutive of adequacy or merely a commitment of one camp — the Humean who wants laws to supervene on the non-modal facts, the anti-Humean who wants them to govern or to carry primitive necessity — and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are optional refinements. Then do the two things I most need. Identify any subset of the requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied, since if such a subset exists the list itself is the result; and name the single requirement that does the most work excluding otherwise attractive accounts, explaining what it rules out.

Close with a brief statement of which one or two requirements most sharply divide Humean from anti-Humean accounts, and what the side that cannot meet them would have to give up in order to keep the rest of its position.
