Ok so background first, because I think the details matter here: I'm on the board of a volunteer ambulance corps that covers a big rural county. Historically, responding to a call is voluntary in the strict sense — the pager goes off at 2am and if you're in bed you're just... not obligated, you don't go, and officially nobody thinks worse of you. People respond because they care, or it's their thing, or whatever. We celebrate the folks who roll out at 3am in a snowstorm; that's kind of the whole culture.

Last month a call came in, cardiac arrest, about 12 minutes from a couple of our certified members who were off-duty and in range. Neither went. The on-call crew was thin and the patient died. Now there's a faction on the board that wants to make non-response a disciplinable thing for any certified member within ~20 minutes of a call, unless you're impaired or already working. Their line: if you've got the training and you're able, you've got a duty, full stop. My gut says something's off but I can't articulate it and I keep getting dunked on in the meetings.

The thing I keep circling: everybody on both sides agrees the person who drives through a snowstorm to do CPR is doing something admirable, above and beyond. And the proposal is basically to convert that above-and-beyond into the baseline. But then what's left to admire? Can "beyond the call" even survive being written into the call? I've seen people say "if it's required it's no longer supererogatory" and that seems too quick, because obviously you can require something that's still hard and still deserves praise — but I also can't tell whether the faction is just not seeing the distinction, or whether they see it and think it's irrelevant to policy. I'd like the distinction spelled out instead of hand-waved, and ideally taboo the word "hero" for a paragraph and see what's actually left.

Also relevant, and I don't want it to be the whole argument: our insurance and our roster are both fragile, half the members are 55+, and if we start punishing people for not showing up I think we definately lose people, which is worse for the county. But I don't want to just win on the practical point if there's a real moral point I'm missing.

Can you draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the next board meeting? I want a concrete recommendation — pass the rule, pass a watered-down version, or something else — with the reasoning actually laid out, and I want the strongest version of the other side engaged rather than strawmanned or reduced to "we'd lose volunteers." In particular, say clearly whether the board is allowed to treat "this is above and beyond" as a fact about the act that no policy can change, or whether it's more like a convention we're choosing to keep or drop — and what turns on which way that goes. If there's a middle option (say: you're required to call it in and give phone guidance, but going to the scene stays optional), say whether that would actually hold up or is just splitting the difference to avoid the fight. Keep it to a page and a half, no citations, write it like a person.
