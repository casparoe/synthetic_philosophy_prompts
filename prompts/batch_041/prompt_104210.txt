I'm putting together the final for my upper-level value theory seminar and I want one long essay question about spectrum arguments — the ones that try to show that "better than" comes out intransitive. Problem is every year I get the same four answers: someone recites the repugnant conclusion, someone says "sorites paradox," someone says the bullet gets bitten, and I learn nothing about whether they can actually think. So I want the question built on a case they haven't seen, so that reciting dosn't get them anywhere.

Here's the case I've cooked up — use it as the spine of the question. Take a spectrum of treatments for some nasty but non-fatal disease. At one end, T1 cures 90% of patients but leaves nearly everyone with a rough fortnight of side effects. Each step down the line you shave a sliver off the cure rate and a sliver off the side effects, so tiny that at each step you'd shrug and say the new one is at least as good as the last (you're trading a hair of cure for a hair of comfort). By the far end, T100 cures only 2% but has no side effects at all — and that one is obviously worse than T1. So T1 is at least as good as T2, T2 as T3, ... T99 as T100, yet T1 is clearly better than T100. If "at least as good as" is transitive, you've got a contradiction.

What I want the question to force them to do: (a) say precisely which relation the contradiction is about, and whether intransitivity of that one forces intransitivity of plain "better than"; (b) seperate transitivity from the other structural properties (completeness/totality, acyclicity) and show they know which one is doing the work; (c) take a stand on which premise to give up and own the cost — I especially want them to engage the vagueness machinery, i.e. whether there just isn't a fact of the matter about where the reversal happens (and whether that's a real reply or a relabeling), and the "on a par" move where adjacent items are neither better nor worse; (d) tell me whether their verdict changes between my treatment case and the population case, and why.

Please give me the essay question itself (make it answerable in 90 minutes), a model answer of about 650 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points worth 20 marks total, plus a one-line note under each about how candidates typically blow it.
