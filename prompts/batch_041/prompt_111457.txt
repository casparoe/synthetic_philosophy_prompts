ok, I'm trying to get my actual position down on paper before a workshop and I want a consistency check first. here's where I've landed:

(1) I think the unilateralist's curse is real and seriously underrated. The shape of it, as I understand it: if a lot of people each go ahead with something whenever it looks net-positive to them, then the thing happens as long as *one* of them thinks it's fine -- so a group like that ends up doing lots of stuff that was a bad bet from the start. So I think there should be a pretty strong default against acting unilaterally on "eh, seems good to me."

(2) I'm an expected-value maximizer about my own choices, and a fairly hard-nosed one. I should do whatever has the best expected outcome by my own lights. I've never been moved by "what if everyone did that?" -- I think the generalization argument is basically a fallacy, and I don't think facts about how many other people there are, or what they'd do, change what I have reason to do.

(3) I'm very pro-transparency. Know-how should be public, hoarding information is almost always the wrong call, and the main value of a group of people is that they pool what they know.

(4) Related: I think information is usually the highest-value thing available, so an agent should almost always gather more of it before acting.

(5) I'm a moral realist. There's a fact of the matter about whether these things are good, not just a bunch of clashing preferences.

(6) I take moral uncertainty seriously: take the theories you take seriously, weight them by how plausible you find them, maximize expected choiceworthiness (the parliament-of-theories picture).

I've got a nagging feeling that some of this can't be held all at once, but I honestly can't tell which bits. Can you seperate the pairs that are really in tension from the ones that only look like it, rank the real ones by how much damage they do, and for each say what the cheapest fix is and what I lose by making it? And if there's one single revision that cleans up the most, tell me which. Please don't just say it's all fine or that it's a package deal -- I want to know which specific commitment has to give.
