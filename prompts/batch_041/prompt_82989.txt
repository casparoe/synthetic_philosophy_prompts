Write a deadpan public notice from a town council announcing that from the first of next month the town will treat omissions as causes: any harm you could have prevented, you caused. Play it completely straight — the council is earnest and a little proud of the reform, no winking, no wacky-bureaucrat gags, and nobody in the document is allowed to notice that anything is wrong. The comedy has to come from the philosophy, and it should still be legible to someone who has never read a word about causation. Include a short section defining what counts as a cause, an FAQ, three worked examples (a child drowning at the reservoir while adults watched, a blocked drain nobody cleared, a house fire nobody phoned in), a penalty schedule, and a closing paragraph on the councils own exposure. The worked examples are the engine: let the reader watch the line between doing and allowing dissolve, and watch the overdetermination problem arrive — if every resident who could have mentioned the loose tile caused the fall, then every resident caused everything, and the word cause stops picking anything out. Take the council's paragraph all the way down: they also failed to prevent the flood, and failed to prevent that failure. Slip in one FAQ about whether a sleeping person is liable and one about whether the old rule, that you only owe a duty to act if you have taken one on, is being repealed. The target is the thought that could-have-prevented is all there is to causing, so let it run to the conclusion it actually has. About 600 words.
