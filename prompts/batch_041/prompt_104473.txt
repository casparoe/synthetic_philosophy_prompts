I host a small philosophy podcast, and next week's guest is a philosopher whose new book defends a heterodox account of why promises bind. Her central claim is that a promise does not add a new first-order reason for the promisor to act; instead, it transfers to the promisee a normative authority to demand performance and, importantly, to release the promisor. The obligation is constituted by that transferred authority rather than by any independent duty of fidelity. She adds an acceptance condition: the transfer succeeds only if the promisee has the capacity to accept it, so promises to infants, to the dead, and to people in irreversible comas are binding, if at all, only derivatively. I would like eight to ten interview questions that go beyond inviting her to restate the view. Include at least one question on how the account distinguishes promising from asserting, threatening, and contracting; at least one on the acceptance condition and its treatment of promises to those who cannot accept; at least one on whether the account can explain the wrongness of breaking a promise when the promisee never learns of the breach and never demands performance; and at least one on the wrong-kind-of-reason problem as it arises for the promisee's authority to demand. For each question, supply a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the questions should be answerable from the content of the view itself.
