I have been trying to write down what I actually take my credences to be in a universe where every quantum outcome is realized, and the verdicts I find myself giving do not obviously issue from a single principle. I would like you to find the principle, or the small set of competing principles, that best fits them, and to say where they come apart.

The cases and my verdicts:

1. A measurement whose amplitudes give squared amplitudes of 1/4 for up and 3/4 for down. My credence in up is 1/4, and I would pay up to $1 for a ticket that pays $4 if up. Two branches exist, but I do not weight them equally.

2. A duplicator containing no quantum amplitudes at all: at midnight it produces a thousand exact copies of the world, each containing someone with my memories. I give each copy 1/1000. Here indifference seems forced; no copy is distinguished, and I have nothing else to go on.

3. I am told that a measurement has two outcomes and that both are realized, but the amplitudes are withheld. I say 1/2 and 1/2.

4. I am then told that the amplitudes were 1/√3 and √(2/3). I revise to 1/3 and 2/3, although nothing about my situation has changed and I have learned nothing bearing on which branch I am in. I take the revision to be correct, and I do not think it is ordinary conditionalization on evidence about my location.

5. Quantum roulette: equal amplitudes, one outcome killing me at once with no further experience, the other leaving me unharmed. I am not certain to survive, and I would be exactly as anxious as with a fair classical coin. The argument that I can only ever experience branches in which I survive, so that I should be certain of surviving, strikes me as a mistake, though I cannot say precisely where it goes wrong.

6. Successor proliferation: equal amplitudes, but the up branch immediately splits again into two qualitatively different continuations, one in which I see a red light and one in which I see a blue light, while the down branch has a single continuation. I say that up keeps 1/2 in total, with 1/4 for each of the two up-successors, and that down does not get 1/3. So I do not count successor worlds.

7. The duplicator of case 2, made asymmetric: one copy will make no further copies, the other will make 999 more, after which everything stops. There are then a thousand copies of me on one side and one on the other. I still say 1/2 for each side, hence 1/2000 for each of the thousand copies, rather than 1000/1001 for the prolific side. This is the verdict I trust least, and the one that pulls hardest against case 2.

8. Boundary-drawing: an outcome whose description as one world or as a great many micro-worlds depends on where I draw the decoherence boundaries, and an amplitude spread continuously over positions. My credence should not move when I redraw the boundaries. So credence cannot be a count of worlds, however finely or coarsely the worlds are individuated.

Two principles I keep oscillating between:

P1. Credence is the fraction of my continuations that have the property in question — indifference over worlds. This delivers cases 2 and 3 at once, and fails cases 1, 6, and 7.

P2. Credence is the squared amplitude, or more generally whatever physical weight the world attaches to the branch, so that credence is fixed by a physical quantity rather than by any counting of continuations. This delivers cases 1, 4, 6, 7, and 8, and leaves cases 2 and 3 looking like coincidences.

Cases I have not decided, which I would like the principle to settle:

9. A quantum split with equal amplitudes, followed, in the up branch only, by a classical duplicator that makes three copies. What is my credence in up, and what is it for each of the up-copies?

10. Two continuations of me that are subjectively indistinguishable from the inside, with squared amplitudes of 1/3 and 2/3. Do I have two worlds weighted 1/3 and 2/3, or one world weighted 1, since nothing distinguishes them? I do not know what to say.

What I want is a precise statement of the principle or principles, each clause tied to the case that motivates it; what it implies for cases 9 and 10; and an honest account of which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate. I suspect I am running together a thesis about the world — that branches come with a measure of existence, supplied by squared amplitudes — and a thesis about rationality — that one is required to be indifferent among one's equally real continuations — and I want to know whether one of these has to be given up, or whether there is a single principle underneath both.
