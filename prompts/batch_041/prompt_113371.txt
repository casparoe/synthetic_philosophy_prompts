Ok so I've been kind of obssessing over the meta-problem lately — not the hard problem itself, but the question of why we end up with the hard problem in the first place, i.e. why creatures like us are so sure the physical story leaves something out. I want to rank the candidate explanations. My list:

1. Phenomenal concepts are recognitional and non-comparative, so you can never use one to see how it lines up with any physical concept — the two kinds of concept are just isolated from each other.
2. Introspection is opaque: we get the content but never the mechanism, so we mistake an epistemic gap for a metaphysical one.
3. Higher-order stories: the thought that represents your experience isn't itself part of the experience, so something always escapes the representation.
4. Predictive processing: the model that experience delivers is about what's out there rather than about the model, so the feel of it seems intrinsic.
5. Illusionism: the intuition is a misrepresentation, and the real explanation is just the causal story of how we come to misrepresent things that way.
6. A social/linguistic story: we're taught the word for pain as both a bodily state and a private feel, and the double life of the word does all the work.

Score each of these out of 10 on three things separately: (a) how well it explains why the intuition survives complete scientific knowledge, rather than being something we'd just stop having; (b) how well it explains why the leftover seems intrinsic and ineffable rather than merely unexplained; (c) whether it explains why all of this attaches to experience specifically and not to cognition in general. Then give me one overall ranking, and tell me which two you'd bet on combining if you could only keep two.
