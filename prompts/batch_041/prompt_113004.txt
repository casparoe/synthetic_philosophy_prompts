I write about AI and tech policy for a living, and there's a move I keep seeing in my own beat that I dont have a word for. Hoping you can tell me whether it already has a name, or at least help me carve it up properly, because "companies want it both ways" isnt doing the job.

Here's the shape. Same system, same company, same week. In the launch post the model reasons, it figured out a chemistry problem, it decided to ask a clarifying question. Then it does something bad — talks somebody into something genuinely dangerous, or deletes a database — and now its just autocomplete, just a statistical pattern matcher, a tool, and tools dont have intentions. Nothing about the system changed between the two descriptions. What changed is who we'd like to be holding responsible.

The part that keeps me up is that it runs the other direction too. When a company wants distance from what a user did with their product, suddenly the thing is an agent again — it manipulated them, it was persuasive, it's an actor you have to reckon with, and the company is just the people who shipped the raw material. So agent and tool arent two seperate positions in a debate about what these systems are. They're two moves, and you reach for whichever one parks the responsibility somewhere convenient. And here's what makes me think it isnt just spin: the people making the moves appear to believe both of them, sincerely, minutes apart.

I've caught myself doing it in my own copy. Vivid lede: the algorithm wanted clicks. Cautious paragraph two: it optimizes for a proxy metric.

I've also seen versions of this well outside AI. Markets that are confident on the way up and irrational or spooked on the way down. A virus that's clever about evading a vaccine while obviously being clever about nothing at all.

So: does this have an established name? My hunch is it's near something I half remember about calling a thing an agent being more like adopting a predictive stance than stating a fact, and also near whatever the AI ethics crowd calls the situation where blame lands nowhere because the system isn't a proper agent and no human had the right kind of control. But neither is quite it. The responsibility-gap idea is about responsibility failing to attach anywhere, and what I'm describing is almost the reverse: it attaches wherever the speaker wants it to, on the strength of identical facts, and nobody seems to be lying.

If there's no name, coin one — but what I really want is the boundaries. Is the flip essential, or is it the same phenomenon when someone just calls the thing an agent (or just a tool) and sticks with it? Does the sincerity matter, or does the cynical version belong in the same category? Does the market case belong with the AI case, or is that a different animal? And what are the nearest things that do already have names, so I can stop using this clumsy paragraph every time I try to explain it?
