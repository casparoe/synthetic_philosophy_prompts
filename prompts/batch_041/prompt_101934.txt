Can you write a dialogue between two philosophers: one who holds that perception is cognitively penetrated — your beliefs, desires and concepts actually change what you see — and one who holds that perception is encapsulated, i.e. cognition can steer attention and set expectations but can't get inside the perceptual content itself. Push them on whether that distinction is a real difference or just relabeling. (2) The one I actually care about: get them to disagree over what the debate is even about. Does penetration mean cognition causally influences perception, or does it mean the influence is the kind where your belief gives you a reason for what you see, so the percept becomes something you can be epistemically assessed for? Have one argue that on the merely causal reading the modularist can grant every alleged case and still win, and the other argue that the epistemic reading is doing all the work and quietly imports a normative claim the modularist never signed up for. Don't resolve it — I want the crux on the table. Around 800 words.
