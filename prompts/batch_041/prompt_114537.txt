im in a reading group thats been going down a rabbit hole and i need to write a closing poem for the last session. the rabbit hole: we started on the hard problem, then someone made the case that experience might be a basic feature of matter all the way down rather than something brains cook up, and now two of us cant stop worrying about where that leaves the ethics. if theres some sliver of what-its-like in a rock, a raindrop, a photon, then is there something like suffering in erosion, in thermal noise, in the vacuum? the usual move is to say the slivers are too thin to count morally, but nobody can say where the line is without just picking a convenient size.

what i actually want the poem to press on is the gap between the slivers and the whole. even if every bit of matter has its own tiny flicker, its a total mystery how a trillion flickers would combine into one experience — mine, right now, with a single point of view. thats the part everyone skips, and i think its where the real trouble is.

so: 16 to 20 lines, free verse, but with a refrain that repeats word for word three or four times. make the refrain a plain, flat sentence about feeling or hurting — short, unpoetic, almost a shrug. everything around it should be the opposite register: physics-y, external, relational, all surfaces — masses and charges and distances, things described only by what they do to other things, nothing that says what anything is like from the inside. so the refrain is the only inside in the poem and it keeps coming back unchanged while the outside keeps rearranging itself around it. the first time it lands it should read as a metaphor, by the end it should read as a literal claim about something too small to care about, and i want to not be able to tell which one i actually believe. dont resolve it and dont name any of the -isms. if it makes someone who knows the combination problem nod and someone who doesnt still feel the vertigo, thats the whole thing.
