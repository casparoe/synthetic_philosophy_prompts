The interest of the domain is that solving it requires slipping from one description of the input to another — a run of like moves, a turn, a rotation of the whole path, a single anomalous move — and the best slip can turn on one element; worse, when a route is symmetric, two descriptions coincide and there is no fact of the matter about which description the solver used. A single pair like N N E → N E E already supports several incompatible readings at once.

Give me the desiderata any theory of conceptual slippage in a microdomain of this kind would have to satisfy: the phenomena it must reproduce (I care about reproducing the errors solvers make and the cases where they disagree about the intended reading more than about returning the answer I had in mind), the distinctions it must not collapse — between the best slip and a merely available one, between an ambiguous stimulus and an underdetermined rule, between a slip forced by the task and one chosen by the solver — and the constraints it must not violate. Say which requirements are genuine and which are the parochial preferences of one research tradition, flag any pair that looks jointly unsatisfiable, and identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude candidate theories of analogy-making.
