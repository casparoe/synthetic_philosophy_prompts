Our lab's reading group has spent the better part of a year circling the same dispute about access consciousness, phenomenal consciousness, and global workspace theory, and I've stopped believing that another round of the usual arguments will help. I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, not a verdict on which side is right.

Here is the shape of it. One camp treats the global workspace as the thing itself: for a state to be conscious is for its content to be broadcast widely enough to guide report, reasoning, and the control of action, and talk of a phenomenal residue beyond that is either a confused way of redescribing the broadcast or a posit we have no independent evidence for. The other camp holds that the two can come apart — that a state can have a felt character while remaining unavailable for report or for the guidance of behavior — and concludes that a workspace theory is at best a theory of access, leaving the question of experience untouched. Both sides cite the same partial-report and iconic-memory results, one reading them as evidence of conscious content that overflows what can be reported, the other as an artifact or as evidence about something other than experience.

My hypothesis, which I'd like you to test rather than simply accept, is that the deadlock is largely verbal and turns on two slides. First, 'access' drifts between mere dispositional availability — the content is poised to be used, could be broadcast — and actual occurrent broadcast and use; the two camps may be operating with different senses while using one word. Second, 'phenomenal' sometimes names the intrinsic character of whatever is broadcast and sometimes names a further fact over and above it, so that one side's denial and the other side's assertion may not even be contradictories. If those slides are the whole story, the debate is about words; if they aren't, I want to know which of the remaining disagreements are substantive and where the fault line really runs.

So: diagnose it. Is this verbal, a disagreement about facts, a case of two camps answering different questions under a single word, or a clash of starting intuitions that neither side can argue for? Is a hidden empirical or formal question doing the work — something about what the overflow data actually show, or about what a theory of consciousness is even supposed to explain? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, an experiment, a formal result, a change of question — or is there reason to think none is coming? Please be explicit about which parts of this are settled among people who work on it and which are genuinely contested, and don't hand me a contested view as if it were the consensus.
