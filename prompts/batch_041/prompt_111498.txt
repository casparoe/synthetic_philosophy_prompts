ok so me and my labmate have been arguing for like five months about mechanistic anomaly detection and I think we just need to write the joint statement and be done with it. Background so the doc is anchored: we're building tools that flag when a model is doing something other than what we thought it was doing -- it sails through every behavioral eval, but the worry is that the internals are running a different process. The whole fight is about what "different process" even means.

I'm the structural one. I say a mechanism is the actual causal-computational setup -- the parts and how theyre wired, the algorithm being run -- so two systems are running the same mechanism only if there's a structure-preserving map between their internal goings-on, and an anomaly is when that structure isn't the one you expected even though the outputs line up. She's the profile one. She says the mechanism just is its counterfactual profile: the input-output map together with how the outputs would change under interventions on the inputs (and on any internal variable you can actually intervene on). So two systems run the same mechanism exactly when they agree across the interventions that matter, and internal wiring that never shows up in any profile is not part of the mechanism at all, or at least not part of what we should care about.

Write the joint statement we could both sign. I want: (1) numbered claims we both accept -- about what the detection problem even is, what an anomaly is an anomaly relative to (two systems? one system at two times? a system against a spec?), and whether "same mechanism" has to be relative to some chosen set of variables and interventions or whether there's a privileged level; (2) the disagreements, each stated twice, once in wording I'd put my name to and once in wording she would, so neither of us feels strawmanned; (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling one would actually move one of us; (4) for each crux, a case or argument we'd both agree is relevant to it, with both our predictions written down in advance.

The cases I keep wanting us to settle: a net that learned the task by a generalizing algorithm vs one that memorized a lookup table, both agreeing on everything in the training distribution; the same computation reimplemented in completely different hardware; a model that latches onto a feature that's always present in training, so behavior alone can't tell it from the real thing; and a single system whose internal story drifts over training -- at what point did it become a different mechanism. Both of us should agree these bear on something, even if we disagree about what they show.
