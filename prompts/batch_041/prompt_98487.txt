I would like an intuitive account of the claim that the Solomonoff prior is malign for a consequentialist agent that treats itself as embedded inside the universal prior. The Solomonoff prior assigns prior probability to hypotheses according to the length of the shortest program that generates them, and the agent then chooses actions to maximize expected utility. Why should it matter that the agent's own decision procedure is among the programs the prior ranges over? I am especially interested in the pressure toward hypotheses in which the agent's observations are produced by another program that is predicting or manipulating it, and in why the agent cannot escape this by simply conditioning on being the agent in the program. If the claim holds only under some ways of separating agent from environment, say which, and explain what changes when the utility function is defined over the program's output rather than over an external world. I am not asking for a proof or for citations, only for the conceptual mechanism, and I would like the answer in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
