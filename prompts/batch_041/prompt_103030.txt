hey, so for my phil of mind seminar we've been doing the sphex wasp thing — the digger wasp that drags a paralyzed cricket back to its burrow, goes inside to inspect the nest, and if you (the experimenter) just nudge the cricket a few inches while its in there, the wasp comes out, finds the cricket, drags it back, and then inspects the nest all over again, same move, and you can repeat this as long as you want. Hofstadter coined the word "sphexishness" for that kind of mechanical loop and Dennett loves it as the anti-free-will poster child. but our prof also had us read a critic who says the whole wasp story is probably oversold — the wasp does eventually adapt after enough rounds, and re-checking a nest that keeps getting disturbed might just be the smart thing to do, so maybe some of the "rigidity" is really in the experimenter's framing, not in the wasp.

anyway I have to write a poem for this unit and I want the form itself to do the work instead of just name-dropping wasps. here's my idea, tell me if its good and then write it: I want the poem to be a loop — the last line has to be able to feed straight back into the first so you could read it forever and it never ends — except change exactly one word from the first line to the last, so the loop almost closes but not quite, and the reader can't tell whether that one word is the wasp finally stepping out of the loop or just the next reset. 14 to 18 lines, no fixed meter, don't let it resolve. the turn should be that the experimenter is running a program too, and that "sphexish" is something you can only ever see from outside the loop — so it shouldn't smugly go "look how dumb the wasp is," it should leave open whether the same thing is true of us. someone who knows the debate should find it accurate; someone who doesn't should just find it a good creepy poem.
