So I'm working through Strawson's essay on the reactive attitudes and I think I've got the big move, but somehting's been bugging me. As I read it, his answer to the determinist is basically: look, we can't help having these attitudes -- resentment, gratitude, indignation, hurt feelings -- and no general thesis about causes is ever going to pry them out of us, so asking whether determinism undermines moral responsibility is beside the point. Fine, that seems right as a bit of psychology. But we obviously CAN switch them off, at least toward particular people. When my brother lied to me last month I made a deliberate decision to stop being angry at him and start treating him like a machine with a faulty wire, and isn't that basically what therapists and addiction counsellors do on purpose? A friend says that's what Strawson calls the objective attitude and that he admits we can take it, which seems to prove my point: if I can just decide to adopt the objective stance toward someone, then determinism can threaten responsibility after all, I only have to make the switch -- and maybe I should. Unless Strawson wants to say the objective attitude is always inhuman and wrong, which is plainly false, since you'd have to take it toward a toddler or someone with advanced dementia. Same friend also claims the real target of the essay is the optimist who says we should keep blaming people because it's socially useful, not the hard determinist at all, and that this makes Strawson a quietist who thinks the whole free will problem is a pseudo-problem -- is that the same as saying the question is meaningless? Can you untangle this for me? I've read the essay, so I don't need a summary of what he says -- I need to know which of my moves is the confused one, and why.
