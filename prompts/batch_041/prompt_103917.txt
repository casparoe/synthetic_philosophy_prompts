**Question.** Throughout, take a model of general relativity to be a triple (M, g, T) consisting of a smooth four-dimensional manifold, a Lorentzian metric field on it, and a matter field; the theory is generally covariant in the sense that if (M, g, T) is a model and d is any diffeomorphism of M, then (M, d*g, d*T) is also a model. Call manifold substantivalism the view that the points of M are genuine individuals existing independently of the fields defined on them, and that the identity of a point is primitive — that is, not exhausted by the metrical and material properties the point happens to carry.

(a) Set out the hole argument, and state precisely what it is supposed to establish about manifold substantivalism and on which commitments of that view the conclusion depends.

(b) Set out the standard response open to the substantivalist, and say exactly what that response concedes.

(c) A fellow student objects that the hole argument is question-begging: it assumes that the points of the manifold are individuals and then draws a conclusion about them, so anyone who individuates points by the fields has no premises to work with and nothing to fear. Assess the objection.

(d) Does the argument create any difficulty for a relationist about spacetime?

**Answer.** (a) Take a model (M, g, T). Choose an open region H of M — the hole — lying entirely to the future of some spacelike hypersurface, and let d be a diffeomorphism of M that is the identity everywhere outside H but not the identity inside it. General covariance guarantees that (M, d*g, d*T) is also a model. The two models agree outside H, and therefore agree on any data that could be specified on a spacelike surface to the past of the hole; they differ only inside H. Now suppose, with the manifold substantivalist, that the points of M are individuals whose identity is primitive. Then the two models do not merely describe one situation in two ways. At each point p inside H they assign different metrical and material properties to one and the same individual. So they represent two distinct physical possibilities that agree on all the data in the past, and the substantivalist is committed to an indeterminism: the theory does not fix the state in the hole.

The conclusion rests on two of the view's commitments. The first is that the points of M have an identity independent of the fields defined on them, so that d moves the fields relative to the points rather than merely relabelling them. The second is that determinism requires a specification of the state on a spacelike hypersurface to fix which field values are assigned to which points.

(b) The standard response is Leibniz equivalence: models related by a diffeomorphism represent one and the same physical situation. The diffeomorphism d is simply a change of coordinates on the manifold, taking (M, g, T) and rewriting it in a different chart; and a change of coordinates cannot alter the physical content of a model. The two candidate models are therefore one and the same model written in two ways, and there is nothing here for the substantivalist to worry about: general relativity comes out deterministic, and the hole argument shows nothing about the theory.

A substantivalist who takes this line is often called a sophisticated substantivalist. He retains the reality of the spacetime manifold but gives up the reality of its points, treating them as no more than convenient devices for indexing the fields: a point is nothing over and above the field values assigned to it, and so the points of spacetime, strictly speaking, do not exist.

(c) The objection is not sound. The argument does not assume that spacetime is substantival; what it assumes is that the points of the manifold have identities of their own, and that is precisely the commitment the relationist denies. The argument is thus not question-begging: it isolates the commitment on which the difficulty turns, and a substantivalist who rejects that commitment escapes the difficulty. That is the shape of a successful reductio.

(d) The argument creates no difficulty for a relationist about spacetime, since he denies that there are spacetime points with identities of their own to permute. He may even treat the argument as a consideration in his favour, since it exhibits the cost of the substantivalist's commitment.
