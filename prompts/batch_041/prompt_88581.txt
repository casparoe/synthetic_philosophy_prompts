I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about whether iterated elimination of dominated strategies earns its keep, and we ended up disagreeing about which part of it is even the live problem. Suppose I have roughly a year of theoretical time and want to spend it on the questions that matter most. Within iterated dominance elimination — especially once mixed strategies are in play — which open questions should I prioritize, and in what order? The candidates I have in mind are: whether a strategy that is dominated only by a mixture deserves to be treated on a par with one dominated by a pure strategy, and what the procedure is tracking in each case; why order-independence survives for strict dominance but collapses for weak dominance and for any ε-relaxation of it, and whether there is any principled iteration of weak dominance at all; whether each round should be run against independent or correlated beliefs about opponents' surviving strategies, and how much the correlated version actually changes the outcome; and whether the epistemic story — common knowledge of rationality — genuinely delivers the iteration or quietly assumes something stronger. Please rank these, say which are upstream of which, and tell me what would move an item up or down my list; I care far more about the ordering and its justification than about coverage.
