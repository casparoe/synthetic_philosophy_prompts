I'm the student rep on my district's school board (I'm a junior, I don't vote but I do circulate memos before meetings) and next week we have to finally decide the admissions rules for the new selective STEM magnet that opens next fall. Our lawyer says that after the Supreme Court killed race-based admissions a couple years ago we can't use race at all, so the board is fighting between three options: (a) no targets, just test scores and grades, (b) targets for low-income, first-gen, and kids from our two lowest-performing middle schools, or (c) my idea, which I got from reading about India's OBC reservations — targets for underrepresented kids but with a "creamy layer" carve-out so we don't count anyone whose parents are both professionals/rich, on the theory that the whole point is social mobility and not just skin color or a zip code. Half the board thinks (c) is unworkable and a little insulting, the other half thinks (b) will just get gamed by wealthy immigrant families who technically qualify, which honestly is already happening at our other magnet. Can you write the memo I'll hand out at Thursday's meeting? It should pick one option and actually recommend it, say what we should do this cycle vs. what we should study longer, give a real answer to the strongest argument against my pick (not just a strawman), and explain in plain language what the difference is between fixing the pipeline and fixing who gets in.
