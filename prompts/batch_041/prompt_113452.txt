I'm a grad student in astro and I just found out Kepler spent a bunch of his life casting horoscopes and defending a reformed astrology, which is messing with me because I've always treated astrology as the thing astronomy grew out of and left behind. I have to close our reading group next week with something short, and I want to read a poem in Kepler's voice. Can you write a sonnet where the volta is the moment he sees that the same geometrical ratios he trusts in the orbit are the ones he's reading in the horoscope -- that the difference isn't in the heavens but in who's asking and what they can use? Use the octave for his frustration with the popular astrologers and the sestet for the defense. It should be accurate enough that an early modern historian wouldn't wince: he thought the soul responds to geometrical configurations, he was skeptical of houses and signs, he believed the aspects mattered, and he kept doing it for money and maybe for comfort.
