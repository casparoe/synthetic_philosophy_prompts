My friend and I spent most of last weekend arguing about whether a rule can validly authorize its own amendment, and we have agreed to stop and write down what we can both actually sign. Draft that joint statement.

The two positions. One of us holds that self-amendment is genuinely paradoxical: a rule that governs its own revision must either presuppose the very authority it confers or defer to a higher rule, and since no system can contain an infinite hierarchy of authorizations, every legal order must bottom out in something that is not itself an amendable rule — an unamendable authorizing norm, a standing practice, or a fiction — so that calling this bottom level a "norm" is either a mistake or a metaphor. The other holds that self-amendment is unremarkable: rules are not sentences but powers conferred and exercised over time, and once an amendment clause is read as the rule in force at the moment of the amending act, the alleged contradiction dissolves, leaving only ordinary questions about who may do what, when.

The document should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, covering the distinction between a rule's validity and its amendability, what counts as a rule, the force of regress arguments, and what self-reference does and does not accomplish in a legal system; (2) the residual disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept, with any that are merely verbal marked as such; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions that, if settled, would move at least one side; (4) for each crux, the argument or case both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Work through the case that started the argument: an assembly whose rules include "any rule may be amended by a simple majority" amends that very rule, by a simple majority, to read "no rule may be amended except unanimously." Is the entrenchment valid? Then say what changes, if anything, if the assembly instead repeals the amendment clause outright, so that no rule in the system states how rules are amended.

No citations and no reading list; if you need a claim from the literature, put it in your own words as a claim rather than an attribution. State how confident you are in each part of the document, and flag the one claim you are least sure both camps would actually sign.
