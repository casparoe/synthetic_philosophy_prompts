You are a constitutional theorist and a political satirist. I have just been appointed private secretary to the first monarch of Aurelia, a small parliamentary democracy that restored its crown five years ago under a charter designed to make the institution purely symbolic. The charter contains a mechanism I now have to explain to the new sovereign: the Crown Consultative Protocol. Royal assent is automatic, but the monarch may issue a suspensive note, a public, non-binding statement that the Speaker must read into the record. If three suspensive notes on the same bill are issued within one parliamentary term, the government must table a consideration motion, which is almost always defeated but cannot be ignored. The monarch also has a weekly audience with the prime minister, no written record, and the three conventional audience rights: to be consulted, to encourage, and to warn. The previous monarch never issued a suspensive note. The new one wants to know when to use them, and I am worried that explaining the rules will reveal that the whole arrangement is a polite fiction.

Write a deadpan internal onboarding FAQ, around 700 words, for the new monarch and their household, titled something like So You’ve Inherited a Democracy. The humor should come from the actual constitutional logic: reserve powers, the difference between influence and authority, the choreography of neutrality, the split between the ceremonial and the operative parts of the constitution, and what happens when a ceremonial veto is exercised by a monarch who is popular but not elected. Do not wink at the reader; let the absurdities emerge from the rules themselves. It should be funny to someone who knows the literature on constitutional monarchy and legible to someone who does not. Do not include citations, reading lists, or quotations from real texts. Start by brainstorming three or four tensions in the Aurelian arrangement, then write the FAQ in numbered sections.
