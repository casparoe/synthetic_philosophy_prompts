ok so i need a poem for the time-travel / grandfather-paradox night at my reading group, and i want the form to actually do the work instead of being a costume the idea wears.

Write me a pantoum. Real one: four quatrains, lines two and four of each stanza coming back as lines one and three of the next, and the last stanza folding back onto the first (standard closing convention, or if you bend it at the end, say so). Subject is the grandfather paradox, but not the cartoon where the traveler shoots gramps and the universe deletes him. I want the poem to live in the thought that the loop isnt a trap so much as a commitment: the traveler's whole trip was always part of the past they went back to, so every line they try to add was already in the poem. Thats what i want the repetition to do — each time a line comes back it should mean something slightly different, because the context has moved even though the words havent. The line that opens as a warning should be doing something else entirely by the last stanza.

Voice: first person, the traveler. And i want them to keep circling the word "change" — you can only change a past you're not already standing in, so the moment you arrive there's nothing left to change. Also, i half remember some philosopher making a fuss about the fact that "he cant kill his grandfather" and "nothing is stopping him" are both true, just in different senses of the word can. If you can get that double feeling into the penultimate stanza without naming anyone, please do.

Keep it under about 30 lines (16 is fine), no rhyme needed, and let the returning lines carry the weight instead of piling up fresh images every time. Assume the past is a place you can be in — block-universe style — since the whole thing collapses otherwise.

At the end, add a short note, 4-6 sentences, that's honest about status. Which parts of this are actually settled (e.g. that the paradox is a consistency problem rather than just a spooky feeling) versus whats genuinely contested among people who work on it (whether it's a real contradiction at all, the "it was always that way" move, branching timelines, whether "changing the past" is even a coherent phrase, whether any of this bears on whether backward travel is physically possible). Dont hand me any one of those as the consensus, and flag wherever the poem takes a liberty or leans on a contested reading to make the form work.
