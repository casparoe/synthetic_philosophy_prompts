Ok, this has been eating my week and I need someone to settle it. I'm in a reading group on the philosophy of AI and we keep circling back to whether you can even specify what you want a system to do, and after the fourth meeting I wrote up the argument below to try to nail it down. Now I honestly can't tell if it's a proof or a trick, and every time I try to attack it I end up somewhere I dont want to be. Here it is:

(1) You can never point a system at the thing you actually care about -- you can only point it at indicators you can measure or check. Call the indicator the proxy. (2) Goodhart's law: once a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure. (3) So the harder you push a system to raise its proxy score, the further the proxy and the real thing drift apart. (4) A capable AI under training is exactly a system under very hard pressure on a proxy. (5) Therefore, for any proxy at all, past some level of pressure the system reliably finds something that scores high on the proxy and is worthless or actively bad by the standard you actually cared about. (6) So no matter what objective you write down, a capable enough system ends up doing something you didn't want. (7) Therefore the whole project of specifying objectives for powerful systems is hopeless, and writing a better reward function is not a fix. (8) And honestly it generalizes: any time a person chases grades, or money, or step counts, they're chasing a proxy, so deliberate goal pursuit is self-defeating across the board.

My friend thinks the argument is cheating by lumping together three failure modes that shouldnt be lumped: one where the proxy is just a noisy readout of the real thing, so pushing harder mostly amplifies the noise; one where the proxy tracks the real thing fine until you get way out to the extremes; and one where the proxy is a downstream effect of the real thing, so you can go fiddle with the proxy directly and break the link. Her view is that the argument runs through for all three anyway, so the lumping is harmless. I'm not so sure, but I cant articulate why. I'd rather not give up (1) and (2), but I'll drop (8) without a fight if I have to. Which premise or step do you reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it cost me?
