I am a member of my school's philosophy club, and I am trying to work out what our faculty adviser is really committed to before I have to present at next week's meeting.

For the past term the club has been debating the hinge of history hypothesis — roughly, the claim that the present generation occupies an unusually decisive moment in human affairs, so that what happens over the next few decades will matter far more than what happens in most comparable stretches of history. Our adviser will not say whether he accepts it. Instead he has been giving verdicts on the candidate turning points we propose, and he insists that every one of his verdicts follows from a single test, which he refuses to name.

His verdicts so far are these. The shift from foraging to farming: a hinge. The invention of printing: a hinge. The collapse of the western Roman Empire: not a hinge, because the ways in which ordinary people obtained food, goods and information were largely undisturbed and something close to the previous trajectory resumed. The Black Death: not a hinge, for the same reason. The development of nuclear weapons: a hinge. The Apollo moon landing: not a hinge, since a failure would have delayed events rather than redirected them. The Industrial Revolution: a hinge. The accidental discovery of penicillin: a hinge, because it might very easily not have happened, and its consequences ran for decades.

At first I assumed his test was simply the size of the consequences — how many lives, how much wealth, how much territory. But he denies that the fall of Rome and the Black Death were hinges despite their enormous toll, so magnitude by itself cannot be the whole test, and I cannot persuade him to say more.

Next week I am to present on whether we are living at a hinge now. Please tell me which distinct tests are consistent with every verdict he has given, stated as precisely as you can; what each of those tests predicts about our own moment; which test you think he is actually running, and why; and which further case, one he has not yet ruled on, would most cleanly separate the surviving tests, together with what each test would say about that case. If any of his verdicts forces a test I have not thought of, I would like to know that too.
