I've been going round in circles with a friend about the careenium — the thought experiment, which I have seen credited to Hofstadter, in which you build something brain-like out of enormous numbers of tiny balls (simmballs, standing in for symbols) tumbling about inside a layered structure, with the symbolic level supposedly emerging from the ball level. Her position is that any talk of symbols pushing the substrate around is a useful fiction: fix the complete ball-level dynamics, she says, and the symbol-level story does no extra causal work of its own. I lean the other way, and we keep talking past each other, so I would like to pin the disagreement down with a number rather than another round of intuitions. This is for a dataset I am assembling, so I want your credence, not a survey of the literature.

To make the question sharp, take this variant. Imagine a careenium whose ball-level dynamics are deterministic but chaotic, and whose mapping from ball configurations to simmball symbols is massively many-to-one, so that the symbolic trajectory is robust to small perturbations in the balls and can be realised by wildly different ball-level configurations. Stipulate the proposition as follows: P is the claim that, in this careenium, the simmball-level description picks out genuine difference-making causal structure that is not exhausted by — and would not be dispensed with by — a complete ball-level dynamical description; that is, the symbols are causally efficacious in a non-deflationary sense, and not merely a convenient shorthand.

Give me your credence in P, decomposed. I would like a rough probability and one line of justification for each of: the probability that the correct theory of causation (interventionist, difference-making, mechanistic, or eliminativist) assigns a non-redundant role to higher-level properties at all; the probability that this particular many-to-one, chaotic design satisfies the conditions of whichever theory you favour; the probability that causal efficacy has a determinate meaning in this setting; and any further terms you think belong. Then give me the combination, and finish by telling me which term you are least confident in and what evidence or argument would move your number most. Please write your answer in American English.
