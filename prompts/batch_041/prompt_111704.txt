A bit of an odd request, so bear with me. I retired from a sensory physiology lab a couple of years ago — thirty-odd years of single-unit recording, mostly monkey visual cortex — and since then I've been teaching myself philosophy, mostly the 20th century stuff. Wittgenstein is the one that stuck, especially the private language business. My reading, probably crude: a word can't get its meaning from a private sample that only I can inspect — the diary case where you jot down a letter every time a certain sensation comes round and treat the letter as the sensation's name, and the box with the beetle in it that everyone can peer into, but only into their own. Meaning needs a public criterion of some sort, and the whole how-do-I-know-my-red-is-your-red routine should dissolve rather than get answered.

Anyways. Here's what I'd like you to do. Take the later Wittgenstein and ask what he would have made of something that only came along decades after he died. Hypothetical — as far as I know nobody sells this yet. A headset records the fine-grained pattern of activity in someone's somatosensory cortex while they feel a peculiar buzzing on the forearm, produced by a strange electrode pattern and unlike anything they've felt before. It can replay that same pattern into a second person, who then reports a buzz in the same spot, also unlike anything she's felt before. The calibration is circular in a way that bothers me alot: the machine gets tuned until each subject says yes, that's the one, so the only criterion in play is the subject's own say-so. Here's the part I really want your take on. The engineers want to flip it around: when a subject gets replayed and says it feels different this time, they want to treat the machine's pattern match as ground truth and say the subject is misdescribing her own sensation. The company line is that the headset has finally dragged the sensation out into the open, so the two subjects can share a word for it.

Would the later Wittgenstein have said the headset gives him the public criterion he was after — the thing is now out in the open, machine-checkable, so a shared word for it is fine? Or would he say nothing has changed, it's the same beetle in a fancier box, and the company line is just the sort of thing that ought to dissolve? Or some third way? I want your best guess at what he'd actually hold, not a survey of the options, and tell me which of his moves you're leaning on to get there. And if you think my framing is already confused before we even start, say where.
