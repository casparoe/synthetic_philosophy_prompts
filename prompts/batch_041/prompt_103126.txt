Construct a case, to the following specification, that separates the three standard ways of defining corruption instead of letting them run together. For reference, those are: (a) any deviation from the formal duties of a public role, whatever its consequences; (b) the abuse of a position of trust by an agent who is meant to act for a principal, where the test is whether the principal's interests are set back; and (c) a distortion of the market allocation of a good that would otherwise be allocated by price. The case itself: two or three paragraphs, realistic — a licensing office, a school, a hospital — with no named countries, statutes, or authors. In it, a client makes a customary payment or gift to an official within a long-standing reciprocal relationship, and the payment is not simply the official's personal enrichment.

Requirements. The case must be describable twice, with one feature varied and everything else held fixed: a version in which the arrangement sets back the interests of the community the official serves, and a version in which it does not. In both versions definition (c) must return no corruption at all, definition (a) must return corruption in both, and definition (b) must return corruption in one and not the other. Nothing may be stipulated that hands any definition its verdict — the verdicts have to fall out of the definitions themselves.

Then, for each version, state each definition's verdict and identify the clause of the definition that does the work; say whether the three are disagreeing about the facts of the case or about what corruption is, and whether that disagreement is substantive or merely verbal; and say what follows for the claim that Western anti-corruption standards are universal, given that the participants have their own term for the practice and do not regard it as a wrong. Finish by naming the feature of the case that does the most work, saying what happens if it is removed, and setting out how someone who holds that corruption is one thing across cultures would attack the construction as rigged. Answer in British English.
