Write me a mock insurance policy — the full fine print of an invented product, something like "Famine & Food-Security Indemnity, Form 7B," sold by a made-up international underwriter to governments and aid agencies. Numbered clauses, roughly 800 words, deadpan legalese the whole way through, no winking at the reader.

The joke should be built into the product: the policy has to decide, in writing and in advance, when a famine counts as an Act of God and when it counts as a political act, because the payout is wildly different in the two cases. So the definitions section should quietly commit to an entitlement-style view of famine — endowments, exchange entitlements, the difference between food not being available in a region and food being unavailable to a particular group of people — without ever announcing that's what its doing. Some things to work in:

- a definition of "famine" with mortality and child-wasting thresholds, plus a note that the thresholds were "calibrated to avoid over-triggering"
- an exclusion for losses arising from "collapse of exchange entitlements," which the policy classifies as a pre-existing condition
- a rider for famines that occur while regional food output is flat or rising — the claims adjuster finds these confusing and the policy says so
- seperate clauses for export bans, requisitioning, and procurement quotas, each assigned a different liability
- subrogation: the insurer reserves the right to recover from the sovereign, with a recovery schedule ranking which governments it considers worth pursuing
- moral hazard: what happens when the insured government is also the body that classifies the event
- a cheerful customer-service page at the end, with a claims form

It should be funny to someone who's read Sen on famines and still readable to someone who hasn't, and the reader should finish it feeling that the natural/political distinction is doing political work rather than descriptive work — but the document itself must never admit that. No real quotations and no citations, obviously.
