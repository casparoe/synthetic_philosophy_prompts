I have been asked to set out my considered position on the ethics of bringing people into existence for a meeting of my school's philosophy society, so that the other members can press me on it. This is what I hold.

(i) It is always morally wrong to bring a new person into existence, even when the life in question would contain more pleasure than pain.
(ii) The asymmetry: the presence of pain is bad and its absence is good, even where there is no one for whom that absence is a benefit; the presence of pleasure is good, but its absence is bad only where there is someone who is deprived by it.
(iii) Nothing is good or bad in itself. A state of affairs can matter only for some existing subject, and an act can be wrong only if it wrongs someone.
(iv) Death is bad for the person who dies, because it deprives them of goods they would otherwise have had.
(v) I give a substantial share of what I earn to charities that relieve the suffering of people and animals alive now, and I regard this as a serious duty.
(vi) I feel no sorrow at the thought that humanity will one day cease to exist, and I do not blame or resent anyone who has children or intends to.

Please tell me whether this set hangs together: which of these commitments are genuinely in tension with one another, which only appear to be, and, for each genuine tension, how severe it is and what would have to be surrendered to remove it.

Give your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, ordered from the tension that most threatens the position to the least, with any apparent tensions that turn out to dissolve placed last. Each item should be one or two sentences. Do not add an introduction or a conclusion.
