Which made me want to fix my final exam, because I teach the upper-level seminar on Augustine's ethics and I'm tired of reading essays that just say "Augustine says all lies are sins" and stop. Here's the case I want to hang the question on, and it's not the usual one: a woman is hiding a refugee in her apartment, and an official knocks and asks whether anyone else is inside. She has two ways to protect him — she can say no (a flat falsehood), or she can say something literally true but designed to make him conclude the place is empty, like mentioning that she just got back from visiting her sister and the apartment's been quiet. Twist: suppose in both cases the official leaves and no one is harmed, and suppose she'd have told the truth if the refugee weren't there. I want students to figure out whether Augustine's framework can even mark a difference between those two answers, given that his definition of a lie turns on intent to deceive rather than on the falsity as such, and whether his absolutism about lying survives once the consequences are neutralized — or whether the real weight is somewhere else entirely (the harm to the liar, the violation of a duty to God, whatever).
