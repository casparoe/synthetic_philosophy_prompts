Write me a short story about a character who takes Berkeley's master argument seriously as a live constraint on their thinking, not as a museum piece from a philosophy survey. The nerve I want the story to find is the seam the argument is usually accused of sliding over: that being unable to conceive an unperceived tree and being unable to conceive that there is an unperceived tree may not be the same inability, and my character keeps getting dragged back and forth across that seam. Give them a job where unobserved things actually matter — an archivist, a night watchman, a surveyor, an insurance adjuster, something like that — and let the argument change what they're willing to write down, what they'll admit happened last Tuesday, how they talk about other people's minds, and what they do about the door at the end of the hall. Two things to avoid: don't let idealism turn out to be magically true at the end (no room rearranging itself to match the description), and don't make the character a stock skeptic who gets corrected in the final paragraph by the sensible materialist. If there's a materialist in the story, let them be smart, and let the thing stay unsettled. Also the philosophy should live in what the character does and notices, not in speeches. Dont bother with citations or references to the Principles, I don't care about the historical Berkeley, I care about the argument as something a person could try to live inside.
