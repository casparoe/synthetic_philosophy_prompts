Ok so I need help writing a speech, and it has to do more than one thing. I run a 45-person software company, and at our annual dinner in a couple weeks I'm announcing that we're going permanently and fully transparent internally -- every salary band, every decision log, every calendar, all of it visible to everyone, no exceptions, forever. It's real, not a stunt, the board already signed off. But the speech has to be honest about what we're admitting by doing it, because a good chunk of the room is going to hear we don't trust you, and they'd be right to hear it that way.

What I want to build it around is Glaucon. Not the version everyone remembers, the one where you ask yourself whether you'd steal if you were invisible. The actual story: the shepherd finds the ring and uses it to seduce the queen and kill the king, so it's regime change, not shoplifting. And then Adeimantus piles on with the just man who gets stripped of his reputation and tortured while the unjust man gets to be thought just. Their whole point is that nobody has ever praised justice for itself, only for what it gets you, and Socrates has to do something heroic to answer them. So my angle is: we're building the ring backwards, and I want to say that out loud. Total visibility is us conceding Glaucon's premise -- we are not going to run this place on the assumption that people are decent when nobody's looking. Fine. That's the admission.

But here's the ending, and this is the part I actually care about. I'm also announcing that we're keeping exactly one thing unlogged: the 1:1s. No notes, no summaries, no recording, nothing written down, ever. Not because we're soft -- because if literally nothing is ever unobserved, then nobody can ever do the decent thing as such. It becomes unobservable, and so unexercisable, and then I've built a company where the only virtue left is the kind that's performed for an audience. So the 1:1 is the one place someone could be good for no reason at all, and I'd rather have a place like that and not need it than need it and not have it. Our general counsel is going to hate this paragraph, so give me a version that survives her.

Write the speech. Six to eight minutes out loud, so maybe 900 words. Audience is engineers, sales people, a few spouses who don't work in tech, and the GC, who did an MA in philosophy and will definately notice if I get Glaucon wrong. Don't quote Plato at them, don't be preachy, and please don't end on transparency is a journey or anything in that register. And don't make it sound like transparency is going to make everyone virtuous -- I don't believe that and it would be smug.
