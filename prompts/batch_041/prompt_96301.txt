My older brother and his wife have a three-year-old and they've been fighting for months about how to raise him, and it's gotten weirdly philosophical, and they keep dragging me in. The setup: the kid is, honestly, kind of a sweetheart — he'll wander over and pat his baby cousin when she's crying, unprompted, and he pulls this distressed face when someone else gets hurt. But he's also a little monster — he shoves kids off the slide, he bit a boy at daycare over a plastic truck hard enough to draw blood, and he hoards toys he hasn't touched in months. My brother has read a small mountain of parenting books and his conclusion is that kids come out with a basically good nature and your whole job is not to wreck it — protect the good inclinations, go easy on the discipline, let the compassion grow by itself. His wife thinks that's sentimental nonsense: she says the kid's default setting is grab-and-hit, that the "sweetness" is just him having worked out what makes adults happy, and that the entire job is hammering habits into him that he absolutely does not have naturally — sharing, sitting still, being polite, none of which he'd ever do on his own. They want a verdict from me and I've been stalling.

The thing that's really bugging me is that when I actually press them, they agree about every concrete decision — same bedtime, same rule about hitting, same handling of the biting incident, same amount of "no." So I genuinely can't tell whether they're having a real disagreement or just dressing the same parenting in two different stories about what's underneath. And when I try to make it concrete they get even more into it: my brother says if you dumped a bunch of toddlers on a deserted island they'd eventually end up comforting each other and sharing, and his wife says no, they'd end up in a Lord of the Flies situation and someone would be eating someone else's snack. So what do I actually tell them? Is there a fact of the matter about what the kid is like underneath, or is "human nature" here just a narrative you hang on the same behavior? If it is a real disagreement, is one of them right, and what would even count as evidence — the comforting thing, the biting thing, both? The wife also keeps insisting that even the comforting is selfish, that he does it because the crying upsets him, and I can't tell if that's a real move or one of those arguments that proves so much it proves nothing. I'd like to hand them something better than "you're both right, it depends," which is what I've been saying, and they're getting tired of it.
