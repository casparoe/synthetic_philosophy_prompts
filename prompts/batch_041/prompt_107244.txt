I am planning a term paper (roughly 7,000 words, one semester) for an advanced metaphysics seminar on the special composition question — when, if ever, do some things compose a further thing? — and I want help turning a half-formed idea into a proposal. My hunch is that the debate keeps stalling because nearly everyone treats "composes" as a single univocal relation, whereas it may be better understood as a determinable with different determinates in different domains: the conditions under which atoms compose a molecule, cells compose an organism, people compose a committee, and sentences compose a proof may be related but not identical, and the search for one answer spanning all of them is what produces the familiar standoff among universalism, nihilism, and the various restrictionist views that let some pluralities but not others compose. Write the proposal: state the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer, explain why the existing positions leave it open rather than settling it, set out the method (what I would actually do week by week, and why this approach rather than simply defending one of the familiar answers), say what a successful result would look like and what would count as the project failing, and give an honest assessment of whether this is too much for one semester. Do not include citations or a literature review; I will add those myself.
