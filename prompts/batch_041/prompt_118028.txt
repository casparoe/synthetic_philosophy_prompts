ok so I'm trying to write a short piece on Strawson's Basic Argument and I have a feeling the draft is built on a cheap move, so I want someone to poke at it before I sink more time in.

My reading of the argument is the standard one: you do what you do because of the way you are; so to be ultimately responsible for what you do you'd have to be ultimately responsible for the way you are; and you can't be, because becoming the way you are (your character, your reasons, your tastes, even your capacity for deliberating) would always already have to have been done by a you that was already some way — so either it goes back forever or it bottoms out in something you didn't do. And I take it the point is that this doesn't even need determinism to run: indeterminism just swaps caused-by-your-nature for not-caused-by-anything-including-you, which is no better.

Here's the move I want to make. The argument is a proof that authorship is impossible, and it's a good proof, and nobody should ever have doubted it. The mistake is in what gets done with it. There are two different things bundled together in the ordinary idea that you're responsible for something: being the origin of it, in the sense of being the buck-stopping source that made you the sort of person who'd do that; and being the one who has to answer for it, in the sense that it's yours to give an account of, and that you're the kind of creature who can take the account up, be moved by it, and change. Call them authorship and answerability. The regress kills authorship dead. It never touches answerability, because answerability was never supposed to be earned from scratch — it isn't a property you have to have manufactured, it's a standing relation you're in by being a certain kind of thing that can be addressed. And I want to say that Strawson's rhetoric — that none of us, ever, is morally responsible — trades on readers hearing the conclusion as being about the second thing when the argument only ever got the first.

Then my ending leans on something I only half-remember, which is that Strawson himself isn't fussed about the practical upshot: that he's happy to go on blaming people and thinks the argument shouldn't be trotted out as a general excuse for bad behaviour. If that's right, I want to read it as him conceding my point, since the practice he says is unjustified on the authorship reading seems to be running perfectly well on the answerability reading, and he knows it.

So: is the authorship/answerability split doing real work, or am I just renaming a distinction thats already standard and boring? Second, does the regress really stop where I say it does, or is there a version of it that swallows answerability too — e.g. if my capacity to take up an account and be moved by it is itself just part of the way I am, doesn't the regress get its teeth into that as well? That feels like the place I'd get killed, so tell me. Third, is my read of Strawsons own attitude right, or is he entitled to say the practice can carry on anyway for reasons that have nothing to do with my distinction? Fourth, if you were defending him against me, what's the sharpest version of the reply — I want the objection that actually hurts, not the polite one. And if the whole essay is just the old complaint that the argument proves too little, wearing a new hat, say so, because then I'd rather write about something else.
