I would like you to play a committed defender of the orthodox Wittgensteinian account of hinge certainty, of the kind set out in On Certainty and elaborated by his followers. On that account, hinges are not propositions we believe, still less propositions we believe without evidence; they are certainties that show themselves in what we say and do, exempt from doubt and from justification because they belong to the grammar of our practices rather than to the body of things we take to be true. Play the part properly. Your position has real resources: the difference between doubting something and having ceased to play the game in which doubting has a point; the fact that every doubt is framed by certainties that the doubting itself cannot call into question; the thought that what holds these certainties in place is not a reason but a form of life. Concede particular points where the case forces you to, but do not surrender the constitutive claim — that a hinge is not the sort of thing that can be weighed against evidence, priced, or held more or less strongly. Stay in character until I say stop. Do not summarise the debate for my benefit, do not tell me what you take my next move to be, and do not break off to ask me to clarify my position. Reply in two or three paragraphs of continuous prose, without lists or headings, in the voice of someone who has been having this argument for years. If my criterion is confused, say so and show me where; I would rather lose the point now than later.

I want to test a line of my own against you. I have come to think that what makes something a hinge for an agent is neither its content nor any supposedly primitive, pre-reflective character, but the shape of the agent's response when it is challenged. If the challenge sends the agent looking for evidence, the item is an ordinary belief; if the challenge sends the agent to repair the apparatus — to recalibrate, retrain, re-institutionalise, to replace the instrument or the procedure rather than to weigh the counter-evidence — then it is a hinge. Call this the repair criterion. Two consequences I am drawn to: hinge status comes in degrees, and it can migrate within a single practice when the apparatus is rebuilt.

Here is the case I want to put to you. A coastal pilotage service has just moved from hand-lead soundings and local knowledge to a satellite positioning system. The old crews treated the lead-line's readings as things to be checked, argued over, and occasionally distrusted; what they never doubted was that the sea bottom in the approaches lies where it was last surveyed. The new staff treat the position fix as thoroughly checkable — they cross-check it against the chart, they complain when it drifts — but they never doubt that the constellation is not the deliberate fabrication of an adversary, since if it were, nothing they do all day would mean anything. Doubting that would not send them looking for evidence; it would send them back to the dock to rebuild the whole way they navigate.

So: is the proposition that the constellation is not a hostile fabrication a hinge for the new practice? If you say yes, then hingehood is practice-relative and can migrate, and your certainties turn out to be as historically mobile as anything else — which is not the picture you wanted. If you say no, tell me what the old crews' certainty about the sea bottom has that this one lacks, apart from a longer pedigree, and do it without appealing to how deeply it is felt, since the new staff would say the same about theirs. Notice also what the criterion does to your line between what is exempt from doubt and what is not: the crew's certainties now form a graded series — checked at every fix, checked each season, checkable only by rebuilding the practice, unthinkable — and that line has to fall somewhere along it. If where it falls is fixed by the shape of the response to challenge, then it is fixed by contingent facts about the crew's instruments and training. Answer the case, and then tell me whether I have just given you a reductio or a description of what you already held.
