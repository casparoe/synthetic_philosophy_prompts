I've been teaching myself type design for a couple of years, mostly by drawing alphabets badly and then fixing them, and there's a thing I keep running into that I don't have a word for. Maybe the word is obvious and I'm just not in the right field to have heard it, but I've poked around and nothing fits.

Here's the thing. How far I can push a lowercase a away from a normal a doesn't seem to depend on the a. It depends on the rest of the alphabet. In the font I'm doing now, the a is basically a circle with a stem on the right, and the only reason it isn't a d or a q is that the d's stem is on the left and the q's tail hangs down -- so one of those letters is carrying the whole distinction for both of them. With b and d it's worse: they're near mirrors, so the entire difference is a tiny flick on the bowl terminal, and I have to spend that flick somewhere, I can't just not decide. In a script face I looked at, a, o, d, q and even e share almost the same bowl, and what makes them different letters is only where the stroke stops and which way the tail flicks. And it feels zero-sum in a way I can't pin down: when I finally get b and d to read clearly, my p and q go mushy, even though I didn't touch them.

The other half of it is the weirdness budget. If the whole alphabet is condensed, my e can be way narrower than I'd ever dare in a normal font and still reads fine. Squeeze only the e in an otherwise normal alphabet and it breaks. So there's some amount of strangeness I can spend, but only if everyone spends it together, and it isn't a per-letter allowance.

The closest thing Ive found is family resemblance, but that doesn't feel right, because the boundaries here are pretty sharp (it's an a or it isn't) and they seem to get set by the other letters rather than by how close you are to some central a.

So: is there a name for this? If there isn't, I'd like one that actually says where the edges are. What makes a letter's identity come from the system it sits in versus from its own shape? What is the "together" in the budget doing -- is it just that readers get used to a style, or is it something about the letters relating to each other? To be clear I'm not asking about legibility research, that's a different question, I mean identity -- when is this shape the letter a at all, and when is it not, and who decides. A bare label won't help me; I need the carving.
