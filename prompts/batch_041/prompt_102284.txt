Write a joke about periodization. It should be a joke that someone who actually works on the reality of the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution would laugh at — the punchline has to turn on the content of the problem, not on the generic observation that history is written by the winners.

The tension I want it to exploit is this. The question of whether these periods are real things or later inventions is a local version of the older question of whether our categories carve the past at its joints or merely leave knife marks on it. Take the Renaissance as the candidate joint — the place where something genuinely new is supposed to have begun — and the Middle Ages as the candidate knife mark, the groove cut retrospectively so that the Renaissance would have something to be a rebirth from. The continuity thesis is the pressure point: if the kinematic and quantitative work of the fourteenth-century schools at Oxford and Paris already anticipates moves usually credited to the seventeenth century, then the cut is chosen rather than found, and the period turns out to be a claim about the present as much as about the past.

Have the joke depend on exactly that — on a period being a move in an argument about who gets to stand after the darkness ends — rather than on historians being fussy about dates. Keep it short. Then append no more than four sentences naming the specific commitments the joke relies on, so that I can see whether it is aimed at the real problem or only at the idea that periodization is arbitrary.
