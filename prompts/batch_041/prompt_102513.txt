I am currently taking a course on population ethics, and we have spent several weeks on the procreation asymmetry. As I understand it, the asymmetry combines two claims: first, it is not wrong to refrain from bringing a happy person into existence; second, it is wrong to bring a miserable person into existence. I have constructed an argument that seems to show the asymmetry cannot be correct, but I cannot identify which step to give up. The argument is as follows.

(1) If a person's life would contain more happiness than misery, then the fact that they would be happy is not a reason to create them.
(2) If a consideration is not a reason to create someone, then it cannot make creating them better than creating no one.
(3) Therefore, creating a person whose life is barely worth living—just barely more happy than miserable—is not better than creating no one.
(4) Nor is it worse than creating no one, since by hypothesis their life is worth living.
(5) So creating such a person is neither better nor worse than creating no one; the two are on a par.
(6) Now imagine a dial that continuously adjusts a possible person's lifetime welfare. At one end the life is extremely happy; at the other it is extremely miserable. Somewhere in the middle is a neutral point, and just above it the life is barely worth living.
(7) For every setting just above the neutral point, by (1)–(5), creating the person is on a par with creating no one.
(8) At the neutral point itself, creating the person is also on a par with creating no one, since a neutral life is neither better nor worse than nonexistence.
(9) Therefore, by repeated applications of the parity relation, every setting on the dial—including settings far below the neutral point, where the person's life is miserable—is on a par with creating no one.
(10) But the asymmetry says that creating a miserable person is wrong, and an act that is wrong cannot be on a par with an act that is not wrong.
(11) So the asymmetry is false.

I would most like to keep (1) and (10), since the asymmetry is the view I am trying to defend. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? If the answer is that I must give up the asymmetry, I would like to know exactly which of its two halves is the one that has to go.
