I'm a science journalist at work on a feature about the unfinished business of Bell's theorem, and I keep running into a formula that seems to be doing an enormous amount of work while committing to very little. One theorist, trying to convey why the foundations community is still arguing, offered it as though it were the theorem itself: "Locality, realism, free choice — you can have any two, but not all three." A philosopher I interviewed invoked the identical triad but appeared to mean something far weaker by it. A third source used the line to wave away a question I had assumed was still open. When I pressed one of them on what it would mean to give up "realism," he said that giving up realism isn't even a coherent third option — which is precisely the kind of remark I need to be able to evaluate before I quote any of them.

Could you lay out for me the distinct things that one-liner could actually be claiming?

- Take the three terms one at a time — "locality," "realism," "free choice" — and set out the separate things each might mean in this context. I suspect the formula trades on the ambiguity of all three at once, and I'd like the candidate readings kept apart.
- For each way of fixing the three, say what the resulting "pick two" claim amounts to: a theorem, a substantive but defensible interpretive claim, a near-platitude, or something false or empty. I expect some of the readings quietly collapse the dramatic three-way choice into something much smaller.
- Tell me which reading is in play when the line is used to conclude that "something has to give" — and whether, on that reading, the theorem really forces a choice among the three, or whether the framing assumes its own conclusion.
- Say which reading I should assume when a working physicist drops the phrase casually in conversation, and which one I should flag if I put it in print.

Please lay this out as a menu of readings, each stated precisely and given a verdict, followed by a short paragraph on which reading I should attribute in the piece. I don't need citations or a reading list — I need the map.
