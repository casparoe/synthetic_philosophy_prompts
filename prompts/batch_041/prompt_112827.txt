ok, i need a satire piece. the kind that a philosopher of action would actually laugh at, not wince at.

write a productivity-coach column — the kind that runs in a business magazine or a LinkedIn-adjacent newsletter, first person, about 600 words, with a title, a couple of subheads, a client testimonial, and a little "common objections" box. the coach's whole method is built on Kavka's toxin puzzle: the eccentric billionaire, the vial that makes you painfully ill for a day but won't kill you or leave lasting damage, the million dollars paid tomorrow morning if at midnight tonight you genuinely intend to drink the toxin tomorrow afternoon. money in the bank hours before you'd have to drink, perfectly free to change your mind after. get that setup exactly right, the joke depends on it.

the joke: the coach has built a whole business on the fact that the payoff attaches to the intention and not to the act, and he's definately failed to notice that this is also exactly why the thing is supposed to be impossible — once the money's in the account, the reason to intend has evaporated, so you can't intend, so you can't get the money. he keeps blaming his clients' mindsets. his case study (someone finally finishing their novel, say) should look like a triumph but quietly turn out to be the vacuous solution: the client already wanted the thing for its own sake, so the contract just paid him for an intention he'd have had anyway. in the objections box he should misstate the real objection — that you can't bootstrap an intention by wanting the payout — and wave it off as limiting beliefs. and the thing he's actually selling by the end should be an irrevocable commitment contract, i.e. you pay us if you don't do the thing, which abandons the puzzle's premise entirely without him ever clocking it.

deadpan the whole way through, no winking at the reader, no citations or reading list, and don't name any real coaches, gurus, or companies — invent the brand. it should be funny to somone who's taught the puzzle and still readable to someone who's never heard of it.
