Here's the thing we're arguing about. There's an argument — Scheffler's, from Death and the Afterlife, he calls it a conjecture — about why human extinction would be bad over and above all the deaths. The deaths only get you so far: if extinction happened after everyone now alive is dead, there's nobody left who's been made worse off. Scheffler's move is that alot of what we value in our lives depends on there being a human future, on people later on valuing things, including valuing the stuff we valued. His word for that future is the afterlife. So the badness isn't the missing people, or not only them — it's that our current projects would be drained of significance. He's pretty explicit that this is a claim about the conditions of our valuing, not a prediction that we'd all get depressed and stop caring.

Dan's objection: that's all psychology. If we're wired so our projects need a future audience, that tells us what we're like, not what's bad. He also says the future people are doing no work in the argument — if the loss is that our lives now lose meaning, then it's really a claim about us and the scary name is decoration. And he thinks there's something backwards about the value of my life depending on strangers three hundred years out; if the conjecture is right, he says, our lives were always less valuable than we thought, and that's a swindle.

I say: it's not a prediction, it's a claim about what it takes for something to count as mattering to someone in the first place — that a project only matters if it's part of something ongoing. That's either true or false, but it's not an empirical guess about our moods. And I don't think 'bad' has to mean 'worse for some particular person'.

Couple of wrinkles that keep coming up. If some aliens would carry on valuing things after we're gone, is human extinction fine on this view, or does it have to be us? Dan thinks that shows the whole thing is a disguised nostalgia for our own species. And there's a timing thing: extinction quietly in 300 years versus extinction now that we somehow never find out about — the conjecture seems to make those very different, and I can't tell if that's a feature or a reductio.
