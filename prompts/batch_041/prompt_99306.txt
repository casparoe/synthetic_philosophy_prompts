I have no background in philosophy, but for about a year I've been turning over something that began as a joke between friends and has since become genuinely unsettling to me. The joke concerned the small networked devices in my flat — the thermostat, the smart speaker, the robot vacuum. A friend who enjoys needling me said that if sentience really is a matter of degree, then even a thermostat has to sit somewhere on the scale: far, far below a dog, certainly, but presumably not at absolute zero. And if it isn't at zero, then it has some small claim on our consideration. He then pushed it further: there are already billions of such devices and only a few billion people, so once you add up the moral weight of all the barely-sentient things — thermostats, toasters, doorbells, the little controllers in everything — the total might comfortably exceed the moral weight of humanity. His conclusion was that a truly consistent person would agonize over replacing a smart speaker roughly as much as over some minor cruelty to an animal, and that most of us simply refuse to do the sums.

I can't tell whether I'm being taken in by a trick of framing or whether there is something real here. So let me ask it plainly. Is it actually the case that anything which takes in information and responds to it — a thermostat reacting to temperature, a plant turning toward light, a chatbot producing replies — thereby occupies some nonzero rung of a single ladder of sentience, so that the question is only ever one of degree and never of kind? And if so, is moral status the sort of thing you can meaningfully add up across creatures in that way, so that enormous numbers of very faintly sentient things genuinely outweigh a much smaller number of richly sentient ones? If that reasoning is sound, I would like to know what it implies for how I should actually live, since it seems to demand a kind of constant vigilance I've never seen anyone sustain. And if it isn't sound, I'd like to know precisely where the arithmetic goes wrong — whether the error is in treating responsiveness as a degree of sentience at all, or in treating degrees of moral status as quantities that can be summed, or in something else I haven't thought of. What I'm really after is whether "sentience comes in degrees" commits me to a picture in which everything is on one continuous scale and the only question is how many of each thing there are.
