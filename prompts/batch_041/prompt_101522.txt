I am a retired geologist — forty years of stratigraphy — who has spent the last several years reading philosophy in a more or less orderly fashion, and I now sit on the board of trustees of our regional museum. Our director has proposed a permanent gallery that sets three traditions side by side: the Trojan War, the Arthurian cycle, and the Exodus. The gallery's working title invites visitors to ask when a story becomes history, and the board must vote on the final framing and labeling at next Tuesday's meeting.

We are split. Several trustees want each tradition sorted into "history" or "legend" and labeled accordingly; the director wants each presented as a tradition with a contested historical core, leaving visitors to judge; one trustee says she will resign if the word "history" appears on any label at all. Complicating matters, our funding agreement with the state heritage office requires that interpretive labels not "present unverified tradition as established fact" — a phrase no one has defined for us.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate before the vote. I want it to recommend a specific, adoptable course: what the labels should actually say, what the board should settle at this meeting and what it should refer to a working group with a deadline, and a rule the board can apply to future galleries rather than one-off decisions about these three cases.

Two things I would especially like addressed. First, the three traditions are not evidentially alike — one has a plausible archaeological anchor, one rests on texts written centuries after the events they describe, and one is a founding narrative of a still-living community — and I suspect a single history/legend axis misdescribes all three, in different ways. Second, in the manner of my former trade, I have drafted a proposal to attach to each label an explicit statement of our confidence and its basis, as one would report a measurement with an uncertainty. The director calls this scientism; I would like an honest verdict on whether it can be made to work here, and if not, why not.

Give me the strongest case for the position you recommend against, not a straw version of it. And if the way I have framed this is confused — if the history/legend distinction is not the dichotomy my colleagues keep assuming, or if my uncertainty-label proposal is a category mistake — say so plainly rather than drafting around it.
