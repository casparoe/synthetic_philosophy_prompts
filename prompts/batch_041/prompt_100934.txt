You are a philosopher of language with a specialty in the semantics and pragmatics of slurs, serving as an advisor to a body that must reach a decision. Before you draft anything, take some time to think through the competing accounts of how slurs carry their derogatory force, and be explicit in your own reasoning about which account you are relying on and why.

I chair the editorial board of a university press. We are reissuing a memoir first published in the 1960s by a Black American author. The text contains a racial slur for Black Americans in two places: once inside reported speech, where the author quotes a white antagonist using it against her, and once where the author uses it herself in what reads as a non-derogatory, in-group register. Our copyeditor has flagged both occurrences and proposed replacing each with a bracketed euphemism; the author's estate objects to any alteration, and one board member wants to postpone the reissue indefinitely. We vote on Thursday.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate to the board. It should recommend a single concrete course of action — print, redact (and if so, which occurrences), annotate, or delay — with a timeline, and it should engage the strongest argument on the losing side rather than dismissing it.

Two constraints on the reasoning. First, I do not want the memo to rest on the bare use/mention distinction; I want it to confront the harder question of whether an occurrence that is not used to derogate can nonetheless retain derogatory force. Second, please work with, and where necessary against, a distinction I have found useful in our earlier discussions: between an occurrence of a slur activating a derogatory perspective and the writer endorsing that perspective. Assume for the sake of the memo that activation without endorsement is possible; the live question is whether activation alone is enough to make printing impermissible, and whether the quoted and the self-referential occurrences should be treated alike. Do not lean on any particular author or citation to settle the matter.

Limit the memo to 700 words.
