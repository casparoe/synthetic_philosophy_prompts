Had a two-hour row in the pub last week about whether the house at the end of our street is actually ugly or whether that's just taste, and my mate Tom killed it dead with 'well, everything is true in some respect, thats the Jain thing, anekantavada — reality is many-sided'. He then used the exact same line when I said his team lost fair and square, so I've gone from thinking it was profound to thinking it's a machine for never losing an argument.

Trouble is I've now collected about five different versions of what the doctrine is even supposed to be claiming, and they can't all be right. Version one, from a mate who does formal verification: it's just 'truth is relative to a parameter' and the mysticism is decoration. Version two, from someone who actually read a book on it: it isn't about truth at all, it's about objects — every object has endless aspects, so any single predicate is one-sided (ekanta), and the seven-way predication scheme (it is, it isn't, both, inexpressible, plus the combinations) is just the logic that falls out of that. Version three, from my sister who did a module on it: the point was never metaphysics, it was a rule for arguing — never claim your view is the only one, because dogmatic certainty is itself a kind of harm, and its tied to the non-violence thing. Version four, which I think I half made up: it's a fuzzy or paraconsistent logic where 'in some respect' is an operator on sentences. Version five, from Tom on his fourth pint: everyone's a bit right, so who cares.

What I want is a menu. For each precise claim that 'everything is true in some respect' could be making — from 'different people notice different things' all the way up to the ones that genuinely mess with classical logic or with truth itself — say whether it's trivial, substantive-but-defensible, or false, and tell me which ones actually give Tom his 'so stop arguing about the house'. And if the doctrine is meant to be more than a platitude, I want to know what's supposed to stop the 'respect' from being whatever you need it to be at the time; I've heard something about the respects being fixed in advance (substance, place, time, state, that sort of thing) but I might have that wrong. Don't just pick the best reading and explain it — give me the whole set so I can tell them apart next time.
