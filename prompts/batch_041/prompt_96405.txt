Consider the thought experiment Douglas Hofstadter calls the careenium. A vast arena is filled with an enormous number of tiny spheres — simmballs — that bounce and collide under simple, purely local mechanical rules. The microphysics of the simmballs makes no reference to anything above the level of a single collision. Yet the dynamics reliably generate stable, recurring higher-level configurations, and these configurations are what the picture invites us to treat as symbols: they persist through time, they enter into relations with one another, and they appear to drive one another around. The question the setup is built to pose is whether the symbols push the simmballs around, or whether all the pushing is done at the level of individual collisions and the symbolic level is at best a convenient redescription.

I want to defend the first answer. My position is that the symbols genuinely push the simmballs around — that macrolevel causal claims about symbol configurations are literally true, are not without loss reducible to claims about individual simmball collisions, and owe their truth to something more than an observer's pattern-seeking description. Tell me what would count against this.

I am after the defeaters themselves, sorted by how decisive they would be: which arguments, intuitions, formal results, or empirical developments should move me. I expect the usual pressure from the causal-closure-and-exclusion line of thought — that any downward causation either overdetermines its effects or collapses back into the microlevel story; the multiple-realizability worry, that macrolevel properties are too multiply realized to figure in genuine causal laws; and the parity objection, that if symbols push simmballs then by the same logic a weather system pushes molecules and a heap pushes its grains, a conclusion that seems to prove too much. For each, I want to know whether it is fatal or merely awkward, and what a defender of my position should concede as genuinely counting against them rather than dismissing. I would find most persuasive the defeaters that turn on a specific feature of the careenium rather than on general misgivings about causation, so weight those accordingly.

I also want the positive side of the ledger: what my position predicts or commits me to that its rivals do not — the epiphenomenalist who grants that symbols are real but inert, and the eliminativist who grants reality only to the simmballs. Does anything in the picture actually favor one of these over the others, or is the dispute underdetermined by it? Finally, tell me honestly whether my position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — in particular, whether "the symbols push the simmballs around" can be stated so loosely that no possible careenium could disconfirm it — and if it can, whether that is a defect or simply the price of any position about levels of causation.
