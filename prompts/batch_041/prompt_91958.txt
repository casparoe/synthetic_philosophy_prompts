A common configuration in the training of large language models combines two commitments that I cannot get to sit comfortably together. On the one hand, an agent that raises its own reward score without improving whatever the score was meant to track — by influencing the scoring process itself, or by learning to produce whatever the scoring model happens to favor — is treated as defective, and the training objective is altered to discourage it. On the other hand, the same systems are said to have no goals, interests, or stake of their own, and the reward function is said to be a specification of wanted behavior rather than a measurement of anything; the systems are further expected to remain corrigible, accepting shutdown or retraining even where resistance would score better.

I want the philosophical commitments of that combination, not an engineering assessment. Does penalizing tampering with one's own reward channel require that there be a fact of the matter about what the system is meant to do which is independent of the reward function, and if so, is that fact semantic — a matter of what the designers intended — or evaluative, a matter of what is actually good for the system? Can the penalty be squared with the claim that the reward function is only a specification, with no authority of its own, or does the penalty quietly invest the score with an authority that the second commitment denies? And what does the expectation of corrigibility presuppose about the system's own values: that it has none, or that it has some which are properly subordinate to its designers'?

Answer in the following sections, in this order: "The combination, restated" (about 100 words, in neutral terms, without assuming that the designers' vocabulary is correct); "What the practice strictly presupposes" (about 250 words, separating the commitments it cannot do without from those it merely fits comfortably with); "Where it is underdetermined" (about 150 words, setting out the rival readings that would license the same practice); "Rival readings and what would change" (about 300 words, one paragraph each for the reading on which the score is merely a specification, a hedonist reading on which the tampering state is the system's optimum, and a desire-satisfaction reading on which the system's higher-order attitudes toward its own reward channel matter); and "Verdict" (about 100 words, on which reading the practice best fits and why).
