Testimonial injustice occurs when prejudice leads a hearer to give a speaker less credibility than they deserve—for example, when a jury discounts a defendant's testimony because of his accent or class. Hermeneutical injustice occurs when a person cannot make sense of their own social experience because the concepts needed to understand it are missing from the culture—for example, a worker who is systematically undermined but has no shared vocabulary for the pattern. It also proposes a third category, which it calls testimonial marginalisation, for cases where a speaker is believed but not understood, and says this is the most serious form because it leaves no obvious remedy.
