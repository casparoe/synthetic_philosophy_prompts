I am trying to understand the hole argument in the philosophy of spacetime, and I have been told that its historical ancestor is the exchange between Leibniz and Clarke. I will paraphrase the relevant Leibnizian argument in my own words, and I would like you to help me see where my understanding is thin.

Leibniz argues as follows. If space were an absolute, real thing—over and above the order of coexistences among bodies—then God could have created the material world shifted by some distance, or rotated, and there would have been no reason to create it in one position rather than the other, since the two arrangements would be indiscernible in all their internal relations. But God does nothing without a sufficient reason. Therefore space is not absolute; it is only the order of coexistences. Clarke replies that God's will is itself a sufficient reason, and that the two worlds are not indiscernible because they differ in their absolute positions.

What I do not understand is the exact force of Leibniz's inference. Is the argument supposed to be a reductio of absolute space, and if so, what is the absurdity? Is it the absence of a sufficient reason for one arrangement over the other, or is it the existence of two genuinely distinct possibilities that are indiscernible in their internal relations? And how does this connect to the modern hole argument, where a diffeomorphism that is the identity outside a region but not inside it produces two models that agree outside the hole and disagree inside? In the modern argument, the conclusion is that manifold substantivalism leads to indeterminism, unless one says the two models represent the same physical situation. I want to understand whether Leibniz's argument is making the same point, or a different one, and what exactly the modern argument adds.

I am not looking for citations, a reading list, or a survey of the literature. I want a clear reconstruction of the logical joints of Leibniz's argument, an explanation of Clarke's best response, and an account of what the hole argument takes from this historical exchange and what it adds. Please explain the key step I am missing.
