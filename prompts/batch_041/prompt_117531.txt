ok so i've been lurking in the suffering-focused ethics corner of the internet again, and i keep running into this view people call tranquilism. rough version: the only thing that matters intrinsically is the absence of craving and suffering. pleasure and other positive states are at best instrumentally useful (they can loosen a craving, they can keep you functional), but they don't add any value of their own. the good life is a life with no craving in it. i find it weirdly compelling and also like it might be eating its own tail.

here's what i can't get straight, and i want the actual conceptual issue here, not a vibe check. 'the absence of craving' is not itself a feeling — absences aren't experienced. so either (a) the view isn't really about experience at all, and the good-making thing is some fact about the shape of a life (no craving occurring, ever, in it), which is a big departure from the hedonism it's usually advertized as a version of; or (b) it sneaks positive value back in, because the state you're actually pointing at when you say 'tranquil' — calm, equanimity, settledness — is itself a pleasant phenomenal state, and if it's the pleasantness that makes it good, then pleasure does matter after all, just under a different name.

also try these two lives. Life A: no craving, no suffering, but flat — nothing is enjoyed, nothing is wanted, it's just quiet. Life B: plenty of craving and some real suffering, but also a lot of joy and engagement. if the tranquilist can't say A beats B without invoking something positive in A, the view is in trouble. but if it says A beats B just because there's no craving, i want to know what work 'no craving' is doing that isn't either 'the absence of a bad thing' (fine, but then why is B worse on the whole?) or a smuggled good.

so: lay out the conceptual issue properly. where exactly is the tranquilist committed to locating value — in moments, in whole lives, in facts rather than in experiences? is the calm-is-pleasant point a real problem or a verbal one, and whats the best repair available? no reading lists or citations, i just want the argument. and make the very last line of your answer exactly \answer{smuggles-positive-value}, \answer{needs-non-experiential-value}, or \answer{no-real-problem} — pick one, nothing after it.
