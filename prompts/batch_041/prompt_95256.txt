I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, but for the past several months I have been reading around the debate over markets in things that many people find repugnant — selling a kidney, selling a vote, price-gouging during a shortage — and I keep noticing that the two sides are not really disagreeing about facts. They are disagreeing about what a theory in this area is even supposed to explain, and nobody ever writes that part down. Before I read another argument, I would like the specification itself: the desiderata that any adequate theory of repugnance — an account of when an exchange is objectionable in itself, as opposed to merely harmful or unfair — has to satisfy. For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether it is a genuine constraint on any such theory or merely a prejudice of one camp. I am most interested in where the demands pull against one another, so please flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied; if the list itself shows that no single theory can meet all of it, that is the result I want to see, more than any scorecard of which contender does best.

A handful of puzzles keep tripping me up, and you may treat them as constraints I want the theory to handle. First, the pattern of permission seems unpredictable: we forbid paying for a kidney yet allow payment for plasma and for genuinely dangerous work, and we are untroubled by paying someone to move our furniture but many people recoil at paying someone to be our friend. Second, the repugnance is usually felt by people who are not party to the exchange at all, and sometimes the very people we claim to be protecting say they would gladly take the deal, so a theory has to say something about whose reaction counts and why. Third, whatever signal the feeling carries has plainly misfired before — plenty of arrangements we now accept were once widely found degrading — so a theory cannot simply treat repugnance as evidence of a real wrong, yet it also cannot wave it away as mere disgust without explaining why some of our revulsion tracks something real and some does not. Fourth, I have read that paying for a thing can sometimes reduce how much of it people are willing to provide, and I want to know whether a theory has to accommodate that or may file it under empirical footnote. No reading list or citations, please — I want the criteria and the reasoning behind them. Write the answer in American English.
