After thirty years as a condensed-matter physicist, I have spent my retirement reading philosophy of mind, and I keep circling back to the causal exclusion argument. I would like you to build me a decision tree that sorts the positions one can take on mental causation, for a reading group of retired scientists who know the physics but not the philosophical literature. Please grow it from the logical space rather than from a list of -isms: each internal node should be one of the argument's load-bearing assumptions — the causal closure (or completeness) of physics, the supervenience or realization of the mental on the physical, the denial of systematic overdetermination, and the premise that the mental is itself efficacious — phrased as a question a scientist could answer without already knowing which position it leads to. Add one node of my own devising, on whether a position treats causation as a difference-making relation or as a productive, mechanism-level one, and say how that choice reshapes the tree above it. Each leaf should name the position it corresponds to (or be marked unnamed) and carry a single sentence on its least comfortable commitment; make sure the tree can reach non-reductive physicalism, epiphenomenalism, and a quietist option that denies the question is well posed. I expect seven or eight leaves, and I would like you to flag any pair the tree cannot separate and any combination of answers that no one occupies, with a sentence on whether the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Please write in American English.
