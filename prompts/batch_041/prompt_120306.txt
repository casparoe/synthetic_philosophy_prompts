I've got two years, a small pot of money and one postdoc to spend on heterophenomenology -- Dennett's idea that you take a subject's reports about their experience as a text, build up the world they seem to be describing (the heterophenomenological world), and then explain why they say what they say, without deciding in advance whether any of it is true. My working bet is to modernise it: treat that world as a generative model fitted to a corpus of the subject's reports -- verbal, button-presses, whatever -- so rival models get scored on how well they predict held-out reports, and maybe on whether they beat a model that never mentions experience at all.

Given that framing, which open questions should we actually take on, and in what order? I want the reasoning more then the list: which of them is upstream of the others, which are doable by two philosophers with some stats rather than a whole EEG lab, and which sound deep but wouldn't change much downstream. Things I keep circling: whether reports have any evidential standing at all given confabulation; whether the bracketing is even coherent when the model has to treat the subject as a believer; what to do about measurement that alters the thing measured (no-report paradigms); whether one subject's heterophenomenological world can be calibrated against another's or whether they're incommensurable; and whether any of this could ever adjudicate between rival theories of consciousness. Tell me which of those, or what I'm missing, deserves the time -- and what would make you reorder the list.
