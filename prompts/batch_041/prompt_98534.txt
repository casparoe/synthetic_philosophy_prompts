I'm a second-year PhD student in HPS and I keep bumping into the same one-paragraph account of how thought experiments work, and I honestly cant tell how much of it survives contact with the actual literature. Its in a pop-philosophy-of-science book I've got on my shelf, and I'm pretty sure I've seen near-identical versions in a couple of blog posts and a lecture handout someone passed around. It goes roughly like this:

"A thought experiment is an experiment you run in your head. Our intuitions have been trained on the real world, so imagining a scenario gives us the same kind of information a real experiment would, just faster and cheaper. The interesting question is whether that imagining can be trusted. One camp — call them the Platonists — says yes: in a good thought experiment we literally perceive the laws of nature. The other camp — the empiricists — says no: a thought experiment contains no new data, and knowledge requires data, so it's at best a heuristic that suggests a hypothesis some real experiment still has to test. Either way, a thought experiment can't confirm or refute anything by itself. That's why Einstein's elevator is really just a vivid restatement of the equivalence principle he already had, and why Galileo's falling-bodies result had to wait for actual measurements before anyone could know it was true."

I count at least two or three things in there that are doing a bait-and-switch, but I've read enough secondary stuff to be dangerous and not enough to be sure. Where I'm coming from: I definately lean toward thinking thought experiments can be genuinely probative, not just suggestive, but I don't want to just assume my side wins. Can you go through it claim by claim — what would a philosopher of science who actually works on this sign off on, what's a fair simplification, and what's flatly wrong? And then please don't hand me a menu of positions and leave me to pick: take a stand. Do thought experiments yield knowledge, and if so, how, and what does that paragraph get wrong about the mechanism? If one of the two "camps" as described is a strawman, say which and rebuild it charitably before you knock it down.
