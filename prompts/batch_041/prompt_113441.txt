I would like to check a solution I have arrived at for Kavka's toxin puzzle, and a generalization that I believe it supports.

The puzzle, as I understand it: an eccentric billionaire will pay you a large sum tomorrow morning if, at midnight tonight, you intend to drink a vial of toxin tomorrow afternoon. The toxin will make you painfully ill for a day, but will not kill you or leave lasting effects. The payment is for the intention and not for the drinking; the money is in your account hours before the time for drinking arrives, and you are free to change your mind afterwards.

My solution is this. What the billionaire pays for is a mental state, and mental states are things we produce at will: at midnight you decide to intend to drink, much as you decide to picture a beach, and the intention is thereby in place. The billionaire pays. Tomorrow, the reason for the intention having lapsed, you deliberate afresh — this time against drinking — and the toxin goes down the sink. The puzzle appears hard only if one conflates intending with doing.

This suggests a general rule. A puzzle that pays for a mental state is trivial when the state is an intention, because intentions are voluntary, and difficult only when the state is a belief, because beliefs are not; Pascal's wager is hard for the very reason that Kavka's puzzle is not. Two questions follow. First, is the general rule correct — are all puzzles of this shape trivial in the intention case and hard in the belief case? Second, if it is correct, why is Kavka's puzzle treated as a serious problem rather than as an occasion for noticing that intending is something we do? I suspect I am missing something about the connection between intending and acting, and I would rather be told what it is than keep guessing. Relatedly, I have seen the puzzle reformulated so that the billionaire pays for the disposition to drink rather than for an occurrent intention; my rule implies that this reformulation is where the genuine difficulty lives, since a disposition is more like a belief than like a decision. Is that the right place to draw the line?
