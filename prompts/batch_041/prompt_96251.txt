In a causal inference reading group, a colleague closed down a discussion of whether socioeconomic status causes ill health with 'no causation without manipulation': since we cannot randomize people into different levels of status, he said, the causal question either has no meaning or can never be answered. I suspect the slogan is being made to carry more than it can. Lay out the distinct precise theses it might express -- what 'manipulation' is meant to cover, whether the requirement is evidential or constitutive, whether it attaches to variables or to events, and whether it is a claim about what we can know or about what there is -- and for each reading say whether it is a platitude, a defensible position, or simply false. Then say which of those readings, if any, would actually license his conclusion, which reading people who repeat the slogan usually have in mind, and how each reading fares when the candidate cause is the moon and the effect is the tides, or when the cause is an unmanipulable genotype. Finally, note where a broadly counterfactual account of causation would agree with the interventionist slogan and where it must part company with it.
