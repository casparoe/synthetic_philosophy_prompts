For the past few years I've hosted a monthly discussion group — a dozen or so people, mostly readers of the same blogs I read, comfortable with statistics in the abstract but with no training in behavioural genetics or labour economics. Next month I want to spend ninety minutes on the gender-equality paradox, and I'd like you to plan the session rather than deliver the explanation itself.

Here is the difficulty the plan has to solve. My group has already absorbed the shorthand version of the finding — that more egalitarian societies show larger gender gaps in career choice — and has, I suspect, quietly cached two opposite reactions to it: so it's biology after all, and so it's a measurement artefact and there's nothing to see. Both are ways of skipping the argument. I want the ninety minutes to leave them able to state precisely what the pattern does and does not license, and what evidence would actually discriminate a biological reading from a cultural one from a merely artefactual one. The philosophical payoff — what follows about nature, culture, and justice — should be earned rather than announced.

So give me the arc. First, the order of the components: what has to be on the board before the paradox can even be stated unambiguously, and what depends on what. My own suspicion is that the opening move is not the paradox at all but the distinction between a relative gap (women are such-and-such a share of a field) and an absolute level (how many women are in it at all), and that most of the confusion lives there; tell me whether that is right, and what has to come before and after it.

Second, the carrying example. I had intended to build the whole session on the standard Nordic-engineering illustration, but I worry the group will pattern-match to it and stop thinking. Evaluate that choice; if it cannot carry the session, specify what the replacement must have and must lack — real or constructed, single case or comparison — and why.

Third, the misconceptions, in the order they will actually surface, with the exact point in the sequence where each should be headed off. I expect at least these: reading a between-country correlation as a claim about individual psychology; the overcorrection that the whole thing is an artefact and therefore empty; and the assumption that once option sets are equal, any residual gap is a preference and therefore just. Give me the fuller map.

Fourth, a device I want you to use, repair, or throw out. I have been chewing on a one-dial world — imagine two societies identical except for a single parameter of equality, and ask what the paradox predicts about the difference in career choice. I think it forces the question of what the paradox is really a claim about: the shape of a mapping, rather than a contest between biology and culture. I may be fooling myself. I also keep reaching for a clean way to mark the gap in the menu versus the gap in the orders, equality of options versus equality of uptake. Decide whether either of these earns its place.

Finally, the check for understanding — one closing question that would show they have grasped the levels-of-analysis point rather than merely been made uncomfortable — and the list of what I should explicitly refuse to cover at this level.

No reading list, please; the group can find sources itself and I would rather the session stand on the argument. Write it as though briefing me the night before.
