I am a novelist, and for some years I have been writing under the spell of a philosophical conviction that has only hardened as I work: that radical interpretation — the business of constructing, from the outside, an account of what a speaker means and believes on the strength of their behaviour in a shared world — is not merely a method for field linguists but a constitutive constraint on what a mind can be. On this reading, the interpreter's charity is not a courtesy one may extend or withhold; any creature we could recognise as thinking and speaking at all must prove to be largely rational and largely right about its surroundings, and the idea of a mind whose beliefs and meanings are wholesale alien to ours is not a hard case but an incoherent one. I have pushed this further than I suspect most of its friends would go. I hold that even the most deranged narrator, the most unreliable voice, the most culturally distant character is — if genuinely a character at all — one whose utterances can be recovered under a charitable interpretation, and that this is a truth about minds rather than a truth about the interpreter's method. I am aware that the argument is commonly propped up by the figure of an omniscient interpreter, and I have never been persuaded that the figure does the work demanded of it.

What I want from you is a map of this position's exposure. Play it straight: what would have to be true for it to be false? Which observations, arguments, intuitions, formal results or empirical findings would count against it; which of its assumptions could in principle fail, and what would the world have to look like for each such failure; and what does the position predict that its rivals do not? Distinguish the defeaters that would be decisive from those that would be merely awkward, and tell me which of them a committed defender would themselves concede rather than brush aside. I am especially interested in whether the move from charity-as-method to charity-as-metaphysics is doing honest work or is quietly insulated by qualification — and, if it is insulated, whether that is a vice in a position of this kind or simply the price of its ambition. Rank the considerations from fatal to faintly troubling, and be willing to tell me that the view risks nothing, if that is what you find. Please write your answer in British English.
