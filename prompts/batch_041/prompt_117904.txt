I'm putting together a handout for a small reading group that meets in a pub and keeps having the exact same fight. One guy says we should celebrate people who do well; another says the whole celebration industry is a con that turns everyone else's ordinary life into evidence of failure; a third says "that's just Jantelagen" as if that settles it, and someone else brings up the tall poppy thing and the American Dream and then we're all just naming countries at each other. It never gets anywhere, I think because everyone is actually talking about four or five different things — envy of what someone has, resentment of how they got it, the feeling that their success is being narrated as a story about *you*, and the suspicion that admiring anyone is a status game you can't opt out of. So what I want is a decision tree that takes a reader from their gut reaction to a named position, built from the logical space first rather than from a list of labels. Each branch node should be a question the reader can actually answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and I'd rather the questions be about concrete cases than about what people call themselves.

The cases I want threaded through it: a hometown friend who got rich and stopped coming back for Christmas; a colleague promoted over you for reasons you think were 60% luck; a scientist who's genuinely brilliant in their field and confidently wrong outside it; a cousin who wins something and then refuses to take any credit for it; a school that posts its top students' names on a board in the lobby; an influencer whose success is literally the product they sell; and someone who wins a pile of money and gives most of it away. Branch questions I want somewhere on the tree, though you should add your own: is the winner's advantage zero-sum or just additive (did they take something, or only get something?); is the reaction aimed at the person, at the audience watching, or at the system that produced them; is your complaint about desert (they didn't earn it), about effects (celebrating them makes the rest of us worse off), or about the admirer's own character (admiring this is corrosive to me); are you a peer in the race, a spectator with no chance, or a beneficiary of their win; and does it matter whether the community they left was actually diminished by their leaving, or whether the Jante norms are protecting the weak or just enforcing mediocrity. Also: does the winner's own stance change the verdict? A person who deflects all praise, or redistributes it, or turns it into an obligation on everyone else — does that land them on a different leaf than the same person who basks?

Then give me the leaves. Aim for 8 to 11 of them and don't let the tree get wider than 9 branch questions. Where a position has a recognizable name, use it; where the combination of answers corresponds to something nobody actually defends, put it on the tree anyway and label it unnamed, with a sentence diagnosing *why* it's empty — incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. I care most about the pairs the tree can't separate, so flag those explicitly instead of quietly collapsing them (I suspect the person who says the Dream is a lie and the person who says the Dream is true but we've stopped running it will end up on the same leaf, and I want to know if that's really so). One line per leaf on its most awkward commitment. No reading list and no citations — I just need the instrument, and the whole thing should fit on one side of A4 when it's drawn out. Also please don't just hand me the three familiar positions (admire / envy / systemic critique); if the tree doesn't produce at least two views that sound identical in ordinary conversation but come apart on one of the questions, it's not doing its job.
