I have a running argument with a colleague in the philosophy of probability seminar, and I want to settle it with a poem rather than another whiteboard. The topic is the principle of indifference and the way it fragments: the same event, described with equal plausibility in two or three different ways, ends up carrying two or three different probabilities, and each assignment looks right at the moment it is made. I want a sestina built so that the form does the work of that fragmentation. The six end-words should be the six things that a re-parametrization actually moves around — the object, the quantity that is taken as uniformly distributed, the parameter that the uniformity is imposed on, the symmetry that licenses it, the probability that falls out, and the question that stays fixed while all of this shifts — and the end-words should be concrete enough to recur naturally (a word or a short noun phrase each, not an abstraction like "uncertainty"). As the permutations turn, the same end-word should keep arriving at the end of a line that means something different, so that a reader who knows the indifference literature can feel the same proposition being re-described in each stanza and can tell which stanza is treating the space as flat and which is treating the parameter as flat. The envoi has to gather all six without resolving the tension; if it lands on one of the probabilities as the answer, the poem has failed at its job.

Please keep the poem philosophically honest: no stanza may rely on a partition that is obviously gerrymandered, and the probability that appears in a stanza has to follow from the symmetry that stanza actually invokes, so that a referee for the indifference literature would not wince. It should be readable by someone who does not know the technical problem — the poem should make them feel why "pick uniformly at random" is not one instruction but a family of instructions — while someone who does know it should be able to identify, stanza by stanza, which parametrization is being deployed and why the numbers differ. If it helps you avoid the three textbook illustrations that everyone uses, invent your own physical set-up for the randomization and keep it consistent; a concrete device (a rod, a field, a set of bins, a spinning pointer) is better than a bare continuum, and I would rather have one clean invented set-up used throughout than a tour of famous cases. After the poem, add a short prose note of no more than two hundred words mapping each stanza to the parametrization it uses and stating, in one sentence, what the poem is claiming about the principle of indifference.
