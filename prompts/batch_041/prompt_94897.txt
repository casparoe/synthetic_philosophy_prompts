I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about the Principal Principle, and I want to turn the dispute into the essay question for the final of my upper-level undergraduate course on philosophy of probability and induction. The case we kept circling is this: an oracle reliably announces the objective chance of some event E, but the chance of E is not independent of what one does with the announcement. Suppose the oracle says the chance of E is 0.4. The Principal Principle tells one to set one's credence in E to 0.4. But suppose that if one sets one's credence to 0.4, the chance of E becomes 0.6—because one's credence is itself a causal factor in E's occurrence, or because the oracle's announcement is self-referential in some way. Then deference to chance seems to require adopting a credence that undermines the very chance to which one is deferring. My friend thinks this is a fatal objection to the Principal Principle, analogous to the undermining problem for Humean supervenience; I think it shows only that the principle needs a qualification. He kept saying it is just Newcomb's problem in disguise, but I think that is a distraction. I want the exam question to force students to take a stand on this, not merely recite Lewis's formulation.

Please write the question, a model answer of about 700–800 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must contain. The question should be answerable in 90 minutes, and the total marks should be 25. In the model answer, I want a clear statement of what the Principal Principle says, why the oracle case is a problem, and an evaluation of at least two possible fixes (for example, restricting the principle to non-undermining chances, or revising it so that it applies to a 'chance' defined independently of the agent's credence). The marking scheme should list five must-hit points, with the marks each is worth, and a one-line note on how students typically get each wrong. Also, I want the question to include a short passage (which you may invent) from a fictional philosopher named 'Dr. Elara Voss' who proposes a 'Principle of Deferred Chance' that says one should defer to the chance only if the chance is not itself sensitive to one's credence. Students should assess whether Voss's principle saves the Principal Principle or replaces it with something else.

One more thing: I do not want the question to depend on students having read any particular paper or knowing any particular citation. It should be self-contained, with the oracle case and Voss's principle stated in the prompt itself. The question should ask students to explain the tension, evaluate Voss's proposal, and say whether the Principal Principle can be saved without circularity. Please make the question and the model answer formal and precise, as befits a final exam.
