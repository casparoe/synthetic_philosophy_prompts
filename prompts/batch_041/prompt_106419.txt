A friend sent me a blog post a while back that was trying to explain the difference between epistemic contextualism and pragmatic encroachment, and I saved the gist of it but not the link. I want to know how much of it I should actually trust before I repeat it to anyone. Here's my reworded version of what it said, so don't take this as word-for-word:

'These two views are basically the same idea in different hats. Both say that whether someone knows something depends on context. The contextualist says the word "knows" changes its meaning with the speaker's context -- so when I say it in a casual chat I mean something looser than when a surgeon says it before an operation. The encroacher says the same thing, except the context that matters is the practical stakes rather than the conversational one. On either view, someone who knows where their keys are when nothing rides on it may fail to know the same thing when a life depends on it, because the standards for knowing go up. What both are really claiming is that knowledge is a matter of what you can act on, which is why they end up as a form of relativism about truth: true-for-you-in-low-stakes versus true-for-you-in-high-stakes. It also says the famous bank cases were experiments that proved ordinary people's knowledge attributions shift with stakes, so that part of the debate is now settled empirically. And the moral it draws is that if knowledge can depend on non-epistemic factors like how much is at stake, then it can't be the truth-tracking state epistemologists always took it to be -- so the view is really a sophisticated kind of skepticism.'

Something about this rings alarm bells but I can't quite put my finger on it. I'm not a philosopher, just someone who reads alot of this stuff. I have a vague feeling that contextualism and encroachment are supposed to be rivals rather than the same view, and that 'relativism' is being used as a slur here rather than a description. Can you go through it claim by claim and tell me what a contextualist would accept, what a pragmatic encroacher would accept, what each would reject, and where the post has quietly merged two positions whose own defenders insist they're different? I'd also like to know whether the high-stakes/low-stakes example is being used correctly or whether it's describing one view and pinning it on both, and whether the line about the bank cases is fair to what those cases actually are. Then the big one: does the final move -- 'therefore it's skepticism' -- actually follow from anything true in the earlier claims, or is it smuggling in an assumption the encroacher definately denies? If some of the paragraph is just a straightforward category mistake rather than a matter of interpretation, say so plainly, and if the accurate bits survive the corrections, tell me which conclusions I can still draw from them.
