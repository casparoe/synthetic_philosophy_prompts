Give an example of a case in which an agent has no preference between two options — nothing to choose between them — but nevertheless has a genuine reason to take one rather than the other, where that reason is not a reason to prefer it. Then say what the example shows about Buridan's ass: does the paradox dissolve once we admit reasons for choosing that are not preferences among the options, or does it only change form? Write in American English.
