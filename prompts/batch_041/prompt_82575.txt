My friend and I had a long, dumb argument last night about whether generations are actually real and I want a tiebreaker. Her position: Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z are just marketing categories, horoscopes for people who read the Atlantic. My comeback: fine, but then why does every survey show Gen Z is way more anxious and way more online than Boomers? Thats a difference between two groups that shows up in the data every single time, so the groups have to be real kinds, and the only real question left is which generation is the most real — like which one is the tightest cluster. She said "you cant tell if thats the cohort or just being 22," which I think is a dodge, because if it was just being young then Boomers would have been just as anxious at 22, and everything Ive read says they werent. Then she gave me a criterion I honestly kind of liked: if generations are real kinds, you should be able to find them without knowing the labels in advance — cluster people by their attitudes, dont tell the computer their birth years, and the clusters should come out as birth-year bands. Which, fine, thats basically what happens; everyone my age agrees about everything and our parents agree about nothing. She also said the categories make themselves true somehow, which sounds like astrology with extra steps to me. So: given that the clustering works, are generations real kinds, and which one is the most real? And if shes right that theyre not, what is the actual argument, because "its made up" cant be the whole story — money is made up and it still runs your life.
