OK so I've been keeping a running list of commons-ish situations and my gut verdicts on wether they're tragedies, and I want to know what rule, if any, is generating them.

- Open pasture, no fences, any villager can graze as many cows as they want, nobody coordinates: overgrazed to dirt. Tragedy.
- Same pasture, but the villagers meet every spring, agree on a cap, and fine anyone who cheats: holds for generations. Not a tragedy.
- International waters, any boat can take as much fish as it can hold: stocks collapse. Tragedy.
- Wikipedia, anyone can read, almost nobody writes: it keeps getting better. Not a tragedy, though something feels off about how few people do the work.
- Antibiotics: each of us wants them when we're sick, and the more all of us use them the less they work for anyone. Tragedy, and scarier than the pasture.
- Rush hour on the highway: everyone would be better off if we all left later, but nobody is using anything up. I want to say this is NOT a tragedy in the same sense, but I can't say why — it has the same shape, individually rational and collectively bad.
- The shared kitchen at my old office: six people, one fridge, one sponge. Somehow it stayed clean. The shared kitchen in my college dorm: same setup, absolute biohazard. Same structure, opposite outcome.
- The shared streaming password among eight friends: I genuinely can't decide. Nobody's grazing anything down, but someone keeps changing the password and everyone resents it.

My provisional rule was: it's a tragedy when the thing is (a) limited and (b) hard to keep freeloaders out of, and the cure is always one of privatize it, regulate it, or let the users write their own rules. But the office/dorm kitchen pair seems to break that — same (a) and (b), different outcome — and Wikipedia and the highway don't fit the limited clause at all.

So: is there one principle that gets all eight, or close? If I have to give one verdict up, which one, and why? And what does the principle say about a case I haven't listed — a neighborhood tool library where you borrow a drill and bring it back?

Please be upfront about where the reasoning is weakest and tell me what would actually change your mind — like if the office/dorm kitchen difference turned out to be something other than what you're assuming. Keep it under 500 words.
