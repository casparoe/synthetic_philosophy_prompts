I chair the general education council at a large public university. The council must vote next month on a proposal from the philosophy department to replace our standalone symbolic logic requirement with a two-course sequence, Reasoning as Practice. The proposal's rationale is a familiar regress: if we justify an inference by adding a premise that licenses it, the same question arises for that premise, so no finite set of premises can compel a conclusion without an act of inference that is not itself a premise. The proponents conclude that deduction is not justified by rules treated as premises; it is justified by training in a practice of going on, and that this is what the curriculum should teach. The opponents say the regress is a philosopher's puzzle, not a pedagogical reason, and that dropping explicit formal rules will leave students unable to distinguish valid from invalid arguments in unfamiliar cases. The pilot would run for three years, with a transfer assessment comparing pilot students to students in the old requirement on a novel argument whose validity depends on a rule neither group was explicitly taught. I need to circulate a written opinion before the vote. Please draft it: recommend a concrete decision -- approve the pilot, reject it, or approve with conditions -- with a time frame and the reasoning for it, and engage the opponents' strongest consideration seriously rather than dismissing it as conservatism about requirements. The memo should also say what the council would be committing itself to, philosophically, if it accepts the proposal's account of what justifies deduction, and what it would be committing itself to if it rejects that account but still wants to improve the logic requirement. Do not include a reading list or citations; the council needs an actionable opinion.
