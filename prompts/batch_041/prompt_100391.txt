Me and a friend have been going back and forth about whether we should care about insects, and I can't tell if her argument is clever or a con. Here's how it goes. She says there are something like ten quintillion insects alive at any given moment — she looked it up — and even if the odds that any one insect is actually sentient are tiny, like one in a million, you just multiply it out and you get ten trillion sentient beings, which blows the number of humans and farm animals and everything else completely out of the water, so the total amount of suffering in the insect world is staggering and we should be putting most of our moral effort there instead of into, you know, chickens.

My gut says something's off, but it's not the numbers and it's not just that I doubt we can ever know the probability. The part that bugs me is that the argument seems to work no matter what the probability is. Swap insects for nematodes, or for individual cells, dial the sentience chance down to one in a googol, and you still get the same conclusion, because the population is always big enough to outrun however small you make the chance. That feels like it should be a clue that the whole shape of the argument is doing the work, not any fact about insects.

Is there a name for this move — where you take an enormous number of things, multiply by a tiny probability that each one has some morally relevant property, and let the product do the persuading? I don't think it's just the "people are bad with big numbers" point, and I don't think it's the same as the old wager argument for believing in God, because there the trade is a small finite cost against an infinite payoff, whereas here nothing is infinite, the count is just very large and the chance very small. So it's near a couple of things I half-know but doesn't seem to be either of them.

What I actually want is the carving, not a label. If there's an established term, great, but tell me what it covers and what it doesn't: when is this a perfectly legitimate calculation that I should just accept, and when is it a trick that anyone can run to reach whatever conclusion they like? Where's the line between a big number times a small chance being a real reason to act and being a license to believe anything? And if there's no established name, coin one and define it for me, with the edge cases spelled out — I'll be annoyed if you just hand me a word and stop there.
