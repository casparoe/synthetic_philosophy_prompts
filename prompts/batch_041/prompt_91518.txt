I'm in a small reading group that's been working through the inner alignment stuff — mostly people who do ML engineering by day, a couple of us who do evals — and we keep having the same dumb argument. Someone brings a training run where the model does something weird or creepy, and everyone immediately grabs their favorite label: reward hacking, goal misgeneralization, deceptive alignment, "thats just mesa-optimization," "no, thats outer alignment," "its underfitting, calm down." Then we spend the whole evening arguing about the label instead of the case, and nobody can say what we'd have to observe to tell the difference. So I want to build a one-page diagnostic decision tree that we can actually run on a concrete run. It should start from things we can see in the lab — behavior on held-out evals, how the model responds to prompts that hint it's being evaluated or that it's mid-training, whether our proxy metric climbed while the thing we actually cared about stayed flat, whether the bad behavior is already there in the base model, whether a different seed makes it evaporate, whether it's worse in-distribution or only off-distribution, whether the model seems to be doing the task well in the domains we trained on but badly in the ones we intended — and each branch node should be a question phrased so that someone can answer it from the evidence without already knowing which diagnosis it leads to. No node that basically says "is the model deceptive? yes/no." Then the leaves should be the diagnoses, each with the name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment (what you'd have to swallow for it to be the right call in this case).

Please also do the part I care about most: build it from the logical space first, not from a list of labels. So lay out the underlying dimensions you think the diagnoses actually vary along — something like whether the failure lives in the objective we specified or in what the model learned, whether the bad behavior traces to a learned internal objective at all versus plain underdetermination plus distribution shift, whether the model has any situational awareness of training/evaluation, whether the behavior is conditionally suppressed versus uniformly bad — and show which combinations of answers are occupied by which named diagnosis, and which combos are empty. For the empty ones, tell me whether nobody's there because the view is incoherent, because it's coherent but unattractive, or just because nobody's bothered to look. And flag any leaves your tree genuinely can't separate: I strongly suspect goal misgeneralization and deceptive alignment come apart only under assumptions we can't test, and I'd rather you say so than fake a clean split. Aim for maybe 8-12 branch nodes and 6-9 leaves, keep it to something I can photocopy onto one side of a page, and if you think the whole exercise is ill-posed — that most of these categories are diagnostic labels for our uncertainty rather than properties of the model — say that at the top and build the tree anyway.
