My friend Ines restores sculpture for a living, bronzes mostly, and she has extremely firm views about when something is "still the same piece." I've started writing her rulings down, because they clearly add up to some rule and I can't work out which one. Squash a bronze figure flat under a falling beam, no metal lost: "The figure's gone, you'd have to recast it. But that's the same bronze — I'd still call it the same bronze." Cut the chipped beak off and weld on a newly cast one, old metal scrapped: "Still the same figure. The original bronze is gone though, that lump doesn't exist any more, and there's new metal on it now." Replace the whole surface of a piece over forty years, one patch at a time, until none of the original bronze is left anywhere: "Same figure." Melt it down and recast it in the original mold so it comes out looking exactly like it did before: "Not the same figure, melting it broke the history. Same bronze, though, all of it." And when a collector had a fresh one cast from the original mold in new metal she said: "Not the same figure and not the same bronze, but it's the same work — that's what the mold is for."

Next week she has to rule on a case I actually care about, and I'd like to know what she'll say before she does. A bronze figure gets dropped in transit and breaks cleanly into three pieces at the ankles and the wrist — no metal lost, and she repairs it eight months later by gluing the pieces back with epoxy, which obviously isn't bronze, so as far as the metal goes nothing has been added or taken away. Was it the same figure once it was back together? Same bronze? And while it was sitting in three pieces in a crate for those eight months, was it still one thing or was it three? I'm less interested in her yes or no than in the rule she's actually running, whether there's a second rule that fits every ruling above just as well, what each of those rules predicts about the broken-and-glued case, which one I should bet on and why, and what further case she'd have to rule on to show the difference between them — ideally something I could put to her without it sounding like a trick question.
