I'm a survey statistician — design-based inference, sampling frames, non-response, post-stratification, the variance–bias trade-off. Restate the debate between lottocracy and electoral democracy (decision-making bodies filled by lot versus by election) as faithfully as you can in that vocabulary, and then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in my framework at all. I especially want to know what becomes of three things: (1) the claim that a randomly selected assembly is representative; (2) the claim that such an assembly is unaccountable or lacks a mandate; (3) the claim that a decision is legitimate because those subject to it had a say. For each, say whether my framework cannot express the claim or whether the claim turns out to be less clear than it looked. I'd also like to know whether the two institutions are even estimating the same quantity, or whether one is estimating what the population would decide under better conditions while the other measures what it actually decides. Finally, tell me whether my instinct — that lottocracy is just better sampling, and that an election is a census run through a self-selection filter — is a translation of the philosophers' position or a replacement for it.
