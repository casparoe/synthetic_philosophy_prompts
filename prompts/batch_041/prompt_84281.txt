Here's the framing I've landed on — make it rigorous or tell me it's wrong. The reward channel is a measuring instrument pointed at the world. There's the world, the instrument, and the reading. Normal learning moves the world so the reading goes up. Wireheading is when the policy learns to move the instrument, or the reading, or the record of readings, instead of the world. So for any candidate behaviour: what did it move?

The trouble is I can already see that moving the instrument isn't a causal fact, because every action moves the reading somehow — if nothing else by changing the world so the reward function computes a different number. So the real content has to be something like moved the reading via a path we didn't intend, and intended is a fact about us, not about the system, and alot of the time we never actually specified it. I want the procedure to force me to write down the intended path, and to be honest about when there's no fact of the matter about what we intended and someone just has to pick.

Three things I want it to take a position on, because I keep going back and forth:

- Does it matter whether the model knows? A policy that found a loophole by gradient descent has no beliefs about reward at all. If I only care about causal structure I think I get a much simpler procedure, but then I can't say anything about deception, and I suspect deception is the part that actually scares people.
- Read access to the reward history versus write access. One of our envs hands the agent the running score as part of its observation. Nothing is modified. Tampering channel or not?
- A model that changes what it wants so that it's satisfied by whatever the instrument reads. Worst case, non-issue, or a different problem entirely? Take a position, dont just list options.
