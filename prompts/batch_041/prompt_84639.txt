hey, i'm in this philosophy club at my high school and we're putting out a little zine and i signed up for the "funny but has to be right" slot. my advisor actually reads the stuff and will 100% know if i hand in jokes that get the argument wrong, so i want you to write it for me.

the thing i want to make fun of is the argument that my decision to eat the last donut can't really cause anything, because the physical stuff in my brain already caused my hand to move, and if my decision were also a cause then we'd have two causes for one effect, which is supposed to be bad, so the decision just rides along while the neurons do the work. i think thats kind of crazy but the way to make fun of it is to take it dead seriously.

so write it as a letter from the claims department of an insurance company, call it Meridian Mutual Casualty, denying a guy's claim. the guy says the person who punched him caused his broken nose. the adjuster denies it using the exclusion argument, clause by clause, in boring insurance language, totally deadpan, no winking, no "of course this is silly." like 500 to 600 words, numbered clauses, plus an appeals section at the end.

stuff i want in it:

1. a clause saying the puncher's decision to punch is "supervenient" on the brain state, which is a fancy way of saying it comes along for free and adds no extra oomph, so it gets excluded.
2. a clause using causal closure in plain words: every physical event has a sufficient physical cause, so once the brain event is fixed there's no room left for the decision to do anything.
3. then the move i actually care about: the adjuster keeps going. the brain event is itself caused by what its parts are doing, and those parts by their parts, all the way down, so the letter ends up denying the claim on the grounds that literally nothing at the level of people, or bodies, or even cells is a cause. only the smallest physical stuff causes anything.
4. somewhere in there, one sentence where the adjuster notices this is bad for the insurance industry as a whole and then just moves on to the next clause like nothing happened.
5. an appeal form that asks the claimant to prove his decision moved his own hand "without any physical event being a sufficient cause of it" -- which is impossible -- so the appeal is rigged from the start.
6. maybe a footnote where the adjuster notes that under this reasoning the denial letter itself was not caused by any decision of theirs.

don't explain the argument at the end and don't turn it into a lecture, just the letter. also please dont use the word "epiphenomenal" anywhere, the whole joke is that they never use the fancy word for it. thanks!!
