I would like to get clear about the paradox of tragedy, because I suspect the whole puzzle is an artifact of a confusion. The paradox is usually stated as the fact that we voluntarily seek out tragedies even though they make us sad, and sadness is something we ordinarily shun — which sounds contradictory. But it only sounds contradictory, I take it, if the sadness is real. What we actually feel when we are moved by a tragedy is not ordinary sadness but a kind of aesthetic or as-if sadness, an emotion we savour rather than suffer, and once that is granted there is nothing left to puzzle over: we are not really undergoing anything we would avoid, so there is no conflict to resolve. My question, then, is whether the paradox survives at all once we stop conflating real grief with the emotions that fiction arouses — or whether I have merely relabelled the very thing that stood in need of explanation.
