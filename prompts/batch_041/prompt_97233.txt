For a 90-minute session of an upper-level seminar in social philosophy, sixteen students, I want a runnable activity that makes the group feel, from the inside, how a convention arises and then hardens into a norm, with the phenomenon emerging from the rules and incentives rather than from anything I say up front. By the end I want them to have lived through three things and to be able to point to the moments where each happened: first, that a purely arbitrary regularity can become the thing everyone does without anyone agreeing to it, and without anyone being able to justify that regularity over an equally workable alternative; second, that what stabilizes it is not shared information but the mutual expectation that the others will conform, so that the group's confidence rather than its knowledge is doing the work; and third, that a regularity can outlive the coordination problem that produced it, once deviations become punishable or once people begin preferring to conform regardless of what the others actually do.

Design a repeated interaction, with no talking during play, in which two or three options are symmetric, so that no option is more natural or salient than the others and a focal point has to do the bootstrapping if the group is to converge at all. Use a points scheme, no real money. Partway through, change the payoffs so that the option the group has settled on is no longer the one that pays best, and give a few secretly briefed participants a reason to start using the alternative; let the rest respond either by switching or by spending points to penalize the deviators. Then run a final phase in which penalties become impossible, to see what survives. I need the rules and materials, a minute-by-minute timeline, the payoff structure in a form I can hand out, the briefing for the planted minority, a debrief that maps each moment of play onto the corresponding piece of the account (arbitrariness, mutual expectation, common knowledge, the shift from regularity to enforceable norm, path dependence), and an honest paragraph on which features of that account the activity cannot reproduce in a room of sixteen over ninety minutes and that I will have to supply in speech. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it.
