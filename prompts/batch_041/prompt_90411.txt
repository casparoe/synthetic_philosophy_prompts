I write about science and ideas, and I have been handed a year and four long features to spend on one question: why do people vote at all, given how little any single ballot can change? I want a defended agenda rather than a survey. Which open questions in this literature most deserve sustained attention, and in what order? Start by disentangling what the label covers, since I keep finding the puzzle about pivotal votes filed together with the one about cyclical majorities and I suspect the two need different reporting. Then tell me the dependency structure: does the paradox survive once votes are modeled as expression or as consumption rather than as instruments, or does that move only relocate the puzzle; and is the turnout problem best treated as a special case of the general puzzle of infinitesimal individual contributions to large outcomes — one vote, one ton of carbon, one dollar — or as something importantly different? Rank the questions, give me your criteria (importance, tractability, how much other work depends on the answer, how much a resolution would move public argument rather than only the seminar room), tell me where those criteria pull against one another, and say what over the next few years would reorder the list.
