I am designing a session for a decision-theory reading group that pairs fiction with technical work, and I want to use Liu Cixin's The Dark Forest, the second volume of the Remembrance of Earth's Past trilogy. Its machinery — the chain of suspicion, the wallfacer project that licenses a few people to lie and to conceal their real strategy from an enemy that can observe everything else, the droplet, the coordinates of a distant inhabited star broadcast into the void and that star's later destruction, and the deterrent that one person must hold and must be believed to hold — is familiar enough that you can work from it directly; no plot summary is needed.

Read the novel through two ideas narrower than general deterrence theory. The first is the acausal threat: a threat that cannot move its target by any causal channel — because the target has already acted, or because the two sides can never exchange messages — and that operates, if it operates at all, through the target's model of what the threatener's policy would be. The second is the surrogate goal, proposed as a defence against extortion: an agent adds to its aims a goal it does not intrinsically care about, so that threats are deflected onto something cheap to sacrifice; the same device might be adopted by a threatener to make its own threat sincere.

Which of these does the novel actually contain? Is the dark forest strike a threat, or an unconditional hazard emitted by no one, and does that distinction determine whether anything in the book could have deflected it? Where does Luo Ji's spell reason acausally, and where is it merely a causal demonstration? Is the wallfacer's secrecy in tension with the legibility that any credible threat requires? Could a surrogate goal have saved anyone here, or does it work only against an extortionist and not against an indifferent hunter? Be honest where the novel resists the reading — my own suspicion is that it stages a threat whose mechanics support only a hazard, and that much of its force depends on that slide. Close by saying what the novel supplies that the decision-theoretic literature cannot, and which scenes I should put in front of the group.
