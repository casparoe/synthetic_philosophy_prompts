I am trying to state, and then test, my convictions about how much authority a community may exercise over its own members and about when the wider political society may properly override a community's claims. My verdicts on particular cases are as follows, and I would like you to tell me what principle, or small set of principles, they add up to.

- A religious community that runs its own schools, teaches its own doctrines, and steers its children away from university should be left alone by the state, provided basic literacy and numeracy are secured.
- A town council that bans shop signs in a minority language acts illegitimately.
- A national government that requires a common civic curriculum in every school, including in minority-language regions, acts legitimately, even though this displaces locally taught content.
- A housing cooperative that forbids its members to sell their units to outsiders does nothing wrong.
- A community whose leaders announce that members who leave the faith will be shunned and cut off from communal aid acts wrongly, even though no one is physically prevented from leaving.
- A community that fines its members for using majority-language loanwords in communal settings does nothing wrong.
- A community that requires its members to bring internal disputes to its own arbitration and forbids them to go to state court acts wrongly.
- A city that refuses a religious community permission to build a house of worship in a residential district, while leaving churches already there undisturbed, acts wrongly.

I notice that some of these pull against one another. I let the insular school alone, yet I want a national curriculum imposed. I protect the cooperative's right to restrict whom it sells to, yet I condemn a community that closes off its own courts. I defend a community's power to fine its members for their speech, yet I condemn it for shunning those who leave. I cannot tell whether I hold one view here or several, or whether some of these verdicts simply have to go.

The case I keep going back and forth on is a community that requires its members to marry within the faith and disinherits those who marry out; I cannot decide whether it belongs with the insular school and the housing cooperative or with the shunning and the closed courts.

Please tell me: first, whether there is a principle, or a small set of principles, that delivers all eight verdicts; second, if there is, what it is, stated precisely, and which of my cases motivates each of its clauses; third, what it implies for cases I have not listed — say, a community that requires members to contribute a fixed share of income to communal funds, or one that forbids members to read majority-language newspapers, or a state that grants a religious community jurisdiction over marriage and divorce for its members while preserving a right of appeal to state courts; fourth, if no principle delivers all eight, which verdict or verdicts I should abandon and what the resulting view looks like; and fifth, whether the view that survives is better described as one in which communities are prior to their members or one in which individuals are prior to their communities, with the cases rather than the labels doing the settling.
