I'm commissioning a poem for a feature on the Anthropocene boundary debate. The poem should be a villanelle. Its two refrains should correspond to the two most prominent proposed start dates: one for the Orbis spike, the sharp dip in atmospheric CO2 around 1610 that some scientists attribute to reforestation in the Americas after disease-driven population collapse, and one for the mid-20th-century plutonium fallout signal recorded in the annually laminated sediments of Crawford Lake, Ontario, which the Anthropocene Working Group proposed as the golden spike. The form's returning refrains should track how these markers keep reasserting themselves, and the final quatrain—where the refrains meet—should turn on the fact that the formal proposal was rejected in 2024 by the Subcommission on Quaternary Stratigraphy, even as the term Anthropocene continues to be used across the sciences and humanities. The poem should be accurate enough that a stratigrapher would recognize the debate, and it should leave the reader with the unresolved question of whether a geological period is discovered or decided. Avoid citing sources or quoting from any text. You may also allude to other proposed markers, such as the broiler chicken or spheroidal carbonaceous particles from coal combustion, if it serves the form. The poem may be spoken by a member of the subcommission, by the term Anthropocene itself, or by one of the proposed markers—your choice, as long as the voice serves the argument.
