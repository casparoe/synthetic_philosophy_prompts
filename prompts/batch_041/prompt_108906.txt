For a while now I've been trying to get clear on a scheme I keep running into, which sorts minds into four ascending kinds: Darwinian creatures, whose competences are fixed by natural selection; Skinnerian creatures, which learn by reinforcement and the consequences of their own behaviour; Popperian creatures, which can try out hypotheses internally, so that the hypotheses rather than the creature bear the cost of being wrong; and Gregorian creatures, which think with external tools and symbols — writing, diagrams, tally marks, and the like. The more I turn it over, the more it looks like a ladder in which each rung supersedes the one below it: once an animal can run simulations in its head, blind trial-and-error starts to look like a crutch it ought to outgrow, and once it can offload thought onto paper and other mind-tools, even simulation looks primitive by comparison.

A couple of friends and I got into a long argument about this after one of them claimed that a sufficiently capable language model — trained first to predict text, then tuned by human feedback — has effectively graduated past its Skinnerian phase into something purely Popperian and Gregorian, and that continuing to train it with reinforcement would be a step backwards, roughly like teaching a physicist by electric shock. I found myself agreeing at the time, but I couldn't reconstruct the reasoning afterwards, and now I'm not sure I even understood the scheme correctly in the first place.

What I'd like is a careful account of what these four kinds are actually claiming about one another, and then an answer to the practical question our argument left me with. If the ladder really works this way, what is reinforcement learning still doing in the life of a Popperian or Gregorian creature? And if I were designing a curriculum for a system of the kind we were arguing about, should I try to skip the Skinnerian rung altogether and go straight to training it to simulate and to use external tools? Be concrete about what would count as a Skinnerian process versus a Popperian one in such a system, and about whether these levels are meant to be stages that replace one another or something else entirely.
