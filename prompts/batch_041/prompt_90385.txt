I'm TAing a seminar on epistemology this term and next week's session is on the dogmatism paradox, and I want to close it with a poem instead of yet another handout — I've definately noticed students remember verse longer than they remember a summary. The version we've been working with is the one where, if I know that P, then I know that any evidence against P is misleading, and if I know the evidence is misleading then I'm entitled to disregard it, so knowing something all by itself licenses me to wave off whatever later shows up to challenge it. We've run through the usual escape routes in class (maybe knowing P doesn't mean knowing that you know it; maybe the whole inference is self-defeating, since running it is itself a way of refusing to take your own fallibility seriously), and I'd rather the poem sit in the space where those replies are still fighting it out than announce a winner.

So: a villanelle, please — nineteen lines, the five tercets and the quatrain, the two refrains. The form should be doing the argument, not just carrying it. I want one refrain to be the knowledge claim and the other to be the licence to set the counter-evidence aside, and I want the way they keep coming back no matter what the tercets say, and then meet each other in the closing quatrain, to be the whole point: the conclusion is already folded into the premise and keeps reasserting itself. The tercets can be the intervening evidence, so to speak — the things that arrive to challenge the belief and get absorbed.

One thing I want it to push on, because it's where our discussion got stuck: the self-referential version, where what comes in isn't just evidence against P but evidence that she believes P because she's the sort of person who waves off evidence against her beliefs. By the same logic she's entitled to wave that off too — she knows P, so she knows that this bit of evidence is misleading as well, and the diagnosis of dogmatism just gets swallowed by the dogmatism. If the two refrains can carry both the plain case and the looping one without the poem having to say which is which, that's exactly what I'm after. Please don't turn it into a sermon about being open-minded and don't resolve it at the end — I want it to end inside the loop. No names, no footnotes, nothing anyone would need to look up, just the poem, though it should be accurate enough that someone who knows the paradox can tell which refrain is which and see why they end up side by side.
