My friend and I got into it last night over beers about whether culture can actually cause anything, and by the end I had no idea what I actually think. Can you help me figure out what my gut is committed to? Here's how I went, case by case.

The classic one: Calvinists get obsessed with predestination, can never know if they're saved, so they work like maniacs and don't spend any of it, and all that unspent capital eventually turns into capitalism. I said sure, that's culture doing real work, a belief changed how people behaved. He said it's a just-so story and the history never supported it anyway.

My grandparents case: they saved and never borrowed a cent. I dont go to church, my parents didn't either, but I save like crazy and get physically uncomfortable signing a loan. I said that's culture plain as day, the belief died and the behavior didn't. He said no, that's prudence plus the fact that nobody in my family has a safety net.

His case: a company with a no-layoffs ethos that survives three CEOs. He says that's not culture, that's a firm that found no-layoffs profitable and the market kept it. I actually agreed with him there, which is what started the fight.

Two towns, same size, same industry, same laws. In one you can leave your bike unlocked outside the shop, in the other you can't, and both have been stable that way for fifty years. I said culture is doing work, it's picking which of two equilibria we live in. He said that's just restating the equilibrium.

Then he hit me with one I'd never heard. Something about the medieval church making cousin marriage illegal, which broke up big extended family networks, and that supposedly being why western Europe ended up with markets and impersonal trust. I said fine, that's a rule changing the world, but notice it isn't a value motivating anybody, it's a rule wrecking a social structure and the economics falling out the other side. He said that's the only kind of culture-cause he'll accept, which seems backwards to me.

And the one I can't shake: I know families that treat working for somebody else as shameful, and they turn down better paying jobs over it. They eat real costs for this. If anything is culture causing something, that is.

Here's the principle I floated, tell me if it's any good: culture counts as a cause only when it costs you. If the behavior pays off anyway, you can't tell the value apart from the incentive. But then my grandparents case dies, since saving is just prudent, and the two towns die, since honesty pays in the honest town. I don't want to give those up. So either the principle is wrong or I have to drop something.

What principle actually fits these, or what's the closest thing? What does it say about cases I havent thought of, like a culture that evaporates the second the paycheck changes, or one that's only one generation old, or a norm that's expensive for each person but pays off for the group? And which of my verdicts does it force me to give up?
