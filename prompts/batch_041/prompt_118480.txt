I'm putting together the menu for a fictional restaraunt and I want it to do real philosophical work rather than just be a bit. The place is called The Gradient: it prices every dish by the presumed moral status of the animal, on a 0 to 1 scale printed next to the price, oysters at the bottom, then shrimp, squid, crab, octopus, farmed salmon, chicken, pig, and a tier above 0.7 that the menu keeps gesturing at but won't serve. Write the menu itself, deadpan, around 550 words: the courses, a short italic blurb about our philosophy, the scale explained in a footnote, and a back-page FAQ (why the octopus is 0.61 and the crab 0.58, whether this is science, whether the sourdough starter counts, whether our own reservation chatbot counts, can you order off-menu). The jokes should come from getting the actual debate right, not from inventing anything: the gap between nociception and suffering, the fact that there's no critical experiment, precautionary reasoning about insects, why one number for a whole species is a fiction, how the ratings suspiciously track what the kitchen already had in the walk-in. Keep the menu internally consistent -- if it says the scale is about suffering, don't let it quietly slide into complexity or cuteness without owning up to it. Then add a short note, 100 words or so, on which ratings are resting on contested science and what would have to be discovered about, say, crabs or insects for the whole scale to have to be rebuilt.
