I have been trying to understand why Jeffrey conditionalization is treated as a distinct rule rather than a special case of ordinary conditionalization. Here is the picture that seems right to me. Suppose I have a partition {Heads, Tails} for a coin toss I did not see. A blurred glimpse leaves me with credence 0.7 in Heads. I revise my other opinions by Jeffrey conditionalization, holding fixed my old conditional credences given Heads and given Tails. A colleague says that nothing distinct has happened. After all, I have learned a higher-order fact: that my credence in Heads is now 0.7. Because that higher-order fact fully specifies my new credence, ordinary conditionalization on it must reproduce the Jeffrey shift. On his view, Jeffrey conditionalization is either identical to ordinary conditionalization on higher-order evidence or a mere computational shortcut. Is he right? If the two procedures coincide, why do epistemologists distinguish them, and what work does the rigidity condition do? If they do not coincide, which procedure models my blurred glimpse correctly, and what exactly is the mistake in the thought that uncertain evidence is always just evidence about one's own credences? I want the conceptual diagnosis.
