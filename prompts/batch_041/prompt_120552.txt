I was a bystander at a graduate game-theory seminar where two participants, A and B, disagreed about the status of iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies when mixed strategies are permitted. I am reporting their exchange without taking sides. The exchange consisted of five moves, in order.

(1) A said: "The solution concept we should teach is iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies, allowing mixed strategies to do the dominating. It is order-independent, and it is exactly what common knowledge of rationality forces: if a strategy is strictly dominated given the strategies that remain, no rational player can play it."

(2) B replied: "Order-independence is a technical property, not an epistemic argument. And the procedure is not the same as iterated elimination of never-best-response strategies if 'best response' means best response to an independent mixed profile of the other players. In two-player games the two coincide, but with three or more players a strategy can be never a best response to any independent mixed profile yet not be strictly dominated by any mixed strategy. So the two procedures can peel off different sets."

(3) A answered: "Even if they diverge, the never-best-response procedure is the one that matches the epistemic idea. Strict dominance is just a sufficient condition for being ruled out by rationality; the real criterion is whether there is any belief that would make the strategy a best response. If there is none, it should be eliminated."

(4) B said: "But that criterion depends on which beliefs are allowed. If players' beliefs about the other players can be correlated, a strategy might be a best response to a correlated belief even though it is not a best response to any independent mixed profile. Common knowledge of rationality does not by itself rule out correlated beliefs. So the never-best-response procedure with independent beliefs assumes more than common knowledge of rationality; it assumes independence."

(5) A concluded: "Then the honest conclusion is that iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies is the right concept for common knowledge of rationality, because strict dominance by a mixed strategy is equivalent to never being a best response to any belief, correlated or not. The independent-belief version is a different, stronger solution concept, and we should stop conflating them."

Please adjudicate the dispute move by move. For each of the five moves, state whether it is correct, incorrect, or correct only with qualification, and briefly explain why. In the fifth item, also say who is more right overall and identify the most important consideration both parties overlook. Do not supply citations, a bibliography, or a reading list. Respond with exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, and no introduction or conclusion; item n should address move n.
