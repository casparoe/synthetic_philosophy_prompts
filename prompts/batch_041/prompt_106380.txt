The usual examples (the soldier who throws himself on the grenade, the stranger who donates a kidney) are fine as far as they go, but they all treat the heroic act as a one-off that leaves everyone else's obligations exactly where they were. I've been trying to cook up a case where that isn't true, and I keep getting tangled up. Has the first person's going-beyond-duty made the second person's refusal something more than merely "not supererogatory"? Or is the second person still free to decline without blame?
