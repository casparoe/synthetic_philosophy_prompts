Here's the position I've talked myself into. Keynes's distinction between the degree of a probability and its weight — the amount of relevant evidence behind it — is a real distinction, and weight is not reducible to some further credence. In the Treatise he says the comparison turns upon "a balance, not between the favourable and the unfavourable evidence, but between the absolute amounts of relevant knowledge and of relevant ignorance respectively", and I think that's exactly right and that nothing since has touched it. All the modern reconstructions — second-order probabilities, sets of probability functions, the smooth-ambiguity models, "confidence" in a subjective estimate — look to me like they model the wrong thing, because every one of them takes weight and converts it back into a probability of some kind. If weight were just a second-order probability then Keynes's whole point would be lost, and I don't think it's lost. I also think weight has to do genuine normative work: two hypotheses can have the same degree and still not be on a par for action.

The part that's more mine than Keynes's is what I actually want tested. I think "weight" gets used for two seperate things and only one of them does any work. Call them coverage and resilience. Coverage = how much of the relevant evidence base an argument has actually digested, how much of the space of relevant considerations has been surveyed. Resilience = how far the estimate would have to move, or how much new evidence it would take, before the conclusion flips. My hunch is that Keynes's weight is coverage, but that whenever weight seems to matter for what to do, it's resilience doing the work — and resilience is a property of the evidence plus the decision, not of the argument. If that's right, then "the weight of an argument" as an argument-relative quantity either collapses into coverage (real, but decision-theoretically inert) or into resilience (does the work, but isn't argument-relative). Either way the Keynesian primitive is in trouble. I want to know if it actually is.
