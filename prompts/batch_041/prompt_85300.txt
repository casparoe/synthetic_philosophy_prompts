I got appointed to our city's Public Memory and Naming Advisory Board and I'm the one who has to write up the reasoning for Thursday's meeting. Here's our track record over the last six years, more or less in it's own words, and then the case in front of us. I need to know what we should do with the new one if we actually want to be consistent with ourselves.

1. Bronze of an 1870s alderman who embezzled from the orphanage fund — removed. Reasoning: the city shouldn't be honoring a man whose worst documented act is that.
2. A drinking fountain in Hollis Park, put up by the temperance league in 1903 for the doctor who ran the smallpox hospital. Doctor turns out to have been a real crank who wrote to the paper saying the poor shouldn't be allowed to have children. Kept, no new plaque. Reasoning: the fountain honors the hospital work, and the crank letters have nothing to do with that.
3. A street named for a man nobody in the neighborhood knew was a person at all — he was a clerk who signed the deed handing the land to the city, and the name was clerical convenience, not an honor. Kept. Reasoning: it was never an honor, so there's nothing to withdraw.
4. A battle monument from 1957 that we now know was paid for by a real-estate syndicate that wanted the surrounding blocks zoned white. Removed. Reasoning: whatever else it is, it's an instrument built to do a specific job and it's still sitting where it was put to do it.
5. Statue of a woman who ran a shelter, put up by the people she sheltered, now on public land; the shelter's ledgers show she turned away families who weren't from the parish. Kept, but we bolted a second plaque to the base. Reasoning: the people who erected it are still around and still hold their yearly picnic there, and pulling it down takes something from them that isn't ours to take.

Now the new one. Bronze of a physician, erected in 1951 by public subscription, honoring his years running the TB sanatorium on the hill. It stands at the entrance to the hospital's outpatient clinic. That neighborhood is now mostly descendants of people who were sterilized under the state program in the 1930s, and the physician sat on the board that ran the program for eleven years and voted with the majority at least twice, per the minutes. He never wrote a word about it either way. The clinic's patients, a lot of them elderly, a lot of them from families that credit the sanatorium with saving them, have a petition to keep it. The descendants' group has a petition to take it down. Our own bylaw says we act only on what's documented.

So: what do we decide, which of the five is the closest analog, and if the precedents honestly don't settle it, what principle are we adopting with each option? And if we end up splitting hairs on something, tell me which case we'd have to see next to find out which principle we're really running.
