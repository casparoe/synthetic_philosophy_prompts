ok so context: I'm a backend engineer, never took a philosophy class past intro ethics, but I've fallen down a rabbit hole reading about how minds work and I keep arguing about it with my brother in law who actually did a cog sci PhD. Here's roughly where I've landed and I want a consistency check, because I have a feeling I can't keep all of it but I can't tell where the break is.

1. Thinking is computation over structured, language-like representations. There's some medium with a syntax and a semantics, and a thought is basically a sentence in that medium — not English, but sentence-like, with parts that combine into wholes.

2. A representation means what it means because of how it's causally wired to the world — it's about whatever reliably triggers it / whatever it was selected to track. No magic reference, just informational and causal hookups.

3. Current LLMs are genuine cognitive systems, not glorified autocomplete. They think, in some real sense. And what's happening inside them is distributed vectors — there is no symbol table anywhere in there, no little sentence sitting in a box.

4. Compositionality is a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have. If you can think about the dog biting the man, you can think about the man biting the dog, and that ability comes for free from the format of the representations rather than from having seen both cases. Same reason you can think infinitely many thoughts you've never thought before.

5. Learning concepts is mostly learning the combinatorial rules, not learning each concept from scratch. Finite stock of primitives, everything else is assembly.

6. There's a determinate fact of the matter about what a given representation represents. Two systems could be behaviorally identical and still mean different things, but for any one system there's a right answer about its content.
