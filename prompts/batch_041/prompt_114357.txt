I've been runing a tiny philosophy podcast out of my garage since I retired from the geology department — four listeners, one of whom is my brother-in-law — and next month I've somehow landed an actual guest. She works on the old puzzle about a statue and the lump of bronze its made of, and she has a line on it that I haven't heard stated quite this way before. Roughly: there is only one object on the pedestal, full stop, the statue just is the clay. What we take for two things is, she says, one thing with two careers — a career as a lump of metal and a career as an artwork — and the troublesome modal claims (the statue wouldn't survive being squashed flat, the lump would) aren't about the object at all, they're about the career. So "the statue would not survive squashing" isn't false, on her view, it's incomplete: it's a claim about a career, and the career isn't a second object sitting next to the first one. She gets visibly anoyed when people call this a merely verbal dispute, and just as annoyed when they file it under four-dimensionalism. As a geologist I spent forty years happily treating "a rock" and "a mineral specimen" as one thing under two descriptions, so I'm sympathetic, but I gather the sympathetic reading is exactly the one that gets you into trouble.

What I want is an interview that actually presses her rather than giving her room to recite the standard moves. Could you draft ten or eleven questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give? I'd like at least a few of them to go at what a career is supposed to be if it's neither an object nor a property, and whether she can keep the view from collapsing into "one career per sortal we happen to care about," which would make the whole thing cheap. Then the counting problem, because it seems to me I can get two different correct answers to "how many things are on the pedestal?" and if counting is that sensitive I want to know what she thinks I'm doing when I count. Then whether anything grounds the careers or whether they're just descriptions we bring along, and whether the view says anything about the lump that predates the statue and outlives it — the career seems to start and stop at rather arbitrary points and I want to know who decides. And I'd especially like a couple of questions on the legal and insurance angle, since that's the one that really nags at me: if there's one object, how is it that I can own the statue and not the clay, or insure one and not the other — does she think the law is simply confused, or does she think ownership is career-relative too? Finish with one that pushes her on the case where the parts get swapped out one at a time: does the career survive, and if her answer is that the career ends and a new one starts, what makes that different from saying the object was destroyed and replaced. None of the "what drew you to philosophy" filler, obviously, and please don't ask her to run through who else has defended the view or what she'd tell me to read, since I can't look anything up mid-recording and neither can my four listeners.
