Here's what I can't stop thinking about. Like take an electron. Or a quark. Or a field, or whatever the real bottom floor of reality turns out to be. Does an electron have a tiny bit of experience? And then the thing I actually want a number on. Here's the claim:

The total amount of morally relevant suffering in the universe is dominated by the experiences of whatever the fundamental physical entities turn out to be — meaning the aggregate suffering of particles/fields/whatever is greater than the aggregate suffering of every animal and person and any other composite being combined.

Give me your probability that that's true. Not "it depends what you mean" — a number. But the number is the less interesting half; what I really want is the decomposition, with a rough probability for each piece and a line on why:

- the probability that experience is fundamental at all, in any form (vs. it being something that only arises in certain complicated systems)
- given that, the probability that the fundamental entities are themselves the ones having experiences, versus the alternative where the only real subject is the whole universe and the "parts" aren't subjects at all (I think this is the cosmopsychism thing? where the cosmos is the one experiencer and particles are more like... not experiencers, the way your neurons aren't you). - given that the parts are subjects, the probability that what they have is actual good/bad feeling rather than some neutral inner "there's something it's like" with no valence either way. I feel like people slide between these two without noticing and it does all the work.
- given valence, the probability that it counts morally when there's no memory, no continuity, no self that persists — just a flash of unpleasantness and then nothing. - the probability that we can even point at the right level of physics for this. If the "fundamental" stuff turns out to be events or excitations or relations rather than little objects, is there even a thing there to be a subject? - the probability that the moral math is additive like that at all — that a zillion tiny bad flashes outweigh billions of animal lives. Vs. there being some threshold below which experience has zero moral weight, or moral weight not stacking that way, or something about intensity mattering more than count.
- and honestly, the probability that the question has a determinate answer in the first place, rather than being the kind of thing where "how much suffering is in the universe" has no fact of the matter.
