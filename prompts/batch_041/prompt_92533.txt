I am putting together a written statement of my commitments in formal epistemology and I want an audit before I circulate it. Here is the package as it stands.

1. Credences are probabilities, and satisfying the probability axioms is all that is required of a rational credence function — no principle of induction, no symmetry or indifference constraint, nothing beyond the axioms together with the updating rules below.
2. Fix the prior and fix the total body of evidence, and exactly one posterior is rationally permissible: two agents who share a prior and share their evidence cannot rationally end up in different credal states.
3. Any prior that satisfies the probability axioms is rationally permissible; I am a thoroughgoing subjectivist about priors.
4. When my evidence is an outright proposition, updating is conditionalizing on it.
5. Take the parachute case. I am dropped somewhere in a square field that I know to be divided into four equal quadrants, and I begin with credence 1/4 in each. A radio message I fully trust then tells me only something conditional — that my chance of the north-west quadrant, given that I am in the western half, is 2/3 — and so settles no outright proposition about where I am. I treat that message as genuine evidence, and I treat the credence function I ought to adopt as the one, among those compatible with the constraint, that departs least from my prior, measured by relative entropy.
6. Jeffrey's rule is legitimate as well: when evidence arrives as new probabilities over a partition, I reweight within each cell while holding the conditional probabilities fixed. I take this to be a generalization of conditionalization rather than a rival to it.

Which of these actually pull against one another, which pairs only appear to conflict, and how severe is each real conflict? Rank the real conflicts by severity, and for each say what has to give and what giving it up would cost. Then say which single revision would clear up the most. Keep what is settled among people who work on this sharply separate from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
