I sit on the board of our province's disaster recovery fund, the public scheme that tops up whatever the insurers won't pay after a flood. On the 14th we vote on staff's proposal for the Hollow Creek claims. About 140 households built or bought inside the mapped floodway, and a chunk of them sat through two evacuation advisories before the water came. Staff want a flat 20% "contributory neglect" deduction on those claims; everyone else gets paid in full. Our enabling statute just says the fund is there to get households back to a habitable standard — it's silent on fault, which is half the problem.

One of my fellow board members, Dara, is against the deduction and her reasons aren't the usual ones. She thinks suffering is the settling of accounts, that there's a ledger and people collect what they've run up, so a household that builds in a floodway and loses the house has simply been paid a debt it owed. Paying them in full, on her view, is the actual injustice, because it wipes out a consequence they earned. She's not a hard case — she's a hospice nurse and I've watched her be the kindest person in a room — she frames it as respect: you don't rescue an adult from being an adult. Two other members have fallen in behind her, and I think at least one of them just doesn't want to pay out.

Ade pushed back at the meeting and I want the memo to deal with what she said too. Ade's line was that even granting a ledger, on the Buddhist version there's no persisting self to hold the account — the person suffering now isn't the person who acted, so "deserves" has nothing to attach to. Dara came back that karma isn't a judge handing down sentences anyway, it's just how causation works, more like weather than a courtroom. Nobody knew what to say after that and we moved on, which is why I'm writing this instead of just voting. I've also had alot of angry email from claimants who did everything right and are now watching their neighbours get a 20% haircut they think is too small.

So please draft the written opinion I'll circulate before the 14th. It needs a bottom line we can actually put to a vote — adopt, reject, or amend the deduction, with a concrete rule and a date — and it has to take Dara's position seriously enough that she'd recognise it, including whatever part of it I think is right. Don't turn it into a lecture on Buddhism; I need something a claims manager could implement on Monday morning.

After the memo, give me a short JSON block I can paste into the minutes, with exactly these fields: "bottom_line" (one sentence), "motion_text" (the exact wording to be voted on), "vote_date", "dara_claim_restated" (her position in one sentence, no strawmanning), "what_she_gets_right" (one or two sentences), "where_it_breaks" (array of at most three points, each under 40 words, each naming the specific step in her argument it targets), "strongest_objection_to_my_recommendation" (one sentence), "conditions" (array of strings — the carve-outs or triggers attached to the rule), "review_by" (a date or a triggering event), "residual_disagreement" (one sentence naming what the board is still split on). Keep it valid JSON and don't put commentary inside it.
