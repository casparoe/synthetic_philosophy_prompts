I teach an upper-level ethics seminar (about 14 students, one 90-minute block) and I want to run a market game that makes them feel the commodification objection from the inside instead of just nodding along to it. The idea is one market, one currency, one trading mechanism, but three goods that are structurally identical and morally very different: a spare ticket to something fun, a spot near the front of a queue for something scarce, and a vote in a decision the group actually has to make together that day. Everyone starts with the same points and the same bundle, and they can buy and sell all three in open outcry for maybe 25 minutes. Points only, no real money. What I want is for the pattern to emerge by itself — the tickets churn happily, the queue spots trade but sheepishly, and the votes mostly just sit there while anyone who sells one gets the side-eye — and then for them to be stuck with the fact that the mechanism was identical every single time. Can you write me the whole thing: setup, materials, the three goods and how to make the vote genuinely matter to them, the trading rules, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the difference between the fairness/coercion objection and the corruption/commodification objection, and then forces them to say whether the repugnance they felt is actually a reason to block a trade or just a taboo. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time — my guess is they'll all refuse to trade the votes and I'll get no data, or someone will take the points way too seriously and get upset — and be honest about what the game can't show. I assume it can produce the squick but not adjudicate it, so tell me what I have to supply myself afterwards.
