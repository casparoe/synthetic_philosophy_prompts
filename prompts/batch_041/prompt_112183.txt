I've been reading rationalist blogs since the old Overcoming Bias days, which is how I ended up as the least credentialed person on my lab's deployment review committee. Next Thursday we vote on shipping a new model, and the team's safety case is entirely a control protocol rather then an alignment claim: a small monitor model reads the big model's reasoning traces and flags anything suspicious, flagged outputs get resampled up to five times, and whatever stays flagged goes to a human. Their own writeup admits the protocol only buys safety in the world where the big model is actively trying to subvert us, and that they can't yet show the model doesn't know when it's being sampled. So we'd be paying a capability tax for a guarantee that only exists if the model is out to get us, and I can't think of an eval that tells those two worlds apart.

Write the memo I'll circulate before the vote. Close with the specific evidence that would flip the recommendation -- not "more research needed," the actual thing that would do it.
