For several years now I have been keeping a record of a colleague's verdicts on which systems are conscious. She works on integrated information theory and is, so far as I can tell, entirely consistent; I have come to treat her judgments as data, and I want to know what they commit her to.

Her verdicts, as I have recorded them:

- A single logic gate, and a photodiode: not conscious, not even faintly.
- A deep feed-forward network of any depth, trained to classify images or to play board games: not conscious, and she insists that no further layers, parameters, or training would alter this.
- The cerebellum, which holds the majority of the brain's neurons and whose wiring she regards as the wrong shape for the job: not conscious.
- A sheet of cortical tissue with dense reciprocal connections: conscious, to some degree.
- A recurrent circuit with rich internal dynamics, wired to nothing at all — no inputs, no outputs, no behaviour, no access to its own states: conscious; she treats this as a central prediction of her position rather than an embarrassment.
- The same circuit implemented in silicon rather than in tissue, with its causal structure preserved: conscious, just as before.
- A recurrent sub-circuit embedded within a larger recurrent system whose parts constrain one another more tightly than the sub-circuit's parts do: the sub-circuit is not itself conscious; only the larger system is.

She has agreed to rule on three further systems, and I should like to anticipate her answers:

(a) a ring of three units, each of which merely passes its activity to the next, with no branching, no memory beyond a single step, and no connection to anything outside the ring;
(b) a network of ten thousand densely recurrent local circuits, each coupled to its immediate neighbours by a few weak recurrent connections, so that the network is connected but behaves like a bundle of nearly independent units;
(c) a very large feed-forward network, with no recurrent connections anywhere in its wiring, that produces fluent and contextually appropriate language.

What will she say of each? I am less interested in the verdicts themselves than in the rule she is running. Which candidate rules fit everything I have recorded above, what does each of them predict for (a), (b), and (c), which reading would you bet on and on what grounds, and which of the three — or, if none of them, what further system — would most cleanly force the candidate rules apart?
