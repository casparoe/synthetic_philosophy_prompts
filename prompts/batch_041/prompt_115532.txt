My historiography prof gave a quick gloss on presentism last week that I want to stress-test, because it felt like it was pulling a fast one somewhere and I cant put my finger on where. He said: 'Presentism is judging the past by today's standards — calling the Vikings war criminals, the founders slaveholders, that sort of thing. It's the cardinal sin of professional history, and the reason it's a sin is that it makes history relative: if every generation judges the past by its own lights, no account is truer than any other and history collapses into opinion. The honest historian tries to see the past as it saw itself, and once you've done that, the question of whether historians can escape the standards of their own time answers itself — you escape them by being rigorous.' My friend says thats basically right and I shouldn't overthink it, but I've also seen presentism used in a way that has nothing to do with moralizing, and the jump to relativism smells. Where's the prof on solid ground, where's he fudging, and does his conclusion actually follow from the parts that are right? Also, is 'escaping the standards of your own time' even the thing presentism is about, or is that a different argument wearing its coat?
