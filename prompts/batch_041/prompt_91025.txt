Longtime lurker on the rationalist blogs/forums, and I've got a pattern I can't find a proper name for.

Here's the thing I keep running into. Two people get into an argument that looks empirical. Say: is a virus alive? Or: does a company using its size to drive a rival out of business count as "coercion"? They go back and forth, one cites facts, the other keeps saying "thats not what alive means" or "thats not coercion, thats just hard bargaining." Eventually they realize they've been using the load-bearing word in two different senses the whole time. At that point the standard move is for everyone to shrug and say "oh, it was just a verbal dispute, nothing was really at stake after all." But thats exactly where it stops matching the standard story for me, because it really doesn't feel like nothing was at stake. Even after they've both seen both senses clearly, they still disagree -- and what they disagree about is which sense the word *should* have, or which one is the one that actually carves reality at the joints. And that disagreement seems as real as any ordinary one. Half the time the whole argument was really an attempt to get the other person to adopt your sense of the word, because whoever's sense wins gets to frame everything downstream.

So: is there a name for this exact thing? I know "verbal dispute" is a term, and I know the whole taboo-your-words thing people on here always recommend, but that advice seems to assume the dispute dissolves once you distinguish the senses. In the cases I'm describing it doesn't dissolve -- distinguishing the senses is where the real fight starts. So is this a species of verbal dispute, or is it something the verbal-dispute framework basically can't handle because it assumes verbal disputes are trivial? How does it relate to plainer cases of equivocation or ambiguity? And does it matter if the word in question is a moral/legal term (coercion, person) versus a natural-kind term (alive) -- is one kind of "which sense should win" fight substantive and the other not? I don't just want the label, I want to know where the edges are: what counts, what doesn't, and what the nearest already-named things are that this is and isn't.
