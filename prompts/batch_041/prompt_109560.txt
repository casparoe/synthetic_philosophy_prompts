Me and my friend got into a huge argument last night about the Sleeping Beauty problem and I'm still kind of annoyed about it. You probably know the setup: she goes under on Sunday, a fair coin gets tossed, heads means she's woken once on Monday, tails means she's woken Monday and again on Tuesday, and every time she wakes her memory of the earlier wakings is wiped so every waking feels like the first. On each waking she's asked how confident she is that the coin came up heads. My friend says 1/2 — it's a fair coin, and being awake is guaranteed to happen whatever the coin did, so the waking tells her nothing about the coin. I say 1/3 — there are three wakings she could be in and only one of them is a heads-waking, and she can't tell them apart. She says I'm double-counting the same toss by counting wakings. I say you can't double-count something you don't know the identity of. Two hours, alot of shouting, no movement. I gather philosophers have been stuck on basically this for decades, which is odd, because it doesn't feel like there's a hard fact we're disagreeing about.

What I actually want isnt a ruling on who's right — it's a diagnosis of what this fight even is. Are we just using "credence" to mean two different things, where I'm computing something like "which waking am I in" and she's computing "what should I believe about the coin"? Are we answering two different questions under one name? Or is there a real formal thing hiding in here — something about whether self-locating beliefs can even carry ordinary probabilities, or about which set of cases counts as a fair random sample? Or is it just two bedrock intuitions about sampling that neither of us can argue for and that's the end of it?

My own guess is that halfers and thirders are quietly using different reference classes, one calibrated by outcomes and one calibrated by experiences, and that's why nobody ever moves. But test that against what each side actually says rather than just agreeing with me.

So on tails she might get one extra waking or six. Does that variant actually pull the two positions apart, or does it just reveal that the original framing was smuggling in a reference class all along? And does it change your diagnosis, or confirm it?
