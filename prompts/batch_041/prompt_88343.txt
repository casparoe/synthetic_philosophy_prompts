My brother and I are the executors of our late mother's estate, and by December we have to decide whether to sell the house she left us or keep it in the family. He spent far more time with her in her last years than I did and is certain she wanted it kept; I have the accounts and am certain the upkeep will ruin us both. Neither of us is inventing anything, and after eight months of this we can barely sit in the same room.

I have read that in the Jain tradition a statement is supposed to be true only from the standpoint it is made from, so that two people who contradict each other can each be saying something true about the same thing. When I tried that on my brother, he heard "we are both right, so we keep the house," and I am not certain he misheard. When I try to say what the idea actually commits me to, I end up with either "it is all a matter of perspective," which I do not believe, or with my original certainty, which is what got us here.

Can the idea be put precisely enough to be usable in an argument like this — precise enough that I could tell him exactly where I think he is right and exactly where I think he is wrong, without dissolving into "both sides have a point" and without pretending my view is the whole truth? And once I have done that, what does it tell me to do when one of us has to sign?

Please answer in no more than 400 words, and do not send me to books or articles; I will not read them.
