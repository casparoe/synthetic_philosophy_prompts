I'm a reporter at a small daily and I've somehow agreed to give the little speech at the opening of the new plasma center out on Route 9 — you know the kind of place, you recline in a chair for an hour and they pay you for your plasma, and I'm told the pay goes up with your weight, which is either the most honest or the most gross thing about the whole arrangement. I covered the zoning fight, so both sides think I'm on theirs. In the room: the mayor, the company's regional VP, a bunch of the first donors (students from the community college, plus alot of people who need the money this week), the church group that faught the thing, and a bioethicist from the university who wrote a letter to the editor asking why it's legal to pay someone for plasma and a felony to pay them for a kidney. That letter's been bugging me for a month, mostly because I can't tell whether my discomfort is about the money, or about the fact that the people taking the money are poor, or about something I can't name that has to do with a needle in an arm being paid in one building and not in the other — the Red Cross does whole blood a few blocks away for free. And I think there's a real difference between "you're turning something that ought to be a gift into a product" and "this is a raw deal for desperate people" and "nobody should have to sell a piece of themselves to make rent," and I keep collapsing all three into one feeling, which is why I've never managed to write the column. I want the speech built on that — its shape should come from not knowing which of those is the real objection, and it should leave the thing open without copping out, and it has to land for the donors without patting them on the head, for the protesters without insulting the people in the chairs, and for the company guy without letting him off. Four minutes, tops — people are here for the sandwiches. And don't quote philosophers at them, this isn't a seminar. Write the speech as continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, nothing I have to squint at on my phone.
