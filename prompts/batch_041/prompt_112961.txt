My brother is scheduled for a corpus callosotomy, and the surgeon has told us the disconnection may be partial and may be performed in stages. The question I keep returning to is not a medical one. Every popular account I have found says that split-brain patients show that one body can house two streams of consciousness, and then stops; I would like the conceptual issue set out properly rather than gestured at. So: what is the unity of consciousness supposed to name — a relation that holds among experiences, a property of a subject, or a constraint on how experiences are had at all — and which of its varieties do the split-brain cases genuinely threaten: phenomenal togetherness, shared access, or singleness of agency? Two things matter most to me. First, whether unity could come in degrees; if it can, is there any non-arbitrary degree of connection at which the count of subjects changes from one to two, and how would that case differ from the disunity we already tolerate in divided attention or in ambivalence? Second, why the fact that only one hemisphere can report is a methodological problem rather than a mere inconvenience — what does the inference from what a patient says to how many subjects there are presuppose? And finally, what evidence would actually favour two subjects sharing a body over a single subject with an intermittently disconnected stream, and is the one-or-two question even well posed when the disconnection is only partial?
