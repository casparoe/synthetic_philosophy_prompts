I direct a small research group on the foundations of rational choice — three postdocs, three years of funding, philosophy only, no experiments and no empirical collaborators — and I have to decide what we should actually spend that time on. The target is the family of coherence arguments for expected utility maximization: the arguments that an agent whose preferences or choices violate certain structural conditions is irrational, or is committed to something she cannot endorse, because she can be pumped or booked by a sequence of transactions each of which she would accept. Everyone in the group already knows the standard moves, so I want no survey and no citations.

What I want is a defended agenda. Rank at most five open questions in this area by how much work the answer would do, and defend the ordering against the criteria you think are the right ones — upstreamness (how many other disputes settle if this one settles), tractability from the armchair within three years, and whether the question is live or merely a verbal dispute that dissolves once the terms are fixed. Candidates worth considering, though I would rather be told that my list is mis-framed than have it ranked politely: whether the arguments are really about preferences at all or about dispositions to choose; whether they presuppose completeness, and what becomes of them if an agent's preferences are only partial; whether their force survives taking plans or whole lives rather than single choices as the unit of assessment; whether the vindication of coherence should be pragmatic (avoiding sure loss) or epistemic, and whether those two routes converge on the same conclusion; and whether the idealizations — no cost of deliberation, no self-ignorance, unlimited computational power — leave the conclusion with anything to say about agents like us.

For each ranked item, give me the crux it turns on, what a plausible three-year project would deliver, and what would move it up or down the list. Close with the two questions you take the field to be overrating, and say why.
