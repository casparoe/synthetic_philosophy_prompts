ok so I want a dialogue between two people who both think there should be a basic income but who violently disagree about what kind. A wants it paid as a dividend out of a big public wealth fund — the fund owns land, resources, maybe spectrum and data and other stuff we collectively inherited — and the payout floats with the fund's returns, so some years it's generous and some years it's almost nothing. her claim is that this makes the money owned rather than given: it's your share of a common inheritance, so nobody can means-test it or condition it or call you a scrounger, and a bad year is just the risk of being a co-owner. B thinks thats a disaster wearing a nice suit. her view: the whole point of a floor is that it's certain; a fluctuating dividend isn't a floor, it's a lottery ticket, and 'property' is a legal fiction here because the fund was bought with taxes and the next government can just sell it off — worse, once you've told people it's their property, clawing it back to pay for anything else looks like theft, so you've made redistribution harder, not easier. then the sub-fight I actually care about: A wants a market in advances — you can sell your future dividends for cash today, because autonomy means you get to convert your share however you like. B says the people who sell are exactly the people who need money now, the price will be brutal, and you've reinvented payday lending with a philosophy degree. and can you sell the share itself, permanently, so a few generations later some people own forty shares and some own none? if it's property that seems to follow; if it's not allowed, then in what sense is it property? let them actually go at each other on all of this — ownership vs charity, certainty vs ownership, the advance market, inheritability — and make sure neither is a dunce: A has to answer the payday-lending point and the 'then it's just charity' point, and B has to answer the 'so you're fine with means-testing and stigma' point. no citations or names of real theorists needed, just the argument. maybe 900 words.
