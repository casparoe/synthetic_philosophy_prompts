I host a small decision theory podcast — credences, imprecise probability, that sort of thing — and next week's guest has spent about ten years defending imprecise probabilities. Her line, roughly: rational credal states are often genuinely imprecise rather than vague-but-secretly-precise; the right decision rule for an imprecise agent is maximality, not maximin; dilation is a feature and not a bug; and (her new book's slogan) an imprecise agent is never rationally worse off for learning something. The book also draws a distinction I haven't seen anywhere else: "evidential imprecision," where the evidence itself fails to pin down a unique distribution, versus "resolution imprecision," where the evidence does determine a distribution but the agent hasn't done the work of resolving it — and she's pretty insistant that both count as rational states for someone to be in.

Draft me eight to ten interview questions. I want them aimed at the machinery, not at "so what is imprecise probability." Specifically: at least one has to be about dilation — the standard case where learning that two coins landed the same way widens your interval for the first coin — with a follow-up for the answer she's definately going to give, which is that the wider interval is right and our pre-theoretic intuition is the thing that's broken. At least one has to be about maximality, since it lets you pick anything that isn't dominated; press on whether that makes "rational" too cheap, and have the follow-up ready for when she retreats to "the agent can adopt a plan." I also want one that puts the "never worse off for learning" slogan up against the usual complaint that imprecise agents can be information-averse, and one that makes her say what resolution imprecision buys her that precise second-order uncertainty couldn't — including what happens when somebody in that state gets offered a bet. Work in something about the diachronic betting / sure-loss arguments, and something about whether her view can really distinguish suspending judgment from having a middling credence. If she's the type to keep saying "that's a feature, not a bug," I want the sequence to make that harder to keep saying. Order them the way I'd actually ask them, follow-up right under each question. American English, please.
