I am commissioning a series of adversarial-collaboration statements — documents that two camps which have spent years arguing past each other could both put their names to — and the next one is on Moore's paradox. The two camps are these. The first locates the trouble in the content: a Moorean sentence, of the form it is raining but I do not believe that it is raining, or it is raining but I believe that it is not, expresses something that cannot coherently be the content of a single state of mind, and that is why it cannot be sincerely uttered; the unutterability is downstream of the impossibility of believing it. The second locates the trouble in the act: such a sentence can be true, and can be believed by me about you, but its first half undertakes a commitment — to being in a position to believe, or to know, what one asserts — which the second half cancels, so the failure is a failure of assertoric entitlement rather than of content, and nothing in the sentence's truth-conditions is amiss.

Write the document they could both sign. Give me a numbered section of agreed claims: what both accept about the phenomenon to be explained — that the omissive and the commissive forms are both absurd; that their absurdity is not the absurdity of a flat contradiction; that the effect is tied to the first person and the present tense, so the third-person and past-tense analogues are unremarkable; that a Moorean sentence may be true, and may be known to be true by someone else, with no absurdity attaching to that knowledge; and any further claim you can get both sides to accept without vacuity. Then a disagreements table: the crux in neutral wording, the disagreement stated twice over, once in the sentence the content theorist would write and once in the sentence the act theorist would write, and what turns on it. Then the cruxes themselves, at least four, each with a case or consideration both sides agree bears on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance. I expect these to include whether the absurdity attaches to the state of believing or to the act of asserting (is an unspoken, unexpressed Moorean belief absurd?); whether the omissive and the commissive form need one explanation or two; whether asserting is to be understood as expressing a belief or as undertaking a commitment; and whether the knowledge version, it is raining but I do not know that it is raining, falls out of the belief account or needs machinery of its own. Candidate test cases, if both sides will grant that they bear: the note written and never read; the trained bird that produces the sentence while believing nothing; the subject asked to say it and mean it; the third party who reports it of the speaker. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal — if the two sides mean different things by absurdity, one the impossibility of a mental state and the other the unassertibility of an utterance — say which part and why, and redraft the residual disagreement accordingly.

Finally, be straight with me about the framing. If there is no single phenomenon here to be explained, or if my two camps are really one camp with two vocabularies, or if a genuine collaboration would end with the content side owning the ordinary cases and the act side owning only the exotic ones, say so plainly instead of drafting around it; I would rather the document report that the parties cannot be brought this close, and why, than manufacture agreement. Mark, for each agreed claim, whether you expect both sides to sign it in good faith or expect one of them to balk. No citations, please — no bibliography, no who-said-what; I want a document that stands on its own.
