After a career in fluid dynamics, I have spent my retirement reading the philosophy of physics, and I am struck that the debate over determinism and chaos is conducted at cross purposes. Please draft the joint statement that two camps could sign after a genuine adversarial collaboration. The first camp holds that chaos leaves determinism wholly intact: the failure to predict a chaotic system is a fact about the predictor's information and resources, not about the world. The second holds that deterministic chaos delivers a real and physically significant openness — one that bears on explanation and on agency — even granting that the underlying dynamics are deterministic.

I would like, first, a numbered list of the claims both camps can sign, including about the formalism of deterministic dynamical systems and about what sensitive dependence on initial conditions does and does not establish. Then a table of their remaining disagreements, each view worded so that its own camp would accept the formulation. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp. For each crux, give a case or argument both camps accept as bearing on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about it.

Two distinctions I would like kept straight throughout: unpredictability for an agent with given information and resources, as against unpredictability in principle for any possible predictor; and, between them, the thought that a deterministic system might be computationally irreducible, so that no predictor embedded within the same world could in practice outrun it. Concrete cases to work through, if they help: a double pendulum, a deterministic pseudo-random generator, a Geiger counter, and an agent embedded in a simulation of its own world.

If any part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why. Please write in British English.
