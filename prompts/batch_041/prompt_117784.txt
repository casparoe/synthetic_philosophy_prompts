I host a monthly philosophy night for engineers at my office -- 12-15 people, mostly backend devs, zero philosophy background, 75 minutes, and I'm the one faciliating it even though I haven't studied any of this. This month I want to do Nietzsche's eternal recurrence, but not the usual journalling bit where everyone imagines reliving this moment forever, because everyone has heard that one and it dies in about five minutes. What I'm after is an activity where the group makes real commitments and then gets hit by the idea twice, in two forms that don't fit together. First as a plain claim about how the world is: the stuff of the world is finite, time is infinite, so arrangements have to come round again, which means your life comes back whether you like it or not and your feelings about that are beside the point -- the only live question is whether the argument even works, and I want the people who reach for the pigeonhole principle to feel how cheap that move is. Then second, as something that isn't a claim about the world at all but a test of whether you'd will your life again, where it's all about your attitude and there's nothing to be right or wrong about. The moment I really want them to feel is when those two slide into each other -- when someone uses the affirmation reading to answer the factual one, or decides the fact has to be true because the test feels meaningful. So give me a game with rules and a payoff for the first half (no real money, but something where they have to commit in front of everyone), a forced re-decision for the second, the actual materials I need to print, and a debrief that maps what happened onto both readings and says straight out which one the activity can't reproduce. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it live. No lecture up front, and don't say the name of the idea until the debrief.
