The first Polymath project -- Timothy Gowers's 2009 open online collaboration to find a new proof of the density Hales-Jewett theorem, conducted through blog comments and a wiki, with many mathematicians contributing small pieces -- is often held up as a demonstration that hard mathematical problems can be solved by many small, mostly independent contributions. I want you to analyze that episode through the lens of HCH and the factored cognition hypothesis. HCH is the thought experiment in which a single agent can delegate subquestions to copies of itself, each of which can delegate further, and the root receives an answer assembled from the tree; factored cognition is the hypothesis that sophisticated reasoning can be decomposed into many small, mostly independent tasks that individual agents can perform without knowing the big picture. Give me a disciplined mapping: who or what plays the role of the root agent, what the subquestions are, what counts as a small and mostly independent contribution, how answers are passed back up, and what plays the role of the shared memory. Then give me the places where the idealization breaks, and say where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak. The project had a visible coordinator, a wiki that served as a common store, and contributors who sometimes needed to see the whole proof to know what was missing; HCH assumes no central coordinator and agents who do not need the big picture. Did the project's success vindicate the factored cognition hypothesis, or did it succeed because of features the hypothesis leaves out? Finally, tell me what the episode shows about the theory that the theory itself did not anticipate -- in particular, whether the coordinator is an accidental feature of this case or a necessary component of any real factored deliberation, and whether the wiki, as a shared artifact, does work that the HCH tree cannot represent. Separate what is settled among people who study HCH and factored cognition from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
