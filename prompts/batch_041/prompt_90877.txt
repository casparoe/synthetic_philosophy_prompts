For some years now I have been sitting in on a fortnightly philosophy reading group at my old institute — a mixed bag of retired scientists and two implausibly patient graduate students — and we have spent the last three sessions on a thought experiment I cannot get out of my system. As I understand it (and I am working from a borrowed copy and a failing memory, so do correct my account of the apparatus if it matters), Hofstadter asks us to imagine an arena — a careenium — filled with an enormous number of tiny magnetic marbles. Left to themselves the marbles collide, cling briefly, form clusters, and break apart again; and it is these shifting clusters, rather than any individual marble, that he calls simmballs and treats as the bearers of symbols. The claim that has my colleagues nodding and me fuming is that the simmball level is not merely a convenient description but a genuine causal level: the symbols, we are told, push the substrate around, and not the other way about. I spent thirty years recording the behaviour of single cells and never once needed a symbol to predict the next spike, so I am not inclined to nod.

What I want from you is an opponent rather than an essay. Please take the part of a committed defender of the view that the simmball level has real causal power — someone who knows the standard objections and is not embarrassed by them, who will concede freely whatever must be conceded about the microphysics, but who will not surrender the claim that the symbolic goings-on do the pushing in any sense worth having. Stay in character until I say 'stand down'; keep each reply to a paragraph or two, so that it feels like an argument across a table rather than a treatise; do not break off to offer me a reading list or a set of references, since I am after the argument and not the bibliography; and write in British English, if only because I cannot read 'analyze' without wincing.

My opening move is this. Suppose I take the careenium seriously as a physical system and build a device — call it a sieve — that registers simmball-level patterns and, whenever it detects one, applies a nudge to precisely those marbles that constitute the cluster. If your symbols really do push, then the sieve ought to be a lever on the symbolic level: I should be able to change what the careenium thinks by acting on its symbols as such. Yet every nudge I apply is a force on a marble, delivered by a coil, at a particular place and time; my integration of the marble equations predicts the entire subsequent history without ever mentioning a symbol; and the symbol enters my account only as a pattern I could have derived from that integration, never as a term within it. So when you say the symbols push the marbles, what have you added to the coil? And if your answer is 'nothing, but the coil-pushing is not the whole story', then I shall want to know what work the extra story is doing that my integration is not already doing. Kindly deal with that before we go any further.
