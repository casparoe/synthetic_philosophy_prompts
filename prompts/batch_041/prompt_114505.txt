For months now a philosopher friend and I have been stuck in the same fight, and at this point I want a diagnosis rather than a winner. It's about whether you should treat yourself as a random sample drawn from all the observers there are — so that simply finding yourself here, now, tells you something about how many observers the world will ever contain — or whether your existence should instead just push you toward there being more observers, period. Every time we run it we hit the same two dead ends. I say: fine, but then some guy sitting alone in a room can settle cosmology by pure reasoning; I even wrote him as a novelist who deduces she has millions of readers without ever checking her sales. She says: fine, but then you're stuck with the doomsday conclusion, and nobody buys that. My hunch is that the whole thing isn't really about probability at all but about whether there's a fact of the matter about which observer I am, and whether possible people are even the kind of thing you can count. Is that right? Or is it a genuine disagreement about the axioms — whether "the chance that I'm this observer" is well defined in the first place, or whether indexical credences have to behave like ordinary ones? Or is it just two sets of gut feelings about huge and infinite cases that neither of us can argue for, dressed up in math? And given your answer, whats the thing that would actually count as progress — a theorem, a distinction, giving up on the question — or is there honestly nothing to be done here. I dont need references, I just want the shape of the disagreement.
