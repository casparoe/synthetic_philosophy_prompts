My buddy and I got into a stupidly long fight last night about a plaque we're putting up in our town. Short version: back in 1928 a factory fire killed a bunch of people, and the town has always remembered it as a freak accident, but the historical society dug up letters showing the owner ignored safety warnings for years. Now I have to give the dedication speech at the unveiling next Saturday, in front of the victims' families, the old-timers who still tell the accident story, and like three actual historians who will notice if I fudge anything. My friend says the record wins, period. I say a community's memory isn't just a bad first draft of history, it's how people hold each other and who gets to count as ours. The tricky thing is the plaque has to be an apology and a factual claim at the same time, and those two things don't succeed or fail in the same way.

Write the speech. Maybe four minutes, plain spoken, no footnotes, no hedging like some say, and it has to do real structural work with the memory/history split, not just mention it. The families need to feel the town still honors their dead, and the historians need to not hear me lie. If there's a way to say both true things at once, find it. End with a single line that starts with Summary: and then one sentence saying what the speech decides about which should give way.
