A friend in my philsophy of mind reading group has been fighting with me for months about whether dreams are experiences. She's a Malcolm person: dreams aren't experiences at all, they're just the story you're disposed to produce when you wake up, and it's a category mistake to ask what was going on in the sleeper's head. I've been the Dennett side, though I'll admit I've never been totally sure whether Dennett ends up agreeing with Malcolm in a fancier way, since he's so suspicious of the assumption that the dream narrative you remember lines up with anything that actually happened overnight.

Write the joint statement the two of us could both sign, as an advesarial collaboration. Specifically:

1. A numbered list of claims we agree on -- about dream reports and what they're reports of, about what the sleep-lab material does and doesn't show (waking people mid-REM, people who insist they never dream, the lucid dreamer who signals with her eyes, the dream that folds in a noise from the room), and about what work the word 'experience' is doing in the question.
2. A short table of the remaining disagreements, each row written so that the side it belongs to would accept that wording as fair to its own view.
3. The cruxes -- the sub-questions where an answer would actually move one of us.
4. For each crux, a case or argument we'd both agree is relevant, with each side's prediction about it written down in advance.

Add a case or two I haven't given you -- a dream you're certain you had but can't recall a thing about, and a 'dream' you only realise was confabulated once you notice you were half awake. If part of our fight is just verbal, say which part and why. And don't smooth over the possibility that we're both wrong in the same direction.

Under 800 words, and write it as the document itself, not a report about a document.
