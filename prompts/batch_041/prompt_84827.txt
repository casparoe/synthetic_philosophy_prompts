I teach a third-year seminar on skill and knowledge — Ryle's old knowing-how / knowing-that distinction, and whether the intellectualist move survives contact with the regress objection. The intellectualist move being roughly: knowing how to do something just is knowing, of some way of doing it, that it is a way for you to do it; and the regress being the worry that every such piece of propositional knowledge needs another bit of know-how to apply, so you never get off the ground. By week eight the students are fried, so instead of a final essay I'm having them write a joke, and I promised I'd write one too rather than just grading theirs. So: write me a joke about this. It has to be funny to someone who's actually read the debate, meaning the punchline turns on the actual content — not "philosophers can't tie their shoes" or "philosophy is useless" or any other generic nerd joke that would work equally well about chemists. The regress is fair game, the coaching case is fair game (the person who can no longer physically do the thing but can still talk you through it, which is the sort of example everyone reaches for), and so is the slightly awkward fact that both sides want "she knows how" to come out true in more or less the same situations while disagreeing wildly about whats happening underneath.

"Two philosophers walk into a bar" is fine if you can make the mechanics land; so is something short and stupid, and dark is fine too. If it helps, the colleague I'm quietly competing with is a serious rock climber and a card-carrying intellectualist, so a climbing joke would be a bonus — but don't force it. Just don't hand me something a first-year could have written after skimming the first three pages; the whole point is that the audience knows where the bodies are buried.
