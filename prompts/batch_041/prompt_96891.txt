I'm a novelist, and I've been keeping a private list of moments where somebody takes another person's word about what's right — sometimes me, sometimes a character — and I keep having different reactions to them without being able to say why. I want a rule. Heres the list, with my gut on each one.

My friend reads a draft of mine and says the way I wrote the fat neighbor is degrading. I don't see it. I take her word and rewrite him. Feels fine. More than fine, feels like what a good writer does.

Same friend tells me my brother is manipulating me and I should stop speaking to him. I believe her, mostly. But it feels wrong to let her settle that one. Like I've handed over something that was mine to do. (Complication: she's never liked him and would like me to herself. Does that change it? I don't think it changes the shape of the thing, but I notice it.)

A grad student I know reads somewhere that eating meat is wrong, isn't persuaded by any argument she could give herself, just trusts the source, goes vegetarian. I don't think she did anything wrong. Maybe she doesn't understand why. So what.

Somebody tells me I was wrong to feel relieved when a difficult person finally died. Not wrong to say it — wrong to feel it. That one I don't think anyone can settle for me. Even if they're right, I can't take it from them.

My six-year-old asks if it's ok to laugh at a kid with a stutter. I say no. She believes me, full stop, no reasons. Completely fine, obviously.

I tell a grown friend the same kind of thing — that a joke he keeps making about a colleague is cruel — and he just accepts it. Doesn't push back, doesn't seem to see it, thanks me and stops. That one makes me squirmy. I'd rather he argued with me.

I pick which charity to give to by copying a friend whose judgment I trust. No research, no reasoning. Fine.

I decide whether to forgive my father by copying a friend. Not fine. Same structure, opposite verdict, and I can't tell you why.

Two things I've noticed that may or may not be the same thing. First, believing something on someone's say-so seems to come apart from letting them make the call for me — the grad student takes a verdict off a page but still makes her own call; my friend who drops the joke takes my verdict and that's the part that bothers me. Second, I've started calling these "hand-me-down verdicts," a moral judgment you're using that you got off somebody else, and I can't tell if that's a real distinction or just a phrase I invented to save my intuitions.

So: is there a principle that gets all eight, or close? If there isn't one, give me the two or three candidates and say which of my verdicts each has to throw overboard — I'd rather lose a verdict than keep a principle with a clause invented for every case. Tell me what each clause is doing and which case forced it. Then tell me what the surviving account predicts for one I haven't listed: an editor tells me my finished novel is morally irresponsible to publish, I believe her, and I pull the book without ever seeing it myself. And say honestly whether "hand-me-down verdict" is tracking anything real or whether I'm just dressing up a muddle.
