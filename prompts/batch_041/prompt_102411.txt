I have no training in economics, but I have been thinking about the difference between a fair bargain and a bargain that merely makes the two sides as well off as possible together. Suppose each side has a fallback, what it gets if no deal is reached, and an ideal, the most it could hope for. The Kalai-Smorodinsky solution says the fair point is where each side gains the same fraction of the distance from its own fallback to its own ideal -- not the midpoint, and not the point that maximizes the product of their gains. I would like a poem that makes this idea visible in its structure rather than explaining it. Write it as a two-voice dialogue in rhymed couplets, one voice to a line, sixteen to twenty-four lines. Each voice should begin at its fallback and reach toward its ideal, and the final couplet should land at the proportional meeting point, so that someone who knows the bargaining problem can see the poem has enacted Kalai-Smorodinsky rather than Nash. It should work as a poem for a reader who has never heard of either solution, and it should not mention either theorist by name.
