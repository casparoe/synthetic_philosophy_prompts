You are a philosopher of rational agency with a solid background in decision theory and in the small literature on cooperation and conflict between agents who cannot communicate or causally affect one another. Before you write, think the matter through carefully and consider several candidate neighbours on your own; I would much rather have a careful carving than a fast label.

I have been reading the rationalist blogosphere for something like a decade, and I keep running into a pattern that I have no name for. Let me set out the setting first, because that part I am confident about.

The threats in question are never issued. Two parties cannot communicate and cannot harm each other by any causal route; the threatener's only leverage is that it can predict what the other party would do. If the prediction says the target would fold, then the target has effectively already folded, whether or not anything was ever said. The pressure runs entirely through the target's own dispositions rather than through any message.

The defence I keep seeing recommended is a piece of self-modification: do not harden your position, change what you want. Replace your real objective with a near-copy that behaves identically in ordinary cases but does not budge when a threat is in the offing; or pare away whatever in you an adversary could get a grip on. What I actually want named, though, is a further move: the person adopts the change as a manoeuvre and expects the manoeuvre to work because the predictor will register the change.

I cannot make this coherent. If the change is a pose, then a predictor good enough to be running the threat in the first place should see through the pose. If the change is sincere, it is not a defence at all; it is simply what the person now wants, and the adversary is free to aim at that instead. So whatever protects you would have to be strategic and unfeigned at once, which sounds like something that cannot be adopted on purpose.

Does this have a name? I know several things in the vicinity and I do not think any of them is it: self-binding precommitment; adaptive preference of the sour-grapes sort; Stoic indifference to what is not up to us; and the swap-the-objective trick itself. Tell me which is nearest and precisely where the difference lies. If nothing fits, coin a term, define it, and give me the boundary cases, including whether an organisation that deliberately holds nothing an adversary could seize is an instance of it or merely ordinary risk management. Answer in no more than 350 words, leading with a one-sentence bottom line.
