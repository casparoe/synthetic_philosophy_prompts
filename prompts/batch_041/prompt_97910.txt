Someone in my reading group keeps saying the mind is to the brain as software is to hardware — same program, different machine — and leans on it to conclude that psychology is autonomous from neuroscience: you could no more read a thought off a synapse than read a spreadsheet off a laptop's circuit diagram, and a mind could in principle run on silicon. I get why its tempting, but I want to know how far it actually holds. Give me the correspondence table first — what plays the role of the program, the hardware, running the program — then where the mapping breaks: is "the same program" something the machine fixes or something we decide by choosing to ignore certain differences, does a brain come with something like an operating system that makes it universal the way a computer is, and does the analogy quietly smuggle in a designed, substrate-independent medium that biological tissue might not have. Then tell me whether the autonomy conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole time — and if there's a better picture for the same point, what it would be.
