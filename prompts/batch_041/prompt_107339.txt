I'm a TA for a mid-level undergraduate metaphysics course, and I'm grading the short-answer portion of the unit exam on dispositions. The question, worth ten points, read: "State the simple conditional analysis of dispositional ascriptions. Explain the finkish-disposition objection to it. Explain how masking cases differ from finkish cases, and say whether the failure of the analysis shows that dispositions are not intrinsic properties of their bearers." The student wrote:

"The simple conditional analysis holds that an object has a disposition exactly when it would manifest the corresponding behavior upon the relevant stimulus: x is disposed to M when S if and only if, were S to occur, x would M. The standard objection is the finkish case. Imagine a glass that is genuinely fragile, but which a sorcerer has enchanted so that the moment it is struck, its internal structure is altered and it does not shatter. The glass is fragile right up until the strike, yet it would not break if struck, so the analysis fails in the disposition-to-counterfactual direction: the disposition is present even though the counterfactual is false. Masking cases, such as a fragile glass packed in bubble wrap, look like the same problem in different clothing. Here, too, an external factor intervenes so that the glass does not break when struck. And here, too, the intervening factor has, for the moment, removed the fragility — the packing prevents the disposition from being exercised, which is just to say it is no longer there to be exercised. So finking and masking are two names for a single phenomenon: an extrinsic interferer that cancels the disposition at the moment of manifestation. Because the analysis breaks down in these cases, dispositions cannot be intrinsic properties of their bearers. If fragility were fixed entirely by what is intrinsic to the glass, then no external sorcerer or layer of bubble wrap could change whether the glass breaks when struck. Since such external factors plainly do change this, fragility must be at least partly extrinsic."
