I'm assembling a diagnostic for a foundations-of-mathematics reading group: a decision tree that sorts a mathematician by which choice principles they actually accept, rather than by the label they would give themselves. Build the tree from the logical space first and locate the named positions in it second.

Each branch node must be a question someone can answer without already knowing where the answer leads. Work through at least these separations: whether every existence claim must in principle be witnessable by a definable object; whether excluded middle is available for arbitrary infinite sets; whether every set of reals may be assumed Lebesgue measurable; whether the well-ordering theorem is a truth about arbitrary sets or only a convenience; and whether there is one true universe of sets or many equally legitimate ones. Do not let any node collapse into asking whether the axiom of choice is true.

The leaves must include: classical ZFC realism; Bishop-style constructivism; the predicativist who refuses impredicative power sets; the determinacy advocate who trades the axiom of choice for determinacy on sets of reals; and the multiverse or formalist view on which choice is neither true nor false but one axiom among alternatives. Add any further positions the space forces. Mark combinations that no one occupies, and say for each whether the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate, and name the extra question that would separate them.

Route these cases through the tree where they bear on a branch: the shoes-and-socks distinction between a countable family of pairs with a definable selector and one without; a well-ordering of the reals; Banach–Tarski; the existence of a non-measurable set; Zorn's lemma as a working tool in algebra; and dependent choice in ordinary analysis.

Return a single JSON object with these fields: nodes, an array in which each element has id, question, and answers (an array of objects with answer and next); leaves, an array in which each element has id, path (the sequence of answers leading to it), position, defining_commitment, and most_awkward_commitment; unseparated_pairs, an array of objects with leaf_a, leaf_b, and missing_question; and unoccupied_combinations, an array of objects with path and diagnosis.
