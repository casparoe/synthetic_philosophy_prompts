I retired from physics a few years ago and have been sitting in on an upper-level seminar on the philosophy of quantum mechanics; somehow they've let me set one of the final questions. I'd like an essay question on quantum immortality / quantum suicide that a sharp fourth-year cant answer by saying that Everett branching means there's always a branch where you survive.

The thing I want them to actually trip over is the limit. In the standard setup the survivor branch carries weight 2^−N after N rounds, so surviving forever is at best an infinite path of measure zero; and if you make the trigger continuous instead of a spin — a decaying atom rather than a coin flip — there's a branch for every finite survival time and none for infinite survival. So the question should make them say whether the survivor argument commutes with taking the limit, and what that does to the claim that QM predicts you never die. The other thing I want them to face is the credence side: if branch weight is what credence tracks, the survivor sits on a vanishing-weight branch and there's no immortality in any interesting sense; if bare existence of a branch is enough for "I'll experience it," they owe an account of why the survivor shouldn't be surprised — and probably of what makes the survivor on that branch the same person as the one who walked in.

Please write the question (one essay, 90 minutes, 20 marks), a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme that names the moves a top answer has to make and the specific errors that should cost marks — e.g. sliding from "for every finite N there's a branch where I survive N rounds" to "there's a branch where I survive all rounds," or treating "low weight" as if it meant "won't happen to me." And if my framing is muddled — if I'm misusing measure zero about branches, or if the continuous case doesn't actually change anything — say so plainly and tell me how to repair the question rather than writing it anyway. I'd rather end up with a good question than one that just flatters my hunch.
