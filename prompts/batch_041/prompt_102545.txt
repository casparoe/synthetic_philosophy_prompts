I'm a PhD student in epistemology drafting a chapter on Williamson's anti-luminosity argument, and I'd like your help getting the central claim straight before I build on it. As I read the argument, it establishes that no condition of a subject is luminous — that is, that for any condition you might be in, there are cases in which you are in it but are not in a position to know that you are. Taken at face value, that seems to mean introspection can never yield knowledge of my own present mental states: when I sincerely judge that I am in pain, or that I now believe it is raining, the judgment never amounts to knowledge. My plan is to treat this as a general scepticism about the mental and then argue that it is more tolerable than it sounds, on the grounds that the states themselves are still there to be felt even if we cannot know them. Is that the right way to set up the chapter, or am I reading more into the argument than it actually delivers?
