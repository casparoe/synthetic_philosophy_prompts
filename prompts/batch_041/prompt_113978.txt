I run a graduate reading group on moral uncertainty and want a one-page handout that sorts participants by the position they actually hold rather than by the label they favor. Please build a decision tree with eight to ten branch nodes, each phrased as a question someone can answer without knowing where it leads, and six to eight leaves, each named and paired with one sentence on its most awkward commitment; build the tree from the questions rather than from a list of names, and flag any pair of leaves it cannot genuinely separate. Branch on at least these distinctions: whether uncertainty about what is right is a species of uncertainty about the world or a separate practical problem; whether the verdicts of the theories one takes seriously can be placed on a common scale at all; whether the unit being chosen is an act, a policy, or a whole life; whether a theory's influence should track credence alone, credence adjusted for how much its internal stakes vary, or neither; and how theories are counted, given that splitting one view into two seems to double its say. The leaves must include maximizing expected choiceworthiness, credence-weighted compromise among theories (the parliamentary-style view), deferring to the highest-credence theory, a hedging view that trades off against the worst case, and a bespoke position I want located: a view on which the highest-credence theory holds a veto over acts it forbids, while among the acts it permits you maximize expected choiceworthiness. Put these cases at the nodes: a tiny credence in a theory that calls eating meat a grave wrong; two theories that agree on the ranking of two options but not on how much is at stake; and a theory that assigns zero value to what another ranks as the single most important thing. Note any unoccupied combinations of answers and say briefly whether they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
