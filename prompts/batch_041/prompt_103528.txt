We just went through the typogenetics bit again — the toy formal system where DNA-ish strands get translated into little enzymes and the enzymes chew on strands and shuffle them around, and if you arrange things right you get a strand that winds up manufacturing copies of itself. Separately I'm TAing a cross-listed grad seminar (something like "formal systems and self-reference") and I have to write the take-home final. I want ONE essay question on the DNA-Gödel isomorphism that a student can't just answer by paraphrasing the book.

Here's the hobbyhorse I want the question to force them to actually test, and I'm honestly not sure I'm right about it. I think when people get worked up about the DNA-Gödel isomorphism they're conflating two seperate claims. Claim one is the von Neumann-ish thing: DNA is a description that's also a set of instructions — it gets read AND executed — so a thing can build a copy of itself by carrying a description of itself plus the machinery to run that description. Claim two is the Gödel thing: you rig up a coding so a system can talk about its own descriptions, and then the diagonal / fixed-point move hands you a sentence that says something about itself. And my suspicion is that claim two is where all the genuine self-referential magic lives, and that claim one — self-replication — has basically nothing to do with self-reference. A photocopier replicates without referring to anything; a quine spits out its own source without its source "meaning" anything about itself in the Gödelian sense. So the tidy equation self-replication = self-reference might just be a con.

What I want from you: write that one essay question, pitched at grad students, that makes them (a) prise those two apart, (b) come up with a case of replication-without-reference and a case of reference-without-replication to actually pin the distinction down, and (c) say where the typogenetics self-copying strand lands — is it doing the Gödel thing, the von Neumann thing, both, or neither, and what would have to be true of it to count as genuinely self-referential rather than just causally self-applying. Keep the question self-contained: assume students can't google anything during the take-home, and I don't want it hinging on whether they memorized the exact mechanics, so build any setup they need into the question itself.

Then give me a model answer (say ~600 words) and a marking scheme. Marking scheme: 5 must-hit points, 4 marks each (20 total), and for each point a one-line note on how students typically blow it. I want an A answer and a B answer separated by named moves, not by length or polish. And pls make sure the question can't be answered by someone who only remembers that "DNA is a code and Gödel numbers are a code" — that recite-y answer is exactly what I'm trying to kill.
