I'm a novelist, and next month I'm giving the toast at my sister's wedding. She's a philosopher and for the last ten years shes been circling Sen's liberal paradox — the collision between letting each person decide a couple of things that are purely their own business, and the rule that if every single person prefers one option to another then the group should too, which blows up the moment people start having opinions about each other's private lives. The problem: half the room is her department, who will notice instantly if I get it wrong, and the other half is our family, who will glaze over if I turn a toast into a seminar. Write me four or five minutes that actually run on the paradox rather than name-check it — so the guests' loud opinions about how the couple ought to live get handled the way the paradox handles them, the couple's own sphere stays theirs, and the aunts still laugh and tear up in the right places. Don't cite anything or say what year the paper came out, I'd only mangle it.
