Hey, I'm a junior and I somehow got put in charge of running the activity for my school's politics club next week, and I need help designing it. I get one 55 minute block, about 18 kids, ages 16-18, one normal classroom with a whiteboard and paper. Maybe half the group is really into politics and already has strong opinions, the other half came because their friends did. No computers, and I'm on my own, so nothing that needs a budget or a second person.

Heres what I want them to walk out feeling, and I don't want to just stand up and tell them it. First, that left and right isn't really one line — it's more like a bunch of seperate questions that got bundled into two packages, and the bundling is kind of a historical accident. Like in some countries the religious voters are the ones who want the big welfare state and in other places religious voters are the small government ones, so the package clearly isn't glued together by logic. Second, that what actually holds a "side" together might be more like a coalition or a bargain — or just being against the same people — than a shared principle. And third, the weird part: apparently when researchers ask ordinary people a bunch of questions across different issues, their answers barely hang together at all, while politicians' answers hang together alot. I want the kids to find that out about themselves, not hear it.

So the activity should get them out of their chairs and taking positions, and then get them stuck — where they've placed themselves on a line and then a question comes that drags them off it, or where they have to write a shared platform with someone on their own side and realize they can't agree on what the side even is. Don't name any of the theory until the very end.

Two things I'm worried about: it turning into a screaming match about a current election or specific politicians (I want it about positions, not parties, and no real names), and the kids who don't have opinions feeling dumb or getting put on the spot.

Can you give me the rules, the materials, a minute by minute timeline, the debrief questions that tie what happened back to the idea, what will definately go wrong the first time I run it and what to do when it does, and an honest note on which part of the idea the game just can't show them so I have to say it myself?
