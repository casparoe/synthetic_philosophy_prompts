I've got two friends who've been going at each other for like two years about moral responsability, and I want to force them to write the document they should've written ages ago instead of just yelling at each other. One's a hard-line incompatibilist who thinks Galen Strawson's Basic Argument basically closes the case -- nobody can be ultimately responsible for anything, because to be responsible for an action you'd have to be responsible for the self that produced it, and that just kicks the can back forever. The other's a compatibilist who thinks the whole thing is a trick of framing -- she'll grant the regress as a description but denies it has the teeth Strawson thinks it has, and thinks responsibility only needs the right kind of reasons-responsiveness and ownership, not some impossible act of self-creation.

Draft the joint statement they could both actually sign. Not a debate transcript, not a verdict on who wins -- a constructive document. I want:

1. A numbered list of agreed claims: what both accept about the structure of the argument, about what ultimate responsibility would even have to be, about what the regress does and doesn't show, about the difference between being caused to be a certain way and being responsible for being that way. Keep them non-vacuous -- if a claim is so watered down nobody would deny it, cut it.

2. A disagreements table, where each row states one live disagreement, with the two positions worded so that the person holding each one would say "yeah, that's my view." No strawmen on either side.

3. The cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling it would actually move at least one of them. My guess is one is whether the regress is vicious or just infinite-but-harmless, and another is whether a person can "own" their character in a way that stops the regress without self-creation. Tell me if those are the real cruxes or if there's something sitting underneath them.

4. For each crux, a test case or argument that BOTH would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Build the whole thing around a case they keep sliding past, which I'll call the Rebuilt Rhea case: Rhea has a character that was deliberately engineered -- pick whatever mechanism, a heavy-handed upbringing, a neural intervention, doesn't matter -- and at some point she wholeheartedly endorses the person she's become and wouldn't change a thing. Compare her with someone whose character came from an ordinary upbringing they also wholeheartedly endorse. Both camps have to answer: does Rhea's endorsement do any work? Can the compatibilist use "she endorses it" to stop the regress -- and if so, why doesn't that same move work for the ordinary person and make the regress vanish entirely? And if it doesn't work for Rhea, what's the principled difference? Make the incompatibilist write out exactly what she predicts about this case and the compatibilist write out exactly what she predicts, and flag clearly any place they agree the case is just silent.

Also, if any chunk of their disagreement turns out to be purely verbal -- like if they mean different things by "responsible" or "ultimately" -- say so and say which chunk. And no papers, no reading list, no citations; neither of them can look anything up right now, so just work it out from scratch.

American English, and keep it tight enough that both of them would actually put their names on it.
