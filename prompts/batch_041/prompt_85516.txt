Ok, I've never studied this stuff formally — I just draw letters for fun and I've gotten weirdly hung up on one question: what actually makes an 'a' an 'a'? I have gut feelings about a bunch of cases and I want to know if there's a rule behind them or if I'm just making it up as I go.

- The double-storey 'a' (the kind with the little hood on top, like in a newspaper font) and the single-storey one (like in Futura — just a circle with a stick on the right): both obviously a's, I'd never even question it.
- The 'a' at the end of a word in really loopy handwriting, basically a small circle with a tail flicking to the right: still an 'a', no doubt.
- A plain circle, nothing else: that's an 'o'. Not an 'a'. Even though in that loopy handwriting the 'a' is almost a circle. The tail matters.
- Now shrink that tail bit by bit until you can't see it. At some point it stops being an 'a' and becomes an 'o', and I can't tell you where — but I feel like there IS a fact about which one it is at each step, not just a fuzzy zone. This bugs me.
- A solid filled-in blob roughly in the shape of an 'a': still an 'a', as long as it reads as one. Same for a heavy blackletter 'a' that's all spikes and diamonds — a.
- Rotate an 'a' 180 degrees: NOT an 'a'. It's a rotated 'a'. Mirror it and same deal.
- Distort an 'a' until it's just a wavy line: not an 'a' anymore, obviously, but there's no clean line between 'very stylised a' and 'not an a'.
- Two shapes with the exact same outline, but one sits in the 'a' slot on an alphabet chart and the other is a doodle I made without thinking about it: I want to say the first is an 'a' and the second isn't, which feels like cheating since they look identical.
- An 'a' with a tiny extra serif, or a dot floating above it: still an 'a', the dot's just decoration.

So is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? I suspect I can't have both 'the shape alone decides' and 'the intention or context decides,' so tell me which one I should give up and why. Then say what your principle predicts for a case I haven't listed, and be upfront about where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong or what would make you change your verdict. Keep the whole thing under 450 words.
