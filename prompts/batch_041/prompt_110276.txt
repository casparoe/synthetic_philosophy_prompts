Okay so I'm a novelist — two books out, a year into the third, which is set inside a big teaching hospital, and I've been sitting in on admin meetings for research and taking way too many notes. The other week the chief of surgery shot down a proposal to tie part of the surgeons' bonuses to a complication-rate metric, and he did it by dropping the old line, "when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure," like it was a law of physics and that was the end of the discussion. Everyone nodded and we moved on. I've heard the same sentence maybe a dozen times since, always right when somebody's about to suggest measuring something, and it always seems to be doing the job of ending the conversation rather than starting it. But I can't tell if its a deep truth or just a truism in a nice suit — because if it literally meant any target instantly turns worthless, then half of medicine (and honestly half of my day job) couldn't function, and yet it can't be nothing either, because you do hear about call centers where everyone hangs up on customers to hit the average-handle-time number, or schools that push kids through so the scores look good.

What I want from you is a menu, not a verdict — the distinct precise claims that one sentence could actually be making, laid out from the boring end to the radical end. Like: it's just reminding you a measure is only ever a proxy for the thing you care about; or it's saying once people know the number counts they'll optimise the number instead of the goal; or something stronger, that targeting a measure changes the underlying reality so the number stops tracking anything real even when nobody's cheating; or maybe the strange one where the number was never a good measure to begin with and targeting just exposes it. For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude, a genuinely contentious empirical claim, or just false — and then say which one the chief was probably leaning on, and whether any reading actually gets him his conclusion (no bonuses tied to the metric) or whether he'd need a much stronger premise to earn it. I also keep hearing there are a couple of near-identical "laws" floating around with different names and I can never keep straight which is which or whether they're even different claims, so if thats relevant, untangle that too. Lay it out as a list I can steal for a scene where my data-scientist character pushes back on him and he has to actually defend the thing instead of just saying it.
