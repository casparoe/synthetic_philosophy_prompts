Here's my opening, two parts.

First: the regress only bites if being responsible for what I do requires being responsible for the self that did it. But nobody except you was asking for that. When I hold the drunk driver responsible, I'm not claiming he authored himself from nothing; I'm claiming the act flowed from his settled character in the ordinary way, so it's fair to demand an account from him and to let my attitudes toward him shift. You need a further premise -- that only ultimate, self-originating responsibility counts as the real thing -- and that premise is the whole ballgame, so it can't just be assumed. Otherwise the argument proves that nobody is causa sui, which nobody ever doubted, and then quietly swaps that in for "nobody is responsible."

Second, a case. My aunt spent about a decade deliberately rebuilding herself: therapy, hard choices, dropped people, changed how she reacts to being crossed. She isnt the same person who used to fly off the handle, she knows it, and she takes credit for it. Now she does something cruel, and it comes straight out of the character she built on purpose. Is she ultimately responsible for that? If your answer is no, I want to see whether the "no" is doing any work -- because it looks like it would be "no" no matter what I described, which makes me suspect the argument isn't a discovery about her, it's a decision you made in advance about what the word is allowed to mean.
