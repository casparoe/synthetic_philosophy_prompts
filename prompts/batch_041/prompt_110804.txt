Alright, I'm trying to write up my view on perception and I want a consistency check before I commit to it. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Perceptual experience represents the world — it has accuracy conditions, it's not just raw feels.
(2) Perception is a species of unconscious inference (Helmholtz-flavored, or predictive processing, take your pick), so top-down influence on what you end up seeing is the rule rather than an exotic exception.
(3) Experience justifies belief non-inferentially: if it looks red to me, I'm prima facie justified in believing it's red, and I dont have to first have evidence that my eyes are working.
(4) Cognitive penetration is real — beliefs and desires change what you actually see, not just what you believe about what you see. A radiologist who's learned what a tumour looks like sees something different in the same scan than I do.
(5) The penetrating state doesn't have to be justified. A baseless belief can shape what you see.
(6) You can't believe your way out of the Muller-Lyer illusion — knowing the lines are equal doesn't make them stop looking unequal — and I take that to show perception is encapsulated.
(7) Most of the 'top-down effects' experiments are confounded with memory, judgement and response bias, and I'm pretty skeptical of that literature as a body of evidence.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest revision that gets me consistent? Rank the real tensions by severity rather than telling me they're all in the same boat, and tell me which single commitment, if I dropped it, would do the most cleaning up.
