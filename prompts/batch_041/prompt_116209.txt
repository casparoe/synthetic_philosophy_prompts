My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about a view she picked up somewhere and calls tranquilism: the claim that the only thing that's actually good is the absence of craving and suffering, and that pleasure, joy, delight, none of that counts as good in itself. She doesn't remember where she got it, so dont worry about who says it or citing anyone, just take the view as I've stated it and write me a poem I can send her this morning. Keep it under a page so it works as a text.

I want a refrain that loses a word each time it comes back, so it thins out stanza by stanza, and the last time it's due there should just be the empty space where it would have gone. However many stanzas that takes. I know it's a gimmick, make it earn it.

Three things from the fight that I want in there, because they're the interesting bits and I don't want this to turn into a poem about being calm: (1) if wanting is the problem, then wanting to be rid of wanting is still a wanting, so the goal seems to eat its own tail; (2) is an absence that nobody is there to have even a good thing, or is that just death with extra steps; (3) a life of steady low-grade misery vs. a life with a lot of joy and a little misery — on her view the joy is simply irrelevant, and I cant get past that. Don't resolve any of them, I'd rather it sit in the mess.

After the poem, a short paragraph: which of these the poem is taking a contested line on rather than a settled one, where you're least sure you've got the view itself right, and how confident you actually are about the rest.
