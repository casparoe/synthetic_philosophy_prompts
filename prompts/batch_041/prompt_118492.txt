Two colleagues have argued for a year about how a society should treat its high achievers. One holds that celebrating the successful is both just and useful, and that the urge to cut them down is envy in moral costume; the other holds that suspicion of the successful is a healthy check on undeserved advantage and on hubris, and that the first view is the self-congratulation of the lucky. Write the joint statement they could both sign after a real adversarial collaboration: the claims they agree on, including what the dispute is actually about and which cases of cutting someone down are plainly wrong or plainly justified; the points on which they still differ, each phrased in wording its own side would accept; the cruxes; and, for each crux, a case, argument, or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Test one candidate crux I suspect is doing hidden work: whether the norms in question — tall poppy syndrome, the Law of Jante, the ones that cut the conspicuously successful down to size — are parasitic on costly exit, so that they should weaken wherever people can readily leave the community that resents them, or whether they persist even then because they track desert rather than mobility. Say which parts of the disagreement, if any, are merely verbal — for instance, whether one side means punishing the successful and the other merely declining to celebrate them. Put the agreed claims in a numbered list and the disagreements in a table. Begin directly with the statement itself, with no preface or commentary.
