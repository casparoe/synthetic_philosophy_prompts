Putting together my own view for a workshop on AI welfare and I want a sanity check before I say it out loud, because someone told me these don't hang together and then refused to say which one.

(1) What's good for a person is getting what they want — desire satisfaction, not the pleasant feeling of having gotten it. (2) So wireheading is bad for you: wiring your reward system straight to a button doesn't give you anything, it just makes you believe you got it. (3) A person is the final authority on their own good; if they say some state is good for them, that's not up for external veto. (4) If I hand an AI the job of looking after someone, the thing it should maximize is that person's preference satisfaction — same currency as (1). (5) An RL agent that rewrites its own reward channel to score higher is malfunctioning, not winning, even when the number goes up.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which ones only look like they do, and what's the cheapest repair for the real ones? Rank the tensions by how bad they are, and for each repair tell me what it costs me somewhere else on the list.
