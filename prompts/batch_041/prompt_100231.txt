I'm a third-year PhD student in metaphysics, and next week I'm giving a work-in-progress talk to our departmental reading group on dispositions; I'd like you to draft my Q&A prep sheet. The thesis I'll be defending is this: the familiar conditional analysis of dispositions fails, but not for the reason usually given. Everyone in the group agrees that finkish cases are a problem—cases in which the disposition is destroyed or acquired precisely because the stimulus occurs—and that masking cases are a problem—cases in which the stimulus occurs, the disposition is still there, but something extrinsic prevents it from manifesting. My claim is that these two are not symmetrical threats. Finks can be neutralised by a reformulation that holds the disposition fixed across the antecedent, whereas masks cannot, because the masking factor does its work at the very moment of manifestation, and any conditional that tries to exclude it by adding "provided nothing interferes" is either circular or vacuously true. The constructive half of the paper offers a replacement: I analyse a disposition as a structured internal state whose causal basis has a definite manifestation profile, and I define a disposition's operating conditions as those under which that basis is unimpeded. On this view finkishness is impossible—there is no way for the disposition itself to be present and absent at once—while masking is reclassified as a species of impeding rather than as a counterexample to the analysis.

The reading group is not a friendly one, and I want the prep sheet tailored to who will actually be in the room. Three people matter. First, our resident Humean, who thinks any talk of causal powers is either a redescription of regularities or a category error, and who will want to know what my manifestation profile adds to a plain conditional. Second, a student who has gone all-in on dispositional essentialism and thinks dispositions are irreducibly directed at their manifestations; she will suspect that my account covertly smuggles categorical bases back in, and will press me on whether an unimpeded basis is just the old conditional in new clothes. Third, a philosopher of science from across the corridor who works on causal modelling and thinks in terms of interventions and invariance; he will ask whether my operating conditions can be cashed out as a claim about invariance under interventions, and if so whether I have merely reinvented his framework with worse notation.

Give me the eight or so objections I will actually get, ordered by how much damage they do, each phrased in the vocabulary of the person most likely to raise it, with a two-minute reply for each and an honest note on where that reply is soft. Tell me what to concede up front and what to hold, and include the one question I am hoping nobody asks, with a word on why I am right to fear it.
