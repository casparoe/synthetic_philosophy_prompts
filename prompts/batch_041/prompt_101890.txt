Hey, so I write a column on science and ideas for a fairly general readership, and the next one is about coordiantion problems -- the kind where everyone does the locally sensible thing and the collective result is something none of them would have picked, and crutially nobody can just stop, because stopping on your own means you eat the whole loss. The thing people online like to call Moloch, though I'd rather not lean on the name.

I want to close the column with a poem, and I want the form to do the work instead of the poem just explaining the idea to the reader. Here's what I'm after: a chain poem. Six stanzas, four lines each, free verse, no rhyme needed. The last line of each stanza has to become the first line of the next one, and the last line of the final stanza has to loop back and be the first line of the whole poem. That closed loop is the point -- I want a reader to finish it and, if they start again, find themselves right back where they began, which is honestly what these situations are like.

Setting: not the usual AI-doom or nuclear-arms examples, those are worn out. Make it small and concrete and a little funny. I'm thinking a strip of food trucks sharing one parking lot, each one going a bit cheaper and a bit louder and staying open a bit later, none of them making money, none of them able to be the one who blinks. Invent the details; don't name any real companies or people.

A few things I want it to get right, because my readers are sharper than my column deserves: there's no villain and no mastermind; each step every truck takes is individually rational given what the others are doing; the trap is stable precisely because unilateral exit is worse for whoever tries it; and the escalation should read as a ratchet, one notch per stanza. No savior ending -- no regulator swooping in, no and-then-they-all-realized.

Before you actually write it, think it through: sketch two or three candidate opening images or lines for the trucks and pick the one that best sets up the ratchet. You're a poet who genuinely understands game theory, so write like both at once.

One more thing: after the poem, in a short note, tell me how confident you are that the poem really captures the structure of these traps and isn't just gesturing at it, and single out the one spot where you're least sure you got the idea right -- or where you knowingly took a poetic liberty with it.
