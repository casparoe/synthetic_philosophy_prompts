Ok, weird question. I'm a software dev, no philosophy training, so if this is some named thing everyone already knows, just tell me the name — but honestly I mostly want the boundries, not the label.

Here's the pattern. I keep ending up in situations where two people who disagree about what's good both walk away feeling like they won, without either of them changing their mind about anything.

Instance 1: my sister's vegan, I'm not, and we used to fight about it constantly. Now we have a deal — I eat vegetarian two days a week, and she does my taxes (she's an accountant, and she thinks my side project is a waste of time, so helping me costs her almost nothing). She thinks she got a chunk of animal suffering off the books. I think I got free tax prep for eating salad twice a week. Neither of us thinks the other was right about anything.

Instance 2: at work, another lead and I disagree about where the budget should go — he wants it in infra, I want it in tooling. Last planning cycle we basically did a swap: I backed his proposal, he backed mine. Neither of us changed our view, and we each got what we wanted.

Instance 3, and I suspect this one is different: my roommate and I split the thermostat down the middle, cold on weekdays and warm on weekends, and honestly we're both mildly annoyed all the time. That one doesn't feel like the other two. Nobody won.

The thing is, I've been calling all of this "compromise," but compromise is supposed to mean both sides give something up and both end up a bit unhappy. In the first two cases nobody gave up anything they actually cared about. Each of us handed over something cheap to us and got back something expensive to us. It's less like meeting in the middle and more like... arbitrage? Seperate people, seperate value systems, and yet a deal both of them think is a win.

So: is there a name for this? And I want the edges of it, not just a word.

- Is it the same as an ordinary favour trade (I walk your dog, you fix my bike), or does it matter that what's being swapped is a *moral* thing rather than a preference?
- Is instance 2 the same phenomenon as instance 1, or something else that just looks similar from where I'm sitting?
- Is there a catch? Because it seems like if my moral behaviour is tradeable, it pays me to hold the most extreme position I can, so I've got more to sell. That feels like it should be bad, but I can't say why.
- Are there things that just can't be traded this way, even when both people would happily shake on it?

If there's no established term for it, coin one and tell me what it covers and what it doesn't. A bare name won't help me — I need to know where the line is.
