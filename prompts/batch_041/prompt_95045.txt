My closest friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether ordinary things persist by enduring or by perduring, and we kept returning to a comparison he treats as decisive. Picture an object's entire history the way a physicist would: a single loaf-shaped thing in a block of spacetime, a worm whose long axis is time. What I call the object at any one moment, he says, is nothing over and above a cross-section of that worm — a temporal part — and the worm is what really exists. On that picture, my own view, that a persisting thing is wholly present at each moment rather than spread out across them, is like insisting that a worm is wholly present at each of its cross-sections: not so much false as confused. From this he draws a strong conclusion — that endurantism is barely intelligible, and that the only reason anyone holds it is a bias toward space baked into our eyesight and our tenses.

I don't want a defense of my side; I want an honest audit of the comparison, because I suspect it is carrying more weight than it can bear. Set out the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of the worm, the cross-section, the long axis, the spatial parts, the observer — and then tell me where the mapping breaks. Separate the disanalogies that matter for his conclusion from the ones that are merely picturesque: whether time is directed while space is not; whether one moment, the present, is privileged in a way no location is; whether we can revisit a place but not a time; whether causation runs along the temporal axis in a way nothing in the spatial case does. Then say which features the worm picture quietly imports — a completed block given all at once, a standpoint outside time, the assumption that the notion of a temporal part is already coherent — and whether endurantism can be restated so that the analogy no longer reaches it. Finally, and most important: once the disanalogies are on the table, does his conclusion survive — is endurantism unintelligible rather than merely false — or was the analogy carrying that conclusion the whole way? If some better comparison captures what is really at stake between us, give me that as well.
