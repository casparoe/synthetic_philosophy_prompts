A friend in my reading group dropped this argument on me and I can't tell if its clever or just wordplay. (1) When we ask whether violence is declining, we're asking about the per-person rate, not the raw body count, since population growth would otherwise dominate the numbers. (2) A harm counts as violence if it's serious harm imposed on someone that other agents could have prevented. (3) A huge share of deaths from malnutrition, untreated disease, and the grinding attrition of pollution and neglect meet that description, and their per-person rate is not obviously falling. (4) So if the decline story is true, either those deaths aren't really violence or they get excluded from the rate. (5) But excluding them just means defining violence down to the harms that powerful states bother to count. (6) So the decline story is either false or a bookkeeping trick. (7) Either way there's no fact of the matter about whether the world is getting less violent -- only a choice of which ledger to use. I can live with (1) and (2) and I really don't want (7). Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Answer in American English.
