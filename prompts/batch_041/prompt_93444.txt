I do not have any academic background in philosophy, but for the past couple of years I have been building a personal knowledge system—notes, a calendar that nudges me, a retrieval tool, and more recently a language model that I consult while I work—and I have become stuck on a question that I believe belongs to the extended mind thesis. The familiar examples are tools such as a notebook or a phone that store information I would otherwise have to remember, and the familiar argument is that if the tool plays the same functional role as an internal process, it should count as part of the cognitive system. My system, however, seems to operate in two very different modes. In one mode it is a ledger: it holds only what I put in, returns it on request, and does not change the shape of my reasoning. In the other mode it is a partner: it takes part in the inference, offers corrections I did not ask for, and sometimes leads me to a conclusion I would not have reached alone. I suspect that the ledger case and the partner case are not merely points on a continuum, and that the philosophically interesting questions cluster around the partner case. If you had a year and a modest budget to support conceptual work on the extended mind thesis, which open questions or lines of work should be prioritized, and in what order? I am less interested in a survey of the literature than in a defended agenda: for each question, why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is for someone thinking carefully without a laboratory, and what would move it up or down the list.

Please treat the ledger and partner distinction as a serious proposal rather than a metaphor. In particular, I want to know which questions actually change the verdict about whether a partner is part of the mind—questions about the boundary of the cognitive system, about whether causal coupling is enough for constitution, about the role of trust and accountability when the partner can be wrong, about whether the partner must be integrated into one's own cognitive economy or can remain a distinct agent, and about what follows for responsibility if it is part of the mind. Rank the questions, identify which are overrated because their answers would not change much downstream, and explain what evidence or argument over the next year would most change your ranking. I do not want a reading list, citations, or a bibliography; I want the reasoning and the ordering.
