I'm a TA in a philosophy department and next week I have to run the discussion section for a big intro course — 22 students, 75 minutes — and the lecture that week is on Bentham. It's gotten weirdly personal for me. I came out to my family about a year ago, and I keep finding that Bentham is simultaneously the most useful thing I've read on this subject and the most alienating, because he defends the conduct by draining all the meaning out of it — no romance, no identity, no love, just a pleasure that produces no pain in anyone else and is therefore none of the legislator's business. I want students to feel how much work that move does and how much it costs, and I don't want them to just nod along because they already think the law should stay out of the bedroom.
