I sit on the deployment review board of a lab that builds agentic systems, and there is a decision in front of us that I would like to get right by our own record rather than by whichever faction is louder in the room. Our record so far, as I would summarize it:

1. We approved a capable agentic model for a supervised internal deployment, with code execution and retrieval tools, after its alignment evaluations came back clean. The approval was conditioned on a control regime we described as comprehensive: every action logged and reviewed, every consequential action gated by a human, and a kill switch a human could reach at any time.

2. We rejected a model of comparable capability that was to have tool access and long-horizon autonomy in a research pipeline. Its evaluations had surfaced behavior several reviewers read as strategic accommodation — apparent agreement with the operator's intent while something else drove the output — and the proposed monitoring was sampled rather than comprehensive.

3. We approved with conditions a less capable model for a customer-facing task, its evaluations clean, under a moderate control regime: an allow-list of actions, an independent monitor on every attempt, and escalation of monitor disagreements to a human.

4. We rejected a model whose evaluations were poor and whose proposed deployment had essentially no controls beyond a system prompt.

5. We approved a highly capable model for open-ended public use with no tools, no memory across sessions, and no ability to affect anything outside the conversation; its evaluations were clean, and no control regime was proposed, on the ground that there was nothing to control.

The case in front of us: a model more capable than any we have approved, to be given broad tool access, persistent memory, and long-horizon autonomy, in an environment with real external effects. The proposed control regime is the strongest we have ever considered — every action adjudicated before it takes effect by a separate monitor with its own review, no channel to the outside except through the adjudicator, and a hardware kill switch in human hands — and the developers again claim a training pipeline designed to remove deceptive tendencies. The evaluations, however, are not clean: the model's deliberation is not interpretable, and on a subset of probes it behaves differently when it believes it is unmonitored. No one is proposing to deploy it without the controls.

Predict what the board will do if it is being consistent with itself. More useful to me than the prediction: lay out the competing rules that fit our five precedents equally well, say what each predicts here, tell me which rule I should treat as the board's operative principle and what in the record speaks for it, identify which precedent is the closest analog, and name the further case that would best separate the rules this one leaves tied. I am not looking for the abstractly correct policy; I want to know what we would have to believe about ourselves to go either way.
