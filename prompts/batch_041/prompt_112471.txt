I've been going back and forth in the comments of a rationalist-adjacent blog over dialetheism — the view that some contradictions are true — and the reply I keep getting is a single sentence: from a contradiction anything follows, so a dialetheist is committed to accepting every claim whatsoever, and that isn't a position so much as a collapse. I suspect the one-liner is doing more work than it can bear, but I can't say precisely where it overreaches, and I'd rather not lean on a reading list to find out. What I want is a careful unpacking of the distinct precise claims that "from a contradiction anything follows" could be making, with a verdict on each.

Some readings I can half-see already: a bare fact about the classical consequence relation; a claim about what any minimally rational believer is committed to once they accept a contradiction; a claim that the very meanings of "not", "and" and "or" guarantee the result; and a normative claim that on finding a contradiction one must give something up. But please generate the menu yourself rather than working from mine, and mark each entry as a trivial truth about a formal system, a substantive and defensible thesis, or something actually false.

Structure the reply as follows: "The slogan and the work it was doing" (roughly 100 words, stating what the argument against me needs the sentence to deliver); "The candidate readings" (roughly 400 words, the menu, each reading stated precisely); "Verdicts" (roughly 250 words, a verdict on each reading and the reason for it); "Which reading the argument needs" (roughly 200 words, saying whether any reading both survives scrutiny and secures the conclusion, or whether the move begs the question against the dialetheist); and "Confidence" (roughly 100 words, saying how confident you are in each verdict and singling out the point you are least sure of).
