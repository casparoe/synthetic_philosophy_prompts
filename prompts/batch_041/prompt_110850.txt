I'm putting together next week's epsiode of the small philosophy of technology podcast I run, and my guest has spent a decade defending a deflationary version of the old artifacts-have-politics line: on her view a thing can entrench a political arrangement but never originate one, so the interesting question is always who built it and why, never what the thing itself does. Write me eight to ten interview questions that make that position harder to hold than she's used to. I want at least one on the entrench/originate distinction itself -- is it a principled line or just a matter of timescale and of who happens to notice -- one on the Berlin tenement key, the one you have to turn twice to get back out, so you can't help but lock the door behind you at night, which looks like an object issuing an order rather than merely enforcing one, and one on the stock parkway-bridge example, whose history critics have since disputed on empirical grounds: if the folklore is shakier than it's usually told, how much of the thesis survives? For each question give me a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, so she can't keep batting them away with "that's people, not things." And at the end, tell me how confident you are that these are the questions that actually press her, and which one you're least sure is even well-posed.
