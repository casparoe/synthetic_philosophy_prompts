I'm a self-taught person, no philosophy degree, mostly podcasts and arguing in comment sections, and I've somehow agreed to give a 15-minute talk at our town library's "AI and Society" night and then take questions. The room: a bunch of software engineers who read a lot of EA blogs and default to "just maximize total welfare" thinking; one retired hospital ethicist who is basically a Kantian and thinks consequences-talk is a dodge; two people from the interfaith council who really care about nobody's values being imposed on them; and a civil liberties lawyer who will find a way to make it about speech. My thesis, roughly: when people say "align AI with human values" they quietly assume you can add everybody's values up, and you cant. So an aligned system should only act on the slice of our values that survives as an overlapping consensus (the Rawls thing, I think) and otherwise stay put and defer to whoever is most local. Then my own extra wrinkle, the part I'm least sure I can defend: consensus is usually just whoever talks loudest or shows up in the data, so I want a "quiet party rule" where anyone affected who never shows up in the feedback at all counts as a veto holder even though we cant hear them.
