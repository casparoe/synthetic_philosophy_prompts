I have no training in philosophy — I keep the books for a small business and have never taken a course in it — but for the last two years a friend and I have been arguing about something I'm told philosophers have a name for. My view is that knowing how to do a thing is not the same as knowing facts about it. You can be a superb skier, or a person who can always tell when someone is lying, and be quite unable to state a single rule that captures what you know; the knowledge is in the doing, not in any proposition you could write down. Her view is that this must be wrong — that whenever anyone genuinely knows how to do something, there are facts they know, and the skill is nothing over and above the ability to act on those facts, well or badly.

We have reached the point where we only repeat ourselves, and I think the honest thing is to produce, together, the document we should have written long ago: a statement both of us could sign without either pretending to agree with the other. I would like it to contain four things.

First, a numbered list of claims we can both honestly accept — about skills, about explaining what one does, about what it is to know a fact, and about what it is to be able to do something.

Second, the disagreements that remain, each written in wording that the person who holds that view would accept as a fair statement of it.

Third, what we take to be the real crux or cruxes — the smaller questions that, if they were settled, would actually move one or both of us — and, for each crux, some case, test, or argument we both agree bears on it, with each of us saying in advance what we would expect to find and what would change our minds.

Fourth, whether any part of our quarrel is merely about words. I have begun to suspect that we are each using "knowing how" for two different things — the sheer ability to do it, and the grasp or understanding of it — and that some of our disagreement dissolves once those are separated. She suspects the opposite: that the difference I am pointing to is really the difference between knowing a fact in the abstract and knowing it in a way that is inseparable from being able to act on it, and that this is a distinction without a difference. I would like the document to settle which of us is right about that, or to say why neither of us is.

Two cases in particular I would ask the document to work through, because neither of us can settle them by appealing to what we already believe. The first is a woman who, after a stroke, can no longer play a piece she once played beautifully, yet can still listen to a recording and point to every wrong note and say exactly what should have been done; she insists she still knows how to play and has lost only the ability. The second is a novice who, by some surgical trick, is given the ability to play the piece faultlessly without ever having learned anything about music; she plays it perfectly and knows no facts about it at all. I want to know what each of us is obliged to say about these two women, and whether there is a way of describing both that we can each accept.
