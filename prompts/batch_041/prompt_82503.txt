Ok, I want to try the problem of dirty hands on a real case, and not the usual ones — Truman and the ticking bomb are too tidy. Take the Allied area bombing of German cities instead, with Dresden in Feb 1945 as the flashpoint: deliberate raids on city centres and the civilians in them, and as far as I can tell nobody at the top ever lost much sleep over it. Run the analysis properly. Who actually has the dirty hands — Churchill and the cabinet, Harris and Bomber Command, the crews who flew the raids, or the public that wanted it? Was the campaign justified (right from the start, so no guilt is owed) or only excused (wrong, but not blameworthy in the circumstances)? And the part I care about: does this case vindicate dirty hands as a real moral category, or dissolve it? My worry is that once you spread a decision across planners, ministers, crews and voters, nobody is answerable and "dirty hands" turns into a flattering name for whatever the state does; I've seen that called the problem of many hands. So dont sit on the fence — give me a verdict, your reasons for it, and what would change your mind.
