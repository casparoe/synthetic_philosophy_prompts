I'm finishing a short essay on the trolley stuff and want your comments before I post it. Here's the arguement in a nutshell.

Everyone drags in the doctrine of double effect to explain why it's ok to divert the trolley but not to shove the big guy off the footbridge. I think double effect is a red herring — the intention/foresight distinction is doing basically no work. What's actually doing the work is whether you're redirecting a threat that already exists or creating a brand-new one. Flip the switch and the trolley is already a lethal thing barrelling down the track; all you do is pick where it lands. Shove the guy and the trolley was never going to involve him — you have to manufacture a whole new lethal mechanism (his body hitting the track) that wouldn't have existed otherwise. So the principle I want to defend is: you may redirect an existing threat, you may not create a new one.

Where I'm stuck: (1) is the intention/foresight bit really doing *no* work, or am I throwing out something real and just not seeing it? (2) A skeptic is gonna say that diverting the trolley also creates a new threat for the guy on the side track, who was perfectly safe and would've lived otherwise — so how do I answer that without making the redirect/create line collapse? (3) Does the principle give an obviously wrong answer anywhere?

Tell me where this is weakest and what you'd push back on. No need to be nice about it.
