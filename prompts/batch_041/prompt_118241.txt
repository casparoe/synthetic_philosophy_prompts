I write software for a living and have no formal training in philosophy, so I want to be careful about how I reason through the following. A friend of mine who follows animal-welfare debates recently put an argument to me, and I genuinely cannot tell whether it is a serious result or a sleight of hand.

The argument, as he gave it:

(1) If you care about animal suffering, you should choose the diet that minimizes the total amount of animal suffering your eating causes.
(2) No diet causes zero animal suffering. Crop farming kills animals: plowing, planting, harvesting, and pest control kill large numbers of field mice, birds, and insects.
(3) A plant-based diet requires more harvested crops per calorie than a diet built around grass-fed beef, because the calories in the beef come from grass that humans cannot eat and that is grazed rather than harvested.
(4) So, per calorie, a plant-based diet causes more field-animal deaths than a grass-fed-beef diet.
(5) The same arithmetic makes chicken worse than beef: a single chicken yields far fewer calories than a single cow, so more individual animals must be killed per calorie of chicken.
(6) Therefore the diet that minimizes animal deaths is one high in grass-fed beef and low in crops and chicken.
(7) Therefore, if you care about animal suffering, you should eat a great deal of grass-fed beef.

He insists this is not a joke and that people who take animal suffering seriously have defended something close to it. But the conclusion is nearly the reverse of what everyone I know in animal welfare believes: it says the people who care most about animals should be eating the most meat, and that a vegan who eats chicken is doing the most harm of all.

I would like to keep (1) and (2) if I possibly can. (1) seems like the whole point of caring about animals, and (2) is hard to deny once you have seen a combine harvester. So I suspect the trouble lies in (3), (4), (5), or the step from (6) to (7) — though I have also been warned that this may be a case where every premise is acceptable and the conclusion is simply something I have to live with.

Tell me where the argument goes wrong, if it does, and be specific about what it costs to fix it. I especially want to know which single move a careful person who cares about animal welfare should be most suspicious of, and whether repairing it restores the ordinary view that a plant-based diet is better than a meat-heavy one, or whether I am left with something uncomfortable either way. Do not give me a neutral survey of positions; give me your own verdict, the reasoning behind it, and how confident you are in it. If you think I really should be eating grass-fed beef, then say so and defend it.
