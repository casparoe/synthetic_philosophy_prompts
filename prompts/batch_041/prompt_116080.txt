I am trying to get clear on a puzzle about levels of description that keeps coming up in my cognitive science reading group, and I would like a careful but intuitive explanation rather than a survey of the literature.

Start from Douglas Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment. A billiard table is filled with a huge number of tiny magnetic balls, called symms, which bounce around and clump together in shifting arrangements. When you zoom out, you can see stable, repeatable clumps of recognizable types, called simmballs, that track regularities in the world outside the table, since the walls change their bounciness in response to outside conditions. Hofstadter asks whether, inside the careenium, it is the symms that push each other around or the simmballs. The deflationary answer is that only the symms do any pushing and the simmball level is just a coarse-grained description of the same events. But the simmball level seems to be the level at which the system represents anything, and representation seems to require that simmball patterns make a difference to what happens next.

Here is the variation I care about. Suppose the careenium is built so that wall bounciness is not set only by the outside world but also by the current large-scale simmball configuration: when a particular type of simmball is present, the walls become stickier in a way that makes that type of simmball more likely to re-form. The coarse-grained pattern is thus one of the boundary conditions on the fine-grained dynamics. Does this make the simmball level causally efficacious, or does it merely give us a feedback loop that can be described entirely at the symm level?

Please do the following. First, give an intuitive explanation of why anyone would think the simmball level is causally inert: the argument that if the symm-level facts fix everything, then the simmball-level facts are either identical with them or excluded as causes. Second, explain the strongest reason to think that is wrong — what work the simmball level does that the symm level cannot do, and why the fact that a pattern is coarse-grained or multiply realizable does not by itself make it epiphenomenal. Third, tell me whether my feedback variation changes the answer, and if so why; in particular, say whether the answer depends on who is doing the coarse-graining — the system itself, an external interpreter, or nobody in particular.

Then lay out the distinct positions one could hold here as a menu, from the most deflationary to the most inflationary: for instance, that simmball talk is just shorthand for symm dynamics; that simmball patterns are real higher-level causes; that they are real but not causes; that their causal status is a matter of our explanatory interests rather than a fact about the system. For each position, say what it is committed to, what it would have to deny, and what conceptual or empirical consideration would tell against it. Finally, tell me which position is actually presupposed by people who insist that symbols push the substrate around, and whether that position can be stated without covertly reimporting the symm level as the only genuinely real level.

Keep the whole thing at a level I could follow without having read the primary sources, but do not water down the disagreement.
