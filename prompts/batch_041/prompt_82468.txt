I'm a backend engineer with zero formal philosophy training, and a friend who's into decision theory sent me a puzzle that has been eating my brain for a week. Its about games where you flip a fair coin until it comes up heads, and the payout depends on which flip the heads lands on. Heres the set, all with a fair coin:

A) Heads on flip n pays 2^n dollars. So 2, 4, 8, 16, and so on. The expected value adds up to 1 + 1 + 1 + ..., infinite. I'd pay maybe $20 to play this, tops. I know the EV is infinite but I'm not handing over $10,000 for it.

B) Same thing but the payout is 2^n / n. So 2, 2, 2.67, 4, 6.4, ... Each flip contributes 1/n to the sum, which still adds up to infinity (harmonic series), just slower. I'd pay $20 for this one too, same as A.

C) Identical to A except the game just stops after 30 flips — if there's no heads in the first 30 you get nothing. Now the EV is a clean $30. I'd pay $25 for this one. More than I'd pay for A. And the case I'm cutting off (no heads in 30 flips) is like a one-in-a-billion thing.

D) The alternating one: payout on flip n is (-1)^(n-1) * 2^n / n, so flip 1 pays 2, flip 2 pays -2, flip 3 pays 8/3, flip 4 pays -4, flip 5 pays 32/5, and so on. If you add it up in the natural order you get ln 2, about 69 cents. I'd pay maybe 50 cents. Though I'm told this one is nasty — the total you get depends on the order you add the terms in, and the expected winnings are infinite while the expected losses are also infinite, so in some sense there's no "expected value" at all.

E) Payout is just n dollars. Flip 3 pays 3. EV is $2. I'd pay $1.50.

The thing that bugs me: C is worse than A in every possible outcome. Same payouts, except if the game drags past 30 flips C gives me nothing while A keeps paying. Yet I'd pay more for C. Same story comparing B and C. And meanwhile I'm happy to pay 50 cents for D even though its expected loss is infinite and its 69-cent "value" is an artifact of the order I summed in, but I flatly refuse to take A's infinite EV seriously.

Is there a principle that gets all five of my numbers, or close? The only rule I can come up with on my own is "cap the tail once the odds get absurd," which sort of gets C's $30 and A's $20-ish, but then it makes D totally arbitrary and I can't tell you where the cap should be. I want something that doesn't quietly switch rules depending on which game its looking at. Then tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed (invent one if you want), and be honest about which of my five numbers it forces me to give up, because I suspect at least one of them has to go.
