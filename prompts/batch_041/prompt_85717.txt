I'm a second-year grad student and I'm in a philosophy of science seminar that's been doing the Bayesian stuff, and there's a shape of situation that keeps coming up that I can't put a name to. First time I noticed it: I do a lot of birdwatching and I'm trying to ID a warbler from a phone photo that came out blurry and a two-second recording of its song that's half drowned out by a lawnmower. I come away thinking it's probably a blackpoll, maybe a bay-breasted. Now, I definately did not learn, with certainty, "the bird is a blackpoll." But my confidence in the two clearly moved -- from something like fifty-fifty to something like eighty-twenty. And it moved in a way that feels like averaging: as if I took how the two species compare in blurry photos like this one and weighted those comparisons by how likely each species was to have produced this particular smear of pixels and this particular squawk. Second time: I have a cheap home blood pressure cuff that keeps giving me readings in a grey zone, and my confidence that I've got high blood pressure goes up, but again there's no crisp fact I've learned. My roommate, who does stats, says I'm overthinking it, which is probably true, but I want to know what the thing is called.

So: is there an established name for this situation and for the rule that handles it? The textbook move -- just condition on what you learned -- seems to presuppose that you're certain what your evidence is, which is exactly what I don't have here, so I assume someone has formalized the averaging idea, and I want to know what it's called and what it quietly assumes. Here's the part that really bugs me, though, and makes me think the standard story is incomplete. In both cases, once I start thinking about how unreliable the source is, my view of how the two options compare *given* the observation starts to shift as well. I realize my phone camera mangles yellow-green warblers specifically, so "given that I'm looking at this smear" stops being a neutral comparison between blackpoll and bay-breasted; the cuff, I read somewhere, runs high on people with thin arms, and now I don't trust the reading the same way I did a second ago. The rule I'm imagining seems to need those comparisons to stay put while only the weights move -- but in real life the comparisons move too, and then I have no idea what to do, or whether there's any principled thing to do at all.

So what I'd like is this. Is there a name for the phenomenon and the rule in the simple case? Is there a name for the extra assumption that rule needs, the one about the comparisons staying fixed? And does anyone have a name, or a worked-out account, for what you're supposed to do when that assumption fails? If the last one doesn't have a name, please coin one, define it, and tell me how it relates to the other two. I care much less about the labels than about the carving: what's actually the same and what's different across the bird, the cuff, and the case where the comparisons shift, and where the edges of each thing are. And please don't just tell me it's all subjective -- I want the structure.
