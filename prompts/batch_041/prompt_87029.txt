I have been trying to settle the problem of future contingents, and a recent conversation has convinced me that I know what the answer must be; what I still cannot do is choose a formalism. I am asking for a definite recommendation rather than a survey of options, and if my reasoning is broken I would rather be told where it breaks than be offered a tour of the alternatives.

The argument that convinces me runs as follows. Aristotle's sea-battle case shows that if a claim about tomorrow is already true today, then tomorrow is already settled. Quantum mechanics has shown that the future is not settled in advance — the decay of a given atom, for instance, is genuinely undetermined until it occurs. It follows that claims about such future events are neither true nor false today, exactly as Aristotle held, and that future-tense discourse requires a third value. Two treatments of that third value seem live. On the first, the connectives are truth-functional: a compound takes the value determined by the values of its parts, so that the disjunction of a future contingent with its negation comes out true even though neither disjunct does, and excluded middle is preserved. On the second, a future contingent is true just in case it is true on every way the future might still unfold, false just in case it is false on every such way, and carries the third value otherwise.

Which treatment should I adopt, and what does each get wrong? Two things the answer must accommodate. First, I need the openness of the future to underwrite genuine agency: if the claim about what I will choose is already true, deliberation looks like a sham, and I take the indeterminacy of the quantum world to be what rescues it. Second, a colleague predicted last week which of two offers I would accept, and she turned out to be right; I want to say that her prediction was not true when she made it, yet it is hard to see in what sense she was right if it was not true. Please commit: say whether the argument holds, where it fails if it does, and which treatment you would defend, with your reasons.
