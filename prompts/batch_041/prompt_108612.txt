Her calls so far, all of which she gives instantly:

- Three people at 10 each, versus two at 3 and one at 30: she takes the three at 10. A bigger pile doesn't impress her if someones stuck at the bottom.
- Three at 10, versus two at 10 and one at 30: she takes the second and thinks it's obvious — knocking the third person down to 10 wouldn't do anything for the other two, so what would be the point of it?
- Three at 10, versus two at 10 and one at 1000: she takes the second again and says inequality by itself isn't what bothers her.

The case I can't call. Six people. Option A brings the worst-off one, currently at 10 while the other five sit at 11, up to 11, so everyone ends at 11. Option B leaves that person at 10 and lifts the other five from 11 to 45 each.

Which way does she go? What I'm really after is the rule she's running. My guess is it's either the worst-off person's level and nothing else, or everyone counts with the worse-off counting for more; I want what each of those predicts here, which one her earlier calls actually support, and what further case would pry them apart. If some third rule fits her three calls better than either of mine, say so.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no preamble and no summing-up at the end.
