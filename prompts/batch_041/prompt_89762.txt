I'm in a Bayesian epistemology reading group and we've got stuck on the same thing three weeks running, so I want to back up and get the specification straight before anyone champions a rule. Here's the shape of it. Take a prior over two cross-cutting yes/no matters -- say, whether a letter came from a friend or from a stranger, and whether it's been franked or not; four cells, and your credences over them. Then you get evidence that isn't a proposition you could simply conditionalise on, but a conditional: the only thing you learn is 'if it came from a friend then it's franked', and maybe you learn it outright, maybe you only become more confident of it. Your credence in that conditional has to move. Now what happens to the four cells? The people I've talked to want to say either (a) just conditionalise on 'either it's not from a friend or it's franked', or (b) leave your credence that it's from a friend exactly where it was and only shift things within, or (c) move to the nearest distribution that respects the new conditional. None of those are obviously daft, none of them obviously wins either, and I keep feeling we're arguing about the candidates when we havent said what a candidate has to do.

So what I'd like from you is the desiderata: write out what any adequate rule for updating on uncertain conditional information has to deliver, each requirement with the case that motivates it and a line on why it's a genuine requirement rather than a prejudice of one camp. The cases I'd want on the list, at least: the outright-learning case, where the conditional goes to certainty and the rule has to say something sensible about your credence in the antecedent; the 'silent antecedent' case, where you'd think learning an 'if' tells you nothing at all about whether the antecedent is true; the vacuity case, where you already know the antecedent is false so the conditional is idle and shouldn't be allowed to drag your other credences about; the case where the evidence is a nudge rather than certainty; the demand that the rule not be hostage to how you phrase the constraint, since 'P(franked | friend) = 1' and some algebraic re-description of the very same constraint ought to land you in the same posterior; and the demand that it sit happily with the known results saying you can't simply treat a conditional credence as the probability of a proposition. Add whatever else you reckon belongs, and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just what one tradition happens to want.

The bit I most want is the incompatibility. I half suspect some small subset of these can't hold together, and if so I'd like you to say exactly which ones and why -- if the list turns out to be unsatisfiable, that's the actual result and I'd rather have it than a scorecard. Also tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the tidy answers. Fix the purpose for me at: a rule an ordinary agent could actually apply, given a prior and a piece of conditional evidence, not just a proof that some such distribution exists somewhere. Skip the survey of named proposals, I'll do that bit in the group.
