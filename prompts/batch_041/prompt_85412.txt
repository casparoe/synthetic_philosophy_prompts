I run a weekly reading group on AI oversight, and for several months we have been stuck on a dispute that I now suspect is less about the evidence than about the shape of the question. The dispute is between process-based supervision — giving a model feedback on the intermediate steps of its reasoning, not just the final answer — and outcome-based supervision — giving feedback only on the final answer, on the ground that any step-level signal is either a proxy for the outcome or an invitation to reward hacking. In our group, the process camp says outcome supervision teaches models to get the right answer for the wrong reasons, which shows up as brittleness under distribution shift; the outcome camp replies that process labels are just a second, more expensive outcome signal, and that a model can learn to write plausible but unfaithful intermediate steps while still getting the outcome right. We built a toy testbed to try to settle it: a multi-step arithmetic and logic task where every intermediate state is inspectable, with one model trained only on final-answer reward and another trained on step-level reward from a human-written solution trace, then evaluated in-distribution, out-of-distribution, and on adversarial problems where a shallow shortcut is rewarded. The pattern is not clean: outcome-only training reaches higher final accuracy in-distribution but fails on problems that require computing an intermediate quantity and then discarding it; step-level training matches the human trace on in-distribution problems but produces locally coherent traces that are globally inconsistent on the adversarial set. Each side reads the same numbers as confirming its prior, and I am beginning to think the impasse is not empirical at all.

I am not asking which camp is right; I want a diagnosis of the disagreement itself. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a substantive disagreement about the generalisation properties of different reward signals, a verbal dispute about what counts as a process — since any process signal can be redescribed as an outcome signal over a longer horizon, and any outcome signal over a trajectory is a process signal over the whole trajectory — a disagreement about which cases count as data (toy arithmetic versus open-ended language tasks), or two groups answering different questions under one heading, one about how to train a model that generalises and the other about how to evaluate whether a model’s stated reasoning is faithful to its internal computation? I would like a diagnosis that engages with what each side actually says, not a survey of the literature, and an honest assessment of what would count as progress: a formal result, a decisive experiment, a conceptual distinction, or an argument that the dispute is ill-posed and should be replaced by two or three more precise questions. If you think the disagreement is at bottom about a value — say, whether we care more about the interpretability of the training signal or about final-task performance — say so and explain why that would make the empirical back-and-forth look like it should.
