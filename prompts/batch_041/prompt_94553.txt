I teach philosophy — logic mostly, a bit of legal-ish stuff — and my neighbor Dana has pulled me into a mess I did not sign up for. Dana sits on the board of a small housing co-op, twelve households, everybody knows everybody, meetings with potluck and someone's dog under the table, and right now they're rewriting the rulebook. Here's the problem. The current book has one clause saying the "core articles" (how votes are counted, how the board gets elected, who owns what) can only be changed by unanimous consent of all twelve households, and another clause saying any other rule can be changed by a two-thirds vote at a properly noticed meeting. Three weeks ago nine households showed up and voted, two-thirds plus, to lower the unanimity requirement on the core articles down to three-quarters. The other three are now saying that vote was void from the start: if unanimity really was required, then the nine never had authority to touch it, and if it wasn't required, then the rule was never doing anything in the first place. There's also an older episode nobody can explain. The book used to say the chair's ruling on any dispute about procedure is final, and a chair once used that line to rule that the line itself had been adopted improperly and was therefore void. And now somebody has proposed adding a new article saying, roughly, that this article and every other article may be amended by the procedure set out in this article — which is either the cleanest fix anyone has thought of or exactly the same trap wearing a hat.

What I want from you is not a seminar, it's ammunition for Dana before next Tuesday, when the three holdouts have said there bringing a lawyer friend. So tell me: is the holdouts' reading right, wrong, or is there simply no fact of the matter about whether that vote counted; if they can't agree, does it help or hurt to re-run the vote under the old rule and then again under the new one, i.e. to ratify it after the fact; what should the new book actually say about how it may be changed, including whether there's a way to write the founding/adoption step so the whole thing doesn't end up resting on a rule that was already in force; and what should Dana say out loud on Tuesday, given that three people are dug in, nine are annoyed, and the co-op has to keep existing either way. If the principled answer happens to also be the one that keeps the peace, say so; if those two come apart, say which to pick and why. Also, one household keeps insisting that whoever chairs the meeting should just decide the dispute and everyone should move on — tell me whether that's a solution or just the old chair trick again.

Write it as one continuous stretch of prose, like a long email to me — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, no numbered lists — and put the bottom line in the first sentence or two before you walk me through the reasoning.
