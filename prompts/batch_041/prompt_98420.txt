Our lab's AI safety reading group keeps invoking "trust but verify" to justify designs in which a possibly-misaligned model proposes actions while a weaker, trusted monitor screens them — that is, the control-style measures that proceed on the assumption that the deployed model may be misaligned, as opposed to alignment work that tries to make it not be. I want a precise account of what that slogan can be claiming here rather than a restatement of it. Set out the distinct theses it could express — from the nearly vacuous requirement that any use of the untrusted model be accompanied by some check, through specific claims about what the trusted monitor can and cannot be relied on to catch and about how much the untrusted model may be permitted to know, up to stronger claims about the model's actual disposition or about whether reliance on it can be reduced at all — and for each reading say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false. Then tell me which of these readings would actually license the monitoring design the group is defending, and which one they most likely have in mind when they say it.
