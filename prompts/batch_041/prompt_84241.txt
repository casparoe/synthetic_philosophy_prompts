I want a case analysis of the Corwin Amendment read through the apparatus Peter Suber built for his game Nomic. The facts, briefly: the outgoing 36th Congress proposed it on March 2, 1861, hoping to hold the Union together; Buchanan endorsed it, and so did Lincoln in his first inaugural; a few states ratified it; it was never adopted; and no ratification deadline was attached, so it is sometimes said to be formally pending still. Its operative sentence: "No amendment shall be made to the Constitution which will authorize or give to Congress the power to abolish or interfere, within any State, with the domestic institutions thereof, including that of persons held to labor or service by the laws of said State."

Treat that clause, together with the Article V procedure it presupposes, as a rule-set of the kind Nomic models -- mutable and immutable rules, transmutation as the licensed route by which an immutable rule becomes changeable, and even the rule requiring players to obey the rules open to rewriting, so that what holds the game together is not itself a rule. Then I want the mapping: who the players are, what counts as a rule and what as a meta-rule, and whether this is threshold entrenchment, which raises the bar for changing a rule, or closure entrenchment, which removes a subject matter from the amendable domain and by its terms covers its own repeal. I want the counterfactual: had it been ratified, what follows for the Thirteenth Amendment, and for an amendment whose only business was to repeal the entrenchment clause -- is there a licensed route, or only a revolution dressed as interpretation? Nomic guarantees a route; does Article V plus Corwin leave an equivalent, and does that disanalogy defeat the model or is it the point the model was built to make? And I want the verdict in both directions: what the sponsors were doing when they wrote a rule meant to put an outcome beyond reach, and where Nomic misdescribes law -- it has a referee, a score, and rule changes that execute themselves, while a constitution has courts that may decline to decide and an enforcement question no rule settles. Where the apparatus falls silent, say so rather than making it speak.

One further case, to test whatever account you settle on: the Equal Rights Amendment, where Congress wrote a seven-year ratification deadline into its own proposing resolution and then extended it by simple majority. The Corwin clause is a high-ranking rule trying to bind the amendment process against itself; the ERA deadline is a low-ranking act trying to bind the same process from below. Say what the account predicts in each case, and whether these are one paradox or two.
