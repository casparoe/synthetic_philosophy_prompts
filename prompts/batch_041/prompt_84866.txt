Longtime lurker on LessWrong and SSC-type blogs, so apologies in advance for the framing. I want a dialogue, not an essay.

Set it up between two people who both know the Gricean machinery cold — speaker meaning vs. sentence meaning, the cooperative principle, the maxims, flouting, and the standard marks of implicature (cancellable, calculable, non-detachable, etc.). Neither is a strawman; each should know the others position better than most of its fans do.

Person A is a Gricean. A thinks speaker meaning is a real, determinate, intention-based thing, that there's a principled line between what's said and what's implicated, and that the maxims are doing genuine explanatory work rather than being a tidy redescription of what we already do.

Person B is a Bayesian / signaling-game type. B thinks of an utterance as a signal in a cheap-talk game, thinks "what the speaker meant" is better modeled as a distribution over the listener's posterior inferences, and suspects the whole Gricean layer either reduces to equilibrium reasoning or is a story we tell after the fact. B should have real, specific replies to A, not just vibes.

Two cases I want them to actually fight about, because I think that's where the interesting damage is:

1) The move where someone says something that could be read as a straight claim or as a joke/implication, watches the room, and then retroactively "meant" whichever reading didn't blow up in their face. (I've seen it called "Schrödinger's douchebag," no idea where the phrase is from.) The speaker seems to deliberately keep their meaning unresolved until the audience reacts. Does A say the implicature was determinate all along and just got cancelled when it went badly — i.e. this is cancellability being weaponized? Does B say there was never a fact of the matter about what was meant until the reaction settled it, and that this is just cheap talk with a mixed strategy? Which one can explain why the move feels so gross, and which one has to bite a bullet?

2) A chatbot spits out something that listeners reliably read an implicature into, and the company says "it didn't mean anything, it has no intentions." If implicature is defined in terms of speaker meaning, what's happening there — is it an implicature at all? A probably wants to say no, or only derivatively; B probably says great, that's the normal case, intention was never load-bearing. Make them have it out over whether anything in Grice survives once you can generate the effects with no intending speaker.

Don't survey the literature and don't cite anything — I don't care which paper said what. I want two competent people genuinely responding to each other's actual points and pushing each other, and at the end I want to know which one I should be updating toward and where each of them had to concede something. A page or two is fine.
