For a graduate metaphysics handout on whether a person could have come from a different sperm and egg, I want a decision tree of the positions rather than a survey of them. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of names: each branch node should be a question a student can answer about a concrete case without already knowing which position it leads to, and the tree should record for each leaf whether the position it names claims that a given origin is required for you, that it suffices for you, or both. The cases I want the tree to turn on are: the same sperm and egg uniting in a dish rather than in a body; a zygote that splits into monozygotic twins; the same sperm and egg uniting but the genome altered early by a mutagen; a person produced by putting a somatic nucleus into an enucleated egg; a person formed when two zygotes fuse; and a different sperm and egg that by coincidence yield a genome identical to yours. The leaves must include gametic origin essentialism, genome essentialism, zygotic essentialism, haecceitism, bare anti-essentialism, and the counterpart-theoretic reading on which the question is not about transworld identity at all. Give each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment, note any combination of answers that nobody occupies and diagnose it as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, including whether the counterpart-theoretic leaf makes the dispute merely verbal. Seven branch nodes at most. Begin directly with the tree, with no preface or commentary, and put the notes after it.
