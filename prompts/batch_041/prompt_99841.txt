I build and maintain large event-sourced backend systems, and the vocabulary I actually think in is this: an append-only log of events, projections that fold the log into a queryable state, materialized views and caches that have to be invalidated when the log moves on, a schema that declares in advance which facts about the world the system is permitted to represent, and invariants that must hold across the whole store. That is the vocabulary I reason in. I have no training in AI or philosophy of mind, so I would like to be addressed in my own terms rather than taught a new one.

I want the frame problem restated in that vocabulary. Not the one-line gloss, but the shape of the discussion as it stands in the AI and philosophy literature: the difficulty of getting a formal system to represent what does not change when something changes, the auxiliary statements people write in order to do that job, the temptation to assume that whatever is not mentioned stays as it was, the various non-monotonic devices for letting a system retract an assumption once it turns out to be wrong, and the more ambitious complaint that grows out of all this about relevance and holism — the claim that what a belief commits you to is not determined locally, so that no finite set of rules can tell a system what it must reconsider. If my vocabulary can carry them, also place the neighbouring problems: specifying the conditions an action needs in order to succeed, and capturing an action's indirect effects.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report.

Which components of that discussion translate cleanly, tightly enough that I could use my own vocabulary to reason about the philosophical point without loss?

Which translate only under distortion — where I have to change what is being claimed in order to make it fit, and what specifically gets bent?

Which have no counterpart in what I do at all?

For each, say whether the residue is a defect of my vocabulary or evidence that the philosophical formulation was doing less work than it appeared to. I am suspicious of one thing in particular, and I want it addressed directly: my framework gets relevance for free, because the schema decides in advance what may be represented and what may change. If the translation only works because my picture has already stipulated the very thing the frame problem says cannot be stipulated, then say so plainly, and tell me whether that is a genuine solution to the problem, a translation of the problem into an assumption, or a change of subject. Do not let my framework's presuppositions pass as translations of the philosophical claims; the value of the exercise lies in what fails to carry.

Concretely, give me one small worked example: a piece of state, a single action, the statement that says what the action leaves alone, and the same thing written as an update or a projection, followed by an honest account of where the two come apart. End with a verdict: of the disagreement among the people who work on this, how much is a disagreement about how to represent things, which my vocabulary can enter into, and how much concerns something my vocabulary has no room for.
