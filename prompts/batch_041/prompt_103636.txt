In the experimental literature on intentional action, the Knobe effect is the finding that people are considerably more willing to say that an agent intentionally brought about a side effect when that side effect is bad than when it is good, even when the agent's knowledge, causal contribution, and indifference toward the outcome are held fixed. Two explanations compete. On the valence account, the asymmetry is driven by the outcome's moral status: a morally bad outcome invites the intentionality attribution, a morally good one does not. On the norm account, the asymmetry is driven by whether the outcome violates a norm the agent is expected to uphold, with moral wrongness figuring only as one species of norm violation rather than as the operative mechanism.

Give an example of a conceptual issue on which these two accounts divide: a vignette, or a minimal pair of vignettes, that holds the agent's knowledge, causal contribution, and indifference fixed while pulling the outcome's moral status apart from its norm-violating character, so that the two accounts must yield different intentionality judgments. For the example you give, state what each account must predict and why; identify the single feature of the vignette that would have to be varied to bring the two accounts back into agreement; and say what your case would indicate about whether the effect is evidence about the concept of intentional action itself or about something adjacent to it. Do not assume that either account is correct, and treat the effect as a phenomenon in need of explanation rather than as established fact.
