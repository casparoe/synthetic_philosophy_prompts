An essay I recently read about machine-made images summarizes the cognitive scientist Margaret Boden's account of creativity and then uses it to draw a conclusion about AI image generators. I find the conclusion tempting, but something about the argument nags at me, and I don't know the material well enough to say where the trouble is. Here is the summary as the essay presents it:

"Boden distinguishes three kinds of creativity. Combinational creativity combines familiar ideas into unfamiliar combinations; exploratory creativity explores the space of possibilities defined by an existing set of rules; and transformational creativity alters the rules themselves, redrawing what counts as possible in the domain. Boden treats the third kind as the deepest, and the history of art bears her out: every genuine artistic advance — the invention of linear perspective, the shift to atonality, the readymade — has come not from working within an existing space but from transforming it. Now consider today's image generators. These systems are trained to reproduce the statistical structure of a vast corpus of existing images, and when they generate they do little more than interpolate between points in that corpus, recombining the visual motifs they have absorbed. They are therefore creative in Boden's first, combinational sense, but they cannot manage the third kind, and it is the third kind that separates art from decoration. So AI images, however arresting, are not art; they are decoration produced at scale."

Is this a fair rendering? I'd like an audit, claim by claim. For each claim, tell me what someone who actually works with Boden's framework would accept as stated, what is a defensible simplification or a contested empirical assertion dressed up as a settled one, and what is simply a misrepresentation — and give the reason for each verdict. I'm especially interested in whether the essay's conclusion follows from whatever in the premises is accurate, or whether it quietly needs one of the parts you flag. Where the summary invokes Boden, say what she is herself committed to and where the essay is putting words in her mouth; where it makes a claim about how these systems work, separate what is true of them in general from what is being assumed for the argument. I'd also like to know what a thoughtful defender of the "AI images are not art" conclusion could say that the essay neglects, and whether that conclusion can be rebuilt on better premises. Please reason from the concepts themselves rather than directing me to sources — I am not after a reading list, only the analysis.
