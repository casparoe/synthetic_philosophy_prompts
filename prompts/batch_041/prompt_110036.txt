Hey, I need a hand with a question for the final in my upper-level seminar (3rd/4th years, a couple of grad students sitting in) on animals, ethics and uncertainty. One question has to be on the scale argument about insect sentience, and I want it to actually discriminate. Last year half the class wrote some version of "insects are incredibly numerous and we don't know whether they feel pain, so the expected amount of suffering is enormous" and stopped, which is just the argument restated.

What I want them to have to do:

- rebuild it as a decision under uncertainty rather than as an empirical prediction about insect neurobiology -- i.e. say exactly what the probability is a probability *of*, and what work each premise is doing;
- deal with the objection I get every single year, which I've paraphrased: "the uncertainty isn't independent per insect -- either the whole class is sentient or it isn't -- so you can't just multiply a small per-insect probability by an enormous number of insects";
- notice that the arithmetic is weirdly insensitive to how small you make the per-insect welfare range, so "they probably barely feel anything" doesn't rescue you;
- and then say whether the thing proves too much, with at least one concrete implication for an individual or a government.

For the setup, just stipulate the numbers so nobody spends the exam arguing about insect census figures -- say on the order of 10^18 insects alive at any time plus the farmed ones, against 8 billion humans and whatever billions of farmed vertebrates; and let them take the sentience credence as a stipulated smallish p instead of defending a particular number.

Deliverables: the exam question itself (one essay, 90 minutes, 40% of the final; split it into parts a/b/c if that helps), then a model answer of 600-700 words, then a marking scheme out of 20. For the scheme I want five must-hit points, 4 marks each, and for each point a one-line note on the specific mistake that should cost the mark -- I'd rather the scheme punish the B answers in specific ways then say "shows understanding". And please don't let the question be answerable by reciting the numbers and concluding that we should care about insects.

One thing to keep out of it: no reading lists, no citations. It's an in-class final, no notes and no internet in the room, and I don't want them guessing at who said what.
