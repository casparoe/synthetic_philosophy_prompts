I need the desiderata for a theory of content's causal efficacy — the question of whether the symbolic level of a system does any work on its physical substrate, or only redescribes what the substrate is already doing on its own.

Set the stage with a case I want the theory to handle. Picture a vast electromechanical contrivance on the scale of a stadium: a pinball-like machine whose ordinary steel balls cascade through a dense field of pegs, gates, and channels. Mixed in among the steel are special balls — call them simmballs — engineered so that their internal states track, in a compressed and coded form, the positions and trajectories of the ordinary balls. The machine's future is thus predictable in two ways at once: by tracing every collision and force among the steel, or by reading the simmballs as a representation of where the steel is heading. Both descriptions are true simultaneously, and both support counterfactuals. The question I care about is whether, in a machine like this and in systems like us, the representational description is only a convenient summary of what the physical level is doing anyway, or whether representing things one way rather than another makes a genuine difference to where the substrate goes — whether the symbols push.

Write me the requirements a theory has to meet before it can answer that question, not the answer itself. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. I want at least: the cases the theory must get right (the simmball machine; an ordinary neural system whose content we take seriously; a case where the content seems to float free of the mechanics, such as a system whose inner states are reliably correlated with a distal condition but which we are unsure whether to call representational); the distinctions it must not collapse (causal production versus causal explanation; a difference in the content represented versus a merely correlated difference in the mechanism; causation by a state's content versus causation by its physical realizer); and the results it must stay consistent with — in particular the apparent completeness of the physical description, and whatever closure or conservation facts you think bear on it.

Then tell me which of these requirements are non-negotiable for any theory worth the name and which are the parochial preferences of one camp, and — the part I most want — whether some subset of the non-negotiable requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. If the demand that content be genuinely difference-making, the demand that the physical story be causally complete, and the demand that content causation not be merely redundant cannot all be met at once, say so plainly and tell me which is doing the squeezing.

Keep the weight on the requirements and their motivating cases. Close with a short verdict on how four familiar stances fare against the list — one that denies content any causal role, one that ties content's efficacy to its physical realizer while denying it any independent contribution, one that locates efficacy in the difference content makes to the probabilities of outcomes, and one that grants content efficacy by treating the symbolic level as a real pattern in the substrate rather than a projection onto it — but a few sentences each is enough.
