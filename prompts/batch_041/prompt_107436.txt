For the past two months a small reading group I co-run (a Discord, a shared spreadsheet of passages, the usual rationalist-adjacent crowd) has been working through the perception/cognition border, and we have hit an impasse I would like help converting into a document. The two camps we keep reading are, roughly, the modularists, for whom the sensory stage is informationally encapsulated and merely hands its output up to central cognition, so that what you believe or expect can change your judgments but never what you see; and the penetration camp, for whom belief, expectation, desire, and freshly acquired category knowledge can alter perceptual experience itself. Every time we think an experiment has settled something, one side has a move that absorbs it: the effect is really attentional, or really post-perceptual, or the task measured a judgment rather than an appearance, or encapsulation was only ever a thesis about the input stage and not about the whole of what is phenomenally given. I would like you to act as a philosopher of mind with real psychophysics literacy and a temperament suited to adversarial collaboration, and to draft the joint statement these two camps could actually put their names to. Think carefully about each claim before committing it to paper, and begin with a short scoping brainstorm of the candidate loci and cruxes before you draft the document itself.

The document needs four things. First, the claims both sides can sign, about the architecture, about what needs explaining, and about which experimental results are not in dispute — and please make sure none of these are vacuous. Second, the residual disagreements, each stated in wording that its own side would accept as its own position, not a strawman. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. I want you to organise this part around a four-way taxonomy of where a purported effect could live — the representational content of the percept, the phenomenal character of the percept considered apart from its content, the encoding at an early sensory stage, and the decision or report threshold — and to say, for each crux, which loci the two sides are actually disagreeing about. I also want a crux on whether encapsulation and penetration are meant to be sense-general: is the whole debate an artifact of taking vision as the model sense, and would a clean demonstration in audition, taste, or smell count, or be ruled out in advance as special pleading? Finally, if any part of the dispute is verbal — for instance, if the two sides are using the word perception for the modular input stage and for the whole of the phenomenally given respectively — say exactly which part and why.

Then the tests. For each crux, give the case, experiment, or argument that both sides would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. I have one design in mind that I would like included: a criterion-matched two-interval forced-choice task with a separate appearance report, in which the cognitive factor is manipulated independently of attention, plus a variant where the same factor is made task-irrelevant. Ask each side to write down what it predicts under that design and what result would count against its own view; and ask them both whether there is any evidence at all that each would accept as decisive, and if there is none, to say so plainly and explain why. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table, then a cruxes-and-tests section. Do not include citations or a reading list — I will add those myself — and keep the prose tight; I would rather have a short document both sides could sign than a long one neither could.
