Here's the thing that's actually dividing us. My friend keeps insisting that grounding is all-or-nothing: either a symbol is hooked up to the world or it isn't. And she has two cases she thinks break the usual intuitions. First, the black-box actuator: imagine a system that has no sensors at all, only a text interface to a warehouse robot it can't see. It types commands like 'grasp the red block' or 'lift the fragile crate', and all it ever recieves back is a string saying success or failure. Over millions of trials it gets very good at predicting and steering what happens out there. Question: are its words for 'red', 'fragile', 'heavy' now grounded, or is this just more symbol-shuffling with a causal side-channel? Second, the division-of-labour case: a person who has never percieved a neutrino, never run a detector, but reasons about them fluently because the whole community's practice is hooked up to the world somewhere. Is she grounded by proxy? My friend says there's a third option we keep missing -- that grounding is relational and social rather than a property of any individual system -- but I'm not sure that isn't just relabelling the problem.

So what I want from the essay: I've been thinking about it as two rival criteria, and I'd like you to take them seriously and pick one. Call them contact grounding -- a symbol is grounded only if it's causally connected to what it denotes through the system's own perception and action -- and leverage grounding -- a symbol is grounded if it lets the system reliably predict and intervene on the relevant part of the world, no matter how the information got in. Argue for one of them, and be honest about the cost. In particular, if leverage grounding is the right criterion, does grounding become a matter of degree, and if so does the original problem just dissolve into a vague 'more or less grounded' story that no longer explains anything? Use the two cases to test whichever criterion you defend, say what happens to the Chinese room intuition under your view, and finish with a short section on what your verdict implies for whether today's language models have grounded symbols.
