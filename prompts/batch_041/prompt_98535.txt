Ok so in my philosophy-ish class we read this thing about the self basically being a story the brain tells, and the teacher said its kind of like a center of gravity — a center of gravity isnt a real thing you can point to, its just a useful way of talking about how a bunch of stuff balances out, and the self is the same, just a made-up main character the brain keeps writing so it can make sense of its own body and behaviour. Not a thing, just a story, and the story is the only thing thats actually there.

Anyway I kind of buy it now and it explains a lot, but Im trying to figure out how much it actually risks, because it feels like it might be the kind of view that can never be wrong. So what would count against it? Like what would have to be true, or what could we find out, for it to be false?

Some stuff I've been chewing on:

- it kind of reminds me of how a company is a fiction — theres no molecule of a company, but it still owns stuff and gets sued and does things in the world. Does the self being a fiction work the same way, and if so does that make the view trivially true or does it actually do any work?
- who's writing the story? if the self is the story, theres gotta be a teller, and if the teller is just another part of the brain then isnt that basically a self again, just moved back a step?
- does it have anything to say about me being the same person who did something dumb last year, or being responsible for it? if its all just a story that gets edited over time, could the story be wrong about who did what?

I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they'd be, like fatal vs just awkward, and I want to know which ones someone who actually believes this would themselves admit count against them vs which ones they'd just wave away. And be honest with me: has this view been qualified so much that nothing could count against it, and if so is that a real problem or is it fine for this kind of view?

Personally the thing that would move me most is if theres some case where the story view just cant describe what's going on at all, not just that it sounds weird.
