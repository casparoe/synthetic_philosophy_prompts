Two parents on the PTA, Dan and Marisol, got into it at the meeting over which of two math programs the district should buy, "Sparks" or "Drill." The district had run both last year and passed around a table, and I copied the numbers down:

Sparks: kids who were already strong in math last year, 85 of 100 passed the test. Kids who were weak last year, 40 of 100 passed.
Drill: strong kids, 320 of 400 passed. Weak kids, 35 of 100 passed.

Dan: "Look at the bottom line. Sparks got 125 of 200 through, that's 62 and a half percent. Drill got 355 of 500, that's 71 percent. Drill wins. Buy Drill."

Marisol: "You can't read it that way. Look inside each group. Among strong kids Sparks got 85% and Drill got 80%. Among weak kids Sparks got 40% and Drill got 35%. Sparks beats Drill in both groups. The only reason Drill looks better overall is that Drill was handed almost all the strong kids, 400 of its 500, versus only 100 of Sparks' 200. Drill's edge is just who it taught, not what it taught."

Dan: "But we're not running some lab experiment where we get to hold strong and weak fixed. Whatever we buy, we're going to hand it out the same way we did this year, the honors track is going to keep getting whichever one we pick, and I don't get to choose which kind of kid I am. If I'm a parent picking the program my kid will sit in, the overall number is what actually happens to kids like mine."

Marisol: "No. Whether a kid was strong last year is settled before they ever set foot in either class. You can't give Drill credit for walking in with better students. The honest comparison holds that fixed."

Dan: "Fine, but how do you know that 'strong last year' is even the thing to hold fixed? The strong kids are concentrated in the two well-funded schools. So maybe the real story is money, and 'was strong last year' is just downstream of that. Freeze the wrong thing and you get the wrong answer. Neither of us actually knows what the real cause is, and yet you're talking like yours is obviously the right one."

And that's where they left it, the meeting moved on to the parking lot. I've been chewing on it since and I dont know what to think. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right at each step, not just who wins overall? And if there's something both of them are missing, tell me what.
