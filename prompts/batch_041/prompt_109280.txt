My reading group has spent basically the whole term on the paradox of tolerance and we're getting nowhere, so I want to try something different. Two of us have turned into the designated camps. One is a Popperian: tolerance, he says, is a bargain and not a gift — a movement that preaches intolerance has opted out of the protection, and a tolerant society is entitled to shut it down, by law if need be, rather than sit there waiting to be eaten. The other is a Millian absolutist: she says the whole point of tolerance is that it's owed to views we hate, that we're fallible about which views are the dangerous ones, and that a society that starts making exceptions for the intolerant has already lost the thing it was defending. Every meeting ends with each of them telling the other that they're smuggling in a premise — about who gets to decide, or about whether "intolerant" is a property of a doctrine, or of a movement, or of what people actually do with their fists once they've got enough of them. My own guess is that at least two of the real cruxes are (i) whether the principle is a moral constraint on individuals or a design constraint on institutions, and (ii) whether "intolerant" attaches to a view or to conduct — but I've been wrong about which bits are load-bearing before, so dont just take my word for it.

Instead of another session of this, I want to draft the document they should have produced and just hand it to them. So: write the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the Popperian and the Millian absolutist about the paradox of tolerance. I want a numbered section of agreed claims — what both of them can sign about what tolerance is, about what the paradox actually is (a contradiction, a boundary condition, or just a hard case), about what the dispute is *not* about, and about whether the answer changes across settings (a state, a university, a publisher, a social media platform) and across the difference between suppressing speech and declining to amplify it. Then a disagreements table: each remaining disagreement stated twice, once in wording the Popperian would accept for his own view and once in wording the absolutist would accept for hers, plus the crux each disagreement turns on — the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one of them — and, for each crux, a concrete case or argument that *both* would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Two things I especially want tested, because this is where I think it usually gets hand-waved. First, one of us floated a rule for institutions: any group may use the room, provided that, if it ever came to power, it would not revoke this rule. I want to know whether both camps could sign that as stated, and if not, exactly which word breaks it, and whether the disagreement there is substantive or merely verbal. Second, whether either side has a self-application problem — the Popperian needs a criterion for "intolerant" plus someone trustworthy to apply it, the absolutist has to say what happens when the tolerated movement wins an election and starts banning things — and I'd like the statement to say honestly what each side has to swallow, rather than papering it over. If part of their fight turns out to be about words and not about substance, say which part and why; that's really the main thing I'm after.
