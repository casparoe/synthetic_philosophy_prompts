I keep seeing rationalist blog commenters say XOR blackmail is just ordinary blackmail with a weird conditional, and then someone else says counterfactual mugging is also basically blackmail, except the blackmailer is Omega and the threat is aimed at a branch of the universe you didn't get. I want to know how far that blackmail analogy actually holds for these two problems. For XOR blackmail, take the version where you get a letter saying a bomb is in your house unless you pay, the letter arrives iff you have termites XOR you would pay, and the bomb is real iff you have termites. For counterfactual mugging, take Omega flipping a coin, asking you for £100 on tails, and on heads paying you £10,000 if it predicted you would have paid on tails. Set out the mapping explicitly: who or what plays the blackmailer, the threat, the payment, the harm, the evidence, the prediction, the counterfactual branch, and the thing that makes paying rational or not. Then tell me where the mapping breaks for each problem, which breaks matter for the conclusion people draw (usually that you should pay up), and whether that conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. If the blackmail analogy is carrying the conclusion rather than supporting it, say so. And if there's a better analogy for either problem, give it.
