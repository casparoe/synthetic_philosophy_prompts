A professor in my epistemology seminar offered the following summary of the Judy Benjamin problem. Judy is a paratrooper who lands in a region divided into Red and Blue, with Blue split into Blue-1 and Blue-2. She has prior probabilities over these three cells. Headquarters tells her that if she is in Blue, she is in Blue-2. The professor said the standard Bayesian answer is to conditionalize on the material conditional, which rules out Blue-1 and makes Red more likely than Blue-2. But the problem was introduced to show this is counterintuitive, because the information only concerns the Blue region, so it should not change the relative probability of Red versus Blue. The professor then said that the minimum relative entropy rule gives the same result as material conditionalization, which is why the problem is a challenge for relative information minimizers. He concluded that the only way to get the intuitive answer—where Red stays the same and Blue-2 absorbs all of Blue's prior probability—is to use Jeffrey conditionalization, which is why the problem shows that Jeffrey's rule is the correct Bayesian response to conditional information. How much of this is right? Go sentence by sentence and tell me what a philosopher working on probabilistic updating would accept, what they would wince at, and what is just wrong. Does the professor's conclusion follow from the accurate parts? Answer in no more than 400 words.
