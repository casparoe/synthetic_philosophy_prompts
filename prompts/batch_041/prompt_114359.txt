My roommate has been on a Moloch kick for like a year now — the thing where everyone acting in their own interest leaves everybody worse off and nobody can stop, arms races, ad auctions, that whole deal. He read some essay about it and now he sees it everyware. We got into it about whether it's a real explanation or just a fancy way of saying "things are bad sometimes," and he wrote his side out as an argument so I'd stop interrupting him. I've read it six times and I can't find the loose thread, but I also can't accept where it lands. Here it is.

He's got a distinction he's proud of: a trap is shallow if a referee can actually fix it (a regulator, a treaty, whoever), and deep if the referee is caught in the same kind of trap as everyone else. His claim is that every shallow trap is secretly deep.

(1) A multipolar trap is any situation where each party does what's best for them individually, and the result is worse for everybody than some other outcome all of them could have had.
(2) In a trap, no party can fix it on their own. If one of them could just do the better thing and come out ahead, they'd already be doing it.
(3) So any fix has to come from outside the parties — from someone with power over all of them at once.
(4) But that referee is a party too, in her own situation with other referees, and she has the same reason to defect that they do.
(5) So her fix is as unstable as the original problem. Whatever holds her in line has to come from outside her, and that someone has the same issue.
(6) Repeat. Every trap has a trap above it, all the way up. Every shallow trap is deep.
(7) So there's no level where it stops. Which means "Moloch" doesn't pick out anything in particular — it's just the noise you make when you've noticed the regress and gotten tired of writing it out.
(8) So the AI race stuff, the arms race stuff, "we're all doomed by incentives" — none of it is a diagnosis. It's resignation wearing a costume.

Here's my problem. I basically buy (1) and (2). I've watched the group chat thing happen — we all agreed to stop sending stuff after 11pm, someone sent one thing, it was over. And I think (4) is real; I've watched regulators get captured and it's as bad as he says. But (8) has to be wrong, because if it's right it's also right about the 11pm thing, and the 11pm thing got fixed. Nobody has power over all of us. We just mostly stopped, after someone said it was making them miserable. Same with mowing on Sundays on our street — no enforcement, everyone just stopped after one summer of passive-aggressive signs. And nobody referees which side of the sidewalk we walk on, and that works fine.

So where's the break? Which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me — because I'd like to keep (2) and (4), and I don't want to end up saying traps aren't real. And tell me straight whether the shallow/deep thing is doing any work or whether "deep" is just the argument's engine wearing a lab coat.
