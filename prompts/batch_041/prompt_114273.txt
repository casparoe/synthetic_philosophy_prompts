Okay so I keep shrimp. Not for a living, just a planted tank in my apartment with a few neocaridina and a couple of amanos, and I fell down a rabbit hole reading about how commercial shrimp farms actually work, and now I can't stop thinking about something that looks like a glaring inconsistency. Pretty much every welfare certifcation scheme for farmed seafood, and a lot of national animal-welfare laws, has a whole section on fish: you're supposed to stun them before you kill them, percussive stunning or electrical stunning or at least a proper anaesthetic bath, plus rules about water quality, stocking density, transport, the whole thing. Then the same document gets to shrimp, crabs, lobsters, crayfish — the decapod crustaceans — and it just... stops. Boiling, live chilling, live sale, whatever. Nothing. And the line everyone draws is basically vertebrate vs invertebrate, which is a taxonomic line, not a welfare line, and it isn't even stable, because in the last few years some places have started pulling octopuses and squid into the protected category and a few have pulled in crabs and lobsters too, while plenty of others still haven't.

So here's what I want from you. Treat that split — stun the salmon, boil the shrimp — as a practice, and tell me what it presupposes. What view of sentience, or of consciousness, or just of what makes a creature's suffering matter, does it only make sense under? Is it committing to the idea that there's a real moral threshold somewhere and vertebrates happen to sit above it, or is the vertebrate line just a tractability heuristic dressed up as a principle? Does it quietly rely on a distinction between nociception and actually feeling pain, and if so is that a defensible distinction or a convenient one? I'd also like you to separate what the practice strictly needs from what it merely fits comfortably with — I suspect several different theories could all license the same neglect of shrimp, and I want to know which ones can't. Then, if you swap out whatever view it presupposes for a different one — say, any animal that can learn to avoid a noxious stimulus counts, or the precautionary line that if there's a decent chance it feels pain we protect it — tell me concretely what would have to change about how a shrimp farm, or a restaurant, or a home aquarist like me operates. Don't give me a reading list, just the argument.
