So I'm in a philosophy of mind reading group and we've been stuck for weeks on multiple realizability, and I want to figure out what kind of fight this actually is before we burn another month on it.

Here's the shape of it as I see it. The original move (Putnam, I think, and then Fodor ran with it) was that mental kinds like pain can't be type-identical with any physical kind, because the realizers differ across species and maybe even across individuals — octopus pain, human pain, silicon pain — so whatever pain is, it has to be picked out functionally rather than by its physical makeup. That's supposed to be the big reason to be a functionalist. Fine, I get the appeal.

Then the critics come back and say the empirical premise is just false or at least unearned: neuroanatomists actually individuate brain states at a coarse enough grain that the "same" state shows up across mammals, so there's no demonstrated multiple realization, and when you look at the actual neuroscience the realization relation is much tighter than the functionalist needs it to be. Then the functionalists reply: sure, but that's because you individuated the physical kind too finely, or the mental kind too coarsely — pick your grain and you can make MR come out true or false at will. And around and around, for decades, and nobody moves.

My hypothesis, which is why I'm asking: I think its really a debate about individuation — about what makes two things "the same kind" — and that both sides are quietly using different, unstated criteria for that, so they're talking past each other. Maybe it's a verbal dispute about the word "realization," maybe it's two groups answering different questions (one about metaphysics and natural kinds, one about how the special sciences explain), or maybe there's a real empirical question hiding under it that nobody's actually answering.

Diagnose it for me. Engage with what each side actually says, not the cartoon versions. Tell me whether my individuation hypothesis is right or a cop-out, whether the impasse means the question is ill-posed or just hard, and — most importantly — what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction, a change of question, a constraint from the neuroscience, or nothing, in which case say why no progress should be expected. And keep it concrete, I don't want a survey of functionalism, I want the diagnosis with reasons.
