I run the internal comms for a mid-size company and I want to write a send-up of the way management talks, but pitched at a fairly specific idea so it doesn't read like every other "corporate speak is bad" joke. The idea is free-floating rationales — Dennett's thing where a bit of behavior can be genuinely rational, there's a real reason for it, but nobody anywhere is doing the reasoning; the reason is just sitting there in the selection history. Heart pumps blood because hearts that pumped blood worked out. Nobody had to think it.

So: write me an internal all-hands memo, maybe 700 words, from a company that has accepted this idea as obviously true and taken it to what it thinks is its natural conclusion — they've dissolved the Strategy department and anyone whose actual job was to understand why the company does what it does, on the grounds that the reasons were apparently already in there the whole time, floating. Voice should be deadpan, the author believes every word, no winking at the reader. I want the funny to come from getting the concept right, not from buzzwords: the difference between a rationale existing and anyone representing it, what it actually takes for a behavior to have a free-floating rationale at all (a history of selection, not just a good outcome after the fact), and the uncomfortable bit that you can't hand yourself a rationale by fiat. Have some skeptical employee push back and let the memo's reply be chillingly reasonable rather than obviously wrong. Format it like a real memo — To/From/Re, a few numbered points. It should land for someone who knows the intentional stance and still be readable for someone who doesn't.

If you think I've mangled the idea or that a memo is a bad vehicle for it, just say so rather than doing it anyway. And tack a short note at the end on where you're taking liberties with the concept or leaning on a contested reading, so I don't go repeating any of it as settled.
