I've been trying to work out something on my own and I cant quite settle it, and I keep landing on Mary Shelley's Frankenstein. There seem to be two ways to say what a thing's "function" is. One looks backwards: a part's function is what it was selected for, so the heart pumps blood because hearts that pumped got their owners to survive and reproduce — no history, no function. The other just looks at what's in front of you: a function is the job a part does in keeping some bigger system going, history irrelevant, so the heart's function is pumping blood and that's that.

The creature seems like a good place to test the two against each other. He's assembled out of parts taken from the dead, so each of his parts has a history — that heart was a heart. But he as a whole has no species, no lineage, no selection, and no job: Victor didn't make him in order to do anything, he just wanted to know whether he could make a living thing. So does the creature have functions? Is he a broken human, or just a new kind of thing doing exactly what it does? On the backwards view I want to say he has no function at all, which would make "he's defective" or "he's a monster" sound like a category mistake — but then the only scrap of history in the room is Victor's intention, and that would hand the creature a function assigned by someone else, which seems worse than having none. On the other view his heart works fine, but so does whatever it is in him that makes everyone who sees him run, and I don't want to call that a function. He also seems to be asking a function question himself when he demands a companion, like he wants a role in something.
