My uncle's been in the ICU on a ventillator for a few weeks now and the family keeps having the 'is he actually gone yet' conversation, which I did not expect to turn into a philosophy problem. It's left me with a view I want you to make the strongest possible case for: the definition of death should be disjunctive, not conjunctive. An organism is dead as soon as it has irreversibly lost either the capacity for consciousness or the capacity to maintain its own internal integration, whichever goes first -- not only once the whole brain is gone. Part of why it grips me is that the UK treats brainstem death as death while the US wants the entire brain, so the 'one biological fact of the matter' story is already doing less work than people pretend. Argue it on the merits rather than by pointing at who said what, and then tell me the objection you'd actually worry about.
