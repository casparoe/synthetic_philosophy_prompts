Ok, so I've been going back and forth on counterfactual mugging for about a year and I think I've finally landed somewhere, which is unsettling because I used to think the other answer was obviously right. Setup I care about: a coin is about to be flipped. If heads, a being that's good at predicting me asks me for $100. If tails, it hands me $10,000, but only if it had already predicted I'd hand over the $100 when asked. It comes up heads, it asks, I hand over the $100, and I think that's right -- not a personal quirk, actually right, the way one-boxing is right. I've also come round on the termite version (the letter only arrives if exactly one of these holds: your house is infested, you pay him $1000), where refusing to pay is evidence you're infested. I used to file that one as an edge case; now I think it's the general shape of the thing. What I choose is a disposition, the disposition is correlated with stuff that isn't causally downstream of my act, and I dont think the correlation stops counting just because the branch where it pays off is counterfactual.

So what would count against it? I want the defeaters ranked, from the ones that should genuinely move me down to the ones that are merely awkward, plus your honest read on which of them someone who holds my view would just concede. Specifically I can't tell whether these are lethal or just noise: the predictor being only 70% accurate (do I still pay? if the answer flips somewhere, was this ever more than a threshold artifact?); the case where there are alot of muggers, some of them bluffing, and being a payer is a known type -- does the policy still come out ahead, or does it die by being farmed?; the case where the $10,000 goes to someone I'll never meet, or to a version of me I'd deny is me; and the determinist's complaint that my disposition was already fixed before I deliberated, so there was never a choice here, just a discovery.

Then the meta bit, which might be the real question. Every qualifier I add -- only when the predictor is reliable, only one-shot, only where the branch probabilities are well defined -- is individually reasonable and also seems to shrink the claim toward risking nothing. Is that what's going on, and if so is it fatal, or just the normal fate of a principle like this? And is there any case where my view and the causalist one actually come apart in a way I could notice, or am I defending a distinction with no difference I could ever be confronted with?
