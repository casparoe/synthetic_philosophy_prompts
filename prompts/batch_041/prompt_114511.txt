I spent thirty-five years as a marine biologist and retired three years ago, and since then I've been reading philospohy every morning like it's a new lab, which is how I've ended up in a mess I can't think my way out of.

My younger brother was diagnosed with early-onset Alzheimer's in the spring. He's 58, lives alone in the town we grew up in, eight hours away. Two things I could do. I could sell up here and move back, be the one who takes him to appointments and sits with him in the evenings and eventually finds him a place when it gets bad — which probably means giving up the last years I have where my knees and my memory still work, and it would be hard on my wife, who has her own life here and no connection to that town. Or I could stay, arrange and pay for good care, fly down every few weeks, and finish the book I've been circling for a decade, which is the only thing I've ever really wanted to make. When I try to weigh the two I feel like I'm adding meters to kilograms.

A retired economist I play chess with says I'm torturing myself for nothing. If one option isn't better than the other and they aren't equally good either, he says, then the word "better" has just run out of road, and past that point the choice is arbitrary — so pick one, flip a coin if I like, and stop. That bothers me alot. I found an argument somewhere that I'll probably butcher: take option A and then imagine a version of it that's a hair better, call it A-plus. You still wouldn't prefer A-plus to B. But if A and B were really equally good, then a slightly better A should beat B. So they aren't equal, and one isn't better than the other, and that's supposed to be a third kind of thing, not a tie and not a stalemate. Is that right? Does it actually change what I should do, or is it just a clever way of restating that I'm stuck?

My daughter says to just decide and never look back, and my brother on a good day says the same. I'm suspicious of that. What I want to know is what "deciding well" could even mean here, whether there's anything actually wrong with the coin, and whether it's a failure of nerve or something else that I'll carry the option I didn't pick with me either way.

Write it back to me as plain continuous prose, please — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just an email I can read on the porch.
