ok so I want you to argue a position for me about the paradox of tolerance. The standard move is Popper's — unlimited tolerance ends up destroying tolerance, so a tolerant society has the right to suppress the intolerant — and everyone from first-year students to op-ed writers repeats it like it's settled. I think that's a bad diagnosis of a real problem and I want the strongest case for a different reading.

The position: the paradox of tolerance isn't really a paradox at all — it's not a self-reference problem or a problem about the limits of a principle, it's a stability/coordination problem wearing philosophical clothes. Tolerance is basically a public good that's only viable as an equilibrium: it holds when enough people expect enough other people to be tolerant, and it unravels when the expected cost of being tolerant — being the sucker, the one who restrains themselves while others don't — gets too high. So the interesting question isn't "who do we exclude", it's "what keeps tolerance a best response for the people who are only marginally committed to it".

Now argue that, and then argue for the policy conclusion I think falls out of it: the correct stance is asymmetric. A tolerant society should be much more permissive about intolerant speech and much more aggressive about intolerant institutions and organised movements — because speech is cheap, individual and (mostly) reversible, whereas a movement is a coordination device that can actually flip the equilibrium by changing what everyone expects everyone else to do. That asymmetry is the bit I most want defended.

Don't just gesture at it. Say what makes something count as an institution or movement rather than mere speech, and be honest about the cases where the line is doing real work and might be gerrymandered — e.g. a political party that in practice only ever talks, or a media ecosystem that's speech all the way down but coordinates millions of people. Also tell me whether tolerance is better modelled as a public good or as an assurance game / stag hunt, and pick one and commit.
