I am persuaded by the handicap principle in its broad form: that reliable signalling of an underlying quality is possible only when the signal is differentially costly to the signaller, so that dishonest signalling is not merely unprofitable but structurally unavailable, and that this account extends from animal displays to human practices such as extravagant consumption, sacrifice, and conspicuous generosity. I want the case against it, not a survey of the literature, and I want to know what the strong version predicts that its rivals do not.

What observations, formal results, or rival explanations would count against the strong version? I am especially interested in four families of challenge. First, signals that appear reliable without being costly, because their reliability comes from a causal or constitutive link to the quality signalled rather than from a price paid. Second, cases where the cost falls on someone other than the signaller, or is imposed by the receiver rather than borne by the sender. Third, honest signalling sustained by repetition, reputation, or the prospect of later sanction, with no waste anywhere in the transaction. Fourth, the suspicion of circularity: that the costliness of a signal is often inferred from its reliability rather than measured independently, so that the theory cannot be tested by the evidence it is used to explain. For each, say whether it defeats the principle outright, forces a retreat to a weaker version, or turns out to be compatible with the strong version after all.

Tell me also what a committed defender would concede as counting against the position — what such a defender would treat as a genuine anomaly rather than a puzzle — and what they would dismiss as a misreading. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and mark which are live rather than merely logically possible.

Then address the unfalsifiability worry directly. If the principle can absorb every case by locating a cost somewhere — in the signaller, in the receiver, in a third party, in a hidden physiological or developmental trade-off — is it still an empirical claim? If it has been insulated in this way, is that a fatal defect or the ordinary fate of a broad framework that has been empirically productive? I would rather have the honest answer than a defence.

Lead with a single sentence giving your bottom line on how exposed the position is. Then the ranked map, each defeater in a sentence or two. Finish with the verdict on insulation. At most 850 words.
