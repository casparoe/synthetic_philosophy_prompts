Quick setup. Mill: the only reason you can restrict someone's speech is to stop them harming other people, and he wants the harm to be something you can actually point at, not just "that offended me." Also something about how the same words are fine in a newspaper but not yelled to a mob outside the guy's house.

Then my teacher says: what if the real reason we protect speech isnt the speaker at all, it's that everyone else needs an information environment that isn't rigged. She calls it the auditorium principle. The point of protecting speech is that the audience gets to hear everything and make up their own mind, so what you're really protecting is the audience's ability to do that, not the speaker's right to talk. And she says speech can wreck that without harming any one person. Her examples: flooding a local election with technically-true-but-misleading mailers; a guy with a megaphone so loud at a protest that nobody can hear the actual speakers; a thousand bot accounts burying a real story under junk. Nobody gets harmed in Mill's sense, but the room gets poisoned, like smog. She calls that epistemic pollution.
