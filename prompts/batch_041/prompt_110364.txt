I have to run a single ninety-minute session for a mixed group of about twenty people: mostly machine-learning engineers who have just moved onto a safety-focused team and are strong technically but have no philosophy, plus three colleagues from our policy side whose profile is the reverse. Our internal documents lean on the contrast between intent alignment and value alignment constantly, and it has occurred to several of us that nobody has ever defined either term. I am not asking you to explain the distinction to me; I want the plan for explaining it. Assume I know the material and can answer questions on my feet, and that I have a whiteboard and no slides.

Begin with the order of ideas, and say why each has to precede the next: what must already be in place before the two terms can be drawn apart at all, given that the word alignment on its own already means something to this audience and that meaning will be doing quiet work in the room. Pay particular attention to the word intent, which I suspect is ambiguous in a way that determines the entire sequence — whether it names the intent of the people who build and deploy the system or the intent of the system itself — and tell me whether that ambiguity is harmless shorthand to be waved through or the first thing I have to nail down. For what it is worth, I have been glossing the pair as follows: intent alignment is the narrow problem of getting a system to do what the people who build and deploy it actually intend, as opposed to what the objective written into it literally says; value alignment is the broader problem of the system's behaviour being the right behaviour by some standard other than the operators' intentions. If that gloss will mislead this audience, or misdescribes the distinction, tell me before you plan the session, since everything downstream depends on it.

Then the misconception map. I expect at least these, and I want each one headed off at a specific point in the sequence rather than in a closing caveat: that intent alignment is about the system having the right intentions; that value alignment is simply the harder version of the same task, so the difference is one of degree; that the distinction collapses the moment you notice that an operator's stated intention is itself a value-laden specification; and that either term is a synonym for whatever our training pipeline already does. On the carrying example: I have ruled out the paperclip maximiser and the literal-minded genie on the grounds that this audience will file both under science fiction and stop doing conceptual work, but tell me if ruling them out is a mistake. My own candidate is the gap between what a person writes in an advance directive and what they would endorse at the bedside; a colleague has proposed instead the ordinary engineering case of a system that does exactly what the specification asked and the specification turns out to have been the wrong thing to ask for. Choose between them or replace both, and say what the example has to have and what it must not have. Finally, tell me what I should refuse to explain at this level and announce that I am refusing — I am thinking of the map of research agendas, the machinery of any particular technical proposal, and the history of how these terms came into circulation — and what I can put in front of the group in the last ten minutes that would show me they hold the distinction rather than the vocabulary, something they could get wrong in a way that only the distinction catches. Do not cite anything or send me to readings; I am not handing anything out, and I would rather not build the session around names I cannot vouch for.
