I am preparing a position paper on the Judy Benjamin problem and want the specification before I evaluate any proposal. Set out the desiderata that any adequate theory of updating on conditional information must satisfy, where the new information is a report that if A then B holds with some probability, and A may be false or may have its probability shifted by the report itself. For each requirement, give the case that motivates it: the original Judy Benjamin case, where the agent is told a conditional about her location while the antecedent remains uncertain; a case where the conditional report is the only new information and the prior probability of A is nonzero; a case where the same conditional is reported by two sources of unequal reliability; a case where the reported conditional concerns an objective chance that depends on the agent's posterior; and a case where the agent must act before learning whether A. Distinguish requirements that any probabilistic update must meet from those that are optional modeling choices, and identify any pair of requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied. Then, briefly, say how Jeffrey conditionalization, Adams-style conditional-probability constraints, and imaging or selection-function accounts fare against the list. Do not supply citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; I care about the criteria and the cases behind them.
