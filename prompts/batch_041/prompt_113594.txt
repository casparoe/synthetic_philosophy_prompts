I've been chewing on the is-ought gap and I think people blow it way out of proportion. My view: Humes point is real but it's a point about words and logic, nothing more. All it shows is that you can't get a sentence with "ought" in it out of sentences that dont have one unless you smuggle in a bridge premise, which is about as interesting as not being able to derive something about dogs from premises about cats. It tells us nothing about the world. A naturalist can just say "ought" picks out some natural property and the gap is a feature of our vocabulary, not of reality. So what would count against that? I want actual defeaters, sorted by how much they should really move me, including ones a naturalist would themselves admit hurts them rather than just waving away. Don't hand me the Moore line as a slogan, tell me what work it's doing. And be honest about whether I've rigged this so nothing could count against it -- if the gap is only about words, what would it even look like for it to be about the world instead? The thing that nags me is that even a creature with no normative vocabulary at all seems stuck when it goes from how things are to what to do, and that doesnt feel like a fact about words.
