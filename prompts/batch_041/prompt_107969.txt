An old housemate of mine is deep in the rationalist blogosphere and keeps forwarding me posts. The latest is a long piece on Dennett's heterophenomenology, and it's got under my skin. The gist: it's behaviourism with better PR — you put the subject in a lab, harvest their verbal reports, and since you're not allowed to assume there's anything it's like to be them, you end up treating the whole inner life as a useful fiction. So the method is eliminativism in disguise: strictly speaking consciousness doesn't exist, only the reports do, and it's the only scientific game in town, which means anyone doing first-person phenomenology is writing literature rather than science. The kicker is that this dissolves the hard problem — once you stop taking your own experience at face value there's simply nothing left over to explain.

Is any of that fair to Dennett? What I want is a line-by-line audit: what he'd sign off on, what's a forgivable simplification, and what's flat wrong. The two bits I most want priced are 'behaviourism rebranded' — does that survive contact with what the method actually tells you to do with the reports? — and the hard-problem kicker, which smells to me like the blogger's own leap rather than anything the method delivers. I lean towards thinking the method is far more modest than the post makes out, but I cant tell if that's just me being generous to a philosopher I already like.
