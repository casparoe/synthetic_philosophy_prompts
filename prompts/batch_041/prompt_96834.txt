ok so quick background: in civics we did the American Dream, and then in my ethics elective we read about jantelagen, this scandinavian idea that you shouldn't think you're anything special or better than anybody else, and the australian version, tall poppy syndrome, where if you get too successful people cut you down to size.

My friend Jaden built this argument to convince me that jantelagen is just honesty and the American Dream is cope. I cant find the hole in it and its driving me nuts.

1. The American Dream says anyone who works hard can make it, and that the people who make it deserve it.
2. But whether you work hard, whether you're smart or healthy, when and where you were born, and whether your work happens to pay off are all things you didn't choose. Nobody picks their own genes, parents, or timing.
3. You can't deserve credit or blame for something that wasn't up to you.
4. So successful people don't deserve their success and unsuccessful people don't deserve their failure.
5. Since success isn't deserved anyway, there's nothing wrong with cutting successful people down a peg. Jantelagen is just what honesty about luck looks like, and celebrating success is the lucky flattering themselves.
6. Same reasoning goes for everything else: nobody deserves blame for anything, so the guy who robs a store or the friend who never tries is just unlucky in his wiring and his circumstances.
7. Nobody deserves love or friendship or respect either. Thats all luck too.
8. But thats crazy. My little sister got into a really good school and I'm proud of her and I don't think I'm being dumb, and I do blame my cousin for stealing from my grandma. If nobody deserves anything then none of that makes sense. But I also can't find the mistake.

Jaden says 6 and 7 are just true and I'm being sentimental, and that 5 is the whole point. I'd most like to keep 8 and also keep 2. So which step do I give up, why did it look so obviously right to me before, and what does giving it up cost me?
