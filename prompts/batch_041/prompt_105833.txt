My friend Rosa and I have been going at this for like two weeks now, ever since we watched a horror movie together: is the stuff you feel about made-up people actually real? I said you can't be genuinely scared of something you know isn't real. She said the feeling is the feeling, who cares what it's about. But her own verdicts are all over the place and I can't work out what rule she's actually running. Best reconstruction I can manage from the argument:

- She sobbed when a character died at the end of a novel, was still low the next morning, and reread the last chapter twice. Real grief, she says, no question.
- She got furious at a character who betrays the hero, ranted about it for days, and posted a whole thing about it. Real anger.
- In a video game she got a character killed, felt guilty for a week, and reloaded an old save to undo it. Real guilt.
- She cried over a memoir that turned out to be partly fabricated, still brought it up days later, and spent an evening reading up on the actual history. She says the crying was real anyway.
- She screamed and jumped at a jump scare in the theater and was laughing about it five seconds later. Not real fear, "thats just a reflex."
- Watching a replay of a match she already knew the result of, sweaty palms the whole time, forgot about it the second it ended. Not real either, she was just "playing along."

So: ever since that movie she's been leaving the hall light on and checking behind the shower curtain before bed, and she keeps calling it silly. If I ask her whether that fear is real, what does she say? What I actually want is the menu — the different rules that fit everything above, what each one predicts about the shower-curtain thing, which one you think she's really running and why, and what I should ask her to find out for sure. I was going to try asking about a book that made her sob and then she was totally fine by dinner — would that seperate anything? And does her "the feeling is the feeling" line even follow from any of these rules, or is it a different claim altogether?
