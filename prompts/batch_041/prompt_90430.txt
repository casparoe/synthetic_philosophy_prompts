I'm retired now — forty years in agricultural chemistry, mostly soil nitrogen — and since I stopped I've been reading history and moral philosophy, which I never had time for when I was working. Something keeps bugging me and I can't find a label for it. Here's the pattern. Take duelling. Every argument against it — it's murder, it settles nothing, it kills the wrong man, it's a barbarous relic — was in print and, as far as I can tell, completely unanswerable for something like a century before the practice actually died out in Britain. Nobody was converted by any of it. Then inside a generation it went from being what a gentleman did to being what a cad did, and that was that. Same shape with foot binding: missionaries and Chinese reformers denounced it for decades and it held on, and then it collapsed quite fast once the meaning flipped — once unbound feet stopped being a liability in the marriage market and started being a sign that a family was modern. And a much more recent one: drunk driving in the States. In the 1970s you could often plead it down to a traffic ticket; by the early nineties it was a stain on your character. I don't think anybody produced a new arguement in between. My wife is a social historian and we've been chewing on this over dinner for weeks.

So what I want to know is wether there's a name for the thing that actually does the work in cases like these, as opposed to the arguments. My hunch is that it has something to do with honour — that the practice is propped up by a reputation system, it survives as long as doing it earns you standing with the people whose opinion you care about, and it dies when doing it starts costing you standing. But that's a hunch, and I can't tell whether it's a real distinction or just a word for the bit we can't explain. What I'd like is the carving, not the label. If there's an established name, fine, but I want to know what it excludes: how do I tell my pattern apart from a plain shift in material interests (maybe duelling just became pointless once the state got good at settling debts and slander in court, and foot binding just became unprofitable when factories wanted women who could walk), and how do I tell it apart from ordinary moral argument finally taking effect after a long lag? Is there something that makes the flip sudden when it comes, or does it only look sudden afterwards? And can it be steered on purpose, or is that just what everyone who wants to ban something tells themselves?

If nothing in the literature fits exactly, I'd rather you coin something than hand me a word that covers everything — give me a definition with edges, a couple of boundary cases, and say plainly which of the near-misses is closest and where it fails. I've been burned before by reading a big satisfying term into a phenomenon and only later noticing that it didn't apply to half of what I wanted it for, so tell me what the thing is not.
