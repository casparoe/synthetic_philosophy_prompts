The bit I keep chewing on is the argument that this prior is basically malign: that if you take it seriously, most of the probability over observer-moments lands in worlds where some agent is running enormous numbers of simulations, and since those simulators are optimising something, the world you should expect to be in is one engineered to score well on their objective rather than a boring old physical universe.

I want to turn this into a proposal, maybe four pages, for one of those grants that fund independent researchers who aren't in academia, six months of work.

The half-formed idea I want it built around: the argument looks like it needs two steps — first, that observers are far more numerous inside simulations than outside, second, that the simulators' goals get to shape which simulation we end up in — and I suspect the second step only goes through if you smuggle in a particular way of counting observers (per program, per observer-moment, or some measure the prior itself doesn't hand you). If that's right, the malignity result is an artefact of a choice about how to count rather than something the prior forces on you.
