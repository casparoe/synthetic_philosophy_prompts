Take the doomsday argument in its plain form: you find yourself roughly the hundred-billionth human, you could have found yourself at any rank in the total sequence of humans, so the total sequence is probably not enormously longer than a hundred billion. I want your credence that this inference — rather than the rival claim that our early rank is an observation-selection artifact carrying no evidential weight about the total — should move a rational agent's estimate of the number of future humans downward by at least an order of magnitude. Decompose it: the probability that self-sampling is the right way to reason about one's own rank; the probability that the human reference class is defined sharply enough for the argument to run; the probability that observation-selection effects defeat it (we could only ever have observed a survivable, quiet history, so our not having gone extinct yet is weaker evidence than it appears); whether the prior over total population is even normalizable; and whether being surprised to be early is the right frame at all. Give a number and one line of justification per term, then the combination, then the term the final number is most sensitive to. For what it is worth, I am near 0.45 and a colleague is near 0.15; tell me which term we most likely disagree about, and what evidence would move you most.
