So I'm a backend engineer and I've somehow ended up in this little reading group where people keep throwing around "conventions" and "norms" like they're the same thing, and it's been bugging me. As far as I can piece together, David Lewis has this account where a convention is basically a solution to a coordination problem that's arbitrary -- nobody cares which side of the road we drive on as long as we all pick the same one -- and it keeps going because everyone expects everyone else to keep doing it. But then there's this other idea floating around where a norm is something you actually get punished for breaking, like people give you dirty looks or you lose standing. My friend insists these are just two names for the same thing, and honestly I can definately see why: at my last job everyone used tabs and new hires who used spaces got gently mocked until they switched, which looks like both at once.

Where I get lost is whether the punishment is doing any real work or whether it's just a convention with extra steps. Take the tabs thing -- is that a convention because it's arbitrary and self-reinforcing, or a norm because there's social pressure, and does the answer change anything about how you'd explain why it sticks around? And here's the case that really breaks my brain: imagine a regularity that everyone is stuck in but that basically everyone would rather abandon -- like we all drive on the left but we'd all be happier on the right, except no one can switch alone and nobody can coordinate the flip. Can Lewis's picture even describe that, or does it only make sense once you add norms and expectations about punishment on top? What I want is a straight comparison of the two ideas -- what each one says a regularity has to have in order to count, where they overlap, where they come apart, and whether one can be reduced to the other or whether they're genuinely different beasts. Don't worry about pointing me to papers or books, I just want to actually understand the difference.
