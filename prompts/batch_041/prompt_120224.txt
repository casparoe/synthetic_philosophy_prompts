My old stats TA used to say Hempel's raven thing is just like looking for your keys. The house has four rooms, you check the kitchen, the keys aren't there, so now you're more sure they're in the bedroom. Same deal with the ravens, he said: every green apple you glance at is one more room you've checked, so it nudges "all ravens are black" up a hair, and the whole so-called paradox is just people being bad at conditional probability. I never bought it and I can never say exactly why.

Break it down for me. What maps onto what -- the keys, the kitchen, the other three rooms, the four-room layout, the fact that the keys have got to be somewhere in the house, the looking-and-not-finding -- and where does the mapping hold versus where does it snap? Then, which features of the keys setup does the analogy quietly import into the raven case that aren't really there? (I have a feeling the "they've got to be somewhere in the house" part is doing all the work.) And does his conclusion survive -- that green apples genuinely do confirm the raven hypothesis, and that this makes the paradox definately evaporate -- or was the analogy carrying that conclusion the whole time? If there's a better analogy for what's actually going on, give me that too. Answer in American English.

Format it as a JSON object with these fields:

- "mapping": array of objects, each with "source_element" (a piece of the keys case), "target_element" (what it corresponds to in the raven case), "status" ("holds" | "breaks" | "partial"), and "note" (one or two sentences).
- "imported_assumptions": array of objects, each with "assumption", "what_it_does_for_the_keys_case", and "what_the_raven_case_would_need_for_it_to_hold".
- "disanalogies": array of objects, each with "disanalogy", "affects_the_conclusion" (true or false), and "why" (say why it does or doesn't matter for the use the analogy is being put to).
- "conclusion_verdict": object with "survives" ("yes" | "no" | "only_in_weakened_form"), "what_survives", and "what_falls".
- "better_analogy": a string, or null if none is better.
- "one_line_summary": a string.
