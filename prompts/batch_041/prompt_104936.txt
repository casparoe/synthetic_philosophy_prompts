I have been collecting a family of explanatory moves that strike me as failures, and I have never found a name for any of them that sticks. They seem to come in two forms, and I cannot decide whether they are one mistake or two.

The first form explains something by redescribing it at a lower level and then stopping. A writer will tell me that romantic love is "nothing but" a cocktail of neurochemicals, or that consciousness is "just" information processing in the brain, or that moral concern is "merely" a set of dispositions that natural selection installed. The lower-level vocabulary is offered as though it had discharged the explanatory debt, but nothing is said about how the lower-level facts add up to the phenomenon in question; the puzzle has been relabeled rather than resolved. And yet I cannot dismiss every such statement, because some of them look like genuine achievements: that heat is nothing but the motion of molecules seems to me a real explanation and not a dodge. So the move cannot be identified by its "nothing but" grammar alone.

The second form explains something by invoking a cause that already possesses the very feature to be explained. The eye is said to be the product of a designing intelligence; the mind is said to work because it runs a kind of virtual machine; life is said to be animated by a vital principle. In each case the proposed cause is at least as mysterious as the effect, so that the thing to be explained appears to have been smuggled in on the explaining side, and yet these explanations often feel satisfying and are sometimes praised as deeper than any mechanical account.

What I want to know is this. Is there an established term for each form, or a single term covering both? Are they the same error or two distinct ones, and if distinct, what separates them -- is it simply the direction of the gap, one reaching too low and the other too high, or is there a sharper criterion? Where is the line between the first form when it is a legitimate reduction and the first form when it is a cheat: what does "heat is molecular motion" possess that "love is chemistry" lacks? And are these genuinely failures, or is there a defensible reading on which each is a respectable explanatory strategy in the right setting?

I am not after a bare label. If the nearest existing concepts fit only partly, I want to know precisely how they fall short and which is closest; if nothing fits, then coin a term, define it, and tell me what falls inside and outside its edges.
