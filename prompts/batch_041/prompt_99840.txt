I am designing a case for a paper that argues the norm governing assertion should be indexed to the subject of knowledge — that whether an assertion is licensed can depend on who counts as the knower — and I want the case built to order rather than lifted from the standard repertoire.

Assume the following, so the case can be assessed against named positions. Four candidate norms of assertion are on the table: the knowledge account permits an assertion only if the speaker knows the proposition asserted; the truth account permits it if the proposition is true; the belief account permits it if the speaker believes it; and a justification (or evidence) account permits it if the speaker has adequate reason for it. Also assume the notion of distributed knowledge from epistemic logic: a proposition is distributed knowledge of a group when it follows from the facts the members know taken together, even if no individual member knows it — as when several people each know one premise of a valid inference and none of them knows the conclusion.

Now construct a case meeting this specification. A single speaker asserts a single proposition, and the case must be engineered so that (i) the knowledge account's verdict on that assertion depends on whether the knower is taken to be the individual speaker or the collective, flipping between permitted and forbidden; (ii) the truth, belief, and justification accounts return the same verdict however the subject is individuated; and (iii) no individual in the case knows the asserted proposition, yet the collective's epistemic position is strong enough that a reasonable reader feels the pull of the knowledge account permitting the assertion at the collective level. Constraints: realistic — a hospital, a newsroom, or a laboratory, with no science fiction, no demons, and no perfect predictors; no lottery cases, no fake barns, no standard Gettier structure, and not a preface-paradox case in which the group merely believes each conjunct; the scenario confined to a single paragraph; and the asserted proposition stated explicitly.

Then deliver the audit. Open with one sentence stating whether the specified verdict pattern is jointly satisfiable as stated and, if some part is impossible, which part and what the nearest satisfiable pattern is. Then give a compact table: each of the four accounts' verdict on the assertion, once with the individual speaker as subject and once with the collective, with a one-clause reason per cell. Then, in prose: name the single feature that makes the knowledge account flip and explain why the other three accounts are insensitive to it; say which notion of group knowledge you are invoking, distributed or common, and whether the case survives under the stricter reading; say what happens if that feature is removed — for instance, if one member comes to know the proposition outright — and whether the pattern collapses; and give the strongest reply available to a defender of the knowledge account who wants the flip to vanish. A case that merely stipulates that the collective knows while the individual does not, without earning the asymmetry from the described epistemic positions, does not count. Keep the whole response under 800 words.
