my reading group has been chewing through the illusionism stuff on consciousness and one guy keeps leaning on the desktop metaphor to sell it. his pitch goes like this: the icons and folders on your screen arent really there, underneath its just voltages in a chip, but nobody thinks the desktop is a deep mystery or that we're missing something when we say there's no folder-shaped object inside the machine. experience is the same deal, he says -- the felt redness, the sting of the pain, all of that is interface, real enough to use, but there's no further fact about whether its "really" like that, and once you see it that way the hard problem just dissolves. so illusionists arent taking anything away from you, he says, any more than a computer scientist is taking away your desktop.

i want to know how far this actually holds. start with the correspondence table -- what in the analogy is supposed to be playing the role of the qualia, the brain, the self, the "illusion" itself -- then give me the places it breaks. two things i keep tripping on: first, doesnt a user interface only make sense if there's someone on the far side of it looking at the screen, and if so hasnt he just relocated the thing he was trying to explain instead of removing it? second, the "real enough to use" move seems to work for a folder icon, where the point is what you can do with it, but does it work when the thing in question is the pain itself rather than a stand-in for a pain?

then tell me whether his conclusion -- illusionism costs you nothing -- survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was quietly carrying the conclusion the whole time. and if there's a better analogy for what illusionists are actually claiming, give me that instead.
