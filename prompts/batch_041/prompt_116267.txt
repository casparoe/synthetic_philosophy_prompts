My mother died last spring, and my brother, my sister, and I are dividing her estate: the house she lived in, some savings, and a few pieces of furniture we all want. Our circumstances differ sharply. My sister has a disability with substantial ongoing costs, and she wants her share to go toward making her housing accessible. My brother is comfortable and says he should get the least. I have two children starting college. We have argued for weeks, and each of us has a different idea of what fairness requires. My brother says we should each end up roughly equally well off, so he should receive least. My sister says that is precisely the kind of judgment about her life she has spent her life resisting, and that what she wants is the same wherewithal as the rest of us to run her own life. I think what matters is that each of us ends up equally able to do the things we care about, and for me that includes paying my children's tuition. Two things complicate it: our mother paid for my brother's graduate degree and helped me with a down payment, but could not do the same for my sister; and my sister wants to keep the house and live in it, which the rest of us would have to allow or be bought out of. How should we decide what we are trying to equalize, and do the earlier gifts and the house change the answer? Close with a single line beginning "Verdict:" that names, in one short phrase, the standard you think we should adopt.
