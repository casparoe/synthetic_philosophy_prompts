I am trying to determine whether a pattern I keep encountering has an established name, and if it does, what that name is — though a name by itself will not satisfy me, since what I actually need is the pattern drawn precisely, with its edges marked.

I notice the pattern in three settings. In the first, a thousand entrants are in a draw and exactly one will be selected. Of any single entrant I am almost certain that they will not be selected, and I would say as much of each one in turn; yet I am equally certain that some entrant will be selected, and I would not say that no entrant will be. In the second, I have just finished a long factual book containing several hundred separate claims. I have checked each claim and would stand behind any of them on its own, and yet I judge it likely that at least one of them is false, and I say so in a note at the front. In each of these two settings every individual item seems to me entirely in order while the collection does not, and I cannot put my finger on the item that is at fault. The third setting feels of a piece with the others and yet will not sit still alongside them: I have made several promises to several different people, and each promise, taken alone, is one I am able to keep; but I have made so many that I cannot keep them all, and still there is no single promise I am unable to keep.

What I want is not merely a label but an account. Is there one name for the general phenomenon, or several? Are the three settings one thing or different things — and if different, what distinguishes them? Is the difficulty a matter of probability, of what I believe, of what I may assert, or of something else entirely, and does the answer come out the same for the third setting as for the first two? I would also like to know how the nearest established ideas differ from what I have described; I suspect the first two settings may have received standard treatments while the third may not, and I do not want the third absorbed into the others if it does not genuinely belong there. If no established name covers the whole, please coin one and define it, marking what falls inside and what falls outside.

Begin with your answer in the first sentence — whether the phenomenon has an established name, and if so which — and then give the reasoning, the distinctions, and the boundary cases, without preamble or restatement of what I have asked.
