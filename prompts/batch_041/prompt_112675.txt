I was at a panel thing last month and two of the speakers, a narrative designer and a systems designer, got into it afterward over whether you can actually wrong a video-game character, and I still cant decide who had the better of it, so I'm outsourcing. Her line: she'd written an NPC whose whole deal is that she trusts you, and on one route you get her killed offscreen and the game just quietly stops mentioning her, and she said that's a genuine wrong done to her, not just sad content. His line: there's no "her," it's a script, so whatever's wrong there is wrong to the player or to the real people the character stands for, not to the character. She came back with the save system -- you can rewind, so the death is optional -- and she said optional doesn't mean harmless, the same way a promise you could have broken still binds, and the betrayal was real inside the fiction. He said that's exactly the problem: promises bind because someone can be let down, and there's nobody there to be let down, just a file; the only one who can end up disappointed is you, and your being disappointed doesn't turn it into a wrong. She said the fiction is where the someone lives, that you can wrong a character the way you can wrong a person in a memory, and that a game which remembers your reloads and has her bring them up is the game itself conceding the point. He said no, that's just the designer messing with your head. They were basically stuck on the rewind thing when I broke it up.
