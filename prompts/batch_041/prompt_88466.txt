my phil of perception reading group is doing Molyneuxs question next week -- the man born blind who learns to tell a cube from a sphere by touch, then gets his sight and is asked which is which by eye alone, with Locke saying he couldn't and Leibniz saying he could. i want to open the session with a poem instead of a summary, so can you write me a pantoum, 4 or 5 stanzas, in the voice of someone who's just had the surgery as an adult and is sitting in a clinic with two objects in front of them. the thing i actually want the form to carry: the repeated lines are things the subject says about the objects, and i want the same words to come back but with the reader losing track of whether a given repeat came from the hand or the eye -- "round" staying one word while becoming two. dont resolve the question, and dont let the last line be a shrug like "who can say" -- the not-knowing should be specific, a real result rather than a dodge. someone whos read Locke and Leibniz should recognize the problem in it, someone whos never heard of it should still get a good poem.
