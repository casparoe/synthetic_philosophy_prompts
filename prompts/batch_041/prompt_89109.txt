In a seminar on Augustine's ethics, a classmate ended a long argument about whether a physician may mislead a dying patient about the gravity of her condition by announcing, "the Augustinian rule is that every lie is a sin," and treating that as decisive. I want to know what that sentence could actually be claiming before I accept that it settles anything. Please separate the distinct precise theses that "every lie is a sin" might be asserting, ranging from the near-trivial to the very strong, and then judge each.

In working through them, make sure the menu distinguishes at least these axes, since I suspect the disagreement is hiding inside them: whether the sentence is analytic (a definition of "lie" that already builds in wrongfulness, so it tells us nothing) or a substantive moral claim; how wide or narrow a scope is given to "lie" (does it cover statements the speaker believes false but no one is actually deceived by, misleading truths, deliberate omissions, figurative or joking speech, and concealment of what one is not obliged to reveal?); what work "sin" is doing (a theological offense against God, a secular wrong, or merely a blemish on the liar's character that need not be blameworthy or punishable); whether the claim is exceptionless or defeasible; and whether it concerns the wrongness of the act or only the culpability of the agent. Several of these readings will turn out to be compatible with each other, and at least one will be a tautology that no one should have been impressed by; be explicit about which collapse into each other.

For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive but defensible, or false, and — this is the part I care most about — whether it would actually license my classmate's conclusion that the physician must not mislead the patient, or whether it leaves that case open. Some readings will condemn the misleading but not for the reason he gave; note that. Then say which single reading the people who repeat this sentence most likely mean, whether that reading is defensible, and what its strongest objection is.

Give me the result as a JSON object with this shape:

{
  "readings": [
    {
      "name": "short label for the reading",
      "precise_claim": "the thesis stated as clearly as possible, in one or two sentences",
      "status": "one of: trivial | substantive_defensible | false",
      "supports_the_classmate": "yes | no | only under the further assumption that ...",
      "commits_you_to": "what else someone who holds this reading is thereby committed to"
    }
  ],
  "collapsed_readings": ["pairs or groups of readings that amount to the same claim, with a one-line reason"],
  "likely_intended_reading": "the reading most people probably mean when they say it, and why",
  "strongest_objection_to_intended_reading": "the best argument against that reading",
  "what_would_change_my_mind": "the specific argument or consideration that would lead you to a different verdict on the hardest case here, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail"
}

I'd rather have a genuinely discriminating answer than a balanced survey, so rank the readings by how well they support the classmate's use rather than presenting them as equally good. Be candid about which parts of this you are confident in and which are reconstructions on your part, and tell me plainly where the reasoning is most likely to break down.
