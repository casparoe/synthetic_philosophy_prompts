I am a high school junior and I write a short column on elections for our school paper, mainly because our student council switched to ranked-choice voting last term and immediately fell into a fight about whether it was fair for a bloc of students to rank a second-choice candidate first in order to knock out the candidate they all disliked. That argument sent me into the mathematics of voting, and now I want to write one joke that is genuinely funny to someone who knows that mathematics.

Here is the material I want the joke to stand on. The Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem says that if a rule must name a single winner, if at least three different candidates can win under some arrangement of the voters' preferences, and if no single voter is a dictator whose favourite always wins, then there is some situation in which a voter gets a better outcome by submitting a ballot that does not match their true preferences. So the only ungameable single-winner rules are dictatorships. I have also collected the escape routes, because I find them funnier than the theorem itself. If the rule is allowed to return a set of winners instead of one, dictatorship is no longer forced; a later result by Duggan and Schwartz shows that a rule of that kind which treats all voters alike, does not exclude any candidate in advance, and cannot be gamed must put every voter's favourite candidate into the winner set, which is a strange way of satisfying everybody. If everyone's preferences are instead constrained to line up along a single left-to-right axis, the favourite of the middle voter wins and nobody gains anything by lying. If the outcome is allowed to depend partly on chance, the rules that treat all voters alike and cannot be gamed are, essentially, lotteries over the voters' own favourite candidates. And for some rules, working out the best strategic ballot is computationally very hard, which is a comfort of an odd kind.

What I would like from you is one joke, no more than about 120 words, with a short setup and a punchline, set in a school or club context — a class election, a student council race, a club choosing its treasurer, anything of that kind. A dialogue or a small scene is fine. The humour has to come from the content above rather than from generic jokes about politicians lying or elections being rigged: a reader who knows the result should find the punchline apt, and a reader who does not should be puzzled rather than bored. Please do not simply announce the theorem in a funny voice. After the joke, give me three or four sentences explaining exactly what the punchline turns on, so I can tell whether it is the joke I want before I print it.
