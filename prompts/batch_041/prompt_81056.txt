ok so I'm writing down my accuracy-first creed for a reading group and I want a sanity check before I get shredded. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) accuracy is the only thing that's epistemically good in itself — coherence, fitting the evidence, understanding, all of that is good only as a means to being closer to the truth. And I mean accuracy now, not accuracy at the end of inquiry or from the perspective of some ideal agent; I don't want any of that smuggled in.
(2) accuracy gets measured by a proper scoring rule, and I figure wich proper rule you use is a matter of taste — they should all spit out the same norms.
(3) probabilism is non-negotiable: incoherent credences are irrational, full stop.
(4) conditionalization is a genuine requirement — learn E for certain and your credence in H should be your old conditional credence — and I want that to fall out of (1)-(3), not be bolted on as a separate axiom.
(5) I'm a permissivist: for alot of bodies of evidence, more than one credence function is fully rational.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, how bad is each conflict, and what's the cheapest way to fix it? Order them by severity and say which side has to move. And tell me which pairs only look like they clash — I'd rather not "fix" something that was never broken.
