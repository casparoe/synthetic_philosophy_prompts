My uncle has been writing a book about our hometown for about three years now, and he's stuck on the chapter everyone in the family agrees is the important one: the winter of '78, when the reservoir froze solid, the mill never reopened, and roughly half the town moved away. I actually like his opening line — he wrote that the cold "did not knock, it came in and sat down at the table" — and he wants to write the whole chapter as if the weather were a character with intentions. He's even got a system where every cause in the story gets ranked on a scale from "backdrop" to "actress" (he means actor, but he's been saying actress for months and now he cant stop). Same idea for the other chapters: the 1918 flu is "The Uninvited Guest," and he keeps bringing up some volcano in Iceland in the 1780s that he says ruined harvests all over Europe, as a kind of template for the whole book.

My sister thinks this is a cop-out. The mill's owners had been clear-cutting the hillside for a decade, they voted down the dam repairs twice, and in her view a book that makes the winter the villain quietly lets every human being in the story off the hook — it's just another way of saying nobody could have done anything differently. I half agree with her and half dont. The winter really did do something to us, and I can't figure out how to say that without it sounding like I'm saying the winter meant to do it; and I'm not sure she's right that talking about weather is an excuse, because sometimes it honestly wasn't anybody's fault, and the flu chapter would be monstrous if we turned it into a morality play about the people who caught it.

I'm sitting down with him on Saturday and I want to be useful instead of just picking a side. Should the book treat the weather, the flu, the volcano as causes, or as characters — and is that even a real distinction, or a metaphor doing sneaky work? How do I tell when an explanation like this is illuminating and when it's an alibi? Give me exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no wrap-up at the end — I want to read them out loud at the kitchen table without any preamble.
