At a seminar on comparative economic systems, the instructor framed Lange's market socialism as the competitive market with a single substitution: the Central Planning Board takes the place of the Walrasian auctioneer, adjusting prices by trial and error in response to shortages and surpluses until equilibrium is reached. On this framing, Lange's model is just the competitive market minus private capital, so its claim to solve the calculation problem follows almost automatically — the same price-adjustment procedure does the informational work. I want an audit of the analogy, not a verdict on the debate. Lay out the correspondence table (auctioneer, traders, price vector, excess-demand signal, equilibrium), then say where the mapping breaks, separating the disanalogies that bear on the claim that socialism can replicate the market's information-processing from those that do not. Tell me specifically what the auctioneer picture quietly assumes about the givenness of knowledge and about who owns and disposes of resources, and whether the instructor's conclusion survives once those assumptions are made explicit. If a better analogy exists, give it.
