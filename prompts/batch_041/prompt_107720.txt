Write me a joke about van Fraassen's Reflection principle. Not a generic philosophy joke with a probability pun bolted on the end — the punchline has to actually depend on what the principle says, i.e. roughly that your credence in something now should match the credence you expect to have later, so your future self's beliefs end up binding on you already.

Ideally it hinges on the bit people usually skate over: Reflection is only plausible when the future self you're deferring to is better informed, and it goes mad the moment you know your future self is just differently wired — drunk, in love, halfway through a cult induction weekend, or simply someone who always feels braver at 2am. So a bloke who knows he'll be 90% sure he can drive home after the pub, and who takes Reflection seriously, is already 90% sure now. That sort of thing, but funnier than that, and with the joke doing the work rather than a lecture.

Set it somewhere concrete — a Wetherspoons, a therapy session, a betting shop, a suport group, wherever you like — and keep it short. Dont explain the joke afterwards; if it needs a footnote its not the joke I want.

No "a Bayesian walks into a bar" openers, and nothing where the punchline is just that philosophers are pedants. Someone who's actually read van Fraassen should laugh; someone who hasn't should still be able to follow it.
