I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so forgive me if I mangle the jargon. I'm writing a book where my protagonist keeps waking up in the same room with no memory of the day before, and a friend told me this is basically the Sleeping Beauty problem, which I'd somehow never come across until last month. Here's the setup as I understand it. They flip a fair coin on the Sunday. If it's heads she gets woken once, on the Monday. If it's tails she gets woken twice, Monday and Tuesday, with her memory wiped in between so she can't tell the days apart. Each time she wakes she's asked what she thinks the coin did.

My friend says theres a big row about this and that some people say a third and some say a half. But I genuinely don't see how there can be a row. The coin is fair and nothing that happens to her afterwards can change that. However many times they wake her, the coin still came up the way it came up, and simply being awake isn't evidence about a coin. So her credence has to stay at a half, and the one-third crowd must be doing something like spreading the principle of indifference over the number of awakenings she might be having, which you obviously can't do, because she's already awake — that's an observation, not a hypothesis you get to hand out probability to. Have I got that right? I'd rather not build a whole chapter on a howler.

What I actually need out of this is a bit of dialogue. There's a scene where a philosopher character explains the whole thing to my protagonist, and the protagonist has the horrible realisation that her life might just be a repeated waking, and I want the explanation to land as obviously correct rather than wishy-washy. So can you write me a tight version of the argument, maybe three or four hundred words of speech, that a smart and slightly arrogant philosopher would give, showing why the one-third answer is a mistake and a half is the only sane one? If the argument needs any tidying up before it's usable on the page, do that first and tell me in plain terms what you changed and why, then give me the speech. (In an earlier draft it was a six-sided die and she was woken a number of times equal to the roll, but my editor said a coin was cleaner, so ignore that unless it matters.) No headings, bullet points, bold text or tables please, just continuous prose the way you'd write an email, and in British English.
