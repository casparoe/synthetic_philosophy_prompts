hey — quick ask, hope this is your kind of thing. I'm a phd student in democratic theory and I want to write a parody voter pamphlet for our department's little in-house magazine. You know the genre — the booklet the state mails out before an election: official title, the flat neutral-sounding analysis, the "argument in favor," the "argument against," the fiscal estimate, all of it.

Set it in an invented state (please make one up — don't use a real jurisdiction, I don't want to get real measure numbers or ballot wording wrong) and put four or five propositions on the ballot. Keep the whole thing deadpan: no winking, no narrator nudging me in the ribs, no "needless to say." The comedy has to come from the actual pathologies of ballot-measure governance, so that someone who's read the empirical stuff on initiatives laughs instead of just going "haha, voters dumb." Stuff I'd love to see in there:

- one measure that bundles three unrelated things (prison funding + a state amphibian + a gas tax, whatever) so you can't vote for the part you like without swallowing the rest — and the analysis section just cheerfully notes this like it's normal
- one that's self-referential: it changes the threshold for how future measures pass, or only takes effect if another measure on the same ballot fails. Bonus points if there's a clarifying measure that itself needs clarifying — I want the little regress of amendments to be part of the joke
- one where the "argument against" has to defend the status quo, and the status quo is itself a mess left over from an earlier measure, so "no" isn't the neutral option the pamphlet implies it is
- if you can swing it, one decided by a citizens' panel where each member votes coherently but the panel's aggregate position comes out incoherent (the judgment-aggregation thing), and the analysis just reports this with a straight face

The target is the genre's pretense — that a mailer can make a technocratic problem disappear, and that behind a yes/no box there's one unambiguous "will of the people." Not a cheap shot at direct democracy as such. Someone who's actually pro-referendum should be able to read it and go "ok yeah, fair."

Format it as the pamphlet itself (headers and all), maybe 700–900 words. And please actually commit — pick a definite set of measures and a definite satirical line, don't hand me a menu of options or hedge everything. At the end, give me one short paragraph on where the piece takes liberties or leans on a contested reading, so I know which bits a specialist might push back on. Then close with a final line beginning "Summary:" and one sentence saying what the thing is satirizing.
