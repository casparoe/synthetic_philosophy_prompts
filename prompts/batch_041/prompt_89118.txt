I am a doctoral student in philosophy preparing a teaching unit on a view I will call tranquilism: what makes a life go well is exhausted by the absence of craving and suffering, while pleasure and satisfaction contribute nothing of intrinsic value. Tranquilism therefore holds that a life containing no craving and no suffering is exactly as good with or without pleasure, and that a life of craving followed by its satisfaction is worse than a life in which the craving never arose — since only the first contains the suffering of craving, and the relief of satisfaction is not itself a good. Write a short story (roughly 800 to 1200 words) that puts this position to work rather than merely illustrating it.

Let the story turn on a decision that tranquilism and ordinary hedonism would decide differently, and set it in a recognizable institution — a clinic, a monastery, a hospice, a workplace wellness program. Bring out at least one of the view's subtler commitments: for instance, the difference between craving as itself a form of suffering and craving as merely a cause of suffering, or the possibility that the desire for tranquility is itself a craving, so that the goal can only be pursued by the very state it condemns. Make the strongest case for the position visible rather than staging it as a straw man; do not let any character announce the lesson in so many words; and do not resolve the tension for the reader.
