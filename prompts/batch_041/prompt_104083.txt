Longtime reader, first-time poster, and I keep getting dragged into futarchy arguments, so I want to know if my own position actually hangs together — I suspect it doesn't but I can't see where. Here's where I've landed:

1. The basic futarchy setup is right: we vote on a welfare metric, then use conditional prediction markets to pick the policies that maximize it. The whole vote-on-values-bet-on-beliefs split.
2. Prediction markets are the best belief-aggregators we've got — a liquid price is basically the rational posterior.
3. For a market to guide an actual decision it has to be conditional: you bet on the metric under policy P versus under not-P.
4. Manipulation worries are way overblown. A manipulator just hands money to the arbitrageurs, so markets stay honest on their own.
5. The metric itself stays out of the market — values are for the voters, that's the entire point of the split.
6. Goodhart's law is basically true: any measure that becomes a target gets gamed.

And here's my proposed fix for the one worry I can actually articulate: to keep conditional markets resolvable, only run P in a randomly chosen subset of jurisdictions and read the metric off the rest as the control. I think that saves the whole thing.

Is this package consistent? Which parts are actually pulling against each other, which only look like they clash, and what's the cheapest thing I'd have to give up? Rank the real tensions by how bad they are rather than telling me they're all fine or all fatal. And tell me honestly whether my randomization fix does the work I want or just relocates the problem. American English, please.
