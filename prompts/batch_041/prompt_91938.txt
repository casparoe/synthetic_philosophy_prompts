In patent law the exclusive right goes to one party, whether the rule is first to invent or first to file, even when several people have arrived at the same invention independently and within months of one another; scientific prizes likewise parcel out credit to a few named individuals even when the result was, as we say, in the air. Reconstruct the theory of discovery that this practice presupposes. Does it commit us to the claim that a genuine discovery is the deed of one particular mind, such that it would not have occurred without that mind, or is it equally at home with the view that simultaneous discovery is the rule and that some competent investigator would soon have reached the same place? Which rival theories does the practice genuinely rule out, and which does it merely sit comfortably beside? Where is it underdetermined — could a purely forward-looking incentive story, indifferent to who deserves credit, license the very same rules? Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
