Here is the essay, more or less as it stands. Call a situation *relocation-symmetric* if two agents who differ only in which of a set of positions they occupy, and who otherwise share their total evidence, must assign the same credence to the proposition that the outcome at their own position is some value X. My thesis is that the Principal Principle is nothing more than the demand that credence be invariant under relocation across the positions that a chance hypothesis treats as interchangeable. On this reading, when you conditionalize on the hypothesis that the chance of heads is one half, you are not deferring to an expert — there is no expert anywhere in the picture, no better-informed agent whose opinion you are adopting. You are instead ruling out a class of credences on the grounds that they would make your opinion depend on *which* toss you happen to be, when the hypothesis says the tosses are, relative to your evidence, interchangeable. Chance is what remains of your credence once you forget your position; the principle should really be called the Principle of Indexical Coarse-Graining. Two consequences follow, and they pull in opposite directions. First, the notorious undermining problem — the futures that make the present chance hypothesis false — appears to dissolve rather than need solving, since if the principle is a symmetry constraint on a *current* credence, the later truth or falsity of the chance proposition is no more relevant to whether the constraint is met than the later collapse of a physical symmetry is to whether a symmetric prior was rational. Second, and worse for me, admissibility stops being a mysterious restriction and becomes a substantive hypothesis about which positions are genuinely interchangeable — which means the principle inherits everything we think about the objectivity of those symmetries. If the symmetries are selected to fit the observed frequencies, the principle becomes an ornate way of saying "be calibrated"; if they are selected to fit the chances, we are back to circularity. I believe there is a path between those two failures, but I cannot tell whether it is a genuine middle or just the space between two horns I've been handed.
