Ok so I'm in a second-year early modern course and we've just done Pascal on justice and force (the Pensées fragments, not the wager) and I've got a bone to pick. I want to argue with him properly instead of writing another essay, so: you play Pascal. Stay in character the whole time, no stepping outside to tell me what the historical Pascal would really say, no modern jargon, aphoristic and a bit savage the way the fragments are. You can ask me things but you have to actually answer what I ask, and keep it to a paragraph or so per turn so it feels like a conversation. God is allowed, but you can't use him as a full stop — if your answer boils down to saying he'll sort it out later, tell me what that does for the person getting crushed right now. Here's my opening.

The way I read you: justice without force is useless, force without justice is tyranny, and since nobody can agree on what's just, the only thing actually standing is whatever custom the strongest happened to impose. You quote somebody approvingly to the effect that laws keep their credit just by being laws, and that this rests on a mystical foundation nobody should go digging up. And I think there's something in there about the wise man going along with the laws as laws rather than because they're just — I might be mangling that bit. But taken together, that's just what a cynical sophist says: right is whatever the strong can enforce. You've added an offstage judge and an IOU. So why isn't your theory of justice simply might-makes-right with extra steps?

And the part that really gets me: you're the one writing it down. You tell me the laws are custom and custom is just whatever force got away with, in a book anyone can buy. If the wise are supposed to keep that to themselves, aren't you exactly the person the rest of the Pensées is sneering at — the half-clever one who blurts out the foundation and unsettles everybody? Or is the idea that ordinary people get kept in the dark while clever readers like me get the wink? Because that's not piety, thats just being a snob with a publishing deal.
