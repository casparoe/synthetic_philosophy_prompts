Ok so I've been chewing on Hofstadter's careenium thing — the giant vat of tiny magnetized balls where some high-level pattern you could write down in symbols seems to be the thing actually steering the balls around. Everyone nods along in the book, but I want to press on it. Give me a tier list of the different ways people try to cash out the claim that the symbol-level pattern pushes the simmballs around, ranked by how much genuine causal work they leave for the symbol (top tier = the symbol is really doing something; bottom tier = it's a nice summary and nothing more).

Here's the twist that I think makes it hard, and its my own setup so don't just hand me the stock answers. Picture a rigged careenium: every single simmball trajectory is fully pinned down by the initial microstate, zero wiggle room. But the system is built so the same high-level symbol pattern can be realized by a huge number of different microstates, and swapping which microstate realizes it never changes the pattern. Call it a locked careenium. So on one side you've got total micro determinism, on the other you've got massive multiple realizability. Rank the candidate stories by how well each survives that without collapsing into either the balls just bounce and symbols are decoration or the symbol is a real cause but its spooky:

- the individual simmballs and their pairwise collisions
- the boundary / initial conditions
- the symbol pattern itself, taken as a bona fide higher-level cause
- the multiply-realizable structure itself (the many-microstates-one-symbol fact)
- a pressure/selection story where symbol patterns that persist get to keep re-constraining future microstates
- whatever else you think belongs on the list, add it and slot it in

For each one, one or two sentences on why it lands where it does, and be honest about the criterion your using to rank them, because I suspect people quietly switch between counterfactual difference-making and real oomph and that switching is doing a lot of the work.
