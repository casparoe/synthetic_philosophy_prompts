I spent forty years in experimental physics before retiring, and I have lately been reading philosophy with more enthusiasm than competence. Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life caught my attention, because his three modes of history — monumental, antiquarian, and critical — describe with uncomfortable accuracy the three ways my old department recounts the life of its founder. I would like you to write me a joke about this, of the sort that would amuse someone who has actually read the essay and merely puzzle someone who has not. The humor should turn on the content of the modes rather than on the mere labels — ideally on the way each mode's characteristic virtue and characteristic pathology are the same trait seen from two sides. Please set it in a scientific rather than an artistic or political milieu, since that is the world I know. And one further thing I would like you to fold in: I have come to think Nietzsche's catalogue is incomplete, and that the fourth mode, the one natural to a working scientist, is neither the search for exemplars nor for origins nor for indictments but the controlled experiment — the deliberate reproduction of the past under altered conditions in order to learn what caused what. If the punchline can land on that addition without the joke collapsing into a lecture, I will be grateful.
