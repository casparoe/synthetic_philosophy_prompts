I cover philosophy of religion, and I keep running into a version of Molinism I have started calling dispositional Molinism: the counterfactuals of creaturely freedom that God is said to know prior to any creative decree are true because they are grounded in each creature's essential dispositions — what that creature would freely choose in each circumstance — while the creature still retains libertarian self-determination at the moment of choice, so those counterfactuals remain contingent rather than necessary. Give me your credence that some defensible version of this position is true: that a middle knowledge survives the grounding objection by being grounded in creaturely essences, without collapsing either into Calvinist determinism or into the claim that the counterfactuals are necessary truths. I want the decomposition, not just the number — the prior question of whether counterfactuals of freedom have determinate truth values at all, whether the grounding objection is actually decisive against the standard view, whether essence-grounding really preserves libertarian freedom, whether a God who knew such counterfactuals prior to decree would be choosing a world or merely reading one off, and whatever other terms you think belong, each with a rough probability and one line of justification, then how they combine. My own credence is about 0.35, and I would like to know where the distance between us most plausibly sits. Finally, tell me which term you are least confident in and what argument or consideration would move the final number most.
