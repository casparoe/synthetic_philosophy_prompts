I recently moved from an alignment-focused team to a control-focused team that designs safety protocols under the assumption that the model may be misaligned. A phrase I keep hearing in design reviews is that we should assume the model is misaligned, and it gets used to justify everything from sandbagging evaluations to strict human oversight to treating every apparent alignment success as worthless. I need a menu of the precise claims that phrase could be making—what kind of misalignment is being assumed (goal, capability, deception, situational awareness), at what stage, with what evidence, and what follows for the safety measures it is meant to support. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible design principle, or false or self-undermining, and which reading actually supports the moves it is invoked to justify. Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this area, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
