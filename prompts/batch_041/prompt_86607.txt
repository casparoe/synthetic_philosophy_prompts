For a graduate seminar I am assembling on the philosophy of church–state relations, I need a structured reconstruction of the commitments that lie buried in a fiscal institution rather than in anyone's professed doctrine. The point is to read a practice the way one reads a text: to recover the theory it would have to hold in order to make sense of itself, and to separate what it genuinely requires from what it merely happens to fit.

Take the German church tax (Kirchensteuer). The tax offices of the Länder collect it on behalf of certain religious bodies. A person registered as a member of a levying body — typically a Protestant regional church, the Roman Catholic Church, or a Jewish community — pays a surcharge of eight or nine per cent of their assessed income tax, withheld by the employer and confirmed at the annual assessment. The taxpayer's affiliation is stored in a database maintained by the federal tax administration, and employers receive extracts of it for withholding purposes. Membership is not something the community adjudicates: one becomes liable by being recorded as a member, and one ends liability by making a formal declaration to a civil authority, after which some communities still decline weddings and funerals to the declarant. Only bodies recognised as corporations under public law may levy the tax, and several communities that hold that status decline to use it. The levy furnishes the large majority of church revenue, and the income on which it is paid is not itself subject to state income tax.

To bring the presuppositions into relief, compare two alternatives, which I describe rather than name. In the first, a fixed fraction of everyone's income tax is directed, at the taxpayer's election, to a religious body, or to the state for social purposes, or to nothing in particular, and the election has no bearing on anyone's membership in anything. In the second, a levy is collected from those registered either with a religious community or with a secular humanist association, on equal terms.

Reconstruct the implicit theory of the first arrangement. Set out what view of religious belonging, of state neutrality, of consent and exit, and of what makes a body eligible for this treatment the practice commits itself to; say which of those commitments it strictly presupposes and which it merely accommodates, so that a rival theory would license the practice just as well; identify the philosophical positions it rules out and the ones it leaves open; flag any places where its parts presuppose inconsistent things; and, for each commitment you isolate, say what would have to change in the institution if that commitment were replaced. The contrasting models are there to help you locate exactly what is load-bearing, so use them to show which feature of the German arrangement carries which commitment.

Return your answer as a single JSON object with exactly the following keys, and no others:

- "practice_summary": a one-sentence description of the practice under analysis.
- "presuppositions": an array of objects, each with "concept" (the concept the commitment concerns, e.g. religious belonging, state neutrality, consent), "commitment" (the view the practice commits to, stated as a proposition), "status" (one of "strictly presupposed", "merely fits", or "underdetermined"), and "justification" (why the practice requires, merely fits, or leaves open that view).
- "ruled_out_rivals": an array of objects, each with "position" (a rival view the practice excludes) and "why_incompatible" (the feature of the practice that excludes it).
- "compatible_rivals": an array of strings, each naming a rival view with which the practice is nonetheless consistent.
- "internal_tensions": an array of objects, each with "tension" (the two features that pull apart) and "assessment" (whether the tension is resolvable, and how).
- "foil_comparison": an array of two objects, one per contrasting model, each with "foil" (a short label for that model) and "what_it_isolates" (the commitment it exposes by omitting it).
- "replacement_effects": an array of objects, each with "commitment_replaced" (a commitment from your list) and "resulting_changes" (an array of strings describing what the institution would have to alter if that commitment gave way).

Keep the philosophical vocabulary explicit and the reasoning tied to features of the practice; I want the reconstruction to be defensible point by point, not a general impression.
