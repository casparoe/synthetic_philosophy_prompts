Draft the joint statement that a committed tranquilist and a committed hedonist could both put their names to after a year of good-faith adversarial collaboration. Treat tranquilism as the view that only the absence of suffering and craving is intrinsically good, where craving is a felt state of unsatisfied wanting that persists until its object is obtained or the wanting subsides, and hedonism as the view that pleasure is intrinsically good and pain intrinsically bad, so that a life containing pleasure is better than an otherwise identical life without it. I want the agreed claims — what each party takes welfare to consist in, why each takes suffering to be bad, and what each would accept as evidence bearing on the value of pleasure; the remaining disagreements, each formulated in wording its own side would endorse; the cruxes, with a case or finding both would agree bears on each and each side's prediction recorded in advance; and any part of the dispute that turns out to be merely verbal. Two cases should stay on the table throughout: a life containing no suffering, no craving, and no pleasure, and a life containing no suffering and no craving but a great deal of pleasure. I expect one crux to be whether pleasure that never generates craving is possible for creatures like us, and another to be whether the tranquilist is committed to holding that all suffering-free lives are equally good; address both, and any further cruxes you find. Work from the positions as characterized here rather than from the literature, and organise the result as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
