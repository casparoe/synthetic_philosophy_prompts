In a seminar on whether an agent's deliberations must be defensible from an impersonal standpoint, a classmate invoked Williams's phrase 'one thought too many' — the case of a man who saves his wife rather than a stranger, whose motivating thought, as she put it, should be simply that it is his wife, not that it is his wife and that saving her is permissible. From this she drew a large conclusion: that impartial morality is not merely demanding but self-undermining, since the very structure it imposes on deliberation would corrode the relationships that supply the agent's reasons in the first place. Two others in the room then used the same phrase for much smaller points — one as a claim about what a well-brought-up person would actually be thinking while hauling someone out of the water, the other as a claim that no theory which asks an agent to consult a principle can correctly describe what there is reason to do.

I suspect these are three different theses wearing one phrase, and that at least one of them is doing no work at all. Please lay out the precise claims 'one thought too many' could be making, as a menu ordered from the nearly trivial to the radical, rather than choosing one and defending it. For each, say what it is a claim about — the psychology of actual deliberation, the content of the agent's motivating thought, the conditions under which an action has the value it has, the structure of practical justification, the relation between a criterion of rightness and a usable decision procedure, or the authority of moral considerations over an agent's life — and whether it is descriptive or normative. Then, for each reading, say whether it is plausible, contentious, or plainly false; which other readings it collapses into or is strictly independent of; and whether it would support my classmate's large conclusion, the two small ones, or none of them. Finally, say which readings survive transposing the example — a stranger in place of the wife, or a professional obligation in place of a personal tie — and which reading the people who repeat the phrase are most likely to have in mind. I don't need citations or pointers into the literature; I want the distinctions and your verdicts on them.
