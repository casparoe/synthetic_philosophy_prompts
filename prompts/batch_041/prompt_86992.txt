In my ethics reading group we have been stuck for the better part of a year on the aggregation question in its starkest form: whether one person's being tortured for fifty years is worse than an unimaginably large number of people — one for every particle in the observable universe and then some — each getting a single barely noticeable speck of dust in the eye. Everyone knows the standard moves and nobody has moved. We tried to break the deadlock with a variant that holds the total quantity of irritation fixed and varies only the number of people it is spread across, and with a variant in which the tiny harms never rise to the level of awareness; neither shifted anyone, which makes me suspect the real disagreement is somewhere other than where we think it is. My own hypothesis is that the two sides disagree about what an intuition is evidence for when it is formed about quantities no one can represent — one side treating the anti-aggregation intuition as a datum, the other treating it as a scope artefact — but I would rather have a diagnosis than have my hypothesis confirmed.

So diagnose the impasse rather than adjudicate it. Say what kind of disagreement this is — substantive and normative, verbal, two sides answering different questions under one heading, or a hidden empirical or formal question in disguise (about the structure of the betterness relation, about transitivity or lexical priority, about the reliability of intuitions at scale) — and support the diagnosis from what each side is actually committed to. Then tell me whether the disagreement is bedrock or arguable, and what, if anything, would count as progress: a distinction, a formal result, a change of question, or an argument that the question is ill-posed. Be explicit about what would not count as progress.

Write the diagnosis in prose, in roughly 500 words, and then append a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- "disagreement_kind": one of "substantive-normative", "verbal", "different-questions", "hidden-formal", "hidden-empirical", "bedrock-intuition", "mixed", followed by a one-sentence justification.
- "side_a": the aggregating side's actual commitments, in three sentences or fewer.
- "side_b": the anti-aggregating side's actual commitments, in three sentences or fewer.
- "crux": the single thing the disagreement turns on, in one sentence.
- "hidden_question": the empirical or formal question, if any, doing unacknowledged work here; "none" if there is none, with a reason either way.
- "status": one of "arguable", "bedrock", "ill-posed", with a sentence of support.
- "progress_conditions": a list of at least three specific things that would move the debate.
- "false_progress": a list of at least two things that look like progress but would not be.
- "confidence": a number from 0 to 1, with one sentence on what would change it.
