I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and I have ended up holding a position about animals that I want stress-tested rather than confirmed. It is a side-constraint view, in the spirit of the passage in Nozick's Anarchy, State, and Utopia where he asks whether there are limits to what we may do to animals and whether they have the moral status of mere objects. My version runs as follows. First, the right structure for the animal case is the same as for the human case: boundary constraints on how we may treat a being, not a sum over welfare. Second, what grounds those constraints is not sentience but something closer to the capacity to have a conception of oneself and of one's life — the capacity to be an end in itself rather than merely a locus of experience. Third, that capacity comes in degrees and is not cleanly absent in the rest of the animal kingdom. Fourth, because our evidence about which animals have it, and to what degree, is poor, and because the downside of being wrong in the permissive direction is severe whilst the cost of caution is modest, we should adopt something close to vegetarianism as a precaution.

What I want from you is the map of this position's exposure. Which arguments, intuitions, empirical findings, or formal results would count against it? Sort them from fatal to merely awkward, and be explicit about which of them a committed defender of this view would themselves concede as counting against it, as opposed to which are pressed only by critics. I am particularly interested in whether the graded-capacity claim can survive the marginal-cases problem without collapsing into a species-typical reading of the capacity or into a bare appeal to potentiality, and in whether the precautionary step is doing independent work or is parasitic on the status claim it is supposed to support. Say also whether the precautionary argument over-generalises — to plants, embryos, artefacts, or anything else whose moral status is uncertain and whose treatment is cheap to revise — and, if it does, whether that is a reductio or a bullet the view can bite. Finally, tell me honestly whether the position as stated risks unfalsifiability, and if so, whether that is a defect here or simply what a view about status under uncertainty looks like.

What would move me most is a demonstration that the side-constraint framing is the wrong shape for the animal case altogether — that a sentience-based view explains everything mine explains and the graded-capacity apparatus earns nothing. Second to that would be an argument that the precautionary step, run properly, does not stop at vegetarianism. I do not want reading lists or citations; work from what you can reason out, and flag anything you take to be a genuine empirical unknown rather than a matter of argument.
