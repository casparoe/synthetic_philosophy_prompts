I am at work on a novel whose central character causes, and clearly foresees, a harm she does not seek, and I find that I cannot predict how a reader will judge her without first knowing what a reader is actually tracking when they ascribe intention. The phenomenon is familiar enough in outline — Joshua Knobe's chairman, who is said to have harmed the environment intentionally by most respondents but to have helped it intentionally by only a small minority, is the canonical demonstration — but I do not want the demonstration explained to me. I want the specification that any adequate theory of it would have to meet.

Please write me the desiderata. I am asking for the full requirements list: the cases a theory of this asymmetry must get right, the distinctions it must not collapse (for instance between intending an outcome and bringing it about intentionally, or between the agent's attitude and the outcome's moral status), the empirical results it must accommodate, and the questions it must answer rather than defer — each with the consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference. Then tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are the commitments of a particular camp, and flag any pair you suspect cannot be satisfied together; in particular I want to know whether the demand that the asymmetry be a rational exercise of the ordinary concept can survive the demand that intentionality itself remain morally neutral.

To keep the exercise honest, here are four cases I have been working with, and a theory that cannot place them is of no use to me. First, a character who knows that her forthcoming book will disgrace her dead mother, where the wronged party no longer exists. Second, a foreseen but morally innocent damage — the flooding of a beautiful valley, no one harmed. Third, a foreseen benefit conferred on a character the reader despises. Fourth, an agent who, like the chairman, disavows any concern for the harm, but who would have proceeded even had the harmful program been unprofitable. For each, say what a theory must deliver and whether the case exposes a gap in the standard accounts. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude candidate theories, since that is the one I will be watching most closely.
