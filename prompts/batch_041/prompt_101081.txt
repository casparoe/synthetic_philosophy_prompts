I want a piece of sustained deadpan satire in the form of an internal guidance memo that a fictional national wildlife agency sends its regional officers, the kind they get when they have to decide whether some local population counts as its own species and therefore gets legal protection. The target is the whole practice of writing species down in law as crisp, natural, discoverable kinds when the biology keeps refusing to cooperate — and the way agencies paper over that with thresholds, committees, and a definately-not-scientific "we know it when we see it" clause. The jokes have to come from getting the philosophy of biology right, so that someone who's read about the species problem laughs instead of wincing. Which means the memo should keep sliding between species concepts without ever noticing it's doing that (biological, phylogenetic, morphological, ecological), hold up interbreeding as the test right up until it hits something asexual or a ring species or a hybrid swarm and then quietly switch tests, set a percentage cutoff for genetic distance and then admit in a footnote-ish aside that the cutoff is a policy choice and always was, observe that the same reasoning applied to fossils would erase half the catalogue, and so on. Someone who's never heard of the species problem should still find it funny just as a bureaucratic document.

Never wink. No narrator's aside, no "of course this is absurd," no author breaking character. It's an agency that sincerely believes it's being reasonable and helpful. Around 800 words. Write it as continuous prose, the way you'd write an email or a letter — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no numbered lists, no tables. If it needs to feel like a document with clauses, just refer to them in the running text ("clause 4(b) as amended", "see the note to section 3") rather than actually laying them out. And lead with the bottom line, if a memo can be said to have one — open with the determination or the instruction to officers and let everything else hang off that.
