I'm putting together a reading-group session on the ethics of blame, and two of my regulars have been circling each other for months now. One of them holds that hypocrisy really does undermine a blamer's standing, in the strong sense: a hypocrite's blame is defective as blame, not merely irritating to hear. The other thinks that's muddled: blame is warranted or not purely by what the target did, and everything people call standing is really a mix of whether it's wise or decent to voice the blame, plus something about moral equality, none of which touches the blame itself. I want the joint statement they could both actually sign. Give me the claims they can agree on about blame, its warrant, and its conditions; then the precise point of disagreement, with each side's position phrased the way that side would want it phrased; then the cruxes, the sub-questions where settling something would actually move one of them; and for each crux, a case or argument they'd both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction about how it comes out written down in advance. I suspect one crux is whether the relevant defect is a forfeited right to blame or instead something about the blamer's relation to the norm they're invoking, and maybe another is whether a blamer who has genuinely reformed counts the same as a current hypocrite — but don't just take my word for it. If alot of their fight turns out to be verbal, say which part and why. Format it as a numbered list of agreed claims followed by a disagreements table. American English, please.
