I'm applying for a small internal grant — three months, one researcher, no archive trips, deliverable is a longish working paper — and I need a short research proposal (a page and a half, two max) that turns my half-formed idea into something a reviewer can actually evaluate. Topic is Rousseau on the theater.

Background, quickly, so you know what I'm on about: in his reply to d'Alembert's suggestion that Geneva ought to have a playhouse, Rousseau runs the familiar objections — a theater feeds vanity, the actor sells himself, the audience sympathizes with characters on stage and goes home feeling virtuous without having done anything — and he sets against all this the open-air public festival, where there is no audience because everyone is participating. People read this as either (a) moralism about actors and the corrupting effects of plays, or (b) a suspicion of representation as such, the bit that later readers find proto-totalitarian. My hunch is that both readings are too coarse, and that the real target is neither the building nor the art form but a relation — the position of the spectator toward something that is happening which he isn't part of, a position that lets him feel all the good feelings without paying any of the costs of acting. Call it 'the spectator position'. On this reading the objection is to a structure, not to stages.

Now the twist I want the proposal built around. If the objection is to the spectator position as such, then Rousseau's own dramatic output is condemned by his own argument — the one-act opera he wrote that the court adored, the comedy he published, and above all Pygmalion, where the sculptor brings his statue to life himself with no god's help, which is basically a story about a spectator who refuses to stay on the spectator's side of the relation and forces the representation to become real. Meanwhile the festival alternative has to be shown not to be theater, and its harder than it looks: it has staging, an audience of sorts, aesthetic shaping, something like a script. So either the critique is narrower than it sounds — about money, professional acting, idleness, the specific institutions of the Parisian playhouse, in which case his own works survive and the whole thing is a far more local argument than people think — or it's as broad as it sounds and it swallows his festivals and his own operas along with the playhouse.

What I want from you: state the question so that someone could disagree with me and be right, rather than leaving me a tension to admire. Specifically: (1) the question, stated so that it has a determinate answer; (2) why the two standard readings leave it open — what each of them can't explain, with the festival case and Pygmalion as the pressure points; (3) the method, concretely, i.e. what I'd actually do for three months. My own idea is to take the critique at whatever level of abstraction makes it bite, then run Rousseau's own cases through it and see whether the criterion is doing the work or whether I've quietly had to add a premise to save it — say whether that's the right approach, or propose a better one and say why it's better; (4) what a result would look like, meaning what I'd be able to say at the end that I can't say now, and what would count as the project failing — including the answer that the critique is inconsistent and he didn't notice, which I'd rather find out in month one than month three; and (5) scope, honestly: is three months enough, and if not, which piece gets cut.
