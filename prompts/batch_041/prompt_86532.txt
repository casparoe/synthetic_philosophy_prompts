my reading group just wrapped a session on anthropics — self sampling vs self indication — and the bit that wrecked everyone was the presumptuous philosopher: a fair coin decides whether the world that gets made has a thousand people in it or a thousand billion, and you turn out to be one of whoever's there. the SIA line is that you should be nearly certain you're in the big one, since your existence — that you're here to be asking at all — is a billion times likelier in a world with a billion times as many people to be, and so, the philosopher says, you should be nearly certain how the coin landed and can do cosmology from your armchair. the other side says no: your being here isnt evidence about the size of the world, the coin is a coin. most of the room thought the philosophers conclusion was a reductio of SIA rather than a discovery, but nobody could say exactly where the slide happens.

i got voluntold to close the next session with a poem, so write me one. what i want:

four quatrains, with a refrain line that comes back in every one, but the crowd inside it keeps multiplying — a thousand of us, a thousand thousand of us, and on — so the form itself does the inflating that SIA does to your credence, and by the third stanza the number should be so big that saying it out loud is the joke, because thats what the credence has turned into: something you cant check against anything. then in the last stanza i want the same refrain back with the count dropped to one, and that one is the SSA voice answering. the speaker should start out as the philosopher doing the arithmetic and end up just one of the crowd. dont resolve it — the room didnt.

two things. it has to be an actual poem, not a sing song with a moral stapled on the end. and no strawman: SIA isnt more copies of me so im probably a copy, its a claim about how likely your evidence — the fact that you exist at all — is under each hypothesis, and the poem has to get that right or the last stanzas reply wont land. someone who knows both assumptions should be able to point at the SIA line and the SSA line and see why they keep sliding past each other.
