I live in a two-person house with a roommate, and we wrote a short charter to avoid recurring arguments. I am a backend engineer, so I tend to think in terms of specs and consistency, but I have no philosophy training. The charter has two clauses. The first says that each resident has final say over their own room, including what goes in their own window. The second says that if both residents prefer one outcome to another, the house should adopt the preferred outcome. We thought this was obviously reasonable, and for a while it was. Now we are stuck on whether to put political signs in our windows, and the two clauses seem to pull in opposite directions. There are four relevant outcomes: neither of us puts up a sign, only I put up a sign, only my roommate puts up a sign, or both of us do. My preferences are: neither sign is best; if there must be a sign, I would rather it be mine than my roommate's; and if I cannot avoid a sign entirely, I would rather both of us have one than only my roommate. My roommate's preferences are: both signs is best; if only one of us has a sign, my roommate would rather it be mine than their own, because they do not want to be the only one visibly taking a side; and neither sign is worst.

When I try to apply the charter, I get a loop. By the first clause, I get to decide my own window, and I prefer no sign to my own sign, so the house should rank no sign above my sign. By the same clause, my roommate gets to decide their own window, and they prefer their own sign to no sign, so the house should rank their sign above no sign. By the second clause, since we both prefer my sign to my roommate's sign, the house should rank my sign above my roommate's sign. But that gives my sign above my roommate's sign, my roommate's sign above no sign, and no sign above my sign, which is circular. If we instead try to satisfy the second clause first, we get a different answer, and if we try to satisfy the first clause first, we get another. I suspect we have accidentally written a decision rule that cannot consistently deliver an answer, but I also suspect there is a deeper issue about what counts as a private matter and whether we are allowed to have preferences about each other's private choices. What should we actually do here? Should we amend the charter, and if so, how, without simply letting one of us overrule the other on everything? I would like advice that treats this as a concrete design problem for two people sharing a house, not just a debate about who is right about the signs.
