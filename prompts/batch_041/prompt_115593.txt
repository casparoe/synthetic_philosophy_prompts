I am drafting a short essay on Molinism and middle knowledge, and I would like your comments. The Molinist picture is that God knows not only necessary truths and what God will actualize, but also what any possible free creature would freely choose in any complete set of circumstances. My essay argues that this middle knowledge is better understood as a constraint on creation than as a forecast: the counterfactuals of freedom are like the rules of a game that God can instantiate but not author, so God’s creative act is more like selecting a language than writing a script. I claim this preserves libertarian freedom because the counterfactuals are not causally upstream of the creature’s choice; they are the logical shape of the choice in the world where it occurs. I also claim it answers the grounding objection—the charge that such counterfactuals would need a ground, and that any available ground, whether God’s will or the creature’s essence, would undermine freedom—by treating them as primitive modal facts. Is this a fair statement of the Molinist position, or have I made it too strong? Is the language/script analogy helpful, or does it quietly collapse into compatibilism? Is the grounding objection actually answered, or only renamed? What is the weakest step, and what would a critic say first? If the essay has a more interesting version hiding in it, what is it? Lead with a one-sentence verdict, then give the main problems in a short bulleted list, then say what to keep. Answer in no more than 500 words.
