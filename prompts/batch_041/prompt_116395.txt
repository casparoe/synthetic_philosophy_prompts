Okay so me and a few friends have this running fight and I want to turn it into a thing for our monthly discussion night at the bar. The fight is about whether lying and misleading are actually different in a moral way, or if "misleading" is just lying with extra steps. My one friend says if you say something false on purpose you're a liar, end of story, but if you say something true that happens to make me believe a false thing, well, thats on me for not asking better questions. My other friend says thats garbage, misleading is worse, because a lie you can at least catch and call out, but when someone walks you into a false conclusion without ever saying anything false, you've basically done it to yourself and they get to walk away clean. I honestly dont know where I land and I suspect my answer changes depending on whos doing it.

So here's what I want: a quiz I can hand out at discussion night, like the ones in magazines, a "which view of lying vs. misleading do you actually hold?" thing. Maybe 10 to 12 questions. Each question should be a little concrete case where you pick between two or three answers, and it has to be a case where the different positions genuinely come apart, not just "do you think lying is wrong?" because nobody answers that one honestly anyway. Then a scoring key that maps the pattern of answers onto named positions.

Things I want the quiz to be able to tell apart, because these are the actual fault lines I keep running into:
- someone who thinks the difference is all about intent to deceive, so lying and misleading are morally the same whenever the intent is the same
- someone who thinks lying is worse because it breaks the thing where we can trust each other's words at all, whereas a misleading truth at least leaves the words intact
- someone who thinks misleading is worse, or at least just as bad, because it turns the other person into a co-author of their own being fooled, and because its harder to catch
- someone who thinks neither one is wrong in itself and its all about who gets hurt and how much
- someone who thinks the real question isnt lie vs. mislead but whether you owed that person the truth in the first place (your doctor, your spouse, a salesman, some stranger online) — like the same sentence is fine coming from one and rotten coming from the other
- someone who thinks the whole technically-true thing is a weasel's move and we should stop pretending there's a difference
- and maybe someone who thinks misleading is sometimes actually required — polite excuses, protecting someone's privacy, not dumping the whole truth on your kid — but lying never is. Or the reverse, if there is a reverse.

Make each question something a person could answer without knowing which position it leads to, so people get sorted by what they actually think about the cases and not by which label sounds nicest. Cases I want in there somewhere: the "I never said that" / "you never asked" move; an ad or a label thats literally true but obviously meant to give you the wrong idea; a friend who says they're "not seeing anyone" and means not seriously; a boss or a doctor softening bad news; somebody answering a direct question with a true statement that dodges it; and one where the misleading person would say they were protecting the other person. Add whatever else you think is needed to split the positions apart.

Then tell me which combinations of answers dont line up with any real position — is that because the combo is incoherent, or just because nobody happens to hold it? And flag any two positions the quiz can't really tell apart, instead of pretending it can. Don't worry about quoting anyone or citing philosophers, I just want the thing to work on my friends. Also, if a couple of my positions above are secretly the same position wearing two hats, say so.
