A colleague who works in AI policy tried to explain the intent alignment vs. value alignment distinction to me. She said: intent alignment is the narrow, technical problem of getting a system to do what its operator actually asks it to do, while value alignment is the broad, ethical problem of getting it to promote human values. On the narrow definition, alignment is mostly an engineering problem that instruction-tuning and RLHF have basically solved, since a model that follows instructions is doing what we intend. Value alignment is then the real remaining issue, but it's really a political question about whose values get encoded, not a technical one. She also seemed to treat intent alignment as the same as outer alignment and value alignment as the same as inner alignment. She concluded that people working on the narrow problem and people working on value alignment are barely in the same field. I think she's oversimplfying but I can't articluate where. Which parts would an alignment researcher accept, which are common simplifications, and which are just wrong? Does her conclusion follow from the accurate parts?
