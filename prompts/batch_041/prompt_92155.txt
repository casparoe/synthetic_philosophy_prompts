I've been trying to teach myself how historians actually figure out what happened in the distant past, and I think I've finally got the basic method down, but I want to check it with someone.

The way I understand it, it basically works like this. You gather up the surviving accounts of whatever it is — say the Peloponnesian War — and then you rank them by how close the writer was to the events and how honest he seems, and then you just beleive the top one. Thucydides was an Athenian general, he was alive for a lot of it, and everyone says he basically invented real history, so for that war you go with him and treat him as the baseline. Herodotus is the other way round — he's got giant ants and gold-digging and talking oracles, so he's mostly just colour and you can ignore him unless you're doing the fun bits. And when two decent sources disagree about something, you go with the earlier one, because earlier means closer to the actual event, which means less time for the story to get mangled. Is that roughly right?

What I can't work out is the endgame. Say you've done all this and picked the best source and gone with the earlier one when there's a conflict — how do you know you've actually got the past right, instead of just the least bad of a bad bunch? Like, at what point does a historian get to stop saying 'probably' and start saying 'this is what happened'? Is there a test, or is it just that after enough sources agree you decide you're allowed to be sure? I keep feeling like there must be a rule for when the doubt is over, and I can't find it.

Also, one thing that nags me: if the whole method is ranking sources by trustworthiness, then doesnt the guy who lies for a living beat the guy who tells the truth? I mean, the dishonest source is the one who tells you what he wants you to hear, so if you're ranking by honesty you'd throw him out — but then you're left with the boring sources who never said anything interesting, and you never learn anything. So what do you actually do with the ones who are clearly pushing an agenda?
