I'm putting together a handout for a hospital ethics committee that keeps getting stuck on advance directives, and I want a decision tree rather than a reading list. The thing I actually care about is what people think grounds the authority — not just "do you respect autonomy", but whether the earlier self's say-so is original (she has standing in her own right, even if her later self would repudiate the whole plan) or derivative (it's evidence about what the later self wants, or a tool for protecting her), and whether the authority attaches to a person, to a promise, to a bundle of interests, or to the institution's habits. So build the tree from the logical space — start with a split everybody has to take, then the next — and only afterwards locate the actual positions in the space.

Let these cases do the sorting work: (a) a woman in early dementia signs a directive refusing antibiotics and artificial feeding; five years later she's cheerful, disoriented, and reaching for a spoon; (b) a man writes "no vent, no CPR", then two years later, still sharp, says he's changed his mind and wants everything; (c) someone lucid but depressed writes a refusal, and everyone agrees the depression was doing the driving; (d) a directive that isn't about the body at all — "don't spend a penny on my care, give it to my nephew" — where honouring it means the later person is uncomfortable but the earlier person's plan is respected; (e) a Ulysses-style directive that says outright "I know I'll beg you to ignore this, and I'm telling you now not to." Worth having a branch on whether it matters that a directive asks for less rather than more.

Positions I want as leaves (name them, or say "unnamed" if nobody really holds it): the one where the earlier competent choice wins outright; the one where it's only a defeasible presumption against the later self's current welfare; the one where the later self wins because welfare is all that's left; the two-selves/replacement view; the view that what binds you isn't the self at all but the promise or relationship with whoever is caring for you; and the view that the authority is really the institution's — we honor directives becuase a system that does is better overall. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't separate.

Format: exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up — the root question, then the next two levels of splits (roll anything deeper into the leaves), then the leaves with the commitment each finds hardest to defend, then the combinations nobody occupies and whether that's because they're incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
