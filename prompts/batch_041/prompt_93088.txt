Everyone's seen the Hex version, so what I actually want is the case that shows the trick needs more than "the board is symmetric and there's no drawing". Build me two games that differ in exactly one rule: in the first the stealing argument goes through, in the second it collapses and the second player genuinely wins. Constraints: both playable with pen and paper in a couple of minutes, each describable in three or four sentences, finite, the two players roles genuinely interchangeable, and no infinite play. No cheating by stipulating the outcome, hiding an asymmetry, or making player two's win come from anywhere other than the first player's throwaway opening move being a liability. Then for each game run the argument the way a student would, mark the exact step where it breaks, and prove who actually wins. Finish with which feature of the board is doing the work, what happens to the argument if I add that feature back, and how someone who wanted to save the argument would try to rule my second game out of bounds.
