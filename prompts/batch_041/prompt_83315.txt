Write an essay that defends a verdict on the following question: does the intentional stance survive a stricter accounting of the reality of patterns than the one Dennett gives it?

Work from the following background, which you may take as given; no outside reading is required. Dennett's proposal is that a pattern is real when describing a system in terms of that pattern compresses the data — when it lets you predict or summarize the system's behaviour more cheaply than the available alternatives do — and that this is all the reality a pattern needs. He applies this test across three stances: physical, design, and intentional. On his account the intentional stance earns its keep because treating a system as a rational agent with beliefs and desires, and predicting what it will do by asking what it ought to do, is often more economical than working out the physics of its parts. It is commonly objected that this makes intentional patterns mere instruments; Dennett's reply is that they are real, only not intrinsic to the world.

Now consider the amendment you are to assess. Dennett's compression test charges only the cost of the description that contains the pattern. It does not charge the cost of the stance itself — the interpretive scheme, together with whatever idealizations and auxiliary assumptions it carries — that must already be in place before the pattern can be applied at all. Call that extra cost the stance overhead. Amend the criterion as follows: a pattern is real only if the total description length of the pattern plus its stance overhead beats the total cost of the alternatives. Your essay must decide what becomes of the intentional stance under the amended criterion — whether it is vindicated more strongly than Dennett claims, exposed as an artifact of a scheme that is cheap to state but expensive to apply, or whether the amended criterion is itself incoherent and must be rejected.

Address at least these three matters. First, whether stance overhead can be measured at all, or whether specifying a stance presupposes a description language whose own selection is stance-relative, generating a regress that trivializes the amendment. Second, whether the accounting favours one stance at the expense of another, and whether that outcome is a problem for Dennett or precisely what his view should predict. Third, whether the claim that such patterns are real but not intrinsic is a stable position or a way of having the matter both ways.

Reach a conclusion and justify it, and state what would count as evidence against your verdict. No bibliography is needed; argue from the material above and from first principles.
