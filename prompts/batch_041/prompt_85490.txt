My parents 40th anniversary party is in three weeks and since I'm the youngest of the three of us my sister has announced the speech is mine. Fine. Except I've spent most of this year stuck on something that makes the speech a lot harder than it should be.

The story everyone tells about how they got together is that dad's bus got cancelled and he ended up sheltering in the doorway of the shop where mum worked. Every version of that speech, including the one my uncle gave at their 25th, lands in the same place: if that bus had turned up, none of us would be here. I've said that line myself at other peoples parties. I'm not sure it's true, and I really don't want to stand up in front of my whole family, my nan included, and say something I think is probably false just because it's what you say.

Because look. Say the bus turns up and they meet a month later somewhere else. Same two people, same everything else. Do they still have me? My gut says no. They'd have a kid with my nose and maybe my name but it wouldn't be me. Which is a strange thing to be so sure about. And it gets smaller and worse the more I pull on it. Even on the actual night there were millions of the little swimmers and only one got there. If a different one had got there first, different kid? Everyone I ask says yes, different kid. So then the bus being late has almost nothing to do with whether I exist, since even with the bus late it could of been a different kid anyway. But if the exact egg and sperm is what makes me me, then that egg depended on what mum ate that week and how she slept and a hundred other things, and now I've let in a whole pile of stuff that sounds ridiculous just to keep the bus story out. And my cousin told me that a fertilised egg can split and become identical twins, and if that had happened there'd be two people where I am, and neither of them would be the egg itself, so it looks like I'm not the egg either. I don't know what that leaves.

So what I need is a speech, about five minutes, that my nan and my aunties can follow and actually enjoy, warm and a bit funny, and that doesn't say anything I'd have to take back about whether any of us could have existed if things had gone differently. Not a lecture. Don't name any philosophers or books, my mums cousin teaches philosophy and would love it, but that's the problem, and everyone else would just be staring at the cake. If the honest version is that some of us wouldn't be here and a different kid would be sitting in our chairs, I want the speech to be able to say that and still be loving. And if there's some smaller version of meant-to-be that survives all this, I'd like to hear it.

Write the speech. Then underneath, in a few lines, tell me which sentences are the risky ones, the ones where somebody who knows this stuff would say I've overclaimed, and what my options are at each of those spots.
