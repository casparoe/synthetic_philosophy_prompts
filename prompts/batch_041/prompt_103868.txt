My block's tenants' union is voting Thursday on whether to merge with the tenants' group in the building next door. The thing we can't get past is who's allowed to vote on the merger itself. The old guard says only current members, since it's our union and our rules. The newcomers, who've been coming to meetings for months and will be bound by whatever we decide, say thats circular — the whole question is whether they count, so you can't settle it by saying they don't count. Someone suggested we just let anyone affected by the decision vote, and then someone else said fine, so the landlord gets a vote, and the shops on the street, and honestly anyone downstream of a rent strike. Now we're all just staring at each other. Is there a way out of this that isn't just whoever happens to have the votes deciding who gets to have the votes? And if there isn't, what should we actually do on Thursday? I suspect this has a name and a literature behind it — tell me what it is and how the people who've thought about it handle it, but what I really want to know is what to do.
