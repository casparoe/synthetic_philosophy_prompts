Sensitivity and the price of the asymmetry

Nozick's tracking theory says, roughly: S knows that p iff p is true, S believes p, if p were false S wouldn't believe it, and if p were true S would believe it. The third condition — sensitivity — does the anti-sceptical work. I know I have hands, because in the nearest worlds where I don't, I don't believe that I do. I don't know that I'm not a brain in a vat, because in the nearest world where I am one I'd believe exactly what I believe now. Nozick accepts the result — I know I have hands, and I don't know that I'm not a handless envatted brain, the "abominable conjunction" — and to keep it stable he gives up closure: knowledge doesn't transmit across known entailment.

My complaint is that the asymmetry between the hands case and the vat case is bought with a circularity in the way the theory individuates the belief-forming method. Read the method widely — "visual perception as such" — and the hands belief comes out insensitive too, because in the nearest world where I'm handless and envatted the same method runs and produces the same belief. Read it narrowly — "looking at my hands in good light" — and the hands belief is sensitive, but only because the method has been specified in a way that presupposes the truth of "I have hands". So either the asymmetry is lost, or it's presuppositional. And this isn't just a scepticism problem. Take the understudy: I believe the man on stage is Olivier, and there's an understudy who would have gone on had Olivier been ill, indistinguishable from him. My belief is not knowledge. It comes out sensitive under the narrow description and insensitive under the wide one, and here it's obvious that the narrow description is cheating. So we need a principled account of individuation, which Nozick never supplies. Let's impose one: the method must be specifiable without presupposing whether p is true — a topic-neutrality constraint. Impose it, and the asymmetry collapses.

The repair I want to propose is to seperate two statuses. Tracking is the sensitivity-plus-adherence business. Warrant is whatever supports the belief, and warrant is closed under known entailment: transmission runs from the ordinary belief to the anti-sceptical belief, never the reverse. The sceptical argument needs reverse transmission, so it fails. And the abominable conjunction stops being abominable, because it conjoins two different statuses: I track hands, I have warrant for not-being-a-vat, but I don't track not-being-a-vat. Knowledge-as-tracking isn't closed; warrant is.

The honest caveat: I never track not-being-a-vat, so the sceptic can still say I don't know it, which is just what Nozick said. What we gain is that closure survives for warrant, and that the asymmetry is explained by the tracking/warrant distinction rather than by a method individuation that begs the question. What we lose is any pretence that tracking by itself defeats the sceptic.

Some questions, roughly in order of how much I care:

1. Exegesis. Have I got the four conditions and the denial of closure right? And what does Nozick actually do about inductive knowledge — does he really bite the bullet that you can't know the sun will rise tomorrow, or does he have a patch for it? I've never been sure and I don't want to misstate him.

2. Is the second half's argument original at all, or have I just reinvented the generality problem for sensitivity? If it is the generality problem, is the standard answer — individuate the method normally, or narrowly, or by the agent's own conception of it — any good, or does it beg the question the way I say Nozick's does?

3. Does the two-status fix hold up, or does it quietly collapse into contextualism or the relevant-alternatives view? I'd rather not end up defending a view I didn't mean to defend.

4. Where's the weakest link? And what would a committed tracking theorist concede to me versus dismiss out of hand?

5. Is there anything in the draft that commits me to something I'd hate? In particular, does splitting warrant off from knowledge license me to assert abominable conjunctions in ordinary conversation, or can I keep assertion tied to something stronger?
