Bit of an odd request, but here's what I'm after. I'm putting together a reader on the Vienna Circle's less famous projects, and for the chapter on Neurath's picture-language stuff I want a dialogue rather than an essay — the museum charts, Isotype, the "Words divide, pictures unite" line he had painted up on his office wall, the rule that you show ten thousand unemployed people by drawing ten thousand little figures rather than one big figure, the fixed pictogram = fixed number convention, the whole apparatus.

Two speakers. First one: Neurath himself, Vienna, early 30s, in the middle of the museum of society and economy, still convinced that a chart can teach a whole city how its own world is put together — including people who don't share a first language — and with no patience at all for the idea that a notation has to be perfectly unambiguous before it's worth anything. Second one: a younger colleague, a logic person, sharp rather than hostile, who thinks the pictorial project rests on a confusion, because pictures can't do the things that make a language a language: they can't say 'not', they can't be quoted, they can't sit inside a 'that'-clause, and an uncaptioned picture of a man running can be read as asserting, asking or wishing just about anything. And he's got a second, nastier line of attack: a chart looks like a piece of the world rather than a set of decisions, so the contested parts — who counts as unemployed, whether each little figure is a person or a household, which comparison the thing is even making — come across as if they'd simply been seen. Which makes pictures better than words for putting one over on people. If he wants a cheap shot he can add that everyone in the circle keeps saying sentences are pictures of facts, so what's the objection to just using the pictures.

Give me 900 words or so of back and forth. Both of them have to be good — I don't want the logic guy winning by default and I don't want Neurath winning by being avuncular. Have them fight over an actual chart, a plausible one from the period, and let them disagree about what it says. Each should land at least one hit the other has to absorb rather than dodge. Neurath should concede something real (he never claimed the charts were a full language, he knows perfectly well the units per pictogram are a convention someone has to be taught) without giving up what he actually cares about, and the logic guy should concede something too, ideally something he'd rather not. Finish with them disagreeing about what would even settle the question, and please don't give me a tidy synthesis at the end, I want the crux still sitting there.
