I have been reading about and arguing over the Nordic model for some time, and before I commit my own view to paper I would like a consistency check on it. Here is what I believe. (1) The Nordic countries show that high taxes and a generous welfare state can coexist with a dynamic economy, so the supposed trade-off between equality and growth is not a law. (2) What actually holds the arrangement together is high social trust: people pay their taxes and follow the rules because they trust one another and the state, not because enforcement is exceptionally good. (3) Because that trust is the precondition, the model cannot be exported; a low-trust country that copies the tax rates and the benefits will incur the costs without the benefits, since you cannot legislate trust into existence. (4) All the same, I believe institutions are what create trust in the first place: universal, competently run programs that treat everyone alike tend to make people more trusting over time, so a low-trust country could grow into the model if it were patient enough. (5) I also believe the reason the Nordic welfare state survives politically is that its benefits are universal rather than aimed at the poor; a program that helped only the poor would lose the middle class, and with it the funding. I can sense that some of these pull against one another, but I cannot tell which, or how badly. Please tell me which of them are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the real tensions by severity, and say which single one is doing the most damage. If something has to go, what is the cheapest thing to give up, and what would giving it up cost me?
