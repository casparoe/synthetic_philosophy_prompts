ok so for my rational choice seminar I have to write up an adversarial collaboration, and I've picked money pumps and diachronic rationality. I've basically got two people in my head who've been talking past eachother all term, and I want the document they should have written together.

Side A: thinks the money pump is more or less the whole argument. If a sequence of choices you'd freely make at each step leaves you strictly worse off than you started, and you could have seen it coming, then something about you is defective, and the usual diagnosis is your preferences (or your decision procedure) are irrational. Cycles are the paradigm case.

Side B: thinks there's no such thing as a requirement of diachronic rationality. Each choice at each time can be impeccable; there's no further demand that your choices hang together across time. Getting pumped is a fact about your environment (somebody's willing to trade with you) or about your inability to commit, not a failure of reason. On this view "you'll lose money" is a prediction, not a criticism.

The case I want it built around, because it isn't the textbook one: my friend has a perfectly transitive ranking at every instant, but it cycles over the summer. In June she ranks the internship above the beach trip above the research stint; in July it's beach > research > internship; in August research > internship > beach; then June comes round again. No instant of her is inconsistent. Assume a broker who knows the pattern and shows up at each flip with a trade timed right. And in May she can either pay a small fee for a binding no-trade contract, or — this is the bit I can't get my head round — sell the broker the right to trade with her future selves for a lump sum bigger than what she'd lose in total. So she can monetize her own pumpability.
