I'm in a reading group on population ethics that's turned into a low-grade war, and I have to write up where I actually stand before Thursday so the others can tear it apart. So here's my position, roughly in order of how sure I am of it, and what I'd like from you is an honest audit of whether the thing hangs together.

(1) Welfare is the only thing that's good or bad in itself — if two outcomes are alike except that people are better off in one, that one is better. (2) Averages are irrelevant to me; I've never understood why anyone would care what the average welfare in a population is. It's the total that matters. And I dont think an unequal distribution is worse than an equal one with the same total. (3) I don't accept the repugnant conclusion. Let A be a world of ten billion people with rich, flourishing lives, and Z a world of 10^20 people whose lives are just barely worth living. A is better than Z. I'm not going to be argued out of this one. (4) Here's the fix, and I think it's what saves me: a life only counts in the sum if it's worth living at all. A net negative life counts against; a net positive life counts for, even if it's only barely on the positive side of the line. (5) Adding a person whose life is worth living to a world, leaving everyone else's life completely untouched, never makes the outcome worse. That one seems about as certain as anything in this area. (6) I don't think there's anything good about bringing a happy person into existence — no one's being done a favour, there was no one there to benefit. It is bad, though, to bring into existence someone whose life will go badly. (7) I'm not a pronatalist: if my brother and his wife decide against having kids, they aren't doing anything wrong, even if the child they would have had would've had a wonderful life.

Two things people keep throwing at me. One said my fix in (4) is doing way more work than I think it's doing, which I didn't understand at all. Another insists the whole A-versus-Z thing is confused, because the people in Z would be different people, so you can't say anyone is worse off — that also struck me as a dodge. So: go through the list and tell me which items are actually pulling against each other, how serious each clash is, and which pairs only look like they clash but don't. For the real ones, what's the cheapest way out and what exactly do I lose by taking it. Rank them by severity rather than treating every tension as equally bad, and then tell me whether there's a single change that would clean up the most — even if it's the one I'd least like to make.
