Im giving a short talk on the Frege-Geach problem and I want to open with a joke, but an actual joke about the problem, not just 'haha expressivists cant do logic'. So heres the setup the joke has to use. The expressivist says that when I say 'kicking puppies is wrong' I'm not describing anything, I'm expressing my disapproval. Fine. But now put 'wrong' somewhere nobody is disapproving anything -- a conditional, a question ('is kicking puppies wrong?'), a report of someone elses doubt ('I doubt kicking puppies is wrong'), 'probably kicking puppies is wrong' -- and the word still has to mean the same thing, arguments containing it still have to be valid, and none of that can depend on whoever says it actually disapproving of anything. Write me a joke that turns on that tension. Prefer one of the less worn embeddings (the question, or the doubt report) over the plain if-then, since that one has been done to death, and dont let the punchline just be 'so expressivism is dumb' -- it should turn on the mechanics, i.e. that the same word has to do its job in an unasserted spot where the attitude isnt there to be expressed. If the punchline can also make the standard expressivist save (the embedded 'wrong' is still an attitude word, just one nobody is currently having) look like a dodge, even better.
