I have been circling a pattern for some time and cannot tell whether it already has a name or whether I need to invent one.

The pattern is a kind of value that attaches to a thing because of what it is a trace of, and it moves in two directions I cannot reconcile. It rises when the thing traced disappears: a photograph of a building since demolished, a plaster cast of a sculpture since destroyed, a letter in the hand of someone now dead. Nothing about these objects changed, and the value was never located in their perceptible qualities to begin with, yet as the thing they point back to becomes unavailable the value goes up. But it collapses when the relation itself is shown not to hold: a painting loses most of its worth the moment it is exposed as a forgery rather than the work of the hand it was credited to, and here nothing in the world changed at all, only what is known about the object's history.

Both changes look external to the object, and both look like the same value being moved, which is what I cannot sort out. Is there an established concept for value of this kind, and how does it divide from the neighbours I know of — aura, authenticity, relic value, ordinary scarcity? Is the first phenomenon the same as the second, or are they two different things sharing a surface? And where do the intermediate cases fall — a cast taken from the original mould, a freehand copy, a forgery of a work that never existed, a photograph of a photograph? If no concept exists I would happily take a coined term, but I care more about where the boundaries fall than about the label.
