I need a case built to order for a seminar on the Humean best-system account of lawhood and the anti-Humean view that laws are primitive relations of necessitation holding between universals. The case is for an argument that in some worlds the two accounts fail in different directions: the Humean account cannot say whether a particular generalisation is a law, while the anti-Humean account delivers a verdict that nothing in the world earns. Everything else in the case must be held fixed, so that a single feature — the exchange rate between the simplicity of a system's general axioms and the simplicity of its particular-fact base — is the only thing doing the work. I don't want citations or a survey of who held what; refer to the accounts by their structure.

Build the world as follows, and state each item explicitly rather than gesturing at it.

The world. It must be finite and deterministic, described precisely enough that a competent programmer could simulate it and check every claim you make: state space, dynamical rule, initial condition. No continuum, no quantum mechanics, no time travel, no infinite past or future, and an ontology small enough that the whole specification fits in a paragraph or two.

The generalisation. Identify a generalisation G that is true of the world, exceptionless, and stated in terms of natural, non-gerrymandered properties. No disjunctive or negated predicates, no predicates defined by their extension, no 'grue'-style constructions. G must be instantiated many times over: the case may not rest on uninstantiated laws, unobserved regions, or single instances, which are the standing battlefields, and I want a new one.

The two systems. State two candidate best systems, S1 and S2, both true of the world, so that fit cannot discriminate between them. Both must carry the whole truth about the world; they differ in which generalisations do the carrying. In S1, G is a general axiom, or follows from the general axioms alone; in S2, G does not follow from the general axioms and holds only as an artefact of the particular facts. S1 and S2 must tie on total simplicity: S1 pays for its general axioms with a shorter particular-fact base, S2 the reverse. Say explicitly how you are reading strength, keep that reading fixed throughout, and show that the tie survives the most natural sharpening of the criteria you can construct; 'simplicity is vague' is not an answer. The aim is that a Humean forced to choose between S1 and S2 has no principled ground for choosing.

The verdicts. Give each account's verdict on G, with the reasoning that produces it rather than an assertion of it. Arrange things so that someone who knew the world completely would call G a coincidence, that the Humean account can reach that verdict only by selecting one of the tied systems, and that the necessitarian account's machinery pushes towards counting G as a law. If a defensible version of either account escapes the bind, say so and show what it costs that account elsewhere. Where an account's verdict rests on a posit rather than on anything in the world's description, name the posit and the price.

Then the demonstration: go through the specifications one by one and show how the case meets each. Finish with the honest note: which feature is doing the work; what becomes of the case when that feature is removed, and whether it then collapses into one of the standing battlefields; how a Humean would attack the case (a principled sharpening of the simplicity metric that breaks the tie, or an embrace of the indeterminacy) and how an anti-Humean would attack it (that the second-order facts are part of the world rather than imported, so the charge of unearned determinacy begs the question); and what would have to be true for each reply to succeed. The verdicts must be generated by the accounts' own machinery: if the lawhood of G has to be written into the world's description to get the result, the case fails.

Begin with the case itself — the world, then the two systems — with no preface and no restatement of this brief; the demonstration and the honest note follow.
