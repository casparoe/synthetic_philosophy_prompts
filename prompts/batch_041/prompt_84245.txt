Im putting together the last meeting of my grad seminar on Frege's puzzle, and I want to close with something that forces the students to reason from Frege's commitments instead of reciting the textbook story. So here's the game: Frege somehow lives long enough to read the post-war literature on identity and reference, and I want your best reconstruction of what he'd actually say about (1) the necessary a posteriori and the necessity of identity, Hesperus/Phosphorus as Kripke runs it; (2) contingent identity, the statue and the lump of clay sort of case; (3) the contingent a priori, Kripke's metre-stick case, which was pretty clearly meant to embarrass anyone holding a Fregean notion of the a priori; (4) hidden-indexical and contextualist treatments of belief reports, plus the Pierre/Londres puzzle; and (5) pretence-based accounts of empty names and fiction. Argue his verdicts from his own commitments -- sense and reference, the objectivity and mind-independence of thoughts, the context principle, his loathing of psychologism, whatever you think is really load-bearing -- and please don't just tell me he'd say the sensible Fregean thing. Tell me where he'd surprise me, where he'd say the question is badly put, and where you're extrapolating rather than arguing. Dont worry about citing anything, I know the literature, and no bibliography.

Format it like this:
- What's load-bearing: roughly 150 words on the Fregean commitments you're reasoning from, and why those and not others.
- Verdicts: about 700 words, one short subhead per item above, verdict first and then the argument for it.
- Where he'd surprise me: about 250 words on the verdicts that cut against the received picture of Frege.
- Badly put: about 250 words on which of these questions he'd refuse as posed, and what he'd replace them with.
- How much of this is a guess: about 150 words, item by item, ranking your confidence.
