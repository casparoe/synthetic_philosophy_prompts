I spent my working life on things that could be measured, much of it on heat, and I never quite got over how much scaffolding had to be in place before anyone could honestly say that one body was twice as hot as another. Ordering came easily; even an interval scale came easily; what made the ratios mean anything was a body of theory and a physical zero underneath, not the numbers themselves. Now I am retired and reading moral and political philosophy, and I keep meeting people who compare the welfare of different persons as though it were the least controversial thing in the world, while others insist the whole enterprise is a category mistake. Before I take a side I want the specification: what would a theory of interpersonal utility comparison have to deliver in order to count as adequate?

Write me that list of desiderata. For each, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference of some camp. I expect the list to cover at least the following, though I want you to reorganize or extend it as the logic demands:

- what the theory must reproduce within a single person before it reaches across persons — the representation of that person's preferences, including under risk, and what it does when those preferences are incomplete or cyclical;
- which comparisons it undertakes to license — bare ordering, differences, or ratios — and by what device it fixes the level, given that a utility function is standardly determined only up to a positive affine transformation of each person's own scale;
- the invariance the theory claims: which rescaling of individual scales leaves its cross-person verdicts untouched, and how it avoids the trap of a comparison that is merely a restatement of the normalization it stipulated;
- the stock cases it must get right — a being who converts any quantity of resources into colossal satisfaction, a person whose wants have shrunk to fit a deprived life, two people with the same ranking of outcomes but opposite attitudes to risk, and a person who wants very little set against one who wants a great deal;
- the aggregation the comparison is meant to feed, and whether the comparison is defined narrowly enough to support that aggregation or only loosely enough to gesture at it.

Then do three things with the list. First, mark which requirements are genuinely part of adequacy and which are the prejudices of a particular camp — I suspect the demand that the comparison be independently measurable, and the demand that it be invariant, are each doing work that deserves scrutiny. Second, tell me whether any two of the requirements cannot be satisfied together, and which pair; if the list is jointly unsatisfiable in that way, that is the result I most want, so say so plainly rather than papering over it. Third, name the single requirement that does the most to eliminate candidate theories, and say briefly how the leading contenders — the summing tradition, the primary-goods and capability approaches, and the attempt to ground comparison in what a person would choose were he imaginatively placed in another's position — fare against it. Keep the emphasis on the requirements and the cases behind them; I will work out the scorecard myself.
