At a reading group dinner, two other members — Nadia and Owen — spent most of the evening arguing over a thought experiment one of them had invented, and I never settled for myself who had the better of it. The experiment runs as follows. A machine offers to take over all your judgments: it will decide which arguments you accept, which music you love, which people you trust, and it will always get them right, and the results will feel like conclusions you reached yourself. Most people refuse, and feel something has been lost. Now suppose instead that nothing about you changes, but the world is arranged so that no judgment you form on your own ever turns out to be mistaken: you keep judging for yourself, and you are never wrong. Most people see little or nothing lost.

Nadia: the cases differ only in where the filtering happens, inside your head or in the world. The verdicts flip while the judgments you end up with are identical — correct in both, and endorsed by you in both — so the sense of loss in the first case must be manufactured by the word "machine" and the picture of something else deciding. A device that can do that isn't evidence about anything; it is a description quietly doing the work of an argument.

Owen: the cases are not parallel. In the first you lose the activity of judging — the weighing of a book that ends in finding it good; in the second you keep that activity and merely get better inputs. A pump is only probative if its cases are alike in everything relevant, and these are not.

Nadia: if "they are not parallel" is always available whenever a verdict flips, the test can never fail; so it is either empty or it needs a stated criterion of relevance, which Owen has not given.

Owen: the criterion is that you must be able to say, before you know the verdicts, which single feature the pump varies and why that feature should matter. Nadia's pump varies at least two things at once — who controls the judgment and whether error is possible — so it is a bad pump, not proof that pumps are bad. He added, though, that one bad pump does not establish her larger conclusion unless someone can produce the same flip from a case that varies only one thing.

Please adjudicate: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and say why, then say who has the better case overall, what each of them is missing, and what the experiment actually shows as opposed to what Nadia wants it to show.
