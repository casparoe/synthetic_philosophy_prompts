Ok so I need you to settle something. I got into a huge fight with my friend Maya last weekend — like three hours, we missed the movie — about whether the world is getting less violent. She's read all the Pinker-adjacent stuff and is very smug about it. I'm not even sure I disagree, but by the end I realized I couldn't state my own view, because every time I tried to say what counts as "violence," my gut pulled in two directions. Here's what I actually said, case by case, and I want to know what principle (if any) I was implicitly using.

- Soldier killed by artillery in a battle: violence, obviously. Counts.
- Kid in a besieged city who starves because food is blocked at the checkpoint: also violence. Feels like the same event as the artillery, just slower.
- Kid who dies of cholera in a refugee camp because the water system fell apart after the war: I said no, not violence... but then I couldn't defend it, because the war put them there. I flip-flopped on this one three times and Maya noticed.
- Overdose death in a town where the plant closed: not violence. I'm confident about this one, wierdly.
- Person shot by police at a traffic stop: violence, yes. But it felt wrong to put it in the same column as the war cases and I don't know why.
- The gas leak at the Union Carbide plant in Bhopal: violence. Something about a corporation doing it.
- The cholera the UN peacekeepers brought to Haiti after the earthquake: not violence?? Even though people died from an institution's negligence in both cases. This is the pair that broke my brain.
- Smallpox wiping out a village in the 1500s: not violence, its a disease. But the people worked to death in the mines that fed the same colonial economy: violence.
- Someone who dies because the road has no guardrail in a country that could easily afford one: not violence. But that has the exact same structure as the cholera case — a death a well-resourced institution could have prevented and didn't.

So my friend's line is basically "violence = intentional harm by an agent," which gets the soldier, the police shooting, Bhopal, and the mines, but throws out the besieged kid and the refugee camp, and I really don't want to throw those out. But the second I widen it to catch those, I can't find a non-arbitrary way to keep out the overdose, the guardrail, and Haiti.

Also, separately, I noticed we were having two arguments at once. By raw numbers more people died violently in the last century than in any century on record, but per capita it's supposedly the safest ever. Maya thinks that settles it. I think it shows we were answering different questions and calling them the same one. And "per capita" of who, exactly? Most of the people who ever lived weren't counted in anybody's census, so the denominator is a guess dressed up as a fact.

What I want: is there a principle that gets most of my verdicts, and which of my verdicts does it force me to give up? And then the harder one — once we've picked a principle, is there still a fact of the matter about whether violence is declining, or does "declining" just mean "declining under whichever definition we stipulated"? I suspect the second and I want you to tell me if that's a cop-out.
