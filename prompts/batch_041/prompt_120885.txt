Ok so I'm in a philosophy of physics class and we've spent like three weeks on the hole argument and at this point I honestly can't tell what we're even arguing about anymore. Setup as I understand it: take a model of some spacetime theory, then apply a diffeomorphism that's the identity everywhere outside some small region and not the identity inside it. You get a second model. Outside the region the two models are exactly alike; if the region sits to the future of some initial slice, you've got the same past and different futures, which looks like a failure of determinism. The substantivalist people in the room say the points of the manifold are real, so the two models are two genuinely different ways the world could have been, and that's a real problem for the theory. The relationalists say the points aren't real over and above the fields living on them, so the two models are just two descriptions of the same thing. Then the "sophisticated substantivalist" line says the points are real but the two models still represent one possibility, because a possibility is fixed by the pattern of properties and not by which point carries which property.

What kills me is that after decades of this nobody in the literature has moved and nobody in my seminar has moved. Every fix seems to cost something: indeterminism, or primitive identities for points, or a notion of possibility that's basically stipulated to make the problem go away. Our TA says the real fight isn't about spacetime at all, it's about modality -- what counts as a way the world could have been -- and that the physics is just scenery. I half buy that, but I also suspect the analogy might smuggle in the identity of indiscernibles, since part of the whole fight is wether spacetime points are discernable at all.
