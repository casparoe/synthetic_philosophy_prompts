My seminar has two people who've been arguing all term and they've finally agreed to write something together, and I want you to draft it. One of them is an accuracy-firster: credences are what epistemic norms govern, they're governed by closeness to the truth, and so probabilism, conditionalization, all of it has to be earned by accuracy arguments -- dominance, expected accuracy, that sort of thing. The other thinks the fundamental norm is that your credences answer to your evidence, that the accuracy arguments only get you coherence, and that whenever you take an expectation of accuracy you've already had to pick a probability function to take it with respect to, which is either the agent's own credences, and so circular, or something you haven't earned. Write the joint statement they could both sign: agreed claims first, numbered, about the formalism and about what the scoring rule does and doesn't settle; then the disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; then the cruxes -- I'd bet one is whether avoiding accuracy-domination is enough for a credence to count as rational, and another is whether the extra principles you need to get from coherence to evidential norms (chance-credence, indifference) are themselves accuracy-based or smuggled in -- and for each crux, a case or argument both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down. Fold in one more thing: a third colleague keeps saying the real epistemic good is calibration, i.e. long-run agreement between credence and frequency, and that the accuracy crowd are chasing a red herring. Make both of them say whether that's a rival view or just accuracy renamed. Finish with a disagreements table. Dont worry about citations, just argue it out properly.
