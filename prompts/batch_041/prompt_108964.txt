A thought experiment I've run into more than once in Hofstadter's writing goes like this. There is a vast table — the careenium — covered with countless tiny magnetic balls, which he calls symms. They careen around, stick together, form clumps, break apart. The walls of the table are sensitive to conditions outside, so the clumping pattern carries information about the world beyond the table. Step back and you see stable, repeating clumps — call them simmballs — that seem to interact with one another: one approaches another, they merge, a third splits off. Hofstadter's question is which level is doing the shoving.

Two friends of mine, both philosophy graduate students, argued about this at a party, and I have been turning it over ever since.

Nadia said the question answers itself once you are careful. All the shoving is done by symms. A simmball is just a pattern that some symms happen to be in. When one simmball appears to push another, what is actually happening is that the symms at the boundary of the first exert magnetic forces on the symms at the boundary of the second, and those forces propagate. There is no additional force at the simmball level. So the simmball story is a convenient redescription, useful for us, but it does not pick out any causal work that the symms are not already doing. It is like saying a hurricane destroyed a town: strictly speaking, the molecules did it.

Jonah said that cannot be right, because the symm-level description, even if complete, does not explain the thing we most want explained. The simmballs are robust: nudge a few symms and the simmball persists, reconstitutes itself, and goes on interacting the same way. The symm-level description would have to list millions of individual trajectories to say what the simmball-level description says in one clause, and it would not capture the fact that the simmball is tracking something outside. The counterfactuals at the simmball level are the ones that support explanation and prediction, and on a counterfactual theory of causation that is just what it is to be a cause. He added that if only symms cause, then the careenium's sensitivity to the world is a coincidence: the simmballs correlate with the world, but nothing at the simmball level is doing the correlating.

Nadia said the coincidence point is a mistake. The correlating is done by the symms — that is what the wall sensitivity amounts to. The fact that we can describe the resulting patterns in a compressed vocabulary does not add a causal agent. Then she pressed the exclusion worry: if the symm-level facts are sufficient for everything that happens, the simmball-level facts are screened off. Fix all the symms and you have fixed all the simmballs. So the simmball level cannot be adding anything to the outcome; at most it is a level at which we usefully describe the outcome.

Jonah said screening off only looks threatening if you think there is one privileged level where the real causation happens. The molecular facts are sufficient for the deaths in the hurricane case too, and nobody concludes that hurricanes do not kill people. What we mean by a cause is a difference-maker at a level of description that supports counterfactuals and generalizes; the hurricane satisfies that, and so does the simmball. Nadia's view, he said, makes causation a matter of vocabulary size, which cannot be right.

Nadia said she is happy to say hurricanes kill people, because that is shorthand for the molecular story and shorthand can be true. But Jonah wants more than shorthand: he wants the simmball to be an additional difference-maker, and that is what the sufficiency argument rules out. If the simmball were different while all the symms stayed the same, nothing would change, so the simmball makes no difference. That simmball-talk is indispensable for us is a fact about us.

Jonah said the antecedent in that conditional is impossible, and impossible antecedents show nothing. The right question is what happens when you vary the simmball-level facts in the ways the simmball-level theory allows — and when you do, the symms follow. He also said Nadia keeps ignoring a further wrinkle: simmballs are not just clumps, they carry content. The whole point of the careenium is that some clumps stand for things outside it. If one simmball represents a predator and another represents flight, then the fact that the first caused the second is a fact about the content, not just about magnetism, and that fact cannot be stated in symm vocabulary at all.

Nadia said content is a relation between a clump and the world, and relations do not push anything. A contentful simmball pushes exactly as hard as an otherwise identical clump with no content. This is the sharpest version of her point: causal power is local and physical, and content is neither. She conceded that this leaves her unable to say why some patterns strike us as the right ones to call symbols — but she said that is a fact about our interests, not about the careenium.

Neither would move, and each of them had an answer to the other's best point that sounded right at the time. I would like you to adjudicate. Specifically: (1) Is Nadia's sufficiency-and-exclusion argument sound as she states it, and does it really rule out simmball causation? (2) Is Jonah's counterfactual-and-robustness argument enough to establish that simmballs are causes, or does it only establish that simmball-talk is useful? (3) Who has the better handle on the content point — is content an idle extra, or does it change the causal story? (4) If you know how Hofstadter himself answers the shoving question, say where his answer lands between them and whether it is stable or collapses into one of their positions; if you would only be guessing, say so rather than guess. Tell me which of them is right about which specific claim, and what both are missing. Also tell me where your own reasoning is most likely to fail, and what argument or consideration would lead you to a different verdict. Please write in American English.
