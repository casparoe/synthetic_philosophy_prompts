My mom is retiring after 22 years as mayor of a small mill town and I've been drafted to write her farewell adress. Here's the thing everybody in the room knows and nobody says out loud: about ten years back she signed off on a doctored environmental report so the last factory in town could stay open. It saved around 900 jobs, and the creek below town has been getting sicker ever since. She keeps saying she has dirty hands, which I guess is some philosophy thing, and honestly I don't know what she wants me to do with it. The room's gonna be half union families who think she walks on water and half the creek-cleanup people who think she belongs in jail. Write her speech, six or seven minutes. I need it to actually come down on whether what she did was wrong even though she'd do it again in a heartbeat, and it can't let either half of the room feel bought off. Don't let her off the hook and don't have her grovel either.
