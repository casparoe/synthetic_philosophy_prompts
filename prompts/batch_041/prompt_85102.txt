My fifth book is nine or ten goes at the same ninety seconds — a car door opening into a cyclist — and I've got to the point where I cant tell some of the versions apart, or tell a real version from a coat of paint. I'm a novelist, not a theorist, but what I've been doing is basically Hofstadter's knob-twiddling thing: you take something that already exists, twiddle one of its knobs, and see whether it's still itself. If it survives the twiddle, that feature wasn't essential; if it falls apart, you've found something load-bearing. That's my cheap version of it, so correct me if it's cheap.

What I want is a procedure I can actually run at the desk on a Tuesday morning, because I do this five mornings a week and I keep burning them. The questions to ask in order, what counts as a yes or a no at each one, where the procedure says stop and dont write this version, and where it has to hand the decision back to me because it's stopped being a question of fact.

Some local details you'll need. My knobs so far are: who narrates; how much they know and when they know it; what they want out of the telling; the order the facts arrive in; what the reader is allowed to see; the weather and the hour; the social occasion. I've also got a rule I half believe, which is that a version is only worth writing if it changes what somebody in the scene could be blamed for. I don't know whether that's a knob or a standard, or whether it quietly replaces the whole twiddling business. And my editor insists every version read standalone, which I suspect fights the idea, since a variation only means anything next to the others.

Run it on two of them: the one told by the insurance adjuster who turns up afterwards, and the one that runs the ninety seconds backwards. Then run it on my favourite, which is the backwards one — I've a feeling you're going to tell me it's a re-skin, and I'd rather hear it now than in a year. Finally, tell me what the procedure can't do: whether the essence-discovery move is circular, since you only know what the essence is by knowing which variations work, and the other way round; and where taste is doing the work while pretending to be method.
