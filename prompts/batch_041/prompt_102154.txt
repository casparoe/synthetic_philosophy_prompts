ok so this is kinda random but im on my schools Governance Review Committee (we're the ones who rewrote the dress code last year, riviting stuff) and we have to decide something by next months meeting, and guess who's the secretary and has to write the memo. me.

heres the situation. our school has a student council thats elected every year and it always ends up being the same like 15 kids - the debate team, the class presidents, the ones whose parents are on the PTA. turnout is maybe 22%. so the admin floated an idea and now theres a real proposal on the table to get rid of the elected council and replace it with a lottery. every students name goes in, 30 names get drawn, and those 30 ARE the new student assembly for the year. they get out of one study hall a week and the school gives them 10 community service hours as comp. you can opt out of being drawn if you have a real excuse, not just "i dont feel like it", and if you do get drawn you can decline once, but then your name goes back in the pool next year.

my committee is completely split and i have to write the memo that recommends one thing. half of us think the lottery is way more fair because it actually looks like the school instead of just the kids who like campaigning. the other half thinks its nuts because if you get picked at random you didnt earn anything and nobody can vote you out, so theres zero accountability - and also 30 out of 1200 students is a tiny slice, so it might not "reflect the school" at all, it might just be 30 random people, one of whom is that kid who vapes in the parking lot.

a few specific things i need you to actually deal with in the memo, cause these are the fights we keep having:
- whether the draw should be stratified, like guaranteed seats per grade, or even per "clique" (someone seriously suggested this and i cant tell if its genius or insane)
- whether the 10 service hours is basically a bribe or actually makes it fair for kids who have jobs after school
- whether there should be ANY way to remove someone, like a 2/3 vote of the assembly itself, or if having a removal option ruins the whole point of picking at random
- whether we should do a hybrid, half elected and half drawn by lot, and if thats the best of both worlds or the worst
- the opt-out thing, because someone pointed out that if the kids who opt out are systematically the shy/uninvolved ones, then we've basically reinvented the election with extra steps

please take the other side seriously, dont just tell me the lottery is obviously better. my principal reads this and shes on the fence, and she HATES when our memos sound like a debate case where one side is a punching bag.
