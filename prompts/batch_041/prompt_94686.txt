Two of my friends got into it at a barbecue about whether insects feel pain, and it turned into a bigger fight about how you'd ever tell which animals are conscious at all. They want me to referee and I honestly cant.

(1) Maya said we can't know, so the sane policy is to treat anything that acts like it's suffering as a candidate. She called it a bet you can't lose — if you're wrong about a beetle you've lost nothing, and if you're wrong the other way you've tortured something for a shrug.

(2) Dev said that isn't a bet, it's a refusal to make one. If you apply it consistently then a thermostat that "wants" to stay at twenty degrees is a candidate too, and a rule that never rules anything out isn't a fact about beetles, it's a fact about your nerves. He also said it flattens the difference between the dog and the beetle, which seems like the one thing we actually do know.

(3) Maya said there's a difference between plain recoiling and having the same kind of wiring we've got, wired into the same kind of chemicals that dull pain in us. Once you've got that, the burden flips, and Dev is basically asking her to prove a negative.

(4) Dev said that's exactly what he doesn't buy: every one of those markers would be sitting there whether or not anything was being felt, so it can't be evidence that something is. And he said the dimmer move is cheating — Maya wants to say the beetle's pain is real but faint, and faint is a dial she's just borrowed from humans, with no way to actually read it.

(5) Maya said that by his standard he can't know his own brother is conscious either, and that "nothing's home" is a claim too, which needs support just like hers does.

Priya, who was half listening, said the whole fight is about the word: "conscious" is doing four or five jobs at once — being awake, feeling pain, having a point of view, being able to think about yourself — and a beetle could score on some and not others, so "how conscious is it" might be a broken question.
