Two people in my book club got into it last week over Mencius and Xunzi and we never got to the actual reading. I was just sitting there listening, so I genuinely can't tell you which of them I think was right. Here's roughly how it went.

Round one, Ines said the whole human-nature-is-good-versus-bad thing is half a fake fight, because they aren't even using nature in the same sense. For Mencius, xing is the thing that makes us the kind of creature we are -- the incipient moral reactions, the stuff that marks us off from the animals. For Xunzi, xing is just the inborn, spontaneous part: appetites, desires, reactions you never had to learn. Say the inborn spontaneous stuff isn't moral yet and you haven't denied Mencius anything, you've just picked up the other word. On the first reading we've got moral beginnings; on the second we've got raw material. Both can be true at once, so whats the fight about?

Ravi said no, that can't be the whole story, because the disagreement is about what explains morality, and explanation doesn't split that neatly. For Mencius, cultivation is mostly protection and watering -- the thing is already the right kind of thing, and the danger is that you damage it. For Xunzi there's nothing moral in there to protect; ritual and deliberate effort do all the work. They aren't fertilizer, they're the whole crop. Then he pushed harder: Xunzi has a bootstrapping problem. If nobody's nature is good, where did the first rituals come from? Whoever made them must already have been good enough to see what was needed. Unless Xunzi says the capacity to be reformed is itself natural -- but then he's handed Mencius exactly the natural moral capacity he was supposed to be denying.

Ines: a capacity to be reformed isn't a tendency to be good. Clay can be made into a pot; that doesn't make clay pot-shaped or pot-inclined. Xunzi's line is that our inborn dispositions push against the rituals, so they have to be bent and steamed straight. Mencius's line is that our inborn dispositions push with the moral life -- that's what we are. That's a real disagreement about which way the grain runs, not a word game.

Ravi: but Mencius doesn't think the grain runs smoothly on its own either. The mountain gets stripped bare and looks barren; the barley comes out differently depending on the soil and the rain. So both of them have environment and effort doing alot of work. What's actually left? Maybe just the order of explanation -- Mencius says ritual is the outward expression of a moral sense that's already there, Xunzi says the moral sense is manufactured by the ritual. Same ingredients, opposite direction of dependence.

Ines: then the question is whether anything is there before the training, and the child at the well case settles it -- the alarm is there before any ritual has done anything. And Xunzi can't just keep saying still bad nature forever; what would even count as evidence against him?

Ravi: a flinch isn't a virtue. Feelings aren't dispositions to act, and Xunzi never denied we have feelings. If Mencius's case needs the sprouts to be more than feelings, he hasn't shown it, and the whole there's-something-there move is doing less work than it looks like.

That's about where it ended. Can you go through it move by move and tell me who's right at each step -- where one of them is right, where the other is, where they're just talking past each other, and what both of them are missing? Point by point is what I want, though if one of them ends up more right overall I'd like to know that too.
