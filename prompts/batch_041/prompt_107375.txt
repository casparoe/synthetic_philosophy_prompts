I'm putting together a two-page dissertation prospectus (philosophy, one year of writing) and I've got a half-formed idea I want you to turn into something I could actually defend to a committee.

The idea: the particularism/generalism fight about moral reasons. The particularist says reasons are holistic -- whether some consideration counts in favour of an act depends on everything else going on in the situation, so there aren't any true universal moral principles; the generalist says there have to be principles or moral thought and moral learning are impossible. My hunch is that a lot of this is a fight about which level of principle is at stake, and neither side says which one it means. Distinguish two things: first-order principles, the ones that say things like lying is wrong other things being equal, and second-order principles, which don't tell you what to do but tell you how the first-order stuff interacts -- e.g. that no consideration is a reason in every context, or that the weight of a reason is a function of X. My suspicion is that the particularist is basically right that there are no true first-order principles but has no good arguement against second-order ones, and that the generalist keeps sliding between the two levels when they claim particularism is incoherent. If that's right, some of the standard "particularism is self-defeating" moves miss the target, and some of the particularist's own arguments overshoot.

So write me the prospectus. I want the central question stated so it has a determinate answer -- something like: are there true second-order moral principles that do real explanatory work, and if there are, does that rescue generalism or is it a position both sides could just accept? Then: why the existing positions leave this open (no lit review or citations needed, I'll add those myself -- just refer to the moves by description). The method -- what I'd actually spend the year doing, in stages, and why armchair conceptual work rather than, say, dragging in empirical moral psychology. What a result would look like, and what would count as the project failing (be honest here -- e.g. if it turns out to be a verbal dispute about the word "principle," I want that said up front). And a realistic scope -- is a year too long or too short for this? Flag where the idea is probably wrong.
