I'm putting together a unit on categories and the 'essence' of a letterform, and I keep circling a puzzle I can't quite nail down. Take the letter 'a'. In most text faces it's double-storey; in lots of geometric and handwriting faces it's a single-storey bowl with a stem on the right, and there the stem is more or less just a shorter version of the stem on a 'd' -- the main difference is how far it rises. Yet nobody reads it as a 'd' or as a meaningless squiggle. Meanwhile a typeface is supposed to hang together as one style, so the 'a' has to look like it belongs with the 'b' and the 'c'. And here's the bit that really trips me up: some glyphs are flat-out ambigious -- a shape that reads fine as one letter inside a word reads as nothing, or as a different letter, once you pull it out on its own. So 'a'-ness can't be a checklist of strokes, and it can't be pure context either. Explain how you'd make sense of this, as exactly five numbered points, no intro or conclusion, a sentence or two each -- and for each one say which simpler theory it rules out (a prototype view, necessary-and-sufficient features, a pure word-shape/context view, whatever).
