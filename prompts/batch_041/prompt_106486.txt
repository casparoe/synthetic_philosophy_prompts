A small foundation has asked me to help design a one-semester theory seminar, staffed by four philosophers and a modest budget, on the foundations of updating on uncertain and conditional evidence. The cluster we have in mind runs from the Judy Benjamin problem through Jeffrey conditioning, Adams-style updating on indicative conditionals, and the family of maximum-entropy and minimum-cross-entropy rules that compete with them. A colleague has proposed that we organize the whole seminar around a single thesis: that any admissible update rule must be invariant to the order in which independent items of uncertain evidence arrive, so that a rule which yields different posteriors depending on the sequence is thereby discredited.

Which open questions in this cluster most deserve the seminar's time, and in what order should we pursue them? I care far less about the list than about the reasoning. For each question, tell me why it is upstream of the others; how tractable it is for philosophers working with pen and paper rather than with proofs about general measure spaces; whether the order-invariance thesis genuinely illuminates it or is a red herring that would distort our priorities; and what a result in the next year would do to move it up or down the agenda. Where you think our framing is itself mistaken -- for instance, if the live questions are not about which update rule is "correct" but about what such rules are for, or about the gap between diachronic constraints and synchronic ones -- say so explicitly and re-rank accordingly. Please give me a defended agenda rather than a survey, and skip the reading lists.
