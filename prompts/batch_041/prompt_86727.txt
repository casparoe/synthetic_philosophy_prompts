In a philosophy of physics reading group, a condensed-matter physicist cut off my question about why we remember the past but not the future by saying "time's arrow is just the arrow of entropy," and then added that the rest of the debate is a verbal dispute. I want a menu of the precise theses that one-liner could be asserting, ordered from the platitudinous to the radical: for instance, that entropy increase is merely one temporal asymmetry among several (radiative, psychological, cosmological), that the others reduce to it, that the direction is a matter of statistical typicality rather than law, that the slogan is definitional and so empty, and any further readings worth separating. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or false, and what it presupposes about the underlying dynamics (say, time-reversal invariance) and about the status of a low-entropy initial condition. Finally, tell me which of these readings, if any, would actually license her conclusion that my question was already answered and that the remainder is a verbal dispute; I suspect she is conflating something, but I cannot say what.
