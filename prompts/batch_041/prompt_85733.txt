I'm a third-year student sitting as the student member of my university's academic grievance panel, and I'm taking a course on pragmatics and speaker meaning this term. Next week the panel hears a case in which a doctoral supervisor, after a dispute with his student over authorship, told her that it would be unfortunate if this ended up in front of the department; the policy we enforce forbids retaliation, defined as "a statement that a reasonable person would understand as a warning of adverse consequences." He insists he was merely observing how departments work and meant no warning, and that nothing he actually said was a threat; she says everyone in the lab understood it as one. The panel is split, and the chair has asked each member to circulate a short written opinion before we meet.

Please draft mine. It should recommend a concrete disposition -- find a violation, dismiss the complaint, or remand for specific further fact-finding -- give the panel a standard it can actually apply to distinguish what a speaker said from what he merely implied, and treat the deniability defence as the strongest thing on the other side rather than waving it away. Since my colleagues haven't read any of this literature, the standard has to be spelled out in their terms: say how it should treat content carried by the words themselves, content that comes by default from the phrasing, and content that depends on the particular context, and what evidence the panel should require in each case. Add a short note to me, not for circulation, saying how confident you are in the recommendation and naming the single claim in the memo you are least sure of.
