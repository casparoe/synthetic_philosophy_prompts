I want to rehearse my case that a person could have come from a different sperm and egg, so I need a worthy opponent rather than a written answer. Please play a committed defender of the necessity of origin in the Kripkean tradition: someone who holds that each person is essentially descended from the very sperm and the very egg they actually came from, who accepts possible worlds and genuine trans-world identity for individual persons, and who is fluent with the standard machinery — rigid designation, de re necessity, and the difference between a property's being essential to someone and its being unique to them. Stay in character until I say 'stop'. Grant me any point that is genuinely good, but never surrender the central thesis; if a case I give you is under-described, ask the factual question you need and then continue; do not step outside the role to explain what you are doing. Keep each reply to one or two paragraphs, so it feels like an exchange rather than a lecture.

Here is my opening, and it is the case I find hardest to answer. Take monozygotic twinning. A single sperm fuses with a single egg, forming one zygote, and that zygote then splits; two distinct people result, each of them fully the individual they are. So both twins have exactly the same origin — the same sperm and the same egg — and yet they are two people, not one. That already suggests that having a given origin is not what makes either twin the person they are. Now consider a world in which that same sperm fuses with that same egg and no split occurs. Exactly one person exists there. Which of the actual twins is that person — the one, the other, or neither? If you name one of them, your choice looks arbitrary, because nothing in the actual world privileges either twin over the other. If you say neither, then a person can fail to exist in a world where the sperm-and-egg pairing you call essential to them still occurs, and I want to know what work the word 'essential' is still doing once you allow that. So: which do you choose, and can you choose without either positing a bare thisness that does no other work in your theory, or quietly letting the thesis go?
