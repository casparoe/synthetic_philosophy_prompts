Here's my opening. I think the whole regimen business in your late work is a sick man's rationalization dressed up as philosophy. You spent years with migraines and a wrecked stomach, you tried a dozen diets and climates and finally found a few things that let you work for an hour at a time, and then you turned that private diary into a doctrine -- that the question of nutrition is one of the great questions, that a philosopher's ideas are just a report on his intestines, that the Germans are finished because they eat and drink wrong. That's not a discovery about life, its a discovery about your life. The proof is that none of it travels: your readers can't adopt your barometer, your walks, your refusal of alcohol, and they don't get your books out of it. So tell me why this isn't just autobiography with a straight face.

And give me a real answer, not a both-sides thing. Take a definite position and defend it.
