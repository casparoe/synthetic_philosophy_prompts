so i've been going back and forth with this guy on a forum (i know, i know) about whether famines are natural disasters or political crimes, and he's built this argument i cant knock down even though i'm pretty sure the conclusion is nuts.

his setup: he's read Sen, thinks the entitlement stuff settles it, and he's also got his own little rule he calls the withholding test — a famine counts as political just in case at some point during it there was food within reach of the starving that somebody with the power to move it decided not to move. fine, whatever.

the argument:

(1) In every famine we have decent records for — Bengal '43, Ireland, the Great Leap Forward — people starved while food existed somewhere within reach: in the next district, in a warehouse, on a ship heading out, or just priced beyond what they could sell their labour for.
(2) So in none of those famines was the binding constraint the sheer absence of food.
(3) If the binding constraint was never the absence of food, then what killed people was the arrangement that kept the food away from them.
(4) An arrangement that keeps food from people who then die is a political choice, and a political choice that kills on that scale is a crime.
(5) By his withholding test, any famine where that happens is a political crime.
(6) Therefore famines are never natural disasters; "natural disaster" is just the word we use to launder them.

the bit that gets me: i tried to feed it a case where it has to come out wrong — a small island, a blight kills every edible plant, no boats, no granaries, nobody to appeal to, everyone starves — and he says the test still fires, because if they'd built granaries ten years earlier, or if the mainland had sent a ship, there was *somebody* who could have moved food and didn't. so the test never comes out natural. which is either the argument working or the test being rigged, and i genuinely can't tell which.

(2) and (3) feel obviously right to me and i really dont want to give them up — i'm not trying to say famines are just weather, that's the whole thing i'm arguing against. but (6) can't be right, can it? point me at the step doing the sleight of hand, tell me why it looked innocent, then tell me what i'm on the hook for if i give it up. and if it turns out every step is actually fine and i just have to eat (6), say so and tell me what that costs me somewhere else, cause i've got a feeling it aint free.
