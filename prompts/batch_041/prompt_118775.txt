So I started a new job about two months ago as a claims assessor for a mid-size insurer, and the reason I'm writing this is that we've just begun writing policies for companies that run autonomous delivery robots and automated forklifts, and I am getting files I have no idea what to do with. The one thats been eating at me: a sidewalk delivery robot clipped a woman at a crossing and broke her wrist. The perception software was off-the-shelf from a vendor that says it performed exactly as designed. The company that runs the robots bought that software and changed nothing about it. The remote operator was babysitting fourteen robots at once and happened to be looking at a different robot's camera for the twenty seconds that mattered. Nobody lied, nobody did anything you could put in front of a jury as reckless, and yet a woman has a broken wrist and a bill and no one to send it to. Every party has a story in which the harm isn't really theirs, and annoyingly, all four stories hold up.

I'm also doing a part-time MA in the evenings and I have to write a position paper, and I've decided I want to argue something that will make my seminar hate me. The line I keep hearing from the people who write about this stuff is that we're in a "responsibility gap" and that the fix is to close it, usually by locating the human who was really in control of the machine. I don't buy it, because the human who gets located is basically always whoever is cheapest to blame, and in my files that's the operator on an hourly wage. So here's the thesis I'd like you to argue for: when an autonomous system causes a harm like this, responsibility should attach to whoever had the cheapest available opportunity, at the time, to make that class of harm *legible in advance* — the best chance anyone had to make the failure visible or predictable before it happened — and not to whoever happened to be nearest to the harm in time. Write it as an argument for a position, not a survey of what other people say. Make the criterion sharp enough that it actually decides my broken-wrist file and tells me who pays, and deal honestly with the objection I keep half-suspecting is fatal, which is that I've just reinvented "blame whoever has the most money" with extra steps.
