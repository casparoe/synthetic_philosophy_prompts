A friend of mine scores old forecasts as a hobby — she keeps a spreadsheet and posts her verdicts every so often. So far: Malthus, 'right but early' (the population/food squeeze was a real constraint, it only got lifted by stuff he had no way of seeing coming, and the framework he built even predicts the way out); Ehrlich's famine forecasts, 'falsified', on the grounds that the deadline just kept sliding and his escape hatch — that it would all have happened anyway without the Green Revolution — can't be tested; Hubbert's peak oil, 'right but early' for the US, still open globally; the prediction that capitalism would drive workers' real wages down and keep them there, 'falsified'; and the 1980s warnings that we were wrecking the ozone layer, 'not falsified', since the reason it looks like a false alarm is that everyone believed it and stopped using CFCs.

Now she's got a new one to score. A food policy group said in 2011 that by 2022 world wheat yields would come in about 8% below their 2010 level, because of salinisation and groundwater depletion. 2022: yields up roughly 15%. Their line is that the salinisation and aquifer numbers were basically right, they just underestimated how fast farmers would move to drip irrigation and salt-tolerant seed; they've reworked the model and it now says the Indus and Nile deltas lose about 6% of yields by 2035. And the 2011 report is generally credited with kicking off the drip-irrigation subsidy programmes in three countries, so part of why the original call failed is that people read it and did something about it.

What's she going to call this one? I'm less interested in the label than in the rules she's actually running: I want the ones that fit all her earlier calls, what each of them predicts here, which you'd put your money on and why, and the sort of case that would split those rules apart if you could feed her one.
