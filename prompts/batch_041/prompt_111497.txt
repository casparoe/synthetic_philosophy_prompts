I write novels for a living, which mostly means I spend my days asking why a character does what they do, and I've noticed I have very firm gut reactions about when a good deed gets spoiled by the reason the person attaches to it — and absolutely no idea what rule those reactions add up to. Any chance you can find one for me? Here's what I keep coming back to.

A man is on a sinking boat, his wife and a stranger are both in the water, he can only get to one, he grabs his wife. Asked why, he says: she's my wife. I think that's perfect. Nothing's missing from it. Same rescue, but he says: she's my wife, and anyway I'd be within my rights to save whoever I was closest to. The deed is identical and I think slightly less of him — as if he'd had to check whether he was allowed.

Flip to the stranger. A woman I don't know swims out for a person she's never met, and afterwards says she did it because every life counts the same, his as much as hers. I don't hear a thought too many there. I hear something admirable. If she'd said she had no idea why she did it, I'd think she was either lying or just running on instinct, and instinct isn't the same thing as goodness.

Now the case that turns the whole thing over for me. A friend of mine spent thirty years on a painting practice that was obviously the point of her life, and gave it up to run her dead brother's failing shop because the money would do more good going there. I think she did something admirable and something terrible at once and I can't say which comes first. If she'd kept painting and told me that everyone's allowed some room for their own work, I'd definately have heard exactly the thought I disliked in the drowning man. And yet when someone else — a nurse I know — won't sign a false form to get medicine into a place that needs it, because lying is wrong, and people go without, I think she's protecting her own clean hands at their expense. But if she signed it and felt nothing at all, I'd think something was missing in her too.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? And which of my reactions does it make me give up? I'd also like to know what it predicts for a case I genuinely haven't settled: a father who does every right thing for his kids, but always because it's his duty and never once because they're his.
