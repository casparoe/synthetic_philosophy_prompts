I have been reading a lot of rationalist and consciousness-adjacent blogs, and I keep running into a distinction that I can state but not really grasp: the difference between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, and the way global workspace theory is supposed to relate to each. My rough understanding is that access consciousness is about information being globally available for reasoning, report, and control, while phenomenal consciousness is about what it is like to undergo an experience; global workspace theory says that when information is broadcast across a workspace, it becomes access-conscious, and perhaps that is all there is to consciousness. But I do not see how the theory is supposed to cross the gap, if there is one. Could you explain the conceptual issue here carefully, without assuming I already accept either the realist or deflationary framing?

In particular, I would like you to address the overflow issue. In experiments on brief visual displays, subjects often claim to have seen more items than they can report, which some take as evidence that phenomenal consciousness overflows access consciousness. If that reading is right, then global workspace theory might be a theory of access only. If it is wrong, then the reportability criterion is doing more work than it seems. Please lay out the strongest version of both sides, and then consider a thought experiment I have not seen discussed: imagine a system that satisfies every functional criterion associated with global workspace theory—ignition, broadcast, integration, availability to a wide range of downstream systems, verbal report, and flexible control—but for which there is no phenomenal character at all. Call this the dark workspace. Is the dark workspace conceptually coherent?

If it is, does global workspace theory have the resources to rule it out, or does the theory simply leave phenomenal consciousness aside? If it is not coherent, what exactly in the theory rules it out, and does that ruling-out depend on a controversial functionalist premise? I am especially interested in whether the theory can distinguish between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness without either collapsing the former into the latter or treating the latter as an inexplicable add-on.
