I'm putting together a batch of stories about concepts and I want one on heritability. Here's the setup I'd like you to build on, because I want the story to do real work and not just restate the usual "heritability isn't destiny" line.

A society ranks every kid at twelve with a single aptitude index and streams them for life. The number everyone cites to defend the system is that the index has a heritability of around 0.85 in their population. Your protagonist is the statistician who computed it, and she has to explain to a panel why that figure is exactly what you'd expect in a society where basicly everyone gets the same food, the same schools and the same screens — that flattening the environment is what pushes the genetic share of the leftover variance up — and why the very same trait would come out far less heritable, and far more responsive to intervention, somewhere the environments are wildly unequal. She keeps getting asked whether the trait is "mostly genetic" and can't get anyone to hear the difference between a fact about variance in a population and a fact about a person, or a fact about what would happen if you changed the environment.

Don't have anyone deliver a lecture or define terms at length; let the misunderstanding live in the dialogue and in what the panel keeps refusing to hear. A few pages is fine. Start with the story itself, no throat-clearing.
