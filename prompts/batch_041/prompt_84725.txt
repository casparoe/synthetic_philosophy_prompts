I've been trying to see why the Frege-Geach problem is supposed to be such a serious threat to expressivism, and I suspect I'm missing the point of it. As I reconstruct the objection, the trouble is with what "lying is wrong" is doing in the antecedent of "if lying is wrong, then telling my brother to lie is wrong": nothing is being asserted there, so the expressivist's story about expressing disapproval has no foothold, and he owes us an account of the unasserted occurrence. But that looks like a problem with an obvious answer — where nothing is asserted, nothing is expressed, so there is no attitude to be accounted for — which makes me think the real difficulty must lie somewhere else, perhaps in a merely technical demand about formalizing the inference. What does the Frege-Geach problem actually require of the expressivist that "unasserted clauses express nothing" fails to supply, and why doesn't that observation simply dissolve the problem? Please lead with your answer in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
