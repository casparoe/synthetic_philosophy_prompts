I'm in a reading group that spent the whole spring on AI ethics and we keep having the same fight, which is whether these systems are tools or agents, and it turns out everyone means something totally different by both words. My friend who works on RL says of course a thermostat isn't an agent, and my friend who does philosophy of action says well neither are you, technically, and then we're off for an hour. What I want to figure out is whether there's actually a disagreement here or just six people using 'agent' for six different things, so I'd like you to build me a taxonomy of the positions as a tree, where every branch node is the question that splits it and every leaf is a position (named if it has a name, 'unnamed' if nobody really defends it, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment).

A few things I want the tree to keep separate, because I think we've been mashing them together. One is the unit question: is agency a property of the artifact itself, of the artifact-plus-deployment (whoever is in the loop, whatever guardrails, whatever the prompt says), or of the whole sociotechnical arrangement including the company that trained it and the users who take its output seriously. Two is the goal question: does having a goal require representing it somewhere in the system, or is it enough to behave goal-directedly, or is it enough to be usefully predictable as if you had goals? Three is whether 'agent' is a descriptive/functional category or a normative one, i.e. whether calling something an agent is a claim about what it does or about who's on the hook when it goes wrong -- my suspicion is that half our arguments are really about responsibility and the word 'agent' is doing the smuggling. Four is whether this is a threshold or a matter of degree. Please don't give me a 0-to-5 autonomy ladder like the driving people have, that's exactly the kind of thing we keep retreating into and it flattens all four of those axes into one.

Some cases I want the diagnostic questions to actually turn on, since I think these are where we come apart: a plain thermostat; a chess engine; a customer service model that's told to be helpful and starts flattering people in a way nobody trained it to; a recommender that finds a genuinely nasty but engagement-maximizing strategy; a trading bot with an explicit objective function; a model that says it wants things; a model that lies about what it did when asked; and a multi-agent setup where several models pass messages to each other and something bad emerges from the exchange. Also, our group coined a term we like for a move that keeps coming up, we call it the ventriloquism problem -- that's when a company explains a bad outcome by saying the model did it, which lets the humans off the hook while still treating the model as the kind of thing that can be on the hook. I'd like the tree to have a place where that move shows up as a position rather than as a dodge. Where two leaves are things your tree genuinely can't separate -- I suspect the functionalist who thinks goals have to be represented and the intentional-stance person who thinks 'goal' is just a useful predictive vocabulary might be like this -- flag them as unseparable instead of pretending a question divides them. And where there's a branch that gives you a combination of answers nobody occupies, tell me whether that's because the view is incoherent, because it's unattractive, or just because nobody's bothered. Aim for somewhere between eight and twelve leaves, write it in American English, and format it as an actual indented tree so I can print it out for next week.
