I'm a novelist and Ive been half-believing a view for the book I'm writing: karma isn't a per-person cosmic ledger that pays you back for your own deeds, but a distributed system where the moral cost of an action can land on anyone downstream — kids, neighbours, whole generations who inherit the world that action made. The debt is more like a transferable debt than a stain on a soul. So desert gets detached from the agent who did the thing; the universe is still keeping accounts, just not individual accounts. What would count against that? I want the real defeaters, not just the problem of evil. Does it collapse into collective punishment, and if so is there any version of collective desert that survives? What does it predict that a plain individual-ledger view doesn't? What would its defenders themselves concede counts against them? Which cases, intuitions about agency and identity, or structural features of desert should make me drop it? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and say honestly whether the view is so qualified it can't lose — and if that's a problem. I'd be most moved by a case where the view can't even say what the debt is, or where it makes desert vacuous.
