I'm in a seminar on personal identity and we've spent two weeks on origin essentialism — the claim that I couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg, that the particular gametes I came out of are part of what I am. I want to practice attacking it against someone who actually knows the position instead of a punching bag, so: play a committed origin essentialist. Be sharp, have real resources, concede the small stuff freely (sure, origin doesn't individuate twins; sure, you have no criterion of transworld identity), but never give up the core claim. Don't cite anything or name-drop philosophers — I don't care about the bibliography, I care about whether the view can take a hit — and if a case I give you is ambiguous, take the reading that's worst for me and say that's what you're doing. One solid paragraph a turn, stay in character till I say stop.

Here's my opening, and it comes in two pieces. Piece one: my identical twin and I came from one zygote, so same sperm, same egg. Your principle is true of both of us, so it can't be the thing that makes me me rather than him — at best it tells me I'm one of the two people who came from that zygote. So you'll want something extra, some fact about which of the two I am, and you'll say thats essential as well. Fine, but notice you just smuggled in an ingredient that isn't origin. Piece two, and this is the one I actually want to see you survive: why stop at the gametes? That sperm was one of a couple hundred million my father made that week, that egg one of a few hundred thousand sitting in my mothers ovaries, and then there's the particular night, the particular act, the particular breakfast he ate — all of it had to happen for me to be here. If I'm essentially from that sperm and that egg, what principled reason stops you saying I'm essentially from that Tuesday as well? "The gametes are the biological origin" is starting to sound like a fact about where we like to draw lines, not a fact about my essence. So: which piece sinks you, or can you take both?

If it helps your side you're welcome to bring in the weird biological cases — people who are the fusion of two embryos and so have two sperm and two eggs in their history, or twins who came from one egg and two sperm — but only if they actually help you. Go.
