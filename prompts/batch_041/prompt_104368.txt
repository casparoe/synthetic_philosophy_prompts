Me and a couple friends have a long-running argument about the lessons of history — Munich, Vietnam, the whole business — and there's a pattern in how people actually use those lessons that I can't find a word for. I'm hoping you can either hand me the established term or help me carve the thing up properly, because I care more about the carving than the label.

Here's what I keep seeing. Someone invokes Munich against a policy: you can't hand the revisionist power the territory, that's appeasement, we've seen how this ends. I point out the disanalogy — the power in question isn't a rising expansionist with an irredentist claim, the concessions aren't territorial, the other players aren't in the same configuration — and instead of the analogy collapsing, it just... survives, as a mood. The word appeasement keeps doing the work even after every structural feature that made Munich Munich has been stripped out. And then the flip side, which is the part that really bugs me: the same person turns around and backs a policy that's structurally the same as the one they just denounced, because it's being sold under a different name — de-escalation, a confidence-building measure, engagement, a humanitarian corridor. The alarm doesn't fire. It's as if the lesson is glued to the label rather than to the features, so it over-triggers on anything wearing the name and under-triggers on the same conduct dressed in a euphemism. A friend of mine calls it selective memory but that's clearly not it, he remembers everything.

I'm pretty sure this isn't just framing or the availability heuristic, because those are about what's salient or easy to recall, and here the person does remember the case, does concede the disanalogy, and still treats the word as decisive. So: is there a name for this? If there is, tell me how it differs from the nearest candidates — I'd guess something in the neighborhood of analogical reasoning, or schemas and scripts, or maybe something about how we treat category labels as carriers of an essence, but none of those quite lands. If there's no name, coin one and define it, and tell me where its edges are. Does it require a proper noun, or does it happen with abstract nouns too — deterrence, aggression, sovereignty, quagmire? Does it survive when the person genuinely knows the structure of the original case, or does expertise dissolve it? And is the mirror image — refusing to draw a lesson because it's dressed in the wrong vocabulary — the same phenomenon or a different one?
