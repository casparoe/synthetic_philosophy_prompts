My opening: You say knowledge is closed under entailment. Fine. Take an agent that knows the Peano axioms and the rules of first-order logic, and basically nothing else that matters. By your closure principle it knows every theorem of arithmetic. Now pick a theorem T whose shortest proof is so long that no physical system could ever write it down, let alone check it — longer than the age of the universe in symbols. Does the agent know T? If yes, then knowledge has stopped being about the agent at all; its just a fancy name for true in all the worlds compatible with the agents information. If no, closure fails. And please dont tell me its an idealization like a frictionless plane, because frictionless planes are limits of real systems, and there is no sequence of real reasoners converging to an omniscient one — every added inference rule just moves the frontier. So which horn do you take, and why doesnt it gut the whole project? Also, if it knows T, does it know that it knows T? Because your semantics usually gives positive introspection, and then a finite agent knows an infinite tower of knowings. If it doesnt, then say goodbye to KK and explain why closure survives where introspection fails.
