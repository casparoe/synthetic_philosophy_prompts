Next month I'm giving a work-in-progress paper to our philosophy department and I'm trying to anticipate teh Q&A, so I want you to draft my prep sheet. The paper defends a view I call scripted coordination. The claim is that joint action doesn't need shared intention, we-intention (the idea that each participant has an intention that's irreducibly first-person plural), or joint commitment (a normative bond that each participant recognises). All it needs is that each participant follows a public script — a mutually known set of conditional instructions, like a recipe, a protocol, a musical score, or a clause in a statute — and that they stay mutually responsive to deviations from it. I say the script is an external artefact, not a mental state; it can be implicit, and it can be renegotiated mid-action. I also run a 'deviation test': if an agent could have deviated without the joint action collapsing, their contribution is joint; if not, it's mere aggregate behaviour. And I use this to say collective responsibility is distributed by script-role and capacity to deviate, not by any group mind.

The audience is mostly philosophy of mind and action people, plus two social ontologists who work on collective intentionality, a legal philosopher who writes on corporate responsibility, and a game theorist who is allergic to anything that sounds like shared intention. They're friendly but territorial, adn some will think I'm just redescribing coordination; others will think I'm denying the phenomenon of acting together. I've got 20 minutes to present and 30 for questions.

Give me the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this lot, ordered by how much damage they'd do, phrased the way each would phrase them. For each one: a two-minute reply, a note on where the reply is soft, and what I should concede up front. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks, and tell me which single reply has to land for the paper to survive. Don't give me a bibliography or reading list — I know the literature and I don't need citations. Please write it in British English (recognise, behaviour, sceptical, etc.).
