I had my first real surgery six weeks ago and the anaesthetic was like a light switch: one second I'm counting backwards, the next I'm in recovery, and there is nothing in between. Not black, not quiet, not even a sense that time went by — just nobody home. I'd always sort of assumed that the lights get dimmed in there but stay on, and it's been needling me ever since. So I've been reading around consciousness-as-a-user-illusion: the picture where the desktop icons and the cursor are perfectly real as far as you're concerned, they're what you interact with, but there's no icon and no cursor anywhere inside the machine, and our impression of an inner show with a viewer sitting in it is a useful fiction of that same kind. Now, my department hands each postdoc a small pot of money — enough for a two-day workshop with six or seven people plus their travel — and the justification page is due next month, wich means I, a person who has been reading about this for six weeks, have to say what the field's live questions actually are. Absurd, but here we are.

What I want isn't a survey or a reading list, I want a defended agenda: which open questions in this area most deserve a workshop's worth of attention, ranked, and why, with the criteria made explicit — what's upstream of what, what's tractable for a room of philosophers rather than a lab with subjects, what's neglected because it's unfashionable rather than because it's dead, and what would change the ranking if it turned out one way rather than the other. Two things keep nagging at me and I'd like them factored in rather than waved at. First, a friend who's much further along than me claims the whole position lives or dies on the specific misrepresentations it's willing to commit to: you don't get to just announce that the inner show is a fiction, you have to say where the fiction comes apart from the machinery — is it wrong about how many things are in the stream, about their order, about there being a stream at all, or about there being someone the stream is for? — and apparently nobody agrees about what the divergences even are, which strikes me as either the central business of the field or a sign it's talking past itself. Second, the self-undermining worry: if introspection is the only evidence anyone ever had for the inner show, what's left to stand on when you announce that introspection is systematically unreliable? I'm told there's an answer to this and I never understand it, so if that question is upstream of the others, say so; if it's a red herring, say that too. And tell me which of the questions people currently find exciting are overrated — I'd rather fund the boring one that everything else depends on.

It has to be a workshop, not an experiment, so weight toward questions that can actually be moved by argument in two days, and flag any where the honest answer is that we'd need data nobody has.
