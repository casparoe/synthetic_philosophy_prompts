In a year-long seminar on self-amending rules, we have stalled on a variant of the Nomic idea in which any rule may be amended, including the rule that sets the amendment threshold, while a stability clause says that no amendment counts if it would render the rule set inconsistent. The literalists hold that this generates a genuine paradox: an amendment can change the threshold that authorized it, so the vote that passed it may be retroactively invalid and the system has no determinate legal state. The pragmatists hold that this only shows the literalists are treating rules as self-executing; once one asks who applies the rules and how disputes are settled, the paradox becomes an ordinary institutional gap. Neither camp has moved, and each thinks the other is changing the subject. My hypothesis is that the impasse is not really about self-reference but about two objects under one name: the formal consistency of a rule set and the social validity of a legal order. Diagnose the disagreement. Is it substantive, verbal, or two questions conflated? Which parts are genuinely formal, which are empirical or institutional, and which rest on bedrock assumptions neither side can argue for? Finally, what would count as progress here, whether a formal result, a distinction, or a change of question?
