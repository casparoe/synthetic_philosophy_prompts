I am drafting a position paper on the ethics of harming and would like a consistency audit of my commitments before I commit to them. Here is where I have landed.

(1) There are genuine deontological constraints: it is morally wrong to intentionally kill one innocent person even when doing so is the only way to save five others.

(2) Those constraints are not brute axioms. They are justified, and the justification runs through what the constraints protect — the fact that each person's life and bodily integrity matter enormously.

(3) That justification must be agent-neutral in form. Whatever reason grounds my constraint against killing is a reason of the same kind for anyone else, and in stating it I am to count the interests of each person equally; it does not give extra weight to the fact that it would be my hand that did the killing.

(4) The constraints are nevertheless not absolute. They have thresholds: at some sufficiently large number of lives at stake, it becomes not merely permissible but required to kill the one.

(5) The constraints are part of the moral truth, not useful rules of thumb. An agent who violates one in order to bring about the best available outcome acts wrongly even when the outcome is in fact better.

(6) In deliberation, a constraint does not simply outweigh the reasons it excludes; it directs the agent not to act on those reasons at all. This is why I am not, every time I consider killing one to save five, silently weighing five lives against one and finding the five heavier.

(7) The doing/allowing distinction is morally significant. I may in some cases allow people to die whom I could have saved at little cost, but I may not kill.

What I want from you is an audit, not a survey. Which of these pull against each other, which only appear to, and how serious is each genuine conflict? Rank the genuine tensions from most to least severe; for each, say precisely which commitment would have to be weakened or abandoned, and what that revision costs. Where a conflict is merely apparent, identify the distinction that dissolves it. Then tell me whether any single revision would relieve more than one tension at once, and describe the package that would result.
