My sister's been writing a history of our family for the past year and it's turned into a proper argument. She's built the whole thing out of our gran's letters, a box of paperwork that came down through my mum's side, and a few parish records. It's a good piece of work, honestly. But my cousin on the other side says it's a lie by omission, because that branch of the family were tenant farmers who couldn't write, so almost nothing of theirs survived, and now the story reads as though the people who owned the land were the ones who did everything. He keeps saying history is written by the victors. My sister says thats a lazy slogan and that somebody has to say what actually happened.

I'm stuck in the middle, and the more I think about it the more I suspect the slogan is hiding about four different claims that don't stand or fall together. There's the boring one, that whoever writes tends to flatter themselves. There's the one about the record itself, that what survives is a function of who was literate and who had a shelf and whose house burnt down, so the gaps are structural rather than accidental. There's a nastier one, that the very categories available for describing a life get set by the people on top, so the tenants' experience isn't only missing from the record but was hard even to put into words at the time. And then there's the full-on one, that there's no fact of the matter about the past at all, only competing stories.

Can you help me sort out which of these are actually true, which ones are true but get wildly overstated, and which are just run together under the one slogan? Then the practical bit, which is what I really want: what do I tell my sister and my cousin? I don't think "include both sides" is the answer, since their accounts aren't the same kind of thing and only one of them is backed by paper. And I'd like to know what the honest move is when the evidence genuinely is one-sided — do you report what the documents support and flag the silence, or do you go further and try to reconstruct what the silence swallowed? Write back to me like an email, please, in plain continuous prose — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — and in British English. And dont send me a reading list, I won't get through it.
