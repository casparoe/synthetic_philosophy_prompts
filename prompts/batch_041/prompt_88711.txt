Ok so I've been watching these online pile-ons for a few years now and there's a shape to them that I can't find a word for. Here's what I keep seeing. Somebody says or does a thing. A bunch of people come after them. They apologize, or delete the thing, or step back from whatever platform they were on. And instead of that buying them any peace, it makes it worse. The apology gets picked apart for being too late, or too polished, or for going to the wrong audience, or for centering themselves. So they apologize again, harder. And the harder apology gets read as proof that the original thing must have been really bad, otherwise why are they groveling like that? Meanwhile the original thing has basically dropped out of the conversation. Nobody's arguing about whether it was bad anymore, they're arguing about whether the person's contrition is sincere, which is not something you can settle.

And here's the part that really gets me: there's nobody you can hand the apology to. In an ordinary dispute there's a person or a body who can say, fine, accepted, we're done here. With a pile-on there isn't. It's a crowd, nobody's in charge, so nobody has the standing to close the file. So the target keeps making concessions and the demand keeps going up. It moves in the wrong direction every single time -- each thing they do that ought to calm it down ratchets it up instead. Defiant people sometimes get bored-dropped, but the ones who comply seem to feed it.

I don't think it's quite the Kafka trap thing, where denying the accusation is taken as further proof of guilt. Here it's the conceding that's taken as proof, which sounds like the opposite but I have a nagging feeling it's the same machine pointed the other way. And it's not a heckler's veto either, I don't think, because there's no specific heckler with a concrete demand you could meet. It's also not just a double bind, or if it is, the two horns aren't symmetrical -- one of them (keep quiet) does nothing and the other (engage) actively makes it worse.

Is there a name for this exact configuration? I care way less about the label than about getting the carving right, so if you coin something, please tell me what it is and what it isn't and where the edges are. Is the load-bearing feature the leaderlessness -- no one to accept the apology -- or the recycling of compliance into fresh evidence, or something else entirely? And does it need the online setting at all, or is this the same thing that used to happen in small towns and churches and workplaces, just slower? If it's an old phenomenon with an old name I'd rather have the old name than a new one.
