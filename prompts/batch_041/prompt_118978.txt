I run a monthly discussion night at a community center -- 12-18 people, 90 minutes, mixed ages, nobody in the room does math for a living. Last time we argued for an hour about whether an algorithm can be fair, and it slowly dawned on me that none of us actually knew what we were asking for. So I want a night where they find out by having to do it. Design me a runnable activity: the group has to settle on one hiring/admissions policy off a shared test score, and the rules should be arranged so they walk into the wall themselves -- there's no cutoff, or set of cutoffs, that satisfies everyone's standard at once, and they should be the ones to discover that, not me. No lecture up front, and no names of theorems until the debrief.

Here's the setup I want you to use, poke holes in it if it doesn't work: 400 applicants, two groups A and B, 200 each. In A, 100 would actually do well at the job; in B, 50 would. The test is equally good in both groups -- it catches 80% of the people who'd do well and wrongly flags 20% of the people who wouldn't, same rates in A and B, no funny business. Three or four teams, each handed one fairness standard to defend in plain words, told to pick whatever cutoff best serves it: (1) among the people you let in, the share who'd actually do well should be the same in both groups; (2) your mistakes should match across groups, same share of good people rejected and same share of bad people admitted; (3) let in the same share of each group; (4) whatever guarantee you give for A and B has to keep holding when you break each group down by some other trait, say part-time vs full-time experience -- make up whatever sub-numbers you need for that one. Then the whole room has to merge into a single policy, and that's where it should get uncomfortable.

I'm definately not an academic and I've been chewing on this on my own, so give it to me as a JSON object with these fields and nothing outside it:

- run_sheet: array of {minutes, phase, what_i_do, what_they_do, materials}
- handouts: array of {team, standard, what_they_compute_on_paper, what_they_find}
- the_crunch: the moment the room has to pick one policy, and why it can't have everything
- debrief: array of {moment_in_the_activity, the_idea_it_lands}
- failure_modes: array of {what_goes_wrong_the_first_time, how_to_handle_it}
- cannot_reproduce: what the activity honestly can't show, that I have to say out loud myself
- notes: anything that didn't fit the fields
