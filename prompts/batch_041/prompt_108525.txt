I am trying to decide whether the naturalistic fallacy names a single mistake or a family of them, and I would like to work from my own verdicts on particular inferences rather than from the textbook statement of the doctrine. Each of the following strikes me as a good inference: from "the heart's function is to pump blood" to "a heart that cannot pump is malfunctioning"; from "this knife is blunt" to "this is a poor knife"; from "you want to catch the five o'clock train and it leaves at five" to "you ought to leave now"; from "the rules of chess forbid moving into check" to "you ought not to move there"; from "you promised to be there" to "you ought to be there." Each of the following strikes me as a bad inference: from "humans are naturally aggressive" to "war is acceptable"; from "everyone in this town disapproves of it" to "one ought not to do it"; from "believing it would make you happy" to "you ought to believe it"; from "this is what our ancestors did" to "this is what we ought to do." And one case I cannot place: from "this causes suffering" to "this is wrong" I find myself accepting, though by the pattern of the others I should not.

My tentative proposal is that an is-premise can carry an ought-conclusion only when it supplies a standard -- a function, a want, a practice, a promise -- and the ought it yields is relative to that standard and never categorical. Please test that proposal against the cases, repair it or replace it if it fails, say what it implies for cases I have not listed (an aesthetic verdict, a legal obligation, an instrumental ought aimed at a monstrous end), and tell me plainly which of my verdicts it obliges me to abandon. Keep the answer under 600 words.
