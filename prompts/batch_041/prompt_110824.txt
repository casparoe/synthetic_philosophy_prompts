I am assembling a two-year postdoctoral position on the question of why there is something rather than nothing, and I want to write the call around a defensible research agenda rather than a topic label. The holder will be a single philosopher working alone — no lab, no computation, no collaborators — so the operative question is what one person can make genuine argumentative progress on in two years. Assume the aim is to advance the arguments rather than to gather further empirical input, and do not give me a reading list; I know the literature.

What I want is a prioritized agenda: which sub-questions within this problem most deserve the two years, in what order, and on what grounds.

Work within the following structure. First, treat the problem as a family rather than a single question: why there is any contingent thing at all; why there is anything concrete rather than only abstract; why there are these things rather than others; and whether there being nothing at all is even a coherent alternative whose non-actualization could call for explanation. Second, organize the agenda around what I will call regress-termination profiles: for each candidate answer, state precisely what is supposed to be non-derivative — a thing, a fact, a principle, a law, or nothing — and whether that stopping point answers the question or merely relocates it. Third, apply a self-application constraint: an answer must either explain itself or exempt itself, and I suspect the exemption is usually smuggled in; tell me how much weight that constraint can bear. Fourth, assess whether the modal collapse worry is fatal: if the answer posits something that necessitates everything else, then nothing could have failed to exist, and the apparent contingency that generated the question evaporates; I want to know whether that is a genuine cost of such answers or a confusion about what was being asked.

Defend the ordering using explicit criteria — upstreamness, tractability for one philosopher in two years, how thoroughly a line has already been worked, and whether a resolution would change anything elsewhere in metaphysics or philosophy of physics. Tell me which sub-questions I should decline to fund, and what argument or result in the next two years would move a line of work up or down the list. Where a line depends on contested physics — whether the universe had a beginning, whether a quantum vacuum counts as nothing, whether a no-boundary style proposal dissolves the question — say so, and say how much of the philosophical work can proceed without settling the physics.
