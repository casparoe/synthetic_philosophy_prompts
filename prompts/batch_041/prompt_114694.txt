I run the tradecraft review desk for a small all-source analysis team. Every product we release states a confidence level for each judgment on a five-band probability yardstick (roughly 5, 20, 50, 80 and 95 per cent); a typical assessment carries thirty to sixty separate judgments, each sourced independently and each written to be defensible on its own; and the products go to readers who commit money and personnel on the strength of them. Two problems keep recurring, and I have to issue a standing procedure covering both. First, our judgments are individually sound, yet any product of this size is known on base rates alone to contain at least one error, and readers treat the report as a package and ask what our confidence is in the report. Second, we sometimes need to say that one of a set of mutually exclusive possibilities will occur when no single member of the set rises above the bottom band, and analysts then try to convert the disjunction into an actionable claim about one member. Two of my analysts have offered mechanical fixes: take the product of the per-judgment confidences as the report's confidence, or take the minimum. The product is a reductio, since 0.95 to the fortieth is about 0.13, below our lowest band; the minimum is a reductio in the other direction. I need to know what belongs in their place.

Please write the procedure: the questions a reviewer asks in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops with a no, where it hands the decision back to a person because the question is one of values rather than evidence, and what the procedure cannot do at all. It has to be runnable in an afternoon by someone with no philosophy and no statistics beyond arithmetic, and it has to tell the reviewer which parts of a judgment must go into the text so that a reader cannot mistake the whole for the sum of the parts. Then run it on three cases: a report of forty judgments all at the 95 per cent band; a warning problem in which we are confident that one of nine routes will be used but no single route exceeds the 20 per cent band; and a case in which the procedure forces us to tell a reader something we would rather not. I do not need the lottery paradox and the preface paradox explained to me; I need the translation, including what is lost in it. Take a definite position on which resolution of them the procedure should be built on, and defend that choice rather than laying the options out for me.
