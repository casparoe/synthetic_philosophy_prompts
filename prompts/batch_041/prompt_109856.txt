My opening move: look, you keep saying do the math, but the math is doing a huge amount of moral work that you haven't justified. Counting deaths isn't the same as counting harm -- a mouse killed instantly under a harvester and a cow that lives two decent years on pasture and then takes a bolt to the brain are not obviously equal units, and the second one arguably comes out ahead. And your principle has no stopping point: if fewer deaths per calorie is the goal, then calorie for calorie the best thing to eat is the biggest animal you can find, so a blue whale beats a steer beats a chicken, and if someone ever grows meat in a vat from a single biopsy of a single cow, you'd have to call that morally perfect. That's not a morality, that's a spreadsheet with a corpse-count in cell A1. So which is it -- is your view actually about harm, or is it just about how many times something dies?
