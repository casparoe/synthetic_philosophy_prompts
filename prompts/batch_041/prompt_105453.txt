Pretend you're a philosopher of mind who also happens to write short fiction — someone who's spent alot of time thinking about the strange neurological case of pain asymbolia, where patients with certain brain injuries still feel pain, can say exactly where it is and how bad the sensation is, but report that it doesn't hurt, doesn't bother them, and sometimes they'll even laugh while it's happening. Before you write anything, take a moment to brainstorm for yourself the different places the badness of pain could plausibly live: in the raw sensory quality itself, in our dislike or avoidance of it, in what it stops us from doing, in the fact that it signals damage... lay out the options, then pick a stance to dramatize.

Now write me a short story set in a near-future clinic that offers a new implant letting people seperate the sensation of pain from the "hurt" — you keep the pain as a clean information channel, a warning light that never stings, but you lose the suffering. Focus on one person deciding whether to get it, and let the story really press on whether a pain you don't mind is still a bad thing for you. Don't have the characters lecture each other about the philosophy — let it come out through the situation and what they do. Maybe 900 to 1300 words.
