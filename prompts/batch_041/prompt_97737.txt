I've been going down the acausal trade rabbit hole lately with a couple of friends from a rationality reading group (we meet at a bar, which is maybe why the arguements get so heated, and I've become the guy who derails every conversation into decision theory). Anyway, I've ended up fairly convinced that acausal trade is real in a stronger sense than people usually grant. My view is that it isn't some exotic quirk of toy decision problems -- it's a fairly general feature of rational agency: any two agents that can model each other's reasoning well enough, and whose interests overlap enough, will tend to act as if they'd struck a bargain, purely because each predicts that the other predicts it, and so on down the line. I think this covers the usual cases (two AI systems trained in separate rooms, an AI and the humans who might build it, maybe even a civilization and some alien one it can never contact), and I even suspect the whole common-priors business is a bit of a distraction -- I think the real engine is just mutual predictability plus overlapping preferences, with acausal trade falling out as a special case of a broader principle I've been calling predictive alignment.

What I want from you is an honest attempt to talk me out of it, or at least to map how exposed the view really is. What would have to be true for acausal trade to be basically a non-thing -- and I don't just mean "but does it ever actually pay off," I mean the load-bearing assumptions (common priors, logical omniscience, whatever decision theory the whole thing needs, the setup itself) and what would falsify each of them. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward: tell me which parts of my predictive-alignment generalization are doing illegitimate work, what the defenders of acausal trade would themselves concede counts against them, which standard objections are cheap shots I can safely ignore, and whether the position has been qualified into something that couldn't be false even in principle. If you think it's been insulated, say so plainly, and tell me whether that's actually a problem for a view like this or just the price of doing business in this area.
