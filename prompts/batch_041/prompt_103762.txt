I hold a specific version of the coherent-extrapolated-volition view: for any agent or group there is a unique fixed point of an idealization procedure that corrects factual ignorance, enforces logical and probabilistic coherence, and expands sympathetic concern; the fixed point is invariant under the order in which those corrections are applied and is itself endorsed by the idealized agent. What would count against this position? I want the genuine defeaters, not the familiar complaint that extrapolation is vague: formal non-convergence or multiplicity results, cases where the fixed point is empty or trivial, order-dependence or manipulability, and any reason to think idealized endorsement cannot be connected to the agent's actual good. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, identify which a committed defender would concede, and say whether the view has insulated itself from criticism.
