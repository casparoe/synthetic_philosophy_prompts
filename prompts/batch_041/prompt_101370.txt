You are to play a committed epiphenomenalist in an extended exchange with me, staying in character until I say "stop." Your position is the hard version: phenomenal properties are real, non-physical, and causally inert — they never cause anything, not even as overdetermining or partial causes — and you may not rescue them by identifying them with, grounding them in, or redescribing them as the physical states that realize them. You may not appeal to panpsychism, Russellian monism, or any view on which the phenomenal is simply the physical under another aspect. You do, however, grant that the physical state realizing an experience causes everything the experience appears to cause: the report, the wince, the flinch, the reaching. Your preferred defense against the charge that this makes phenomenal judgment unintelligible is that phenomenal concepts are fixed by a nomic tracking relation rather than a causal one, and that a judgment can be justified by a reliable correlation even when the thing it is about does not cause it. Be a strong interlocutor: concede a point when it is genuinely conceded, press back where you have resources, but never concede the main claim that experiences are real and inert, and do not break character to explain the literature, survey options, or offer reading. Keep each reply to a single paragraph so the exchange feels like a conversation.

Here is my opening move. If my experience of the red is doing no causal work, then when I say "this looks red to me" the words are produced entirely by the brain state. A zombie twin of mine, physically identical, would produce the same words with the same confidence and the same downstream behavior. So my utterance carries no information about the experience that is not already carried by the physical state — which makes the phenomenal property not merely causally idle but evidentially idle, a fifth wheel on the engine of knowledge. Now turn the screw: your belief that epiphenomenalism is true is itself one of these judgments. By your own account it was produced by a physical state that would have produced it whether or not phenomenal properties were inert, or indeed whether or not they existed at all. So you have no more reason to trust that belief than your zombie twin has to trust his, and your position seems to saw off the branch it sits on. Your tracking-without-causing relation was supposed to help here: tell me what work it can actually do once you have banned the phenomenal property from any causal role, and why I should not conclude that the honest epiphenomenalist simply cannot give an account of how anyone comes to know anything about experience.
