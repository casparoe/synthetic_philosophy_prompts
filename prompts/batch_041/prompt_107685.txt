ok, I've been reading a bunch of outside-view / base-rate stuff and I want to actually use it for one specific job: forecasting how long states, regimes and empires last. And I keep hitting the same wall. Every account tells you to "find the reference class" and take its base rate, and then nobody says what makes one reference class better than another. Pick states, you get one number. Pick states of a certain size and era, another. Pick states ruled by one dynasty right after a civil war, a third. It's infinitely malleable and I suspect the malleability is doing more work than the method admits.

So before I read any more of this stuff, write me the desiderata. What does any adequate account of reference-class selection have to deliver? I want the requirements list, each one with the case or consideration behind it, and your honest call on which are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp.

Things I'm half-convinced belong on the list, so take a stance on each rather than just repeating them back: (1) the class has to be fixed before you know the outcome, so no shopping for the class that happens to fit; (2) the members have to be exchangeable with the target in the relevant respect, which means you owe me an account of what "relevant respect" is; (3) it has to survive the fact that the historical record is itself a biased sample — the states that left us records are the ones that lasted long enough to keep archives, so base rates read off the record may be biased toward longevity and I'd like to know how much that matters; (4) it has to cope with class drift, where the criteria defining membership change over time, so you're averaging over a moving target; (5) it has to cope with failures that aren't independent draws — one hegemonic war can end five empires at once, which wrecks the arithmetic that treats the class as n tickets from an urn.

And flag any pairs on that list I can't have together. I have a feeling (1) and "use all the information you have" are in tension, and (4) and (2) might be straight-up incompatible, but tell me if I'm wrong.

One more thing I want the list to confront, and this is the part I care most about: I suspect alot of this is broken because people treat the class as a set of draws when the things in it are trajectories with internal clocks. A thirty-year-old state and a three-hundred-year-old state aren't two tickets from the same urn. If that's right then a single base rate for the class is the wrong object entirely and what you want is a hazard profile conditioned on age or phase, which means the "class" isn't a set of similar cases so much as a family of trajectories. Is that a genuine requirement, or is it my prejudice? If it's genuine, say what it knocks out.

Then tell me: which single requirement on the list does the most damage to the naive practice of "get the base rate, then nudge it with your inside-view reasoning"? Seperate that out from the rest, I want to see how much of the practice it takes down.
