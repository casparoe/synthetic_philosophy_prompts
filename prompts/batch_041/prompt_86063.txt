A friend of mine runs a undergrad cognitive science reading group and I said I'd do the session on those experiments where the brain seems to make the call before you do — Libet's setup, the readiness potential, all that. I dont want to just lecture at them though. I want to hand out a decision tree: everyone starts at the top accepting the basic timing result (a brain signal ramps up a few hundred milliseconds before I'm even aware of deciding — I think it's around a third of a second, don't quote me on the number), and then by answering questions about what that does and doesn't show, they land on a labelled position. Please write me that tree, something like 5-7 nodes and 6-9 leaves.

Things I want built in, because otherwise it just turns into the usual 'so is free will real then' mush:

- The nodes have to be questions someone can answer without already knowing where the branch leads. So no node like 'are you a compatibilist?'. They should be about the case. For instance, one node should split the three claims people constantly run together: that my conscious intention shows up late, that my conscious intention isn't what causes the movement, and that the movement wasn't decided by me at all.
- One branch has to take seriously the people who think the readiness potential is a artefact — that averaging a pile of noisy trials manufactures a smooth ramp, and there's nothing there until the thing tips over the threshold. I want that as a real leaf, not a footnote, and I also want it to be possible to hold it while still thinking the experiment is interesting for other reasons.
- One branch on the 'the signal is real but it's an urge or a bias, not a decision' move — maybe the ramp is the build-up of a tendency to move rather than a decision to move, and those aren't the same thing.
- One on whether the action is still yours if the decision was unconscious — the 'it was still me, just not the part of me that narrates' answer.
- One on the veto: whether the interesting capacity is starting an action or calling it off.
- And one on whether the relevant unit is the moment of initiation or the standing policy/character that produced it, since I think that's where a lot of the compatibilist moves actually live and I want them located rather than smeared over everything.

The leaves should be named positions where a name exists — I don't need citations, just the label, e.g. epiphenomenalist about conscious will, the view that the felt experience of deciding is a construction rather than a report of a cause, the veto/'free won't' line, the compatibilist who thinks the experiment tests a kind of freedom nobody should care about, the hard incompatibilist who says the data is beside the point because the argument never needed it, the artefact deflationist, and so on — and 'unnamed' where nobody defends it. Each leaf gets one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Two extra things. Flag any leaves my tree can't actually separate from each other; I'd rather know than have it papered over. And tell me which combinations of answers nobody seems to hold, and whether that's because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Keep the jargon off the nodes and only on the leaves, since the students shouldn't need to know any names to get through it.
