I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, writing a chapter on whether unity of behaviour and unity of information between the hemispheres are sufficient for unity of consciousness. What I need from you is a case designed to order, together with a demonstration that it does what I ask; I would rather have that than a survey of the literature, and I don't need citations or reading lists.

The direction I want is the difficult one. Ordinarily the inference runs from disunity — competing actions, non-transferring information, divergent reports — to the conclusion that there are two streams of experience. I want a case in the family of the split-brain cases in which the subject is behaviourally and informationally unified, passing every standard test, and in which there is nevertheless an independent case for two streams of consciousness. Everything that usually carries that inference should be absent or neutralised, and the two-streams verdict must be earned rather than stipulated. The two positions whose verdicts I need you to generate are these: (A) co-consciousness is constituted by joint availability to a single system, so integration of the relevant kind settles the matter in favour of one stream; (B) joint availability is not sufficient for co-consciousness, so a subject may be integrated and still have two streams.

Constraints:

- The disconnection may be surgical or otherwise, but the case must be clinically and physically plausible, with no appeal to new physics and no stipulated mental facts. Where you lean on a real phenomenon — cross-cueing, spared or subcortical routes, atypical connectivity — state what you are assuming, and do not rest the argument on a contested claim about which tracts carry what.
- The scenario itself in a single paragraph. Assume the reporting channel is unilateral, so that anything the patient says about her experience is produced by one hemisphere; the case must therefore either specify an evidential route by which we could in principle learn something about the other stream, or explain why there is none and what that costs the argument.
- The case must not be idle between (A) and (B). It should contain one feature that gives the two-streams reading an independent foothold and one that gives the single-stream reading a foothold, and (A) and (B) must disagree about the outcome of some intervention that you specify.
- Fix the mechanism of inter-hemispheric exchange exactly, so that both readings can agree on the description of what happens even as they disagree about what it amounts to.

Then:

1. Set out the scenario.
2. Give each of (A) and (B) its verdict and the reasoning that generates it.
3. Identify the single feature that does the work, and say what becomes of each verdict when that feature is varied or removed.
4. Give the strongest attack a defender of (A) could make on your case, and your reply to it.
5. Finish by saying which consideration would change your mind about whether the case succeeds, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
