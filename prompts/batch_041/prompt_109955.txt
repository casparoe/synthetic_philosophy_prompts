I'm in the second half of a graduate seminar on distributive justice, and we have spent the past two weeks on the levelling-down objection to prioritarianism — the charge that a view ranking outcomes by how well off the worst off are cannot call it an improvement to pull the better off down when no one gains by it. I keep offering verdicts in section that my classmates insist do not hang together, and before I write my response paper I would like to know whether they do. Confiscating a thousand dollars from someone earning a million and burning it is worse than doing nothing at all — not merely wasteful, but a real worsening of the world, even though no one ends up worse off than they were. Confiscating the same thousand and handing it to someone earning ten thousand is plainly good. Abolishing a school's honour roll, or a firm's published productivity ranking, strikes me as an improvement even when I stipulate that no one's income, health, or felt happiness changes at all — the ranking itself was the harm, and I want to say the same of a village that quietly stops holding its beauty contest. But when I imagine a tax that simply leads the top earner to work less and earn less while everyone else is untouched, I feel nothing whatever; I cannot bring myself to say the world got worse. When I try to justify the honour-roll verdict I find I am treating a comparative advantage as intrinsically bad, which is precisely what I denied in the burning-money case, and a classmate tells me I have quietly switched to a view on which equality is a matter of how people stand to one another rather than of how much each of them has.

Please tell me what principle, or what small set of principles, captures these verdicts, and be candid about which of them I would have to give up. I would like exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion: the principle or principles themselves; the case that forces each clause of it; your verdicts on two further cases I have not mentioned; the verdict of mine that it cannot accommodate; and, finally, whether you would keep every one of my verdicts with a more complicated principle or drop one for a cleaner account, together with your recommendation.
