So I've been reading up on Grice's stuff about how we work out what people mean beyond the literal words, and I think I've stumbled into an argument that shows the whole thing can never get off the ground. Problem is the conclusion is nuts, so I know a step is bad, but I cant figure out which one. Hoping you can help.

Here it is.

(1) The way you figure out what a speaker is getting at past the literal words — the implicature — is by assuming they're being cooperative and following the maxims (be relevant, be informative, don't say stuff you think is false, be clear, etc.), then asking what they'd have to have meant for the utterance to make sense.

(2) But before you can do any of that, you have to know what they actually said. Not the words themselves — the proposition. Which thing "it" picks out, which sense of an ambiguous word is in play, what time "now" picks out, that kind of thing.

(3) And figuring out which thing "it" picks out, or which sense of an ambiguous word, is not something the words alone hand you. "Meet me at the bank" doesn't tell you whether it's the riverbank or the money place. You have to work out which one the speaker plausibly meant.

(4) But "work out which one the speaker plausibly meant" is exactly the reasoning in (1). You're asking what a cooperative speaker in this situation would have to have intended for the utterance to make sense.

(5) So in order to run the reasoning in (1) at all, you already have to have run the reasoning in (1). It's circular. It never gets started.

(6) And if it never gets started, you can never pin down what was said, so you can never get an implicature either, so you can never understand anybody.

(7) But we understand each other constantly. I understood like six people before I finished my coffee.

So (7) is clearly true and (5) seems to follow, so something in (1)-(4) has to give. Where does it actually break, and why did the bad step look so reasonable? I'd most like to keep (1) and (7). If I have to give up (3) or (4), tell me what that costs me — what I'd owe in its place.
