Bit of a weird one, hope this is the right place to ask. I'm not an academic, I do ops at a small company and I sit on our hiring panel, and I've also been buying way too much stuff online this year, and the same shape keeps turning up in both and I can't find a word for it. The shopping one first. I wanted a coffee grinder. There were about six hundred reviews, mostly four and five stars, and I read the first twenty or so and they were genuinely thoughtful — different reasons, different grinders they compared it to, different complaints — and I felt like I was reading the pooled experience of hundreds of people. Then a friend who owns the same grinder told me she bought it because it had six hundred good reviews. When I pressed her, she hadn't read any of them; she'd looked at the stars and the number. And it hit me that the later reviewers probably decided the same way. So the six hundred isn't six hundred people's worth of experience. The first handful did the actual work and everyone after them is the same evidence wearing different hats. The annoying part is you can't tell from the outside, because nobody writes "I bought this because everyone else did" — the later reviews read exactly like the early ones, same length, same little details. So: is there a name for a crowd whose evidence has stopped piling up while its output still looks like it's piling up? And separately, is there a name for the mistake I made, counting the size of the crowd as if it were more testimony?

The hiring thing is the same shape but I think there's a difference that matters. We interview in a fixed order and then discuss, and I've noticed that by the time the fourth person speaks they almost never push back on what's already been said, even when they walked in with a strong view — I've literally watched a colleague flip in real time, and later she couldn't really say why. The difference from the grinder is that in the meeting everyone hears the actual reasons, one after another, and in the grinder case everyone only ever sees a number, so no one's reasons get aired at all, the whole thing gets squashed into a statistic that the next person just takes as given and moves on from. Does that difference have its own name, or is it the same phenomenon at a coarser resolution? I keep feeling the second one deserves its own term — the crowd's knowledge compressed into one figure, so nothing can ever get un-said, and the next person's contribution is really just a vote to keep the figure where it is.

What I'm really after is the carving, not the label, so please don't just hand me a name and stop. If the honest answer is that this is only the ordinary thing where people copy each other, tell me why, and tell me where the edges are: what would have to be true for six hundred reviews to actually be worth six hundred people's worth of experience, is there a term for the case where a group's agreement is really just agreement and not an echo, and is there a term for the state of affairs where the usual giveaways of copying are absent so a reader has no way to check? I'd be just as happy with a made-up name as a real one, as long as you define it and tell me what it excludes.
