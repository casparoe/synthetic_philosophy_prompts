Ok, so I'm grading a stack of short essays for a 300-level philosophy of religion course and I want a seperate read on this one before I commit to a grade. The question on the assignment was:

"Freud's The Future of an Illusion is often boiled down to the claim that religion is an illusion. Explain what Freud means by calling religion an illusion, why he thinks that diagnosis matters for how we should treat religious belief, and what he expects to happen to religion going forward. Be precise about how illusion relates to delusion and to error."

Here's the student's answer:

"Freud argues that religion is an illusion, by which he means a belief that is false and is held in the teeth of the evidence against it. On his account, religious doctrines are not the product of reasoning about the world but of emotional needs. Human beings are helpless before nature and death, and this helplessness revives the child's feeling of dependence on the father. God is, in effect, the exalted father figure, and belief in him is a wish-fulfilment: we want there to be a protector, so we believe there is one. Freud says this makes religion the universal obsessional neurosis of humanity, which is to say that religious ritual and the compulsive rituals of neurotics are literally the same thing, and that both should be treated the same way.

Because the illusion is a delusion, it's in direct conflict with reality. Freud thinks that as science advances the illusion will lose its hold, since the intellect can be trained to check the wishes that produce it. He talks about the primacy of the intellect, and expects that eventually people will give up religion the way an individual gives up a childhood neurosis after analysis. He also thinks the moral teachings of religion can be retained without the beliefs that originally carried them, since we can now see the social reasons for the prohibitions.

He admits that the masses may not be able to sustain morality without the threat of divine punishment, but he thinks this is a temporary problem that education will solve. The overall picture is Enlightenment optimism: religion is a stage of human development, and we are outgrowing it."
