My mate Dan and I spent about two hours arguing about the Anthropocene last night and now I want to turn it into a flowchart before we pick it up again, because the whole time we kept thinking we disagreed about the start date when we actually disagreed about who gets to decide, and vice versa.

Here's the setup, roughly, so you don't have to guess at it: the word got popularised around 2000 by an atmospheric chemist who basically meant "since the industrial revolution, late 1700s"; there's a camp that says early farmers were already shifting greenhouse gases about 8000 years ago; there's a 1610 camp pointing at the dip in atmospheric CO2 after disease collapsed populations in the Americas and forests grew back over the farmland; a working group spent about fifteen years on it and in 2023 formally proposed an epoch starting in 1952, marked by plutonium from bomb tests, in the sediments of a lake in Canada; and in 2024 the stratigraphy bodies voted the proposal down and the international geological union confirmed the no, while adding that everyone would keep using the word anyway. Two bits of jargon you'll want: a "golden spike" (GSSP) is a boundary pinned to a physical marker in one specific rock or sediment section, whereas a GSSA is a boundary where you just agree on a date, which is what geologists do for deep time where there's no usable signal in the rock. And there's a third option in the air: don't make it a unit at all, make it an "event" -- a brief, sharp, high-magnitude thing that doesn't need a boundary point or a date.

What I want is a sorting instrument, not an essay. Build the logical space first and put the names in second: start from the questions that actually split people, and only then locate the positions in the branches. Every node has to be answerable by someone who doesn't already know which label it leads to, so no node like "are you a Capitalocene person?". And keep the two axes seperate -- which marker or date, and who has standing to fix it. If a leaf can sit with either of two answers about authority, say so instead of forcing it.

Leaves I want to see, at minimum: industrial revolution; early farming around 8000 BP; the 1610 CO2 dip; the mid-20th century Great Acceleration with the plutonium spike; the end-Pleistocene megafaunal extinctions; the broiler chicken / plastics / technofossils marker (the one where the marker is something we made deliberately); "event, not epoch"; "the name is wrong -- it's capital, or plantations, not 'anthropos'"; "leave it informal, don't ratify anything"; and "this is a political period and stratigraphy is simply the wrong instrument for it". For each leaf, one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Then, at the end: the combinations that are logically available but that nobody defends, with a one-line diagnosis of whether that's because the combo is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored; and any leaves your tree can't really separate, flagged rather than fudged.

No citations, no reading list, no "see the literature" -- I can't look anything up right now and neither can you, so argue from the structure. Whole thing under 900 words, tree first, notes after.
