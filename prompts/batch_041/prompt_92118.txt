Please give me a careful, self-contained explanation of the Newman problem for epistemic structural realism, together with an assessment of whether the standard repairs to the view actually work.

Epistemic structural realism asks us to be realists about the structure of our best scientific theories while withholding belief about the intrinsic natures of the unobservable entities those theories posit: what we are entitled to believe, on this view, is that the world instantiates certain relations, not what the relata are in themselves. The Newman problem is the charge that this position, once made precise, collapses into near-triviality. The objection was first pressed against a Russellian version of the view, and it runs roughly as follows. If a theory is rendered as a Ramsey sentence -- existentially quantifying over its theoretical terms so that the theory asserts only that there exist objects and relations standing in the pattern the theory specifies -- then the resulting sentence is satisfied by any domain of the right cardinality, since any collection of sufficiently many objects can be assigned relations that realise an arbitrary pattern. If so, the structural realist's epistemic commitment amounts to little more than a claim about how many things there are, which is not the sort of content that could explain the success of science or survive theory change.

I would like the following explained. First, why the Ramsey-sentence reconstruction of a theory seems to license this collapse, stated in a way that makes the underlying combinatorial point intuitive rather than merely formal. Second, why the charge, if it succeeds, is fatal rather than merely embarrassing -- that is, what exactly the structural realist needed the appeal to structure to accomplish, and why triviality would defeat that purpose. Third, whether the familiar escape routes genuinely help. Consider in particular: restricting the commitment to relations that are natural or otherwise privileged rather than arbitrary; denying that the objection applies once a principled distinction between observational and theoretical vocabulary is in place; and shifting from an epistemic to an ontic reading of structure, on which structure is not something we know about the world but something the world is. For each, say what work the move is doing and whether it evades the problem or merely relocates it.

Keep the discussion self-contained. I am not looking for a survey of the literature or for pointers to who said what, but for a tight, opinionated reconstruction that ends by saying which of these responses, if any, survives, and why.
