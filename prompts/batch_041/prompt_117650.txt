I'm a novelist — two books out, a third due in the spring and currently stalled at 61,000 words in a condition I'd charitably call a draft. Last month a friend of mine who works in AI safety (he does something with evals, I've stopped asking) tried to explain to me, in a loud bar, a picture he said a lot of people in his field think with. As I understood it: take some big task too hard for one person to do in one sitting, factor it into a pile of small tasks that are each easy enough for an ordinary person to do, then combine the answers; and then the fancy version is that the person doing the factoring is allowed to consult a more capable version of themselves about the hard bits, and that version is allowed to consult a more capable version of itself, and so on down (or up) forever, so the whole thing is just an infinite tower of ordinary people asking someone a little smarter what to do. He called the first part factored cognition and had an acronym for the second, HCH, which I have since forgotten and probably got wrong. I nodded, changed the subject, and have been thinking about it ever since, because it is more or less a description of how I write, or at any rate how I pretend to write.

What I want from you: restate that picture — the factoring thesis and the infinite tower of consultation — as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use, which is the vocabulary of making a novel. Outlines and scene cards, drafting out of order, structural passes versus line edits, POV, continuity, handing chapter twelve to a co-writer, giving a draft to a beta reader or an editor, cutting the sentences you love, the way you can only fix the opening once you know the ending and only fix the ending once you know the opening. Do it piece by piece rather than as a vibe: here's factoring, said in my terms; here's consultation, said in my terms; here's what the two together amount to. Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Which parts come across cleanly? Which parts I can only say by distorting them, and what gets squashed in the distortion? Which parts have no counterpart in my world at all — I suspect the assumption that a task has subtasks whose answers can be checked and added up doesn't survive contact with a novel, since a paragraph's whole quality depends on the book it's in, though possibly I'm telling myself that so I don't have to outline. And then the reverse: what I do that their picture can't say. Then the real question, which is whether the stuff that won't translate means my vocabulary is missing something they have, or means their idealization is thinner than it sounds and I've been doing something they haven't yet found a way to talk about.

For what it's worth I'm not after a reading list or citations — I can't follow up on any of it anyway, and I'd rather you just tell me what you think than point me at things. I don't need this to be definately right, I need it to be honest. Say how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the one point you're least sure about.
