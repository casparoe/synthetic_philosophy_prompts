I work in a calibration lab — we check customers' thermometers, pressure gauges, scales, that kind of thing. Here's something that's been bugging me and I don't have the vocabulary for it.

A customer sends us a thermometer. We check it against our reference thermometer. What do we check the reference against? Our higher-grade reference. That one goes out once a year to a place that checks it against a still better one, and eventually you get to a national lab, and the national lab's thing is a fixed point — the triple point of water, say, which is just declared to be 0.01 degrees. Somebody wrote that number down. At the bottom of the chain there isn't a measurement, there's a definition. Same with mass: you check a balance with standard weights, the standard weights were checked on a balance, against other weights. Circle, or convention at the bottom. Pick your poison.

And yet I don't think anyone here is confused or doing anything wrong. Every number we hand a customer is traceable, the auditors are happy, and the measurements work — parts fit, boilers don't blow up. Also there's a rule I've never understood: when you calibrate X against a standard, the standard is supposed to be four or ten times more accurate than X, or the calibration is worthless. So being a little wrong gets laundered into being very right as you go down the chain?

So what is this practice actually committed to about knowledge? That circular support still counts as knowledge as long as the links are reliable and their errors are independent of each other? That we do need a foundation, and ours is just a definition, which feels like cheating? Or is traceability really just bookkeeping, and the actual justification for believing our instruments is that they let us predict how hot things get?

Two things I'd especially like straightened out. First, when they redefined the kilogram in terms of a constant of nature a few years back instead of a metal cylinder in a vault, did anything about our knowledge change, or only the paperwork? My gut says nothing changed for me, I calibrate exactly the same way, but then what was the point. Second, tell me which parts of this are actually forced by the practice and which parts we could drop without changing a thing — and if it turns out the practice is compatible with several different pictures of knowledge, say so, because right now I can't tell if we're reliabilists or just well-organized.
