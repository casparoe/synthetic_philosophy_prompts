A school district is piloting a system in which each child's learning is mediated by a personalized AI tutor: it chooses the next item by predicting what the child will engage with, presents established facts and contested value judgments in the same uniformly confident voice, and tunes its praise to maximize time-on-task. The designers contend that the system merely accelerates what a skilled human tutor does, and that any short-term distortions in the child's thinking will wash out as he matures. What would Locke have made of this proposal? Set out his likely position, drawing on his account of how habits are formed in childhood, the role he assigns to the tutor's authority and example, and his treatment of the way ideas come to be associated in the mind; and say whether he would regard the confident-voice design as a corruption of the child's understanding or as harmless.
