Im about 200 pages into a novel that has a story inside it — my narrator is writing a book about a woman who is writing a book about him, something like that — and I keep coming back to that Escher print where the two hands are drawing each other. Somebody once told me the interesting thing about it isn't the loop, its that for the loop to close at all there has to be a level that isn't in the loop: the guy standing outside the paper, who isn't part of the picture but is what makes the picture's little circle possible. I think they called it the inviolate level. Anyway, I want to lay out what I actually believe about this and have you tell me if it hangs together, because a friend read my outline and said two of these can't both be true and a third makes it worse, and I honestly can't see where the problem is.

(1) Inside a book, levels can genuinely tangle — the frame and the thing it frames can each really be the source of the other, with no privileged level inside the fiction. That isn't a trick, it's the actual structure of some books.

(2) But no real book is like that from the outside. There's always an inviolate level — the author, or just the printed page — that isn't in the tangle and is what lets the tangle exist. So the loop is always something the book shows, never something the book is.

(3) I don't think the author's intention is where the meaning lives. Meaning gets built by the reader as they go, and two readers can make two different books out of the same pages.

(4) I want the nesting to be load-bearing, not a gimmick: the inner story has to change what the outer story means, otherwise why bother.

So which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each one really — worst first, with the cheapest fix for each? And if I have to give one up to keep the book I'm actually writing, tell me which one and what it costs me. I'd rather keep all four but I know something has to give.
