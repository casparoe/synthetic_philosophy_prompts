I host a small interview podcast on metaphysics, and the next episode is with a philosopher of science whose account of dispositions is deliberately out of step with the way the debate is usually run. I'd like a set of interview questions built around her actual position rather than around the standard literature. Her view, as she states it: to ascribe a disposition is to attribute a causal power; the power is individuated by its manifestation type together with the intrinsic conditions under which it can be exercised; and the truth of the ascription is settled by the presence of that power, not by any counterfactual about what the object would do if the stimulus occurred. Counterfactuals are symptoms of the power, never its truthmaker. On that basis she treats the finkish cases that wrecked the simple conditional analysis as evidence that the conditional was the wrong instrument from the start, and she treats masking cases as uninteresting — the power is present, its enabling conditions simply are not met, and nothing about what would happen follows. Her standing reply, whenever an objection lands, is: "that's a masking case, and I never offered a conditional analysis."

Please draft ten questions, each followed by a single follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. The questions should be answerable out of her own commitments, and should press where her view is actually weakest rather than where the textbooks press. I do not want questions that ask her to survey the literature, name papers, or recommend readings; and no question should presuppose that either of us has read any particular article.

I specifically want:

- A question that forces the issue of whether the fink/mask distinction can be drawn at all without the counterfactual she rejects — if a fink is a case where the object would lose the power on the stimulus, and a mask a case where it would keep it, her taxonomy seems to borrow the very modality she disowns. Anticipate the reply that the distinction is metaphysical rather than counterfactual, and follow up on it.
- A question built on a case of my own, which she should classify: a molecule whose active site is open when the substrate is absent, but which, on binding the substrate, changes shape in a way that occludes the site, so the reaction never occurs. Nothing is destroyed and no intrinsic property is lost; the very event that would have been the stimulus is what prevents the manifestation. Is that a fink, a mask, or neither, and how does she decide without appealing to what would happen?
- A question about mimics: a system that reliably produces the manifestation without having the power. What in her account rules that out, given that she has given up the conditional that would normally do the ruling out?
- A question about the identification problem: what makes this power the power to produce this manifestation rather than some other one, and can she individuate powers by their enabling conditions without covertly reintroducing conditionals?
- A question about the regress: if the intrinsic enabling conditions are themselves powers, where does the regress stop, and what work is "intrinsic" doing?
- A question about falsifiability: what observation would show that the power is absent rather than masked or permanently unexercised, and if nothing would, what does she take the cost of that to be?
- A question about degrees: can a power be had partially, and what would "partly disposed" mean on her account?
- A question that makes her standing reply hardest to use — namely whether her view and a reformed conditional analysis agree on every possible case, and if they do, what turns on the difference between them.

Order them so that the deflections get progressively harder to sustain, and end with the one question that would leave even a sympathetic listener uneasy. Keep the tone fair and genuinely curious; no gotchas that misrepresent her view. Write in American English.
