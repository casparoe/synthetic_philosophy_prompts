I'm giving a talk in three weeks at our consciousness science center's monthly seminar, and I need a Q&A prep sheet. My thesis is basically Dennett's quining-qualia line, but rebuilt so it doesn't lean on the old verificationist reading people always pin on him: I argue that qualia in the standard sense — intrinsic, private, ineffable, directly and incorrigibly apprehended by the subject — is not a posit we could ever be forced to make, and that every case that's supposed to force it (inverted spectrum, the Chase and Sanborn coffee tasters whose preference flips while they insist the taste didn't change, Mary in the black-and-white room) can be described completely in terms of discrimination capacities, judgments, and reports, with no residue left over. So the Chase and Sanborn case isn't evidence of a constant quale plus a changed attitude; it's evidence that same-taste talk has no determinate application until you say which of several distinct things you mean by it.

The audience: about 20 people. Two hard-problem people who will hear this as changing the subject rather than answering the question, one of whom is a Chalmers-style property dualist and will press the line that I've just defined qualia out of existence. A handful of predictive-processing / active-inference folks who like the deflation but think I'm reinventing their wheel and will ask why I need the word judgment at all. Two vision scientists who run color constancy and metamatching demos and who have actually done a coffee-tasting version of the Chase and Sanborn thing with undergrads, so they'll push on whether my reading matches what subjects actually report. One clinician (anesthesia and pain) who cares about whether patients underreport or misreport experience, and who will ask what my view implies about assessing pain in non-verbal patients. Plus assorted grad students.

Give me the 8 or so objections I'll actually get, in the order they'll come, phrased the way each of these people would phrase them, ranked by how much damage they do. For each: a two-minute reply, and a flag on where the reply is soft or where I'm bluffing. Tell me what to concede up front so I don't look evasive. And include the one question I'm hoping nobody asks — I think its something about whether my own argument depends on an intuition I can't defend on my own terms, but tell me if you think it's something else. Dont bother with citations or reading lists, I just need the prep sheet.
