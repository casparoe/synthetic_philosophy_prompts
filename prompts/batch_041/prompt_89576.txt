I need a designed case, not an illustration, for a seminar on whether behavioral flexibility can ever amount to more than rigidity one level up, and what that would mean for the free-will debate. Build the case on the following framework. Call a routine cue-locked when it runs to completion once triggered and can be deflected by a cue that is irrelevant to the routine's goal, the deflection never registering as a failure. Call a system first-grade sphexish when its first-order routines are cue-locked, and second-grade sphexish when the meta-routine governing when to abandon or revise a first-order routine is itself cue-locked in the same way, so that nothing in the system can register that its policy for giving up has become the trap. Use these terms functionally; the case should not depend on any particular author's coinage or formulation.

Construct one case with the following target. The system is second-grade sphexish but not first-grade: in what it does it is genuinely interruptible, novel, and reason-tracking, while in when it reconsiders it is running a fixed trigger. The case must make two accounts of free will come apart in the reverse of the usual direction — the account normally cast as the friend of freedom must come out against it, and the account normally cast as the skeptic must come out for it:

(a) a reasons-responsiveness account: an action is free when it is produced by a mechanism that is appropriately sensitive to the reasons bearing on it, whatever the provenance of that mechanism;
(b) a source or ultimacy account: an action is free only if the agent is the source of the operative mechanism rather than merely its faithful executor.

On (b) the verdict must be free, and on (a) not free. Note that the two demands pull against each other — (b) wants the agent to have authored the operative mechanism, (a) wants that same mechanism to fail a test it would ordinarily pass — and finding a case that satisfies both is the task. The split must be generated by the structure of the case, not written into the setup.

Constraints. The subject must be a normal adult human being or a machine of comparable behavioral complexity: no insects, no thermostats, no disembodied minds, no demons, and no appeal to indeterminism or quantum chance — hold determinism fixed so that the disagreement cannot be traced to randomness. A reader must be able to see both the first-order flexibility and the second-order trigger rather than take the narrator's word for the machinery. Give the scenario itself in a single paragraph, and characterize each account by its core claim rather than by naming or paraphrasing a particular theorist; do not rest anything on a specific author's formulation.

Then do the work. Walk through why each account returns the verdict it does, being explicit about which mechanism each account treats as the operative one. Identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work. Say what happens if that feature is removed, and whether the case then collapses back into the ordinary pairing of rigidity with unfreedom. Give the strongest line of resistance available to a defender of the account that comes out badly — either that the case is misdescribed, or that the account was never meant to cover a system of this kind.

Commit to a verdict: say which account you think gets the case right and why, and whether the two-grade distinction earns its keep or is a distinction without a difference. Do not close by calling the case ambiguous unless you can point to the exact step at which the ambiguity enters.
