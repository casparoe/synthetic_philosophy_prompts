My mate Dan and I ended up in a two-hour arguement last night about sortition — he's been on a tear ever since he read up on citizens' assemblies, and now he reckons elections are basically a scam and we should pick our legislators the way we pick juries. Honestly I half agree with him, which is exactly why it wound me up so much, and now I want to write something taking the piss out of the whole idea. What I have in mind is a parody job advertisement for the position of Member of the Citizens' Chamber, in a country that's just abolished elections and gone over to a lottery-selected legislature. It should be written in the flat, cheerful voice of a government recruiting office that's trying to make a randomly drawn parliament sound like an ordinary job — careers-page stuff, probation periods, "what we're looking for", performance reviews — and it must never, ever wink at the reader. Every joke has to come out of something real in the debate rather than invented absurdity: the expertise problem (nobody draws their surgeon out of a hat), the fact that you can't be re-elected so nobody can reward or punish you for anything you do, what happens when no one in the room has a mandate and the real agenda-setting gets done by the permanent staff and the lobbyists who'll still be there long after you've gone, whether a stratifed random sample is "representative" in any sense that matters, and the small matter of a twelve-month term that ends before you've finished learning the job. I'd also like the answers sortition fans actually give folded into the HR-speak, so it doesn't read as a hit piece — someone who's read the deliberative-democracy literature should laugh at it without thinking the writer has missed the point. No real politicians or living people, and don't bother with citations.

Give it to me in two parts. First the listing itself, roughly 600-650 words, laid out like a real job ad with a heading and then these subheadings in this order: About the role, Who we're looking for, Terms and conditions, How to apply — with that last one quietly undoing everything above it. Then a second section titled What I was going for, about 150 words, telling me which of the standard objections you planted and where, so I can check that you actually know the debate and haven't just strung together generic anti-politics jokes.
