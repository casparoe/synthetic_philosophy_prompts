theres a pair of moves that always get lumped together under the heading of competence without comprehension, and I want you to pull them apart. first, Darwin's: getting something that looks designed -- an eye, a wing, an organism that fits its niche -- out of a process with no plan and no designer, just variation plus selection, so you no longer have to explain the design by positing a mind that intended it. second, Turing's: a machine that follows rules and turns out flawless arithmetic, or plays a decent game, without having the faintest idea what its doing -- no grasp, no understanding, just symbol-shuffling that happens to come out right. the standard claim is that these are the same kind of inversion, the same dethrowning of the idea that competence requires comprehension.

are they though? compare them properly. where exactly is the inversion in each case -- what actually gets turned around? in Darwin's, is the comprehension really absent, or only relocated (into the selection process, or the environment, or deep time)? in Turing's, is the competence even the same kind of thing -- is a calculator getting the right answer doing arithmetic the way an eye does seeing? and then the part I really care about: what does each inversion do to the other. does Darwin's show that Turing's is just a special case, or do the two come apart at some joint -- say, at whether the resulting competence is real or merely apparent, or at whether theres any fact of the matter about what the system is *for*?

then drag in a third case that usually isnt in this family and tell me whether it belongs: the invisible hand. Smith's picture is self-interested agents, none of them intending the general good, producing a well-ordered market -- competence of a sort, without anyone comprehending it. is that the same inversion as Darwin's, a cousin, or a different thing wearing the same clothes? be honest about the disanalogies, dont just wave at emergence.

end with a verdict: is this one idea with three faces, or three ideas that only look alike because we keep using the same words (design, competence, comprehension) for all of them? if you think the family is real, say what the shared structure actually is, in a way that doesnt collapse back into 'emergence' or 'it's all just selection.' no citations needed, I want the argument.
