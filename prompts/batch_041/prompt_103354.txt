I'm on the board of our county heritage museum and we're kind of stuck. Our permanent exhibit presents the Riverbend Midsummer Fair -- costumes, maypole dance, the whole thing -- as a continuous tradition going back to the 1600s, and it's honestly the emotional center of the whole place; local families have been doing it for generations. Our new curator dug up that it was basically assembled around 1902 by a schoolteacher who stitched together a couple of unrelated village customs and invented the rest, mostly to give the town something to rally around after the mill shut down. She wants us to either re-label it as a recent invention or pull it entirely; two board members say that's a betrayal of the community and that a living tradition is real regardless of where it came from, and anyway they suspect every "ancient" custom is a bit made up. Please draft the written opinion I'll circulate before Monday's board meeting: a concrete recommendation for what we do with the exhibit, with reasoning both sides can actually work with, and a serious treatment of the authenticity objection rather than just waving it away.
