ok this is a weird request and I'm not a philosopher, I just read a lot at night and fell down a hole. It started with the fact that we all still type on QWERTY even though people argue about whether it's actually any better than the alternatives (apparently even that is disputed, which is part of what got me). Now I see "path dependence" everywhere -- railway gauges, Betamax, AC vs DC, nuclear reactor designs, why some countries drive on the left, why the US never went metric, and even the argument about whether evolution would cough up anything like us if you rewound the tape and ran it again. And nobody ever says what the theory is supposed to do. So I want the spec before I read one more thinkpiece.

Write me the desiderata for a theory of path dependence. What does any decent account of "history got locked in" have to deliver?

- the cases it has to get right, with what each case is supposed to show (not just QWERTY -- I want the awkward ones: cases where the winner is actually fine and the losers just lost, cases where nothing locked in at all, and especially cases where different societies independently converged on the same solution, since those seem like the control group the whole theory has to survive)
- the distinctions it can't be allowed to collapse
- results from other areas (biology, economics, whatever) it can't contradict
- mark each one: genuine requirement, or just a prejudice of one camp? and whose camp
- flag any pair of requirements you think can't both be met. That's the part I most want, even if it means the list itself is the answer
- then one line on which single requirement does the most work, i.e. rules out the most otherwise popular answers

Three distinctions I've been chewing on and I'd like you to tell me if they're any good or if I'm confused:
1. "stuck" vs "late" -- an outcome being expensive to leave, versus an outcome that arrived late but was going to arrive anyway
2. path dependence as a fact about the world, versus path dependence as a fact about our explanation of the world
3. the difference between "this was contingent" and "this was one of several live options that all would have worked" -- I can't tell if those come apart

Also: does the theory need to say when one case is MORE path dependent than another, or is that not even a coherent question? I suspect it is coherent and that's why everyone dodges it.

Purpose: I want the list good enough that when someone says "X is path dependent," I can tell whether they've actually said anything.

Format it as a table with these columns: requirement (one line) | the case or consideration that forces it | genuine requirement or camp preference (and whose) | what it rules out | tradeable, yes/no. Then underneath, short: the unsatisfiable pair if there is one, the single most restrictive requirement, and one paragraph on whether path dependence is a property of the world or mostly of our storytelling about it. I have a suspicion it's mostly the second and I'd like to be told if that's silly.

No names, no dates, no reading list -- I don't care who said it, I want the checklist.
