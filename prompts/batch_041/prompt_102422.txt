I've been trying to place various creatures and systems on a four-grade scale of cognitive sophistication, and my judgments refuse to settle into any tidy pattern. The four grades, as I understand them, are these. A Darwinian creature has its behaviour fixed by natural selection: it meets its world through mechanisms built in advance, with no learning worth the name inside its own lifetime. A Skinnerian creature is shaped within its lifetime by reinforcement, its behaviour selected by the consequences it has actually met. A Popperian creature can represent possible courses of action and their likely upshots and choose among them before acting, so that its hypotheses die in its stead rather than itself. A Gregorian creature is a Popperian one that can additionally take on mind-tools — language, numerals, notation, the accumulated conceptual equipment of a culture — from other creatures, so that its inner environment is vastly enlarged and it runs its simulations with borrowed machinery.

Here are my verdicts on particular cases. A bacterium that swims up a sugar gradient: Darwinian. A rat that learns to press a lever for food: Skinnerian. A bird that caches food and, if it was watched while hiding it, later re-hides it elsewhere, apparently reckoning with what the watcher could have seen: Popperian. A toddler who works out where a rolled ball must have gone by imagining its path: Popperian. An adult doing arithmetic with Arabic numerals and a zero, arithmetic she could not manage with Roman numerals: Gregorian. A chess engine that searches millions of lines before moving: Popperian but not Gregorian. A large language model that has soaked up the notation, idioms, and conceptual vocabulary of a whole culture and reasons fluently on demand: Gregorian. A market that discovers prices: not a mind at all, none of the four, even though its outputs have the flavour of Gregorian cognition.

Two of these pull against each other and I cannot make them agree. I call the bird Popperian for acting on a represented possibility, yet I withhold Gregorian status from the chess engine even though it plainly traffics in notation; and I grant Gregorian status to the language model while doubting it has any inner environment to enlarge. My suspicion is that I have been running two questions together — how sophisticated the machinery is, and where the machinery came from — and grading on whichever one is salient.

My tentative principle is this: a creature is Popperian just when its behaviour is selected by anticipated rather than actual consequences, and Gregorian just when the anticipating is done with machinery it acquired from others. Test that against the cases, repair it as needed, and if no single principle survives, give me the smallest set of clauses that does.

I'd also like the resulting principle applied to a case I haven't listed: a thermostat, an octopus solving a puzzle it has never met, an ant colony, and a pocket calculator. Finally, tell me plainly which of my verdicts the principle cannot keep, and which I should abandon.
