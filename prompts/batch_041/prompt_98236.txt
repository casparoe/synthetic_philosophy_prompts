I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have been trying to work out a puzzle about value comparisons on my own. I invented a case I call the ladder of listening. Imagine a single piano sonata performed one hundred times. Each successive recording is slightly worse in one respect: it contains one additional tiny timing error, one additional slightly missed dynamic, and so on, so that each recording is a little less technically clean than the one before. But each successive recording is also slightly better in another respect: the performer takes one additional small expressive risk, one additional subtle rubato, one additional colouring, so that each is a little more moving than the one before. When I compare adjacent recordings, I find I cannot confidently say that one is better than the other; the gains and losses seem to cancel, or at least to fall within a fog of indeterminacy. Yet when I compare the first recording with the hundredth, the first seems clearly better overall: its technical cleanliness outweighs the expressive gains, or at least I am much more confident in that judgment. A friend tells me this shows that better than is not transitive. Another friend insists that transitivity is built into the very meaning of better than, so my description must be confused.

I would like you to write a dialogue between these two friends. They should be equally intelligent and equally well informed, and neither should be a strawman. The first should defend the possibility of nontransitive betterness, perhaps by appealing to multidimensional value, vagueness, or the way pairwise comparisons can be indeterminate while overall comparisons are determinate. The second should defend transitivity, perhaps by distinguishing vagueness from nontransitivity, by treating better than as a partial order, or by arguing that the appearance of nontransitivity comes from shifting between different senses of better. They should respond to each other's actual arguments, not talk past each other. I would especially like them to discuss whether the ladder of listening is really a sorites paradox in disguise, whether better than is a relation or a predicate, and whether the case shows that value is incommensurable or merely incompletely ordered. Please do not simply rehearse familiar examples from the literature; use my ladder of listening as the central case. End the dialogue without a forced resolution, but with the disagreement sharpened.
