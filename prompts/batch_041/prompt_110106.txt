I work on anti-corruption programmes, and I keep running into a pattern I have no word for. What decides whether a transfer of value is condemned seems to have little to do with its size, its motive, or its consequences, and a great deal to do with whether the value can be given a price. A favour returned in kind over years — a relative found a job, a contract steered to an old schoolfriend — is absorbed into ordinary social life, or at worst called nepotism; the same favour settled with a small envelope of cash is corruption. A gift left with an official who will decide one's case is a bribe, while the identical gift left after the decision is a courtesy. And when I ask people to justify the line, they never talk about the amount: the envelope, they say, makes the obligation exact and finished in a way that a returned favour never is.

What I cannot tell is whether I am looking at one phenomenon or a family of them. I know the contrast anthropologists draw between gifts and commodities, the argument that corruption is a Western label imposed on local reciprocity, and the complaint that pricing things corrupts them; but none of these isolates what I keep noticing, which is a property of the transfer itself that flips its standing while value and outcome are held fixed. Nor does the legal vocabulary — bribery, conflict of interest — get at it, since that names a crime or an official's compromised position rather than a feature of the exchange.

Does this property have an established name? If it does, I want to know what it rules out as well as what it covers: why the unreciprocated gift is often judged more harshly than the reciprocated one, why the same gift is read differently when given openly and when given privately, and why the direction of the flow — from the person who wants something to the person who can grant it, rather than the reverse — should matter at all if the property really is about priceability. If there is no name, I would rather have a term with edges, defined against the gift/commodity contrast and against the relativist reading, than a loose label that swallows every case.
