I belong to a year-long reading group on modal metaphysics, and two of its members have been circling each other since the autumn over Gödel's modal ontological argument — the one that takes a primitive notion of a property's being "positive," defines a being as Godlike when it has every positive property, works in an S5 modal framework, and tries to move from the bare possibility of a Godlike being to its necessary existence in every possible world. One of them, call her the defender, treats the proof as a serious contribution: she holds that the axioms governing positivity are independently motivated rather than gerrymandered, that the step from a positive property to its possible exemplification is not a disguised assumption of the conclusion, and that the argument is valid with premises a theist can reasonably accept. The other, the critic, thinks it is a curiosity at best: either it quietly assumes what it sets out to prove at the point where positivity is claimed to guarantee possible instantiation, or it collapses into the result that everything that is true is necessarily true, in which case the conclusion costs nothing and tells us nothing about God in particular. They have now spent months talking past each other, and I want to force them to produce the document they should have written instead of another seminar skirmish.

Please draft that joint statement, the one both of them could sign in good faith. It should set out the claims about the formalism, about the notion of positivity, and about what a proof of this shape could possibly establish that both sides can endorse; then state the residual disagreement, with each side's position phrased so that side would accept the wording as its own; then identify the cruxes — I suspect one is whether the possibility step can be supported without already treating the relevant existence as possible in the needed sense, and another is whether the modal-collapse charge is fatal or merely an artifact of one particular way of axiomatizing positivity — and for each crux give the argument or test both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If it turns out that part of their dispute is verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why.

Give me the result as exactly five numbered items, each one to two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
