I am a high school student preparing for a debate on whether moral status comes in degrees, and I have built the following argument. (1) If two creatures differ only by an arbitrarily small amount in their capacity for experiences that feel good or bad to them, then they cannot differ by a whole discrete step in moral status; moral status must track that capacity continuously. (2) A thermostat, a houseplant, an insect, a mouse, and a human form a chain in which each adjacent pair differs only by a small increment in information processing and responsiveness to stimuli. (3) Applying (1) along that chain, there is no principled place to draw a line at which moral status begins. (4) If there is no line, then every item in the chain, including the thermostat, has some positive moral status. (5) But it is absurd to say that a thermostat has moral status, and absurd to treat switching one off as a moral matter. (6) Therefore (1) is false, and there must be a threshold. I would rather keep (4) than (5), because I suspect the absurdity of (5) may just be prejudice, but I want to know what I would have to pay to keep it. Which step should I reject, why did it look plausible, and what does each alternative cost?
