I have been working through the failures of Athenian democracy on my own, and I want the specification before I go any further. The episodes I have in mind are these. Mytilene, where the assembly voted to kill every adult man on the island and enslave the rest, and then, the next day, voted to kill only about a thousand it judged most responsible. Sicily, where the assembly voted to send a great expedition, sent a second force when the first was already failing, and lost the whole thing. Arginusae, where the assembly punished its own victorious generals for failing to pick drowning men out of the sea, voting on all of them together in a single motion, with Socrates, then among those presiding, refusing to put the illegal motion to the vote. And the trial of Socrates himself, a few years after the Athenians had sworn an amnesty to stop the settling of old scores, convicting a man who had refused that illegal motion and had also refused an illegal order from the Thirty.

What I want is not a verdict on Athens but the requirements list: what would an account of what went wrong have to deliver to count as adequate at all? I want the cases it must get right, with the case behind each requirement, so that I can see which requirements are doing real work and which are just things people say. I want the distinctions it may not collapse: a bad decision and a bad procedure; a mistake and a culpable mistake; the assembly, the democracy, and the population; a defect in this one decision and a defect in the design; a failure another constitution would have avoided and a failure any state at war would have made.

Tell me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely commitments of a camp, naming the camps as you go: the crowd-is-fickle camp, the institutions-were-misdesigned camp, the empire-was-the-problem camp, and the camp that says the sources are hostile enough that there may be no defect to explain.

Above all I want the pairs that cannot be satisfied together. My suspicion is that any mechanism strong enough to explain the loss of Sicily will also predict a Mytilene that never reversed, and I want to know whether that is right and what an account must give up in order to keep both. And tell me which single requirement, if I insist on it, eliminates the most candidates; my guess is that it is the requirement that the account survive the fact that the men who wrote these deliberations down were not themselves democrats.
