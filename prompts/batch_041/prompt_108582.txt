My city council is about to pilot futarchy on three contested budget decisions. The design we have sketched is to fix a welfare index in advance, let residents trade contracts that pay out according to whether that index is higher two years after a proposal passes than if it had failed, and then implement whichever proposals the market prices as welfare-improving; the index itself would be compiled by the departments that would spend the money. Since prediction markets have a better forecasting record than pundits or polls on well-defined outcomes, this looks to me like a way to take the factual part of our budget fights out of politics: the council settles the values once by choosing the index, and thereafter the market tells us which policies actually work. Is it right that the only serious design question left is how broad to make the index, or is something important being smuggled in by that way of dividing the work between the council and the market?
