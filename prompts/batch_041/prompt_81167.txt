For most of my working life I was a quantitative biologist — morphometrics, allometry, and the long argument over what happens when you reduce an organism to a number — and since retiring I have spent three years reading moral philosophy. I keep circling back to the question of whether brain size is morally relevant, and I keep suspecting that what I am hearing is not really a disagreement about brains. As I understand the positions on offer: brain size, or neuron count, or some encephalization index is evidentially relevant because it serves as a proxy for the capacities that ground moral status; moral status attaches to the capacities themselves rather than to their physical realization, and a realization is a contingent implementation, so brain size is not relevant in itself; brain size enters only at the level of a species-typical capacity and not at the level of individuals; and it matters only above a threshold, as a precondition for sentience, rather than as a graded quantity.

What I want is for each of these to be restated as faithfully as you can manage in the vocabulary I actually think in: proxy variables and construct validity, direct and mediated effects, within- and between-group variance components, measurement invariance, dose-response curves and threshold detection, and the dependence of a residual on the reference population. Then give me the residue report. Which claims come through intact, which come through distorted, and which have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary? I expect the normative core to be where the translation fails, but I would like to know whether that failure is a defect of my framework or a sign that the original question is less clear than it looks. In particular: have the philosophers simply neglected to say which construct their measures are supposed to track, or does the question of what may be done to a creature genuinely outrun anything a measurement framework can express? My working hypothesis, which I would rather you test than flatter, is that this is a measurement-validity dispute in disguise. If it is not, tell me what my training is causing me to miss.
