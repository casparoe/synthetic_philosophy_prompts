A stronger postmodern version goes further: if race is constructed by our concepts, and all our concepts are socially shaped, then there's no objective truth about race, or about anything else. Critics reply that this is self-refuting, since the claim that everything is constructed can't itself be objectively true, and that without an objective standard we lose our grounds for condemning racism. The defensible middle position is that race is real as a social fact but not as a natural kind — a convention, like which side of the road we drive on, though with worse consequences."
