A colleague who works on cooperative AI and I have argued for two years about safe Pareto improvements, and we have finally agreed to write the document we should have written at the outset. Take the standard bargaining picture: a set of feasible joint outcomes, a payoff for each party at each outcome, a disagreement point, and some rule that selects a resolution. Call a modification of that problem a safe Pareto improvement (SPI) if, relative to the unmodified problem, every party ends up at least as well off under every resolution rule in a specified class and for every admissible configuration of the other parties' preferences. I hold that this is the right target for cooperation between agents whose preferences we cannot know in advance, and that such improvements are often available; my colleague holds that the requirement is either vacuous — satisfied only by changes already implied, or by nothing at all once there are three or more parties — or else quietly smuggles in a controversial prior over resolutions, and that real progress requires committing to a solution concept instead.

Write our joint statement: first a numbered section of claims we both accept about the formalism, about what an SPI is and is not, and about what the relevant impossibility or triviality results do and do not establish; then a disagreements table in which each remaining dispute is stated as the exact claim each of us asserts, worded so that each of us would sign our own row. We take the cruxes to be (i) whether robustness must hold over all resolutions or only over a defensible class of them, (ii) whether SPIs are generically plentiful or generically empty as the number of parties grows, and (iii) whether a change that leaves the feasible set alone but alters the disagreement point, or alters what is common knowledge, counts as an SPI at all — a distinction we have been calling structural versus informational. For each crux, set out a concrete case, construction, or small computation that we both agree would bear on it, with each of our predictions recorded in advance. If part of our quarrel turns out to be verbal, say which part and why.
