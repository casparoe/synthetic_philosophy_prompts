My hunch is that the argument is happening in the wrong place. In ML terms, a historical analogy is a model fitted to a single training example, and the step nobody examines is the retrieval — which past cases even get considered in the first place. The analogy that comes to mind seems to get picked by surface features (a dictator, a demand, a piece of territory, an ultimatum), and by the time you're arguing about whether it's apt, the conclusion is basically already locked in by which case got retrieved. So I want to pull apart two things I think get run together: why a particular analogy gets retrieved rather than some other one, and whether it's a good analogy once you've got it — and then ask whether the second question is doing any real work if the first one is deciding everything. The shape of it feels like the reference class problem in probability, where any probability you give depends on which class of similar cases you count yourself in and there's no neutral way to pick the class. I have a feeling that's exactly what's going on with historical lessons, but I dont know if anyone has framed it that way, or whether the framing just collapses into something boring people already say.
