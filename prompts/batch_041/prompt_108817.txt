Quick background so you know where this is coming from: I write about science and ideas, and for the last month I've been lurking around a decision-science lab for a feature, which means I've been filling out their choice sheets myself instead of just interviewing the people who design them. Now I have a page of my own answers that dont obviously hang together and it's bugging me. Case one, the urn thing everyone calls Ellsberg's: an urn with 50 red and 50 black, versus an urn with 100 balls, red or black, and nobody will tell me the mix. Bet on red, I take the first urn. Bet on black, I take the first urn. Case two, the lab's 90-ball version: 30 red, the other 60 are black or yellow, mix again unknown. Bet on red against bet on black, I take red. Bet on red-or-yellow against black-or-yellow, I take black-or-yellow, which I'm told contradicts my last answer and which feels obviously right. Fine, so far that's textbook. Here's where I stop being able to tell a story about myself. Case three: two machines, one has pulled 7 reds out of its last 10 draws, the other is sealed and I don't know its program, same payout either way. I take the one I've watched, even though 7 out of 10 is basically noise, so a lousy estimate is fine with me and an unknown mechanism isn't. Case four: a trivia bet, same payout, my pick of a capitals-of-the-world question or a question about the Croatian pop charts. I'll take capitals every time, and not because I think I'll know the answer, but because I know the shape of what I don't know, so there I'm running toward the fuzzy option. Case five: the two machines again, except now I'm choosing for my sister and her money, and I want the clean 50/50, no fuzz at all. Case six: two weather forecasts, both 60% rain, one for the city I've lived in for twenty years and one for a city I've never set foot in. No flicker of difference, I'd take either. Case seven, the one that really gets me: a machine in a sealed vault that flashed either red or green at midnight and the record is gone, so the answer exists but no living person knows it or ever will. I still flinch at that one.

The best I've managed on my own is something like: I don't mind the world being random, I mind a person or a process knowing something and not telling me, so my flinch tracks someones silence. That does okay on one, two, three and five. It's terrible on seven, where literally nobody knows, and terrible on four, where I actively prefer the murk, and six doesn't fit it either. I don't want the standard writeup of the urn stuff, I've read that and it doesn't cover the rest of these. What I want is the principle, or the two or three principles, that actually gets the most of these verdicts, stated precisely enough that I can see which clause is doing the work for which case, plus what it predicts for a case I haven't listed, like me betting on whether my book sells more than five thousand copies by Christmas, plus an honest list of which of my seven verdicts it can't keep. I'd rather drop one of my gut calls than keep a principle whose only content is that it depends.
