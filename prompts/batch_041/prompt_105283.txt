so I keep coming back to this thing I half-remember from that Gödel Escher Bach book, that every message is really three messages stacked: an inner message (the actual content), an outer message (the code or convention that tells you how to get the content out), and a frame message (the invisible background that tells you it's a message at all, and how to hold it). Nobody ever sends the frame message; it just has to already be there in the reciever. I've been testing this on cases and here's where my gut lands, and I'd like you to tell me what rule, if any, is behind it:

1. A vinyl record: music = inner, "grooves encode sound, decode them like this" = outer, "I'm a record, play me" = frame.
2. The same record on a planet with no players and no memory of players: I still want to say the music is in there somehow. But I also feel like it's nowhere.
3. DNA in a living cell: the organism = inner, the genetic code = outer, the whole molecular machinery of the cell = frame.
4. The exact same DNA sequence sitting in a text file on a laptop: I say there's no inner message at all, even though the letters are identical to case 3.
5. A stop sign: inner = "stop", outer = the convention that red octagons mean stop, frame = that you're on a road with rules.
6. The same red octagon in a warehouse: not a message. The same one hung on a gallery wall: maybe a message again, maybe a different one. I can't tell which.
7. A sentence in a language I don't speak: frame yes, outer maybe, inner no. I feel the meaning is right there, just out of reach.
8. A sentence in a language nobody has ever spoken: now I say there's no meaning there at all.
9. A photo of a page of text vs the page itself: I say the inner message is the same, which feels wrong.
10. A password, a joke nobody laughs at, a smile: no idea what to say, and the smile especially makes the whole inner/outer thing collapse — what would the outer message of a smile even be?

My worry is that 2 and 4 are the same case and I'm answering them oppositely: in 2 the meaning survives with no reader, in 4 it dies even though the marks are identical. So one of those has to go, or the rule has to be finer than I've stated it. Is there one rule for when something has an inner message at all, and what does it say about the password, the unfunny joke, and the smile?
