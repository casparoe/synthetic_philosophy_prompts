I'm a novelist — I pay rent teaching comp on adjunct contracts, so I'm on the organizing committee for our fledgling adjunct local, and I'm also 200 pages into a novel about it.

What I want is the actual decision procedure, not the inspirational version. I dont buy "unions are good because bargaining power is asymmetric" as the foundation. I think the right to organize bottoms out in the wrongness of being subject to someone else's arbitrary will in the terms of your own working life — the republican non-domination idea — and I want the procedure built on that, including the bit most organizers hate: the same test has to run on the local itself, since a body that can obligate dues, bind people who voted no, and call a strike holds power over its own members.

So give me a checklist we can actually run in a 25-minute committee meeting, in order — the questions to ask, what counts as a good enough answer, where it hard-stops, and where it hands off to a vote or to the parent union because the rest is a value call. We keep relitigating the same three things: whether to go for strike authorization over healthcare (and whether we can demand no replacement hires during a walkout), whether to take a deal that covers the full-timers and leaves the rest of us out, and whether a handful of us can do something the unit already voted down.

Run it on all three. I want at least one answer I won't like, and I want you to say plainly which steps are just my politics dressed up as method. And tell me what the procedure can't do — my protagonist has to lose an argument in chapter 12 and I need to know which one.
