Alright, here's an assignment I want to try out on you before I inflict it on my seminar.

Write me a short story, say 900–1400 words, dosn't have to be exact, that's about conceptual slippage inside a tiny made-up microdomain. What I mean by that: you know those toy analogy puzzles where you get a little string of symbols plus a transformation of it, and you have to produce the corresponding transformation of another string — the whole point being that nothing anywhere tells you what corresponding means, you just have a feel for which moves are in the spirit of the thing. The Hofstadter/Copycat crowd lived in that neighbourhood for years. Don't reskin the letter puzzles though, invent your own microdomain. It should be small and concrete enough that a reader learns its moves in a paragraph, and those moves should feel genuinely rule-governed, not vibes.

The story I'm after is one where the concept doing the work is nowhere written down. It holds because of a web of past precedents and a bunch of people who all take themselves to mean the same thing by it — and then it slides. Not because someone redefines it, not because of a villain, but because the domain itself starts pushing back. Nobody can point to the moment the meaning changed, including the person who keeps the records.

I want three things in it:

- one move that any competent insider would call obviously correct, and one they'd call obviously wrong, where the difference between the two is genuinely hard to state;
- a character whose job is to keep the file of past judgments, who only sees the drift in hindsight and can't reconstruct where it started;
- an ending that doesn't dissolve into well it's all subjective. A reader should be able to reconstruct both a conservative and a revisionist reading of the same sequence of moves.

No framing essay, no moral tacked on at the end. If the story needs the moral explained to you, it hasn't worked. Just the story, thats all I want.
