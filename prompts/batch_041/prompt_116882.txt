Write a job listing, in the deadpan register of a museum's careers page, for an institution that has adopted Tolstoy's account of art in What Is Art? as its acquisitions and exhibitions policy. The museum holds that a work is art only if the artist transmits to others a feeling he has himself experienced, and it grades works by how reliably that feeling is caught — by how individual the feeling is, how clearly it is expressed, and how sincere the artist was. Beauty, difficulty, and novelty count for nothing; works that merely borrow, imitate, aim at effect, or divert are filed as counterfeit; obscurity is treated as a defect of transmission rather than a mark of depth. Only feelings that unite people qualify for the top tier, with a further tier reserved for simple everyday feelings accessible to everyone, ranked above the partisan.

The advertisement should lay out the role, the duties, the requirements, and the benefits without ever winking, in roughly 600 words. Let the comedy arise from the policy meeting the actual collection and the actual staff: the certification panel of ordinary viewers whose job is to report what they felt; the sealed Beethoven and Wagner storerooms; the advertising jingle that keeps outscoring the paintings on the museum's own transmission metrics; the donor whose gift comes with conditions; the fact that no one can be hired to assess sincerity. A reader who has never heard of Tolstoy should be able to follow the piece, and a reader who has read the book should be able to see that the author has the theory right, weak points included, without the piece ever stating them.
