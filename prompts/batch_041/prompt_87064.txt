I'm in a metaphysics reading group that's been grinding through dispositions for a few months now and we keep hitting the same wall, so I want to try running an actual case through it instead of arguing about intuitions. Quick statement of the apparatus, since I don't want to assume we're using the same words: a disposition (fragility, solubility, irascibility) is a real property tied to a characteristic manifestation under characteristic conditions, but the naive if-then analysis fails for two different reasons. Fink cases are the ones where an external intervener would, if triggered, either make the thing manifest a disposition it doesn't have or stop it from manifesting one it clearly does -- so the conditional comes out false while the disposition is plainly still there, which is a problem for the analysis. Mask cases are where something interferes with the manifestation without touching the disposition at all: the sugar in already-saturated water, the generous person who happens to be asleep. The mask isn't a threat to the analysis, but the disposition survives it. So finks threaten, masks merely annoy.

Now take the situationist critique of character traits and run it through that. The episodes I mean are the well-known ones: Milgram's obedience studies, where ordinary people went on administering what they believed were escalating shocks because a man in a lab coat told them to; the seminarians who were on their way to give a talk about the Good Samaritan and mostly walked past the man groaning in the alley when they'd been told they were running late, and mostly stopped when they hadn't; and the dime-in-the-payphone version, where finding a coin in the return slot made people noticeably more willing to help a stranger who dropped her papers. The situationists' line was that this shows compassion, honesty, cruelty and the rest aren't real dispositions, just stories we tell afterwards about behavior the situation was driving all along. The virtue people said the experiments are just noisy. I don't want either of those moves restated, I want the dispositional vocabulary actually applied: for each episode, is the situation functioning as a fink, as a mask, as neither, or as something the vocabulary can't classify at all? And if "compassionate" is a disposition, what precisely is its characteristic manifestation and its characteristic conditions -- does that come out of the theory, or do we just stipulate it, because if it's stipulation I want to know why that isn't cheating.

Then run it the other way and tell me what the case does to the theory. Is fink-and-mask a genuine rescue for trait-talk, or is it a machine for making trait-talk unfalsifiable -- every failed manifestation gets absorbed as a mask, so nothing could ever count against the trait? Or does the case show that the apparatus was built for physical dispositions with fairly clean stimulus and manifestation conditions and doesn't transfer to character at all, where there's no canonical eliciting condition and no single canonical response? Be disciplined about the mapping and say plainly where the apparatus is silent rather than making it speak, and at the end tell me which of the two positions the episode actually supports once the analysis is done -- I suspect it's neither of the ones on offer, but I want the argument, not the hunch.
