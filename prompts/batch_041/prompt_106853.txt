I'm in a philosophy of science reading group and we've been going in circles on the pessimistic meta-induction for what feels like forever. The anti-realist line is: the history of science is a graveyard of theories that were successful and turned out to be false or to refer to nothing — phlogiston, caloric, the aether, all of it worked well enough and all of it got ditched — so our current best theories are probably false too, and success doesn't track truth. The realist comes back with: those theories weren't really successful in the sense we mean, or what survived was the structure, the equations, the relations, and only the discarded ontology was ever the problem, or the whole meta-induction is just an induction and we don't trust those about the future of inquiry. Everyone has heard everyone's moves. Nobody has moved in months.

My own suspicion is that this is really a fight about theory individuation that neither side will own up to — the realist says 'the same theory was retained' while counting the bits that survived, the anti-realist says 'that theory was abandoned' while counting the bits that didn't, and neither ever argues about what makes two theories the same theory across a revolution. Is that the right diagnosis, or is the real fault line somehwere else — a disagreement about what counts as 'success' (maybe there are two different things being called success and the induction only runs on one of them), about whether the induction is over theories or over terms and reference, or about what would even count as evidence here? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that could actually move this — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or should I accept it's one of those fights where the two sides want different things out of an explanation and stop expecting convergence? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of who said what.
