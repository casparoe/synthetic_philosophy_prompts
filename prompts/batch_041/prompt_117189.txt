I want the specification before I evaluate any particular rule for updating on uncertain evidence. Write out the desiderata that any adequate account must meet, with the consideration that motivates each and a verdict on which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp.

Treat the following as part of the specification rather than as an objection to be answered. An instrument reports its evidence as a probability distribution over a partition of the possibilities, not as a single proposition. Its calibration drifts, so what a given reading means is itself something the agent learns only from further readings of the same instrument. Readings arrive in sequences, and the partition a reading is expressed over is sometimes coarse and sometimes fine, with the agent unsure which.

Cover at least these candidates, and add any you think belong: (1) agreement with conditionalization when the evidence is certain; (2) the posterior is a probability function over the same algebra; (3) an uncertain report never makes the agent certain of something she was not already certain of; (4) conditional probabilities within each evidence cell are left unchanged; (5) the posterior is the point closest to the prior among those the evidence permits; (6) the order in which two uncertain reports arrive does not affect the result; (7) a report already absorbed changes nothing when it is repeated; (8) the prior is recoverable from the posterior together with the evidence; (9) the result does not depend on whether the same information is expressed over a coarse or a fine partition; (10) the rule is defined when the evidence gives positive probability to something the agent had ruled out; (11) the update is uniquely determined by prior and evidence, including when the report concerns the agent's own posterior. Then identify which pairs or larger subsets of these cannot be jointly satisfied and why, and say which single requirement excludes the most candidate rules.
