I chair the appeals panel for a mid-size VR social platform -- we're small enough that the panel is three people and I write the opinions myself. A case came up that I keep going back and forth on. A 15-year-old user found a player-built memorial garden in a public world (candles, a bench, a plaque) that another user had made for her brother, who died last year, and spent about twenty minutes taking it apart in front of a small crowd: throwing the bench around, glitching through the candles, renaming the plaque to something crude. Nothing was actually broken -- the world resets nightly, the garden was back the next morning -- and our terms say player-built content is a "licensed fiction" that users don't own, though the same terms also say avatars and their things are "extensions of the person." The kid says he thought the whole thing was NPC set dressing and that anyway you can't damage pixels. The sister says it's the closest thing to a grave shes got and she can't sleep. Moderation suspended him for 30 days, he's appealing, and honestly that number was picked out of thin air. Write the opinion I'll circulate to the other two panel members before our Thursday call: recommend something concrete on the suspension and on whether the sister gets anything (an apology, in-world repair work, a policy change, nothing), give reasons the kid's side could actually respond to, and take the strongest version of his defense seriously rather than just telling me it's a game. Also say how confident you are in the call and flag the part of it you're least sure about.
