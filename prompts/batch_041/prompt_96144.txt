Everyone seems to carry their own "now" around with them, and that now isn't a point, it's a whole slice through the entire universe at once, including things billions of light years away that you couldn't possibly see yet. And because we're all drifting about at different speeds, those slices tilt differently. So if you and me walk past each other on the pavement, one of us has a decision being taken right now in a galaxy 2.5 million light years off, and the other one has that same decision already made, years ago, done and dusted — and neither of us is wrong about it. That's the bit I can't stop chewing on: not that time is weird in general, but that "now" apparently works like "here", a word about where you're standing rather than about what's real. The conclusion people draw is that the past isn't gone and the future isn't open, it's all just sitting there like a road you can see the whole of from a hill. I think it's called the Rietdijk–Putnam argument but I might be mashing names together.
