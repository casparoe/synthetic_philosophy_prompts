Start from a society where everyone has an exactly equal share. Then a million people each voluntarily pay a quarter to watch this one guy play basketball, and now he's got $250,000 more than everybody else. Nobody was robbed, nobody was forced, every deal was fine, and the equal distribution is just gone. His conclusion: any principle that says the distribution has to sit in some shape — equal shares, shares by need, whatever — either gets thrown out the moment people start making deals with each other, or it has to be held in place by constantly interfering with what people choose to do. So the levy is either pointless or it's coercion.

I pushed back five times and he came back at each one.

(1) The example only works from a baseline thats already just. If the reason some people are behind today is a history of stuff that wasn't just, then the quarter isn't coming out of a clean starting point and the example just assumes the entitlement view in order to prove it. Dan said it's a conditional, so the baseline is a premise I'm free to reject but not to call circular: if the starting holdings are just and everyone moves voluntarily, the result is just, and it's the patterned theorist who has to say a just outcome is unjust, which is the absurd bit.

(2) Even granting all that, liberty only upsets a pattern sometimes, not always. If everyone in town voluntarily pays into a fund that tops everybody back up, you've got your pattern back with zero coercion anywhere in the story. So the real claim is narrower than the slogan: you cant guarantee a pattern without interfering, but liberty and patterns aren't enemies as such. Dan said that's the claim he's been making all along, and guaranteeing is exactly what a levy does; and if I want to say the tax isnt interference because rules are just rules, then property rights are rules too, so the slogan has quietly decided that the market result is the free one and the tax is the unfree one.

(3) The liberty in the slogan is only the liberty of the buyers and the seller. A city voting to tax itself to house people is also a bunch of people doing what they want, so the slogan is taking a side rather than proving one. Dan said by that logic nothing is ever coercion, because every bit of coercion is somebody's collective choice, and if we voted for it means its free then there's no such thing as taking someones stuff.

(4) The Chamberlain case has new money coming in from outside. The fans are paying for a show that wouldn't have existed otherwise, so the pie grows and nobody's holdings actually shrink. A levy on existing rents is a different animal, the money comes out of someone's pocket rather than out of nowhere. Dan said the size of the pie is irrelevant, entitlement isnt about that: if the fans had a right to their quarters then he had a right to what they paid him, and if the landlord has a right to the rent then the levy takes what's his, full stop.

(5) The example only bites against rules that care about the shape of the distribution right now. A rule like to each according to what they produce is a pattern too, but it looks backwards at how you got where you are, and Chamberlain's pile of quarters tracks what he produced, so the example leaves that kind of rule standing.
