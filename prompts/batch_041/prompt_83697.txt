I keep seeing this "torture vs. dust specks" thing referenced, so I finally read a popular write-up of it. Here's roughly what the piece said: "You have to choose between two futures. In the first, a single person is tortured for 50 years. In the second, 3^^^3 people -- a number so large it might as well be infinite -- each get one speck of dust in the eye for a moment. The question is which future is worse, and the point is that utilitarianism is forced to say the dust specks are worse, because that's just what aggregating utility means: enough tiny harms will always eventually outweigh one big one. So the thought experiment basically refutes utilitarianism, since obviously 50 years of torture is worse than any number of dust specks."

Something about this feels off and I can't quite say where. (My gut says 50 years of torture is obviously worse, but I'm wary of trusting my gut about astronomically large numbers.) Can you go through it sentance by sentence -- what would someone who actually works on this accept, what would they wince at, what's just wrong -- and then tell me whether the "so it refutes utilitarianism" part survives once the mistakes are fixed, and what a utilitarian would say in reply? And if it isn't a refutation, what's the actually interesting question the thought experiment is getting at?
