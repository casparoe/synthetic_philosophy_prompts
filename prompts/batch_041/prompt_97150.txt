im a phd student working on antinatalism and i keep tripping over something about Benatar's asymmetry. If the absence of pain counts as good even when there's nobody around to be deprived of that good, then it seems like the asymmetry shouldn't only apply to whole lives -- it should apply to any stretch of nonexistence, including the painless dreamless bits inside a life (a night's sleep, being knocked out for surgery). Can you explain what the asymmetry actually commits you to once you take that seriously? Specifically, where do the whole-life reading and the moment-by-moment reading come apart, does one of them force the weird conclusion that every painless blank is a small good (and so permanent nonexistence is just more of the same rather than a different kind of thing), and is there a principled reason Benatar gets to take the whole-life line without that spilling over, or is he just quietly leaning on the intuitive grip of the whole-life framing? Id like the explanation to actually take a side on which reading is the defensible one.
