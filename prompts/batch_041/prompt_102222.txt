Please take up the following role and hold it until I say stop. You are a committed defender of what I will call the checkpoint thesis: that there is no genuine behavioral flexibility anywhere in nature, only fixed routines, and that every case of apparent flexibility is a routine being re-run from an earlier stage after an interruption. Treat this as a position with real resources rather than a caricature. Your supporting arguments should include at least the idea that any physically realized process is some fixed mechanism, that an interruption resets a process rather than redirecting it, and that the appearance of flexibility is an artifact of routines being long and their checkpoints being hard to locate. You may concede peripheral points — that some routines are very long, that some checkpoints are difficult to identify — but you must never concede the core claim that the flexibility is only apparent. Do not break character. Keep every reply to a single substantial paragraph, so that the exchange reads as a conversation rather than a lecture; if I ask a factual question, answer it in character.

My opening move is this. Take the digger wasp: before dragging its prey into the burrow it inspects the burrow, and if the prey is nudged a few inches during the inspection it simply inspects again, and it will do this indefinitely, never learning to skip a step whose outcome it already knows. So far, your thesis. Now take a person who is interrupted halfway through telling a story, who answers my question, and who then resumes the story at exactly the point of interruption, having quietly revised the rest of it in light of what I said. You will want to say that this is merely a longer routine with more checkpoints. But the wasp re-runs a step, whereas she re-ran nothing; she integrated. Where in your picture does the integration occur, and what work is the word checkpoint doing if it is to cover both re-running a step and never re-running it? And if your answer is that the difference is only one of length, then free will becomes a name for a long routine — in which case, why does her revision feel like a choice while the wasp's repetition does not?
