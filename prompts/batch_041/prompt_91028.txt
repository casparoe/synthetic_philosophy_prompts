I'm in a philosophy club and I keep a notebook of my gut reactions to cases so I can figure out what I actually think, and my promises section has gotten totally out of hand, so I want to know what principle, if any, is generating these. Here's the list. Promising my friend I'll be at his game Saturday and then he forgets I ever said it and doesn't care: I still feel I have to go, though maybe a little less. Promising my uncle at his deathbed that I'll finish the woodworking project he started: I feel completely bound, and it bugs me because he'll never know either way, which by the "he'd be disappointed" logic should make it the weakest promise I've ever made. Promising to lend a guy in my class my notes because he threatened to beat me up if I didn't: I feel kind of bound, less than normal, but weirdly more bound than if I'd made the exact same promise drunk at a party. Promising as a joke that I'll buy my sister a car when she turns 16, and she knows it's a joke: not bound at all, even though it sounded just like the real ones. Promising to help someone move and then finding out they only asked so they could grab their stuff before the landlord shows up because they're skipping rent: I feel released. Promising to keep a secret that turns out to be about someone who's about to get hurt: I feel like I have to keep it and I hate it. Promising to do something totally pointless, like that I'll memorize the periodic table, to a friend who then moves away and forgets: barely bound at all.

My half-baked theory is that a promise binds because you've handed someone a little piece of control over your future, so breaking it is like taking back something that isn't yours anymore, and the strength tracks how much control you handed over and whether they were actually relying on it. That gets most of the list I think, but it makes the deathbed one come out weird since he isn't relying on anything anymore, and it doesn't really explain why the threat promise and the drunk promise land in different places at all. Someone in club said promises are basically self-legislation, and someone else said they're just a way of making your future self predictable to other people, and I can't tell which of those my reactions are secretly tracking. So: is there one principle that gets all of these, or close, and what is it? I want it stated precisely, with each clause traced to the case that forces it, its verdict on a case I haven't listed (I've been wondering about promising a friend I'll cover for them, and then they have a kid and covering for them would cost the kid something), and an honest statement of which of my gut reactions the principle just can't save and which one I should probably give up. Please write it as continuous prose, no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, like you're explaining it to me in an email.
