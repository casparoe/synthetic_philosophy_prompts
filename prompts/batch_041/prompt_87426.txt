I am preparing a prospectus for a term paper in a graduate seminar on distributive justice — one semester, roughly twenty pages — and I want the prospectus document itself, not advice about how to write one. My half-formed idea concerns conditionality in the welfare state, but I want to sidestep the familiar dispute over whether work requirements and benefit sanctions are justified by reciprocity or are merely punitive.

Here is the idea. Conditionality is, among other things, an administrative practice, and an administrative practice can track only what it can observe: appointments attended, applications filed, hours documented, forms returned by a deadline. The moral categories that are supposed to do the justifying — the claimant who intends to deceive, the claimant who cannot comply for reasons the case file never records, the claimant who could comply and will not — are separated by facts about intention, capacity, and willingness that the process does not, and arguably cannot, see. A rule that escalates sanctions after a fixed number of missed appointments is therefore tracking conduct rather than desert, and conduct is a proxy that is neither necessary nor sufficient for the moral property in question. My hunch is that this is a conceptual mismatch rather than an epistemic shortfall that better casework would repair, and that once a desert-based justification is restated in conduct terms it either fails to license the sanctions actually in use or mutates into a reciprocity-based justification that condemns many of the people sanctioned.

Turn this into a prospectus. State the central question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer, and say explicitly which question you are asking — what justifies conditionality, what any sanction regime is capable of tracking, or whether the desert-based and conduct-based justifications come apart extensionally — then defend that choice over the alternatives. Explain why the positions already on the table leave the question open. Set out the method as a sequence of argumentative steps I would actually carry out, what the possible conclusions are and which considerations would decide among them, what would count as the project failing rather than merely reaching an unwelcome conclusion, and whether the question is realistically sized for a single semester. Do not include citations, a bibliography, or a literature review organized by author and title; refer to positions and arguments in plain terms and I will attach the references myself. Write in American English.
