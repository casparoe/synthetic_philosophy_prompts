My old friend Dana — we did a philosophy degree together years ago, she's now a solicitor, which may or may not be relevant — and I have a running arguement about whether doing nothing can ever be said to cause what happens next. Because she's stubborn about it, I've started keeping score of the cases she's confident about, and I think I've got them all down now. Her track record: a father who stands at the edge of a pool and watches his own toddler sink, and does nothing — she says he caused the drowning. A nurse assigned to watch a monitor who reads a magazine while the alarm sounds unnoticed — she says the nurse caused the patient's brain damage. A driver who sees a kid dart into the road and just keeps his hands off the wheel and off the brake — caused the crash. A man who'd promised his neighbour he'd water her greenhouse while she was away and then forgot, so the plants withered — caused their death. And on the other side of the ledger: a woman who has no idea the valve behind her shed has jammed open, so the neighbour's basement floods — Dana says she did not cause the flood; a non-swimmer on a dock who can only watch while a stranger goes under — not a cause; a man asleep two cities away while his flat burns, who couldn't have got back in time anyway — not a cause. It's a remarkably consistant list until you push on it, which is the fun part.

Here's the case I want to spring on her next. A woman is walking a quiet canal path and comes across a cyclist who's just gone over the handlebars and is bleeding badly but is plainly saveable — one phone call does it. She has a phone and signal, there's no danger or cost to her, nobody else is around, and she walks on; he dies. No prior relationship, no role she took on, nothing she set in motion. Will Dana say the walker caused his death? I honestly don't care that much about the yes or no — what I want is the rule she's actually running, the other rules that fit every verdict above just as well, how each of those rules comes out on the cyclist, which rule you think is really doing the work (and why you'd back it over the others), and what further case I could put to her next week that would actually separate those rules from each other. If the list genuinely underdetermines it, say so and tell me what she'd be committing to either way.
