My reading group has been chewing on that four-kinds-of-creatures picture — Darwinian ones, whose behaviour is just whatever natural selection wired in; Skinnerian ones, that also learn from what happens to them during their own lifetime; Popperian ones, that can run the trial inside their head and let the hypothesis die instead of themselves; and Gregorian ones, that get a whole extra boost because they pick up thinking tools from other people, words and numbers and maps and now presumably all sorts of other stuff. My friend says the whole ladder is one trick repeated: generate and test, generate and test, and the only thing that ever changes is the price of a failed test. The amoeba pays with its life, the rat pays with a shock, the scientist pays with a hypothesis, the engineer pays with a simulation. He also reckons the fourth rung is a bit of a cheat, that a Gregorian creature is just a Popperian one with an external hard drive, so culture is nothing but a bigger simulator. His conclusion is that there is no interesting joint anywhere in the ladder, that a rat learning a maze and a physicist testing a theory are the same kind of event with a different price tag, and that anything doing trial and error with a cheap enough test is already thinking — which means we don't need any special story about understanding at all, definately not one about inner models or representations.
