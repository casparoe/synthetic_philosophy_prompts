I've been playing a lot of that game where the rules are the game — you take turns proposing rule changes, everyone votes, and the rules about how to change the rules are themselves changeable. My friends and I keep arguing over which changes actually "take", and I want to know if there's one principle behind my gut calls. A proposal that raises the score you need to win: obviously valid. A proposal that lowers the threshold for future changes from a majority to a third: also valid, it's just a rule change like any other. A proposal that says from now on the proposer decides alone: gross, feels like a coup, but I can't call it illegal. A rule that declares itself passed no matter how the vote goes: no, that's just cheating — a rule can't be its own permission slip. A rule that retroactively says last week's failed proposal actually passed: I... kind of think that one's fine? Which is weird, since it's the same bootstrapping as the self-passing one I hated. A rule that removes the requirement to vote at all, so there's no procedure left for anything: I feel like the game is just over and nothing after that counts. And a rule that says no future rule may ever change the amendment procedure again: I can't tell if that's a clever winning move or a paradox. Is there a principle that gets all these verdicts, or close? And which one do I have to give up — I'm guessing the retroactive case, but tell me.
