Ok so I've got a sabbatical next year — nine months, no teaching, tiny travel budget — and I've decided to spend it on the transitivity of 'better than'. You know the sprectrum stuff: a day of agony vs. a very long run of mild headaches, most people say the agony is worse; now tweak each case a little — a bit less intensity traded for a lot more duration — and step along the series until you're claiming an eternity of barely-noticeable discomfort is worse than the agony, which nobody believes. Fine, that's the setup, everyone knows the setup. What I actually want to know is what's worth working on once you take the setup seriously.

So: give me a ranked agenda of the open questions here — what someone with my time, and a background in philosophy plus a bit of decision theory, should actually try to settle — and defend the ranking. Rank by what's upstream of what, not by what's fashionable. If question A gets settled, does B get easier, or turn out to be a non-question?

Some things floating around in my head that I'd like you to place on the map, promote, or throw out:

- Do we keep transitivity and give up something else instead — say, give up the assumption that 'better than' is always defined between any two items, or go vague about the comparisons, or declare the spectrum's items simply incomparable? Versus keeping full comparability and just letting 'better than' be intransitive?
- Is 'parity' — the idea that two things can be neither better, worse, nor equal, yet still comparable in some positive sense — a genuine fourth relation, or incomparability with nicer branding? Does anything downstream depend on which it is?
- The money-pump / Dutch-book style argument that intransitive or cyclic preferences leave you exploitable. Is that a real constraint on a theory of value, or only on a particular model of choice? This one seems load-bearing to me and I want to know if I'm wrong about that.
- The formal side: what structures do you actually need if you want threshold-y comparisons where small improvements don't register? I'm thinking of semiorders / interval orders, where 'better than' stays transitive but indifference doesn't, or of acyclicity without full transitivity. Does that machinery dissolve the spectrum cases, or just push the problem into the indifference relation?
- A colleague's draft claims the spectrum only looks intransitive because we hold the comparison class fixed — let what an item is being compared against shift, and the apparent cycle disappears. Real escape, or does it just relocate the problem (or make an item's value depend on what else happens to be around, which is its own can of worms)?

What I want back: for each question, why it's upstream or downstream of the others, how tractable it is for one person with no lab and no experiments, how many other things in the area hinge on the answer, and what would move it up or down your ranking. Then tell me which one you'd bet the sabbatical on, what the first month of work would look like, and what I'm wrong to think is central. Be honest about which of these are dead ends that people keep talking about mainly because they're fun to argue about.

I can't read everything, so I want the three or four where progress would actually unblock other people's work. Not a survey, and please don't hand me thier reading lists — I want the argument for the ordering.
