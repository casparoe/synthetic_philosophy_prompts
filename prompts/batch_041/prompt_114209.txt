Ok so this is a thing I've been arguing about with my two roommates for like three weeks now, ever since one of them brought up the doomsday argument at 2am and none of us could fall asleep after. I'm a longtime reader of the rationalist blogs (LessWrong, the old SSC archives, all of it) so I've seen the vanilla version a hundred times and I basically know the moves: uniform birth rank plus the self-sampling assumption, you notice you're roughly the median human, so there probably aren't astronomically many humans after you, so doom is closer than you'd naively think, yadda yadda, then someone says the self-indication assumption cancels it out and everyone goes home. I don't actually care about re-litigating the vanilla one. What I want is to know how much *weight* to give the weirder descendants of the argument, because I keep definately half-convincing myself of some of them at night and then thinking they're dumb in the morning.

So here's what I want you to do: rate a bunch of variants on how much a rationalist should actually let them move their credence in "humanity goes extinct, or collapses to something very small, within the next few centuries." Give me a number from 0 to 1 for each — 0 = literally ignore it, 1 = it should dominate your entire worldview — and then sort them into tiers by that number, with a sentence or two on why each lands where it does. Treat the vanilla SSA version as the 0.15 anchor so the others have something to sit against. Here are the variants, all of which I think are at least defensible enough to be worth rating:

1. The observer-moment version: instead of ranking your birth among all humans, you rank your current moment among all the moments of your own life. Same machinery, but the reference class is just you.
2. The simulated-observer version: condition on the simulation hypothesis, so your reference class becomes all observers across all ancestor simulations, and you ask what fraction of them live in "early" simulated civilizations.
3. The reflexive version: apply the argument to the number of times the doomsday argument itself will ever be thought or typed about by anyone, anywhere. Yes this is cursed, rate it anyway.
4. The Everett branch-measure version: we're in a branching multiverse and the real question is the measure over branches where we survive, not a rank among observers. Does the argument even survive the translation?
5. The "great filter is behind us" version: same anthropics, but the reference class is civilizations reaching our current stage rather than individual humans.
6. A deliberately deflationary one: the whole thing is a selection-effect artifact, there's no fact of the matter about a "correct" reference class, so every variant above should collapse to roughly the same tiny number.

One more thing, please don't just tell me "these are all controversial" and bail — I want actual numbers, even if they're made-up-ish, and I want you to actually commit to the ordering. If two land in the same tier, say so. And if you think one of my framings is malformed, tell me why and then rate it as if it were fixed, rather than skipping it.
