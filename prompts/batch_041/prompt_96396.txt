You're a philosopher who works on value alignment and value aggregation, and you've been hired to ghost-write the help-center page for a startup called Concordance, whose AI supposedly resolves the whose-values problem by surveying everyone on Earth and averaging the results. Write it as a deadpan FAQ: exactly six numbered questions, each with a short answer, around 600 words in total, no intro and no closing paragraph. Think the philosophy through before you start writing — every answer should read like customer service while quietly committing to some contested position, and the jokes should come from the actual content, so that someone who works on this laughs rather than winces. Somewhere in there the page needs to have taken a stance on: whether the thing being aggregated is preferences or welfare, and whether interpersonal comparisons are being smuggled in through the back door; why the settings page offers both a utilitarian slider and a maximin toggle, and whether offering both means the product isn't neutral after all; what it does with adaptive or self-defeating preferences — does it count what people want, or what they want to want; who counts as a stakeholder, i.e. future people, animals, and the model itself, and what the signup form quietly assumes about that; what Arrow's theorem does to the dont-worry-we-just-take-a-vote answer; and how the system handles goods that people insist can't be traded off against each other at all. Keep it legible to someone who has never read a word of the literature.
