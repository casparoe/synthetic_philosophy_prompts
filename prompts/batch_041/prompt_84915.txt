I work in the policy unit of a civil liberties charity, and every week we have to sign off or oppose something — curfews, ID checks, workplace rules, benefit conditions — and the arguement always turns into a shouting match about freedom. I want a reusable screening proceedure my team can run in half an hour, before anything goes to the director, that actually uses the three ideas of liberty: negative liberty (no one interfering), positive liberty (being one's own master and having the real capacity to act), and republican liberty (not being dependent on anyone's goodwill, even if they don't actually interfere). Give me the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a clear yes or no, and where it has to hand the decision to a human because it's a value call rather than a fact. Keep it usable by people who haven't read the theory, don't let positive liberty collapse into just having resources — unfreedom and inability aren't the same thing — and be honest about where it will misfire. Then run it on three proposals: a night-time curfew in a high-crime neighbourhood, mandatory digital ID for social media accounts, and a workplace ban on managers dating their subordinates. I especially need it to handle the case where a policy reduces interference but increases domination, and the case where positive liberty seems to justify paternalism. One of the worked examples should come out in a way our campaigners won't like. British English, please.
