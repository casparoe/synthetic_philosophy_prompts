I want to know whether the following has an established name, and if it does not, I want it named and defined.

Here is the pattern. Someone asserts a probability: "there is roughly a one-in-five chance the cancer comes back." Pressed for the basis, they appeal to a class of similar cases. When a disanalogy is raised, they adjust the class — narrowing it here, widening it there — and announce a revised figure. Each adjustment is individually defensible, but the adjustments share a direction: they move the estimate toward whatever the speaker wanted to claim, and the estimate can never be refuted, because for any challenge there is another class that yields it.

So far that is close to the reference class problem, and I can name that much myself. What I cannot place is what happens when you ask what the number *is*. The same speaker will defend it with frequencies ("in patients like this, one in five recur"), rely on it as a degree of belief ("I would bet at those odds"), and yet insist it is a feature of the case itself ("the real chance for *this* tumor") — switching among the three as the objection requires. Frequentism answers one objection, subjectivism another, propensity a third, and no single one is ever held long enough to be tested. This is not the reference class problem, which is a symmetric point about the underdetermination of frequency claims; here the interpretation is chosen after the fact to protect a figure that is already fixed.

Does this have a name — in the philosophy of probability, in the statistics of forecasting, or anywhere else? I am not after a label alone. If there is an established term, tell me which, and say exactly how the thing I have described differs from its nearest neighbors: the reference class problem, the problem of the single case, and principled pluralism about interpretations, which I take to be a different animal because it is held consistently rather than opportunistically. If there is no established term, coin one, and give me a definition with edges — what must be present, what merely tends to accompany it, and two boundary cases: one that resembles it but should be excluded, and one that is easy to miss. A bare name will not help me. I need the carving.
