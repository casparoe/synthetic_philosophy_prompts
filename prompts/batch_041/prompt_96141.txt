I've been reading Lewis on convention: a regularity in behaviour that everyone in a population follows, expects everyone else to follow, and prefers to keep following given that the others do — held in place by mutual expectation rather than by agreement, enforcement, or anyone's decree — and I want to test how much of it survives a change of vocabulary.

Restate that account as faithfully as you can in the terms I actually work in. Keep the translation honest: don't quietly swap Lewis's coordination problem for "what audiences happen to like," and don't let my vocabulary's assumptions pass as his.

Then the residue report. Which parts come through intact, which only with distortion, and what has no counterpart at all? Two things interest me particularly. First, common knowledge: I know the difference between a reader recognising that a trope is in play and every reader knowing that every other reader knows — pastiche, camp, and the twist everyone has heard about before opening the book live somewhere in that gap. Does that distinction do any of the work Lewis needs it to do, or is it doing something else? Second, how a convention gets established rather than merely sustained: does my vocabulary say anything about that, or does it only relocate the puzzle? Close by telling me whether the residue points to something Lewis's account leaves out, or to something about literary convention that was never a norm in his sense to begin with.
