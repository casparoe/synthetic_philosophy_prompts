I am preparing a paper on the value of originals and want to fix my target before I evaluate anyone's account of it. Take the purpose to be this: a theory good enough to adjudicate, in a principled way, the recurring disputes over whether a copy may stand in for an original — a digital surrogate for a fragile manuscript, an authorized replica on display while the original is in storage, a restoration that replaces most of the original material, an indistinguishable forgery discovered only after decades on the wall.

Write me the desiderata such a theory must meet. Be specific about the phenomena and the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, the puzzles it must dissolve rather than merely relocate, and the considerations it must remain consistent with (including, for instance, whatever we learn from the market, from conservation practice, and from the fact that we routinely treat some non-art objects — relics, specimens, first editions, source data — as originals whose copies are deficient). State the case or consideration behind each requirement, so that I can see which ones are doing work and which are inherited prejudice.

Two framings I want you to take seriously, since I suspect much of the literature conflates them. First, keep three questions apart: why we care about the original at all; when a copy fails to do what the original does; and when something's value is diminished by its being a copy (a fake, a replica, a reproduction). A theory may answer one without answering the others, and I want the desiderata to force it to say which it is answering. Second, treat "original" as possibly a relational rather than an intrinsic property, and say what the theory owes us once that possibility is on the table.

Where you can, flag any pair of desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — that is the part I most want to see — and mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are commitments of one camp rather than genuine constraints. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; a brief scorecard of how the main extant positions do against the list is welcome but secondary.

Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Where you are unsure whether a requirement is load-bearing or merely customary, say so rather than asserting it. No citations or reading list — I will supply those.
