I have no training in philosophy, but for about a year I have been stuck on a puzzle I cannot put down, and what I want from you is a piece of satire about it rather than an essay.

The puzzle is this. Suppose a group of people wants to decide something together, democratically. Before they can, somebody has to settle who is in the group. But whoever settles it is already deciding something on behalf of a group, so the settling must already have been done by the right group. Every way I try to get around this, the same thing happens: the people drawing the circle are standing inside a circle that somebody else drew. I am told that people who study this have written a great deal about it; I have not read them.

So here is what I would like. Invent a country, give it a name, and imagine that after a long and ugly dispute over who was entitled to vote on its founding constitution, it did the bureaucratic thing and created a permanent government office whose sole job is to answer the question of who belongs to the people. The office has a website. I want you to write the FAQ from that website.

The office must be entirely sincere, helpful, and quietly proud of its procedures. The comedy should come from its taking its own logic seriously, not from it being stupid or self-aware. Each answer should satisfy a reasonable citizen while making the original problem a little worse. Please work in the following, at least:

- The test for membership is being affected by the decisions of the people, applied as literally as the office can manage, which pulls in people who have never lived there, people not yet born, children, and foreign suppliers, and forces the office to keep drawing lines anyway.
- Any change to the boundary must be approved by a referendum of the people as currently defined, so admitting new members requires the consent of the very people whose votes would be diluted.
- The office's own authority rests on a vote taken before it existed, by an electorate whose membership is one of the things it is supposed to determine.
- A category of provisional members whose status has been provisional for decades, and a form for appealing it.
- At least one answer in which the office explains that it cannot answer, because the question is about who is allowed to ask it.

Requirements: deadpan throughout, no narrator, no winking at the reader, no real countries and no named thinkers or works (the office would never cite anyone). It should be funny to someone who has read the academic literature on this problem and readable to someone who has not. Where it is absurd, the absurdity should exaggerate something genuinely at stake rather than being invented from nothing.

Please give me:

1. "The FAQ" (about 750 words): a heading for the office and then the FAQ itself, in the office's voice, eight to ten questions with answers.
2. "Form D-1" (about 150 words): an appendix reproducing the office's application form as plain text, laid out the way the office would actually lay it out.
3. "What each joke is aimed at" (about 250 words): out of character, plainly, saying what real difficulty sits behind each of the main jokes, so I can judge whether the piece is fair or merely silly.
