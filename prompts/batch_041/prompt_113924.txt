My partner died last spring. A friend told me something I can't shake: that a person isn't a thing that ends when the body does, but a pattern copied into the brains of everyone who knew them, so the copy running in my head is still her, only coarser. I half believe it — I've absorbed her vocabulary and can predict her opinions on things she never lived to see — but the copies have drifted. Mine is wittier and kinder than she actually was, and her sister's has drifted somewhere else entirely, so I no longer know which of us holds the real one. I'm giving the eulogy in two weeks. Near the end she asked me to name the difficult parts of her life and not make her a saint; her sister wants the saint. If my memory really is what's left of her, what do I owe it — fidelity or mercy? Give me a direct recommendation with your reasoning, and say how much turns on whether my friend's claim about persons is true. Write in American English.
