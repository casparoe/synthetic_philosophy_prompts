Hey, I'm a third-year phil student currently doing a course on social ontology and norms, and in two weeks it's my turn to run the seminar. The topic is the convention/norm thing — whether a norm is just a convention with teeth, or whether "norm" adds something a convention can't have. Last year the same session turned into everyone defending whatever author they'd happened to read, and nobody actually found out what they thought. So I want to build a decision tree / taxonomy that sorts people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like.

Setup: everyone starts from the bit we all agree on — that lots of social life is coordinated, and that people comply with rules nobody is forcing them to follow. Treat that as the root, and make every branch node a question a classmate could answer without knowing where it leads them. Aim for 6–8 nodes and 6–7 leaves; each leaf should carry the name of the position, or "unnamed" if nobody defends it, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Names of positions are fine, I don't need citations for them, and no reading list please — I've got the reading.

Distinctions I need the tree to actually turn on, because these are the ones that matter for the class: whether the underlying situation is one where interests line up (any equilibrium is as good for everyone) or one where they conflict; whether compliance is driven by "I expect others to do it", "I expect others to expect me to do it", "I'd be punished", or "it would be wrong even if nobody else did it"; whether deviation gets no reaction, re-coordination, or blame; whether the rule is arbitrary in the sense that a different one would have worked just as well; and whether the practice needs participants to represent each other's expectations at all, or whether a population of very dumb reinforcement learners could converge on it with no representing going on.

Cases I want used, at least most of them: which side of the road to drive on; who speaks first in a conversation; tipping in a city you'll never come back to; a footpath across a field that everyone uses and nobody owns; standing for an anthem; burying the dead; a college tradition everyone privately hates but nobody breaks; a rule still on the books that nobody follows. And one case I want in because it makes the tree interesting: a rule everyone complies with only because each person thinks everyone else approves of it, when in fact almost nobody does.

Build it from the logical space first — the questions and the combinations of answers they allow — and only then locate positions in it. If a combination has nobody standing in it, tell me whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Please lay it out as:

Bottom line — about 80 words on which one or two questions do most of the sorting work, and the single distinction I should hammer on the board.
The tree — roughly 700–900 words. Nodes as questions, branches labelled, leaves named with the awkward-commitment sentence.
Branches nobody's standing in — about 200 words.
Pairs the tree can't split — about 150 words, and what extra question would separate them if I added it.
If I have to cut it to five questions — about 120 words on which nodes go.
