Before you write a word, brainstorm for a few lines and get clear in your own head on why the human case is supposed to be different from classifying, I dont know, quarks, and what exactly is supposed to be looping back onto what. Then write the poem.

The form I want is a pantoum, and I'm picking it on purpose. A pantoum is built out of lines that keep returning, but every time a line comes back its in a new spot and its been quietly recontextualized by everything around it, and the standard pantoum closes by folding its last line back into its first, so the whole thing is a closed loop. That's the looping effect. Use that. Four or five quatrains.

The recurring lines should be split between two voices: one is the voice of the manual — the flat clinical register, the kind of sentence that gets written down about somebody — and the other is the voice of the person that sentence is written about. As the poem loops, those two lines should migrate toward each other, so that by the end the manual's line sounds like it's being spoken by the person and the person's line sounds like it's being filed. That migration is the whole point: the classification reshapes the person, the person reshapes the classification, and neither one holds still. If a reader can feel the swap happen in the repetition itself, you've done it.

A few things I care about:
