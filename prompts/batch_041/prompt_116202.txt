I'm a novelist, and next month I'm leading a two-hour session at a writers' residency on a question I keep circling in my own work: if moral intuitions differ across cultures, does that tell against the idea that any moral claim is objectively true? The audience will be intelligent non-specialists — novelists, mostly, a couple of translators, one retired physician — with no formal training in philosophy, anthropology, or experimental psychology. I don't want you to explain the material to me. I want a plan for how I should explain it.

Lay out the order in which the pieces have to go, and say why each depends on the one before it. My instinct is that the whole thing collapses if I let them hear the empirical finding before they have any grip on the difference between disagreeing about a verdict and disagreeing about the principle behind it, but tell me if that's wrong. Specify the single case that should carry the session, and what it has to have and lack. I had thought of the cross-cultural ultimatum game work, where in some small-scale societies offers above half have been refused, apparently because accepting an unusually generous gift creates an obligation rather than discharging one. It needs to be a case where the local response has its own rationale, so the audience cannot file it under irrationality, and it must not be so exotic that it reads as mere curiosity.

Then map the misconceptions in the order they will arise — I expect relativism to be confused with tolerance, objectivity with universal agreement, variation with arbitrariness, and the WEIRD sampling problem to be treated as a debunking of the whole enterprise rather than a constraint on it — and tell me exactly where in the sequence to head each one off. I would also like the work on culturally varying susceptibility to the Müller-Lyer illusion placed somewhere in there, as a way of showing that variation alone does not settle what is real; tell me whether that earns its place or is a distraction.

Finish with the check for understanding that would show the question has landed rather than just the discomfort, and with what I should openly refuse to explain at this level and say so.

Please answer in British English.
