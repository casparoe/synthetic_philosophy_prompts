So: what should I fund?

Here's the shape of it as I understand it — tell me if you think that's the right shape. There's the simple version, where you just can't trace the causal ripples far enough to say whether an action did more good than harm, and the complex version, where even with the full causal story the probabilities you'd need aren't determinate — not unknown but not well-defined, so the expected value isn't a number you could in principle write down. And then there's the thing where, if you take the far future seriously, microscopic differences in how likely your action is to shift it swamp everything else, which makes any expected-value ranking wildly unstable.

Which of the live responses is most worth developing? The ones I can see: hunt for actions that are robustly good across a wide range of hypotheses; keep a consequentialist criterion of rightness but let the decision procedure be something completely different (rules, heuristics, act as though you weren't clueless); rework the axiology so value isn't so sensitive to distant and improbable effects; or just say this is a special case of decision-making under deep uncertainty and import whatever the decision theorists have. Some of these feel like they're answering different questions from each other, and some feel like cheating.

So what I want: rank the open questions here by how much other work depends on them, tell me which are actually tractable for philosophers working alone with no empirical data, and which I should leave alone because the answer wouldn't change anything downstream.
