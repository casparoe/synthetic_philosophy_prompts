I'm a science and ideas journalist putting together a long feature on "low-impact" AI — the research programme that tries to build agents which pursue their assigned goals while leaving the rest of the world as undisturbed as they can. The more people I interview, the more I suspect that "side effect" and "impact" are doing an enormous amount of unexamined work, and that every proposed definition quietly smuggles in a picture of what the world ought to have looked like anyway. Before I write the piece, I want a specification rather than a verdict: set out the desiderata that any adequate account of side effects, or of low impact, must meet. What cases must it get right — an agent that moves a box it was asked to move versus one that shifts the same box merely to reach something behind it; an agent told to cool a room which, in cooling it, ruins a sculpture; a cleaning robot that "tidies" a child's toys into a bin; an agent that hits its target by a route leaving a trace nothing will ever notice? Which distinctions must it respect — between a side effect and an intended means, between a change that is irreversible and one that is merely unwanted, between impact on a single object and impact on a shared resource that others depend on? And which results must it be consistent with — that an agent cannot know the full consequences of its actions, that "no change" is not itself a well-defined baseline, and that any measure of deviation from a baseline will be sensitive to how the baseline is chosen?

I'd also like you to bring in, and take a position on, two framings I keep encountering. The first is what a colleague calls the charitable completion view: a side effect is any change to the environment that the agent's own objective would not reward, once the user's instruction has been completed in the most charitable way consistent with it — the thought being that we can read "what was asked for" off the instruction itself, rather than off a prior theory of value. The second treats impact as statistical distance: measure it as the divergence between the distribution over futures with the agent present and the distribution without it, so that low impact simply means that the world looks as it would have had you not acted at all. Say what each would have to assume in order to work, where each breaks down, and whether either escapes the charge that specifying a baseline is already specifying a value.

Then give me the list itself: each desideratum with the case or consideration that motivates it; a note on which requirements are genuine and which are merely prejudices of one camp in the safety-versus-capability debate; the ones you take to be non-negotiable; and — the part I most want — any subset that cannot be satisfied together, together with the single requirement that does the most to rule out the popular proposals. Please write in British English.
