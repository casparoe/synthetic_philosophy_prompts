I teach a grad seminar on philosophy of mind and we've just finished a unit on predictive processing and the free energy principle. I'll admit up front that I've always been a bit of a skeptic: the formulations of the FEP I can actually get my hands on seem to oscillate between something almost tautological (any system that persists has to keep itself within certain bounds) and something empirically loaded about brains in particular, and I can never quite tell which one is doing the real work. One of my students, who comes at this from computational neuroscience, keeps insisting it's the most important unifying idea in the cognitive sciences, though when I ask him to finish the sentence "since..." he never quite manages it.

Anyway, here's what I actually want from you. Take it as given that the free energy principle is at least roughly right, and that it's meant to apply not just to brains but to any self-maintaining system with a boundary that persists by minimizing surprise. What are the most important implications of that? And I don't mean the shopworn stuff about perception as controlled hallucination or brains as prediction machines. I want the downstream consequences a philosopher would actually care about: what it does to the boundary between self and world, whether such a system can meaningfully be said to have interests or be harmed, what it implies about mental disorder and whether "misperception" is even the right frame there, and whatever else you think is most consequential that I haven't thought to ask about. Order them by how much they actually follow from the framework rather than from the enthusiasm of its advocates.

A couple of things. If you think some of these "implications" are really just restatements of the premises in new vocabulary, or that the FEP can't bear the weight people put on it, please say so plainly rather than dutifully producing a tidy list -- I'd genuinely rather you push back on the framing than comply. And flag wherever you're extrapolating well past what the actual framework licenses, because I suspect that's most of the interesting territory here.
