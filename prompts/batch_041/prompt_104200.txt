Im teaching a food ethics module to about 18 undergrads in one 80-minute block, and I want a runnable activity that makes them feel the force of the wild animal suffering point instead of just nodding along while I assert it. Heres the rough idea, please rebuild or improve it.

Set up: teams of 3-4 play a college dining committee. Fixed weekly budget, fixed calorie target, and they have to submit a menu drawn from beef, chicken, eggs, and a plant-heavy mix. Their brief says only: minimize animal suffering. They dont get told how suffering will be scored. Give them 15 minutes, make them commit, and have each team defend their ranking.

Then reveal the accounting in three stages, letting them revise between stages but recording what they said before each reveal. Stage one: per calorie, the number of individual animals killed is far higher for chicken and fish than for cattle, so you trade one big death for hundreds of small ones. Stage two: plant agriculture isnt a death-free baseline, field animals get killed in cultivation, so a vegan menu relocates the deaths rather than removing them, and since cattle eat a lot of feed crops, beef carries a multiplied crop burden on top of the slaughter. Stage three: the total number of wild animals, overwhelmingly small and invertebrate, dwarfs every farmed animal on earth, and if you take r-selected life histories seriously, enormous broods and brutal juvenile mortality, many of those lives plausibly go badly. Now the committees carefully minimized number is a rounding error against a background none of them chose.

I want the debrief to do the real work: map each reveal onto a live issue (number of deaths vs number of lives, total vs average, invertebrate sentience, replaceability), show how their confidence moved, and be honest that the exercise cant settle whether totalism is right.

Two things. Keep the numbers structural and transparent rather than precise, per-calorie death counts scaling with body size, feed conversion multipliers, that kind of thing, and have me flag which figures are contested and present them as bands instead of facts. And tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time: I expect at least one team to cry foul that the scoring was rigged, and the wild animal reveal sometimes flattens people into a so nothing matters mood that I need a move for. Also say which part of the idea the game cant reproduce and I have to supply myself.
