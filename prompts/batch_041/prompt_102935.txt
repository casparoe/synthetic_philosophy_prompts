And here's what the student wrote:

(a) Assume there's a truth nobody knows: p and not-Kp. By the knowability principle every truth is possibly known, so it's possible to know this one: possibly K(p and not-Kp). Knowing a conjunction means knowing each conjunct, so possibly (Kp and K(not-Kp)). Knowledge implies truth, so from K(not-Kp) we get not-Kp. So possibly (Kp and not-Kp). That can't be — p can't be both known and unknown, and a contradiction can't be possible in any normal modal logic. So the assumption that there is an unknown truth has to be rejected: there are no unknown truths, which is to say every truth is known.

(b) The load-bearing assumptions are three: knowledge implies truth, knowledge distributes over conjunction, and the knowability principle itself. The modal background is minimal — normal modal logic plus the fact that a contradiction can't be possible. We also need a transparency principle, i.e. if you know that p then you know that you know that p; without it the move from K(not-Kp) to not-Kp has nothing to lean on, and the whole thing only goes through for an idealized knower. Other than that nothing controversial about knowledge is needed.

(c) Swap knowledge for rational belief and factivity is gone — you can rationally believe something false. So it has to be replaced by two things: transparency (if you rationally believe p then you rationally believe that you rationally believe p), which does the work factivity was doing, and consistency of rational belief (you can't rationally believe p and not-p at the same time). With those in place the same derivation runs and you get: if every truth could be rationally believed, then every truth is rationally believed.

(d) The derivation is intuitionistically fine right up to the end. The last step — from there are no unknown truths to every truth is known — goes through a classical equivalence between not-(p and not-Kp) and p implies Kp, so an intuitionist never gets the omniscience conclusion. That's the standard intuitionist line and it's enough: the knowability principle no longer collapses into omniscience.
