My friend and I got into it for like three hours last night about whether sentience comes in degrees, and it ended with neither of us able to say what we were actually disagreeing about. Her line was that it's obviously a dimmer switch — a thermostat has some tiny sliver, a bee has more, a dog more, a person more — and that asking is-it-conscious is a leftover of thinking about it as a switch. Mine was that even if experience admits of degrees, that tells you nothing about whether moral status does, because moral status might be threshold-y even if sentience isn't, or superlinear in it, or the shape of that curve might just be unknowable. Her other move, which I couldn't answer cleanly, was: forget a single scalar, just use a checklist — nociception, integration, self-modeling, valence, whatever — and rank creatures by how many boxes they tick. My objection was that a checklist doesn't aggregate into a ranking without you smuggling in weights, and the weights are the whole ballgame.

We also kept circling the thermostat: it has a goal state, error correction, and represents its environment in some thin sense, so if you insist it sits at zero, what exactly is missing? Integration, valence, a self-model, something it's like to be there, or none of those because they're all circular?
