I'm in an intro survey on Chinese philosophy and we just wrapped the School of Names unit, and I'm stuck on the White Horse Dialogue. The translation we used runs the main exchange like this:

[Advocate] "'Horse' is what designates shape. 'White' is what designates color. What designates color is not what designates shape. Therefore I say: a white horse is not a horse."

[Objector] "If there is a white horse, one cannot say there is no horse. If one cannot say there is no horse, does that not mean there is a horse? Since having a white horse counts as having a horse, how can the white [one] not be a horse?"

[Advocate] "If one seeks a horse, yellow and black horses may all be delivered. If one seeks a white horse, yellow and black horses may not be delivered. Suppose a white horse were [just] a horse. Then what is sought [in the two cases] would be the same. If what is sought were the same, then 'white' would not differ from 'horse.'"

Our prof spent maybe ten minutes on it and basically said "it turns on whether 'is' is the 'is' of identity or the 'is' of predication" and moved on, which I don't find realy helps me do anything with the argument.
