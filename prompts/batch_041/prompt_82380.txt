ok this one is a bit odd. a friend of mine runs a tiny zine where each issue is poems about formal systems (issue 3 was TNT, this one is typogenetics) and ive been voluntold into writing for it. quick refresher in case you havent thought about it since college: typogenetics is hofstadters made-up toy biology from gödel escher bach, short for typographical genetics. strands are just strings of the letters A C G T. enzymes are strings of amino acids, and the amino acids get read off a strand two letters at a time. an enzyme grabs a strand and does stuff to it: snips it, deletes a letter, inserts one, swaps two neighbours, copies a stretch into its complement. and the trick is that a strand can code for the enzymes that copy it, so the same marks are also a program for making more of those marks. which is the same shape as gödel numbering -- you assign numbers to symbols, and then a string of symbols can be read as a claim about strings of symbols, itself included. nothing means anything until you read the marks as instructions for rearranging marks.

what i want is a pantoum, four stanzas, maybe five if you need the room. the interlocking repeats (lines 2 and 4 of each stanza coming back as lines 1 and 3 of the next) should be the copying, and the fact that a pantoum closes by hauling its own opening lines back at the very end should be the loop snapping shut -- the enzyme coming back around to act on the strand that built it. one voice should be the strand (marks, no meaning yet) and one should be the enzyme (a reading of those marks that turns out to be orders). and since pantoum repeats are word for word, i want at least one line that reads as a plain description of marks the first time it shows up and as an instruction the second time, purely because of whats sitting next to it -- no paraphrase allowed, thats the fidelity. the two voices should get harder to tell apart as it goes, since thats the isomorphism: same stuff, two levels. by the end, someone who actually knows typogenetics should see why a strand that codes for the enzyme that copies it is pulling exactly the same move as gödels sentence, and someone who's never heard of any of it should still get a decent poem out of it. dont rhyme if it fights the repeats.
