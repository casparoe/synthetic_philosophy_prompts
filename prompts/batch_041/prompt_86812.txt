I'm in a philosophy of mind seminar this term and we've spent like three weeks on mental causation, and the whole time I've had this nagging feeling that the thing everyone is fighting about is one instance of something way more general, and I want to know if the general thing has a name.

Here's the pattern I keep seeing. You describe an outcome in two ways, one coarse and one fine, and the coarse one looks like it's doing real causal work: the bridge collapsed because the design was flawed. Then someone gives you the fine story — each crack propagated the way it did because of the local stress at that exact point — and the design flaw turns out to be nothing over and above a summary of all those local facts. Now the coarse story feels demoted. It isn't false, it's just re-describing something the fine story already fully fixed. It doesn't add any push of its own. It's a label for a pattern, not a further cause.

Two other places I've run into the same shape. In a bio lecture someone asked whether the heart's function is to pump blood, and the TA said sure, but everything the heart does is settled by the proteins and the ion gradients, so pumping blood is a description of what's happening, not an extra cause on top. And in a stats class we watched a demo where you predict an outcome from some broad variable and it looks strong, then you add the detailed variable sitting underneath it and the broad one's coefficient basically goes to zero — the detailed one absorbs it. That felt like the same shape as the bridge.

So what I want is the carving, not just a label. Specifically:

- Is there a name for this general pattern — the finer description always seems to take the causal credit, leaving the coarser one idle even though we clearly can't do without it? And what exactly is the claim about: causation itself, or our explanations, or our concepts? Everyone in my seminar slides between these three without noticing and I suspect that's most of the disagreement.
- How is this different from overdetermination? Overdetermination is two causes that are each sufficient and independent; here the two candidates aren't independent at all, one is made out of the other. And how is it different from just saying the fine story is the real cause and the coarse one is a mere symptom or correlation?
- Is the stats demo the same relation or a different one? "It absorbs the other variable in the model" and "it makes the other one causally idle" sound similar but I have a feeling they come apart.
- Where does the pattern fail? I'd like at least one clear case where the fine story does not crowd out the coarse one, and an account of what's different there. My guesses would be something about selection, or about the fine facts being a mess while the coarse ones are the ones that support counterfactuals, but I don't trust either guess.
- If there's no single established name for the general thing, coin one and define it sharply enough that I can tell when it applies and when it doesn't.

Don't just name-drop at me — I can tell when someone is gesturing at a literature instead of doing the work. American English, please.
