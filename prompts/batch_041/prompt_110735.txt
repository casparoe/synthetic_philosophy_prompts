I've been going back and fourth on the procreation asymmetry for years, and the thing that bugs me is that everyone argues it case by case without ever saying what a theory of it would have to do to be any good. So before I read another EA forum take: give me the desiderata. Not a survey of who's said what, just the spec.

What does any theory that wants to underwrite the asymmetry have to deliver? I want the list, with the case or consideration behind each item: the cases it has to get right (the miserable life it's wrong to start, the happy life nobody has to start, the existing person whose welfare we obviously do have to care about), the distinctions it can't collapse (creating vs. continuing, what's good for a person vs. what makes the world better, an asymmetry in reasons vs. an asymmetry in values), and the results it can't contradict (no repugnant conclusion, no story on which the never-conceived are deprived, no duty to breed, and once someone is here their welfare counts the same as anyone else's).

Two things I especially want pinned down, since I suspect they're where it falls apart:

1. Uncertainty. Imagine a coin: heads, the kid gets a wonderful life, tails, a life of unrelenting pain. The asymmetry seems to say the tails branch counts against and the heads branch doesn't count for, which makes the gamble look bad -- but every act of procreation is a gamble with some tail risk, so now I need a story for why having kids is OK at all. Is there a version of the asymmetry that survives this, or is asymmetry + risk + ordinary permissibility just an inconsistent set?

2. Where the asymmetry stops. It's supposed to be about bringing someone into existence, not about making an existing person worse off, so at what point does the maybe-person become someone who can be made worse off -- conception, viability, birth, first preference? And does that answer also decide whether the asymmetry applies to minds we make instead of birth, like an AI we might train that might suffer?

Flag any pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied -- that's the part I actually want. A table is fine.
