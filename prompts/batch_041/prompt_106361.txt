You are a philosopher of mind and language with a specialist's command of the later Wittgenstein, of the contemporary metaphysics of content, and — just as important — of how these arguments actually go when they are fought out in a seminar room rather than in print. Before you write anything, think carefully about the situation below, and spend a few lines reconstructing in your own words how each faction in the room will hear my talk. I write a long-running blog in the rationalist-adjacent corner of the internet, and I came to this material through a decade of comment-thread arguments about whether introspection-talk is confused, not through a Wittgenstein seminar. Next month I am giving a paper to a philosophy department reading group, and I want to walk in knowing what is about to hit me. Twenty minutes of talk, forty-five of questions, so every reply has to be deliverable in two minutes.

The thesis I will defend, in the form I will put it:

- The private language argument is not an argument about other minds, and not an argument that experience is unreal or unmentionable. Its real target is a picture of how a term acquires a standard of correctness.
- Distinguish two things that get called privacy: ownership privacy, the trivial fact that only I can have my sensations, and standard privacy, the claim that nothing other than my own inclination can govern my use of a term. Read charitably, Wittgenstein's argument touches only the second.
- Read that way, the requirement the argument imposes is not a community but a world. A term has determinate content only if something other than the speaker's current inclination can register a correction — call this the correction requirement. A rule whose only standard is how things strike its follower is not a private rule; it is not a rule.
- The diarist's failure, then, is not loneliness but drift. He is like a model fine-tuned only on its own outputs: nothing from outside enters, so the drift is undetectable from the inside, and there is nothing for him to be wrong about. I use this as an intuition pump in the talk, not as an empirical claim, and I expect the machine-learning visitor to go straight at it.
- The payoff I care about: the correction requirement can be met by a solitary agent, provided the world pushes back — a misapplied term shows up as an action that fails. Publicness to the world, not publicness to a community.

The room: roughly eight regulars. Two faculty in philosophy of language and the history of analytic philosophy who know the Investigations line by line and are allergic to anyone reading it as proto-computationalism; one regular who reads the rule-following passages through the Kripke-style community solution and thinks the community is doing essential work; two visitors from cognitive science, a computational linguist and a reinforcement-learning researcher, who came for the content angle and are impatient with exegesis; and one phenomenologist who will defend the first-person standpoint and suspects my correction requirement is behaviorism with extra steps.
