Right, so here's where I've landed and I want it stress-tested. I think my origin is essential to me: there's no possible world in which I exist and came from a different sperm and a different egg. I got there the Kripke-ish way, via the intuition that a person assembled from other gametes would be someone else, a sibling at best, and I definately buy it. What I want is what would count against it, ranked, not a summary of the view.

I already know the cheap objections (conceivability, how could you know what's possible). What actually worries me is whether the thing I'm essentialising is even well defined. Three cases bother me:

First, twinning. Identical twins come from a single fertilised egg, so the two of them share an origin — same sperm, same egg, same zygote — and yet they're two people. Fine, origin was never supposed to be sufficient. But then something else has to be doing the individuating for them, and if that something else can fix identity within a world I don't see why it can't fix it across worlds, in which case origin isn't carrying the weight I thought it was.

Second, fusion. There are real people whose bodies are a patchwork of two cell lines because two early embryos merged into one. One person, two fertilised eggs. Which sperm and egg are their origin? If the answer is 'both, vaguely' then the claim has a vagueness problem; if it's 'they're really two of us' then I think the view has said something false about an actual human being.

Third, the gametes themselves. The egg that made me wasn't a finished object sitting around waiting — the oocyte only completes its division after the sperm arrives, and throws off a second cell in the process, and the sperm that matters is basically a nucleus that gets delivered. So which object is it whose identity is supposed to be essential? If it's the DNA, then a world where the same genome is assembled without those two cells should still be a world containing me, which sounds wrong. If it's the cells, then a world where those cells exist but carry different DNA should still be a world containing me, which sounds even wronger.

So: what would count against the view, sorted from decisive to merely awkward? Which of these would an essentialist themselves concede as a real cost, and which would they brush off as not even a defeater? And be honest about whether the whole thing has quietly turned into a claim about how we use names rather than a claim about me — if the necessity is downstream of our naming practices I want to know that, and I want to know whether that's actually a problem or just what all essence claims turn out to look like. The thing that would move me most is a case where the natural verdict is obviously 'same person, different gametes' and I only resist because I've been marinated in the essentialist line.
