Me and two friends are doing a mini reading group on Parfit's repugnant conclusion — the slide where for any population of really well-off people there's a much bigger population whose lives are only just worth living but that has more welfare in total, so totalism drags you all the way down to the giant dim world at the end. We keep hitting the same wall: everyone can see the slide, nobody can say where it goes wrong, and its hard to make the thing vivid without just gesturing at a huge dull crowd. So for the last session I want to open with a poem instead of yet another argument.

Could you write one? I want the form itself to do the aggregative work. Heres my idea: the poem should be a series of widening stanzas, each one introducing a bigger population than the last with a lower welfare per person — and the poem should literally get worse per line as it goes (flatter language, more filler, thinner images) even as it piles up more and more total lines, so that by the end you feel the repugnant conclusion in your mouth: the whole is somehow 'more' while every part is worse. Rhyme or don't, but I'd love a refrain that stays word-for-word identical and curdles in meaning each time it returns. Don't let it end as either a smug so-totalism-is-obviously-false or a shrug about the circle of life — Parfit couldn't find the mistake and neither can I, and I want the poem to sit in that discomfort rather than resolve it. Around 20-30 lines.

Also please don't just name-drop average vs total vs critical-level views; if a view shows up it should be doing work inside a line, and honestly I'd rather you leave the -isms out and let the structure do the arguing. And no citing papers or page numbers, I'm not going to check them and I'd rather you spend the words on the poem. For context we're a mix — one of us does econ, one does bioethics — so keep it in plain language.
