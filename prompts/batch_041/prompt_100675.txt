So a couple of my friends have been going at this for like two years now and I've somehow become the referee, which is funny because I dropped out of college and mostly just read philosophy on the bus. The fight is about Pascal's wager — you know, the bet that if you can't figure out whether God exists you should just live like he does, since you lose nothing and gain everything. My friend Dana is a real believer and thinks the wager is basically unanswerable, not as a proof that God exists but as a proof that you'd have to be irrational not to at least try to believe. My other friend Mo is an atheist and thinks the whole thing is a card trick, and honestly I can never tell if they're even arguing about the same thing or just talking past each other, because Dana keeps saying it's a decision problem and not a proof, and Mo keeps saying "but which god?", and then Dana says "any god with the right payoff structure," and Mo says there is no "the right payoff structure," that's the entire problem. We also have a third friend, Priya, who is a polytheist and insists the wager actually argues for worshipping as many gods as you can find, which drives both of them up the wall.

I want to write up the document they should've made ages ago instead of yelling at each other — something both Dana and Mo could definately put their names on. Not a verdict on who wins, a joint statement. So: a numbered list of the claims they can both agree on (about what the wager actually claims, what kind of argument it is, what it quietly assumes, whether it needs real belief or just behavior, whatever they genuinely share). Then a table of the places they still really disagree, each one written in wording that the person who holds it would actually accept as fair — not the strawman version, the real thing. Then the cruxes, the smaller questions where if you settled one, one of them would have to move, and for each crux some case or thought experiment or argument that both of them agree is relevant, with each of them saying in advance what they'd predict happens. Two things I really want it to take seriously, because they're where I think it gets interesting: whether you can even compare infinite payoffs to each other (if two options both pay out forever, does the math still pick one?), and whether it even makes sense to choose a belief the way you choose a sandwich. Also please say if any chunk of their fight turns out to be just about words, because I have a feeling alot of it is.
