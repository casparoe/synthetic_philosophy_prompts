I'm a PhD student in bioethics, and I've been asked to circulate a short position statement on enhancement before a departmental workshop. Before I do, I want to be sure the commitments in it actually hang together. The commitments I'd defend are these.

1. The therapy/enhancement distinction is not morally fundamental: whether an intervention restores species-typical functioning or exceeds it does not by itself change its moral status.
2. All the same, I want to keep the distinction in clinical practice and in insurance. Medicine's remit is to restore health, not to satisfy preferences for better-than-normal traits.
3. I accept a broadly biostatistical account of health, on which health just is statistically normal functioning for a reference class. I take the relevant reference classes (age, sex) to be fixed by biology rather than by social values, so the baseline is a descriptive fact about organisms and not a normative construct.
4. I take the value of health to be derivative: health matters only as a means to the abilities and activities it enables, so what is good about any intervention is the abilities it produces.
5. In disability contexts I use the individual's own baseline rather than the species': restoring someone to their own previous level of functioning counts as therapy even when that level sits well below species-typical.
6. The only legitimate ground for restricting enhancement is third-party harm, paradigmatically the positional harm that arises in competitive contexts.

What I want is an audit, not a survey. Which of these are genuinely in tension with which, and how severe is each tension? I would rather you discriminate than flag everything: if a pair only looks inconsistent and is in fact compatible, say so and explain what was doing the misleading. For each genuine tension, tell me which commitment has to be weakened or abandoned and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set -- I care about the cheapest repair, not just the diagnosis. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and if one single revision would clear up more than one tension, say which and why. Finally, tell me how confident you are in each of your main claims and single out the one point you are least sure of.
