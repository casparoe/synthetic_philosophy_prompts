I have followed the cultural-evolution debates on and off since the old Overcoming Bias days, and there is a point I cannot get past, because it may be a verbal quibble or may be the whole ballgame. The standard line is that cultural evolution is Lamarckian—we acquire ideas during our lifetimes and pass on the acquired versions—whereas Darwinian evolution is supposed to be defined by the absence of exactly that, with blind variation and selection doing the work. So is a Darwinian theory of culture an oxymoron? Suppose I write down a toy model: each generation copies the most successful practitioner's method, improvises an improvement, and the improved method is what gets copied next—inheritance of acquired characters plus selection on success. Is that process Darwinian, Lamarckian, both, or neither, and does calling it Darwinian add anything beyond a vague appeal to selection? If the answer is that selection can operate on Lamarckian inheritance, has 'Darwinian' become so thin that it stops doing explanatory work, leaving the meme's-eye view as a metaphor? I would like to know which move is standard and whether the standard move survives scrutiny.
