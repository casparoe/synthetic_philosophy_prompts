I'm making a teir list of decision rules for a reading group on the weird cases. Rank these four from most to least trustworthy on counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail, with a score out of 10 and a one-sentence reason for each. Counterfactual mugging: Omega flips a coin; if heads, he asks you for $100; if tails, he gives you $1000 only if you would have paid in the heads branch. XOR blackmail / termite dilemma: a reliable predictor sends you a letter saying exactly one of two things is true, either you will pay him $1000 or your house has a terrible termite infestation. The rules: causal decision theory, which picks the act with the best causal consequences; evidential decision theory, which picks the act that is best news about the world; updateless decision theory, which picks the policy you would have precommitted to before knowing which branch you're in; and a rule I just made up, algorithmic solidarity, which treats any agent running a decision procedure similar enough to yours as you, even in branches you can't causally affect. Which would you actually want to run if you had to bet your house on it?
