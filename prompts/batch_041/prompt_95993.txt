I chair the novel-neural-systems oversight committee at our university's neurotech center — we're the body that has to sign off before any lab here runs a protocol on living human tissue in a closed loop. Tuesday we vote on the Kirsch lab's protocol: they want to train cortical organoids to steer a simulated agent, and the training signal is electrical, one burst pattern the tissue learns to seek out and a seperate one it learns to avoid, delivered through the same electrode array, with the aversive burst escalating in intensity until the behavior changes. The scientific point is how far the learning generalizes. Last year we adopted a precaution framework that has never been tested on an actual case, and I have to write the opinion that frames the vote. As written: Tier 0, no indicators, routine logging. Tier 1, attributable avoidance — the tissue's behavior changes in response to the aversive burst and the change carries over to new stimuli of the same class — in which case we cap stimulation intensity and log every aversive trial. Tier 2, integration — responses to one modality depend on the system's overall state and on its responses to another modality, and the system emits a learned signal that tracks its own preference — in which case the lab must file a shutdown protocol and justify any protocol whose only scientific purpose is to elicit the indicator. Kirsch's protocol plainly trips Tier 1 and maybe Tier 2, and we're split. One member says the framework is a category error: no arrangement of computations is the kind of thing that can matter morally, only a subject can, and we're burning committee hours on a dish of cells while our animal facilities go uninspected. Another says the tiers are substrate chauvinism in disguise — if we'd protect a mouse on those same behavioral markers, consistency demands we protect this too, and the burden sits on whoever wants to withhold it. Draft the written opinion I'll circulate before the vote: a recommendation we can act on (approve, approve with conditions, or hold the protocol), the reasoning behind it, and an honest engagement with the strongest thing the losing side has going for it. No citations — I need the argument, not a reading list. Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with the bottom line in item one and no intro and no conclusion. American English, please.
