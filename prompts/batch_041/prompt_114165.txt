Not a philosopher, just someone who works with data and reads philosophy of science on the side, so forgive me if I'm mangling terms.

I keep noticing a move that I don't have a word for. Someone comes up with a model or a theory, and the way they come up with it involves staring at some particular body of data until the pieces fit -- they try this, try that, tweak until it lines up. Then, once it lines up, they turn around and present that same body of data as the evidence that the theory is right. The numbers come out great, of course they do, and it gets written up as "the theory predicts X" when really the theory was reverse-engineered from X. The striking thing is that the support they claim doesn't seem to move anyone's mind if you actually watched the sausage get made, but in the retelling it looks like a genuine hit.

Two flavors of it that I've run into. One: the datum was already widely known before the theory existed -- everybody knew about the perihelion thing, or the fossil, or the anomaly, for years -- so it can't have raised anybody's confidence at the time the theory showed up, and yet it's trotted out as the theory's crown jewel of confirmation. Two: the datum wasn't just known beforehand, it was actively used to build the theory, so the theory couldn't have failed to fit it, and yet fitting it is offered as a mark in its favor.

I've heard of the problem of old evidence, and of the prediction-versus-accommodation thing, and in my own world we'd just call it overfitting or double-dipping. But none of those feels like it's exactly the thing I'm circling. So: does this have a name? If it does, I want to know how it differs from each of the neighbors -- is the real sin that the datum was already known, or that it was used in the construction, or something else? Is it one phenomenon or am I lumping two together? And where's the line between this and the perfectly respectable case where a theory does accommodate a known fact in a way that genuinely should raise your credence? If there's no established name, coin one and give me a definition with some boundary cases, because the label is the least of it -- I want to know exactly what the thing is and what it isn't.
