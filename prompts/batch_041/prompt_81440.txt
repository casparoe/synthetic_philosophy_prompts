My friend, a nursing student who works to pay her tuition, has received an unusual offer. A wealthy acquaintance has proposed to underwrite a year of her volunteering at a homeless shelter: he will cover her living expenses and pay her a salary, and in exchange he will be credited, in the shelter's materials and in his own account of his life, as the person who made the work possible, and he will treat it as the charitable act that cancels out the harm he believes his business does. He adds that a good deed is a good deed, whoever pays for it and whoever gets the credit. Nothing is concealed and no one is deceived; the shelter and the people it serves are better off than they would otherwise have been. My friend sees a clean win for everyone: she gets to do work she believes in and be paid for it, the shelter gains a full-time volunteer, and he gets what he wants. I am uneasy, but the objections that come to mind — exploitation, deception, harm to third parties — do not seem to apply. Can you explain what, if anything, is genuinely wrong with the arrangement, and whether she should accept?
