Ok, I've been down a rabbit hole on the bootstrapping / easy knowledge thing — the move where I look at a red wall, believe it's red, do that a bunch of times, and then conclude my eyes are reliable, and it feels like I've justified trusting my eyes without any independent evidence. Something's obviously off but I can never say exactly what, and every writeup I attempt just restates the puzzle.

Build me a decision tree / taxonomy that actually sorts the positions, for a blog post aimed at people who've read a bit of epistemology but not a ton. Start from commitments a reader can answer without knowing the labels — whether justification has to transmit step-by-step from premise to conclusion, whether the bootstrapper gets to lean on many instances or only one, whether 'my eyes are reliable' is a genuinely new belief or just a restatement of what the individual perceptual beliefs already gave you, and whether we're after knowledge or only justification/rationality. Leaves should be named positions where there's a name (dogmatism, externalist reliabilism, transmission-failure views, hinge-type views, out-and-out skepticism about the inference) and a one-liner where there isn't.

One thing I really want the tree to force apart, because I think this is where people cheat: two different anti-circularity moves that keep getting conflated — the 'package deal' move (reliability and the first-order beliefs get justified together, so nothing has to come first) versus the 'no new knowledge' move (the conclusion adds nothing to what the individual perceptual beliefs already gave you, so there's no illicit gain). Flag which leaves the tree can't separate, and flag any combinations that come out empty, with a guess at whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Maybe eight to ten branch nodes. Dont bother with citations, it's a blog post.
