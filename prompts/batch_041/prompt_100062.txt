I'm a software engineer — I work on tooling for social VR — and I have no formal training in philosophy, though I've ended up reading a fair amount of it on my own after a long-running argument with a friend who did a PhD in the subject. Our argument is about whether anything that happens in a virtual world is genuinely real, and it always ends the same way: she says the objects aren't real and therefore the harms aren't either; I say the harms are obviously real because the person on the other end is real; and we agree to disagree. I want to break the deadlock by having you construct one designed case, built to my specifications, rather than trading intuitions.

The specifications are these. I've been working with two homemade criteria — I coined them myself, so don't assume they line up with anything in the literature; just take them as written. Call a thing object-real when (i) it can be individuated, (ii) it persists through time, and (iii) it constrains the actions of agents who did not create it. Call an event harm-real when it sets back an interest that its subject actually holds and would still hold once fully informed of everything relevant. I want a single scenario — contemporary-ish VR hardware is fine, but no uploading, no brain-in-a-vat setups, no exotic physics — whose natural verdicts come out asymmetrically: some virtual object is object-real while the apparent harm bound up with it is not harm-real. That is the direction people find hard, because the lazy move is to think that granting the object grants the harm, or else to deny both together. Make the asymmetry fall out of the criteria rather than stipulating it: the object should genuinely satisfy all three clauses, and the harm should fail one specific clause, which you name.

Then do the demonstration. State exactly which object and which harm you have put in play; walk through why the object passes each clause; say why the harm fails the clause it fails; and identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work. Tell me what happens to the verdicts if I remove that feature, and give me the strongest attack on the case from someone who thinks virtual harms are plainly real — what would she claim is being smuggled in? Keep everything else fixed, so that the case turns on one variable and not on the subject being deceived, mistaken, or caught unawares. Please open with the scenario itself, with no preface or commentary, then give the demonstration, and write the whole thing in British English.
