Last night a friend and I argued for four hours about what it is for something in an organism to have a function, and we ended further apart than we began: she holds that a function claim is a claim about a trait's history, and I hold that it is a claim about what the trait is currently doing for the system it sits inside. Neither of us will give way on that, but we suspect we have been talking past each other about a great deal else, and we would like a document we could both sign. Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed selected-effects, or etiological, theorist and a committed systemic, or causal-role, theorist. I am one of the two parties, so write it so that my friend would sign it as well, and add a closing note flagging any sentence whose wording quietly favors my side.

Part one, agreed claims, numbered. Include what both sides accept about the bearer of a function, whether that is a token part, a type of part, a character, or a trait in a population; what both accept about the normativity of function talk, that is, whether saying what a part is for implies that it ought to do it, and whether malfunction is a phenomenon distinct from disuse, damage, or mere failure; what each side concedes to the standard objection against it, the charge that causal-role accounts make every effect a function and the charge that etiological accounts make every function an adaptation; and which of the cases below we both regard as uncontroversial. We already agree, for instance, that pumping is a function of the heart and that the noise it makes is not.

Part two, a disagreements table. One row per point of genuine disagreement, with the etiological position and the causal-role position each stated in wording its own side would accept, and a third column marking whether the row is a substantive disagreement or a difference in what we mean by the word.

Part three, cruxes. The ones I take to be live are: how much and what kind of history is required, whether a single generation of selection, a persistent lineage, a designer's intention, or somatic selection inside one body that is never transmitted to offspring; whether a trait's function can be fixed without first choosing a level or an analysis of the system it belongs to; and whether a function survives the loss of the effect. For each crux, state the case or argument both of us would accept as bearing on it, then record each side's prediction about it in advance. I would rather be told that a crux is indecisive than be given a padded list.

Part four, the cases. Apply the whole apparatus to each, saying plainly where a case cuts against my side.

- A beetle's gland secretion was selected over many generations for sealing wounds in the cuticle. Two generations ago a parasitic worm arrived, the beetle population is now booming, and the same secretion turns out to dissolve the worm's egg cases. Does the anti-worm effect have a function?
- A self-maintaining, membrane-bound, microbe-like system assembles itself by accident in a laboratory's mineral medium, repairs its own membrane, and holds a pH gradient, and it has never reproduced, so no lineage of it has ever been selected. Its descendants, after it is found to divide, are then selected for a thousand generations. Compare the verdicts before and after, and say whether it would matter if chemists had built the system on purpose rather than stumbled on it.
- The antibody-producing cell lineages inside a body are selected for binding affinity and are never passed to offspring. Do they have functions?
- A frog's heart one hour after the frog has died; a patient's own heart after a mechanical pump has taken over the circulation. Does the function remain?
- A bacterial surface protein keeps the cell from drying out and also holds a biofilm colony together. If the two candidate systems give different answers, which system is the right one, and by what right?

Part five. If some part of our dispute is verbal, say which part and why, and say what would remain to disagree about once that part is set aside. Do not give me reading lists or citations; neither of us is going to settle this by appeal to authority.
