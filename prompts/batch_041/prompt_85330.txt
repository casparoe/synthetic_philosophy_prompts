Over the last few months I've been designing a two-player board game in my spare time, and I've ended up with an argument that I can neither fault nor believe. The game is this: players take turns placing one stone on a square grid, and both players are trying to do the same thing — build an unbroken chain of their own stones, edge to edge, joining the top edge of the board to the bottom edge. Stones never move and are never removed. Here is the argument:

(1) Either the second player has a plan that beats whatever the first player does, or the first player has one.
(2) Suppose the second player has such a plan. Call it S.
(3) The first player then puts a stone anywhere at all, and from that point on plays as though they were the second player, following S; the stone already on the board is a free gift that can simply be ignored. If S ever calls for the square the free stone is sitting on, the first player plays any other empty square instead.
(4) A spare stone of your own can never do you harm in this game: it can only help your own chain and get in your opponent's way.
(5) So the first player beats S, which contradicts (2).
(6) So the second player has no such plan, and by (1) the first player has one.

The trouble is that on the smallest board I can draw — two squares by two — the first player cannot force a win. They take a corner; the second player takes the square directly below it; and after that, whichever way the first player goes, the board fills up with neither chain reaching from top to bottom. So (6) comes out false there, which means some step above must be false as well. The step that feels like cheating to me is (3), though I can't put my finger on what is wrong with it; the step I would most like to keep is (4), which seems plainly true of my game.

Give me a definite verdict rather than a survey of options: which single step should I give up, why did it look so convincing, and what does giving it up cost me — what would I lose that I could have kept if I had sacrificed a different step instead? Please answer in British English and commit to a position.
