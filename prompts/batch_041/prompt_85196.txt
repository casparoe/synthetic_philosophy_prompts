I am attracted to the view that the replication crisis in the human sciences is fundamentally a problem of frequentist inference—that null-hypothesis significance testing answers the wrong question and that a shift to Bayesian methods, with explicit priors and model comparison, would substantially mitigate it. What would count against this view? I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, ranked by how decisive they would be: cases where Bayesian methods have been gamed or have failed to replicate, arguments that the real culprits are incentives and publication bias rather than the inference framework, and any formal results showing that Bayesian and frequentist procedures converge in the relevant cases. Also tell me what defenders of the view would themselves concede as counting against it, and whether the position risks becoming unfalsifiable—if it has been qualified into the claim that Bayesian methods are better when used well, does that empty it of content? Please answer in American English.
