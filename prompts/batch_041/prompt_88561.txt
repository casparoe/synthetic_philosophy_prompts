I'm a third-year PhD in philosophy of physics (stat mech foundations, mostly) and I've got a draft chapter on Maxwell's demon that I think has exactly one real idea in it, and I've stared at it so long I can't tell anymore whether the idea is good or whether I've just dressed up the standard story. I'd like you to go at it hard. Don't recap Szilard or Landauer or Bennett's reversible-measurement move for me, I know all that; what I want is a verdict on the four claims below — which one is load-bearing, which ones I should cut, and what the strongest surviving version of the thing is. Please actually take a position rather than laying the options out and leaving me to choose.

The argument, in ugly shorthand:

1. Every exorcism of the demon works by pulling something inside the system: the memory, the correlation between memory and gas, the apparatus, the bath. Leave it out and the demon wins; put it in and it loses. So nothing in the physics singles out the boundary — the accounting convention does.

2. The two exorcisms that get run together are not the same exorcism. The Szilard-Bennett one is a bookkeeping claim about a closed system: somewhere in the cycle, kT ln2 per bit gets paid. The other is a typicality claim: the second law is statistical, so a demon riding fluctuations in a finite system really can push entropy down locally, and the only "cost" is the improbability. A demon that is a rare fluctuation and a demon that has to erase a memory register are different objects, and I think a lot of the literature slides between them without noticing.

3. The bookkeeping exorcism looks circular to me. To charge the demon for its memory you have to assign thermodynamic entropy to the memory register, i.e. you already have to grant that information-bearing degrees of freedom count as thermodynamic ones — which is the thing in dispute. And if you don't grant it, the word "information" is doing no work at all: a ratchet or a trapdoor doesn't need any bits.

4. So the real question isn't "why can't the demon win" but "what fixes the boundary of the thermodynamic system", and I want to say the answer comes from outside thermodynamics entirely — from what we can control, reset, and re-use.

What I want you to rule on: is 4 just claim 1 restated in a grander voice? I keep expecting someone to tell me that the cycle requirement settles it — that a demon which doesn't return to its initial state isn't a demon at all, so the boundary isn't a convention — and I need to know whether that objection lands, or whether "cyclic" is itself a stipulation about where the system stops. Does the feedback version of the second law, the ones with an explicit term for the correlation between the demon's memory and the gas so that entropy reduction is bounded by the mutual information, actually rescue 1 by making the boundary explicit, or does it just presuppose the same convention? And is there any non-trivial version of 3, since obviously everything is physical — what would it even mean for information not to be physical?

So: tell me which of these to cut, which one you'd keep and how you'd restate it so it survives contact with a referee, and if the whole chapter is the standard view wearing a nicer jacket, say so straight out. If I have to give up 1 and 4 to keep 3, fine, tell me that.
