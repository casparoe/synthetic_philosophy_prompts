I'm a novelist — literary fiction, first-person unreliable narrators are pretty much my bread and butter — and I've gotten weirdly obsesed with Moore's paradox, the whole "it's raining but I don't believe it's raining" thing. It started as a craft problem: I wanted a narrator who states a fact and disavows his own belief in it in the same breath, and a friend told me that's just Moore's paradox and that philosophers have been fighting over it forever without setling anything. So I went and read around, and now I honestly can't tell whether the fight is even a fight. One camp seems to treat the sentence as contradictory all by itself — there's something wrong with its content, or with what "believe" means in the first person — while another camp says no, the sentence is perfectly consistent, it's just that you can't assert it, or can't hold the whole thought at once, or that it breaks some norm of assertion, or that the oddity lives in the act of judging rather than in what's judged. They all agree on the data — nobody would say the thing, everybody finds it absurd — and then they part ways over what that data is even evidence of.

Is this a real disagreement that just happens to be hard, or is it two groups answering different questions under one heading, one about truth-conditions and one about the norms of speech? My own suspicion (and I don't know nearly enough to defend it) is that it's the second, and that the tell is that each side can explain the absurdity perfectly well by its own lights, so the absurdity itself can't be what's doing the discriminating work. What I want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict: say what kind of disagreement this actually is, back it up with what each side is committed to, and then tell me honestly whether anything could move it — a distinction, a formal result, some weird case — or whether it's the sort of impasse where the right move is to stop asking. And if you can, say whether the same muddle is going on with the other-person versions, the "I believe it's raining but it isn't" and the "it's raining and I believe it isn't" forms, because I have a hunch the three don't hang together the way people assume, and that might be the clue I'm after.
