ok so I'm supposed to write a short response paper for a grad seminar on cluelessness and I want to know if my own position is even stable before I commit to it, because right now I suspect I'm just telling a story that sounds deep.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Act consequentialism: an act is right iff it has the best consequences, counting everyone who will ever exist. And I mean the actual consequences, not the expected ones.

2. I take cluelessness seriously, but I split it in two. The boring kind is causal — I can't trace how my coffee purchase ripples out over the next three centuries, and neither can anyone else. I think that's a practical limitation and not that interesting. The interesting kind is evaluative — I dont have a grip on what the distant outcomes even are, or which ones would be better than which, so I can't rank the scenarios I'd need to rank in the first place.

3. Expected value maximization over my own subjective probabilities is the right decision rule under uncertainty, including when the probabilities are tiny and the stakes are astronomical.

4. I use dumb rules of thumb all day and I don't feel bad about it. I don't compute consequences when I pick a sandwich or when I lie to a friend about liking their band.

5. Cluelessness doesn't push me toward deontology or virtue ethics. Whatever theory you've got, you still have to say something about what happens to people, so nobody escapes this thing.

6. I'm more confident that I should be doing long-run-flavored stuff with my career and my donations than that I should be doing anything else, and I'm willing to act on very small probabilities of very large outcomes there.

My worry is that some of these are quietly doing incompatible work. Go through them and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like it, and rank the real ones by how bad they are. For each real one, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me? Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most of the mess. Be explicit about how confident you are in each diagnosis and flag the one you're least sure about. American English please.
