I have no academic training in philosophy or Buddhist studies. I have been keeping a notebook about a puzzle that keeps returning to me: the four-cornered pattern of negation in Nāgārjuna and the Madhyamaka idea of emptiness. As I understand it, the four corners are: a claim is true; it is false; it is both true and false; it is neither true nor false. And emptiness, as I understand it, is the claim that things do not have an independent, intrinsic nature of their own; whatever they are, they are dependently arisen. What I cannot settle is what the four-cornered pattern is for, and what emptiness is supposed to be. I would like a decision tree, not an essay.

Please write a decision tree that takes a reader from a few plain starting commitments to a named or described interpretive position. Each branch node should be a question that can be answered without already knowing the label of the position it leads to. Each leaf should be a position about what the tetralemma is doing and what emptiness is, with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. The tree should be built from the logical space first, not from a list of famous names: start with the distinctions, then place the positions in the space, note combinations that are empty, and say briefly whether a combination is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot really separate.

I need the tree to use at least these distinctions, because they are the ones I keep confusing:

1. Whether the four corners are meant as four possible truth values, four possible answers to a metaphysical question, or four moves in a therapeutic or rhetorical practice.
2. Whether the rejection of all four corners is distributive (none of the four is true) or collective (the whole four-cornered question is being rejected as badly posed).
3. Whether emptiness is a property that things have, a lack that things have, a relation among things, or a second-order claim about our concepts and language.
4. Whether conventional truth is a kind of truth, a useful fiction, or merely whatever we happen to agree on.
5. Whether the point of the tetralemma is to produce a piece of knowledge, to remove a craving or confusion, or to bring questioning to a stop.
6. Whether emptiness itself is empty in the same sense, and if so whether that makes the view self-undermining or self-correcting.

Please include leaves for at least these positions, described in your own words rather than assumed: a quietist or therapeutic reading that refuses to assert any thesis; a semantic reading on which the four corners fail because they presuppose an independent nature; a two-truths reading that distinguishes conventional and ultimate description; a paraconsistent or dialetheic reading on which some contradictions can be true; a mystical or ineffability reading on which the tetralemma points beyond what can be said; and a deflationary or pragmatic reading on which the tetralemma is a recommendation about how to stop asking a certain kind of question. Also include one leaf for a position you think is coherent but that no one seems to hold, and say what makes it unattractive.

Use concrete cases as branch nodes wherever you can. For example: Does the tetralemma apply to itself? If emptiness is empty, is that a fifth corner or a cancellation of the whole game? Can a conventional truth be false in the ordinary sense? If two people agree on all conventional truths but disagree about ultimate truth, is there a fact of the matter between them? If the tetralemma is only therapy, can it be wrong? If it is a logic, what does it say about the law of non-contradiction? I do not need a bibliography, reading list, or citations. I need the instrument.

At the end, state how confident you are in the overall classification and in the placement of each leaf, and single out the one step or distinction you are least sure about, explaining why. If any of the positions I asked for are not actually distinct, say so rather than forcing them apart.
