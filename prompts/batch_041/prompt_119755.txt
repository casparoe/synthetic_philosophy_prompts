I don't have any kind of academic background, I just read a lot of blog posts and argue with people online, and something about AI alignment keeps bugging me. Everyone says 'the alignment problem' like it's one thing, but I've started to think it's several problems wearing the same name. Heres my rough split. (a) We can't write down what we actually want, or we disagree about it and there's no fact of the matter about whose version the machine should listen to. (b) We can write it down, but the machine does the literal thing instead of the thing we meant. (c) The machine understands perfectly well what we want and just doesn't care. (d) There isn't one machine, there's millions, each one fine on its own, and the mess comes out of all of them together. The pair I genuinely can't separate is (b) and (c) -- the only difference I can see is whether the thing is trying to help, and I don't know if that difference is real or just a story we tell.

Write an essay on whether that's one problem or four, and actually land on an answer -- don't tell me it depends how you define the terms, that's the move I'm suspicious of. If they're really four, tell me what they still share that makes one word for them more than a marketing label, and which of my four people usually mean when they say it. If they're really one, tell me which of mine collapse into which and why the line I'm drawing is fake. And say what difference it makes: if I'm wrong and it's one problem, what exactly am I getting wrong when I read about it? I'm not asking for a reading list, just the argument.
