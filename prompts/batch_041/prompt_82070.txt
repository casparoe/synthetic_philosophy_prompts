I'm making a handout for a grad seminar on the axiology of suffering — the family of views where only the absence of suffering and craving counts, and positive wellbeing adds nothing. I want a decision tree / taxonomy of the positions, built out of the logical space rather than a list of names. Root it in "suffering is bad, and that's the whole of what makes a life go badly," then branch on the questions that actually split the views — at least these: does a life with no suffering but also no positive states at all score as high as one that's equally suffering-free but full of joy; is craving bad in itself or only when it hurts (use the case of a craving that is never frustrated and never noticed as unpleasant); is a craving you'd never trade away, the kind that's savored and pleasant to have, still bad on this view; does the absence of suffering count as a good or merely as the absence of a bad; is the claim about intrinsic value or about what we have reason to promote; and does a world with no sentient beings at all come out tied for best. Each leaf should carry a position name or "unnamed," plus one line on its most awkward commitment. Phrase every branch as a question someone could answer without already knowing where it leads. Flag any two leaves the tree can't really separate, and mark which answer-combinations are unoccupied and whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Seven or eight branching questions is about right.
