I host a small philosophy of mind podcast and next weeks guest is someone who's spent about twenty years defending a heterophenomenological approach to consciousness — Dennett's line that you take what a subject says about her experience as data about what she believes, not as data about what her experience is actually like, and then build up this heterophenomenological world the way you'd build up the world of a novel from its text. I don't want her just restating that for forty minutes. Which she takes to show that phenomenal properties are never part of the best explanation of anything — not merely that we never get to observe them.

Give me 8 to 10 questions with a follow-up after each one, the follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. I want the questions to turn on the actual content of her position, so press where the literature actually presses. A few things I specifically need covered somewhere in there:

- a subject who sincerely reports having no experiences at all
