Ok so my grandad died in Febuary and the strangest part of the whole thing has been the tax stuff. He bought a pile of shares back in the 80s and never sold a single one, so nobody ever paid capital gains on any of it, and now that he's gone the basis gets reset to whatever the stock was worth on the day he died — forty years of growth just evaporates, tax-wise, and me and my sisters can sell and owe basically nothing. My uncle, who's a bit of a character, keeps saying the real move for someone with that kind of money is to never sell anything ever: just borrow against the shares when you want cash, since a loan isn't income and there's nothing to tax, and then die and let the reset do its thing. Which means the single best tax event in a rich family's life is somebody dying, and there's no second-best event that comes close.

So I want a joke to tell at the funeral lunch, mostly for my uncle.
