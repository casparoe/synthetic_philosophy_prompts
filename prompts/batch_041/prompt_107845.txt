I'm making a handout for a first-year metaphysics tutorial on the special composition question — when do some things add up to a further thing — and I want it as a decision tree, not a list of isms. So: each node a question a student can answer without knowing where it leads, each leaf a named position plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and the cases have to do the sorting. Please don't stop at universalism and nihilism, I want these leaves too: composition happens exactly when the parts constitute a living thing; composition happens when the parts are stuck together or attached; a by-cases answer that gives different conditions for different kinds of thing; and the deflationary one that says the question is confused or basicly verbal. Give me a branch for vagueness as well — whether 'these parts compose something' can be vague — because I have a hunch that saying yes collapses the middle answers into universalism or nihilism, and I want to see that happen on the tree. Cases to build it from, and please make each one actually split something: two bricks stacked and touching, a heap of sand, a live cat, the same cat a minute after it dies, a lichen (fungus and alga living as one patch), and a chess set. If two positions come out identical on every case, say so and tell me what case would prise them apart, and mark any leaf nobody occupies with a note on whether that's incoherence, unattractiveness, or just nobody having bothered.
