I design recommendation and ranking systems for a large consumer platform, so I think in terms of engagement objectives, revealed preference, retention, incentive compatibility, positional competition, and externalities that the objective function does not price. I know the outline of Rousseau's case against the theater in his reply to d'Alembert's Encyclopédie article on Geneva, but I keep half-persuading myself that it is a precursor of the ordinary complaint that an optimizing system flatters the preferences it serves, and that whatever moral instruction the system appears to offer is either epiphenomenal or a Goodhart artifact.

Restate his position in my vocabulary, claim by claim: that a spectacle must please before it can improve and so cannot correct the taste it serves; that spectacles suit a corrupt people and harm a virtuous one; that the actor's profession is a life of dissimulation and dependence on applause; that the theater differs from civic festivals in which participants see and are seen by one another rather than watching; that spectatorship discharges sympathy vicariously and leaves real obligations untouched; and that comedy in particular trains an audience to ridicule sincerity. For each claim, say what maps cleanly onto my vocabulary, what must be distorted to fit, and what has no expression there at all. Do not let the target vocabulary do the translating for you: where my terms make one of his claims look obviously true or obviously empty, flag that as an artifact of the translation rather than a verdict.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of his case survive as ordinary claims about incentives, externalities, and self-presentation, and which depend on commitments my framework cannot even state — for example a distinction between the wants an audience has and the wants it ought to have, a notion of a people being virtuous or corrupt as a collective property rather than an aggregate of individual states, or a picture of the self as degraded by being displayed? Where I am inclined to call the leftover residue mere moralizing, tell me whether that verdict is doing real work or is an artifact of the vocabulary I brought with me.
