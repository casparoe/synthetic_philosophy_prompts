I have been cataloguing the verdicts of a biologist whose implicit criteria I am trying to reconstruct, on a single question: is this trait an adaptation? Her rulings so far. The vertebrate camera eye: adaptation. The human chin: not an adaptation, but a byproduct of the differential growth of the jaw bones. The thickened wrist bones that brace the forelimbs in a burrowing frog lineage: not an adaptation, even though they do that job well, since on her account the byproduct was co-opted rather than built for it. A trait with nothing engineering-like about it, and no developmental constraint that would explain its size, which a long-running selection experiment drove in one direction while the relevant function was measured independently: adaptation. A byproduct whose accidental dimensions happen to fit a use so well that the fit gets cited as evidence of design: still not an adaptation.

The new case is a bony chamber in the skull of a small lineage. It is a byproduct of the differential growth of two bones: a sister lineage in which the chamber has no acoustic role has the same geometry, and a growth model with no selection term reproduces it. In the lineage of interest, however, the chamber resonates with a species-specific call, and the match between chamber volume and call frequency is tighter than the growth model predicts, though chamber volume still follows an allometric law documented across the whole clade. Predict her verdict, tell me which rules fit her earlier rulings equally well and what each predicts here, say which rule I should expect her to be running and why, and name the further case that would separate whichever rules survive.
