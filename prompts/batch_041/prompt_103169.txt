Two camps in our class have been talking past each other all term. One camp says this is basically a window into deep-seated or evolved sex differences in interests and preferences: once you remove barriers, people's natures show up more clearly. The other camp says that's a massive overread, that the pattern is mostly about institutions, labor markets, gendered stereotypes, and how equality itself is measured, and that it tells us almost nothing about innate differences, let alone what justice requires. I have to write something for our final project, and I'd much rather do an adversarial collaboration than another hot take.

Draft the joint statement that a committed member of each camp could sign after a good-faith collaboration. I want a numbered list of claims both can accept, covering what the empirical pattern actually is, how the key variables get measured, what would count as evidence for or against each side, and what the pattern does and doesn't imply about nature, culture, and justice. Then a disagreements table with the precise remaining disagreements, each phrased so the side that holds it would accept the wording of its own view. Then the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions where settling one would actually move at least one camp. For each crux, give a concrete study, comparison, or thought experiment that both sides agree would bear on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts we'd see. And maybe there's a third possibility neither camp likes, namely that more egalitarian countries are also more gender-essentialist in some cultural beliefs or marketing, so the country-level index is picking up two things at once.
