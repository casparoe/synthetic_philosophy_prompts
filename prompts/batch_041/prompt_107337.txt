I'm a backend engineer at a small AI startup, and I sit near two people on the ML team who got into a long argument at lunch that I've been chewing on ever since. We'd just been looking at a model we fine-tuned for our support bot that keeps telling users a refund window is 90 days when the actual policy (and the docs it was trained on) say 30. Call them Dana and Marcus. Dana says the real question is whether the model "knows" the true number underneath, and that the only honest way to find out is to poke it behaviorally: rephrase the question, ask in another language, give it examples that imply 30, have it roleplay someone reading the policy. If some prompt gets it to say 30, then it knew; if no prompt does, it doesn't, period, and "latent knowledge" is just knowledge that some prompt would surface. Marcus says that's backwards. The number is in the weights and shows up in the activations; the training signal just made "90" the thing it tends to output. A verbal report is a lossy readout of the internal state, so you should train a linear probe on the hidden states, or do causal interventions, to find out what's actually represented, instead of just asking harder.

Dana came back that a probe is just a classifier you fit to your own labels — if it fires, all you've shown is that your label was linearly decodable from the activations, which could easily be some surface feature that happens to correlate, not a belief. And anyway, if the model never acts on the number, never says 30 and never uses it to answer anything, in what sense does it know it? That's a homunculus hiding in the weights. Marcus said Dana is begging the question by defining knowledge as "whatever the model would say," and pointed to the case where a model gets a fact right in English but blows it on a translation task: we still want to say it knows the fact and the knowledge just doesn't transfer, which shows knowledge isn't "says it everywhere," it's something in the computation. Dana said no, that's still just two behaviors, and any internal-state notion of knowledge is unfalsifiable, so it's not a scientific claim at all.
