I am putting together a position statement on distributive justice for a workshop and would like an audit of it before I circulate it. Each of the following I hold with some confidence on its own; what I want to know is whether they can be held together by a single person.

1. The central distributive requirement is that inequalities traceable to unchosen circumstance — where a person's relative position is attributable to brute luck rather than to their own choices — should be neutralized, while inequalities that result from choices made knowingly and with adequate alternatives may legitimately be left standing.

2. A conception of justice that shields people from the costs of their own choices fails to treat them as responsible agents, and this is a serious defect rather than a merely aesthetic one. The imprudent, as I see it, have no complaint of justice when they bear the costs of their imprudence.

3. What underlies the egalitarian enterprise is the equal moral worth of persons and their entitlement to equal concern and respect. Equality of condition matters as an expression of that equal standing, not as an intrinsic good to be maximized.

4. Certain goods — a decent minimum of health care, basic education, a subsistence income — should be guaranteed to everyone unconditionally, regardless of how they came to need them.

5. The relevant metric of advantage is neither resources alone nor welfare alone but something encompassing both: a person counts as worse off than another, for the purposes of (1), if they have fewer resources, or if their unchosen constitution or circumstances make their life go less well than the other's even at an equal level of resources.

6. Equality gives no reason to make anyone worse off. If one distribution is more equal than another but leaves no one better off, there is nothing to be said for it on egalitarian grounds.

7. I am not a skeptic about responsibility. People genuinely make choices, those choices are genuinely theirs, and a theory of justice is entitled to rely on that. I insist only that we be cautious about any particular attribution of responsibility in practice.

Please tell me which pairs of these are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to be, and how severe each genuine tension is. I would like the genuine tensions ranked by severity rather than merely listed; for each, I want to know precisely which commitment would have to be surrendered or weakened to resolve it, and what the cost of that concession would be. For the pairs that only appear to conflict, I want the diagnosis of why the appearance is misleading. Then tell me which single revision would resolve the most while conceding the least, and state plainly what that revision costs me. I am not asking which of these views is true in the abstract; I am asking whether this particular combination is jointly tenable, and if not, where the fault line runs.
