I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, and I've been reading Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life. The gist, as I understand it: we relate to the past in three ways. Monumental history treats the past as a gallery of great deeds and great men, meant to spur you to do something great yourself, though it can curdle into a fantasy that just makes you feel small. Antiquarian history reverently preserves everything that was, because it's *yours* -- it gives you roots, but it can also mummify you, make you incapable of letting go of anything. Critical history puts the past on trial, judges it, and tries to break with it -- sometimes necessary, but a dangerous operation, since you're cutting into yourself as well. And the whole essay is really about how much past a living thing can carry before it can no longer act; forgetting is part of health, not just a failure.

I want you to restate that whole scheme, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually think in: version control. Commits and history, `git log` vs a hand-written changelog, tags and release notes, rebasing and squash-merges and force-pushing, deprecation, vendored and legacy code, `git blame`, tech debt, forks, archives, and the reflog (the record that survives even the force-push you used to make something disappear). Assume I know git well but zero philosophy.

Dont give me loose vibes -- actually map each of the three modes onto something concrete in how a repo lives with its past, and say how faithful the fit is. Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. What in Nietzsche has no counterpart at all in how we treat a codebase's past? Is there anything his account needs that my vocabulary has no slot for -- a notion of what the past is *for*, say, or of memory as a burden -- and if so, is that a hole in version control's way of thinking, or does alot of his anxiety just evaporate once storage is free and you can rewrite anything you like? Tell me where the translation earns its keep and where it's decoration.
