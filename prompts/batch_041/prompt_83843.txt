Radio spectrum and the atmosphere are both finite and rivalrous in use, and neither can be fenced off — if at all — without a serious apparatus of law and enforcement. Yet most states treat them in opposite ways. Spectrum is cut into exclusive, tradeable licences and sold to the highest bidder, with monitoring and penalties to make the boundaries bite. The atmosphere is left open: in most places, emitting into it costs nothing, even where the same government has legislated that it is a shared and finite resource. The contrast is sharper still within spectrum itself, since the regulator that auctions exclusive bands also leaves other bands unlicensed, open to anyone who obeys technical rules — power ceilings, listening before transmitting — and those open bands, which carry Wi-Fi and much else, have not been ruined by overuse.

What theory of the commons does this pattern presuppose? In particular: does it presuppose that a commons is discovered — a natural fact about a resource, that it is rivalrous and cannot be fenced — or that a commons is made, a status the law confers by declining to enforce boundaries and can just as easily withhold? The auctioned licences look like the first; the open bands and the unpriced atmosphere look like the second, or else like a claim about the cost of enforcement. Reconstruct the implicit theory, or the several theories, as sharply as you can, including how the practice classifies these resources — public good, common-pool resource, club good — and whether that classification is a finding about the resource or a decision about it. Say which rival accounts of the commons each half of the practice rules out and which it merely accommodates; whether the halves can be held together consistently, and if so how, and if not, which one is doing the real work. Separate what the practice strictly requires from what it merely happens to fit. Identify where it is underdetermined, so that incompatible theories would license it equally. Ask, too, where the practice supposes order comes from — law, shared norms, or the design of the devices themselves. Then suppose the presupposed view were replaced by its rival: what would have to change in the assignment of spectrum and in the treatment of the atmosphere? And which half of the practice does the fact that the open bands work rather well tell against?

Argue it on the merits; I am not interested in who said what. Please write in British English.
