I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and I keep returning to the HCH/factored-cognition picture of deliberation: an idealized reasoner may defer any subquestion to another reasoner, who may defer in turn, and a large task can be factored into many small, independently checkable subtasks. Suppose we take that picture seriously but impose two realistic constraints: each sub-agent has a fixed budget and can pass upward only a short summary, and the summaries are optimized by the sub-agents rather than by the principal. What are the most important implications for how a single reasoner should structure their own inquiry—when to decompose, when to defer, how to choose the granularity of subtasks, and how to guard against adversarial or merely lossy summarization? I'm especially interested in the implications for trust: under what conditions does a factored deliberation deserve more confidence than an unaided judgment, and when does decomposition become a way of laundering uncertainty? Finally, which of these implications survive when the sub-agents are all copies of the same bounded reasoner rather than a diverse pool?
