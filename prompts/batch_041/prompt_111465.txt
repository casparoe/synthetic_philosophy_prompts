Ok, so my friend and I got into a two-hour arguement last night about Quine's gavagai example, and I've decided the only way to win is to write a joke about it. Can you write me one?

I don't want the lazy version where the joke is just "haha, gavagai, weird word, who knows if it's a rabbit." The punchline needs to turn on what Quine actually says, so that someone who's read Word and Object laughs and someone who hasn't can still tell I know what I'm talking about.

Specifically, here's the stuff I want it to hinge on, i.e., everything my friend kept getting wrong:

First, he thinks the point is that we can never be sure whether the native means rabbit, or rabbit-stage, or undetached rabbit parts, so it's a limit on what we can know. But Quine's claim is stranger than that. Every possible bit of behavior fits both manuals, so there's no further fact of the matter — the linguist isn't failing to discover the truth, there just isn't any truth of that kind to discover. That gap is the whole joke, so it has to land.

Second, why can't you just break the tie by pointing and asking the native whether this is the same gavagai as that one over there? Because that question already assumes you can translate his apparatus for sameness and individuation, which is exactly what's in dispute. You can't fix the indeterminacy from inside the language. I'd love a joke that uses that.

Third, please don't write something premised on Quine thinking you literally can't translate gavagai. He thought observation sentences like that were the easy part. The indeterminacy bites on reference, identity, and the theoretical scaffolding. Getting that backwards makes the joke wrong, not just unfunny.

One more thing: don't set it in the jungle with a linguist pointing at a rabbit. Everyone's heard that one. Move it somewhere else — a translation app, a vet's office, a museum, an archaeologist with a dead language, an AI company, whatever — as long as the philosophy survives the move. And make it an actual joke with a setup and a punchline, not a sketch or a monologue. It should be funny to tell at a party where half the room has a PhD and half doesn't.
