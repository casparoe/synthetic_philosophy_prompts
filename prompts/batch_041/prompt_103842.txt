I'm making a handout for a philosophy of science reading group that's about to tackle the natural kinds / 'does science carve nature at its joints' question, and I want to avoid the standard tour of essentialism vs pluralism — everyones read that a million times and it makes the real disagreements look shallower than they are. What I actually want is a decision tree that starts from something very thin and walks someone to a named position, where every branch node is a question they could answer without already knowing which position it leads to.

Here's the angle I care about, so dont just list the famous views. Build the logical space first and then drop the positions into it. I want the tree to seperate views along (at least) these axes, in roughly this dependency order:

- first, whether there's a mind-independent fact of the matter about which taxonomy is best at all, or whether 'carving at the joints' is just a metaphor that's quietly smuggling in realism (I want a leaf for the view that the question is confused or that the metaphor does no work — a quietist / deflationary option);
- then, for those who are realists, what actually unifies the members of a kind: a shared essence (necessary and sufficient intrinsic properties), a cluster of properties that tend to co-occur and reinforce each other, or a relational / invariant structure — i.e. the kind is the role the members play in a causal or counterfactual structure rather than anything they share intrinsically;
- then whether kind boundaries are sharp or graded, and whether the vagueness is in the world or in us;
- then monism vs pluralism: is there one privileged taxonomy, or many that are equally legitimate for different purposes?
- and finally whether social kinds get handled by the same machinery as chemical or biological ones, or whether they need something else entirely.

Every branch question has to be answerable by someone who doesn't know the labels, and each leaf should carry the name of the position (or 'unnamed' if nobody defends it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I want roughly 8–12 leaves and 6–8 branch nodes.

Some specific cases I want the tree to actually use, so it doesn't float free of any science: a chemical element, a biological species, a psychiatric diagnosis (depression or PTSD, your pick), and one social kind (money or race — either). At least one branch question should turn on each of these, and each leaf should say which of the four it handles well and which it handles badly.

Two things I don't want papered over: (a) if there's an answer-combination that no actual position occupies, say so and diagnose it — is nobody there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored? and (b) if two leaves can't be separated by your tree, flag the pair rather than pretending some branch divides them.

Also, don't lean on bibliography — I don't need citations or reading lists, just the structure.
