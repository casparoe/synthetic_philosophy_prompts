I'm opening a two-day workshop on inconsistency in logic on Thursday and I've got the welcome — fifteen minutes, and its the usual mess of an audience: maybe eight people who actually work on non-classical logic, a handful of linguists and CS students who came for the word "paradox" in the title, and two funders who are there because the grant says "logic and reasoning." I want the welcome to do something rather than be housekeeping.

Here's what I want it built around. I've been arguing that the interesting question about true contradictions isn't "are there any" but "who pays when you get rid of them." You can always restore consistency by giving something up — a premise, a piece of testimony, an institution's self-description. My line is that a contradiction is load-bearing exactly when the cost of consistency lands on someone who has no business paying it. I want the remarks to make that case, and to include one contradiction I assert as true and stand behind — not a rhetorical "he was and wasn't generous," not a Liar joke. The logicians in the room will be checking whether it's a real contradiction or just two senses of one word, so it has to survive that, and it can't slide into anything-goes.

Also, a few lines at the end for me: what in there is settled among people who actually work on paraconsistent logic, and what's genuinely contested — especially anything I'd be asserting that a dialetheist would push back on. Don't sell a live disagreement as the consensus.
