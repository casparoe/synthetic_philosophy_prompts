I've been stuck on the Judy Benjamin problem for months and the more I read the more I think the literature has stalled in a way nobody has diagnosed properly. You know the setup: a soldier is dropped into enemy territory that's divided into red and blue, and within each there's the headquarters area and the rest; she starts with a flat prior over the four cells; then an informant tells her something like, I dont know which territory you're in, but if you're in red the odds are three to one that you're in the headquarters area. Bayes' rule doesn't apply directly, because what she got isn't a proposition she can conditionalize on — it's a constraint on a conditional probability. Van Fraassen's move was to update by minimizing the relative information from the prior subject to that constraint, and that gives a definite answer. The fight is about what that answer does to the cells it wasn't about: it doesn't leave the blue cells alone, and a lot of people think that's already a reductio, while others think it's exactly right, since the report is evidence about how the territories are laid out or about what the informant knows. Then there's a third camp that says the whole thing is a modelling artifact: if you conditionalize on a richer proposition about what the informant said and how reliable she is, you never face the problem at all, and the conditional-constraint formalism is just a lossy way of writing down something that should have been a likelihood. My working hypothesis is that the camps aren't disagreeing about rationality at all, they're disagreeing about what counts as the same learning event, but I want you to tell me if that's too neat.

What I want from you is a diagnosis of why this hasn't been settled, not a verdict on who's right. Is this a substantive disagreement about diachronic norms — what a rational agent is required to do when she receives this kind of report — or is it a disagreement about what the phenomenon even is, i.e. about which of those three camps is answering the actual question? Is anyone's position even well-defined without a prior commitment about how to represent the information a report carries? And is there a hidden formal question here — some result about which constraints are admissible, or about whether infomin is the only rule satisfying some plausible set of axioms — that would actually settle it if it were proved, or is the impasse a sign that updating on a conditional picks out several different things and the literature has been equivocating?

Also tell me what progress would look like given your diagnosis — a distinction, a theorem, a change in how the question is posed — or whether you think this is one of those debates that just shouldn't be expected to converge.
