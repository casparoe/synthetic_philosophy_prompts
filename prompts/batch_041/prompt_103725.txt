I'm presenting a paper at our divinity school's faculty seminar in three weeks and I want a Q&A prep sheet. My thesis: the problem of divine hiddenness is normally posed as a question about how much evidence God gives any individual seeker, and I think that's the wrong axis. The real problem is distributional and intergenerational -- the live option of belief is concentrated in particular times and places, so what mostly determines whether someone has a serious chance to believe is when and where they're born. I argue that reframing makes the standard free-will and soul-making replies misfire, because both are built to answer "why isn't God more evident to me?" and not "why is God so unevenly evident across history?" The twist that keeps this from being a straight atheistic argument: I think it actually favors a view on which God's presence is mediated through the ongoing practices of a community rather than available as direct experience, so it's ment as an intra-theistic correction too. The audience is about fifteen people -- mostly systematic theologians and biblical scholars, a couple of pastoral theologians who care about what this does to actual congregations, one analytic philosopher of religion who'll want the argument formalized, and one Jewish studies colleague who'll press on whether the whole framing is Christian-specific. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased in each person's vocabulary, ordered by how much damage they do, with a two-minute reply for each and a note on where the reply is soft. Include what I should concede up front and the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
