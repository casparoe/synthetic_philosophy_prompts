Ok so I'm a science-and-ideas journo and I'm working on a feature about the long-running spat between the counterfactual crowd (cause = the thing without which the effect wouldn't of happened, Lewis and co) and the interventionist crowd (cause = the thing you can wiggle, Woodward and co). I keep getting told they're 'basically the same picture' once you do the semantics, and I don't buy it, but I need a number, not a vibe.

Here's the claim I want your credence on, stated as precisely as I can: 'For actual/token causation, the interventionist framework and the counterfactual-dependence framework, each repaired in the least ad-hoc way, pick out the same extension -- every case that one calls a cause the other does too, with no case where the disagreement is substantive rather than just a difference in what we've stipulated the words to mean.'

And I want the decomposition, with a number and one line each for: (a) whether 'an intervention' can be characterized without quietly helping yourself to counterfactuals about what the intervention would do; (b) whether the counterfactual account can handle preemption and backup cases without repairs that themselves presuppose causation; (c) whether token causation is even one determinate relation or a family of partly stipulative ones; (d) whether our case-judgments are stable enough that there's a fact of the matter to be right about; (e) whatever else you think belongs. Then how they combine.

To make it concrete, here's teh toy case that's been bugging me: two switches wired in parallel to a light. Switch 1 is closed, switch 2 is open. Open switch 1 and the light goes out (2 can't carry it), so switch 1 is counterfactually necessary. Now add an auto-repair: whenever switch 1 opens, a mechanism closes switch 2. Now flipping 1 does nothing to the light -- no counterfactual dependence, no intervention sensitivity. Is switch 1 still 'the cause' of the light being on? Every engineer I ask says obviously yes, but neither framework gets there without bolting on extra machinery, and the bolted-on bits start to look like they're just presupposing the causation they were meant to explain. Tell me how much that case should move me, and which sub-question my final number is most sensitive to.

For reference I'm sitting at about 0.3 myself. Also tell me what would actually shift your number most -- a formal result, a new counterexample, or just people's intuitions turning out to be alot less stable than we assumed.
