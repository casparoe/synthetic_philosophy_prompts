Write a short story of roughly 1,000 words set in a fictional island town whose artisans have for centuries quarried and worked a soft blue-grey mineral they call ondrast, prized as a pigment, a glaze, and a celebrated remedy for stomach ailments; over the generations the guild's masters have accumulated many confident generalizations about it. A visiting naturalist arrives with a new instrument and demonstrates that ondrast is really two chemically distinct substances, indistinguishable to the naked eye, one of which behaves as the guild's claims require while the other does not — including in the body, where the old remedy turns out to be harmless for one substance and poisonous for the other. The story should turn on the quarrel that follows about what has actually happened. The guildmaster insists that nothing he ever asserted was false, since the word always picked out whatever he was holding in his hand; the naturalist insists the guild was simply mistaken about a natural kind; a logician from the mainland suggests the word was never a kind term at all, so there is no fact of the matter about what it really named. Stage at least two of these positions through argument rather than summary, include a moment in which someone asks whether the mineral would have been any different had the islanders called it something else, and end with a character grasping that the discovery was not a contingent fact about how the world happens to be arranged but a fact about what ondrast had always been — while conceding that this fact could only have been learned by looking. Do not adjudicate the dispute by fiat.
