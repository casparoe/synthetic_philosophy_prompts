I'm putting together a seminar on Aquinas and tyrannicide and I want a prediction exercise for my students. Here's the track record I've extracted from his discussions, as I read him. Tell me what he'd say about the new case at the end, and which rule he's implicitly running. I'm less interested in the verdict itself then in the competing rules that fit the earlier cases, and which further case would settle it.

Earlier verdicts:

1. A general seizes the throne by force, killing the royal family. He then rules brutally, executing opponents and taxing heavily. The senate authorises a plot to kill him, and the plot would cause some unrest but far less suffering than his continued rule. Aquinas's verdict: the plot is permissible; the killer would be honoured.

2. A king inherits the throne legitimately. He becomes a tyrant, executing opponents and taxing heavily. No public authority authorises killing him, and a plot to kill him would trigger a civil war that kills far more people than his tyranny would. Aquinas's verdict: the plot is not permissible; private individuals may not kill him.

3. A king inherits the throne legitimately. He becomes a tyrant, but the harm he causes is relatively mild — heavy taxes, some unjust imprisonments. No public authority authorises killing him, and a plot to kill him would cause a civil war that kills many more people than his rule would. Aquinas's verdict: not permissible.

New case:

4. A usurper seizes power by force, but then rules moderately for twenty years. The country is stable and prosperous. No public authority authorises killing him, and a plot to kill him would cause a civil war that kills more people than his rule would. Is it permissible to kill him?

What would Aquinas say about case 4? Which rule is he implicitly running across the earlier verdicts — usurper versus legitimate ruler, a proportionality condition, the presence or absence of public authority, or something else? Are there other rules that fit the earlier verdicts just as well? Which of those rules does case 4 pull apart, and what further case would best tell them apart?

Please seperate what's actually settled in Aquinas scholarship from what's genuinely contested, and don't present a contested reading as the consensus.
