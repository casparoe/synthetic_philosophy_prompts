Last month, in a committee meeting about a shared scheduling system — the question was whether the interface should display who holds each reserved slot — a colleague ended the debate with the line that "Technology is neither good nor bad; nor is it neutral," usually cited as Kranzberg's first law. He drew two conclusions from it at once: that the software itself bears no moral charge, and that we should therefore stop arguing about how it is built and argue instead about how people will use it. I suspect one sentence cannot carry both conclusions, but I could not say precisely where it bends, so I said nothing. I would like a menu of the distinct claims that sentence could be making, with verdicts attached, that I can bring to the next meeting.

Please lay the readings out from the nearly trivial to the radical. Some of them will be claims about artifacts as physical things, some about the practice of designing them, some about what happens after deployment, and some about the social arrangements a technology needs in order to function at all; sort the readings into those groups and say for each which group it belongs to.

"Neutral" is doing all the work, so keep its senses apart: neutral with respect to what, for whom, and under whose description of the artifact. Say which of those senses are independent of one another — can something be neutral in one sense while failing to be neutral in another? I think that is the crux, and if it is, I want it stated plainly.

For each reading, give a verdict: definitional or platitudinous, substantive and defensible, contentious, or simply false, together with what would have to be the case for it to fail.

Finally, tell me which readings, if any, actually license my colleague's move from "the tool is neutral" to "argue about use, not design," and which reading would have licensed the opposite conclusion from the same sentence. If what he did was slide between two senses rather than reason from either one, say so — and tell me which of these readings the people who repeat the line usually have in mind.
