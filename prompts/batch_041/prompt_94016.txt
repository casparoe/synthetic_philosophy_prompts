I'm a TA for a first-year seminar on historical responsibility, and the week on 1914 always goes badly — everyone reaches for the same two moves, "Germany was guilty" versus "it was the system, not people," and then we just trade those back and forth for fifty minutes. (We spend one session on the Fischer business, the historian who said Germany's leaders went to war on purpose to become a European hegemon and set off a national argument about guilt and the national past — but the students mostly just take away "some guy said it was Germany's fault.") What I want instead is a decision tree a student can actually walk down, starting from the one thing almost everyone in the room already accepts: nobody in July 1914 sat down and decided they wanted a general European war, and the crisis spiraled through mobilizations nobody felt able to stop.

From there every branch should be a question the student can answer without knowing where it leads. Does responsibility need a culpable intention, or can you be answerable for something you never foresaw? Can it attach to a collective — a state, a general staff, a generation — or only to individuals? Is the thing we're handing out guilt (deserved blame), liability (who pays), or answerability (who has to explain themselves)? Are we asking about who caused the war to *start*, or about who made it as long and as horrible as it turned out? And is the point of assigning it backward-looking (settling the record) or forward-looking (making the next war less likely)?

I don't need a tour of the schools of thought, but if a leaf happens to match something people actually call themselves — structuralism, the primacy-of-domestic-politics crowd, the no-fault "the alliance system did it" line, the view that the *state* can be responsible even when no individual was — go ahead and name it in a word or two. What I really want is the tree itself: first the questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then the actual positions dropped into that space, with a note on any combination nobody occupies and a guess at whether it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any two positions your tree can't separate — I'd rather know the instrument's limits than have it fake a distinction. Keep it to around ten leaves, and make sure it can place both the person who says "the war was nobody's fault and everybody's responsibility" and the person who says the opposite. Oh, and it has to handle my favorite awkward case: a single mid-level staff officer whose one memorandum, had it not been lost in the post, would have changed the whole July timeline — does *he* carry the war's responsibility, or only his memo's?
