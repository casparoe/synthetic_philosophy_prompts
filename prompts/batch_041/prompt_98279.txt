I have been in a seminar on theories of well-being, and we spent a session on the experience-machine thought experiment and what it is supposed to establish about hedonism. The standard use of that case is entirely one-directional: it is deployed to show that hedonism is too permissive, because the hedonist is supposed to say that a life spent plugged in is a good life, while desire-satisfaction and objective-list theorists demur. I want a case that runs the intuition the other way, and I want it built to specification rather than merely described.

Construct a single realistic life — no machines, no brains in vats, no exotic neuroscience, just an ordinary pursuit such as a demanding career, a long apprenticeship, or a caretaking commitment — that holds three features together at once. First, by the lights of an objective-list theory the life scores very high: it contains real achievement, genuine knowledge, deep relationships, and autonomous choice in abundance. Second, by the lights of a desire-satisfaction theory the life scores very high as well: the person forms a stable, informed, central desire and, over the course of the life, fulfills it, with few of their other significant desires frustrated. Third, by the lights of a hedonistic theory the life scores low: the felt quality of the person's experience is on balance painful, anxious, and joyless, so that the balance of pleasure over pain comes out negative. The hard part — and the reason I am asking you rather than merely noting that such a case is conceivable — is that the pain must not be an incidental add-on. It should be generated by the very activities that produce the achievement, fulfill the desire, and constitute the relationships, so that the experience cannot be varied while the rest is held fixed without thereby changing the life; the three theories are meant to be looking at one and the same life and disagreeing, not at three different lives.

After the scenario, do the following. State the verdict each of the three theories gives about this life and name the precise feature of each theory that generates that verdict, so that it is clear the verdicts are derived rather than stipulated. Identify the single variable doing the work, and say honestly whether the entanglement just described prevents the case from isolating that variable — that is, whether this is a fair test of hedonism or a rigged one. Tell me what becomes of the case if the entanglement is removed and the felt experience is allowed to float free of the achievements and relationships. Then give the strongest reply available to a hedonist who wants to reject the verdict, and say whether that same reply would also rescue the hedonist from the standard experience-machine argument or whether it costs them something there. Finally, tell me which feature of the case a desire-satisfaction theorist would press on, given that their theory lands on the same side as the objective-list theory here but for entirely different reasons.
