Ok so I'm on the commitee that writes our department's grading bands, and we keep running into the same wall: whatever line we draw between "excellent" and "good" looks arbitrary, and moving a comma can flip a paper from one band to the next. One member's proposed fix is to stop drawing lines entirely. Require that a paper's band be stable under trivial edits, so two papers that differ by a comma have to land in the same band, and where that constraint can't be satisfied the grader writes "unclear" rather than picking a side. Sounds nice, except it seems to force us either into a growing pile of "unclear" verdicts or into a rubric where the only bands anyone ever uses are "perfect" and "worthless". I'm fairly sure this is the sorites paradox wearing a committee badge, but I'd like you to lay out the most important implications of the sorites paradox for the actual design of grading bands. Not a tour of the competing philosophical positions (epistemicism, supervaluationism, degrees of truth, all that, I've heard it) but what it implies for what a rubric of this kind can and can't do, and what it implies about that specific proposal. Two things I'd like folded in. First, does it matter that graders disagree with each other, i.e. that the "same band" relation is being applied by fallible people rather than by one ideal judge? Second, does the problem look any different if we stop scoring papers against a fixed standard and only ever ask which of two papers is better, comparative judgements all the way down, no absolute bands at all? Does that dodge the paradox or just relocate it?
