I am working through the section of Turing's 1950 paper in Mind where he turns from the imitation game to the practical question of how a thinking machine might actually be built. Rather than simulating an adult mind, he proposes building a child machine and then educating it, comparing the process to the schooling of a child, involving rewards and punishments, a built-in capacity for inference, and a degree of randomness, and yielding something far simpler at the outset than the adult intelligence one hopes to end up with. He adds that we cannot expect a good child machine at the first attempt, and that educating it would be a slow and substantial undertaking. Here is the gist of it in my own words rather than a quotation, and I would rather have help with the argument than with the exact wording or with any references.
