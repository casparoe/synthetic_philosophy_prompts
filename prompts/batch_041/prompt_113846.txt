Analyze the Christmas Truce of 1914 through the lens of the centipede game and the backward induction paradox. On the Western Front, soldiers on both sides stopped firing, fraternized, and in places played football, even though the war continued and their commanders disapproved. Model this as a centipede game in which each side can either fire (take) or hold fire (pass), with payoffs that grow if both pass and a known terminal node. Backward induction predicts firing from the first move, so the truce is an anomaly. Give the mapping in detail -- players, moves, payoffs, terminal node -- and then say where the model breaks: is the truce better explained as an indefinitely repeated game, a coordination game, or a case in which common knowledge of rationality fails? What does the episode show about the backward induction paradox -- does it vindicate the critics who say the paradox depends on an unrealistic terminal node, or does it show that the truce was not a single centipede at all? What assumptions about soldiers' preferences and beliefs would make the truce rational, and do those assumptions dissolve the paradox or merely relocate it?
