My roommate is a brilliant engineer with a thoroughgoing materialist streak, and we keep re-litigating the same disagreement. He insists that any explanation which doesn't bottom out in the lowest-level physical facts is at best a placeholder for our ignorance and at worst a ghost smuggled in through the back door. When economists invoke 'market sentiment' or 'bubbles,' he says the only real account is a blow-by-blow of individual traders' neurons; when a biologist says a trait is 'for' something, he says that's just shorthand for gene frequencies and that anyone who speaks of function is quietly cheating. I think he's making a mistake, and not the obvious one of denying that the physical facts are there. The demand that every good explanation be the lowest-level one seems to dissolve the very regularities that make the higher-level sciences possible, as if the only honest map were one that reproduced every molecule of the terrain. But when I try to say this out loud, I end up sounding as though I'm defending spooky emergent forces, which I'm not. I've read enough of the usual blogs to suspect there's a name for his move, but the treatments I find are either too abstract or degenerate into turf wars. Help me frame the disagreement so that I'm not just pleading for mystery. I want an argument I can actually hand him, not a slogan. Answer in American English, in 400 words or fewer.
