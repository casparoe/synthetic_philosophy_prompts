OK so I'm a science journalist, I've been reporting around this long-running fight in philosphy of science over inference to the best explanation — the idea that we should believe whichever hypothesis best explains the evidence. The thing I keep tripping over is whether that's actually a distinct kind of inference or just Bayesian updating wearing a trench coat.

Here's what I want from you. Take this claim: inference to the best explanation is not reducible to Bayesian inference — there are cases where accepting the best explanation is rational even though no combination of priors and likelihoods makes that hypothesis the most probable one.

Give me your actual credence in that, and please don't just hand me a number with a shrug. Decompose it. The pieces I'd want numbers on:

- the odds that any IBE verdict you like can be reconstructed as a Bayesian one once you're allowed to pick the right priors (the "capture" thesis)
- the odds that explanatory virtues like simplicity and unification are genuinely truth-conducive rather than just aesthetically nice
- the odds that the "bad lot" problem is fatal — that the best explanation on offer is often just the best of a bad bunch, so "best" tells you nothing about truth
- the odds that the whole fight is verbal, i.e. "best explanation" just *means* "most probable hypothesis" and we're arguing about a word

One more thing to fold in, cause a guy I had coffee with pitched it to me and I can't tell if it's clever or a dodge: he says the whole dichotomy is fake, because IBE isn't competing with Bayesianism at all — it's the story of where the priors come from. Explanation-shaped considerations generate the prior, and then Bayes does the updating. On his view, "IBE vs. Bayes" is like asking whether the foundation is competing with the house. Does that move your number? And should it be its own term in the decomposition, or does it just collapse into one of the ones above?

Then tell me which single term you're least sure about and what would actually move it — a result, an arguement, whatever it is. And if the number you land on is basically "this is 50/50 and always will be," fine, say so, but tell me why it's stuck there instead of pretending to a precision you don't have.
