Hey, I'm a backend enginerr with no philosophy background, and I'm designing a little reasoning agent that keeps a store of facts and rules and answers queries off it. I keep running into this logical omniscience thing people bring up, and before I lock in the design I want a sanity check on how I actually think about belief, because I have a suspicion I'm contradicting myself somewhere and can't see where. Here's my set:

(a) A belief is just a sentence in the store. If it isn't stored and isn't computed on demand, the agent doesn't believe it. I dont think there's such a thing as an implicit belief, thats just a query you haven't run yet.
(b) If the agent believes P and believes P implies Q, it ought to believe Q. Refusing isn't a stylistic choice, it's an error.
(c) A perfectly rational agent believes every logical consequence of what it believes. Thats just what rationality is, at the limit.
(d) No real agent, mine or a human, can do that. We're finite. That's the human condition, not a defect.
(e) The store must never hold P and not-P at the same time. Contradiction is the one thing I won't tolerate.
(f) But I also want it to swallow whatever a user tells it without re-checking consistency on every write, and keep deriving answers off the store anyway.

Which of these actually conflict, how bad is each one, and what's the cheapest thing to drop to fix it? If some of them only look like they conflict, say so and tell me why. Rank the real ones by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and spell out what each commitment is really dragging in that I probably didn't notice.
