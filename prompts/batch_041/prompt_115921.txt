I'm preparing a student-led interview with a philosopher who defends a nonstandard version of illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. Her view is not simply that phenomenal properties do not exist; it is that introspection misclassifies its own predictive model of a state as a non-representational given, and that this misclassification, rather than any sensory content, is what we mistake for phenomenal character. Draft eight to ten interview questions that engage this specific position instead of letting her restate illusionism. Include questions on why the misclassification is not just a false belief about one's own representations, on how the account distinguishes access consciousness from whatever the illusion is supposed to be an illusion of, on whether introspective reports can be reliable enough to support the view that they are systematically misleading, and on how the illusion of phenomenal character differs from the illusion of selfhood if both arise from predictive processing. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and make at least two follow-ups harder if she deflects by saying the issue is really about our reports rather than about consciousness. No citations or reading recommendations. Finally, tell me which of your questions is most likely to misfire, and what answer or consideration would lead you to abandon or substantially revise it.
