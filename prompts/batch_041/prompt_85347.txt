For a seminar I am preparing on the bearing of a philosopher's life on the assessment of his work, I would like your credence — a number, with its derivation — on the following proposition about Frege.

The proposition: the diary Frege kept in the spring of 1924, with its antisemitic and authoritarian contents and its conception of the individual as properly subordinate to the nation and the state, bears on the interpretation and assessment of his logical and semantic doctrines — that is, an interpreter of his anti-psychologism, of his logicism, or of his account of thoughts as timeless and independent of any thinker would be getting something wrong in setting the diary aside as merely biographical.

I take the strongest form of this to be a bridging thesis of roughly the following shape: the same anti-individualism that the diary applies to political life is already at work in the doctrine that thoughts and logical laws are answerable to nothing in the mental life of actual thinkers. My own number is about 0.3; a colleague who works on the period puts herself at 0.05, and I suspect we may not mean the same thing by bearing on.

Please decompose. I would like a rough probability and a one-line justification for each of at least the following, plus any term you think I have omitted: (i) that the diary's views are attributable to Frege as settled convictions rather than as an old man's irritability or the commonplaces of his class and moment; (ii) that the bridging thesis is defensible on the texts — that anti-psychologism really does subordinate the individual thinker to an impersonal order, rather than merely denying psychology pride of place in logic; (iii) that the connection is explanatory rather than a coincidence of two independent tastes; (iv) that, granting (ii) and (iii), this touches the justification or credibility of the doctrines rather than only their causal history; (v) that the diary's politics form a coherent enough position to be said to have implications for abstract doctrines at all; and (vi) whatever weight you assign to the disambiguation of bearing on as interpretive illumination rather than epistemic relevance.

Then combine them, tell me which term my number is most sensitive to, and name the evidence or argument that would move it most — including whether what separates me from my colleague is a disagreement about Frege or a disagreement about what bearing on ought to mean. If the proposition as I have stated it is ill-formed, say so, and give the number for the reading you consider most defensible.
