I am a novelist at work on a book about a field biologist who has spent her career insisting that the elaborate deceits of the natural world — the moth that mimics a wasp, the plover that drags a feigned broken wing across the ground to draw a predator away from its nest — are not merely the downstream effects of selection but genuine strategies, things done for reasons, even though nothing in the animal is doing the reasoning. In the course of researching her I have fallen into a dispute among philosophers that seems to have run for decades without either side yielding: one camp holds that a free-floating rationale is a real and objective feature of the world, that selection can lodge a reason in a creature that never entertains one, and that this is precisely why we may say the plover's display is for misleading the predator; the other camp holds that such language is either a harmless shorthand for a history of differential reproduction or a covert re-importation of a designer's intentions, and that a disciplined account ought to paraphrase the teleology away altogether. Each side seems to grasp the other's arguments perfectly and to have a prepared reply to each of them, and still neither moves; my own suspicion, offered only as a hypothesis, is that the quarrel is not finally about evolution or animal minds but about what a reason is even permitted to be, and perhaps about whether an explanation must terminate in an agent.

I would like a diagnosis rather than a verdict — an account of what kind of disagreement this actually is, grounded in what each side genuinely says. Is it a verbal dispute over how to use a word, a substantive one, or two camps answering different questions under a single heading? Is some hidden empirical or formal question doing the real work — a fact about selection, or about the logic of functional explanation? And given your diagnosis, what, if anything, would count as progress: a distinction, a theorem, a change of question, or nothing at all, so that I should stop expecting the argument ever to end and simply dramatize the impasse in the novel? Please give your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion.
