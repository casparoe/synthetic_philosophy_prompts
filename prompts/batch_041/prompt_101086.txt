I'm a third-year PhD student in a microbiology lab, and I've gotten stuck on a disagreement that I increasingly suspect is not really about our data. We work on a soil bacterium that, in its natural environment, uses a particular enzyme to break down a class of plant-derived compounds it encounters when it colonizes root tissue. A couple of years ago someone in the lab engineered a strain in which that same enzyme also happens to detoxify an industrial solvent that contaminates some of the sites we study, and we now rely on that activity in a bioremediation project. My advisor says the enzyme's function is the plant-compound job, full stop; the solvent activity is a side effect we happen to exploit, and calling it a function of the enzyme in our manuscript would be misleading. My co-author, a visiting postdoc, says that is backwards: the function is whatever the enzyme actually does in the system we are studying, and since our engineered strain lives and grows by detoxifying the solvent, that is now part of what the enzyme does. We have to write one sentence for a genome-annotation database entry, another for a patent disclosure, and a third for the paper's abstract, and the three sentences seem to demand different answers. The same tangle shows up in a colleague's project: a regulatory sequence in this organism no longer appears to do anything like what its counterpart does in a related species, yet it is clearly bound by a protein and measurably shifts expression of a nearby gene, and nobody can agree whether to call it functional at all.

I keep feeling that both of them are right about something, and that the quarrel is really about what kind of question a function claim is answering, but whenever I try to state what the two kinds of question are, I lose the thread. Could you lay out the competing ways biologists and philosophers have tried to make function talk precise, and then say, for each of my cases in turn -- the engineered enzyme in the remediation strain, the same enzyme in the wild-type bacterium, and the regulatory sequence that is active but no longer ancestral -- which descriptions are licensed by which account, and where an account delivers no verdict or two conflicting ones? I would also like to know which parts of this are genuine disagreements about the world and which are just people using one word for different jobs. Please begin with a single sentence telling me what you think I should put in the abstract, then give the rest in no more than 600 words total.
