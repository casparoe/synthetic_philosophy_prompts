My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether knowing something gives you a licence to stop listening to arguments against it, and I've been trying to write down where I actually come out, so I can see whether the whole thing holds together before our seminar meets again. Here is the package. (1) Knowledge is factive: I can't know a proposition that isn't true. (2) If a proposition is true, then any evidence that tells against it is misleading evidence — evidence that would push me away from the truth rather than toward it. (3) It's rationally permissible to disregard misleading evidence; you aren't epistemically criticisable for ignoring something that is, as it happens, leading you astray. I take it that (1)–(3) together entail (4): if I know that p, then it's rationally permissible for me to disregard any future evidence that tells against p. I'll readily admit that (4) sounds monstrous, but I can't yet see which of the first three to surrender, and I don't want to give one up merely because I dislike where it leads. (5) Dogmatism is a vice: someone who resolves in advance to ignore counter-evidence, who treats a present conviction as beyond revision, is being epistemically irresponsible, and a rational believer proportions belief to the total evidence and stays open to being shown wrong. (6) I'm an evidentialist about rationality — what it's rational for you to believe is fixed by the evidence you actually have, not by how the world happens to be independent of your evidence. (7) And I think knowledge can survive misleading evidence: you can still know that p while carrying evidence against p that you haven't yet recognised as misleading.

Go through these and tell me what is actually pulling against what. I suspect not every apparent clash is a real one, and I'd rather you discriminate than flag everything: say which pairs merely look inconsistent, which one or two are genuinely incompatible, and rank the genuine tensions by how much damage they do. For each real tension, say what would have to give — which commitment I'd have to revise, and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the package. Then, if there's a single change that would clear up the most at the least cost, tell me what it is and what it would still leave unresolved. Please don't just pick whichever item is easiest to drop; I want to know which commitment is doing the load-bearing work, and I genuinely haven't decided which one I'd give up.
