I retired a few years ago after a career in experimental chemistry, and I have spent much of that retirement reading philosophy of mind — slowly, and with the suspicion of an outsider who has watched too many tidy models collapse under a dirty measurement. I would like you to write a satirical piece for me, funny to someone who has thought hard about the timing of experience but still legible to a reader who has not.

Here is the material I want it built on. In the color phi phenomenon, two dots — say a blue one above and a red one below — are flashed in quick succession, and the viewer reports a single dot that moves between the two positions and changes color abruptly halfway along the path. The difficulty is that the color change is reported as occurring before the second dot has even been presented. The two broad ways of accommodating this are usually labeled the Orwellian and the Stalinesque readings: on the first, the experience is assembled in real time and the memory of it is later rewritten; on the second, the brain withholds judgment, waits for the second stimulus, and edits the experience before it is ever had, so that nothing is rewritten because the record never contained the discrepancy. The feature I want the piece to exploit is that for everything a subject can report or do, the two readings appear to predict the same thing.

I want a deadpan FAQ — a customer-support page, in the numb register such pages are written in — for a company that sells experience revision to consumers. It should offer at least two tiers mirroring the readings above: one in which the event was genuinely experienced and the record is later corrected, and one in which, per the fine print, the customer never experienced the event at all. The humor must come from getting the content right rather than from winking: the two tiers should be practically indistinguishable from the inside, the color-phi demonstration should be the company's flagship demo, and the page should quietly take positions on the questions it pretends to deflect — whether "never experienced it" is a coherent thing to sell, what was happening during the delay in the second tier, and why a customer cannot tell which tier they received. About 600 words, as an FAQ with questions and answers.

Then, under a heading titled "Where the parody leans on contested readings," write roughly 150 words taking a definite position, with reasons, on which of the two readings the piece commits to and where it takes liberties with the philosophy to make the joke land — I would rather have your judgment than a survey of the options.
