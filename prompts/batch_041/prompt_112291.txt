ok so I'm a backend dev, never taken a philosophy class, and I've got a thing that's been bugging me that I'm pretty sure is secretly a philosophy question.

I'm trying to pick between job offers and I keep going in circles, so I wrote out pairwise comparisons to try to be systematic about it. Now I trust my gut less, not more. Heres what I got. Offers A through J, arranged so that moving one letter down you give up $2,000/yr of salary and gain 10 minutes a day of commute (5 min each way). And every single time I look at two neighbors I pick the higher salary. A beats B, B beats C, C beats D, all the way to I beats J. Every one feels obvious when I look at it — 2 grand is real money, 10 minutes a day is nothing.

Then I actually sat in traffic and thought about A vs J head to head and picked J. J pays $18k less but I get 90 minutes a day of my life back, and at that size the money stops mattering to me, I'd take J over A without thinking. But if A>B and B>C and so on down to I>J, doesn't that force A>J? So either the nine neighbor calls are wrong or the A vs J call is wrong, and they all feel right when I'm looking at them.

Heres where it gets worse. Same two endpoints, different framing, opposite answer. If someone says "we'll cut your pay 18k and cut your commute by 90 min a day, yes or no" I say yes. But if someone says "I'll offer you nine separate raises of 2k, take them or leave them" and then "or instead, nine separate commute cuts of 10 min a day, take them or leave them," I take every raise and no commute cuts. Identical endpoints, and I answer differently depending on whether its one big move or nine small ones.

And a totally different pair my roommate threw at me: offer K pays $50k more but its in a city I'd hate; offer L pays way less but I'd love the city. I couldnt rank them. Not that one is better — theyre just not the same kind of thing. But I ranked every other pair fine, including way bigger money gaps.

So is "better than" even transitive, or is that just a math property that doesn't survive contact with real choices? What principle would actually spit out all my calls — the nine neighbor picks, the A vs J pick, the framing flip, and the K/L "can't compare"? If theres no single principle, which of my verdicts should I give up, and what does the survivor predict about a pair I havent thought about, like A vs F (five steps down: 9k less, 50 min a day)?

My best guess so far is that I've got some threshold where small commute gaps lose to money and big ones win, and its somewhere around an hour a day. Does that actually get everything, or am I fooling myself? Also if it matters, I dont think I'm being irrational exactly, it feels more like "better than" is one word doing two jobs and I only own one word. idk. thanks.
