Im putting together a seminar session on Lakatos and I want to try a thought experiment with my students. Take Lakatos exactly as he was at the end of his life — hard core, protective belt, progressive versus degenerating problem-shifts, the whole apparatus — and tell me what he would have made of how machine learning research actually runs today. In particular I'm thinking of the so-called bitter lesson, that idea which is popular among the people who build these systems: that the methods which win are the ones that just scale a general learner with more data and compute, and that every time somebody hand-builds domain knowledge into the system it eventually gets steamrolled. And then the pattern lately, where scaling stalls, so people bolt on hand-crafted scaffolding — chain-of-thought prompting, external tools, little bolt-on modules — to keep things improving. What I want is your honest verdict: would Lakatos file this whole business under a progressive research programme, a degenerating one, or would he say the bitter lesson isn't a research programme in his sense at all? Don't give me "he might have said various things" — commit to a verdict and argue it with his own machinery. Write it as plain continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, the way you'd write it to me in an email.
