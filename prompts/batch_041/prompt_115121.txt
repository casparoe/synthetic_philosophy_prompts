I'm the editorial lead for a clinical reference database, we add two or three new condition entries a month, and the label really matters, clinicians code from us, insurers pay from us, and patients read themselves into us. Lately every argument is the same argument wearing a different hat. Someone wants a proper entry for persistent grief after a loss, someone else wants one for obesity, and the menopause crowd are pushing for a rewrite of what we already have. I keep hearing two camps, disease is a biological function going wrong versus disease is harm plus a willingness to call it a problem, and a third lot insisting you need both. Write me a decision procedure my editors can actually run on a proposed entry, grounded in that debate but usable by someone with no philosophy training and an hour to spare. The questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, which of them are empirical and which are value calls dressed up as empirical, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts. Then run it on the three cases above, including at least one where it gives an answer I won't like. Be honest about what the procedure can't do, especially where the underlying biology is unsettled and the dysfunction claim is really just a placeholder for something we haven't worked out yet.

Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence, the single question that does most of the work, then the procedure itself, no preamble and no restating of the brief.
