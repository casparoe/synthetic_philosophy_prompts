Question posed to students (45 minutes, closed book):

"Social construction" is often glossed as the claim that something is not real, or is merely a matter of convention. Explain what is actually being claimed when we say that money, race, and gender are socially constructed. In your answer, distinguish constitutive from causal social construction, and say whether the fact that a kind is socially constructed tells against its being real or causally efficacious. Use money as a comparison case for race and gender.

Student answer:

The claim that something is socially constructed is not the claim that it is unreal or fictional. Money is the clearest case: pieces of paper and entries in ledgers have no value in themselves, and they are money only because we collectively accept them as such. So "socially constructed" means that something exists only because we treat it as existing. This is what it means to say that a kind is constituted by social facts, as opposed to merely caused by them. A kind is causally constructed when social arrangements cause the members of the kind to have certain features — for instance, when the way we classify people shapes what they turn out to be. A kind is constitutively constructed when the kind itself is made of social stuff, as money is made of collective acceptance.

Applying this: race is not a biological kind, but that does not mean race is not real or has no effects. Racial categories are real social positions, and being assigned to one affects where you live, what you earn, and how long you live. So social construction is compatible with reality and with causal efficacy. Gender is the same: it is constituted by social roles rather than by biology.

Because these kinds are constituted by our beliefs and practices, they are conventional in the way money is conventional: there is nothing in the nature of things that requires us to use dollars rather than euros, or to divide people into these particular racial categories rather than others. It follows that, just as a society can adopt a new currency by collective decision, it could abolish race by deciding to stop using racial categories. The only obstacle is that the relevant beliefs are deeply entrenched, so the change would take time.

The mistake people make is to think that if race is constructed, then racial inequality is not real. But the construction is real, and its effects are real.
