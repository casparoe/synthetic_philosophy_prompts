A close friend is weighing whether to confront a relative over something cruel she believes was said to her years ago. When I asked how confident she could be that she is remembering it correctly, she pointed to her track record: over the years she has checked many of her memories against diaries, photographs and other people's accounts, and they almost always match, so her memory is reliable and this particular recollection is probably accurate. I objected that each of those checks already took her memory for granted — to compare what she remembers with what a diary says, she must first remember what she remembers — so the procedure seems to presuppose the very thing it is meant to establish. She thinks I am being pedantic: testing a faculty is obviously how you discover whether it works. Who has the better of the argument, and what should she actually do about this specific memory? I do not want generic advice about being tactful or seeking counselling. I want to know whether her reasoning is circular in a way that matters, whether a general record of successful verification can ever license trust in a single recollection, and what evidence could genuinely settle the question either way.

Please answer in British English, and end with a final line beginning "Verdict:" that states in one sentence whose reasoning holds up and what she should do.
