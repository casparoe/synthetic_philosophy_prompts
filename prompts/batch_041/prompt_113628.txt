I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of medicine preparing a chapter on the concept of disease, and I have run into a colleague's draft section that summarizes the naturalism–normativism debate in a way that seems partly right but leaves me uneasy. The draft says: Naturalists such as Boorse treat disease as a value-free biological category. On the biostatistical view, a condition is a disease when it reduces an organ's or system's functional efficiency below the statistically normal level for the species; the judgment is descriptive, and talk of illness or deviance is a separate normative layer. Normativists deny that biology does any real constraining work: on their view, disease is whatever a culture or medical profession decides to count as disease, so the category is fundamentally social and political. The harmful-dysfunction account tries to split the difference by requiring both a biological dysfunction and a harm, but because harm is a normative notion, the account cannot preserve the naturalist's value-free core and ultimately reduces to normativism. The real issue, then, is whether values can be excluded from science: naturalism says they can, normativism says they cannot.

I would like an accuracy audit rather than a general overview. Go claim by claim through the draft: identify what a philosopher of medicine would accept, what is a defensible simplification, what is a subtle misrepresentation, and what is straightforwardly wrong. I am especially interested in whether the draft conflates the value-freedom of the concept of disease with the value-freedom of science, whether it overstates what normativists actually claim, and whether its treatment of the harmful-dysfunction account is fair to a defender of that account. Please also address two points that the draft passes over: first, how the naturalist's reliance on a reference class—species-typical for whom, under what environmental and developmental conditions—bears on the claim that the judgment is purely descriptive; second, how a careful normativist would distinguish strong from weak versions of the view, and whether the draft's all-or-nothing framing obscures that distinction. Finally, tell me whether the draft's concluding claim about values and science follows from the accurate parts of the summary, or whether it depends on the misreadings you identify.
