I've pretty much settled on a Humean view of laws of nature: laws aren't some extra thing layered on top of the world, they're just whatever generalizations earn their keep when you try to write down the whole history of the universe as compactly and informatively as you can. No governing, no forcing, no little nomic glue — just the mosaic of particular facts and the best summary of it. Honestly the main reason I hold it is that I've never once been able to make sense of what "governing" is supposed to add; it feels like a word doing no work.

So talk me out of it, or at least show me where the soft spots are. What I want:

1. Which objections actually count against it, as opposed to ones that just restate the anti-Humean intuition? For each, tell me whether a committed Humean would themselves grant it as a cost or just shrug it off.

2. Rank the defeaters. Which would be fatal, which merely awkward, which are just "yeah, that's the view"?

3. Here's a case I dreamed up that bugs me. Imagine the total mosaic admits two systematizations that are exactly tied on simplicity and strength, and one of them yields deterministic laws while the other yields chancy ones. Does the Humean get to say there's no fact of the matter about the laws there? If so, is that a bug or is it exactly what you'd expect from a view that treats laws as summaries? And if the anti-Humean says "obviously the laws are one or the other, determinism isn't a matter of taste," do they have any principled way to pick, or are they just reading a fact off the mosaic too?

4. A friend keeps needling me with this: any old regularity can be padded into some system, so unless I can actually hand over the simplicity metric, "best system" is just a promisory note. How much should that worry me? Is there a version of the view that doesn't owe you a metric?

5. I also worry about the explanation direction. If the laws are a summary of what happens, how can they explain what happens — isn't that circular? And does the quantum-entanglement stuff (correlations that don't seem to be grounded in any local facts) threaten the whole "everything supervenes on the mosaic of local matters of fact" bit, or can the Humean just widen what counts as the mosaic?

6. Finally, be honest: has the view been qualified so much that nothing could count against it anymore? If it has, is that a real problem for a view like this, or is insulation just the price of any broadly empiricist package?

The thing that would move me most is a case where the Humean is forced to deny something I can't bring myself to deny — some consequence of the view that's just unacceptable. If there isn't one, say so, and tell me why the anti-Humean is the one holding the bag.
