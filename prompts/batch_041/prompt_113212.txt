I serve on the interpretation review board of a regional heritage authority. The board rules, case by case, on whether a proposed archaeological inference is warranted for publication and for public display, and over the years it has built up a track record that I now need to extend.

Here is how it has ruled. It approved the inference that a set of standardized cubical weights, recovered in matching form from dozens of separate households, indicates a shared system of measurement. It rejected the inference that a single anthropomorphic figurine with exaggerated female features indicates the worship of a fertility goddess. It approved the inference that thick ash layers sealing several settlements in one valley indicate a destructive fire. It rejected the inference that an assemblage of unused, undamaged fine vessels buried beneath a house floor indicates a deliberate ritual of concealment. It approved the inference that marked uniformity in house size and in grave goods across a cemetery indicates limited hereditary inequality. And it rejected the inference that the absence of monumental architecture and of iconography at a site indicates an aniconic religion.

The new case before us comes from a burned house. Excavators recovered a set of small clay figurines of animals, each pierced and strung on a cord. The species represented match, in rough proportion, the butchered animal bones recovered from the same house, and the figurines show heavy wear consistent with repeated handling. The excavators propose that the occupants believed the figurines stood for their herds and used them in a ritual intended to protect the animals.

I would like your analysis of how this board is likely to rule and why. Please write it in the following four sections, in this order.

The candidate rules (roughly 300 words). Identify the distinct rules that fit the board's six earlier verdicts equally well. State each rule precisely enough that it could be applied mechanically, and explain for each how it accounts for all six rulings, including the ones it handles least comfortably.

Predictions for the new case (roughly 250 words). For each candidate rule, state the verdict it yields on the figurine case and the reasoning by which it gets there. Make clear where the rules agree and where they come apart.

My verdict and reasoning (roughly 300 words). Give your best guess at the board's actual ruling, and defend it: say which candidate rule you take to be doing the real work in the board's practice, what evidence from the track record supports that reading over the alternatives, and what the board would be committing itself to if your reading is right.

The case that would settle it (roughly 150 words). Describe a single further case -- artifact type, context, and proposed inference -- whose verdict would distinguish the candidate rules that your preferred rule does not already eliminate. Explain what each remaining rule predicts about it and why that makes it the decisive test.
