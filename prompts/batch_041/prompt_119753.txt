I'm putting together a fill-in-the-blank handout for a theory-of-history reading group — mixed crowd, two economists, three historians, one physicist who keeps insisting history ought to be a science. I want a decision tree that sorts someone from whatever they say about the messiness of the past to a named position on whether macrohistory can be a predictive science, and I want the sorting to run off questions they can answer without knowing where they're being led. Branches I care about: are there regularities in history that outrun what the people inside them believed, or is every pattern just the average of choices? if there are regularities, are they law-like or only mechanism-sketches that die when you transplant them into another century? is the payoff retrodiction or actually calling the next few decades? do the cycles come from inside the social system (elite overproduction, demographic pressure on land) or from outside shocks (climate, plague, new tech)? is a 'cycle' a clock — fixed period — or just a recurring shape with no set length? do you think the data-generating process is stationary enough to pool centuries, or does every era need its own model? and one node on reflexivity: if the state starts steering by your model, does the model survive?

Leaves should include the Turchin-style structural-demographic cliodynamics line, the cliometric 'measure carefully, don't prophesy' line, the Annales / longue-durée structure-over-events line, the rational-action 'we don't need social laws' position, plain idiographic 'each event is its own thing', and one unnamed leaf for the combination nobody seems to occupy. Cap it at ten leaves, flag any two the tree can't seperate, and for each empty branch say whether you think it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. No readings, no citations — it's a handout.
