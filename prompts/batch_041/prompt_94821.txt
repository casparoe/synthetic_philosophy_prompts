I am a formal verification engineer, and since my mother’s death last year I have been sitting with a small Buddhist group. Madhyamaka has become personally important to me, but I keep wanting to translate it into the vocabulary I actually use. Please restate Nāgārjuna’s tetralemma and the Madhyamaka account of emptiness in the vocabulary of programming-language semantics and formal logic. My working vocabulary is typed lambda calculus, operational semantics, model theory, three-valued Kleene logic, Belnap’s four-valued logic, and the semantics of SQL NULLs; I can read a sequent calculus but I am not a logician by training. I have also been tinkering with a bilattice whose four values are true, false, both, and neither, ordered both by truth and by information. Use that apparatus where it helps, but do not assume that a four-valued logic just is the tetralemma; if the match is only surface-level, say so.

Then give me the residue report. For each of the four corners, for the claim that phenomena are empty of svabhāva, for dependent origination, and for the distinction between conventional and ultimate truth, say what translates cleanly, what translates only with distortion, and what has no expression at all in my vocabulary. Treat the residue as the interesting part. In particular, tell me whether the therapeutic or soteriological point of the tetralemma survives the translation, or whether it becomes a merely formal puzzle about negation and truth values. Is the residue a defect of my vocabulary, or a sign that the Madhyamaka claim is less clear than it looks? Where my apparatus forces a choice between paraconsistent and paracomplete readings, make the choice and defend it.

I want a committed response, not a survey of options. Take a definite position on whether the translation succeeds, which residue is fatal, and what the target vocabulary would have to gain to carry the original. No citations, no reading list, and no hedging.
