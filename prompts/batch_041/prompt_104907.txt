Heres a case I keep chewing on: the reclassification of birds. For most of the twentieth century, the claim that birds are not reptiles was treated as near-definitional — Aves was its own class, Reptilia was the scaly stuff left over. Then the cladists redrew the boundries so that Reptilia means the whole branch, and suddenly birds are reptiles, and if you keep going, dinosaurs. Same fossils, opposite verdict. Was that a change in what we knew or a change in what we meant? Use it to stress-test Quine's Two Dogmas argument — the idea that the analytic/synthetic distinction is a dogma, that nothing is immune to revision, and that definitions and empirical findings are tangled together in the web of belief. Which parts of the reclassification look like discovery and which look like stipulation, and can the line be drawn here at all? Then argue the other way: is there a reading on which the distinction survives, maybe as a matter of degree or as relative to a framework or a choice of definitions? And last: is deliberate conceptual engineering — changing a concept to serve some end — really a different animal from ordinary scientific revision, or just the same thing with better branding?
