I have been trying to understand the Judy Benjamin problem, and I think it exposes a fatal flaw in Bayesian epistemology. The setup, as I understand it, is that Judy is dropped into a territory divided into Blue and Red, with Blue further subdivided into Blue-1 and Blue-2. She begins with some prior over these three regions. She then receives a radio message to the effect that if she is in Blue, the probability that she is in Blue-2 is one-half. The problem, I take it, is that her prior together with this information does not determine a unique posterior. But is that not just a straightforward consequence of the fact that conditionalization only applies when one learns a proposition with certainty? Since the message is not a proposition she learns with certainty, conditionalization simply does not apply, and so there is no Bayesian answer. Does that not mean Bayesian epistemology is incomplete, because there are possible evidential situations it cannot model? And if Jeffrey conditionalization is supposed to be the generalization that handles uncertain evidence, but it also fails here, then what resources does Bayesianism have left? I am trying to understand why this is not treated as a decisive refutation of the entire Bayesian program.
