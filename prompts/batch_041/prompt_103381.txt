I am a distributed-systems engineer, and my working vocabulary is the one I use every day: processes that exchange messages over channels which can drop, delay, reorder, or duplicate them; acknowledgments and timeouts; failure detectors; the distinction between what a process has actually received and what it can only infer about what others have received; consensus protocols and the impossibility results that bound them; and coordination that is achieved eventually or with high probability rather than at a definite instant.

I would like you to restate the central claims of epistemic game theory -- the branch that models agents' rationality itself as something the agents can have knowledge about, and treats reasoning about other agents' reasoning as the substance of the problem -- entirely in that vocabulary. Take the following in turn and translate each one: the notion of common knowledge, as distinct from mutual knowledge held to some finite depth; the idea that a particular outcome is sustained by common knowledge of rationality among the players; the role of common knowledge in allowing agents to solve a coordination problem; the results about what rational agents who share a prior can and cannot come to agree on; and a scenario in which agents successfully coordinate even though the relevant facts are only almost common knowledge, the acknowledgment chain being long but finite. Do not name theories, authors, or papers; I will not recognize them, and they add nothing to my understanding. Give me the structure of each claim in my terms.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. For each claim, tell me (a) what translates cleanly, (b) what translates only with distortion, and (c) what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Be specific about the direction of any distortion -- which of my concepts you had to stretch to make the fit, and what was lost in the stretching.

Finally, take a definite position on the residue. My hunch is that common knowledge is simply a special case of the message-delivery problem I already understand, and that the philosophers' infinite hierarchy of "everyone knows that everyone knows..." is just a way of saying that the acknowledgment chain never terminates -- which I would call an unattainable condition rather than a deep one. Tell me whether the residue supports that hunch or refutes it: is the part of the theory my vocabulary cannot express a genuine defect of my vocabulary, or a sign that the original notion was less substantive than it looked? Commit to one answer and defend it, rather than laying out both sides and leaving the choice to me.
