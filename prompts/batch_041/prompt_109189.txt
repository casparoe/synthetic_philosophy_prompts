ok so for my metaphysics class we've been doing the special composition question — the "when do some things add up to one thing" one — and my prof keeps saying the answers look silly until you have to actually administer one, and then they look worse. Our syllabus has a creative component ("satire or equivalent", which is definately the most philosophy-department sentence ever written), so I want to write a deadpan municipal FAQ page from a city that has legislated an answer to it.

The setup: after a referendum, the council adopts the "any two things compose something" answer (universalism, I think my prof called it) for property tax and zoning. Now the Department of Objecthood and Assessment has to run it. I want it formatted like a real government FAQ or bylaw notice — numbered sections, cross-references, a form number, an "amnesty period", that kind of thing. 600-700 words.

The jokes have to come from the actual consequences, not from silly names. So: the moment my keys touch the table a new object exists, and so does the city-plus-the-moon, and the tax base explodes, so there's a deadpan bit about whether you owe property tax on the fusion of your house and your neighbour's fence, and whether you can be sued for damaging an object that existed for four seconds. Then the "nothing composes" faction (nihilists — only the simple parts are real, no tables, no citizens) files the appeal, and the FAQ has to answer them without conceding anything, something like "residents who hold that there are no tables should still complete the table section". And a section for the organicists, who say composition only happens when the parts make up a life — so anything alive needs a seperate permit, and there's an ugly edge case involving a pregnant resident that the FAQ should visibly refuse to resolve and punt to a subcommittee.

I also want it to graze the vagueness problem, because that's the part that actually bugs me: if composition happens when parts are "arranged right" or caught up in a life, and arrangement comes in degrees, then either there's a precise instant when the new object pops into being or the question hasn't really been answered — so the city had to legislate an arbitrary cutoff, the registry closing at noon on the day the bylaw passed, with a grandfather clause for "legacy objects" (i.e. everything, but they can't say that). And the problem of the many: someone complains that there are clearly 10^23 different objects in the same place as their sofa depending on which atoms you count, and the office's reply should be a masterclass in not answering.

The target is the fantasy that these answers come free of consequences once you have to administer them, plus the way every position about composition has some place where it gets embarrassing — not any particular philosopher. Deadpan all the way through, no winking, no narrator nudging me, and I should be able to hand it to someone who's never read any of this and have them still get what's being mocked. Can you write it?
