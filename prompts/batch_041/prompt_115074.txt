A few of us started a lantern procession in my town back in 1994 — first Saturday of October, we made it up more or less on the spot because the mill had just shut and the place needed something to do. Thirty years on its the biggest night of the year; kids who marched as toddlers now bring their own kids. We applied to the county heritage register and got knocked back. Their rule is that a tradition has to be at least a hundred years old, and the earliest documentation anyone can find is our own minutes from the first meeting. My neighbour says thats obviously right, a made-up thing isn't a tradition, it's a hobby with a march. My brother-in-law says every single entry in that register was made up by somebody once, so the hundred-year rule is just measuring how far back you managed to hide the paperwork. I don't know which of them I believe, but I've noticed I feel different about the procession now that I know its dated — like it's ours to cancel instead of something we're keeping. Is something real actually lost when a tradition turns out to be manufactured, or is my brother-in-law right and I'm just mourning an illusion? And tell me what would change your mind about which of us has it right.
