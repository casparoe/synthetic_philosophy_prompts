Okay so this is going to sound like a stoner conversation but I actually want a real answer. At a party last weekend my friends Dana and Marcus got into it about whether you could've been born from a different sperm and egg. Neither of them is a philosopher, but honestly they were both landing good points and I couldn't referee.

Marcus's line: obviously you couldn't. The exact sperm and the exact egg that made you aren't just the cause of you, their part of what you are. If a different sperm had won the race that day, a different person develops — someone who'd be more like a sibling of the actual you than like you. He said picture your parents deciding to wait a month and conceive then instead: nobody thinks that later kid is you, they call it your little brother. So where you came from has to be part of who you are.

Dana's line: that's just bio-mysticism. The sperm that got there was one in a million, a lottery, and the you-ness isn't sitting in the particular cell — it's in the pattern. If some other sperm had fused with that same egg and the resulting person ended up with your brain, your memories, your whole personality, that person is you, and it's only our weird fixation on the exact cells that makes us say otherwise.

Marcus: then a perfect clone with all your memories is you, and you don't believe that. He says Dana is smuggling in "if it's psychologically continuous then it's you," which proves way too much. Dana shot back that he's the one smuggling in a magic 'thisness' that nobody can point to.

Then it got more interesting. Dana brought up identical twins — one fertilized egg splits, two people, same origin and same genes. So origin plus genes isn't enough to pin down a person, and if it isn't enough then "your origin is essential to you" is doing less work than Marcus thinks. Marcus said that only shows origin isn't sufficient, not that it isn't necessary, and anyway before the split there was just one thing, so it's not even clear that either twin is 'the original.'

Dana's last move: take the egg and imagine it had split when it actually didn't. Now there's two people. One of them is you and the other isn't — but which one, and what fact could possibly decide it? She says if identity can be genuinely indeterminate like that, then "you couldn't have come from a different sperm" is claiming a precision we just don't have. Marcus said weird fission cases don't mean there's no fact of the matter in normal cases, and that if she wants psychology to define you then she's stuck with the clone.

They asked me to settle it and I said I'd think about it. Can you go through it move by move — who's right about what, where each of them is overreaching, and whether one of them is more right overall?
