I spent a decade building compilers and back-end systems before I moved into journalism, so I think natively in terms of recursion, fixed points, quines, reflection and self-modifying code, and I have almost no philosophy of mind. Hofstadter's proposal, as I understand it, is that the self is not a thing but a self-referential symbol, a strange loop that arises when a system's operations fold back onto the level that generated them. Please restate that account as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary — recursion, fixed points, quines, reflection, bootstrapping compilers, levels of description — and then give me the residue report. Which parts translate cleanly? Which survive only by distortion? Which have no counterpart at all? I am especially interested in two things. First, does Hofstadter's distinction between a genuine strange loop and mere circularity or feedback have an honest analogue in my terms — say, a program that inspects and rewrites its own code versus one that simply runs forever? Second, is the first-person, felt point of view — the thing that seems to matter most about having a self — something my vocabulary can name at all, or is that precisely the residue? I want to know whether my instinct that this is just a quine that describes itself is the correct reading, or whether I am quietly discarding the interesting part in the translation.
