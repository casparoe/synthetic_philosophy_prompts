I'm not a philosopher, I studied accounting and I fix up old motorcycles, but I've been thinking alot about this thing I read in some pop-science book about consciousness years ago, the "careenium". I can't remember who wrote it and I honestly don't care, so please don't tell me, I'm not trying to get the source right. The setup, as I remember it: there's a big arena, and in it are millions of tiny magnetic marbles called simms. They bounce around. When two of them bump at low speed they stick, and then more stick, and pretty soon you get these big blobs he calls simmballs. The simmballs drift around, grow and shrink, they merge, they seem to chase each other, and here's the part that got me: a simmball is a symbol, it stands for something, even though no single simm means anything on its own. Then he asks who shoves who: do the simms push the simmballs around, or do the simmballs push the simms around?

So I wrote up my own take on it, and I want you to tear it apart. Here it is. I claim the whole fight happens because "push" is doing three seperate jobs and nobody says which one they mean. First, contact push: simm hits simm, momentum transfers. Only simms ever do this. A simmball never touches anything as a simmball, it just is a lot of contacts. Second, difference-making push: if the simmball hadn't been there, things would have gone otherwise. But both levels pass this one for free, because yank out a simm and the simmball changes too, so it can't tell the two levels apart. Third, what I call bookkeeping push: the level where you can actually track what happens without going insane. Simmballs obviously win that, but that sounds like it's only about us and our tiny brains, and I don't think it is. My reason is what I've been calling the wobble test. Take the careenium and jiggle it: swap one simm for a simm that's 1% heavier, or nudge one of them a hair. Now ask what stayed fixed. If the simmball-level story (the red blob swallowed the blue blob) comes out the same across a whole range of jiggles, while the simm-level story is completely different every time, different marbles, different contacts, then the simmball is latching onto something real about the system and not just a lazy summary. I call that having grip. And when a simmball has grip I want to say it really does push the simms around, not by adding a secret extra force, but by being the thing that stays put while the simms underneath it get swapped out. Sort of like a whirlpool: every water molecule in it is gone a second later, but the whirlpool is what explains why the next molecule goes the same way round, and the molecule that already left can't explain that, it's gone.

What I want from you is comments on the draft. Is the three-way split of "push" actually doing work or am I just relabeling one thing three times? Is the wobble test a real test or is it rigged, since I'm the one picking which jiggles count and how hard to jiggle (jiggle hard and the simmball dies, jiggle soft and nothing ever dies, so where's the line and isn't the line just whatever level I already cared about before I started)? If you think both levels always pass every test, then say so and tell me whether grip is a fact about the careenium or just a fact about me. And the opposite worry: if grip is real, have I accidentally given simmballs a spooky extra causal power that everyone sensible says you shouldn't give them, and if so which of my steps let it in? I don't want a reading list and I won't read it, so no citations and no "see the literature on X", just tell me in plain english where the thing breaks and which paragraph is the weakest.
