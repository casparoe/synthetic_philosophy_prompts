I just got roped into drafting bylaws for a co-op I'm in (shared workshop, long story) and because we're all nerds we made the thing self-amending, Nomic-style. I'm a backend enginer, so my instinct is to model it as a mutable codebase: rules are functions, amendments are commits, the vote is CI, the chair is whoever has merge rights, the minutes are the log. I can already feel the weird parts and I want to know if my vocabulary can actually carry them.

Here's the concrete knot. Rule 101 says any rule can be amended by a two-thirds vote. Rule 102 says Rule 101 can only be amended by a unanimous vote. Rule 103 says if two rules conflict, the lower-numbered rule wins. Rule 104 says the person holding the gavel is the final interpreter of the rules. Rule 105 says no rule may be amended while the workshop is on fire. And Rule 106 says Rule 104 can only be amended by the gavel-holder. We also have a rule saying the rules are in force because Rule 101 says so, which is circular and we know it. Now: if Rule 103 makes lower numbers win, Rule 101 (lower) beats Rule 102 (higher), so Rule 102's unanimity requirement gets ignored whenever they conflict. But Rule 102 is supposed to entrench Rule 101. So is entrenchment just impossible here? Or did we write a bug?

I know Git, CI, dependency graphs, bootstrapping compilers, self-modifying code, runtime reflection, immutable logs vs mutable state, cyclic imports, and quines, but I'm not a PL theorist. Restate the standard self-amendment paradoxes -- entrenchment, self-reference, validity, the rule/meta-rule distinction, the interpreter regress, whether a rule can bind its own amendment -- in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can. Then give me the residue report: what translates cleanly, what you had to fudge, and what has no counterpart at all in software. In particular, tell me whether legal validity is just whatever the current HEAD says or whether that loses something, and whether a rule that protects itself is really just a quine with a vote or is doing something different. Don't just lay out the options and leave it to me; take a definite position, make the translation you think is right, and say which residue is a defect of my vocabulary versus a sign the legal notion was doing real work.
