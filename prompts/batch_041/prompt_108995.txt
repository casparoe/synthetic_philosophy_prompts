I keep going back and forth on when I should just take the objective chance as my own credence, and my gut verdicts don't add up to anything clean. Fair coin, nothing else known: 50/50 for me. Coin from a factory that randomizes the bias so there's a 50% chance the bias is .9 heads and a 50% chance it's .1: still 50/50, even though I know the chance is exactly .5. Same coin, but I peeked at the factory record and know the bias is .9 heads: 90/10. A flip that already happened and I watched come up tails, even though the physics of it gave a 50% chance beforehand: 0 now, obviously. A reliable predictor tells me the next fair flip will be heads: I go 90-something percent heads even though I know the chance is .5. My half-formed rule is "match the chance, unless your extra evidence is correlated with the outcome instead of with the chance" — does that hold up, and is there something better?

And what does it say about these two, which I probaly shouldnt have built: a coin machine that scans my credence at the moment of the flip and makes it come up heads iff my credence in heads is below .5, and a near-identical one that makes it come up heads iff my credence is above .5. I can't just decide to believe something, so "pick the credence that makes the machine do what you want" isn't available to me. In the first machine no credence seems to sit still; in the second, some look self-supporting. Which of my verdicts do I have to give up, and what should I actually believe in the scanner cases?
