Take the Clever Hans affair -- von Osten's horse, the one who seemed to add and tell the time in Berlin around 1900, and Pfungst's 1907 finding that Hans only answered correctly when the person asking knew the answer and Hans could see them, because the horse was reading the questioner's posture and the little release of tension at the final tap -- and run it through Dennett's four grades of creature: Darwinian (wired by selection), Skinnerian (shaped by reinforcement), Popperian (can run the options inside its head before acting, so a failed hypothesis costs it nothing), Gregorian (Popperian plus culturally transmitted thinking tools -- words, numerals, notation). I dont want the taxonomy recited back at me, I want the mapping. Which grade is Hans actually at, and which grade were the people putting the questions to him -- given that the signal was one creature's mental life leaking out through his shoulders and eyebrows without him knowing it? And does the scheme have any room for a case whose whole mechanism is a loop between two creatures rather than a mechanism sitting inside one of them -- if not, is that a real gap in the taxonomy or am I just misapplying it? Then run it the other way. Pfungst's fix -- blind questioning, varying whether the questioner knows the answer, and the standing methodological warning that ends up named after the horse -- is that Gregorian thinking in the intended sense, or something the four grades weren't built to capture? Same question in miniature about the ape sign-language work from the 60s through the 80s, where the cueing worry came back and never quite left. Where the scheme goes silent, say so instead of forcing it to talk. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble and no restating of the question.
