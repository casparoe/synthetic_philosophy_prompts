I've spent a lot of time in alignment-adjacent reading groups and comment threads, and I've come to think that a large fraction of the disagreement under the heading "the alignment problem" is really a disagreement about which problem is even being named. One reading is narrow and intent-shaped: build a system that reliably does what its operator actually means, where the canonical failure is the system optimizing a proxy that satisfies the letter of the instruction and defeats its point, and the natural fix is better intent elicitation, interpretability, or corrigibility. The other reading is broader and value-shaped: bring about outcomes that are actually good, where the failure can survive perfect fidelity, because the operator's stated intent was thin, self-undermining, or simply not what the operator would endorse after reflection — and where the fix is not to ask better but to get the values right, which may mean overriding the stated intent. These come apart in both directions, and I've watched smart people talk past each other for an hour because they never felt the gap in their hands. I'd like to fix that with a runnable session rather than a lecture, and I want your help designing it.

Context: I run a mixed reading group of about eighteen adults — a couple of ML grad students, a few philosophers, several effective-altruism and rationalist-adjacent people, and a couple of complete newcomers — and I have them for one two-hour session, in a room with movable chairs and a whiteboard, no computers required. Please take the role of an expert in both AI alignment and experiential learning design, and produce a complete, runnable activity that makes participants feel the intent/value distinction from the inside, so that the divergence shows up in what happens to them rather than in what I tell them afterward. Start by brainstorming two or three candidate mechanics and briefly say why you reject the ones that merely illustrate the distinction instead of forcing it; then commit to one and give me the full design: the setup and any materials, the roles or rules, the incentives or stakes (no real money — points, tokens, reputation in the room, or something equally cheap will do), a minute-by-minute timeline for the two hours, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the two readings of the alignment problem and names the point where they come apart. I especially want the design to produce both directions of failure: a case where a participant gets exactly what they asked for and discovers it isn't what they wanted, and a case where a system does the right thing by values but visibly overrides what someone actually said, so that the room can feel the paternalism objection from the inside rather than reading about it.

Finally, be honest with me in two ways. First, tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this — where the analogy will break, which participants will try to lawyer the rules, what the group will misread as the point — and how I should handle each. Second, tell me which feature of the intent/value distinction the activity cannot reproduce and that I will have to supply myself in the debrief, and give me a short, careful way of saying that part out loud. Please think through the mechanic carefully before you write the timeline; I'd rather have one design that actually bites than three that gesture at the idea.
