My brother-in-law has spent the last year going deep on one of those conspiracy things — I'll leave out which one — and a few weeks ago I sat with him for three hours trying to talk him out of it. I couldn't. Not because he had good evidence, but because everything I threw at him already had a place to land. He has an answer for why the experts say otherwise, an answer for why I believe what I believe, an answer for why the evidence looks the way it does. It's a closed loop, and it's a really well-built one.

My two best friends have been useless about this. One is a coherentist: she says yeah, that's what justification is, a belief is justified by fitting with the rest of what you believe, and you don't get to demand some magic foundation underneath it, so stop looking for one. The other is a foundationalist: he says no, coherence alone is worth nothing, some beliefs have to be justified without being justified by other beliefs, or the whole tower is standing on air — and that's the exact difference between my brother-in-law and me. They've been going back and forth for two months and at this point I don't think they're even having the same arguement anymore.

I want the thing they should have written. A joint statement the two of them could both put their names on. What do they both actually accept — about the regress itself, about what justification is even for, and about my brother-in-law's system in particular? I have a feeling they'd both say it's unjustified, for totally different reasons, and I want to see if that's true. Where do they really split, each stated in words that side would sign? What are the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would move one of them? My guess is one is whether a chain of reasons has to be linear and non-circular at all, and the other is whether a belief that's justified without being justified by another belief is even a coherent idea — but tell me if I've got the wrong cruxes. For each one, give me a case or an arguement they'd both agree actually bears on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts about that case. If part of their fight turns out to be about words rather than about anything, say which part. And there's a third friend, an infinitist, who keeps saying both of them are just scared of an infinite chain — I want to know whether she's actually a third position or just a way of changing the shape of the other two.

Number the agreed claims, then put the disagreements in a table. Just give me the document — start with it, no preamble, no "here's the statement."
