d) Two programmers each write a one-shot prisoner's dilemma strategy, each knowing the other will run whatever program they themselves would have written, and each knowing they'd write the same program. I write the cooperate one.

e) The counterfactual mugging. It flips a coin, it comes up tails, so it isn't asking me for anything; but it says that if the coin had come up heads it would have asked me for $100, and it'll hand me $10,000 right now iff I'd have paid in the heads branch. I say no, I wouldn't have paid. Feels like extortion.

c and e feel like the same case to me and I answer them oppositely, so something in there is doing work I can't see. What principle actually fits? My gut says donate anyway and I dont trust that gut, since it's the same shape as (a) and I go the other way there.

Give it back as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro and no wrap-up: (1) your best single principle, (2) the best runner-up, (3) what each says about the c-versus-e clash, (4) what your preferred one says about the troll, (5) which of my five gut calls I have to give up.
