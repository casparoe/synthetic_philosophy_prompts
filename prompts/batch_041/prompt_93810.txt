So I'm a backend engineer at a mid-size fintech, and there's this thing our security lead does that has turned into an ongoing argument on the team. At standup on Monday she announces that there's going to be a surprise audit some day that week, and that it'll be a real surprise: on the evening before, we won't be able to tell that it's coming the next day. She's never once lied about this, the audit always does happen, and its always a surprise. Last quarter it landed on Wednesday and caught half the team with prod credentials pasted into a shared doc, which was a whole thing. Anyway, a friend of mine from a previous job who minored in philosophy and never lets anyone forget it heard about this and produced the following, and now it's living in my head rent free. (1) Suppose the audit hasn't happened by Thursday evening. Then Friday is the only day left, so we'd know it was coming Friday. (2) An audit we know about the night before isn't a surprise, so a Friday audit would break her promise. (3) Her promise is good: she's never lied, the audit does come, and it is a surprise. (4) So the audit can't be Friday. (5) Now suppose it hasn't happened by Wednesday evening. Given (4), the only day left is Thursday, so we'd know it was coming Thursday, which isn't a surprise, so it can't be Thursday either. (6) The same move knocks out Wednesday, then Tuesday, then Monday. (7) So the audit can't happen at all. (8) But it does happen, so her promise was false, which contradicts (3). (9) And nothing in any of this turned on it being an audit, or on the window being a week: for any announced event with a known deadline plus a clause that we won't see it coming, the same reasoning says the announcement can't be true. (10) So no announcement of a surprise can ever be true. (11) Except last quarter's, which came on Wednesday and definately surprised everyone.

Which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? I'm fairly sure something is wrong here, because surprises obviously do happen and our audits obviously do happen and do surprise us, and if it's a straight choice between (11) and the chain, I'd rather keep (11). But I can't tell if the problem is at (1), where we go from "we'd know it's Friday" to "so it can't be Friday," or at (3), or somewhere in how the whole thing loops back on itself. And I want to know what I'm actually buying with each fix. If I drop (1) or (5), am I saying we wouldn't really know it's Friday once Thursday night passes with no audit, and if so, why not, what would we be doubting at that point? If I drop (3), am I saying the announcement is the kind of claim nobody can know in advance even when it's true, which sounds like a different and weirder thing than a normal prediction? Is there a version of this where the announcement comes out true and knowable, or do I have to pick one? Lay out the honest options, tell me which one you'd take and why, and be specific about what the others are paying that you aren't.
