Ok so I'm a third year phil of science student and this term I'm doing a placement instead of a dissertation — I'm at our national statistics office, in the team that makes the charts for the monthly dashboard. My supervisor found out I did a module on the Vienna Circle and now I've been asked to write the internal note on when we're allowed to turn a statistic into a picture. Its due Friday and I don't want it to be useless.

The way Neurath's picture-language thing came across in the seminar: the point of a statistical picture is that you shouldn't need training, a legend, or any particular language to read it. You draw the thing being counted as a small identical symbol repeated as many times as there are things, each symbol standing for the same fixed amount, so the reader can count the symbols and check the number themselves instead of trusting the chart. Making one big symbol bigger than another is a con, because who knows if you're meant to compare height or area, and perspective is out for the same reason. I'm fairly sure he was also against symbols that carry a mood or a metaphor — a skull for deaths, a fat body for obesity, an arrow for pressure — because then the picture is doing rhetoric rather than counting. And I remember he never thought this could replace words; it was meant to help language, not be one. If I've got any of that wrong, say so up front, because I'll be embarrassed if the whole note rests on a misreading.

What I actually need is something my collegues can run. Not 'be honest with your charts' — a procedure. Something like: the questions to ask about a chart, in order; what counts as a good enough answer at each step; where the procedure stops and says no; and where it has to hand the decision to a person because the question is about values and not about pictures. Ten minutes, by someone who has never read a word of Neurath and doesn't care.

Run it on three charts we really put out, and don't rig the results:

1. Heatwave deaths this summer compared with the previous ten summers, by week. Countable, I think — one symbol per death, or per ten deaths.
2. Average waiting time in A&E. That's a duration, not a count of things, so what exactly gets repeated? If the answer is that nothing does, tell me what to do instead, because we have to publish it.
3. The gender pay gap, which is a difference between two averages expressed as a percentage of one of them. I have a strong feeling this one can't be done in his terms at all, and that anything we could draw for it is already an argument. If the procedure says don't publish, fine, but our head of comms has already promised the minister a graphic for that one, so tell me how to write the refusal in a way that doesn't sound like the intern being precious.

Two more things. Where does this procedure just encode Neurath's taste rather than a real requirement of honesty? For instance, if the 'no metaphors' rule is a truth-preservation rule, explain why; if it's just that he didn't like being moved, say that, because then I can drop it without losing anything. And be blunt about what the procedure can't do — I'd rather the note have a section saying 'this step is a judgement call and here is who has to make it' than pretend it's mechanical.
