My friend Dana has been arguing on a forum about "which futures are better," and she's given a bunch of verdicts I can't get to fit together, and I want to know what she'll say about a new case. Here's her track record, all about hypothetical futures with different numbers of people at different levels of wellbeing (she uses a 0-100 scale where 0 is a life not worth living at all, 50 is a life that's clearly good, and 100 is about as good as a life gets):

1. She says a future with 1,000 people all at 80 is worse than a future with those same 1,000 people at 80 plus another 1,000 people at 70. Adding happy people, she says, is just a good thing.
2. She says a future with 1,000 people all at 90 is better than a future with a billion people all at 2. She's very firm about this — she thinks the billion-person future is a nightmare even though every single one of those billion lives is, strictly speaking, worth living.
3. She says a future with 1,000 people at 80 plus 1,000 people at 10 is worse than the future with just the 1,000 people at 80. So she doesn't think adding people is always good — only when they're doing well enough.
4. She says a future with 1,000 people at 90 is worse than a future with 1,000 people at 90 plus a million people at 45. So she's fine with a big crowd as long as they're all solidly in the "life is good" zone, even if they drag the average down a lot.

Now here's the new case. Two futures:

Future P: 1,000 people at 90.
Future Q: 1,000 people at 90, plus 500,000 people at 20.

And a second new case, to be safe:

Future R: 10,000 people at 60.
Future S: 10,000 people at 60, plus 10 million people at 25.

I want you to act like a really careful philosopher of population ethics — someone who knows the whole literature on the repugnant conclusion inside and out — and think through this step by step, maybe even start by brainstorming several different rules that could explain Dana's four verdicts before you commit to any of them. Then: what do you predict she'll say about P vs Q and about R vs S? Which rule is she most likely running, and what are the rival rules that fit her first four verdicts just as well? For each rival, tell me what it predicts about P vs Q and R vs S, and then tell me which further case would best drive a wedge between the surviving rules. I care less about which answer is "correct" and more about you nailing down the rule she's implicitly using and being honest about where her track record genuinely underdetermines things.
