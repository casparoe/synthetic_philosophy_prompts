I am writing a commentary on sphexishness — the disposition of a system to be run by routines it cannot break out of — and before I commit to a line I want my own position audited rather than summarized. Here is what I hold.

1. Sphexishness is a property of the mechanism, not of the performance. Whether a stretch of behavior is sphexish depends on the character of the routine that produces it, not on how varied or stereotyped it looks from outside: a rigid-looking performance can issue from a flexible architecture, and a fluid-looking one from a fixed routine.

2. A system is sphexish to the degree that its behavior is generated by routines it cannot suspend or revise when the situation calls for it. The familiar story of the digger wasp, who drags the cricket back to the threshold and begins the inspection over again whenever it is displaced, is the paradigm case — and the number of repetitions is diagnostically irrelevant.

3. The difference between that wasp and a human deliberator is one of degree, not of kind: more nested loops, more layers of representation, more routes from input to output. I would say the same of a foraging robot whose stuck-routine cycles through a fixed list of escape maneuvers.

4. The freedom that moral responsibility requires is reasons-responsiveness: the capacity to register reasons and to adjust behavior as they change.

5. Responsibility has a threshold. Below some level of responsiveness the reactive attitudes are unwarranted, above it they are warranted; the threshold is vague, but it is real.

6. Sphexishness and unfreedom are one phenomenon described at two levels: to be sphexish is just to lack the control that responsibility requires.

What I need is an audit, not a survey of the literature — I don't need references, I need the argument. Which of these six actually pull against each other, and how badly? Order the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere. Distinguish those from the pairs that only look like conflicts and in fact come apart on inspection, and make clear what licenses the distinction. If one of the six is quietly carrying more weight than the others can bear, say which, and tell me which single revision would leave the fewest of the remaining commitments damaged.
