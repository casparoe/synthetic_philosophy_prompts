Construct a thought experiment in population axiology that isolates a single variable: whether the value a theory assigns to adding lives depends on those lives falling below a threshold the theory treats as a net loss, rather than on the total welfare those lives carry. The case is meant to show that one well-known escape from the repugnant conclusion is parasitic on the presence of lives that are barely worth living, and disappears once those lives are moved comfortably above the escape route's threshold.

Use the following three definitions throughout, and derive every verdict from them rather than stipulating it:

Totalism: the value of a population is the sum of its members' welfare.
Averagism: the value of a population is the average welfare of its members.
Critical-level totalism: fix a positive critical level c; the value of a population is the sum over members of (welfare minus c), so that each life below c contributes a net loss and each life above c a net gain.

Place all welfare on a scale on which zero is the neutral level (a life neither worth nor not worth living) and positive numbers are lives worth living.

The scenario must compare exactly three populations, each described by its size and a single uniform welfare level: P1, few people at high welfare; P2, a much larger population at a moderate welfare level; and P3, an intermediate population at an intermediate welfare level, so that the case is not a bare two-way comparison.

Impose these requirements:

1. Totalism and critical-level totalism must both rank P2 above P1, while averagism must rank P1 above P2.
2. No member of any population may fall below c; the escape from the repugnant conclusion must not be available here.
3. The welfare level of P2 must be high enough that no one could reasonably describe its members' lives as barely worth living, and the repugnance must survive anyway.
4. Give explicit population sizes and explicit welfare levels, and keep every welfare level positive.

After presenting the scenario, do the following. State the verdict each of the three views returns for every pairwise comparison, and show the arithmetic that produces it. Identify precisely which feature of the case does the work, and then lower the welfare level of P2 until it sits just below c: show that this single change reinstates the escape and say what that reveals. Say whether the case establishes that the barely-worth-living qualifier attached to the repugnant conclusion is essential or inessential to it, and defend that verdict. Finally, describe how a committed critical-level totalist would resist the case, for instance by disputing where c should be fixed or by accepting the conclusion, and assess whether the resistance succeeds.

The purpose of the case is to show that critical-level totalism blocks the repugnant conclusion only when sub-critical lives are available to penalise, and not through anything intrinsic to the trade between numbers and quality. A case that stipulates its verdicts instead of deriving them from the three definitions does not meet the specification.
