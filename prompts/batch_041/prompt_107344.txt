I'm a novelist and I'm writing a chapter where a character -- a lapsed theologian of the Ash'arite sort -- stops believing that anything causes anything. Fire doesn't burn the cotton, God just makes the burning happen on the same occasion every time, and what we call necessity is only a habit He's free to break; thats the whole reason she thinks miracles are possible at all. I want a poem in her voice to close the chapter, 16 to 20 lines, no fixed form, but one hard constraint: no causal connectives. No because, so, therefore, makes, leads to, since, causes. Only and then, when, after -- bare succession. It has to still read as a poem and still move, while never once claiming that one line brought the next one into being, and the reader should feel the missing glue as a kind of vertigo rather than spotting a gimmick. The turn should be her admitting she can't stop expecting the second thing after the first, and the last line should leave it open whether that expectation proves anything or is just a groove worn into her. Someone who knows the occasionalist move about habit and the miracle it's meant to save shouldn't wince, but someone who's never heard of any of it should still get a good poem.
