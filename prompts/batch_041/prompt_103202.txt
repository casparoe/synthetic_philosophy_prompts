ok so this has been nagging me for a while and I can't tell if it's a real thing with a name or just me being muddled.

You know the choice where it's one person being tortured for fifty years vs. an astronomically large number of people each getting a single speck of dust in the eye. I'm not asking which side is right, I've seen that fight a hundred times and it never goes anywhere. What I keep noticing is a smaller pattern underneath it, and it's the smaller pattern I want a name for.

Take some fixed total of mild annoyance, say a million specks across a million eyes, one speck each. Most people I argue with will say fine, that total counts, and weighed against one person being tortured it might even come out worse. Now keep the total identical and the intensity of each speck identical, but put all million specks into one persons eyes, spread over his life. Same sum, same triviality per speck, only the ownership changed. Those same people now say of course not, you can't weigh one man's agony against a pile of trivialities. And I get the mirror image from other people: they'll add up annoyance inside a single life without blinking (it's all his, so it adds) but refuse to add it across lives (each person's bit is nothing, so there's nothing to add).

There's a second flip that looks like the same flip to me. A guaranteed speck in each of a billion eyes gets treated as obviously fine, a speck is nothing, nobody would notice. A one-in-a-billion chance of a speck for each of a billion people, same expected number of specks, gets treated as obviously unacceptable, because now there's a real chance somebody gets a speck they never agreed to. The arithmetic is the same and the verdicts are opposite.

So: does this have a name? Not the torture-and-specks puzzle itself, I assume that's well covered and I'm not after a reading list, I can't look anything up right now. I mean the pattern where the verdict on a huge pile of individually negligible harms tracks who owns the bits (one life or many lives) and whether the bits are certain or merely probable, with the totals held fixed. If it's one recognised thing, what is it and whats it called. If it's actually three different recognised things that I've mashed together, tell me which three, where the seams are, and which one is doing the real work. If there's no name, coin one and tell me exactly what it covers and what it doesn't, I'd rather have the boundary than the label. And tell me the nearest thing that isn't it, because I suspect I'm half-remembering something and I want to know what I'm half-remembering.
