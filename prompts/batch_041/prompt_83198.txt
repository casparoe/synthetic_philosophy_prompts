Ok so I need a satirical piece for a collection I'm putting together, and I want one that a pragmatics person actually laughs at rather than winces at.

Write a deadpan employee handbook — call it the Global Candor Standard, maybe 600-700 words, numbered sections like a real policy doc — from a fictional multinational that has decided all its offices worldwide will communicate in the Dutch style: say the thing, no hedging, no softening, no "maybe we could consider". The humour has to come from getting the content right. So: the handbook treats conversational implicature as a loophole ("staff do not lie; they merely under-implicate"), includes a White Lie Exception Form whose fields quietly show how much admin politeness would cost if every softener had to be justified, and has a section on the Tokyo office that files "reading the air" as non-compliance when it's really a different way of being considerate — the policy just has no vocabulary for it, which is the joke. Include one grouchy note from a Dutch employee who's annoyed his directness keeps getting read as rudeness, and one leaked memo from a Tokyo manager, both internally consistent with their own positions. The target isnt Japan vs the Netherlands — its the assumption that bluntness is the absence of norms rather than a norm of its own, and that a white lie is just a small lie. By the end I want a reader to have felt the real question, which is whether white lies are wrong at all, and if they are, whether the wrong is in the falsehood or in the condescension. No quotes from real people, dont invent citations, and keep it readable for someone who's never heard the word implicature.
