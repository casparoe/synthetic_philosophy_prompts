Longtime reader of the rationalist blogs, first time posting something like this. There's a move I keep running into, mostly from the consciousness crowd, and I don't have a word for it. Someone leans hard on a piece of vocabulary they clearly need — 'experience', 'what it's like', 'qualia' — but the moment you ask whether the thing is actually real they do a shuffle: 'I'm not making a metaphysical claim, it's just a useful way of talking / we need some word for it / the reports have to be about something.' Ok. Except then the entire explanation runs on teh word — the zombie differs by lacking the experience, the blindsight patient is missing the experience, the anaesthetised brain isn't having any — and if you strip it out there's no story left. Say 'so you're a realist about experience after all' and they look at you like you've misread them. I've seen the same shape with free will ('not saying it exists, I just hold people responsible'), with numbers, with God.

Does this have a name? My instinct is fictionalism, but a fictionalist usually cops to the pretense or admits the thing is false, whereas this person seems to want the explanatory payoff of realism and the immunity of neutrality at the same time, which can't both be honest. If the real answer is 'that's just fictionalism/instrumentalism, you're overthinking it', then give me the carving — how it's different from those, from the old 'as if' philosophy, and from Dennett's intentional-stance line, where he at least gestures at what makes a stance track a real pattern. And if nothing fits, coin a term and tell me where its edges are, because 'they're a realist in denial' doesn't feel quite right either.
