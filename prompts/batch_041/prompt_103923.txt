write a dialogue between two people who disagree about whether factored cognition is the right idealization of deliberation. one of them is defending the HCH-style picture: for any question a human can answer, there's a tree of strictly easier questions, each handed to a fresh human who sees only that question and the answers to its children, and the answer at the root comes out the same as what the unaided human wouldve said. the other thinks that picture has already assumed the thing it's supposed to explain, and keeps dragging them back to one worked example — deciding whether to tell a friend that the novel theyve been working on for four years doesnt work. let the defender give real replies, especially when the other presses that chopping the question up one way rather than another can flip the answer at the root, so the ideal only tells you what to think once youve already made the hard call about how to carve the question up, and that the human sitting at each node is being asked to do exactly the thing the tree was supposed to account for. neither of them is dumb, no strawman, they should actually answer each others points, and dont let it end with a handshake.
