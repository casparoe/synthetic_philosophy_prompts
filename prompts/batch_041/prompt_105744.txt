I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I keep circling a puzzle about forgeries and the value of originals. Let me put a specific case in front of you, state a position, and then ask you to attack it.

The case: a painting has hung in a museum for a century and is universally received as the crowning work of a celebrated artist. Scholars have built an interpretive tradition around it, viewers report transformative experiences in front of it, and it shapes how the artist's entire body of work is understood. It is then established beyond any doubt that the canvas is a copy — made centuries ago, directly from the artist's original, which has since been destroyed. Crucially, it is not a copy of a copy: in every perceptible respect it is a perfect stand-in for the lost original, and it is in fact the most faithful record of that original that still exists.

My position: the artistic value of a painting supervenes entirely on its perceptible properties, together with whatever structural and expressive features those properties carry. On this view, once we learn the history above, nothing of aesthetic value has been lost, and the canvas is worth exactly what the lost original was worth. The intuition that something has been "devalued" or "lost" when a forgery is exposed is an artifact of our interest in causal history rather than a genuine aesthetic fact.

Tell me what counts against this. Be specific about which intuitions, which possible cases, and which results or distinctions from neighboring areas — the ontology of artworks, the role of the artist's intentions, the epistemology of testimony about art, the economics and ritual of the museum — the position stands or falls with. Separate the defeaters that a committed defender of the position would themselves concede from the ones they would dismiss as question-begging, and rank the whole set from fatal to merely awkward. Say what the position predicts that its rivals do not, and give an honest verdict on whether it can be defended without being quietly qualified into something unfalsifiable — and if it cannot, whether that is a real problem or merely the price of the view. Lead with the single most decisive defeater. Please answer in American English and keep it to at most 700 words.
