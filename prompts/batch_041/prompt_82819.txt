Me and a friend have been going round in circles about something I half remember from a podcast. Two spheres, identical down to the last atom, floating alone in an empty universe some way apart. He says obviously there are two of them, and obviously neither one has anything the other doesnt have, so two things can differ without there being any difference between them. I can't work out if that's trivial or if it's cheating. I think the principle has a name — the identity of indiscernibles — but I don't want an essay about it.

What I want is a flowchart, like a sorting quiz, that takes you from plain questions to one of the actual positions. Each question should be answerable by someone who's never read philosophy and doesn't know where it's heading, and each end point should be a position with either a name or a one line description of it. The questions I care about are: does there being two of them need anything to make it so, or is it just a fact with nothing behind it? If something makes it so, is it something about each sphere on its own, or about how the two sit relative to each other and the rest of the world? If it's about the sphere itself, is it something you could in principle detect — in which case they were never really indiscernible — or something no amount of looking could turn up? Does it matter whether the spheres are simple or have parts? Does dropping a third, obviously different object into the universe suddenly make the first two tellable apart, and if so were they distinguishable all along? And what about the version where the two swap places and nothing else in the world changes — is there a fact about whether they swapped, and does your chart give the same answer there?

If some combination of answers doesn't land on anything anyone seems to hold, say so and say whether that's because the view is incoherent or just unexplored. If two end points are really the same view in different clothes, tell me rather than pretending they're separate. Keep it to maybe ten questions. No reading list please, I wont read it.
