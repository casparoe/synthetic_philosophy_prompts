I've been reading about al-Ghazali and this view people call occasionalism that gets pinned on him — the idea that we never actually see one thing *making* another thing happen, we only ever see one thing regularly following another, and it's God who does the real causing on each occasion, with the fire or the match or whatever just being the "occasion". My mate thinks this is obviously daft, because you can just strike a match and watch it catch, and if it works a hundred times out of a hundred then surely the striking is what's causing the flame, end of story. But I'm not so sure — it feels like the occasionalist and the normal person might predict exactly the same stuff happening in the kitchen, and if they do, I genuinely can't see what the disagreement is even about. The other view I keep bumping into is the one where the created thing really does have a power of its own to bring the effect about, and God isn't doing it instead of the thing but keeping the thing going and working alongside it — I don't know the proper name for it but you get what I mean.

So what I'd like is a thought experiment built to order that actually makes these two come apart, with the split falling out of the theories themselves rather than just being asserted. What I want from it: (1) an ordinary situation — a kitchen, a garden, a workshop, that sort of thing — no miracles, no appeals to scripture or to what God might be thinking, no fancy physics; (2) it should turn on one specific claim like "the striking of the match caused the flame" or "the frost killed the seedlings", where the occasionalist and the "the thing really has a power to do it" person give *opposite* verdicts about whether that thing caused that outcome; (3) crucially, both sides have to agree on everything that actually happens and on all the "if you'd done X instead, Y wouldn't have happened" facts, because the whole point is that the disagreement isn't an empirical one — if my case gets them disagreeing about the facts then I've cheated; and (4) since I keep wanting to shout "but I can just *intervene* and make it happen, so I've seen the power", please make the case one where intervening is perfectly possible, and have the occasionalist still give a decent account of why that doesn't settle it.

Then, after the scenario itself (keep that bit to a paragraph), walk me through it: state the claim, say what each side says and why, tell me which single feature of the case is doing all the work, what happens if you take that feature out, and finish with the two rebuttals — how the occasionalist answers someone who says "I literally just watched the match light the flame", and how the other side would hit back if they think the case has been rigged against them. If there's a third view lurking in the background — the "it's all just constant conjunction, no powers anywhere and no God needed" one — mention in a line or two what it would say, but don't let it take over.
