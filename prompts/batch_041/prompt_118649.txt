"Zorn's lemma says that if you have a partially ordered set in which every chain — every totally ordered subset — has an upper bound somewhere in the set, then the set contains a maximal element, i.e. a biggest element that dominates everything else. It's equivalent to the axiom of choice, so it isn't really something you prove, it's something you assume; it's the workhorse behind every vector space having a basis and every field having an algebraic closure. The proof is a transfinite recursion: you build a chain up one element at a time and, when it can't be extended any further, the lemma hands you the maximal element you were climbing toward. One caveat: the chain condition has to be checked for finite chains as well, since a finite chain needn't have an upper bound."

I'm inclined to think the gist is fine and that I'm just being paranoid, but two things nag at me. Second, that last sentence about finite chains feels backwards, though I can't put my finger on why. Also, a friend told me it's basically the same statement as the Hausdorff maximal principle, which the book never mentions — is that the kind of "same" that matters?
