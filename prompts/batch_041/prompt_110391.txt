I'm assembling a handout for a graduate reading group on the moral status of beings whose inner lives we cannot confirm — insects, plants, a thermostat that holds a setpoint against disturbance. Write me a decision tree that sorts the positions by what they actually claim rather than by which creatures they happen to name. Treat the root as the question of what kind of gradation is in play, since this debate routinely runs together at least four: a mental state that comes in degrees, evidence that comes in degrees while the state is all-or-nothing, a moral status that comes in degrees, and a value function that is graded even where status is not. Every subsequent node should be a question a reader can answer without knowing which position it leads to. I want the tree to route through at least these splits: whether mere goal-directed regulation of a setpoint counts as the kind of state that can matter; whether status tracks the capacity for welfare or its exercise; whether status scales with the intensity of a state or with the complexity of the self that has it; and whether uncertainty about a creature's inner life calls for expected-value reasoning or a precautionary rule.

The tree should end in eight to ten leaves, each named and each carrying one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Include: a vertebrate-threshold view with binary status; graded sentience with status graded by intensity; graded sentience with status graded by cognitive complexity; functionalist inclusivism, on which a suitably organized thermostat is in; biological naturalism; a precautionary leaf; a deflationary leaf on which status is assigned rather than discovered; and a relational leaf on which standing depends on membership in a community rather than on inner life. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and mark which combinations of answers nobody occupies, with a one-line diagnosis of whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. I care most about the thermostat, since it is where functionalism and biological naturalism come apart cleanly, so the tree must not route around it. No bibliography is needed. Begin with the tree itself, without preface, and put the notes on unoccupied and inseparable leaves after it. Write in American English.
