For the last three years I have moderated a large online community, and I have kept a private log of how a single joke format mutated across hundreds of variants. That experience has led me to a position I now hold with some conviction: cultural change is a Darwinian process in the strict sense, and the trajectory of an item such as a joke format is best explained by variation, selection, and inheritance — with the important qualification that the inheritance system includes not only the item’s content but also the transmission infrastructure surrounding it (the platform’s ranking algorithm, the shared template, the convention about crediting an origin). I am not asking for a general defence of the meme analogy. I want to know what would count against this specific, infrastructure-inclusive Darwinian view. What observations, arguments, or formal results should move me? Which would be decisive, and which merely awkward? Please distinguish, in particular, (i) evidence that cultural items are better explained by content biases or attractors than by selection, (ii) cases in which inheritance is so blending or so guided that the Darwinian apparatus adds nothing, and (iii) the possibility that transmission infrastructure belongs to the phenotype rather than to the inheritance system. Tell me what a committed defender of the view would themselves concede as counting against it, and whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if it has, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind. Please answer in British English.
