Retired three years now, after 35 in a biology department. Since then I've been reading philosophy on my own, mostly a pleasure, exept for one thing that keeps stopping me up.

In my old world everybody talked as if rewards track merit. The grant, the chair, the prize went to the person who deserved it, full stop. Fine. But the more I read -- Rawls, and then whatever the desert people are on about -- the less I can find a version of that sentence that survives contact with what I actually know about how people end up the way they are. So before I pick a side I want the spec first. Lay out what a theory of desert -- who deserves what, and on what basis -- has to be able to do before it's worth taking seriously.

I want the requirement list, and for each item the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference. Things I'd want it to handle, just to get you started:

- the grad student who worked harder than anyone in the building for six years and got nothing out of it. Owed anything or not?
- the guy whose big result landed in his lap because a rival's lab burned down the month before. Same paper, different luck.
- the fact that the qualities we reward -- persistence, whatever you want to call it -- are partly handed to you at birth and shaped by a childhood nobody picked.
- the technician who ran the one experiment that mattered and whose name never made the paper. Did she deserve the discovery? What exactly did she deserve?
- a man who'd flatly refuse the award everyone agrees he deserves. Can you deserve something you don't want?

And the distinctions I dont think a theory is allowed to just collapse: deserving something vs. being entitled to it; what you deserve for what you did vs. what you deserve for who you are; and whether desert can even get off the ground before there are institutions handing out the positions people are said to deserve, or whether it's prior to all that.

Then the part I care most about. Tell me which of these requirements can't all hold at once. My guess is the control requirement -- you only deserve what was up to you -- collides head-on with what I know about where effort and character come from, and I want to know whether that collision is fatal or whether the theory just shrinks to almost nothing. If it's fatal, say so and say what's left. That's the result I'm after, not a tour of the answers. And flag any item on my list that isn't a real requirement at all but just a habit of one camp -- I'd rather not build the spec out of somebody's prejudice.

One last thing. "Meritocracy" gets used two ways around me -- as a description of how things do work and as a goal for how they should work -- and I suspect those are doing different jobs. If a theory of desert is meant to underwrite the second use, add that to the list and tell me whether it can.

Answer in American English, and skip the throat-clearing -- I've read enough of that already.
