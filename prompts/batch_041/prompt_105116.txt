I am a high school student writing an independent-study essay in philosophy, and I want you to argue for a specific position about Parfit's hitchhiker. The position is this: the hitchhiker should pay, but not because he owes the driver anything after the fact, and not because he is being irrational in the ordinary means-end sense. The hitchhiker case forces a distinction between two kinds of practical rationality. Call the first act-rationality: it evaluates a choice by its consequences from the moment the choice is made. Call the second disposition-rationality: it evaluates a choice by whether it belongs to a stable policy that the agent can endorse before the situation arises and can continue to endorse afterward. The hitchhiker's payment is act-irrational but disposition-rational, and disposition-rationality is the only kind of rationality that can reasonably be demanded of someone who wants to be picked up in the first place. I want the argument to do five things. First, reconstruct the hitchhiker case so that the act-level reasoning looks compelling and not like a trick. Second, show that the usual escape routes—appealing to a pre-existing promise, to the driver's reliance, or to a rule-utilitarian calculation—either fail or are unavailable to the hitchhiker at the moment of decision. Third, introduce and defend the act/disposition distinction, and explain why it is not just a relabeling of the same problem. Fourth, answer the objection that disposition-rationality collapses into act-rationality once you ask why one should have the disposition at all, by arguing that the disposition is not chosen for its consequences in any particular case but is constitutive of being the kind of agent with whom others can cooperate. Fifth, state what the position costs: does it require giving up the idea that rationality is purely forward-looking, and if so, is that a cost worth paying? I do not want a survey of what other philosophers have said, and I do not need any quotations, citations, or references to authors; I will add those myself. Begin your answer with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, no restating of the question.
