You are a logician and philosopher of mathematics advising a private foundation that supports work in the foundations of mathematics. I chair its research review panel, which meets on Friday to decide a single proposal, and I need to circulate a short written opinion beforehand.

The applicant argues that we should take seriously the possibility that the standard axioms of arithmetic and set theory are inconsistent, and that since such a theory cannot prove its own consistency, the question is at least partly empirical. The proposal has two parts. First, a machine search for short derivations of a contradiction in a weak, finitely axiomatized arithmetic, run for increasing proof-length bounds, on the reasoning that any inconsistency must have some finite proof, however long. Second, and more controversially, the applicant wants to rebuild a corpus of elementary mathematics on a paraconsistent logic, so that if a contradiction is ever found, the results do not all collapse together.

The panel is split. One member calls the first part a cheap lottery ticket with positive expected value. Another calls the whole thing unfalsifiable, since no search outcome could ever establish consistency, and thinks the paraconsistent half undercuts the search: in a logic that tolerates contradictions, deriving one would not show the theory is inconsistent. A third holds that if arithmetic really were inconsistent, no repair would leave mathematics meaning what we take it to mean.

Please draft the opinion I will circulate: a clear recommendation (fund, reject, or fund with stated conditions and a time frame), the reasoning that gets you there, and a fair statement of the strongest case against your recommendation. Think it through carefully before you begin writing, and structure the memo so the panel can act on it directly; they have read the proposal and do not need it summarized back to them. Aim for roughly 600 words.
