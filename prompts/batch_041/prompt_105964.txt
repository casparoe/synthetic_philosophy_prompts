I cover science and ideas, and the story I keep running into is the one about whether a research program is still going somewhere or has settled into marking time. Reporting on those fights has pushed me toward a Lakatosian position — but a stronger, more operational version of it than the one usually attributed to him — and I want to know what would count against it.

Here is the position.

1. The distinction between a progressive and a degenerating research program is real, not a retrospective courtesy we extend to winners.
2. It can be drawn prospectively, in real time, by an observer who knows the field — not only by a historian writing after the fact.
3. The way to draw it prospectively is a commitment device. A critic who invokes the hard core, the protective belt, and the positive heuristic should be willing to state in advance which novel predictions the program's heuristic commits it to, over what interval, and what would count as the belt absorbing an anomaly rather than the core generating new content. The critic should then be scoreable on those calls the way a forecaster is scoreable.
4. A critic who will not accept that discipline is narrating the methodology, not applying it.

What I want is a map of this position's exposure. Which observations, historical episodes, arguments, or formal results would count against it — and which of those would a committed Lakatosian concede as counting against him rather than dismiss as a misunderstanding? Sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say what each would have to look like to be decisive. I would especially like to know:

- Does the prospective version survive the Duhem–Quine problem? If any core can be protected by enough belt adjustments, is "that modification was ad hoc" ever a fact rather than a verdict?
- What do the standard cases actually show? Wegener's drift, the invented planet Vulcan in the Newtonian program, cold fusion, the long argument over string theory — do they support prospective judgment, or do they show that the verdict is only ever available in hindsight?
- Is the position self-applicable? If someone asks what would make me give up Lakatosianism, is there an answer that is not just "more history," and if there isn't, is that fatal or is it simply the price of any methodology of this kind?
- Has the apparatus been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if it has, is that a problem for a view whose whole selling point is that it discriminates better than Popper's?

Tell me what I would have to see to abandon this, and be honest if the answer is nothing.
