ok so I'm drafting a short thing on guilt vs shame for a reading group and I want a consistency check on my actual views before I commit to them. here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) guilt is the better moral emotion, better than shame I mean. it's about what you did rather than who you are, it points at repair, and it keeps the wronged party in view instead of collapsing you into a puddle of self-loathing. the empirical moral psych stuff seems to back this up: guilt motivates apology and reparation, shame motivates hiding and lashing out.

(2) but I also think shame is the more serious moral emotion. it's the one that's actually about the self and its ideals, and a person with no capacity for shame seems to me to be missing something, not just spared an extra burden.

(3) I'm a Strawsonian about responsibility: to hold someone responsible just is for them to be apt targets of certain reactive attitudes (resentment, indignation, gratitude, that family), and that's the whole story — there's no further fact about desert underneath it.

(4) whether a culture runs on guilt or on shame isn't something the culture decides. it's downstream of its institutions, its religion, its family structure, how much anonymity you have, etc. nobody sat down and chose it.

(5) all the same, I think the broad historical drift from shame toward guilt in the modern west was a moral improvement, I'd defend it, and I'd want something like it to keep happening elsewhere.

now, which of these actually pull against each other? I'm pretty sure they can't all stay as stated but I can't see which one is the weak link. rank the tensions by how bad they are, tell me which pairs are only apparently in tension (I have a feeling one of them is), and for the real ones say what the cheapest fix is and what I'd be giving up by taking it. and if there's one single revision that would clean up the most, tell me what it is.
