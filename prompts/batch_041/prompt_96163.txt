My friend Nadia and I have been working through a philosophy of mind course together online — I'm a backend engineer, she did a psych degree, so between us we know just enough to get ourselves into trouble. She's become a total convert to the zombie argument: she thinks that because we can coherently imagine a world that's physically identical to ours but with nobody home in any of the bodies, physicalism is basically dead. What I actually care about is the rule she's running underneath, because she keeps handing down verdicts and I'm not convinced they're all coming from the same place. Her track record so far, all cases where the physics is held fixed at exactly ours: (1) no experiences anywhere, possible, obviously, that's just the zombie world; (2) everyone's colour experiences permuted, red for green and so on, possible; (3) all experience one percent less vivid, possible; (4) one extra subject of experience floating around over and above all the ordinary people, no brain, no body, nothing detectable, just an additional someone having experiences, possible — her reasoning being that if the physics doesn't fix how much experience there is, it definately doesn't fix how many experiencers there are. The one case she won't touch is the China brain thing where the whole population of China passes messages around in a way that mimics my brain's wiring; she says she can't get the scenario into focus well enough to say whether anyone is home, and she's careful to add that she isn't denying it's conscious, she just doesn't think she's actually imagining anything determinate there. I honestly can't tell if that's the same rule doing more work or her copping out.

Anyway, there's a new case coming and I want to get out ahead of it. Call it the unowned pain world: physics identical to ours, everything happens exactly as it happens here, but the experiences aren't had by anyone — there are pains and colour sensations occuring, they're just not anyone's. There's pain, but nobody is in pain. So what does she say, and is it the zombie verdict over again or a different move?

My suspicion is she's running one of two rules and I can't tell which. Either conceivability is cheap — if I can describe it without contradiction and I'm not just stringing words together, it's possible — in which case the unowned pain world sails through the same door the zombie world did. Or the only gap she'll really buy is between the physical facts and experiences of the kind she's been acquainted with, so what's conceivable is just rearranging the quantity or arrangement of experiences she already knows, and anything positing a new kind of thing — a sensation that belongs to nobody — isn't conceivable in the relevant sense at all, and the argument goes quiet on it rather than answering no. Which rule fits all five of her data points (the four verdicts plus the China brain shrug), what does each predict for the unowned pain world, which would you bet on and why, and what's a different case I could put to her that would actually seperate the two rules — because I don't think this one does it cleanly.
