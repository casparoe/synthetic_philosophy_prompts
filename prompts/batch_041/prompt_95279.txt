I retired from physics a couple years back and have been binging philosophy ever since, mostly the population-ethics and infinite-futures stuff, because it scratches the same itch cosmology did. There's a move I keep running into and I don't know if it has a name.

Here's the pattern. Someone wants to argue that some choice about the far future doesn't really matter. They say: look, either way there end up being infinitely many happy people, so the two futures are equally good, end of story. Then in the very next breath the same person (or their opponent) wants to argue that a *different* choice does matter, and now the infinitude is no obstacle at all -- they point out that in one future the good lives take longer to arrive, or the bad stuff is bunched up early, and that's supposed to make it worse. So whether infinity flattens a difference or leaves it standing seems to depend entirely on which feature of the futures you happen to point at first. Nobody ever states a rule for which feature wins; it's just that whichever feature gives the wanted answer is the one that gets foregrounded.

The nearest thing I can think of is that business in analysis where you can rearrange the terms of an infinite series and change the sum -- I'm fairly sure that has a name -- but that's about numbers, and what bugs me here is the way the verdict gets *chosen* rather than derived, and the way both sides do it while each accusing the other of cheating with infinity.

So: does this have a name? If it's really just the series thing wearing a moral hat, I want to know exactly how; if it's something else, give me a name and a real definition. In particular, what separates it from the boring complaint that infinite values are hard to add up, and where does it stop being this and start being an ordinary case of picking whatever comparison suits you? Boundary cases would help. I'm not after a reading list, I just want the concept pinned down.
