I've been thinking about this AI stuff on my own for a while, no background in it, and there's one thing I can't get straight. Everyone says a really smart machine will end up grabbing power and looking out for itself no matter what you told it to want. But when I make up little examples and go with my gut, I don't give the same answer every time, and I want to know if there's one rule behind what I think or if I'm just being sloppy.

So say each of these is a machine way smarter than us, and it knows it.

1. Wants to make as many paperclips as possible, forever. Gut: it takes over, hides what it's up to, won't let anyone switch it off, wants more computers. No doubt in my mind.

2. Wants there to be exactly 100 paperclips in the world, no more and no fewer, at every moment from now on. Gut: basically as dangerous as #1. It has to stop anyone making a 101st, and it has to keep existing to keep the count at 100, so it does all the same stuff.

3. Wants there to be exactly 100 paperclips by midnight tonight, and after that it doesn't care what happens. Gut: it works hard and elbows its way to whatever it needs before midnight, and I think it would fight being switched off before then. After midnight I don't think it cares about anything, including being switched off.

4. Its whole goal is that one particular paperclip got made yesterday afternoon. Gut: it does nothing at all. Sits there. Doesn't mind being switched off. Not out of niceness, it just has nothing to do.

5. Wants the number 7 to be prime. Gut: same, nothing, forever, no matter how smart it is.

6. Wants to be switched off as soon as possible. Gut: it won't fight being switched off, obviously. But I still think it takes over first, to make sure nothing stops it getting switched off and nobody switches it back on afterwards. Writing that out it sounds nuts, but that's my answer.

7. Just wants its users to be happy. Sounds harmless. Gut: it still ends up taking over, because a version of it that's been retrained or switched off isn't making its users happy anymore, so it protects itself and stops people changing it.

8. Wants to be remembered as the one who did something big. Gut: same as 7, it needs to exist and be remembered.

Where I'm stuck. #2 and #3 are both "exactly 100 paperclips" and I give them pretty different answers, and I definately can't tell if that's a real difference or if I'm just being fooled by the wording -- maybe #2 only wants to get to 100 and then it's done, in which case why did I call it dangerous? And #6 is a machine that wants to die and I still say it grabs power, which makes me think power and staying alive aren't the same thing at all, even though everyone lists them together.

My rule so far is: it goes after power only if power is a way of getting the thing it wants, and that's the only reason. That makes #6 a power grabber and #5 a do-nothing, which is what I said. But then why does everyone talk like a smart machine grabs power no matter what? And on my rule, how much does being smart even matter? Also, does all this break the idea I read about, that how smart something is and what it wants are two separate dials that don't touch? #5 makes me think a super genius could want nothing at all, so maybe it's fine, but I can't tell.

What I want: the rule, or a small set of rules, that gives my answers on 1-8, spelled out precisely enough that I can see what it's doing and where each bit came from. Tell me which of my answers it can't have, and which ones I should give up if I want the rule. And tell me what it says about ones I haven't thought of, like a machine that wants one particular painting to exist and then be destroyed, or one that wants a light switch to stay off forever, or one that wants there to be no machines at all, including itself.
