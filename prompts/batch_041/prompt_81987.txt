I have been trying to understand anekāntavāda, the Jain doctrine that reality is many-sided and that any assertion holds only in a certain respect. It seems to me that this comes to saying no standpoint is superior to any other, so that the Jain teachings are themselves just one partial perspective among many and each person is entitled to whichever view suits them. A colleague tells me the doctrine's sevenfold scheme of predication states the point more precisely, but as far as I can see it only multiplies the same relativism. Yet the Jains I know treat ahimsa as binding without exception, and their teachers plainly hold that some positions are more adequate than others. How is that consistent with many-sidedness, and if the doctrine does not entail that all views are equally valid, where exactly does my reading go wrong?
