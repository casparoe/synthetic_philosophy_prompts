Alright, I'm trying to write down my actual views on whether virtual stuff is real, and I keep worrying they dont all fit together, so please audit them for me:

1. Virtual objects are real objects. The rare mount my character has in an MMO isn't a fiction the way Sherlock Holmes is a fiction — it exists, it got created at a particular time, it persists when nobody's logged in, it can be traded, destroyed, duplicated. Holmes can't be any of those things; he's just a pretense we all share.
2. I'm a physicalist. I don't think there's any non-physical stuff, so whatever "real" amounts to for the mount, it's got to be grounded in physical goings-on — servers, bits, electricity, brains.
3. Virtual harms are real harms. Getting griefed, having your stuff stolen or your account wrecked, getting harassed in VR — those wrong the actual person and aren't just "part of the game".
4. But virtual murder isn't wrong. If I kill your character in a game where you respawn, or take your loot in a fair fight, I haven't wronged you — even though it's the same kind of in-game act that (3) counts as a real harm when it's griefeing.
5. A wrong needs a victim whose interests actually get set back. No interest setback, no wrong.
6. My rough test for whether something is a real object rather than a fiction: does it have persistence conditions, and can it earn its keep in causal explanations? A government passes this; a unicorn doesn't. I think of a virtual object as real the way a government is real — brought into being by collective recognition riding on top of a physical substrate, and causally efficacious once it's up and running.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest way to make the set consistent? Order the real tensions by how bad they are, and for each one tell me what I'd have to give up and what it'd cost me elsewhere. I don't already know which one is the actual problem, so please discriminate rather than just flagging everything as in tension.
