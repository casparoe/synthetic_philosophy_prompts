I keep running into the same move in procreative-beneficence arguments: yeah, pick the embryo with the best expected life, but nobody said you have to keep making babies, and anyway the whole thing is a little creepy. Argue for a position I think is underexplored — that if you take procreative beneficence seriously as an impersonal maximizing principle, it doesnt stop at choosing among the embryos you've already made; it turns into a standing duty to create *more* people for as long as the next one's expected welfare clears the bar, even when no existing person is made better off either way. Call it open-ended procreative beneficence. Argue that this is just the original principle followed through, so it's the view and not a reductio, and that the standard objections — Habermas-style worries about the designed child's authorship of her own life, the non-identity problem, "playing God", disability-rights objections — either miss it or boomerang back onto the original principle. Then say whether this makes you a liberal eugenicist or something further out, and name the one objection you can't fully answer.
