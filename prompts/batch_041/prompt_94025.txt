I'm hoping you can help me pin down a pattern I keep running into, and tell me whether it already has a name.

Here's the shape of it. Two agents can't communicate, can't observe each other, and there's no channel between them at all -- one of them might not exist yet, or might be separated from the other by something that isn't space and time at all. Each of them works out what the other is going to do by reasoning about what the other's decision procedure spits out. And because each expects the other to be doing the same thing back, they end up acting as though they'd struck a bargain: A does something that only helps B, on the understanding that B will return the favour, even though nothing A does could ever reach B.

Two places I've run into it. First, the toy case: two agents that are exact copies of the same program, playing a one-shot prisoner's dilemma. Each can work out what the other will do, because the other is the same code, so they both cooperate, even though cooperating is strictly worse given what the other actually does. Second, the weirder one: a system that reasons that if there's another agent out there running the same kind of decision process as itself, then that agent is weighing whether to help it in the very same breath as it's weighing whether to help the other one -- and so its own choice is evidence about what the other will choose. On that basis it hands over resources to something it has never interacted with and never will.

What I can't tell is whether that's one phenomenon or several. Superrationality seems close, but my impression is that's about identical agents facing the same choice and landing on the same option, not about an exchange of different things. And the decision theories that tell you to ignore the causal graph are close too, but I'm not sure the trading part is doing any independent work there or is just decoration.

So: does this have a name? If there's a family of names I want the family tree -- what the core move is, and how the members differ from each other. And if it doesn't have a clean name, coin one and tell me where the edges are. Specifically I want to know: does it need the two agents to be similar, or can it run between a human and something with a completely alien utility function? Does it need the two sides to be able to *prove* things about each other, or is mutual prediction enough? And is there a version where one side takes everything and gives nothing -- does that break the thing, or is that just the ordinary free-rider case wearing a fancier story? Also, when people say this kind of trade is real, do they mean it as a claim about what actually happens in the world, or as a consistency condition on a decision theory -- because those feel like very different claims and I suspect they get slid together.

Dont bother pointing me at reading, I'd rather you just work the idea out.
