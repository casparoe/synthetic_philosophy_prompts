I am a high school student who runs a philosophy club and hosts a small interview podcast for our school's humanities program. Next month I will be interviewing a decision theorist who has argued that the Pasadena game is not just a curiosity but a genuine counterexample to expected utility theory, and that rational choice theory needs to be rebuilt so that it can handle cases where an option's expected value is undefined rather than merely infinite.

For those who have not seen it, the Pasadena game works like this: a fair coin is tossed until it lands heads. If heads first appears on toss n, the player receives a payoff of negative one raised to the n, times two to the n, divided by n. The expected value is the alternating harmonic series, which converges to negative the natural log of two, but only conditionally: reorder the terms and you can get any sum you like. So the expectation is not well-defined. A companion game, sometimes called the Altadena game, removes the sign alternation so that every payoff is positive, which makes the expected value diverge to positive infinity, even though the Altadena game dominates the Pasadena game outcome by outcome. Expected utility theory seems to have nothing to say about the choice between them, while a simple dominance principle says Altadena is better.

I want to prepare questions that go beyond having my guest restate the puzzle. Please draft eight to ten interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer the guest is most likely to give. I want at least one question each on: (a) why the failure of absolute convergence matters, rather than being a mere mathematical technicality; (b) whether the dominance principle can do the work that expected utility theory cannot, and what happens if the two conflict; (c) whether fine-graining the state space, that is, describing outcomes in more detail, can restore well-defined expectations; (d) what a relative expectation approach would look like and whether it generalizes; and (e) whether it is acceptable for a theory of rationality to remain silent when an agent must choose.

Write everything in American English.
