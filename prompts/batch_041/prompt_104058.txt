Occasionalism — the view, often attributed to al-Ghazālī, that God alone produces every event and that created things are merely the occasions on which he acts — faces a familiar difficulty: if God is the sole producer of the burn, what is gained by citing the fire rather than the presence of oxygen? Please explain the conceptual issue of whether denying that any creature's action necessitates its effect really commits one to this view, or whether a position granting creatures genuinely efficacious but non-necessitating powers is coherent. Then say what work the notion of an occasion is doing in the occasionalist's account of why citing the fire explains the burn better than citing the oxygen.
