I'm a PhD student working on collective action and individual responsibility, and I'm trying to pin down what I actually believe about my own tiny contributions to big harms, because my intuitions are all over the place and I want to know if there's a principle behind them or if I'm just rationalizing the stuff I like doing.

Here's my gut on a bunch of cases:

- Me eating a beef burger when there's a perfectly fine veggie option: wrong, I feel genuinely bad about it.
- Me taking one more long-haul flight for a holiday I don't really need: wrong, but honestly it feels *less* wrong than the burger, which is weird since the flight is way more emissions.
- Me driving to the corner shop instead of walking ten minutes: not wrong, no guilt at all, even though it's the same kind of tiny climate contribution as the burger.
- Me leaving the tap running while I brush my teeth: not wrong, don't care.
- Me not voting in a general election where my seat is a safe seat for the party I'd vote for anyway: totally fine, skipping it.
- Me not voting when the seat might come down to a handful of votes and I don't know which way: still I think it's fine to skip on the day, but I'd feel awful if I later found out my vote would have decided it, and in *that* case I think I did something wrong. So maybe the wrongness depends on stuff I can't know at the time, which bugs me.
- Me being one of 50,000 people asked to chip in $10 to a fund that saves a kid's life if it hits its target: if it's gonna hit the target without me, fine to skip; if my $10 is literally the one that pushes it over, I have to give. And I usually can't tell which situation I'm in, but my verdict flips hard between them.
- Me buying a fast-fashion t-shirt instead of the pricier ethically-made one: feels a bit wrong but I do it anyway, and I don't feel as bad as I do about the burger.
- Me and nine friends each dumping one bucket of (legal, harmless-ish) dye into a lake, where only ten buckets make it visibly change colour: I think we've each done something wrong and we're each responsible for the colour, but I can't say which bucket did it.
- Me throwing one recyclable bottle in the general trash: not wrong, zero guilt.

The tentative principle I keep reaching for is something like: an act of mine is wrong if it's part of a pattern of acts by many people that together does something bad, I could reasonably foresee that, and there's an easy alternative. But that seems to make the bottle and the corner-shop drive wrong too, which I don't believe, and it doesn't explain why the burger and the fast-fashion shirt feel worse to me than the flight when the flight does more damage. It also doesn't obviously handle the "my contribution is the one that tips it over" cases versus the "it would've happened anyway" cases, where my verdicts differ but my foreseeable info is basically the same.

So: is there a principle (or small set of them) that gets most of these, including the ones I keep flip-flopping on? I want it stated precisely enough that I can tell what it says about cases I haven't listed -- say, me choosing to have a kid (big lifetime emissions) versus me eating one more egg. And please be honest about which of my verdicts the principle can't save, because I suspect at least one of them has to go, and I'd rather know which.
