Write a joke whose punchline turns on the substance of the disagreement between the compatibilist Daniel Dennett and the free-will skeptic Gregg Caruso about just deserts and criminal punishment, rather than on the mere fact that two philosophers disagree. Both reject libertarian free will, and both are willing to keep dangerous people confined; the live dispute is whether an offender ever deserves to suffer for what they did, independently of any future benefit, or whether the justification for confinement is entirely forward-looking. Ground the joke in one concrete scene — a sentencing hearing, a parole board, a shared taxi, the two of them splitting a restaurant bill — and let the punchline turn on one of the specific moves each of them makes: Caruso's insistence that abandoning basic desert leaves our practices essentially intact, or Dennett's insistence that something is lost once we stop treating people as agents who can be held to account. A reader who knows the debate should find it funny; a reader who does not should still be able to follow the setup without concluding that the author has misunderstood either position. Keep it under 200 words.
