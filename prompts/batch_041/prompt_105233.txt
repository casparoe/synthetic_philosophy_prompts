I spent 32 years at the bench — structural biology first, then a decade in biotech — and retired a few years back. Since then I've been going through philosophy properly for the first time, philosophy of medicine mostly plus some ethics, and honestly it's been a revelation, because I'd always assumed the interesting questions in medicine were empirical. Anyway, I've ended up as the one non-clinician on our hospital's medical ethics committee, and it's definately my turn to write the opinion we circulate before Thursday's meeting.

The case: a 12-year-old boy, healthy, no endocrine abnormality anyone can find, predicted adult height around 5'4". His parents want us to endorse starting growth hormone now to push him taller. They'll pay cash, so for them it's not a money question, but our committee has to say whether the hospital should be doing this at all and what our policy should be for the next family that asks.

Here's what's making it hard for me. Our endocrinologist has proposed a rule she calls the "but-for test." Something counts as treatment, she says, if it moves the kid toward the height he would have reached but for some specifiable pathological process, and counts as enhancement otherwise — and then she tacks on a second clause, that even a treatment-qualifying intervention shouldn't push anyone above the 50th percentile for their reference population. I can't tell if that second clause is doing any real work or is just squeamishness dressed up as a threshold. I also can't shake the feeling that "pathological process" is carrying all the weight in the first clause and nobody's actually defined it.

So please draft the short written opinion I'll hand round. Give me a concrete recommendation — yes, no, or yes-with-conditions — plus the reasoning, and engage the strongest case for the other side rather than just dismissing it. Don't just tell me what some famous philosopher said about the natural/artificial distinction; assume I've read the obvious stuff and want the argument itself, in your own words. And say something about the kid's own say in this, since he's 12 and has opinions. A couple of paragraphs is fine, I'm not writing a dissertation.
