Ok so I'm taking this philosophy of medicine course and we've spent two weeks on whether calling something a disease is a fact about the body or a value judgement, and honestly the readings are so abstract that I started just listing what I actually think about people I know, and now I can't tell what my own pattern is. Here's the list. My cousin's nearsightedness: disease, obviously, she gets glasses and nobody argues. My dad going bald at 45: not a disease, not even a mild one, he's just bald, even though I guess his hair follicles have stopped doing their job. My mom needing reading glasses since she turned 50: also not a disease, which is weird because its the exact same problem as my cousin's, just the other direction, and she also just gets glasses for it. My grandpa's high blood pressure, which he can't feel at all: disease, definitely, he should be taking the pills. My uncle's lactose intolerance: not a disease, even though his body genuinely can't do something a body is normally supposed to do, it's just a fact about him. My roommate's grief a month after her dad died — can't sleep, can't eat, can't go to class: not a disease, I'd feel like a monster calling it that. But the exact same symptoms with no death and no reason: disease, get her to a doctor. My little brother being in the bottom percentile for height but perfectly healthy: not a disease, even though my parents got offered hormones for it. Wisdom teeth coming in sideways: not a disease, just a thing that happens, but a cavity in one of them is a disease. A kid who can't sit still in class but is fine at home building legos for three hours: not a disorder. A kid who can't sit still anywhere and can't keep a single friend: disorder. A hangover: not a disease. Drinking every day and shaking when you stop: disease.

My half-formed theory is that it's a disease when some part of you isn't doing the job it's supposed to do. That makes baldness a disease though (isn't hair's job to grow on your head?) and makes grief not one, which I like, but then myopia and presbyopia are the same kind of failure with opposite verdicts from me. The other theory I keep trying is that it's a disease when you're worse off in a way we all agree is bad — but that makes baldness a disease for my dad, who actually does hate it, and makes my grandpa's blood pressure not one, since he feels completely fine. Also, what do I do with stuff that used to be in the diagnostic manual and now isn't? If it was never a disease then a whole profession was just wrong about a fact, which is a strange thing to say about a fact, and if it was one and stopped being one then it was never a fact to begin with.

Is there a single principle that gets all of these, or close? And what does it say about the ones I'm least sure about — aging itself, and a kid who's miserable because his parents are divorcing, is that a disease or just a bad time? I suspect I have to give up at least one of my verdicts and I'd rather you tell me which one than pretend they all fit.
