They always get discussed together, as if one fix would do for both: either give up the rule that lets you conjoin your beliefs, or give up the rule that your beliefs have to be consistent. My half-formed hunch is that they acutally come apart. The preface case is self-referential in a way the lottery isn't — the author's belief that one of her claims is wrong is a belief about her own error rate, aimed at the very claims she's making, whereas the fact that one ticket will win is a fact about a machine that doesn't care what anyone believes. So maybe you can rationally believe a statistical fact about a chance setup without believing any of its instances, but that trick won't work when the statistical fact is about yourself.
