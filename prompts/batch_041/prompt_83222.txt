I host a small interview series that grew out of a reading group, and the listeners are mostly people who came to epistemology sideways, through rationalist blogs and forums. They already know what sensitivity and adherence are, so a guest who spends the first twenty minutes restating the tracking theory will lose them; what they want is to watch a position being pressed. Next month's guest is a philosopher who has spent several years defending a tracking theory of knowledge and repairing it against the closure objection. Her repair is to treat knowledge attributions as elliptical. On her account the tracking relation is really three-place, holding between a subject, a proposition, and a question, or a set of live alternatives, so that sensitivity is always assessed against the alternatives in play in the subject's inquiry. She takes this to explain why it sounds absurd to say that one knows one has hands but does not know that one is not a handless brain in a vat: on her view the two conjuncts answer different questions, so their conjunction never expresses a single claim, and the absurdity is an artifact of reading them as though it did.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, so that I can keep the pressure on instead of letting her retreat into a general statement of the view. I want at least one question on each of the following. First, whether the relativization dissolves the abominable conjunction or merely relabels it: what does she think an ordinary speaker is doing when they assert the conjunction in a single breath, and is that speaker making a mistake or speaking a different language? Second, what fixes the relevant alternatives — the subject's psychology, the attributor's conversational context, or something objective — and, relatedly, what fixes the method by reference to which the counterfactual is evaluated; each option costs her something, and I want the question to say what. Third, knowledge of necessary truths, where there is no world in which the proposition fails and the counterfactual condition looks either vacuous or undefined. Fourth, whether she is prepared to say in her own voice that she does not know she is not a brain in a vat, and what that does to the rest of what she claims to know. And fifth, the normative register: if knowledge is not closed under known entailment, can it still be what licenses assertion, or the use of a proposition as a premise in practical reasoning? Several people in the group think tracking was refuted long ago and attend these sessions hoping to watch it die, so I would rather hand them the strongest version of the case than a caricature; make the objections bite. Nothing biographical, and no invitations to summarize her book.

Give the questions as continuous prose, the way one would write them in a letter — no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables.
