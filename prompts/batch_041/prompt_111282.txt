I've come round to thinking that in the careenium set-up — the box of tiny magnetised balls (simms) that clump into bigger balls (simmballs), where the symbols are supposed to live at the simmball level even though no simm ever reads anything — the symbol level doesn't push anything around. The simms push; the simmballs are a pattern we describe, not a further cause. So: what should count against that? I want the defeaters sorted, most decisive to merely awkward, including anything already on the table rather than hypothetical, and I want to know what someone who holds my line would themselves concede counts against them, not just what critics shout. Does my view predict anything its rivals don't?

Two things I keep circling. First, a substrate swap: rebuild the box out of balls with a completely different micro-physics, arranged so the simmball pattern is the same, and the behaviour comes out the same. Does that help me or hurt me? Second, the reverse, micro-surgery: hold the simmball pattern fixed, fiddle the micro-physics, and the behaviour changes. Which of these, if either, is a real defeater, and which is just multiple realisation dressed up? And am I cheating — I notice that whenever something threatens to make the symbol level matter, I redraw where 'the substrate' stops. Does it include the environment the box sits in, or its history? If that's a dodge, say so, and say wether it's fatal or the sort of dodge views like mine are allowed. What would move me most is a case where a purely semantic intervention changes what the thing does with no simm-level difference at all; tell me if that's even coherent.
