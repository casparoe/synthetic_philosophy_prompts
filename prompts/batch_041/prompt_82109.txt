I'm not a philosopher, I just end up in a lot of arguements — group chat, twitter, my brother — and I keep going back and forth about how much I should actually change my mind when someone who seems about as good at the thing as I am disagrees with me. I've got a pile of gut reactions to specific cases and I can't get them to line up. Is there one rule behind them, and which of my reactions do I have to give up?

Here's what I think in each case.

1. A coworker and I spent a whole afternoon on the same spreadsheet, same numbers, no stakes for either of us, and we come out with opposite readings of whether the trend is real. I think we should both end up somewhere in the middle, and I should be noticeably less sure than I was going in.

2. Same setup with my cousin, who is equally convinced the moon landings were faked. He says he's seen all the same stuff I have. I don't move an inch. He's just wrong and I'm not going to pretend it's a coin flip.

3. A friend who's been wrong the last three times we disagreed about this kind of thing. I move a little, but way less than with the coworker.

4. Someone who knows the area better than I do and is just generally sharper than me disagrees. I move a lot — close to all the way over to their side.

5. Two people, each about as good at it as I am, both disagree with me and both agree with each other. I feel like I should move more than if it were only one of them, but not twice as much, because I suspect they just talked each other into it.

6. My brother says grandma's ring was in the drawer, I'm sure I put it in the box. I don't move at all, I'm just certain. But if you ask me why my memory of that day is better than his, I've got nothing, and that bothers me.

7. I'd happily bet $10 at even odds that I'm right about something, and she'd happily bet $10 at even odds that she's right about the same thing. Something feels off about that and I can't put my finger on what.

8. My friend Sam's whole thing is that when someone disagrees with you, you back your own read and don't budge. Honestly I think he's got a point. But if I take his advice then I shouldn't take his advice, since I disagree with him.

The rule I came up with is: you should end up where you'd be if you treated the other person's opinion as worth exactly as much as your own — basically averaging the two of you. But that seems to get cases 2, 6 and 8 wrong, and it also says that in case 1, if I'm at 90% and she's at 10%, I should land on 50%, which feels like throwing away everything I know. I feel like I should end up closer to my own view than to the middle, unless I've got literally nothing to go on.

So: is there a principle that gets all eight, or close? If you have to drop one of my reactions to get a clean rule, tell me which and why. And tell me what the rule says about a case I haven't thought of — pick one where my gut would probably be a mess. Also, is there any principled way to say how far I should move, or is everyone just guessing at that part? (I've heard philosophers have names for the two sides of this, one of them sounds like conciliate and the other is steadfast something, but I never remember which is supposed to be which.)
