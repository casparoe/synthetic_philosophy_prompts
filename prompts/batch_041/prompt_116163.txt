Hey — I'm running a session for a creativity-and-cognition seminar next month and I need a real activity for it, not slides. The crowd is about 18 grad students, mixed design, CS and philosophy, 75 minutes in one room with a whiteboard and tables they can move around. What I want them to walk out feeling is Hofstadter's basic picture — that a concept is a point in some space of knobs, and creative work is reaching for the right knob and twisting it the right amount — but I dont want to just tell them that, they should bump into it.

Here's the wrinkle, and it's the thing I want the whole thing built around. The fun half of the picture is easy to feel: grab a knob, twist it, look at what you made. But I've come to think the real action is a level up, and I want them to feel that too. Twisting an existing knob gets you a competent variation. The creative move is often to add a knob — to re-describe the space so that a dimension nobody was even varying turns out to be the interesting one. The trouble is you can't tell those two moves apart while you're doing them, because the space doesn't announce its own edges. I want the activity to put them on the wrong side of that distinction at least once before it clicks.

So design it. Rules, materials, a way to score or judge that doesn't need money and doesn't collapse into "well its all subjective" (half this room will say that), a timeline for the 75 minutes, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the two levels — twisting a knob vs adding one, and the separate question of how far to twist before the variation stops being a variation of the theme at all. My default for the raw material is a short, flat, boring story I write on the board in five sentences, with the obvious story-knobs as the starting set, but if you have something better that needs no skill and no materials, use it.

Also: they shouldn't hear the words "knob" or Hofstadter until the debrief. And tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time I run this and how to handle it, and be honest about what the activity can't reproduce — I suspect it can't give them the thing that makes a real variation valuable, which is knowing a tradition well enough to feel what counts as a fresh move inside it, but tell me if I'm wrong. Write it in American English.
