In a graduate seminar I called a colleague's revulsion at a certain practice a bias rather than a considered moral judgment, and she answered that there is no disputing about tastes. Before I concede the point, I want to know what that answer could actually be claiming. Lay out the distinct theses it might express — that agreement is unattainable, that the response is not truth-apt, that no reasons can be given for or against it, that its holder is not answerable for it, that criticizing it is inappropriate, that it is immune to revision by argument — and say which are trivial, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false. Treat a bias throughout as a preference that misrepresents its own provenance, and say which readings would let the slogan deflect my charge, which would leave it standing, and which reading she is most likely relying on. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then set out the readings and your verdicts on them, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
