I have been reading Mill on India — the East India Company correspondence he drafted as an examiner, the discussion of dependencies in Considerations on Representative Government, and the essay on non-intervention — and I have arrived at a reading I would like tested. On this reading, Mill's case for British rule in India is a self-liquidating trust: it is defensible only if (i) it is directed at the improvement of the governed, (ii) it is provisional, held only until the governed can govern themselves, and (iii) since the governed cannot yet hold the trustee to account, the trustee must answer to an external check. Whether Mill merits the label liberal imperialist is not the interesting question. The interesting question is which later arrangements these three conditions would license and which they would condemn.

Predict Mill's verdicts on five developments that postdate him: (1) the mandate and trusteeship systems, in which a single state administers a dependent territory on behalf of an international body and answers to that body for its conduct; (2) post-conflict international administration of the kind attempted in Kosovo and East Timor, in which an outside authority runs courts, police, and ministries with no local electoral mandate and no settled exit condition; (3) the policy conditionality attached to development lending by the IMF and World Bank, under which a borrowing government must adopt specified policies as the price of funds; (4) the transfer of power in India in 1947, conceded to a nationalist movement that demanded it rather than granted after the preparation Mill anticipated, together with the partition that accompanied it; and (5) the case for official apologies and material reparations for colonial rule, pressed long after the trust has ended.

Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, one per development, each item one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. For each item, state the position you predict Mill would take, give the strongest Millian reason for it, and — where that reason strains one of my three conditions — note the strain in a clause. Where two of the cases look alike but come apart for Mill, especially (1) and (2), make the difference explicit.
