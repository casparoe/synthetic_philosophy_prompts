I'm putting together a handout for the opening meeting of a graduate seminar on the timing of conscious experience, and I want a decision tree rather than another list of -isms. The tree should carry a student from the starting commitment that the colour phi phenomenon is a genuine effect — two differently coloured dots flashed in quick succession at two locations, with subjects reporting a single dot that appears to change colour in mid-flight, and reporting the change as occurring before the second dot is even presented — to a named position on when, if ever, the experience of the colour change is fixed. Please build it out of the logical space rather than out of a roster of authors: first the branch nodes, each phrased as a question a student could answer without already knowing which position it leads to, then the leaves, each carrying the name of the position (or "unnamed" if nobody defends it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I'd like six to nine branch nodes and no more than eight leaves, and where two leaves cannot actually be separated by any of your questions, I'd rather you flag the pair than paper over the gap with a question that only looks diagnostic.

The distinctions I care about most are these: whether there is a fact of the matter about the timing of a content's being experienced that is independent of what the subject can report; whether the brain's interpolated content is genuinely experienced or merely judged and then reported as if it had been experienced; and whether the revision, where there is one, targets memory or targets the experience itself. Make sure the tree can reach at least the Orwellian option (a real experience at the time, later falsified in memory), the Stalinesque option (the experience itself is staged only after the second dot is registered), the deflationary option on which those two are not genuinely different, a direct-realist option that denies any filling-in and treats the report as confabulation about a gap, and a metacognitive option that locates the colour change in a judgment rather than in sensory content. I'd also like one split I have not seen drawn cleanly elsewhere: distinguish a pre-emptive Orwellianism, in which the memory trace is laid down already falsified so that nothing is ever overwritten, from a corrective Orwellianism, in which a faithful trace is genuinely overwritten — and say whether the colour phi data, or any of the neighbouring cases, could in principle tell these two apart. You may use the cutaneous rabbit, metacontrast masking, the flash-lag effect, and Libet-style timing paradigms as extra branch cases, but keep the tree anchored on colour phi.

Where a combination of answers comes out unoccupied, note it and diagnose it briefly — is the view incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unexplored? Because students will read this as a settled map, please close by saying how confident you are in the overall structure and singling out the one leaf or branch you are least sure is correctly drawn, with your reason for the doubt.
