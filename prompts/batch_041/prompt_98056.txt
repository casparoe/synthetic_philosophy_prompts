I hold that betterness is transitive — if A is better than B and B is better than C, then A is better than C — and that the spectrum arguments against this are not counterexamples but artifacts of context-sensitivity: at any fixed context the relation is transitive, but the standard of comparison shifts as we move along the spectrum. I also think this contextualist package is the only way to keep transitivity while accepting the premises of the spectrum. What would count against it? I want the cases, formal results, and rival explanations that should move me, ranked from decisive to merely awkward. In particular: are there spectrum cases that can be made context-invariant without begging the question, and do they generate a genuine cycle? Does the contextualist move predict anything that a simple denial of transitivity, or a vagueness account on which betterness is transitive at every precisification, does not? Which objections would a committed contextualist have to concede, and has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability?
