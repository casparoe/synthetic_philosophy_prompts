Ok, longtime lurker of the old rationalist blogs here (LessWrong, the slatestarcodex comment section, all that), and I want to make one of those "which position do you actually hold" things for the epistemology of markets. Build it as a decision tree that starts from someone accepting the no-trade theorem: if two agents share a common prior and it's common knowledge that they're both rational, they can't agree to a bet/trade on purely informational grounds. Where does that leave you? Each branch node should be a question a person can answer without already knowing which camp it drops them into. Nodes I want on there: whether the common-prior assumption is a harmless idealization or is quietly where all the action is, whether a market price is a probability or a stake-weighted belief with a risk/capital adjustment baked in, whether prices track truth or merely coordinate action, whether the theorem just assumes away the interesting case (everyone's already pooled their info), and how they handle the paradox that a perfectly efficient market gives nobody any reason to go gather the info in the first place. Leaves: efficient-markets/rational-expectations, Hayekian dispersed-knowledge, the "efficiency and the incentive to acquire info are in tension" view, the behavioral "no-trade assumes away the phenomenon" camp, "prices are coordination devices, not credences", and the political-economy "markets track power not truth" camp. Keep it to 8-10 nodes, and flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really seperate, plus tell me where the empty branches are and why nobody's there -- incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.
