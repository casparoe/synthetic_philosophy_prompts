I want you to stress-test a position I hold about dispositions, one I have not seen stated in quite this form. The position is this: the difference between finks and masks is not a difference in the world but an artefact of where we place the boundary of the disposition-bearer. Take the two standard cases. A wire is connected to a device that would interrupt the current the instant it began to flow, so the wire never conducts; a glass is fragile but sits in foam packing, so it never breaks. The first case is usually treated as one in which the disposition is absent just when it matters, the second as one in which the disposition is present but blocked, and the two are held to pose different problems for any conditional analysis of disposition ascriptions. I say they are a single phenomenon under two descriptions. Whether the interfering mechanism counts as part of the bearer or as an external circumstance is not settled by the case itself; it is settled by our choice of description, and nothing in nature dictates that choice. The surrounding taxonomy of finks, masks, mimics and antidotes should therefore collapse into one notion of interference, and a disposition ascription should be analysed not as a conditional but as a claim about the causal structure of a bearer under a chosen description.

Tell me what this exposes me to. What would have to be the case for the position to be false? Which counterexamples bite and which merely irritate? Which of my other commitments does it stand or fall with — about the intrinsicality of dispositions, about their modal force, about the explanatory work they do in the special sciences? What does it predict that a conditional analysis, or a powers-based account, does not? And is the redescription doing genuine work, or have I made the view immune to refutation by definition? If the latter, say so plainly, and tell me whether that is a defect here or simply the price of a metaphysical claim at this level of abstraction. If the position turns out to be a familiar one under another name, or to collapse into a reformed conditional analysis that builds in the absence of interference, say which and what, if anything, it adds.

I want the objections ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with the ones I should concede as a defender marked as such, and an honest verdict on whether what I have is a substantive thesis or a redescription of the problem. What I would find hardest to answer is a case in which the redescription would require me to count as part of the bearer something that no defensible account of object-hood would count as part of it; if such a case exists, I want it set out in full.
