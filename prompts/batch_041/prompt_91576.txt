I've been chewing on Pascal's mugging for years and I think I've finally landed somewhere, so I want someone to try to knock me off it.

Here's my position: the mugging isn't really an argument against expected value maximization as such. It's an argument against unbounded utility functions, or more precisely against pairing ordinary real-valued credences with a value structure that has no ceiling. If you bound the utility function — or do something lexicographic / non-Archimedean so that arbitrarily tiny chances of arbitrarily huge goods can't outvote finite, certain goods — then the mugger's offer just falls below your floor and you say no. And you're not being irrational or "shutting down reasoning," you're being the kind of agent whose value structure actually has a bottom. That feels like the sane resolution to me and I've basically adopted it.

So: what counts against this? I want the real defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I especially want ones that a committed bounded-utility person would themselves have to concede rather than just dismiss as critics' cheap shots.

A few things I'm already suspicious of, which you should either develop or tell me why they're not as bad as they look. First, does bounding utility even help against a St. Petersburg–flavored mugging, where every individual payoff is bounded but the structure still yields a divergent expected value? If so, doesn't that mean my fix isn't really about boundedness at all? Second, the many-muggers / aggregate case: a world with a whole population of independent muggers each offering a tiny chance of a huge harm seems like it could mug a bounded agent anyway over a long enough life. Third, what does bounding utility cost me elsewhere — continuity, the representation theorems, Dutch-book-ish exploitability, preference reversals? If boundedness buys safety from the mugger by giving up something I actually want, I need to know what the trade is.

And be honest about the meta-question: has this position been qualified into something unfalsifiable — "whatever the mugger offers, it's below my floor, by construction"? If it has, is that actually a problem for a view like this, or is it fine? The thing that would move me most is a mugging that survives the bound without obviously being a cheat.
