I have just published my fourth novel, and my circumstances have changed in ways I would rather not spell out. My difficulty is this. My younger brother trained as a cellist, gave up a secure orchestral post three years ago to freelance, and has now exhausted his savings. My mother believes that I should support him indefinitely — not lend him money, not help him find work, but simply send enough each month that he can continue as he is. Her reasoning is that I can easily afford it, that the family went without when I was young so that I could write, and that anyone with plenty who refuses to share it is merely selfish. I am not, in fact, close-fisted: I underwrite a residency for young writers, and I have quietly paid medical bills for two friends. So the objection cannot be that I never give. Yet this particular request angers me in a way I cannot defend, and whenever I try to explain myself I sound like a miser counting coins. Is my mother right that I owe him this? And if she is not, what precisely is wrong with her argument — because I suspect the trouble lies neither in the sum nor even in the family tie, but in the shape of the demand itself.
