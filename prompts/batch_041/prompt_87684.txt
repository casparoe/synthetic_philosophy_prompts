I'm building a fake exhibit for a museum-studies workshop on how archaeology gets written up, and I need you to write the interpretive wall panel that sits next to the display case — the deadpan, official-voice label, the thing with the header and the little paragraphs and the sidebar.

The site is invented. Make it up entirely: some late Bronze Age settlement, call it whatever you like, with finds that are plausible but don't need to be real — pierced bone discs, a burnt clay floor, a cache of polished axes, storage pits, some of them with animal bone in them and a few with nothing at all, and one deposit that's a bit odd. Don't reference any actual excavations, sites, or scholars, and no further-reading list or bibliography.

Here's what I'm after. Real panels state a probable function and then quietly upgrade it into an institution, and I want the panel to do that all the way down. So: the bone discs are standardized, standardization implies a workshop, the workshop implies attached specialists, attached specialists imply an elite, the elite implies a hereditary priesthood. Each inferential step should be stated in the standard form — the presence of A in context B indicates C — so a reader can watch the chain creak. And 'ritual' has to do a lot of load-bearing work: it's the answer for the burnt floor, the odd deposit, and anything else the panel can't otherwise explain. Give it a couple of those weirdly precise numbers people use (a population of 120-150, a deposit dated to a specific fifty-year window). Never use the word 'speculate' until the very last paragraph, which should be the obligatory caution paragraph where the panel admits we can't know what these people believed, after spending the whole thing telling us exactly what they believed.

Also a sidebar for kids — 'What the objects tell us!' — that's even more confident than the panel is.

No winking and no scare quotes, that's the whole point; the flat institutional register is what makes it work. Someone who's actually done this should laugh and also wince, because the leaps have to be the real ones people make, not invented nonsense. Around 650 words.
