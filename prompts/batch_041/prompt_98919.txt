I'm in a small reading group on conditionals and probability, and one guy keeps settling the big question — whether the probability of a conditional just is the conditional probability of the consequent given the antecedent — with a betting story. The pitch is: asserting "if A then B" is like placing a bet on B that only goes live if A turns out true and gets called off, stake returned, if A turns out false; so the fair odds you'd quote on the conditional are exactly the odds you'd quote on B restricted to the A-cases, which is the conditional probability; hence the probability of the conditional equals the conditional probability. It's very persuasive in the room and honestly it's doing all the work — nobody has a separate argument, the bet picture *is* the argument. I want a proper audit of it rather than another round of everyone nodding.

So: give me the mapping explicitly, ideally as a table — what plays the role of the wager, the payoff, the cancellation clause, the bookie's fair odds — and then where the analogy breaks, sorting the breaks into the ones that don't matter for what it's being used to prove (I don't care that bets involve money and assertions don't, or that you can bet on anything) and the ones that would actually sink the conclusion. My nagging suspicion is that the picture only ever delivers the equation one conditional at a time, considered in isolation, and quietly smuggles in that a conditional is the kind of thing that has a truth value and a well-defined place inside one big probability space — which is precisely the assumption the triviality proofs go after (the results that say you can't have that equation hold across a whole probability function, for every pair of sentences, unless the function is degenerate in some way). So the real question: once the disanalogies are on the table, does the equation survive, or was the betting analogy carrying the conclusion the whole time? And if there's a better picture that keeps what's right about the betting idea — the sense that a conditional is a kind of conditional commitment rather than a plain factual claim — tell me what it is and what it still can't buy you.
