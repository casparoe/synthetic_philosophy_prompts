OK so I've been reading about societal collapse for a while now — Bronze Age, Maya, Easter Island, all the greatest hits — and I keep hitting the same wall, both in the literature and in arguments with friends. The debate about whether these societies "collapsed" or just changed seems completely stuck. The Maya case especially: some people say the Classic Maya collapsed, others say the southern lowlands depopulated and political authority fragmented while the north kept ticking along fine, so calling it a collapse is a modern projection. Easter Island has the whole ecocide-versus-resilience fight. The Bronze Age has the Sea Peoples and systems-collapse stuff. And it feels like everyone has the same evidence and nobody's mind moves an inch.

I want a diagnosis, not a verdict on any one case. Is this a verbal dispute about the word "collapse"? Are the two sides answering different questions under a single heading — did the state fall, did the population drop, did life get worse for ordinary people, did complexity decline? Is there a hidden empirical question doing the work, or is it about what even counts as evidence (monuments and texts vs. settlement surveys and paleoclimate)? Or is it bedrock stuff about what we think a society is and whether it can "fail" at all?

Heres my hypothesis, take it or leave it: I suspect the whole thing is muddled by the fact that the records we have were made by elites, so when elites and their administrative apparatus disappear we read it as the whole society vanishing, when actually a lot of people's lives may have carried on or even improved. If that's right, then "collapse" is partly an artifact of who was doing the writing. But I might be wrong.

Also, please be upfront about what's actually settled among the people who study this and what's genuinely contested — don't hand me one camp's line as if it's the consensus. And if you think no progress should be expected here, say so and say why.
