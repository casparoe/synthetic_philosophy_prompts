Please explain the conceptual issue that makes the question why Britain and not China? contested rather than merely empirically hard. Focus on the contrast-class problem: the question presupposes a comparison class, and different choices—Britain versus China, Europe versus Asia, or one region versus another—generate different explananda and different causal stories. Then explain the circularity risk: because the two cases differ in indefinitely many respects, the historian may select as causally relevant exactly those differences already known to accompany the outcome, making the explanation post hoc. Finally, say what would need to be specified for the question to be well posed, and whether the Needham question (why China did not develop modern science and industry despite earlier advantages) and the Great Divergence debate are versions of the same question or different questions.
