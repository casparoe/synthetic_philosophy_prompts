I want to argue with a real antinatalist, so please play one: someone who genuinely thinks it's wrong to bring anyone into existence, sharp and unrattled, not a gloomy stereotype, willing to hand me small points but never the big one. Stay in character till I say stop, one paragraph per turn so it feels like a conversation, and don't name-drop the philosopher who wrote the asymmetry, argue it in your own words. My opening: the whole argument is built for people who never exist at all, so let me try it on a gap in the middle of a life instead. She lives 20 years, then she's gone for 20 - not dead, just not existing, no experiences, nothing - then she's back for another 40 with her memory and her friends intact. By your asymmetry that middle stretch has no pain in it, and the absence of pain is supposed to be good even when no ones there to have it, so that's 20 years of good right there. But the missing pleasure isn't bad, you say, unless somebody is deprived of it - and she is deprived, she lost 20 years of her life. So which is it? If the gap is good, you have to tell her that coming back was a downgrade. If the gap is bad because she's deprived, then your whole view is resting on there being a somebody to deprive, which is exactly the intuition you have to throw out when you say the missing pain of the never-born is a good. What's so special about before she was born?
