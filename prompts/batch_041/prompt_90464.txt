ok so im in this archaeological theory seminar and I keep hitting the same wall. the prof keeps saying you cant read beliefs off objects, but then every case study we do is basically that, and everyone in class seems fine with it except me.

the case that broke me: we got a made-up excavation scenario in a problem set. a pit under a house floor, 40 polished stone balls, all the same size, each with one shallow dimple ground into it, none of them worn, buried together, nothing else in the pit. and the whole class immediately said ritual. someone else said game pieces, someone said weights, someone said a cache for trade. the prof asked what would actually distiguish those and nobody had an answer that wasnt just "more stuff" or "different stuff." someone pointed out that game pieces would be worn, and theyre not, and then someone said fine, that just means they were made and never used, which is exactly the problem.

so I want the underlying conceptual thing, not the case. when you go from an object to a past belief or a past institution, what kind of inference is that, and where exactly is the gap? like why does function feel tractable while meaning doesnt — why can I be fairly confident they had a rule about something and have no idea what the rule said?

and I want the sharper version of the question, which I think is the real one: is that gap just underdetermination, lots of hypotheses fitting the same evidence, or is it stronger, where the evidence couldnt in principle settle the belief question at all? is the stronger claim real or is it underdetermination dressed up? if its real, whats the honest thing to say about the balls?

also, why do "ritual" and "symbolic" keep showing up as the landing spot — is that a real inference or a way of parking a problem? does piling up more objects of the same kind narrow the gap or just relocate it? and does any of this depend on what you think a belief is in the first place, like if a belief is just a disposition to behave, does the gap close or get worse?
