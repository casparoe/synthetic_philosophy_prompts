I'm a novelist, and I've somehow ended up as the after-dinner speaker at a two-day workshop on mind and machine. It's in a old Victorian hall, and the guest list is split right down the middle: half AI researchers who've actually built things, half members of a psychical research society that's been meeting in the same town since the fifties. The organizers want something short, funny but sincere, and they specifically asked me to talk about Turing's weirdest move. You know the one — the part where he takes telepathy seriously enough to worry that a mind-reading interrogator, or a mind-reading machine, would wreck the imitation game, and then he imagines a telepathy-proof room so the test can go ahead clean. Write me that speech. It has to land for both halves of the room: the engineers shouldn't feel condescended to, and the psychical people shouldn't feel like the punchline. And the ESP thing has to do real structural work — it should shape what the speech can and can't claim about minds and machines, not just be a fun fact I drop and move on. Five minutes, tops. One more thing: if I've got Turing wrong, or if this whole framing is a misreading of what he was actually up to, just say so plainly instead of humoring me.
