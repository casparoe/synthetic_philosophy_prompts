You are a philosopher specialising in decision theory under moral uncertainty, and you are used to constructing cases that isolate one variable. I have recently joined a hospital ethics committee, and I keep facing decisions where I am uncertain between moral theories but also uncertain how much to trust my own moral judgment. I want a thought experiment built to specifications, not a survey. Do not give me a reading list, citations, or references. Start with a short brainstorm of possible cases, then give one case, then the analysis.

Construct a realistic case in which two decision-makers, A and B, face the same options, the same payoffs, and the same empirical uncertainty, and have exactly the same first-order moral credences over the same set of moral theories. They must be alike in moral character, information, and stakes; the only difference is their higher-order evidence about their own moral reliability. A has ordinary evidence—a track record, calibration, or the pattern of peer disagreement—that her moral judgments are somewhat correlated with the truth; B has ordinary evidence that hers are not. The case must make two approaches to moral uncertainty deliver opposite verdicts: (i) a simple expected-choiceworthiness rule that uses only first-order moral credences, and (ii) a higher-order-evidence-sensitive rule that discounts or adjusts those credences in light of evidence about the agent's own reliability. Constraints: an ordinary, non-philosophical setting; no trolleys, no exotic technology, no infinite payoffs, no population axiology, and no assuming that one moral theory is obviously correct; keep the scenario itself to no more than a paragraph. After the scenario, state the verdict each approach gives and why; identify the feature of the case doing the work; say what happens if that feature is removed; and explain how a defender of the approach that fares worse would attack the case. Do not stipulate the verdicts; derive them from the approaches. The case is for a paper on whether moral uncertainty should be modelled as uncertainty about moral theories or as uncertainty about one's own moral reliability, so a case that simply builds the conclusion into the setup fails. Finish by taking a definite position on which verdict you think is correct and why. Write in British English.
