I am preparing to interview, for a podcast, a philosopher who defends an externalist version of infinitism: on her view, a belief is justified just in case there exists an infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons supporting it, whether or not the believer can produce, grasp, or even be aware of most of that chain. She claims that only this version of infinitism escapes the objection that finite minds cannot possess infinite chains. Draft eight to ten interview questions. I want at least one question on why an infinite chain is required at all, rather than a very long finite one, with the follow-up you would ask once she gives her standard answer; one on the dilemma that either the chain may repeat its content, in which case the requirement is vacuous, or it may not, in which case very little is justified; one on whether dropping the availability requirement collapses her position into reliabilism, into foundationalism with the whole chain playing the role of a basic belief, or into coherentism; one on what justifies the thesis of infinitism itself, given that any finite defence of it would violate its own standard; and one on how experience is supposed to constrain the chain at all. For each question, include the follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. Keep the questions on the substance of the position rather than on her career or motivations, and please answer in British English.
