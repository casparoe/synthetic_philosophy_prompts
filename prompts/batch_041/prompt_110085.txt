Write a dialogue between two philosophers of mind who examine one case and reach opposite verdicts about it. The case: a large language model has been trained on a corpus that includes its own earlier outputs; it now reliably asserts false things about that corpus, yet a linear probe on its internal activations recovers the true contents with high accuracy, and under some prompts its behavior fits the recovered truth. The first interlocutor reads belief attribution instrumentally: to say the model believes P is only to say that predicting it as a believer works well, so the probe is a lower-level description of the same system and the question of what the model really believes has no further content. The second reads it realistically: attribution is warranted when an internal state plays the right causal role — carrying the information, guiding behavior across counterfactuals, interacting with other such states — and the probe is evidence that it does, so the instrumentalist is declining to answer a question that does have an answer. Both should be as strong as their position allows, each responding to the other's actual points rather than restating a line, and the exchange should stay anchored to this case rather than to general slogans about prediction versus explanation. End with the disagreement sharper rather than resolved; about 800 words.
