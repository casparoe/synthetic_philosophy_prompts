I'm a novelist, and the narrator of my new book is a burnt-out classicist who pays the rent doing stand-up, so I need an actual joke I can put in his mouth. Put on the hat of a philosopher who's written for a late-night show. Brainstorm a few angles first (you can keep that list to yourself), then give me one joke — setup plus punchline, 4-6 lines, no more.

It has to turn on Aristotle's real account of money, not on lol-philosophers-hate-bankers. The bits I want to use: he says exchange needs a common measure because my need and your need never show up at the same moment (the shoemaker doesnt want a house today), so money is basically a stand-in for a want that hasn't arrived yet, an IOU to the future. And he says money is money by convention rather than by nature, which is precisely why it can go from being wealth to being a paperweight overnight. And my favourite: he needs the things being exchanged to be commensurable, then more or less admits they aren't really, we just pretend, and he shrugs that this is good enough for practical purposes — if you can make the punchline turn on that, do it, but the nomos bit or the money-is-barren-so-lending-is-monstrous bit are also fair game. Just make sure the payoff depends on one of the actual moves.

Bar for funny: it should get a real laugh out of someone who's read the acquisition chapters of the Politics and the money section of Ethics V.5. A polite ha from someone who hasn't is fine. And please dont do the interest-is-just-money-having-babies thing, thats been done to death.
