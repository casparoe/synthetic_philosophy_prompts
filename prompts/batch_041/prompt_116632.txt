I'm putting together a handout for a reading group on logical empiricism and politics, and I want it to work as a sorting instrument rather than a summary. Build me a decision tree that starts from one commitment — value claims aren't true or false, they express practical decisions — and ends in named positions about socialism, planning, and the role of experts. Every branch node should be a question someone can answer without already knowing the labels, something like: does the non-cognitivist still get to call one plan better than another, and if so is it better as a means or as the expression of a shared decision; is the scientific world-conception politically neutral, or does it carry an attitude of its own; when ends clash, who settles it — experts, markets, or a democratic procedure; is choosing a social arrangement like choosing a language framework, where there's no truth of the matter, only fruitfulness and convenience. I want leaves for roughly these: means-end engineering socialism (Carnap's own line), Neurath-flavored planning as applied unified science, instrumentalist socialism that sneaks cognitivism back in through facts about welfare, non-cognitivist quietism about politics, market proceduralism, and a democratic proceduralism that keeps the planners subordinate to the people whose ends are being decided. Around 8 to 10 branch questions, each leaf with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Note any combination of answers nobody actually occupies and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really pull apart. Audience is grad students who've read some Carnap but none of the political material, so don't let any node hinge on knowing a particular essay.
