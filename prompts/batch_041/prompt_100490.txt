Trying to pin down where I actually land on free speech, going off gut reactions, and I want the principle behind them if there is one.

A guy in the park yelling that the local factory gave him cancer, no evidence: fine, let him yell. The same guy camped at the factory gate with a bullhorn all day so nobody can get to work: not fine. A newspaper knowingly running a false story that sinks a small business: not fine. A blogger calling that same business 'a total scam' with nothing to back it up: fine, somehow, even though it might do just as much damage — that pair I cant square. Posting a private person's home address online because you think they did something terrible: not fine, even though the address is true. 'I'll kill you' in a bar fight: not fine. The same words in a song: fine. Leaflets at the mall saying some ethnic group is inferior: fine, hate it but fine. Emailing that same leaflet over and over to one coworker: not fine. A company advertising a supplement as a cancer cure: not fine, though it barely feels like speech to me at all. A neighbor blasting political rants through a speaker at 3am: not fine, but I don't think it's the content that bothers me.

My rough guess is it's about whether the speech is aimed at one person who can't get away from it, but that doesn't explain the blogger vs the newspaper thing. Is there one principle that gets all of these, or do I have to give one up? And what would it say about a case I didn't list — a bot that endlessly reposts true, already-public, but damaging facts about one person, no threats, no lies?
