Ok, I need a second opinion on something that's been bugging me all week. I'm teaching the essential properties week in my metaphysics course, and I keep writing two things on the board that I'm no longer sure can both be true.

The first is the Kripkean line: that you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg, that the particular pair of gametes you actually came from is part of your essence and not just a biographical detail — so there's no possible world where you're your parents' kid but made from different cells, no world where you're somebody else's child, though there are plenty of worlds where you're a plumber. The second is the thing I use to wake the room up at 9am: that the odds of you in particular being here are something absurd, one in a few hundred trillion, that one particular sperm on one particular night, one egg, one bit of timing, and any tiny difference and you're simply not here. But if the first thing is true, the second now seems like nonsense to me. If those gametes are what make me me, then nothing was a fluke — once that egg and that sperm were in play, it was already settled that I'd be the one who came out of it. Which means my existence wasn't lucky at all, it was necessary, and the trillion-to-one talk is either a category error or just a nice story we tell first-years. It seems to spill over into ordinary life too: my parents couldn't really have decided not to have me, and their choosing to wait another year before trying again couldn't have been a decision about anything, since there was definately no alternative outcome on the table.

So which do I drop? A colleague says the honest move is to give up essential properties altogether — that origins can't be essential, since it would make us all necessary beings — but that feels like throwing out the whole week to save one joke. I'd rather understand what's actually going wrong. Where's the mistake, and what's the cleanest way to explain it so I don't sound like I'm having it both ways? And is there any room left for the luck talk once you've accepted that origins are essential — can the essentialist still say it was lucky that you exist, or does that really have to go?
