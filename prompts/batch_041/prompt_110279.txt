Here's the half-baked bit. Everyone waves around cranes vs skyhooks (Dennett's image, you know it) and everyone agrees greedy reductionism is bad, but I have never once seen anyone give necessary and sufficient conditions for either, and I've started to suspect that's because the picture is missing a box. As it stands the vocabulary seems to have only two slots:

- skyhook: does work without drawing on any regularity that's already there. Fine, allegedly these don't exist.
- crane: produces a structure by exploiting regularities that are independent of the structure, so it can get the thing out of materials that don't already have it.

And the box I want to add:

- treadmill: a process that holds a structure in place once it exists, but which would produce nothing at all if you ran it on the raw materials. Stabilizing selection holds a trait at its optimum and would never have built the trait in the first place. A thermostat holds a room's temperature and can't warm a cold room without fuel. A property regime enforces a norm it didn't invent. Memory maintains a skill rather than acquiring it.

The thing I actually want to test: cranes seem to need treadmills underneath them. Selection can't ratchet anything unless replication is faithful enough to resist drift, and that's a maintenance job; then a ratchet — anything that blocks the backslide — converts the treadmill's reliability into cumulative construction. So maybe "crane" isn't a basic category at all. Maybe a crane is just a treadmill coupled to a ratchet, and the real difference between a crane and a skyhook is that the crane is parasitic on something that already works, which is a much thinner claim than the picture usually advertises.

If that's right, greedy reductionism gets an actual criterion instead of being a term of abuse: skipping a level is greedy when the level you skipped was doing crane work, since you've thrown away the part that explains the origin, and not greedy when it was only doing treadmill work, since the lower-level story can supply the persistence. But honestly the opposite reading seems just as available to me — if all you've explained is maintenance and you then announce you've explained the origin, that looks like the paradigm case of a greedy reduction, not the licensed one. I can't tell which survives contact with the cases and that's the point of the project.
