I sit on the ethics advisory comittee of a mid-sized food company -- we do ready meals, some pet food, and on Thursday the board votes on whether to build our own insect-protein plant. The proposal is black soldier fly larvae in stacked trays, and at the end they get chilled and milled, which the engineers assure me is standard practice. Our supply-chain welfare policy has a species list attached to it, invertebrates aren't on it, and so the investment memo says the whole question is out of scope by definition -- which is the kind of sentence that makes a committee like ours feel decorative. I need to circulate a written opinion before Thursday: a clear recommendation (build, don't build, or build with conditions and a review date), reasons the board can actually act on, and the general rule we'd be committing ourselves to for next time, since we'll get more of these.

Here's where it got interesting. Our one neuroscientist member circulated a draft test: protect a taxon when its nervous system has receptors that respond specifically to tissue damage, a central place where those signals converge with the animal's overall arousal and motivational state, and evidence that the signal can be traded off against other goals rather than just triggering a fixed reflex -- and he says a larva fails that third part, so we're clear. Our sustainability lead thinks a test like that is unverifiable at our scale and wants a blunt precautionary rule: if there's a non-trivial chance the animal suffers and we'd be doing it to trillions of individuals a year, we act as if it does, and the burden falls on whoever wants to build. A third member keeps noting that if we say no, the chicken we'd have sold instead comes from a supplier with a worse welfare record than ours, so our abstention might leave the world worse off overall. I genuinely don't know whether the capacity to suffer is a line you're on one side of or a matter of degree, or how much that should change the bottom line, but the memo has to take a position. Keep it to a page and a half, the board won't read more than that, and please don't just tell me both sides are respectable -- they want a recommendation.
