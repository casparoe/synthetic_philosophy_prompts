I'm a science journalist but I guest-teach one session a year on a final-year 'science and ideas' module, and this year's topic is Schrödinger's Vedanta phase — the thing where the man who gave us the cat also spent his later years arguing that consciousness is only apparently many, that the Upanishadic 'thou art that' is roughly right, and that any 'I' you meet is the same one thing looking out. (He was also adamant that quantum indeterminacy has nothing to do with mind or free will, which is a tension I want them to feel.)

I need one essay question for the end-of-term paper, and I want it to be unanswerable by reciting 'Schrödinger was a mystic influenced by Eastern thought'. Here's the move I'd like them to have to make. Distinguish two readings of his monism: a shallow one, where all minds share the same form or structure of subjectivity, and a deep one, where there is literally one subject numerically identical across people. Then ask whether the deep reading can survive what I've been calling the partition problem — experience comes to us in sealed compartments, you can't feel my toothache, and physics seems to explain why (seperate brains, no informational link), whereas if there's only one consciousness the partitions look like an illusion that needs an account of its own. I'd also like them to say what the shallow reading buys him, and whether it's still a Vedantic claim at all or just a claim about the grammar of 'I'.

So: the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 that tells me the specific moves that separate an A from a B, plus a line on how students typically go wrong (last year half of them had him endorsing the observer-made-the-cat-real stuff, which doesnt seem to be his point at all). British spelling please, it's going into a UK magazine. Ta.
