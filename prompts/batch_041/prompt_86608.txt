My reading group's doing the hedonistic imperative this term — Pearce, abolitionism, engineer the suffering out of the world rather than patching up its causes. I'm mostly on board, which is what makes the following annonying. A friend boiled the case down to this and I can't find the loose thread.

(1) Suffering is bad in itself, not because of what it leads to or what it stops us doing. Delete its causes and its consequences and what's left is still bad, and bad in proportion to how bad it feels and how long it goes on.
(2) So a state that's bad in itself makes the world worse wherever it turns up, other things equal; and if we could get rid of it at a cost we can bear, getting rid of it is what we should do.
(3) Grief, remorse, disappointment and fear feel bad. Not mildly — some of them feel every bit as bad as the toothache, the nausea and the panic attacks that everyone already agrees we should be engineering away.
(4) Therefore the imperative is commited to engineering those out as well: not just the pain of them, the states. No grief, no remorse, no fear, no disappointment.
(5) And not merely permitted — by (2) we're required to want it and work towards it as soon as the technology's there. He says it also gets rid of the fear of dying, which he counts as a bonus.
(6) But a person who can't grieve has lost something, and I don't think the loss is the feeling. He says that's sentimentality: if I want to mark a death I can keep the memory, the funeral, the flag at half mast, and drop the affect. I think the affect isn't a tax on the grief, it's part of what grieving is; grief with the sting surgically removed isn't grief with a cost taken out, it's not grief.
(7) I'd give up almost anything to keep (1) — if suffering is only bad because of its effects then I've no idea what the position is any more. But I can't swallow (4) and (5), and the grief bit least of all. (The wild-animal business I can live with, so don't tell me that's where it goes wrong.)

So which line do I drop, and what does dropping it cost me? If it's (2), I want to know what's left of the practical case for the whole project. If it's (3), I want to know what I'm now saying suffering is, such that it's always bad and always worth abolishing, but grief isn't it. And I'd like to know why the step I give up looked so obvious in the first place.
