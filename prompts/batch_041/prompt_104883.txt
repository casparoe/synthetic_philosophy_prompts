I've been going back and forth with my flatmate (she does vision science, I'm doing an MA in philosophy) about the colour phi phenomenon, and I think I've talked myself into Dennett's conclusion, but I'm not sure I should be comfortable there. The setup: you flash a blue dot up top and a red dot down below in quick succession, and people report a single dot that travels between them and changes colour about halfway along the path — and crucially they report the colour change as happening before the second dot even appears, which is impossible if it's the colour of the second dot that determines the colour of the travelling dot. Dennett's line, the one I've come to accept, is that this shows the whole Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction is a distinction without a difference: you can say the brain edited the content before it ever reached consciousness, so the doctored version is the only one you ever had (Stalinesque), or you can say you genuinely experienced something and then memory quietly rewrote it (Orwellian), and no possible evidence favours either, because every finding is compatable with both and the question only looks well-formed if you've already smuggled in a single finish line in the brain where content gets shown to the audience. Asking whether the revision happened before or after the experience is, on this view, like asking which of two identical copies is the original.

What I want from you is a serious map of what would count against that. Not the usual objections that Dennett is ignoring qualia or that he's a eliminativist — I want the specific things: what kind of result, argument, or theoretical development would make the Orwellian/Stalinesque distinction a real distinction again, and which of those are live rather than merely logically possible. Things I'm already suspicious of and want you to adjudicate: (a) whether the indeterminacy is supposed to be merely epistemic (we can't tell) or metaphysical (there's nothing to tell), and whether the argument only delivers the second if you accept something verificationist, in which case a realist about phenomenal facts should say the distinction is real but forever inaccessible; (b) whether the arguement proves too much — if the same reasoning shows there's no fact of the matter about whether a masked word was seen, or whether a patient under anaesthesia felt anything, then either that's a feature or it's a reductio, and I want to know which; (c) whether some independent, non-Cartesian criterion for when content becomes conscious — a global-workspace-ish or higher-order or recurrent-processing kind of criterion — would, if it could be applied at these tens-of-milliseconds timescales, make the question determinate again; (d) whether there's any manipulation that should seperate the two hypotheses, e.g. if the pre-conscious edit has to pay a processing-time or capacity cost that a later memory rewrite wouldn't, or the other way round, and how you'd tell; and (e) what happens to the whole thing if the colour phi effect turns out to be less robust than the textbooks suggest, or explicable by boring low-level motion mechanisms (I've heard there are behaviourist and computational explanations that don't need any of Dennett's machinery). Rank these from fatal to merely awkward, and say which of them a committed Dennettian would themselves concede as counting against the view and which they'd wave away as question-begging.

And please be straight with me about your own confidence — tell me what would actually change your verdict here, and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
