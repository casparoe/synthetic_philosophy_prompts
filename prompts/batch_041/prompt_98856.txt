ok so I'm guest-editing a special issue for a phil-sci journal on the problem of old evidence, and I need to work out what actually matters before I write the call. The framing I keep bumping into is the standard one: a theory formulated *after* the data can't seem to get a confirmation boost from the very data it was built to accommodate, and everyone trots out the same handful of moves. Fine. But a mate of mine on a mailing list keeps insisting the real issue isn't "old" evidence at all — it's that conditionalisation quietly presupposes you can seperate your credences about the evidence from your credences about the theory, and that once a hypothesis partly *constitutes* what you count as the evidence, the whole bookkeeping goes weird. He's calling it the "archive problem" and thinks everything else is downstream of it. I'm not sure I buy it, and I don't want to build an issue around a rebranding.

So here's what I need. For someone with about a year of research time — a philosopher who's comfortable with a bit of probability theory but is not a probabilist — which open questions around old evidence and the confirmation of already-known data are genuinely worth working on, and in what order? I care much less about the list than the reasoning: which questions are upstream of which, which are tractable with the tools such a person actually has, and which only *look* deep but would collapse into something terminological once you pushed on them. Tell me too which ones I should just not bother with, either because they're effectively settled or because the answer wouldn't change anything else downstream. And give me a verdict on the "archive problem" reframing — if it's a real distinction that reorganises the field, say so; if it's the old problem in a new coat, say that instead, and tell me what would have to be true for the reframing to earn its keep.

One more thing: I want the ranking to be sensitive to what would move it — like, what result or argument over the next couple of years would push something up or down. A defended agenda, basically, not a survey.

Please write it in British English (colour, maths, analyse, that sort of thing).
