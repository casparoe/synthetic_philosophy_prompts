ok so i write for a tiny zine that's like halfway between a corporate satire newsletter and a philosophy blog, and i want a piece about judgment aggregation — the discursive dilemma specifically — but i don't want it to read like a wikipedia summary of List and Pettit. i want it as an internal corporate memo.

setting: a fictional mid-size company (you invent it, give it a bland wellness-brand name) whose board has just adopted a new decision procedure they're very proud of. the gimmick is that instead of voting on the final decision, they vote separately on each of the considerations that feed into it, and then the decision is whatever follows from the winning considerations. a rival faction wants to just vote on the decision itself. last week the two procedures gave different answers on a real motion and the memo is the comms person spinning it as a triumph.

stuff the piece needs to get right, because the jokes have to come from the actual structure and not from made-up nonsense:

- give a concrete case with specific numbers — three (or five) members, two premises, one conclusion that's just the conjunction, arranged so that each premise wins its vote but the conclusion loses its vote. the classic three-judge breach/damages pattern is fine but re-skin it as something corporate (e.g. "did the vendor miss the SLA" + "did the contract cap damages" → "are we liable"). the tallies have to actually work.
- the memo should be genuinely pleased about the inconsistency, not embarrassed — it should treat "we reached a coherent conclusion" and "we counted the votes" as if they were the same achievement.
- work in, deadpan, at least two of: that whoever controls the order/timing of the votes controls the outcome; that the "just vote on the conclusion" faction has its own problem (nobody can see why the group concluded it); that a member could vote strategically against a premise they believe in order to protect the conclusion they want.
- the "fix" the memo proposes should be one of the real cheap fixes, presented as an HR initiative. pick your favorite: reclassify the conclusion as a premise; add a tie-breaker member who only appears when outputs don't cohere; or appoint a "Coherence Officer" empowered to retroactively flip one member's vote and log it as a "scheduling correction." any of these is funnier if it's announced in the same breath as a new cafeteria vendor.

voice: deadpan corporate comms, no winking, no scare quotes around the paradox, no "of course, this is absurd." a reader who's never heard of the dilemma should still follow the story; a reader who has should be nodding at the mechanics, not wincing at them. don't name any real philosophers or real companies. around 650 words, with headed sections like a real memo (subject line, "background," "what changed," "what it means for you," etc.).

after the memo, add a short structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: "company" (string), "procedure_name" (string), "the_case" (object with "propositions", a list of objects each having "id", "text", "votes_for", "votes_against", plus "premise_based_outcome" and "conclusion_based_outcome" as strings), "the_fix_proposed" (string), "what_the_fix_quietly_assumes" (one sentence), and "the_one_technical_claim_that_must_be_right" (the specific vote-counting fact the whole joke depends on).

(sorry if this is over-specified, ive been burned before by pieces that were funny but technically wrong)
