I keep going back and fourth on the species problem and could use a hand sorting out two pictures that seem to pull in different directions. On the first, a species is really just a segment of a lineage — one underlying sort of thing, a chunk of a branch through time — and all our various species concepts are just operational criteria for spotting where those segments start and stop. On the second, a species is a homeostatic property cluster: a bundle of traits that travel together because some mechanism keeps them together, which makes species look more like kinds than like particular individuals. Can you compare these two views for me? What I care about most is how each one copes with the ugly cases — microbes and archaea trading genes sideways, asexual organisms, ring species — and what each of them owes us when we press on whether a species is an individual or a kind. Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, and skip any intro or wrap-up.
