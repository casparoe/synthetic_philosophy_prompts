I work in quantitative risk at a reinsurer — loss distributions, tail risk, discounting future cash flows to present value, and a house culture were every number gets compared in today's money. I keep getting dragged into arguments about strong longtermism: the view that the far future dwarfs the present in moral importance, that we might actually be able to steer it, and that we shouldn't discount future people's welfare just because they're distant in time or not born yet. Restate that position in the vocabulary I actually use — discount rates and NPV, expected-value maximization, tail risk and ruin, concentration vs diversification, model risk and unknown unknowns, and the difference between a numeraire and a utility — as faithfully as you can, and don't just slap my jargon on their view; if a term doesn't really fit, say so. Then the residue report: what maps cleanly, what I have to fudge, and what has no counterpart in my language at all. Is my framework being built on positive time preference a genuine disagreement with them or just a different parameter choice? And where things won't translate, is that a hole in my vocabulary or a sign the longtermist claim was never as sharp as it sounded?
