I retired from physics a few years ago and have been sitting in on a philosophy reading group, and we've been stuck on Nietzsche's eternal recurrence for months now. We've split into two camps that mostly talk past each other. One side says it's a straight cosmological claim, and that the argument he scribbled in his notebooks is the real proof: the total energy is finite, so there are only finitely many ways to arrange things, and with infinite time every arrangement has to come round again — and the whole point of the love-your-fate stuff is that it's literally true. The other side says it's a demon's thought experiment, an existential test of how you take your life, and that whether it's cosmologically true doesn't matter in the slightest.

Write the joint statement the two camps could both sign. What do we agree on, including about what the notebook argument actually claims and what work it's doing? Where do we really seperate, each side's view written in wording that side would accept? What are the cruxes — my hunch is one is whether the argument survives contact with physics (finite energy doesn't obviously give you finitely many states when positions and momenta vary continuously, and expansion and heat death seem to spoil it regardless, though I know closed finite systems do wander back near old states) and another is whether the person who comes round again is you or just someone indistinguishable from you — and for each crux, a case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Number the agreed claims, then a table for the rest. If part of the fight is just about words, say so. And say what would actually get either side to drop its view. Don't bother with references, we all have the texts.
