I teach a seminar that pairs Buddhist philosophy of mind with contemporary work on personal identity, and I want to build a session around Christopher Nolan's Memento. Read the film through the doctrine of anattā and tell me what it shows about no-self, what it complicates, and where it misleads.

The reading should come into contact with three things that the film's premise bears on directly.

First, the doctrine is contested in a way that matters here. Some readings treat anattā as a straightforward metaphysical denial that any self exists; others treat it as a soteriological strategy, a way of disidentifying from the aggregates, with the person surviving as a conventional designation (the two-truths framework, conventional versus ultimate). Leonard Shelby, who outsources his identity to tattoos, Polaroids, and written instructions, is a test case for the difference. Is he a bundle theorist in practice, or does he show that a constructed self is not an illusion to be seen through but an achievement to be maintained? Say which reading the film supports.

Second, consider the option the tradition largely rejected: the Pudgalavāda claim that the person is neither identical with nor distinct from the aggregates, and is what carries karmic continuity, memory, and rebirth. A protagonist who is continuous through external records and discontinuous in memory is a natural thought experiment for that position, especially given that the Sammy Jankis story may be Leonard's own history misremembered and reattached to someone else. Does the film dramatize the pudgala, or refute it? Note also what the reverse chronology does for the narrative-self picture: we are made to reconstruct a forward story out of backwards evidence, which is arguably what Leonard does with himself.

Third, accountability without a bearer. Leonard writes notes to a future self who will not remember writing them, and by the end he is arranging his own evidence. If there is no self, who is betrayed, and by whom? Does the film make the Buddhist problem vivid (karma and responsibility without an owner), or does it quietly rely on our sense of Leonard as one continuing agent, the very thing the doctrine denies, to make its ending land?

End with a verdict rather than a survey. Does Memento vindicate the anattā reading, resist it, or illustrate only its preliminaries? And say what the film contributes that Parfit-style argument cannot: what it shows about what it is like to be a self made of records, and whether the conventional self turns out to be something we cannot do without. Pick a reading, defend it, and be explicit about which scenes you are reading that way; I want your reasoning, not a synopsis.
