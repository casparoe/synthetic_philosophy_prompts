I am a retired experimental physiologist, some years into a late education in philosophy, and I would like you to build me a case rather than write me an essay about one. My question is whether the line between bringing about a harm and allowing it carries any moral weight of its own, or whether it is always a proxy for something else — a special obligation, a difference in what the agent could foresee, or a difference in the baseline against which the harm is measured. I have a specification, and I would like the case built to it.

The specification. Two agents, one patient, one ward, one outcome: the patient dies at the same time and of the same underlying condition in both scenarios. The agents differ in exactly one respect, namely the order of two events — whether the sustaining intervention was ever begun. One agent begins it and later discontinues it; the other, meeting an identical patient on an identical day, never begins it. Hold everything else fixed: prognosis, the agents' competence and information, the alternatives available, the wishes of patient and family, the documentation of the decision, and any antecedent commitment either agent has to this patient. The sequence must be the only variable, so that whatever moral difference the case displays cannot be attributed to anything but the sequence.

Then do the following, in this order. First, state the case in a single paragraph, in a realistic hospital setting: no trolleys, no organ lotteries, no invented technology, and I would prefer an intervention more mundane and ongoing than the familiar ventilator — and please say whether the choice of intervention matters. Second, give the verdict each of three positions must return, together with the reasoning that generates it: (i) the doctrine that it is harder to justify bringing a harm about than to allow it; (ii) the view that, other things equal, bringing a death about and allowing one are on a par; (iii) an account on which the classification of a contribution as doing or allowing is fixed not by causation but by the norm in question — roughly, if the norm requires the agent to maintain something, then letting it lapse counts as a doing, and if the norm requires only non-interference, then interfering is the doing. Third, tell me which feature of the case is doing the work, and what becomes of the three verdicts once that feature is removed. Fourth, tell me how a defender of the first position would attack the case — in particular, whether it can be dismissed as smuggling in a special obligation — and whether the attack succeeds. Fifth, if you conclude that the sequence cannot carry the weight on its own, say so plainly, and tell me which additional premise it needs.

I want the case for a reading group, to see whether the asymmetry has any force independent of the obligations attached to it, so a case whose verdicts are stipulated rather than generated will not help me. No citations, please; I want the argument worked out from the specification.
