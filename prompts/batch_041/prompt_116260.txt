My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about Ulysses contracts, and I want to send him a joke about them this morning. It should turn on the actual mechanics rather than on the word "Ulysses": that a precommitment only holds if my future self cannot revise it, that any procedure for revising a commitment is itself a commitment that can be revised, and that the binding is therefore always done by something outside me — a mast, a court, a bank, a spouse — whose interests must diverge from mine at the moment of temptation, since an enforcer who wants what I want has nothing to gain by holding me to it. Keep it dry and short, with a punchline that someone who has thought about self-binding will find genuinely funny and that someone who has not can still follow; if you can work the constitutional version in as well — the amendment clause that can itself be amended — so much the better.
