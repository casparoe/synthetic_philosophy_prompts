I hold that the Everettian reading of quantum mechanics does not need probability inserted by hand: the Born rule is supposed to be recoverable from within the theory, either by a decision-theoretic argument from the agent's rationality or by treating probability as self-locating uncertainty across branches whose weights the theory already fixes. Treat that as my position and tell me what would count against it. I want the load-bearing premises identified — the rationality axioms and how rich the agent's preference space must be, the assumption that a branch has a determinate weight at all, the step from a unique additive measure of the kind Gleason-style results supply to a measure that governs credence, and the choice of preferred basis — together with what the world would have to be like for each to fail, and which failures would be fatal to the derivation rather than awkward for one version of it. Distinguish what Everettians themselves would concede as counting against them from what critics merely assert. Finally, be straight about the charge that the derivation is circular, or that if it succeeds it renders the view unfalsifiable: say whether that is a defect or a sign that the claim was never empirical to begin with. No citations needed; I want the structure of the exposure, ranked from fatal to cosmetic.
