I have been trying to work out the color phi effect on my own. Two dots are flashed in different places in quick succession, each a different color, and people report seeing one dot travel across the gap and change color halfway. Some writers take this to show that the brain revises the experience itself before we are conscious of it — the Stalinesque picture, where the edit happens before the show — while others say the experience is untouched and only the memory of it is rewritten, which they call Orwellian. Please argue that the effect gives no real support to the Stalinesque picture over the Orwellian one, and that there is no fact of the matter about when the color change was experienced. Here is the point I cannot get past: suppose the second dot's color is chosen at random only after the first dot has already been flashed, so the brain could not have predicted it, and yet subjects still report the change as happening before the second dot appears. If the only evidence is what they say afterward, what could decide whether the experience or only the memory was revised? Argue that nothing could, and that the dispute is verbal. Then take the strongest objection you can think of — that the two pictures must differ in what a subject could in principle notice — and answer it.
