Consider a thought experiment. An arena the size of a cranium — a careenium — is filled with countless tiny spheres, simmballs, each bearing a symbol. The simmballs move and collide according to simple mechanical laws; their collisions rearrange the symbols, and certain higher-order patterns in the arrangement and motion of the symbols are what we would describe as thoughts, inferences, and decisions. Suppose the simmball level is causally closed: every motion of every simmball is fully determined by the prior motions of simmballs together with the mechanical laws, with no additional input from any other level. Suppose further that a particular symbolic pattern — a decision, say — is reliably followed by a simmball-level motion that we would describe as the corresponding action. Argue that the symbolic pattern genuinely pushes the simmballs around, rather than being merely along for the ride, in a sense that is robust to the causal closure of the simmball level. In your argument, address the objection that any causal role for the symbolic level would be either redundant or a violation of that closure, and consider whether it matters if the symbols are physically inert marks that do not affect collisions, as opposed to physical features that do affect them. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among philosophers who work on mental causation, levels of explanation, and downward causation; do not present a contested view as the consensus.
