I run intake at a small legal NGO in a country thats about four years out of an authoritarian regime, and we're getting a steady stream of complaints against mid-level functionaries: a judge who rubber-stamped capital sentences under a decree that made defeatism a capital crime, a records clerk who forwarded denunciations that put neighbours in prison, a warden who enforced a rule keeping prisoners incommunicado for months. Most of them say, correctly, that they were just applying the law as it then stood. My board wants a one-page intake procedure so a junior lawyer with a week and no jurisprudence background can run it on a fresh complaint and come out with take the case, drop it, or send it to the board. Please build it out of the Hart-Fuller fight over whether a sufficiently evil rule can count as law at all: the positivist line that it was law, and if we want to punish people under it we should say plainly that we are legislating backwards and own that cost, versus the natural-law line that a rule failing the basic conditions of legality -- not published, not general, retroactive, contradictory, impossible to comply with, ignored by the very officials who invoke it, rewritten weekly -- never was law to begin with. Give me the questions in the order we should ask them, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands off to a human because the question is moral or political rather than legal. Then run it on the judge and the warden, and tell me straight where the procedure is just laundering a political judgement as a legal test, and what it can't do. Fair warning: my board is sympathetic to victims and will not enjoy a procedure that tells us to drop cases, so don't soften the stops.
