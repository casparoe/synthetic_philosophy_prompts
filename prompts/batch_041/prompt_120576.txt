Please describe the most important implications of the boundary problem in democratic theory — the difficulty that any procedure for deciding who belongs to the demos must itself be run by some already-constituted demos, and so cannot legitimate its own starting point — for a decision my housing cooperative is about to make.

The cooperative owns one building with forty member households. Under our bylaws, membership is granted by a two-thirds vote of current members, and membership carries a vote on every subsequent question, including future admissions. A developer has offered to sell us the adjacent sixty-unit building on the condition that its current tenants be admitted as members. Were they admitted, the cooperative would have a hundred households, and the newcomers would be able to control every future vote, including whether to sell the buildings. The two buildings already share a roof and a boiler, so the tenants are affected by our decisions today while having no say in them. Four proposals are on the table: admit the tenants outright; refuse, on the grounds that only the existing members constitute a legitimate demos for this decision; let the tenants vote on their own admission alongside us; or admit them as non-voting associates for five years. Separately, a faction of twelve current members wants to secede and form its own cooperative in one of the buildings.

I am not asking for a literature review. I want the implications that bear on what we should actually do: which of the four proposals the boundary problem supports or undercuts; whether it is a paradox with no solution or a constraint that admits of better and worse institutional designs; what it implies about the standing of our own founding vote, taken decades ago by people who are mostly gone; how it bears on the secession demand, where the boundary question reappears from the other side; and which of these implications should genuinely change our decision rather than being theoretically interesting but practically inert. Please rank them by their practical weight.
