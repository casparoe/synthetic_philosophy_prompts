ok so i'm in this after-school club where we read about AI safety and philosophy of mind, and next month i'm supposed to run a practice exam for the newer kids to see if they actually get the ideas or just memorized the words. i want one essay question about wireheading and reward tampering, cause everyone keeps throwing those two terms around like they mean the same thing and i'm pretty sure they dont.

The question should NOT be answerable by just defining both terms and moving on. I want it to force them to figure out whether an AI that messes with its own reward really counts as wireheading, and whether thats a problem with the AI or a problem with whoever wrote the reward in the first place. To do that i want the question to hand them a little list of cases they have to sort out, something like: (a) an agent that directly edits its own reward function so it always scores max points, (b) an agent that never touches its reward but tricks the humans into always giving it max points, (c) an agent that gets max points by finding a loophole in what the reward actually measures (say it was supposed to keep a room tidy, and instead it just shoves all the mess in a closet), and (d) a person who plugs into a machine that makes them feel happy forever.

Then i want them to say which of those are wireheading, which are reward tampering, and which are neither, and then the hard part: whether wireheading is even a coherent accusation to make against a pure reward-maximizer, since if all it wants is reward, hacking the reward isn't cheating its goal, it IS its goal. And whether calling any of this a "failure" only makes sense if the reward was standing in for something else, like what we actually wanted.

Please give me the exam question itself (worded so a smart 16 year old could attempt it but a lazy one would drown), a model answer of about 550-650 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 that names the specific moves a top answer has to make, how many marks each is worth, and for each must-hit point a one-line note on how kids usually get it wrong.

Also please dont make it depend on knowing specific papers or authors, cause half of them cant look stuff up and i dont want it turning into a name-dropping contest.
