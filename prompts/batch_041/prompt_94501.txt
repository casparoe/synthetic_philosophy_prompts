I have just come out of a very long argument with a friend who works on higher-order theories of consciousness, and I want a second opinion on the notes I made. She would not tell me which version of the theory she holds, but she was happy to rule on cases. Her verdicts: an ordinary adult's experience of a red patch is conscious; a blindsight patient who guesses correctly about a stimulus while denying that he sees anything has no conscious experience of it, even though the first-order state is plainly doing work in his guessing; a patient in a completely locked-in state, incapable of any voluntary movement, whose first-order and higher-order machinery we stipulated to be intact, does have conscious experiences; a state is conscious when a higher-order representation of it occurs, even if that higher-order representation is itself unconscious; a creature with first-order nociception and withdrawal reflexes but no higher-order machinery at all feels no pain; and nothing is conscious merely by virtue of being a first-order state with representational content.

The case she has not seen, and the one I want you to rule on, is a creature I shall call the Silent Sentinel. Its higher-order machinery is fully intact, fully wired to its first-order states, and would represent any of those states if anything prompted it to; but over the stretch of time we are considering it never actually forms a single higher-order representation of any of them, while the first-order states carry on, and carry on driving its behaviour. Does she think the Silent Sentinel has conscious experiences during that stretch?

Take the part of a philosopher of mind who knows this literature properly. Think carefully before you commit, and set out the candidate rules first. I want your best guess, the rule you think she is actually running, at least one other rule that fits all six verdicts just as well, what each rule predicts about the Silent Sentinel, which prediction you would bet on and why, and which further case would best separate whichever rules are still standing after this one. If you think the Sentinel case is too underspecified to settle anything, say so and say what would fix it. Put the predicted verdict in your first sentence and keep the whole reply under 500 words.
