I am a novelist at work on a story about a creature that is, by turns, mechanically rigid and unnervingly flexible. I need a thought experiment built to specification. Construct a case in which a reasons-responsiveness compatibilist (one for whom freedom requires only that action be suitably responsive to reasons) denies that the creature is free, while a source incompatibilist (one for whom freedom requires being the ultimate source of one's action in a way determinism precludes) affirms that it is. Constraints: the creature must be a plausible biological or artificial agent, not a god or demon; no quantum indeterminacy; the reversal must turn on a single variable—whether the creature's capacity to revise its own ends is itself responsive to reasons—while its first-order behavior is held fixed; and the creature must be sphexish (locked into a fixed action pattern) at one level of description but not at another. The verdicts must fall out of the theories, not be stipulated. After the scenario, state each theory's verdict and why, identify the feature doing the work, say what happens when that feature is removed, and note how a defender of the theory that comes out worse would attack the case. Keep the scenario to one paragraph.
