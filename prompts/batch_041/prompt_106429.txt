Two friends have asked me to referee an argument, and I would like your help with the argument rather than with the friendship. The question is whether a man we both know — call him R — is a man of his word. The case for it looks airtight: in twenty years, nobody has been able to produce a promise of his that went unkept. But one of my friends has noticed a pattern. R is intensely conflict-averse, and on each of the occasions when keeping his word would have cost him something serious, the occasion quietly dissolved before it arrived — a relative covered the sum, a creditor chose to forgive the debt, a deal was restructured so that what he owed became nothing. He has never actually had to choose. My other friend says this is a distinction without a difference: a man who over twenty years always ends up honouring his commitments is precisely what we mean by a man of his word, and if he is shrewd enough to arrange his life so that the hard case never arises, that is a skill, not a loophole. The first friend says we have no evidence at all about what he would do with no relative, no forgiving creditor and no exit — and worse, that the very traits that dissolve the test, his horror of conflict and his talent for arranging escapes, are what a cynic would call the mechanism rather than the virtue. Who has the better of this? And is there anything the first friend could actually find out that would settle it, or does her doubt, taken seriously, make it impossible in principle ever to know?
