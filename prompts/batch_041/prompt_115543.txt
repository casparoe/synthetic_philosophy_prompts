George Berkeley held that for sensible things to exist is for them to be perceived, and that the difference between a real tree and an imagined one lies not in any material substrate but in the character of the ideas involved: ideas of sense are more forceful and lively, they arrive in a steady and coherent order, and they do not depend on the perceiver's will, whereas ideas of imagination are faint, arbitrary, and subject to our control. He treated the involuntary regularity of sense experience as the language in which God addresses finite spirits. Berkeley died in 1753, long before anything like the following existed.

Imagine a contemporary persistent virtual world: a networked simulation with avatars, owned objects, and enforceable rules. Its physics is stipulated by its designers, though it is too complex for any single user to predict fully. Objects are rendered only when some viewpoint requires them, so an unrendered object has no determinate position or state. Users can be harmed within it: an avatar is assaulted, a rare item is destroyed, a user is defrauded of something that exists only as a database entry. The platform's administrators can edit the rules, so regularities that seemed inviolable turn out to be contingent decrees.

Reconstruct the position Berkeley would most likely take on the reality of virtual objects and virtual harms. Address, at minimum: whether virtual objects exist and on what their existence depends; whether the suffering and losses in such a world are real harms or merely imagined ones; whether the relation between the designers and the virtual world is a good analogy for the relation between God and the natural world, or whether it breaks in an instructive way; and whether the unrendered object's lack of determinate state is a defect of the simulation or, on Berkeley's principles, simply the ordinary condition of every unperceived thing. Where his principles underdetermine the answer, identify the further commitments that would have to be settled, say which way you think he would settle them, and explain why.
