What I'd like is a tier list. Some items are recognizably drawn from Borges: his Berkeley-and-Hume-flavored claim that time is a mental projection; the parallel claim that the self is no more real than time; the denial that succession is anything over and above our perception of it; and the self-undermining turn in which he concedes that the refutation may be as unreal as what it refutes. Four others are my own inventions, which I've stated plainly so you can judge them on their merits: (a) that time is real but its direction is a fiction imposed by memory, so that order is objective while the arrow is not; (b) that Borges's claims about time are best read as claims about narrative rather than metaphysics — that all stories coexist and linear reading is the illusion; (c) that time is a relation among events rather than a container, and that Borges's error is to treat it as a medium; and (d) that the unreality of time and the unreality of the self stand or fall together, so that anyone who keeps a robust self should keep a robust time.
