Now retired from a career in the natural sciences, I have spent the last several years reading philosophy, and I have arrived at a position I would like you to try to refute. I hold that life is not a natural kind and that there is no fact of the matter about which things are alive: the criteria on offer — metabolic self-maintenance, self-reproduction, evolution by natural selection, thermodynamic self-organization — are defensible stipulations that carve the same middle range of cases differently, and the familiar boundary disputes (viruses and viroids, prions and plasmids, mitochondria, sterile hybrids such as the mule, spores and frozen embryos, self-replicating digital programs) are artefacts of whichever stipulation is in use rather than evidence of a hidden essence awaiting discovery.

What would count against this, ranked by how much it should move me? Is there a discovery that would show life to be a natural kind after all, and would it have to be a single property present in everything we pre-theoretically call alive and absent from everything we do not, or would something weaker suffice? How far does the comparison with water and gold extend: if a term's extension can be fixed by the stuff we originally pointed at and then handed over to empirical science, why does that move fail here, and what would the analogous discovery look like? Take two cases that pull the criteria apart — a system that maintains and repairs itself indefinitely on fuel but reproduces only by splitting, with no heritable variation, and a bare self-replicating polymer with no metabolism — and tell me which verdict each criterion delivers, and whether any of them feels like a misdescription rather than a stipulation. Do the contexts in which the concept must actually decide something (planetary protection, biosafety, patent law, the determination of death) tell for or against me? And if the position is insulated, because a conventionalist can always reassign an awkward case to a different concept, is that a defect of the position or of the question — would I do better to say that life is a genuinely vague cluster concept rather than a merely conventional one? I would be most moved by a case in which something has to be decided and there is a plain fact of the matter about the answer.
