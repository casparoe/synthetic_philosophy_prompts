Hey, I'm taking a seminar on markets and ethics this term and there's a pattern I keep bumping into that I dont have a word for, so I figured I'd ask someone who knows the literature.

The pattern is this. Whenever someone floats a new market in something — kidneys, surrogacy, paying kids for good grades, selling your spot in a queue — the objection that actually gets people riled up is never quite "this is unfair" or "this hurts the buyer." It's that the market changes what the thing *is*. The kidney becomes a spare part, the baby becomes a product, the place in line becomes an asset you can trade. And the people making this objection are usually not the ones buying or selling — they're bystanders who just feel the practice has become a different practice. The other half of it is that the objection seems to hinge on what the payment gets *called*. Pay a woman to have your baby and it's monstrous; pay her expenses and lost wages and it's fine. Offer a donor fifty grand and it's repugnant; "compensate" him for his time and risk and suddenly we're having a reasonable policy debate. So its not the money and it's not the amount — it's what the money is doing, or what it makes the thing turn into.

Is there a name for this exact thing? I know "commodification" is in the neighborhood, and I've heard "crowding out" (pay people for something and they stop doing it for its own sake), but crowding out seems to be about the motives of the people *in* the exchange, whereas whats bugging me is about what the thing comes to *mean*, including to people who aren't in the exchange at all. "Exploitation" is about one side getting a bad deal, which isn't it either. And "some things just shouldn't be for sale" feels like it's naming the verdict rather than the mechanism.

So what I'm after is: (1) is there a settled term, and if the closest ones are commodification / crowding out / corruption / incommensurability, can you say precisely where each of those misses what I'm describing; (2) if nothing quite fits, coin me something and define it; and (3) tell me where the edges are — is the "we're just covering her costs" version genuinely a different practice, or the same practice laundered, and is there any principled line between a fee for a service and a price for the thing itself, or is that line just whatever we happen to find gross?
