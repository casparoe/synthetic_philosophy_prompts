My old roommate got me into Nozick a while back, and the part of Anarchy, State, and Utopia I keep coming back to is the bit where he says we should treat animals on broadly utilitarian grounds while treating people as ends in themselves. Animals feel pain, their suffering counts, but they dont have the kind of rights that make it wrong to use them as a means, because what grounds rights for him is a capacity to shape your life according to some overall conception of what your life is about, to give it meaning, and animals lack that. I've basically swallowed this. It's tidy, it explains why I'm against factory farming but not against the idea of eating meat as such, and it doesn't force me to say a mouse has the same claim on me as a stranger does.

But I want to know what would count against it. Not the standard marginal cases stuff, I've heard that, and I have some reply that turns on the difference between a capacity that's absent altogether and one that's temporarily or accidentally undeveloped. I want the stranger stuff. Heres a specific thing I've been chewing on: suppose we find out that some corvid or cetacean has a genuinely temporally extended, articulated sense of its own life. It plans across decades, it grieves for a companion it lost years ago, it has something like a sense of what it wants its life to add up to. But it has no capacity to grasp moral reasons at all. Does Nozick's ground of rights apply to it? And then the flip side: imagine a being with full moral agency, responds to reasons, can be held responsible, but has no conception of its life as a whole, just lives moment to moment with no projects and no narrative. Does the view say that being has rights? If the answer is no in both cases, I want to know what that does to the view. If the answer is yes in the first case, I want to know whether the view is now doing something very different from what I thought it was doing.

So lay it out for me. What would count against the position, which objections are decisive and which are merely awkward, which ones a Nozickian would themselves concede, and whether the view actually risks anything or has been quietly immunized by qualifications. Rank them if you can. And be honest with me if it turns out this isn't really a view about animals at all but a view about the ground of rights that just happens to have animals as a footnote. Please write it as continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just the way you'd write it out in an email to a friend.
