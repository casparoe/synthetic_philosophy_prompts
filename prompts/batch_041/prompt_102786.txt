Ok, so I'm a retired chemist (spent 35 years doing spectroscopy, mostly) and I've spent the last few years reading philosophy, and I've gotten quite taken with this imprecise-probabilities business. I want to write down where I've landed and have someone tell me if it actually hangs together, because I have a nagging feeling that it doesn't but I honestly can't see where the seam is.

Here's my position, roughly:

1. A rational set of degrees of belief shouldn't expose you to a sure loss - no betting book that loses no matter what happens. That's the coherence constraint and I'm confident about it.

2. But I've come to think it's often dishonest to pretend you have one precise probability. If all I know is that the coin is either fair-ish or biased toward heads somehow, the honest thing is definately a whole set of distributions, i.e. an interval like [0.5, 0.9], not some made-up point value. Imprecise credences are legitimate and you shouldn't be forced to sharpen them.

3. When you learn something, you should just take every distribution in your set, condition it on what you learned, and keep the whole updated set. That seems like the obvious generalisation of Bayes and I don't see what else you'd do.

4. I recently read about a result that bothered me a lot: sometimes, when you learn that some event happened that you'd have sworn was completley irrelevent to X, your interval for X gets wider, not narrower. Learning irrelevant stuff makes you less sure? That strikes me as a reductio - if a framework says that, the framework is broken somewhere.

5. When I genuinely have no idea, I'm happy to use the principle of indifference or maximum entropy to pick a distribution. That's what we always did with degenerate priors in the lab.

So: does this hang together? I've got a nagging feeling some of these pull against each other but I can't see which ones, or whether it's just me being a novice. Don't just tell me it's all fine - I want to know which ones actually collide, which ones only look like they do, and what I'd have to give up.
