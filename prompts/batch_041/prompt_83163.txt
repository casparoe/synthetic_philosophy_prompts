ok so I'm supposed to write up my position on laws of nature for a seminar next week and I want a sanity check first, because I have a feeling it's not as coherant as I'd like. here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) the fundamental facts are just the local, non-modal ones — what's where, particles/fields at points, no necessary connections between distinct existences. the whole mosaic, nothing over and above it.
(2) laws supervene on the mosaic. two worlds with the same mosaic have the same laws, full stop.
(3) but laws aren't just any true generalization. "all the coins in my pocket are silver" doesn't have the kind of necessity that "all electrons repel" has.
(4) the right story about what a law *is* is the best-system one: laws are the axioms of the true deductive system that best trades off simplicity and strength against the whole mosaic.
(5) laws explain their instances, and they govern — the reason the regularity holds is that it's a law, not the other way round. the pattern doesn't make the law, the law makes the pattern.
(6) laws support counterfactuals, accidental generalizations don't.
(7) I think simplicity and strength are partly *our* standards, which makes me uneasy that the laws end up relative to us.
(8) I also think there's a possible world with exactly our mosaic in which all the same regularities hold but purely as coincidences, with no laws at all.

so: which of these actually pull against each other, how badly, and what's the cheapest revision? I suspect at least one of these pairs only *looks* like a conflict, so please don't just flag everything as equally bad — rank them and tell me what has to give.
