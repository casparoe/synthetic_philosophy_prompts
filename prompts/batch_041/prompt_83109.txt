You are a philosopher of technology and AI ethics. I want a rigorous analysis, not a survey. Consider a fictional but realistic AI lab, Meridian, whose deployment review has two separate gates. The first, the Intent Gate, tests a model on ambiguous but benign user requests; reviewers penalize the model whenever it substitutes what the user should want (safer, healthier, more prudent) for what the user actually asked for, even when the substitution would benefit the user. The second, the Values Gate, tests the same model on requests that are legal and within the user's rights but harmful to third parties or to the user; reviewers penalize the model whenever it complies. Meridian calls both gates alignment work and insists they express one commitment. Your task: reconstruct the conception of alignment each gate presupposes, say whether the two conceptions are consistent or merely co-satisfiable under special assumptions, and distinguish what each gate strictly requires from what it merely fits. Then address the narrow alignment problem: if alignment means getting a system to pursue the user's intent, what does the Values Gate add or change? If it means getting a system to pursue what is objectively valuable, what does the Intent Gate add or change? Finally, tell me what would have to be revised in the review process if Meridian adopted a purely intent-aligned standard, and what would have to be revised under a purely value-aligned standard. Begin with a short brainstorming section that lays out the rival readings before you commit to one, and be precise about the philosophical commitments concerning intention, value, deference, and paternalism that the practice cannot avoid.
