I'm a novelist — third book, and for once I'm trying to write about people who are actually good at their work instead of just tortured by it. The two leads are AI alignment researchers at the same lab: a funder got spooked after a bad deployment and shoved them onto a joint "values framework" working group, and they've been circling each other for months now. One of them thinks that if you're building a system to act in the world at scale, you basically have to build a utilitarian — maximize expected welfare, aggregate across everyone affected, accept that some people get traded off against the numbers. The other thinks that's precisely the design that ends in horror, and that what the system actually needs is deontological structure: constraints it won't trade away no matter how good the arithmetic looks on the other side. What I need from you is the document those two could actually co-sign at the end of the working group — not a summary of a debate, the thing itself, with both of them still holding their positions when they put their names to it. This scene is the hinge of the book, so it has to be good enough that a reader who works in the field wouldn't laugh at it.

So: what can they both sign? I'd like the agreed-claims section to cover the ground where they're actually on the same page — what the choice even is (is it about the system's decision procedure, or about what the humans deploying it can be justified in doing? I have a feeling they're partly talking past each other there), what the formal apparatus can and can't settle for them, and what failure modes each concedes on their own side: the utilitarian conceding that a system optimizing an aggregate is going to game whatever proxy you hand it, the deontologist conceding that a constraint set tight enough to be safe is also tight enough to bind catastrophically in the wrong case. Then the disagreements, stated so that each one is in wording its own side would accept — no stacking the deck. Then the cruxes, and for each crux a case or test both of them would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

I've been assuming there are at least two real cruxes and I want to know if I'm right. One is whether any deontological constraint set can be specified precisely enough to actually implement without the implementer quietly sliding back into weighing consequences at the margins — i.e. whether deontology collapses into consequentialism with a hard prior bolted on the front once you sit down and write the thing. The other is whether aggregate-optimizing systems are unavoidably Goodhart-prone in a way no amount of training or oversight fixes, so the real choice is between two failure modes rather than between right and wrong. If either of those turns out to be a verbal dispute rather than a substantive one, say so and say which part, because that changes what my characters are even fighting about. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a table for the disagreements with a column each for the crux, the shared test or case, and what each of them predicts about it.
