I am a backend engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I have somehow ended up in a long-running disagreement with a colleague who studied physics as an undergraduate before moving into software. He maintains that the question whether space is a genuine entity or merely a system of relations among bodies was settled by Newton, and that anyone still defending the relational view is refusing to look at the evidence. His argument is the bucket. Suspend a bucket of water from a twisted cord and let it spin: at first the bucket turns while the water sits still and the surface stays flat, and only later, as the water is dragged around until it is rotating along with the bucket, does the surface become concave and climb the sides. The concavity is therefore at its greatest precisely when the water is not moving relative to the bucket at all, and it cannot be chalked up to the water's motion relative to the room either, since you can spin the room along with it and get the same paraboloid. What the water is rotating relative to, on his account, is space itself. So space has to be something real and absolute, and relationism is dead.

I would like to know whether I should simply concede the point. If the experiment does what he says it does, why does the substantivalism versus relationism question keep turning up as a live topic in things I read, rather than as a closed historical episode? Is that just philosophers keeping a dead argument on life support, or is the bucket being asked to carry more weight than it can bear? I suspect there is a slide somewhere in his reasoning, but I cannot locate it, and I would rather understand precisely which claim the experiment establishes, which further claim he is quietly helping himself to, and what the strongest remaining relational reply actually amounts to. He also told me that general relativity changed everything and that there is something called the hole argument that people now argue about, which I could not follow at all, so if that is where the real disagreement lives, I would like to know how it connects to the bucket, if it does.

For context on what I am after: I do not want a reading list or a survey of who said what. I want to know whether his conclusion follows, what the honest state of the question is, and how I should think about it the next time it comes up over lunch.
