I have been trying to get clear on advance directives, and I think I have found the shape of the problem, though I suspect I have it backwards. It seems to me that everything turns on personal identity. If the woman now in the nursing home, who no longer recognizes anyone, is the same person who signed the directive ten years ago, then the directive is her own earlier decision about her own life and it binds; if she is not the same person — as I understand some philosophers hold, that identity is not what fundamentally matters — then the document comes from a stranger, and a stranger's preferences cannot govern her. On that picture the ethics is downstream of the metaphysics: settle whether identity survives the dementia, and the moral question answers itself. What I cannot fit into the picture is the case I keep returning to. She now seems content, and she added a clause to the directive saying that if she ever appeared content in that condition, her contentment was not to count. On my picture I have to decide which of the two of them is really her, and I notice that whenever I say it is the earlier one, I am quietly assuming the very authority I set out to derive. Which step should I give up, and if not identity, what is the authority of an advance directive resting on?
