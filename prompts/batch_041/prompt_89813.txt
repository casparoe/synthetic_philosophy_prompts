Here is the proposition I want a credence on. Take two people, A and B. A prefers situation X to situation Y, and B prefers situation X to situation Y (the situations may differ between them, and each may care about different things). The proposition is:

There is a fact of the matter, independent of any convention of measurement and independent of what anyone believes or would judge, about whether A's gain in preference-satisfaction (or in felt well-being) in moving from Y to X exceeds B's.

By "independent of any convention of measurement" I mean that the fact would not be created or destroyed by our choosing a different scale, a different zero point, or a different unit, and that it would hold even if no one ever measured or considered it. Give me your credence that this proposition is true, on a 0–1 scale.

Then give me the decomposition, not just the number. I want each of the following sub-questions to receive its own rough probability and a one-line justification, plus any further sub-question you think belongs:

Realism about utility: is there a genuine quantity — hedonic intensity, preference strength, whatever you take it to be — that exists in a person in a way analogous to, say, blood pressure, rather than being a bookkeeping device we project onto choices?
