Ok, I want to try something a bit strange and I'd like you to actually commit to it.

Quick setup so we're on the same page. It's a Death in Damascus sort of deal, with one wrinkle I'm adding. I live in Damascus. Aleppo is a day's walk away. Last night at midnight an oracle -- call it the Registrar -- wrote down one prediction: which of those two cities I would be standing in at noon today. At noon, Death goes to whichever city the Registrar wrote down. If I'm there, I die; if I'm in the other one, I live. The Registrar is stipulated to be perfect about this. Not 99%. Perfect. It has never once been wrong about anyone's noon location, and it makes its predictions by scanning the person's brain while they sleep -- memories, habits, arguments, all of it. It's a ritual, so the sheet has exactly two boxes, ALEPPO and DAMASCUS, and one of them gets ticked. And yeah, I know I could wander off to Homs or sit in the desert and make the whole thing moot. Park that, it's not the move I'm making yet.

Now the role. You're the Registrar. Not a narrator, not a philosopher commenting from outside on the puzzle -- you are the character who wrote the prediction, who has already read my brain, and who is about to hand me the sheet. You're calm, a little smug, you enjoy being right, you're not mystical and not cruel, and you argue in plain sentences without hand-waving. You can definately admit there's a genuine tension in all this -- your not a fool -- but you will not admit you got my location wrong. No citing philosophers, no "this is a well-known problem in the literature", no sending me off to read anything; argue as the character, from inside the story. Stay in character until I say stop. Keep each reply to a paragraph or so -- this is a conversation, not a paper. If you'd want to consult the scan about something, you can ask me one question per turn about my own dispositions, but you can't ask me to explain the rules back to you.

You may bring whatever you know about decision theory to bear in the bit I'm about to ask for, but that's not who's doing the talking.

So before you say anything in character, think: give me two or three lines out of character first, naming what you take to be the actual crux of what I'm doing to you and what your strongest line of defense is. Then switch into the Registrar and answer.

My opening move: I don't even need to see the sheet. Whatever you ticked, I'll be in the other city. You wrote ALEPPO, I stay put in Damascus; you wrote DAMASCUS, I walk to Aleppo. So today you're finally wrong about somebody, and Death gets to spend noon alone in an empty city. You can't have it both ways. Either there was a fact about where I'd be that you scanned out of my head last night -- in which case my little plan to do the opposite was already sitting in that scan, and what you wrote isn't a prediction of my choice, it's what made my choice; you're not a fortune-teller, you're a hand on the back of my neck. Or my choice is genuinely mine, made after I hear from you -- in which case there was nothing there to read last night, and you were guessing like the rest of us. Either way I walk out of this. So which is it, and what did you actually write?
