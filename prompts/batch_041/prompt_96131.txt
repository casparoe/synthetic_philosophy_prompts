I've been going round in circles on the anthropic stuff for years now, definately too long, and I want someone to extract the principle I'm actually using, because I suspect I havent got one. Treat my gut calls as data.

The incubator: a fair coin. Heads, I'm created alone in a white room. Tails, I and nine other people are created, one per room, ten identical soundproof rooms, no way to tell which is which and no contact between them. I wake up. Ten to one on tails, no question.

Same setup, except on tails the other nine are copies of me — same memories, same everything, all waking at once. Still ten to one. That they're copies rather than strangers doesn't shift anything.

Now the reverse: heads, only the people alive today exist; tails, the people alive today plus a trillion people born in the far future. I'm alive today either way. Fifty-fifty, I say. People I'll never meet don't get a vote. (Yes, I know that's close to the doomsday move, and I've always thought doomsday was bunk — I'm roughly the hundred-billionth human and I don't for a second think that means we're nearly finished.)

The presumptuous philosopher: I've got solid evidence for a theory on which there are a few billion observers over a theory on which there are 10^30. Someone says my existence is about 10^21 times likelier on the big theory, so the big theory wins. No it doesn't.

Boltzmann brains: I'm meant to be a fleeting brain rather than a real person, because fleeting brains vastly outnumber real people. Also no.

And the one I genuinely can't call: in the incubator, do the extra nine have to be conscious? If on tails they're nine unconscious bodies, or nine very dim minds, does the ten-to-one survive?

So what's the principle? I've got exactly one verdict where "there are more of them, so it's probably them" feels obviously right, and I'm rejecting that move everywhere else. Either there's a principled line splitting the incubator off — identical rooms? contemporaries? copies? — or the incubator verdict has to go. I know the moves have names, self-sampling this and self-indication that, but naming them hasn't helped, and the reference class always seems to be doing work nobody accounts for. Give me the principle, or the smallest set of them, each clause pinned to the case that forces it; say what it predicts for cases I haven't given you (do the extra observers have to be people like me? does any of it survive infinite numbers? what if they're in other universes entirely?); and be honest about which of my verdicts it just can't keep. British English, please.
