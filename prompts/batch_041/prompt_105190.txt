ok so this is a weird one but hear me out. i've somehow ended up as one of the moderators of this fairly large online community (long story, mostly an accident) and i've been in a running argument with the other mods about how we should think about our own power over people's posts. the majority view on the mod team is basically "don't interfere unless someone's actually being hurt — leave stuff up, don't touch it, everyone stays free." i think that's wrong, so i wrote up a short essay-ish thing arguing why, and i want you to tear it apart before i post it. below is the gist, not the full text.

my argument. i've been reading about the difference between negative and positive liberty — berlin's old distinction, freedom as nobody stopping you vs freedom as being your own master — and then the neo-republican stuff (pettit, skinner, that crowd) where the key idea is supposed to be non-domination rather than non-interference. as i understand it the republican point is that you're unfree not only when someone actually interferes with you but when someone has the *power* to interfere with you arbitrarily, even if they never exercise it. the standard illustration is the slave with a kind master: no actual interference is happening, and the slave is still not free.

that's my case for how we should run the place. the problem isn't whether we delete a post — it's that we *can*, at our own discretion, with no real accountability or recourse, and that's true whether or not we ever actually do it. so "we left your post up" doesn't mean you weren't dominated. and the fix isn't "less moderation," it's "non-arbitrary moderation": rules the members have some say in and can appeal against.

things i'm unsure about and want you to weigh in on:

- is my reading of the republican view right? especially the "arbitrary" part — everything seems to hang on what counts as arbitrary, and i worry i'm just smuggling in my own definition (roughly: not answerable to the people affected). is that a legit reading or am i making it up?
- someone on the mod team will say this is just negative liberty with extra steps, i.e. that non-domination collapses back into non-interference once you look closely. is that a real objection or a cheap one?
- i have a vague memory that the later republican work leans on "contestability" — that it's enough if you *can* contest a decision, you don't need to have consented in advance — but i might be misremembering. is that a thing, and does it rescue my account or is it a cop-out?
- i also have a half-baked extra thought that we need a fourth thing here — call it "epistemic liberty," the freedom not to have your sense of what's true shaped by people who can punish you for disagreeing with them. is that a real distinction or am i reinventing something that already has a name? please be honest if it's the latter.

when you comment, be clear about which bits are actually settled among people who work on this and which are live disagreements — i don't want a confident summary that makes it sound like there's a consensus when there isn't. and if my central argument is just a known objection with a name already attached, say so. also tell me if it's boring.
