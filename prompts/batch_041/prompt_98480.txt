You are a philosopher of logic and mathematics, with deep expertise in formal systems, self-reference, and the limits of formalization, and with a solid working knowledge of computability theory. I would like you to perform a careful translation exercise for me. Because the value of the exercise lies in what the translation fails to carry, I care far more about fidelity and honesty than about a tidy result; resist the temptation to make the mapping look smoother than it really is. Before you write your answer, think the problem through in a scratch area: enumerate the primitives of the target vocabulary, enumerate the claims of the source material, and only then attempt the mapping item by item.

Source material. Take the following as given; do not rely on recalling any particular text, and do not treat the exercise as an opportunity to cite literature.

- A formal system, often called MIU, over the symbols M, I, and U. Its only axiom is the string MI, and it has four rewriting rules: append a U to any string that ends in I; double everything after the initial M; replace any occurrence of III with a single U; and delete any occurrence of UU.
- The string MU cannot be derived from MI by any sequence of these rules. This is established not by searching within the system but by reasoning about the system: one finds an invariant. Counting the I's, the initial string has one I; doubling a number not divisible by three keeps it not divisible by three, and subtracting three preserves that too, so no derivable string has a number of I's divisible by three, whereas MU has none. In fact a complete criterion is available: a string is derivable from MI just in case it begins with exactly one M, contains only M, I, and U, and has a number of I's not divisible by three.
- What interests me is the move by which one establishes such facts: reasoning about a formal system from a vantage outside it, rather than deriving theorems within it. This move is sometimes called jumping out of the system. Note also that the outside vantage, once described, can itself be treated as a system, which can in turn be jumped out of, and so on.

My vocabulary.
