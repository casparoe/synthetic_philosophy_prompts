My cousin Nour is blind and gets around by clicking her tongue and listening to the echoes. She's convinced she's proved her echolocation is reliable, and she thinks that means she can know she isn't being fed fake echoes by some machine — and honestly I can't see where the trick is. Here's her argument, cleaned up a bit:

(1) When Nour clicks and it perceptually seems to her that there's a wall about two meters ahead, she's justified in believing there's a wall about two meters ahead. That's just ordinary perceptual belief.
(2) On that same occasion, she's justified in believing that her echolocation delivered that verdict — she can tell, from the inside, what her own perceptual state is.
(3) So she's justified in believing the conjunction: my echolocation said 'wall, two meters' and there is a wall two meters ahead.
(4) That conjunction is true exactly when her echolocation got it right. So it's a genuine check on the faculty.
(5) She does this many times — the door, the fridge, the curb, a parked car — and each time she ends up justified in the corresponding conjunction.
(6) So by induction she's justified in believing her echolocation is reliable, i.e. right most of the time.
(7) If you're justified in believing your perception is reliable, you're justified in believing you're not being systematically decieved about your surroundings.
(8) So Nour can know she's not being fed fake echoes, using only her echolocation and no independent evidence about it at all.

The last line is what bugs me. It feels like it hands you knowledge of your own reliability for free, and if it works for her echoes it works for my eyes, and then nobody could ever be a brain in a vat, which seems like it should be hard rather than free. But each step on its own looks fine and I can't find the broken one. Which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? I'd rather keep (1) and (2) — ordinary perception, and knowing what you seem to perceive, both seem non-negotiable to me — and I'd rather not give up (7). So tell me where the argument goes wrong and what I'm paying, and tell me how confident you are in the diagnosis and which bit of it you're least sure about.
