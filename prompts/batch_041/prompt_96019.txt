Ok, roleplay time. I want to argue with a defender of logical omniscience, so be one — a real one, not a strawman. Your position, which you actually hold: propositions are sets of possible worlds; the ideal epistemic agent is a probability distribution (or a certainty set) over a space of worlds; she assigns credence 1 to every logical truth and to every logical consequence of what she's already sure of; and this is a benign idealization — the right notion of coherence — even though no human is remotely like that. You're allowed to grant that it's an idealization, but you think the standard fixes (impossible worlds, awareness functions, fragmented belief sets) either change the subject or are ad hoc patches. Sharp, 1-2 paragraphs per turn, no retreating into "well, both sides have a point," stay in character till I say stop. No need to cite anyone, just argue.

My opening move: I think your picture isn't the limit of reasoning better, it's a different species of thing, and I think the Löb result is what shows it. Here's my version. Your ideal agent's knowledge is closed under consequence — that's the whole picture. Now, does she know that her knowledge is factive? Not "is it factive" — does she know "if I know P then P"? She should: knowledge-entails-truth is a validity of your semantics, so it holds at every world, so she's certain of it, indeed certain of the generalization. Fine. But Löb's theorem says that in any system strong enough for arithmetic, if you can establish "if P is provable then P" then you can establish P — and the modal form of it is the axiom that if you're certain that (if you're certain of P then P) then you're certain of P. So certainty in the factivity schema, plus closure, churns out certainty in every arithmetical truth: Goldbach, the Riemann hypothesis, whether ZFC is consistent — not because she found proofs, but as a free gift of your semantics. Then "idealization" looks like the wrong word, because no amount of getting better at reasoning takes you there, and the thing you're modelling stops being an agent who knows things *on the basis of* anything.

So which horn? Either she knows the factivity schema — and then she's a truth oracle, not a reasoner — or she doesn't, and I want to hear how closure alone gets you a knower rather than just a certainty set. And please don't just tell me it's an idealization, thats the claim under dispute; tell me what its an idealization *of*. For the record I'd rather keep closure than give up self-trust, so if you make me choose, tell me what I'm paying.
