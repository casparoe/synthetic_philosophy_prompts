I'm in this seminar on death and the good life — mostly grad students plus one very opinionated emeritus professor who loves telling us what's bad for whom — and I have to present next week, which means predicting his verdict on my case before I even open my mouth, or he'll pounce. The trouble is I cant work out which theory of death's badness he's actually running. He's definitely no Epicurean; he thinks some deaths really are bad for the person who dies. But here's what he's said so far. A healthy 25-year-old, engaged, halfway through a PhD she loved, killed in a car crash: bad for her, obviously. A man in an irreversible coma, no awareness, no chance of coming back, whose body finally gives out: not bad for him at all — he said there's nobody there to lose anything. A 50-year-old with an untreatable illness whose remaining months would be nothing but pain, who begs for it to end, dies: not bad for her, he says, maybe even a relief. A 30-year-old killed instantly and painlessly in her sleep, never knowing, with a good life and a pile of unfinished projects she cared about: bad for her. A 95-year-old, still sharp, who's had a wonderful life but is frantic to finish the book she's writing, dies before she can: bad for her. A man who would have spent his remaining twenty years in a grey flat fog — no pleasure, no pain, no desires either way — dies: not bad for him. (He also waved away the idea that you can be harmed after you die; once you're gone, he says, there's no you left to harm.)

Now my case. An 85-year-old in good health, at peace, no unfinished business, nothing left she wants to do — she'd tell you she's had her life and she's ready to go — dies quietly in her sleep. The thing is, she actually had several more years of quiet, genuinely pleasant life ahead of her, nothing dramatic, no big highs or lows, just good unremarkable years, and she had no idea. So: what does he say, what rule is he actually running, and what case should I put to him next to nail it down? My hunch is that two different rules fit every verdict he's given so far and they come apart on mine, but I honestly can't tell which one he'd go for.
