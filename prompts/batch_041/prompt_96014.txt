I work on the engineering side of machine learning and have no formal training in philosophy. My team routinely attributes beliefs and knowledge to the models we build — we say the model believes the database is empty, or knows where the credentials are cached — and we have started to disagree about whether we are entitled to talk that way. I recognize that this is the old problem of the intentional stance, but what I want is an agenda, not an introduction. Suppose you had a year and a small group — a few engineers and two philosophers — to work on the conceptual problems of attributing beliefs to AI systems. Which open questions should we take first, and why? Defend the ordering against criteria you state explicitly: how many other questions depend on the one being settled, how tractable it is without waiting on new empirical results, and whether settling it would actually change what we do when we write evaluations, assemble safety cases, or describe what an interpretability finding has shown.

To keep this concrete rather than abstract, I would want the agenda to place three puzzles I keep running into.

First: we can paraphrase the same question thirty ways and the model answers consistently. Does that license a belief attribution, or does it only show a stable disposition to emit text? I want to know what a defensible behavioral criterion would even look like, and whether any candidate criterion survives scrutiny.

Second: the model will assert that a piece of code contains a bug and then complete that code without avoiding it, so its stated and its revealed commitments come apart. Is that a failure of the attribution, or evidence that we should be attributing something weaker than belief?

Third: the model retains nothing across contexts, so whatever it believes is re-derived on each forward pass. Is belief attribution to such a system coherent at all, or does the vocabulary have to be replaced before the other questions can even be asked?

Tell me which of these is upstream of which, which are distractions I should drop, and what argument or result over the next year would move a question up or down your list.
