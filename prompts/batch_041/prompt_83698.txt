I'm a doctoral student in New Testament studies, and I want to write a diagnostic piece on the impasse in historical Jesus research rather than another reconstruction. For decades the criteria of authenticity — dissimilarity, embarrassment, multiple attestation, coherence, and the various linguistic and environmental criteria — have been attacked as circular, as smuggling in Protestant theological preferences, and as self-defeating, since dissimilarity strips away precisely what would make a saying distinctive of Jesus; several scholars have declared the criteria dead, yet they keep being used and defended, and the field has not converged. I want a diagnosis of why this debate has stalled and what kind of disagreement it really is. Is it a dispute about how the criteria are applied, about what they could ever license, about whether authenticity is even the right target — a rift between those who want the historical Jesus and those who want the memory of Jesus — or a theological disagreement in disguise about whether the Christ of faith can be detached from the Jesus of history? My hypothesis is that the camps are not really disputing the evidence but what kind of object the Gospels are: testimony to be sifted for kernels, or social memory to be read as it stands. Test that against what each side actually claims, and tell me what would count as progress — a distinction, a reframing of the question, or an argument that none should be expected. I don't want a survey of the criteria or a list of who said what; I want the structure of the disagreement.
