Not sure if this is a real question or me just being confused about terminology, but here goes. There's a shape of disagreement I keep running into and I dont have a name for it.

Both sides agree on every checkable fact. They both agree that the word at the centre of the fight — 'person', 'driver', 'speaker', 'fruit' — has no settled answer for the case in question, and neither is under any illusion about that. And yet they argue as though one of them is right about how the world is, and whoever wins is the side whose usage ends up in the statute, the form, the intake questionnaire. The old customs fight over whether a tomato is a fruit is the one I always come back to, and lately it's fights over whether a corporation is a 'speaker' or whether a software system is a 'driver'.

I know people call this sort of thing a verbal dispute, and I know there's a literature on arguing about what a word should mean. Neither quite lands for me. A verbal dispute sounds like something you can shrug off, and this isnt shrug-offable — the losing side gets treated as simply wrong, sometimes for decades, and it changes what actually happens to people. But it's also not someone proposing a new and better concept, because neither side thinks theyre proposing anything; each thinks they are just reporting what the word obviously means.

So: is there a name for this, and if not, coin one. Either way I want the edges, not just a label. What has to be true for a disagreement to be this, rather than an ordinary factual dispute with a word stuck on top of it, or a straightforward disagreement about which concept we ought to be using?
