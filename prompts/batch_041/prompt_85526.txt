I'm putting together the probset for my Kant seminar and I want one exercise where students have to build a case rather than recite the four textbook ones (false promise, suicide, wasted talents, refusing to help — all off limits). Write the question, a model answer of about 500 words, and a marking scheme out of 20.

The exercise: construct a single ordinary-life scenario, with the agent's maxim in the standard "I will do X in order to Y" form, such that universalizing that maxim yields a contradiction in conception only on the practical reading — the practice the maxim leans on can't survive everyone acting on it — while a strict logical reading finds no contradiction at all. Ideally the action is one most people think is fine or even kind. The obvious cheat is to over-generalize the maxim until a contradiction pops out, so the answer has to defend its level of description and say what happens when the same maxim is restated one notch narrower and one notch wider. Then run the universalization both ways, name the feature of the case doing the work, and say whether the case embarrasses FUL specifically or the categorical imperative as a whole — does the Formula of Humanity give a clean verdict here, and if so does that rescue anything? Finish with the strongest Kantian reply and what it costs them. In the marking scheme, seperate an A from a B by the specific moves each has to contain, and note what a merely competent answer typically gets wrong.
