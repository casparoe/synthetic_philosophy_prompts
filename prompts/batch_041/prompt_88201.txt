I've been in a small reading group on thermodynamics and information and we keep going around in circles on Maxwell's demon, and I want an outside take on why.

Here's the shape of it, which everyone knows. Demon sits at a trapdoor, sorts fast molecules to one side and slow to the other, and you get a temperature difference for free, which looks like a straight violation of the second law. Then someone says: fine, but the demon had to measure each molecule, store the result, and eventually its memory fills up and it has to erase, and erasure costs kT ln 2 per bit, so once you count the erasure the books balance and the second law survives. Half the group treats that as the end of the story -- Landauer's principle exorcises the demon, done. The other half says the accounting is rigged. Their line is that the erasure cost is itself derived from the second law, or from assumptions that already smuggle it in, so using it to rescue the second law is circular -- you've assumed the thing you're trying to prove, and the demon was never really defeated, just redefined out of existence. Nobody's moved in months and the more we talk the more I suspect we're not even disagreeing about the same thing.

My hypothesis, and I want you to tell me if it's right: this isn't really a physics dispute. It's a dispute about where to draw the boundary of "the system." The demon's memory is either part of the environment, in which case its entropy counts and the second law holds, or it's part of the demon, in which case the demon is a bounded agent and the erasure is a cost it pays -- and I don't think anything physical decides which way to draw that line. And underneath that, maybe a disagreement about what entropy in statistical mechanics even is: a fact about the world (an objective randomness) versus a fact about our ignorance. If the second law is really a statement about what we don't know, then "exorcising" the demon with an information cost is close to trivially true and tells us nothing about the world; if it's about the world, then the erasure argument has to be a genuine physical claim, and the circularity objection actually bites. Those two readings make the whole debate look different, and I bet most of the shouting is people sliding between them.

So what I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. Is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one about the word "system" or "entropy," or two different questions wearing one name -- one about whether an idealized device can beat the second law, and one about whether thermodynamics is epistemic through and through? Work it out from what each side is actually committed to, using the one-molecule Szilard setup or whatever concrete version makes the commitments clearest. And then tell me honestly what would count as progress: a formal result, a distinction that makes the circularity worry precise instead of just gesturing at it, a version of the demon both sides agree is exorcised, or a real argument that no such thing is possible and the impasse is just permanent. No reading list please, I can't look anything up right now -- I want the diagnosis and the argument for it.
