I have recently joined the board of a small open-source software foundation, and I am trying to make sense of a pattern we keep hitting: maintainers burn out, security researchers race to disclose, forks compete on features nobody wants, and every participant insists they are only doing what they must. I would like to hand the board a diagnostic taxonomy of the positions people actually hold about these so-called Moloch traps, so that we can stop arguing past one another. Please write a decision tree or taxonomy of the positions on the question: what is the fundamental structure of a multipolar trap, and what, if anything, escapes it? Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names. Each branch node should be a question that separates the positions—for example, whether the trap is a prisoner's dilemma, a coordination failure, a tragedy of the commons, a commitment problem, a status or zero-sum competition, or a dynamic in which the option set itself shrinks. Each leaf should carry the name of the position (or 'unnamed' if nobody defends it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Include the main escape routes: centralized authority or Leviathan, binding commitments, norms and reputational mechanisms, exit and voice, technological fixes, changing the payoff matrix, changing preferences or values, and the deflationary view that 'Moloch' is not one thing at all. Flag any leaves your tree cannot separate, and note which combinations of answers are unoccupied and why—whether they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Because our foundation is a volunteer body with no enforcement power, wherever a position depends on a central authority, say what it would look like without one. Aim for about ten to twelve branch questions and no more than fifteen leaves, and phrase every node as a question a board member could answer about our situation without already knowing which position it leads to. Please do not give citations, reading lists, or references; I want the diagnostic structure itself.
