I've ended up on the board of a small land trust near me — we own about 40 acres, and the whole thing has turned into a fight between the people who want it left as habitat and the people who want houses on it (our county is definately desperate for housing). I'm the one stuck in the middle, and everyone keeps telling me to "just find a compromise," which has started to seem like a much stranger idea than it did a month ago.

Here's what I've been chewing on. A normal split-the-difference compromise gives each side half of what it wants. But I read something about moral trade a while back arguing that compromise between people with different values can be more like trade than like splitting: if I care alot about X and only a little about Y, and you're the reverse, we can each give up the thing we care about less and both end up better off than at 50/50. That seems to need a double coincidence of wants and some way of comparing the two things, and I genuinely don't know whether values are the kind of thing that can be exchanged at all. Other bits I've half-understood and can't fit together: whether some commitments can't be traded without corrupting the person who trades them (like buying your way out of a principle), whether a bad thing can be "offset" the way a carbon offset offsets a flight, whether people with different values should just live in separate spheres and each pursue their own thing rather than blending, and whether agreeing to negotiate over something already concedes part of it.

I've got four or five months before the vote and maybe three or four hours a week I can actually give to reading and thinking about this, on top of the boring work of running meetings. So: which of the questions in that cluster deserve those hours, and in what order? Which ones would actually change what I do in the room, and which are philosophy I'd enjoy and never use? Which would you demote as sounding deep but not touching the decision? Give me a committed ranking with your reasons — I'd rather have a definite ordering I might disagree with than a balanced survey. If you think the trade framing is a dead end for a case like this, say so and say why, and tell me what would have to happen (at the next meeting, or in how the other side behaves) to move something up or down the list.
