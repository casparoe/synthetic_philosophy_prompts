I'm a second-year PhD student in political theory (I hang around the philosophy dept a lot) and I'm trying to write down my own view on distributive justice before it goes in a chapter, and I've got a nagging feeling it doesnt quite hang together but I can't pin down where. Could you audit it for me?

Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. I'm a luck egalitarian. An inequality is only just if it traces back to people's own choices. So I want to compensate people for brute bad luck — illness, a bad start in life, being born somewhere awful — but I don't think we owe anyone a rescue from the consequences of a gamble they knowingly took.

2. Natural talent doesn't ground desert. Being born with a fast brain or a good ear is as morally arbitrary as being born into money, so the market paying a talented person more doesn't mean they deserve more.

3. But effort does deserve reward. Same job, one person busts their ass, the other coasts — the hard worker deserves more of the proceeds, even if the coasting is arguably not the coaster's fault.

4. I'm completely fine with surgeons earning many multiples of what checkout staff earn, and I don't think that's a shameful concession to greed — it's just what you have to pay to get people to do the training and the work.

5. A just society has to keep the worst off from ending up worse off than they'd be under a flatter arrangement — something in the neighbourhood of Rawls's difference principle. But the claim is on institutions, not on individuals: a talented person who holds out for market rate isn't violating anything, even if a more self-denying choice would help the poor more.

6. I don't think the worse off can demand that the talented work harder for them. That looks like conscripting someone's life for other people's ends and I'm against it on liberal grounds.

7. At the same time I'm not willing to let the imprudent starve. I don't actually want a society where someone who blew their savings on a bad bet ends up on the street, even if they chose it.

My worry is that 2 and 3 pull against each other, or maybe 1 and 3, and I keep flip-flopping on whether 4 is consistent with 1. And 5 and 6 feel like they might be letting the talented off the hook in a way 1 wouldn't want.

Can you tell me which of these are actually in tension and which only look like they are? For the real ones, how bad is each, and what's the cheapest repair? I'd much rather hear which single commitment is doing the damage than get a list where everything is equally guilty. And if a pair that looks contradictory is actually fine, please say why — I suspect I'm muddling something.
