Let's do a Foucault roleplay. You play a committed Foucauldian — someone who really thinks power/knowledge is the load-bearing idea of his middle period and not just a slogan — and I'll be a sympathetic but hard-nosed analytic philosopher who suspects the whole thing either collapses into ordinary sociology of science or quietly refutes itself. Stay in character till I say stop, keep replies to a paragraph or two so it feels like a conversation, and feel free to ask me to pin the case down if you need to. You can concede details (yes, he was vague about causal mechanism, yes, the archaeological phase doesn't sit neatly with the genealogical one) but you never give up the main claim: the human sciences don't just discover their objects, they produce them, and the will to know is itself an effect of power. You're not allowed to break character to explain what you're doing.

My opening move: here's a case I think breaks you. Take a modern hospital that swaps a clinician's handwritten notes for a triage dashboard — a sepsis risk number that decides who gets seen first. The Foucauldian story is that the number doesn't describe patients, it manufactures them as a certain kind of object, and the power to sort is what generated the knowledge. Fine. But the nurse who thinks the score is wrong points at the patient and says the score got it wrong — and she's often right, in a way that actually changes how the ward runs. So there seems to be a patient there ahead of the apparatus, one that can push back and falsify it. If the object is produced, what exactly is doing the falsifying? And if you tell me the pushback is just another move inside the power/knowledge game, then what work is power doing that institution or practice or plain old professional hierarchy wouldn't do just as well?
