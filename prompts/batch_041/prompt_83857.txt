I'm closing a two-day workshop on measurement — half the room works on evaluation for machine-learning systems, half on performance indicators in public services — and I've decided to end with a poem rather than a summary slide. I'd like you to write it. The form should carry the thought rather than decorate it.

The thought is the one usually filed under Goodhart's law: a measure gets introduced as a way of tracking some good, everyone starts steering by it, and it stops tracking the good. What I want the poem to hold onto is that this is not one failure but four, and that the four get run together constantly:

- The measure correlates with the good without being it, so selecting on the measure also selects for whatever gap is left over — and the harder you press, the wider that gap gets, unavoidably.
- The correlation was established in the range we happen to have experience of, and stops holding once we push past it: the measure flattens, saturates, or turns around.
- The measure is a downstream sign of the good, so acting on the measure directly cuts the causal chain that made it informative in the first place.
- The measure is being optimized by parties whose aims are not those of whoever set the target, so it gets satisfied to the letter while the point goes missing.

The form I want is a pantoum, in the voice of the measure itself: first person, unsentimental, not a villain and not sorry for itself, just exact about what it has become. Four quatrains, like this:

A1 B1 A2 B2
B1 C1 B2 C2
C1 D1 C2 D2
D1 A2 D2 A1

— lines two and four of each quatrain becoming lines one and three of the next, and the last quatrain taking its second and fourth lines from the first quatrain's third and first, so that the poem ends on the line it began with.

The reason for the pantoum is that in this form every line gets spoken exactly twice. Make that do the work. No line should mean the same thing on its second hearing as on its first, and the change has to come from what the intervening lines have done to it, not from any change in the words. The first line, which returns as the last, is the crux: it should read as a promise at the top of the poem and as a diagnosis at the bottom, and a listener should be able to say exactly what shifted.

Each of the four quatrains should be enacting one of the four mechanisms above, in whatever order serves the poem — don't name them, don't gloss them inside the poem, and don't let any quatrain announce which one it is.

It will be read aloud to a mixed room, so keep the lines close to equal in length and let the repeats land in the ear, in plain words throughout. No named laws and no named effects, no specification gaming, no reward hacking, and nothing lifted or quoted from an existing text.

Give me a title, then the poem, then no more than four sentences I can say when I introduce it — which quatrain enacts which mechanism, and where the first line turns.
