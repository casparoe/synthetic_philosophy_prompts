Hey, I've been going down a rabbit hole on prioritarianism vs egalitarianism and I've definately convinced myself there's a problem, though I can't tell if I'm just confused. The usual story is that prioritarianism escapes the levelling-down objection -- the one where you make the better off worse off without helping anybody, purely to close the gap, and egalitarianism is supposedly stuck calling that an improvement. Prioritarianism is meant to be immune because it isn't comparing people; it just cares how well off each person is on their own, and weights the worse off more. Ok fine. But it does give extra weight to the worse off, right? So picture a weighting where the weight you put on the better off drops off a cliff as their level rises -- or take the extreme where the worst off get absolute, lexical priority and basically nothing above them counts at all. My worry: if the better off's level barely registers, isn't the prioritarian going to be pretty much fine with dragging them down toward the poor? Not because equality is good, just because their loss hardly counts for anything. And if that's right, doesn't prioritarianism end up being egalitarianism wearing a hat after all, inheriting the exact objection it was invented to dodge? What am I missing -- is there something in the structure of the view that stops this from happening?
