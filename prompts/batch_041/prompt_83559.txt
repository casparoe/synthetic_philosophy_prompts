Ok so I'm a junior and I somehow got volunteered to run the philosophy club meeting next Thursday. It's 45 minutes after school, in the chem lab because the library is being used for testing, and there's usually like 17 or 18 of us, plus Mr. Alvarez who sits in the back and grades papers. Everyone hated it last time when I talked for twenty minutes about free will, so I want to do a game where they actually have to make choices and I barely talk at all. The thing I'm obsessed with right now is the unilateralist's curse: the idea that when a bunch of people can each, on their own, do a thing that affects everybody else, and each of them has their own private read on whether that thing is actually good for the group (their read being decent but not perfect, with people erring in both directions), then the more people who are in a position to do it, the more likely it is that at least one of them does it in a case where it turns out to be a disaster. And the really annoying part is that nobody has to be dumb or selfish, everyone's trying to do the right thing for everyone, and the obvious fix, like requiring everybody to agree first or picking one designated person to decide, also makes it way less likely that the group ever does the good version of the thing. So I want them to leave thinking that every single person in the room made a reasonable call and it still went bad, and that the fix isn't free either.

So design me the game. Rules, materials (I have a printer, index cards, envelopes, and a bag of lollipops as prizes, we can't use real money), how each person gets their own private info so they have a genuine reason to think the action is a good idea, and a scoring system in points or lollipops, with the bad outcome landing on the whole group and not just on whoever acted. The main thing is they should walk into the problem themselves: we play it, the group gets wrecked by one person's decision, and only after that do I say what actually just happened, and then we play a second round with some kind of conformity rule so they can feel that the fix costs something too. After that I want a debrief that goes back through the specific moments of the game and lines them up with the parts of the idea, like this is where you're being completely reasonable, this is where the number of people who could act is doing the damage. Also give me a heads up on what will definately go wrong the first time I run it, since I've never run a game before and I'm worried it'll either be total chaos or everyone just refuses to pull the trigger and we get no data at all. And please be honest about which part of the curse the game can't actually reproduce and that I'll have to just explain out loud.
