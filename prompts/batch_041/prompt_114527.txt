My two housemates have been arguing all week about low-impact AI — the idea that you might keep an AI out of trouble by getting it to avoid changing the world much, without first having to write down what you actually want from it. I've somehow ended up as referee and I cant tell who's winning. Can you adjudicate it move by move?

Sam says you can define impact with no values at all. Pick a baseline — the agent doing nothing, or the agent doing whatever the human would have done — and let impact be the distance between what happens and that baseline. Side effects are just the deviations. No appeal to good or bad anywhere.

Rae says the baseline is exactly where the values sneak in. Doing nothing isn't neutral: an agent that stands still while the kitchen burns down has caused a side effect, and a bad one. And 'what the human would have done' is worse, since computing it means modelling the human's preferences, which is the value spec Sam claimed to skip. Even distance isn't neutral — calling a moved mug small and a deleted file big already ranks what matters.

Sam: you never get a full spec and you don't need one. Low impact is a proxy, and proxies get to be partial; every safety measure encodes something. What matters is whether the proxy stays sane when the agent optimizes against it.

Rae: that's the whole problem. Once the agent optimizes the proxy, the proxy's unstated assumptions become its values. An agent maximizing similarity to what the human would have done turns into a human-imitator, which is exactly wrong when the human would have ignored the fire. And you can't fall back on corrigibility, because corrigibility is itself value-laden.

Go move by move — who's right on each step, not just overall — and then say whether any version of an impact measure comes close to value-free (forbidding irreversible changes, forbidding the agent to create new agents, requiring it to keep options open) and what even those quietly presuppose. Start with your overall verdict in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble.
