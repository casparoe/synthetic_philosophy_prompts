Ok so my buddy and I got into a long thing last night about the death penalty and now I want to check whether my position actually hangs together or whether I was just talking. Here's where I've landed:

1. I'm a retributivist, at least about murder. Some killings are so bad that death is the only thing that fits. I don't mean "death deters" — I mean desert. A guy who tortures and kills a child has it coming, and a life sentence is a discount on what he's owed.
2. But I'm against the death penalty as an actual policy. We get it wrong. We've executed people who were innocent and we will again, and one is too many.
3. That said, I don't think life without parole is nothing. It's a serious punishment, and I don't think it's a "soft" substitute or that it fails to take the crime seriously.
4. The deterrence research doesn't move me either way. It's a mess, and even if capital punishment did deter, that wouldn't fix the problem in (2).
5. And I'm not one of those people who thinks the state killing anyone is inherently wrong. If I could be absolutely certain of guilt, I don't think I'd have a principled objection to it.

My friend says I can't have all of this. His line is that the retributivist in (1) is doing all the real work and should push me toward supporting the death penalty, and that (2) is really a consequentialist argument I'm smuggling in without admitting it. He also pounced on the combo of (2) and (5) — said it's incoherent to be against it because we might get it wrong while also saying I'd be fine with it in the certain case. I think he's wrong on that one but I couldn't say exactly why, and honestly I'm not sure anymore which of these is the load-bearing one.

So: which of these actually clash, which only look like they clash, and which has to go? Rank the tensions by how bad they are, tell me the cheapest fix for each, and then tell me what I lose by taking it. Don't just lay out the options and leave it to me — take a side and tell me which commitment I should drop, and why that one rather than the others.

One more thing I keep going back and forth on: is (3) secretly doing a lot of work for (2)? Like, my whole reason (2) feels tolerable is that I think life without parole is a real punishment — but if I really believed (1), that death is the only thing that fits, then wouldn't (3) be false, and wouldn't (2) start looking like a rationalization? Tell me if I'm overthinking that or if that's actually the crack in the whole thing.
