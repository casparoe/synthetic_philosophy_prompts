Ok so I've been teaching a grad seminar on grounding and fundamentality and I've hit a wall I cant think my way past. Here's my worry. Everyone agrees the mental supervenes on the physical — fix all the physical facts and you've fixed the mental ones, no mental difference without a physical difference. But if that's true, haven't we just said that the physical facts ground the mental facts? Supervenience plus a direction of dependence seems like exactly what grounding is supposed to be, and I honestly can't see what grounding adds over supervenience with an arrow drawn on it. My students keep telling me I'm conflating something but I can't see what.

And here's the second thing, which might be the same thing. Grounding is supposed to be well-founded, no infinite descending chains, right? Doesn't that mean there has to be one bottom level — one set of facts that grounds everything else and isn't itself grounded? Like, the fundamentality hierachy has to bottom out in a single floor? Because if there were two independent fundamental things sitting there with nothing grounding either of them, grounding wouldn't really be doing its job of explaining everything else. So which of these am I getting wrong, and what's the actual distinction I keep missing? I'd genuinely rather understand it than keep nodding along in seminar.
