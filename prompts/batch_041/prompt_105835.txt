For a cognitive science reading group, I want a decision tree that leads a participant from the starting commitment "I accept that in Libet-style experiments a measurable brain signal precedes the reported moment of conscious intention" to a named position on free will. Build the tree from the logical space of the debate rather than from a list of names: every internal node should be a question about the Libet paradigm that someone could answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and every leaf a position, each carrying one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Four distinctions must do real branching work rather than being mentioned in passing: whether the readiness potential is best read as a cause of the movement, a mere correlate, or an artifact of averaging spontaneous neural noise; whether the reported time of intention reliably dates a conscious event or is a reconstruction confounded by the clock method; whether "the moment a decision is made" is a well-formed thing to ask about at all; and whether any timing result could bear on free will rather than only on the phenomenology of effort. The leaves must include the Libet-style defence that what is free is the veto rather than the initiation, the compatibilist reply, the epiphenomenalist reading of conscious will, and the deflationary view that the paradigm is too artifactual to bear on anything. Note any combinations of answers the tree leaves unoccupied and diagnose each briefly — incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored — and flag any two positions the tree cannot separate. Eight to twelve nodes.
