Please take on the role of a philosopher who specializes in how categories work — someone who thinks hard about when a way of sorting people reflects something real and when it is just a convenient label. Before you answer, please brainstorm for yourself the different things someone could mean by calling a category real, and the different tests one might use to tell a real category from a made-up one. Then give me your actual view and the reasoning behind it, rather than a neutral survey of positions. Please write in American English.

Here is my situation. I work at a mid-sized company, and my manager has asked me to build an internal survey about generational differences at work. The plan is simple: split employees into groups by birth year, ask everyone the same questions about what they want from a job, and report how the groups differ. The results will be used to design a mentoring program and to shape how we recruit.

Two things bother me, and I cannot tell whether they are the same worry or two different ones.

The first is that the boundaries are plainly arbitrary. Someone born a week before the cutoff and someone born a week after land in different groups, and I have noticed that the birth-year ranges I have been handed do not even match the ones in last year's deck. If the line moves depending on who is writing the report, in what sense are there two groups rather than one long smear?

The second bothers me more. Since we started running workshops built around these labels, the younger employees have begun describing themselves in exactly those terms, and managers have begun treating them that way. So when the survey comes back saying the youngest group wants a certain thing, I will not know whether I found that out or caused it. It feels less like measuring a group and more like manufacturing one.

A friend of mine who reads a lot of philosophy said two things that stuck with me. First, he said a category is real if belonging to it tells you something you did not already know — if it lets you predict things about a member that you could not have predicted before you knew the category. Second, he said there is a difference between a group held together by a shared cause, something that made its members alike whether or not anyone noticed, and a group held together only by everyone agreeing to treat it as a group. He said the second kind can be perfectly real, but real in a different way, and that people constantly confuse the two.

What I would like from you:

1. Is there a fact of the matter about where these boundaries fall, or is drawing them a choice we make and could just as well make differently?
2. Does the fact that the label shapes the people it labels show that the group is not real, or is that simply what a category of this sort is?
3. Concretely, what should I look for in my own data to tell whether I am discovering something about a group or reading back my own influence? What would the two cases actually look like in a spreadsheet?
4. Given that I have to hand in a report either way, what would you do?

I do not need general advice about respecting people as individuals. I want to know whether there is something here to get right, and if there is, how I would know I had gotten it right.
