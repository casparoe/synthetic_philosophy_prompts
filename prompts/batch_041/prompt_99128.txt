Been stuck in a seminar that keeps circling the same question about incommensurable values and I'd like a actual diagnosis rather than another tour of the literature. One side says: when neither of two options is better than the other and they aren't equal either -- two careers that would fulfill you in different ways, two people you could love -- the values are incommensurable and reason has run out. You can still pick, but nothing guides the pick, and there's no fact of the matter about which one you should take. The other side says that's a false trichotomy: better, worse, and equal aren't the only options; there's a fourth relation, which they call parity, where two things are comparable and it's rational to choose either one without either being better or the two being equal. My hypothesis is that they aren't really disagreeing -- one is doing value metaphysics (is there a fact about which is better?) and the other is doing the norms of choice (can a choice be rational without such a fact?), and they're just using 'incommensurable' for different things. But then one of them raises a chaining worry I can't just dismiss: if A is on a par with B and B is on a par with C, what about A and C, and can't you string small improvements together until something obviously worse comes out better? The other shrugs and says that just shows parity isn't transitive and nobody ever promised it was. So: is my verbal-disagreement hypothesis right, or is there a real substantive fight hiding in there (say, over whether parity is a genuine relation or a dressed-up way of saying you can't compare)? And either way, tell me what would actually count as progress here, or whether the whole thing is ill-posed.
