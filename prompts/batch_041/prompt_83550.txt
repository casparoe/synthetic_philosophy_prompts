I spent thirty-five years at the bench before I retired, and the habit I cannot shake is the question I was trained to ask first: what is the control here? Since retiring I have been reading my way into philosophy, and I have been stuck for some weeks on Weber and the Protestant ethic. My difficulty is not that I doubt the historical material; it is that I cannot see the design. He wants to say that a body of religious conviction was a cause of a form of economic conduct, and yet the case as he gives it is comparative and retrospective, so I never know what would have had to be otherwise for him to be wrong. What I would like from you is a thought experiment built to order: not an illustration of the thesis, which I could find in any introductory chapter, but a designed case in which a value-commitment does autonomous causal work in producing economic behaviour. Here are my specifications, and I should say that I take them to be jointly satisfiable but not easily so. First, the case must be realistic — a plausible historical episode or an existing sort of community, not an imaginary island whose conclusion is stipulated. Second, it must be a controlled contrast: two populations alike in geography, technology, market access, legal and property institutions, and prior wealth, differing only in doctrine — or else one population observed before and after a doctrinal change. Third, the doctrine must not be the creature of the economic conditions it is invoked to explain; either it must arrive from outside for reasons internal to theology, or you must show why the reverse causal arrow cannot run. Fourth, the mechanism must be the one Weber actually posits rather than a vague appeal to religious thrift: the doctrine must generate in the believer a specific pressure — a duty, an anxiety, a demand for assurance about one's standing — which expresses itself as methodical, self-monitoring labour and as a bar on enjoying the proceeds, so that any surplus has nowhere to go but back into the enterprise. Fifth, and this is the specification I care most about, the content of the doctrine must be doing the work, not its incidental correlates: not literacy, not sectarian minority status, not the mere fact of belonging to a tight community that can police its members. Sixth, the case must be one in which the two available readings of Weber come apart — the strong reading, on which the ethic is a cause, and the weak reading, on which there is only the mutual attraction he called elective affinity, ideas and interests selecting one another with no causal priority. Construct the case so that the weak reading cannot account for the outcome, since that divergence is precisely what the experiment is for. And let me say plainly what I would count as cheating: any version in which the agents are stipulated to behave as the thesis requires, rather than behaving as the doctrine, reasoned through by someone who holds it, would lead them to behave.

Once the scenario is on the table, walk me through it the way I would walk through a protocol. Say what each element of the design is for; then identify the single feature on which the result depends, the one that if removed would let the whole thing collapse back into ordinary profit-seeking with theological decoration. Tell me next how the strongest critic would attack the case — I take that critic to be someone who holds either that the convictions were themselves selected by economic circumstances, or that they merely rationalised conduct that interests had already dictated — and say honestly whether the case survives that attack or only appears to. Finally I want a calibration, not a performance of confidence: state how confident you are that the construction succeeds on its own terms, and single out the one point in it about which you are least sure.
