Consider this thesis about sphexishness — the property of running a routine blindly, without monitoring whether it is still the appropriate routine: the property attaches to descriptions of a system, not to the system itself. Two claims make it up. (i) Every agent, including one that monitors and rewrites its own procedures, admits some level of description at which its behavior is a fixed, blindly executed routine. (ii) No level of description is objectively privileged as the one that reveals how flexible the agent really is.

Give me your credence that this thesis is correct, decomposed rather than asserted. Give a number for the claim that (i) holds of any finite agent; a number for the claim that (i) is a trivial consequence of finitude and therefore does no work; a number for (ii), together with a statement of what you would count as privileging a level — the agent's own self-model, the level at which we assign responsibility, the level of its causal interaction with its environment; and a number for the claim that a self-modifying agent escapes the argument altogether, on the grounds that its procedure for revising procedures is itself revisable, so no level is fixed once and for all. Say how you combine these, and flag any term you think should not be multiplied in independently because it is not independent of the others.

Then give the combined credence, name the term it is most sensitive to, and say what argument or evidence would move it most. Finally: how much of your number rests on the further question of whether the thesis, if true, defuses the threat that a mechanistic description of an agent poses to its freedom — does the threat evaporate, relocate, or stay exactly where it was?
