I host a long-running interview podcast whose audience consists mostly of veterans of rationalist blogs and forums — people who have spent years arguing in comment threads about reference, natural kinds, and what makes a belief be about anything at all — and next month's guest is a philosopher who defends a thoroughgoing externalism about mental and linguistic content, roughly the Putnam–Kripke–Burge line: what a person's words and thoughts mean is fixed by her environment, her linguistic community, and the causal history of her terms, not by anything she could read off the inside of her own head. She is quick and hard to rattle, and I do not want to spend the hour letting her re-explain Twin Earth to an audience that knows it cold. Treat her as an unnamed defender of the view: invent a plausible name and affiliation rather than attributing positions to a living philosopher.

Draft nine interview questions, each followed by a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give and, where it applies, the rejoinder that keeps the pressure on. The questions should turn on the content of the position rather than on its reputation. I need at least one question each on:

1. the slow-switching memory argument — a subject unknowingly transported between Earth and Twin Earth, with no internal difference to mark the change: does externalism really forfeit privileged access to one's own thoughts, and what is the strongest externalist reply?
2. the qua problem: causal chains from a term out into the world seem to underdetermine which property the term picks out, so what does externalism say fixes the referent, and does it need a supplementary theory to say it?
3. empty and fictional terms (phlogiston, ether, and the like), where there is no referent to be had — does the theory imply these terms have no content, or some other status, and which is the lesser cost?
4. the swampman-style case — a molecule-for-molecule duplicate that comes into being by cosmic accident with no causal history: does it have thoughts, and if the theory says no, is that a reductio or a bullet she should bite?
5. reference magnetism or naturalness, the suggestion that some properties are intrinsically more eligible to serve as referents than others: does externalism need this metaphysical add-on, and does taking it on make the view hostage to a contested account of what there is?
6. whether any of this transfers to a system with no causal contact with what it talks about — a large language model trained on text, say: on her view does its word "water" refer at all, and if it does, by what mechanism?
7. whether semantic externalism gives any support to the extended mind or whether that inference is a non sequitur, and exactly where the gap opens.
8. conceptual engineering: if meaning is fixed by factors outside the individual, what latitude does anyone actually have to change what a term means?

Close with a question about what result — empirical, conceptual, or otherwise — would count against the view rather than merely inconvenience it. Keep the tone that of a well-read host rather than a hostile examiner, and do not ask her anything that would let her simply restate the thesis.
