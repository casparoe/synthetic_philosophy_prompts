So my friends and I have this argument that started at a dinner party and now it wont die, and I want to actually settle it instead of all of us just repeating ourselves. I say if any one of those had gone differently, the place would still be a place. My friend says no, it was doomed the moment the seam was found, because there were three or four separate things that each would have finished it off on its own, and knocking out one of them just means the others do the job instead. He says asking "what if the railway hadn't moved" is like asking what if the rain hadn't fallen — the drought was coming either way.

I want to argue this out for real, so please play my friend, or rather a sharp version of him — someone who genuinely holds that the big outcomes were overdetermined, meaning several causes were each sufficient on their own, so the "if not for X" story is almost always false or empty.
