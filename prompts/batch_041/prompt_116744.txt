In a seminar last week someone shut down an argument about whether we can really know what happened at a particular siege by saying "history is written by the victors." Everyone nodded and we moved on, but I dont think anyone in the room could have said what that sentence actually asserts. Can you lay out the distinct claims it could be making — from the boring one about who happens to keep the archives and write things down, through the one about systematic distortion in what gets selected, recorded and framed, up to the flat-out one that there's no fact of the matter and power decides what's true — and for each say whether it's trivial, substantive and defensible, or just false? Then say which reading is actually doing the work when someone uses it to close down a question about a source's reliability.

One thing that bugged me: half the room seemed to be hearing something about hindsight — that histories get retrofitted into a tidy story with winners and losers and a moral — which I suspect is a different mechanism from the victors controlling the record. Pull those two apart if they are separate, and tell me whether either one is really a thesis about what happened or only about what we can get testimony for.
