My thesis is a version of counterpart theory that I call lineage counterpart theory. The standard debate treats possible worlds as isolated containers and asks whether one and the same object can appear in more than one of them. I want to replace that picture with a branching version-control DAG. A possible world is a branch or maximal path through the DAG. An ordinary object is not a single node but a lineage: a connected subgraph of versions. What we call transworld identity is not literal identity between world-bound objects; it is the relation between a version and its ancestors, descendants, or merge-base relatives along the lineage. Counterpart theory is right that cross-world identity is not literal identity, but wrong to model counterparts as separate individuals in separate worlds; counterparts are versions of one lineage, and the counterpart relation is the branch-and-merge structure of the DAG. De re modal claims about an object are claims about which versions of its lineage appear on which branches under a contextually selected counterpart relation. Essential properties are properties invariant across all versions of the lineage under that relation; accidental properties vary. My argument has three steps: first, the standard debate assumes isolated worlds and world-bound objects; second, a version-control DAG gives a better formal picture of modal space; third, this picture preserves the Lewisian insight that cross-world identity is not literal identity and the Kripkean insight that de re necessity is not merely a fact about how we describe counterparts. The cost is that modal space is not a set of disjoint worlds but a graph with merge points, so S5 may fail and the necessity of origin becomes a claim about lineage roots.
