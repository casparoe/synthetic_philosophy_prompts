My town had a vote last month on whether to let the orchard co-op spray a new pesticide on the hills above the reservoir. Two friends of mine, Dana and Marcus, have been going at it ever since, and I got roped in as the guy whose supposed to say who's winning. I can't. Here's roughly how it went.

(1) Dana: the whole idea of the precautionary principle is that when we don't know a thing is safe, we don't do it — the burden is on the company to show it's safe, not on us to show it's dangerous.

(2) Marcus: you can't prove any chemical is safe, that's an infinite study. If "not proven safe" means "don't do it," your rule bans every new thing anyone ever invented, including the pesticides that got us off the worse ones. And not spraying isn't free — the stuff they'd use instead is nastier, and it drains into the same reservoir.

(3) Dana: that's not precaution, that's a cost-benefit spreadsheet with a thumb on the scale. Precaution is for the special case where the possible harm is big and you can't take it back. Poison the reservoir and it's poisoned for a generation; block the spray and we get a few bad years of aphids. You can undo aphids. You can't undo the reservoir. That asymmetry is the whole point.

(4) Marcus: so "big and can't take it back" is doing all the work. Everything's potentially catastrophic if you squint hard enough, and somebody has to decide which harms get to count as the scary ones. Also you keep saying not knowing is a reason not to act, but not knowing cuts both ways — we don't know the spray is unsafe, and we don't know the status quo is safe either. If ignorance is a reason to hold off, it's equally a reason to go ahead.

(5) Dana: it's not symmetric, because the two mistakes don't cost the same. Being wrong about the spray costs us the reservoir. Being wrong about the ban costs us some yield. You weigh the mistakes, and one of them is recoverable and the other isn't.

(6) Marcus: then you've given up the principle and you're back to weighing costs — you've just decided in advance which cost is the one that counts. Fine, but call it what it is: your politics with a fancy name.

Go through it move by move and tell me who's got the better of each step, not just who wins at the end. And tell me what both of them keep talking past.
