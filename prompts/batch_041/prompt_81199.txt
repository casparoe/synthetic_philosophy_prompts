I retired from the chemistry department four years ago and, having run out of other things to do, I've spent a lot of that time reading philosophy. My wife says I've become insufferable, which is probably fair, but she's the one who started it by lending me the books.

Here's the thing that's been nagging me. On the walk above our house there's a steep bit of path, and every time I go up I add a stone to the pile at the top. It's now a proper cairn, maybe thrity or forty stones, the bottom ones mossy, the top ones balanced so they don't slide off. Last week my grandson asked me when I "made" it and I couldn't answer. Then I couldn't answer a lot of other things either.

If I nudge the top stone with my boot it's still held on by friction, same as before, just half an inch over. Is the cairn still there? If I pull one stone out of the middle and the rest stay up, how many stones can I pull before I've destroyed it? Twenty-nine stones arranged cairnwise is a cairn and twenty-eight is a pile? There's no number there, surely. And the whole lot is sitting on an outcrop, which is part of the hill, which is part of the ground, and at some point I'm fairly sure I have to say there's just the ground and everything I think of as a thing is a pattern I'm projecting onto it.

I spent my career on glasses and supercooled liquids, where the entire point is that the transition isn't sharp — you pick a convention, another lab picks a different one, and nobody's wrong. So my instinct is that the cairn question has no answer and I should get over it. But when I tried that line on my daughter she said, so there's no you either, then. I did not enjoy that.

So: is my instinct right, and if it is, is it right in the pick-a-convention way or in some other way I don't know about? Is there a standard move here — something that says when a heap of stuff does and doesn't add up to a thing in its own right, and that survives being pushed on? I'd rather have one honest answer than a menu of five, but if the honest answer is a menu, tell me which item you'd bet on. One constraint: whatever it is has to let me keep molecules. If the answer is that there are no molecules, only nuclei and electrons arranged moleculewise, I'll want to hear how that differs from saying there are no chairs — and if it doesn't differ, fine, but then say so plainly, because my whole trade was built on the other assumption. Is there even a name for this, or does everyone just rediscover it on walks? And what do I say to my daughter at Christmas.
