I'm helping run a workshop on AI ethics and need a one-page decision tree for people drafting the conversational policy of a system they expect to be far more persuasive than any human interlocutor. Build it so that a reader who knows nothing of the literature can answer each node from their own commitments, and let every leaf name a position on how such a system should talk. At a minimum the tree should reach: an honesty-first view (state what it takes to be true, then leave the hearer's own reasoning to decide); a consent view (it may press a case only when asked); a paternalist view (it may steer the hearer toward what serves them); and a refusal view (it withholds counsel in high-stakes matters). Take as a fixed distinction the tree must respect the difference between influence that works through the hearer's own weighing of reasons and influence that works around it, and note where that line fails to separate the positions. Branch on the questions that actually do the separating — whether being moved by something other than one's own assessment of the reasons is itself a harm; whether a speaker's superior persuasiveness makes its testimony more or less trustworthy; whether the goal is the hearer's reaching a correct answer or the hearer's keeping their agency; and whether knowing that one is talking to a machine changes what is owed. Drive each node with a concrete case (a medical decision, a political opinion, a financial choice). Flag any leaves the tree cannot separate, and any combinations of answers that no one occupies, saying briefly whether each is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Write in American English.
