I'm a science and ideas journalist at work on a long feature about whether a mind could be built out of something other than neurons. I've spent a fortnight in a lab that records nociceptive responses in octopuses, and I keep hearing the same move made in both directions: an octopus's pain is the same state as ours because it plays the same functional role, while a chatbot's claim to suffer is not, because there is nothing real underneath. I've come to think I can't adjudicate any of this until I know what an account of multiple realisation would have to deliver in the first place — what the thesis actually owes us, as opposed to what its friends and its enemies happen to want from it. So, before I talk to anyone else, give me the desiderata: the cases any adequate account of realising a mental state in a different physical substrate must get right, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the background findings it cannot contradict, with the consideration behind each requirement and a verdict on which are non-negotiable and which are merely the prejudices of one camp.

I am particularly interested in whether some of these requirements cannot be satisfied at the same time as others; if two of them pull in opposite directions, that is the part I would build the piece around, so say plainly which pair collides and what each side would have to give up. I also want to know which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the positions people actually hold. Where a requirement disposes of a familiar line, name that line inside the requirement itself rather than saving it for a scorecard — I don't need the scorecard, I'll be doing the interviews for that. Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, a sentence or two each, with no preamble and no closing paragraph.
