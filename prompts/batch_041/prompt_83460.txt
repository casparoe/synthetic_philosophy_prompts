I work for a small food-safety regulator. Each January we publish a notice promising every licensed kitchen exactly one unannounced inspection before the year is out, though in practice we do not know in advance which weeks we will have the staff to spare. A colleague with a philosophy background says our notice is self-undermining in the way the surprise exam is, and that once our rationality is common knowledge our timing becomes predictable rather than surprising. I do not want a recap of the paradox. Give me the most important implications of the paradox and of the main attempts to resolve it for how we should word and run this policy: whether to fix a window or draw the date at random, what randomization would and would not buy us, and what the notice can and cannot do as a deterrent once kitchens believe it. Then generalize to any public announcement of a future action that is meant to stay unknown until it happens, and say which piece of practical advice rests on which resolution, flagging anything that holds only on the assumption that the audience reasons perfectly.
