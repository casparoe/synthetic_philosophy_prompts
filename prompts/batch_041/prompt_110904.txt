Alright, I keep getting into fights about whether tech and design can be "political" and I want a principle that fits my actual gut calls instead of a slogan. Armrests across the middle of the benches in the train station so nobody can lie down: political. Speed bumps on my street: not political, thats just traffic calming. The famous story about the low parkway bridges built to keep the buses (and the people riding them) out of the park: political — and yeah, I've heard the whole story may be made up, just take it as given. The old church down the road with steps and no ramp: not political, just careless and old. A car app that quietly logs everywhere you drive and sells it to insurers: political. Subway entrance on the poor side of town is stairs only while the rich side gets an escalator: political. QWERTY: not political, just an accident that got frozen in place. Ignition interlock that won't start the car if you've been drinking: not political — paternalist maybe, but not political. My half-baked principle is: it's political when the design sorts people along a line society already draws (class, race, disability), and not when it's cost-cutting or accident or straight paternalism. Does that actually hold up? The bench and the church steps are the ones that bug me — both shut a group out and I call one political and not the other, and I suspect I'm just softer on the church. And is that even the same sense of "political" people mean when they say some technologies only work with a certain kind of state behind them, like a big national grid versus rooftop solar? Give me the principle that saves the most of these, tell me which verdict I should drop, and what it says about a pedestrian bridge over the highway that only lands on the wealthy side.
