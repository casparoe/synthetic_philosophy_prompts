I did a physics degree years ago and have recently been reading philosophy of mathematics on my own, which is how I have ended up stuck on something that everyone else seems to find straightforward. The illustration I keep meeting is the hotel with infinitely many rooms, every one of them occupied, which nonetheless takes in a new guest by shifting everyone along one room, and then takes in infinitely many new guests by moving each guest to the room with twice the number. It is presented as a demonstration that a completed infinity is perfectly coherent and even rather charming. But if the hotel can always accommodate further arrivals, then it is never full; and if it is never full, then it is not an actual infinity at all, only an indefinitely extendable one — precisely the kind of infinity that the finitists and constructivists are willing to accept. So it seems to me that the hotel does not so much illustrate a completed infinite as quietly concede that only potential infinities make sense, and that the example cuts against the position it is meant to support.

There is a second thing I cannot fit together with this. I am told that the hotel has just as many rooms as it has odd-numbered rooms, and just as many as a hotel with twice as many rooms on every floor, so that an infinite collection can be exactly the same size as a proper part of itself. Yet I am also told, on the strength of the argument that the real numbers cannot be matched one-for-one with the counting numbers, that some infinities are strictly larger than others. Both claims seem to require that infinite collections have a determinate size, whereas the hotel seems to show the opposite: a collection that is at once the same size as its own parts and yet manifestly contains more than they do, and that is full while never being full. If size is not a determinate matter once we are past the finite, how can one infinity be strictly bigger than another? And if it is determinate after all, in what sense is the hotel 'full'?

I suspect I am running several distinct questions together, and I would much rather be told precisely which, and where each of my inferences fails, than be given another version of the same story about the rooms. What is the hotel actually supposed to establish, and what does it leave untouched?
