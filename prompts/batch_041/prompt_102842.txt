I've retired after forty years as a geologist and I've spent the last two of them reading philosophy, so please be patient with the vocabulary. I keep running in to this cluster of ideas — Moloch, multipolar traps, races to the bottom — and I honestly can't tell whether I've grasped a mechanism or just caught a mood. So I'd like you to build me a case to order, and then show your work.

Here's the spec:

The setting should be small and ordinary — a fishing fleet, a handful of dairy farms, rival building firms, a few hospitals in a region, a standards committee. No science fiction, no god's-eye coordinator, no superintelligence, no one who can see the future.

Everyone involved must be well-informed, roughly rational, and not especially selfish or nasty. No villains and no fools. If the bad outcome needs somebody to be stupid or spiteful, the case doesn't count.

It must not be a prisoner's dilemma. I don't want a payoff table where each person's best move is the bad one whatever the others do — I want the trap to fall out of the structure instead: an externality, a threshold, a timing or commitment problem, a positional good, something of that kind. I may be wrong that these come to different things; if I am, say so plainly and adjust.

It has to be a real race to the bottom: a drift that goes further than anyone wants, and that nobody can stop on their own.

Then, in the very same scenario, change exactly one thing so the race halts by itself. No seperate regulator, no agreement, no one turning nicer, no new technology — one feature only, everything else held fixed.

After the scenario, walk me through what each party knows and prefers at each step, why the bad outcome is an equilibrium in the first version and why the single change breaks it in the second. Then tell me which feature is doing the work, what happens if you put it back, and how somebody who thinks these traps are inescapable would go about attacking the case — where would it fall apart if I've smuggled in an assumption? The whole point of the exercise is to see whether Moloch-talk adds anything to plain game theory, so a case that's just a prisoner's dilemma in a costume defeats the purpose; and if the spec can't be met as stated, tell me which condition you'd drop and why.

Last thing: tell me what would change your mind about which feature is doing the work, and where in your reasoning you'd put the least confidence.
