Camp one, the behaviorist-ish side: honesty and deception are properties of outputs and of the dispositions that produce them. A system is being deceptive when its outputs tend to install false beliefs, or when what it says diverges from what it would say under some truth-tracking condition. No inner 'beliefs' required -- the mentalistic vocabulary is at best a useful gloss and at worst a category error, and you can build and score evals without ever settling what the model 'thinks'.

Camp two: no, deception is parasitic on the system having internal states with content plus something like a goal, or at least a direction of fit. Calling a stochastic text generator 'deceptive' when there's nothing for the output to contradict is like calling a stopped clock a liar, and the whole moral sting of 'deception' comes from the mismatch between what's represented and what's communicated -- strip that out and you've just renamed inaccuracy.
