For a graduate seminar on belief, assertion, and the semantics/pragmatics boundary, I want a specification rather than a survey: the desiderata any adequate theory of Moore-paradoxicality must meet. Treat the phenomenon as the felt absurdity of first-person present-tense sentences that conjoin a proposition with a disavowal of the speaker's relation to it — the omissive form, "It is raining, but I do not believe that it is raining," and the commissive form, "It is raining, but I believe that it is not raining." Give me the requirements list, with the case or consideration behind each, organised in three groups. First, the cases the theory must classify correctly: the two forms above; the knowledge variant, "It is raining, but I do not know that it is raining"; the evidential variant, "It is raining, but I have no evidence that it is raining" (if you judge the absurdity to be absent or diminished there, say which requirement accounts for the contrast); a future-directed variant; the same contents in the third person and in the past tense; and the same contents embedded under negation, in a conditional, in a disjunction, and under "it might be that ...", where the oddity is generally felt to vanish. Second, the distinctions it must respect: absurdity versus falsity; absurdity versus contradiction; defect in content versus defect in assertion versus defect in the believer's epistemic or rational position; and the difference between a sentence that cannot be sincerely asserted and a content that cannot be thought. Third, the general facts it must be consistent with: that both conjuncts can be true together; that each conjunct is individually believable and assertible; and that the oddity is tied to the first-person present tense. Then mark which requirements are negotiable and which are not, name the single requirement that rules out the most existing accounts, and tell me whether any pair of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied — in particular, whether a theory can locate the defect in the propositional content while granting that the content can be true. Weight the requirements and their motivating cases more heavily than any scorecard of named views.
