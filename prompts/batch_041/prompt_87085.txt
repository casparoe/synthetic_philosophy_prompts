Some stuff I want you to press on specifically, cause I think this is where it gets interesting and everyone in my seminar glosses over it:

- the analogy seems to need every civilisation to be a single agent with one will and one voice. Real forests have hunters, not species. If a "civilisation" is millions of factions with different interests, who exactly is the hunter, and does the argument survive that?
- the "dark" part. In an actual forest the hunters can see each other, find footprints, hear shots. The whole name of the thing seems to be smuggling in a claim about detection being hard and annihilation being cheap and decisive. How much of the argument actually hangs on that?
- it's set up as a one shot game with no talking. Real forests are repeated games, the same hunters come back season after season, and there's often more to gain from not shooting. Does letting them talk break the picture, and is the no talking assumption part of the analogy or an extra premise bolted on?
- the presenter also leaned on the three axioms (survival comes first, civilisations keep expanding, total matter is fixed) plus the chain of suspicion thing and technological explosion. Are those part of the forest picture or independent premises being smuggled in under cover of a nice image? Does the forest image do any work once you have them, and do they do any work without it?
- one of our readings said Brin had a version of this in the 80s with self replicating probes and Bear had one where humanity is a baby crying in the woods and what comes is wolves. If this image keeps getting reinvented, does that tell us anything about whether it's a good analogy or just a sticky one?
