For most of my adult life I have kept a symptom diary. The entries are not prose: each bad episode gets a single letter, and the letter stands for a sensation I have never found a satisfying public description for — not quite pain, not quite pressure, and always in the same place. I began the diary because I wanted to know whether the episodes were getting more frequent, and I recently realized that I have quietly been treating the diary as the authority on what the sensation is: I check whether a new episode matches the letter, and if I hesitate, I go back and reread old entries.

I then read Wittgenstein's Philosophical Investigations and found the following, in §243: "The words of this language are to refer to what only the speaker can know — to his immediate private sensations. So another person cannot understand the language." Shortly afterwards, in §258, he asks me to imagine someone keeping a diary about the recurrence of a sensation: the diarist marks the sign S in a calendar on each occasion and, as Wittgenstein puts it, defines the sign by concentrating on the sensation and inwardly naming it. The question he presses is whether this ceremony has conferred any meaning on the sign at all.

I would like help understanding exactly what is supposed to have gone wrong, because my own case seems to have a feature his does not. I do not merely name the sensation and leave it at that; I use the old entries as a standard against which new episodes are judged, and the diary also does public work — I bring it to appointments and it shapes my medication. What I want explained is this. If the original act of marking S was empty because there was no independent check on whether the same sensation had recurred, does that emptiness get repaired later, when the entries acquire a use and other people begin to rely on them? Or does Wittgenstein's point instead imply that whatever I am doing with the diary, it was never a case of naming a private object in the first place, so that the question of whether the sensation recurred is the wrong question to ask of the entries? I am especially unsure how the memory objection is meant to bite: my uncertainty is not that I might misremember the sensation, but that I have no way to tell what would count as remembering it correctly, and I cannot see whether that is the same problem or a different one. Please explain the passage and the surrounding argument, and then tell me which reading of my diary case the argument actually supports, and whether it leaves room for the diary to be meaningful in any sense.
