Ok so I run this little monthly discussion thing at the community centre - mostly retired folks, a couple of nurses, my neighbour who used to be a social worker, that kind of crowd. No one has any philosophy training but everyone has opinions. Next month I want to do advance directives, you know the forms you sign saying what you want done to you if you end up unable to say it yourself. I've been thinking about this a lot since my uncle went into care and honestly the interesting part isn't the paperwork, it's this: when the person writing the form and the person lying in the bed are the same body but basically seperate people now, who wins?

I want you to plan the session for me, not give me the talk. Like what order do the ideas have to come in so it doesn't just turn into a shouting match about "pulling the plug"? What's the one example I should build the whole evening on - I was going to use a made up case of a woman who signs a form refusing antibiotics, then years later has dementia and is cheerful as anything and says yes to the antibiotics when the doctor asks, and her kids are split down the middle. Is that the right example or is there a better one, and what does a good carrying example need to have and not have?

Also there's stuff I keep wanting to smuggle in and don't know where it goes. One is the difference between what a person cares about for their whole life story versus what they happen to enjoy minute to minute (my uncle now loves custard and that really is his life now, whatever his old self would've thought). The other is the idea that the earlier self is basically writing a letter to a stranger and demanding the stranger obey it. Where do those two go in the sequence, and do they depend on something coming before them?

And what will they get wrong? I'm guessing they'll think the form is legally binding no matter what, and that a person with dementia has no self left at all, and they'll mix up the form with giving someone power of attorney. Tell me the misconceptions in the order they'll actually come up and exactly where in the evening I head each one off. Oh and should I even mention the weird case where the form itself says "ignore any form I sign after this one" - does that break their brains or is it a good closer?
