I don't need the history, I've got the history. What I need is for you to run that episode through the fight in Buddhist ethics over whether the tradition is really a form of consequentialism.

The other side says no, karma is cetanā, it's what you intended, and the tradition is really about the state of the agent's mind — which means the arithmetic doesn't decide anything. Take those two and tell me what each says Quảng Đức was actually doing, and whether he should have done it. Was it a precept violation at all, or is the standard move — that he wasn't committing suicide, he was bearing witness — doing real work here?

Which reading survives that better?
