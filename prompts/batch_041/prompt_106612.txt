I run compliance for a mid-size international NGO that does health work in a few countries where gifts and looking after people are just how things get done. We have a zero-tolerance gifts-and-hospitality policy written at head office that nobody in the field follows, and I've been told to either enforce it or rewrite it. I want a decision procedure my country teams can actually run when someone offers or accepts something: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a real answer to each, where it stops with a clean yes or no, and where it has to hand the call to a person because the question has turned into one about values rather than facts. It needs to take the relativist objection seriously -- one of my colleagues argues that corruption is a Western category, that the very idea of a public official abusing their office assumes a line between public and private that the places we work dont really draw, and that a zero-gift rule is just us imposing our idea of how politics should go -- but without quietly licensing everything either, and without pretending the anthropologist has the last word. Run it on three cases: a village chief who wants to give our field nurse a chicken after a clinic day; a customs official who hints that a small payment would move a stuck vaccine shipment; and a local partner who funds a training workshop and then asks us to hire his nephew for it (that one feels more like patronage than bribery and I'm not sure our policy should treat them the same). Tell me where the procedure will misfire, which steps are just my judgment dressed up as a flowchart, and flag the case where it hands me an answer I'd rather not have.
