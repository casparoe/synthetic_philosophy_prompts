I write a column on puzzles and paradoxes for a science-and-ideas outlet, and I've been stuck on the surprise exam for weeks now. You know the setup: the teacher says there'll be an exam next week, on a day you won't be able to predict the night before; the clever student runs the backwards induction, rules out Friday, then Thursday, all the way down to Monday, concludes there can't be an exam at all, and then gets blindsided on Wednesday. I'm not after "the solution" -- I've read enough hand-waving about knowledge and common knowledge to last me a lifetime. What I want, before I write a word, is the spec. What does any theory that claims to resolve this actually have to deliver? Give me the desiderata list. Which versions of the story must it get right -- the full five-day case, the stripped-down one-day case where the teacher says surprise exam tomorrow, a case where the announcement is made to only one student while the others hear nothing, a case where the teacher is known to be truthful versus known to be a joker who sometimes cancels, and two I made up: one where the teacher makes the announcement and then the principal backs it up by announcing that whatever the teacher just said is true, and one where two teachers each promise a surprise exam in the same week. And the distinctions it has to keep straight -- between the students not being able to derive the day and the day not being derivable, between the announcement being true and the students knowing it's true, between a surprise about the fact of the exam and a surprise about its timing, between the teacher being an ideal reasoner and the students merely believing he is one. For each requirement, tell me the case or consideration that puts it on the list.

Then the part I actually care about: are any of these jointly unsatisfiable? I have a hunch that the announcement being consistent, the students being ideal reasoners, and the students knowing the teacher is telling the truth can't all hold at once in the one-day version but might in the five-day one, and I want to know whether thats a real tension or just me confusing myself. Flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just something one camp happens to like, and tell me which single requirement does the most to kill off the tidy answers -- the ones that either declare the announcement simply false or declare the student's reasoning simply silly. Oh, and if a candidate theory can only save the puzzle by quietly redefining what surprise means, say so plainly, because that's the move I most want to call out in the piece.
