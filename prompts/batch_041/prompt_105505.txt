I'm making a handout for a first-year ethics tute on the utility monster — the imagined being whose happiness is so vast that, on a simple summing view, we ought to hand it everyone else's recources. I want a decision tree that takes someone from 'happiness is what makes a life go well and we should add it up across everyone' to a named position on whether the monster is a genuine implication or a reductio. Build the branch nodes out of the real choices that split the views, not out of names: whether welfare is cardinally measurable and comparable across people, whether the aggregation is additive or has thresholds or priority weights, whether gains to one being can ever outweigh losses to another however large, and whether the monster only needs to be conceivable rather than possible for the argument to land. Leaves should be named positions — hedonistic total utilitarianism, the average view, prioritarianism, lexical/threshold views, a rights-based view, desire-satisfaction — each with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Then add a variant where the monster's happiness needs no sacrifice from anyone, it just enjoys existing, and say which leaves collapse when you run that through. Flag any combinations nobody occupies and whether that's because they're incoherent or just unpopular. Around eight nodes please.
