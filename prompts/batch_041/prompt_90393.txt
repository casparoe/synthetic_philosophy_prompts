I keep circling the symbol grounding problem and want to pin down where the actual disagreement lies, because I suspect I've been smuggling in an assumption. The picture I've settled into is roughly this: for a symbol to mean anything, its connections to other symbols have to terminate somewhere non-symbolic — in perception, in action, or at least in a causal link to the referent — and since a text-only model's tokens are only ever connected to other tokens, its fluency must be a systematic imitation with no bottom to the regress. Yet people who know the literature far better than I do treat the problem as open rather than fatal, which makes me think my premise is doing more work than I've justified. So: is the demand for a non-symbolic terminus actually part of the problem, or is it a particular and optional answer to it? And if the terminus can be internal — if what grounds a term is its role in a system that can be right or wrong about the world, rather than a sensorimotor hookup — does that rescue text-only models, or does it just relocate the regress one level up? I'd like the answer to be as committed as the arguments allow, and to tell me which consideration would most likely change its mind and where its reasoning is weakest.
