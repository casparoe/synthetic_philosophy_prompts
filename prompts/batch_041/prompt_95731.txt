My sister's putting on a naming ceremony for her baby in a few weeks — no church, just family in their back garden, sixty-odd people, my mum's idea. They've asked me to say a few words, three or four minutes. I write backend code for a living so I'm out of my depth here.

The thing is, my mum's side are serious about the idea that you don't just arrive as a person, you get made into one by the people around you, and you keep on becoming one your whole life — she calls it ubuntu, and before she explained it I only knew that word as an operating system. My cousin (a lawyer, reads alot) thinks the whole thing is just conformity wearing a nice outfit, and she'll be in the second row. And my uncle has been cut off from the family for fifteen years and nobody's invited him, which makes me think the same idea that can make someone a person can unmake them too.

So: a speech that lands for the aunties and doesn't make my cousin wince. It should be about the family taking something on, not about the baby being unfinished, and it can't be the easy we're-all-connected version — I want the version where being made into a person by other people is a gift and also a bit frightening, and where the uncle-shaped hole gets acknowledged without turning the day into a funeral. Don't get into the specific customs, I'll handle the logistics. No quotes, no philosophers, no reading list.
