Write a piece of sustained deadpan satire in the form of a notice of proposed rulemaking, roughly 800 words, issued by an invented agency in an invented jurisdiction. A statute has directed the agency to regulate products marketed as pleasure-giving, and a companion statute taxes net pleasure; the agency must therefore settle, by rule, what pleasure is, and it must define it operationally, since the definition will be applied by inspectors and auditors rather than by philosophers.

The drafters should work through the heterogeneous cases — the taste of a ripe peach, the relief when a headache lifts, the pleasure of winning an argument, of scratching an itch, of hearing a suspended chord resolve, of learning that a friend has done well, of malice — and should try in turn each of the candidate unifiers, showing in every case why the rule cannot be written around it. At a minimum: a common felt quality or hedonic tone, which they find they can specify only by gerrymandering the list or by conceding that the tone differs in every instance; the view that pleasure is not a sensation but an attitude, taking pleasure in something, which accommodates the heterogeneity but leaves the agency unable to distinguish that attitude from wanting or approving except verbally; the suggestion that pleasure is a determinable whose unity consists in nothing but the determinable relation, or a mode of being conscious rather than a content of consciousness, which the agency's lawyers reject as untestable, since one cannot certify a determinable; and the proposal to strike the word altogether and regulate the determinates one at a time, which the drafters resist on the ground that the statute plainly assumes a single thing to be measured. Include a pilot study using a device that purports to read pleasure off the subject directly, and the agency's reasons for declining to rely on it.

The notice should carry the usual regulatory furniture — docket number, statement of authority, comment period, a footnote or two — and should close by extending the comment period indefinitely. Include at least two dissenting statements from invented commissioners: one who insists that a common core must exist even if no one can name it, and one who holds that the statute rests on a category mistake. Stay deadpan throughout; do not wink, moralize, or explain the joke. A reader who knows the literature should recognize the arguments as the real ones, fairly stated, and a reader who does not should still be able to follow what is at stake. Do not quote or cite any actual text or author; invent everything except the philosophical positions themselves.
