I moderate a fairly big online support group for people with a chronic illness — 40k members, six volunteer mods including me, and we're making calls on threads basically every day about whether to step in. We keep having the same fight. Sometimes a thread is a closed loop: everyone's quoting the same one blog post and the same two anecdotes, and nobody in there seems to know that actual doctors disagree with each other about this stuff. Other times it's a thread where people *do* know about the outside disagreement, and the response is that the study was bought, the specialist is a shill, that person was never really sick in the first place. From the mod chair those feel like the same problem, but I'm pretty sure they're not the same thing and probably don't have the same fix. What I need is a procedure my mod team can actually run on a thread — twenty minutes, no philosophy degree — that tells us which of the two we're looking at (or both, or neither), what to do about each, and what to do when they're tangled together. It should give us the questions in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it stops with a "no, leave it", and where it hands the thing to a human because it's a values call and not a factual one. Some constraints: we only remove posts for breaking the rules, never for being wrong, and anything that smells like a medical claim has to go past our one volunteer nurse practitioner before we act on it, so the procedure has to say clearly where that sign-off comes in. It also has to cover the case where the honest answer is to do nothing even though the thread is making my teeth grind, and the case where the problem is something one of us mods pinned to the top ourselves. Give me two or three worked examples, including one where it tells me something I won't like, and be honest at the end about where the procedure definately breaks down and a person just has to decide.
