ok so I've been going down a rabbit hole on suffering-focused ethics and I think I might actually buy the strong version of it. Roughly: suffering — pain, distress, that kind of thing — is the only thing that's intrinsically bad, and it's lexically prior to pleasure, meaning no finite amount of happiness outweighs even a single moment of intense agony, kind of like how some people think no amount of money justifies one murder. And pleasure isn't intrinsically good at all; the good thing is just the absence of suffering.

Assuming I really commit to that, what are the most important implications? What does it actually force me to believe or do — does it make me an antinatalist, does it mean I should be quietly rooting for a painless end to all life, does it change where my charity money should go (animal suffering vs future people vs humans suffering right now), does it change how I think about my own death? That last one really trips me up: if only suffering is bad, and being dead involves no suffering, where does the badness of dying even come from? And how does the view compare different sufferings — is a long stretch of mild misery worse than one short blast of agony, and does it matter whether the same total is spread across a million people or concentrated in one? I definately want the load-bearing implications, not the slogans, and I especially want to know which implications don't follow even though people assume they do.
