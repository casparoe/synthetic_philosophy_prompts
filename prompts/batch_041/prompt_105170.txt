Ok, I need help with something I've been chewing on for a couple of weeks. I'm a science and ideas reporter, and I'm deep into a feature about a startup that sells what it calls "humane" pest control -- they gas the rats and they freeze the insects that turn up in restaurant kitchens, and they're pitching the whole thing as the ethical alternative to glue boards and poison. The founder is charming, the photos are great, and the story really wants to end on this one line my editor keeps pushing: that with the insects at least there's no welfare question, because a bug isn't really feeling anything anyway, so that part is just cleanup.

I keep stalling on that line and I can't quite articulate why. I have this nagging feeling that both of those dismissals -- the bug one and the thermostat one -- are the same move wearing different clothes, even though the two cases look nothing alike to me. And then my brother-in-law, who's a postdoc in an insect neuro lab, told me over dinner that the honest state of things is that nobody knows where the lights come on, and that it's probably not a switch at all, it's a dial -- bugs have the reflex wiring that jerks your hand off a hot stove, but whether anything is home to feel it is a different question entirely. Honestly I found the dial picture more disturbing than either a clean yes or a clean no.

So what do I do with the piece? I don't want to chicken out with "scientists disagree," which is both lazy and a little false. Give me an actual recommendation for how to frame the insect line -- and tell me straight whether the thermostat comparison is a fair one or whether I'm conflating two things that shouldn't be conflated. And be careful to seperate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this; I dont want a live disagreement dressed up as the consensus view.
