ok so I've been reading alot of the Moloch / multipolar trap stuff for years and nodding along, and I want to actually stress test the frame on a real case instead of admiring it. Take the collapse of the northern cod off Newfoundland and Labrador. Quick recap so you dont have to guess at facts: for centuries it was small inshore boats and it was fine; from the 50s on, big foreign factory trawlers with sonar and radar worked the Grand Banks and took in fifteen years what had taken a few centuries before; the catch peaked around the late 60s; Canada pushed through the 200 mile limit in the late 70s and shoved the foreign fleets out; then instead of letting the stock rebuild, the feds let the domestic fleet (and some American boats) expand into the gap and kept the quotas high while the scientific assessments kept getting revised downward; in 1992 the minister shut the whole northern cod fishery down, tens of thousands of people lost their work, and the stock has basically never come back. Meanwhile foreign fleets just kept fishing the straddling stocks right outside the line, so the problem didnt go away, it moved. Give me exactly five numbered items, no intro, no conclusion, a sentence or two each, tight and no padding:

1. the mapping first, done properly: who the players actually are and at which level (skipper, company, provincial government, federal department, foreign fleet), what each one's real options and payoffs were, and flag where the Moloch story gets slippery because people slide between those levels without admitting it.

2. what the frame genuinely nails here, i.e. the parts that only make sense as a multipolar trap rather than ordinary greed or stupidity.

3. here's what I keep coming back to. I split these in my notes into cant-move traps (the bad outcome is a stable equilibrium because literally nobody can move alone - arms races, doping, ad spend) and wont-move traps (somebody could have stopped it and just didnt, because the cost lands on them now and the benefit lands on everyone later, which isnt a coordination problem at all, it's an agency problem where the referee is also a player). Which one is this, and does the split survive contact with the case or is it a distinction without a difference.

4. second split, and I think this is the one that breaks the blogs: positional traps, where the loss is only relative and if everyone stopped tomorrow everyone would be fine, versus traps where something physical is actually being destroyed, so the loss is absolute and possibly irreversible. I suspect nearly all Moloch writing is built on the positional cases and quietly smuggles their properties into the absolute ones. test that on the cod and tell me if I'm right.

5. what the case shows the frame is missing: the single sharpest thing Moloch-talk cant account for here, and the minimal patch you'd add to fix it.
