my reading group has a resident negative utilitarian and her one-liner is 'suffering is the only thing that really matters.' last week she used it to argue we should stop funding the arts and put the money into pain research instead, and when i said that seemed like a big leap she said i just wasn't taking suffering seriously.

can you take that sentence apart for me? i want the different precise claims it could be making -- what exactly the 'only' is doing (the only thing that's intrinsically bad? the only thing worth caring about? the only thing we can all agree on? the only thing that's urgent? the only thing that can motivate anyone?), and how the reading shifts depending on whose suffering we mean -- mine, everyone's, animals', people who don't exist yet. some of these readings will be things nobody would deny, some are real philosophical positions people defend, and at least one of them seems to commit you to wanting the whole thing to wind down as painlessly as possible. say which is which, which are actually defensible once you spell them out, which are just false, and which one she most likely means when she says it.

and for each reading, tell me whether it actually gets her to 'so defund the arts', or whether that step needs something extra the slogan doesn't give her -- an empirical claim about where the money does the most good, or a claim about priorities rather than about value. lay it out as a menu with verdicts, i want to be able to point at the one she's using in the meeting, not get a essay about utilitarianism.
