I have arrived at a position on Goodman's grue puzzle that I do not think I have seen stated in quite this form, and I want an honest map of its exposure. The position has three parts. First, the entrenchment account of projectibility is descriptively right — which predicates we project does track the history of our use of them — but it carries no justificatory weight, and this is not a defect, because the demand for a non-circular justification of our inductive practice taken as a whole is itself a mistake: any proposed answer must either reinstate a projection we have already made or decline to project anything at all. Second, the real bite of grue is therefore not sceptical but taxonomic: it shows that evidential support is not a relation between propositions that holds independently of a practice of inquiry, so any account that treats it as one, Bayesian or otherwise, is missing something. Third, the standard responses to grue — natural kinds, simplicity, inference to the best explanation, prior probabilities over hypotheses — are all answers to a question that cannot be answered, and each either presupposes projectibility or covertly reintroduces entrenchment.

What would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and sorted by what they attack: cases where entrenchment appears to misdescribe our practice, whether predicates we project despite no history of use or entrenched predicates we refuse to project; formal results that would falsify the taxonomic claim by exhibiting a language-independent account of support; and arguments that the illegitimacy of the justificatory demand is itself a substantive and dubious thesis rather than a dissolution. Where the position and its rivals would predict different things about what inquirers actually do, say what those divergent predictions are. Tell me which of these defeaters a defender of the position would concede as counting against it, not only which ones critics would press. Tell me also whether the third part does any work of its own or merely restates the first: if the position cannot be distinguished from Hume's conclusion plus a change of vocabulary, say so plainly. I would be most moved by a counterexample showing entrenchment predicting the wrong projection, since that breaks the descriptive claim on its own terms; I would be least moved by a formal counterexample requiring a predicate no inquirer could ever have learned. Finally, tell me whether the position risks anything at all or has been insulated by its qualifications — and if it has, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind.
