I'm building the criteria section of a handout for a first-year seminar on "the West," and I want the spec worked out before we look at any particular story about what "Western values" are (clash-of-civilizations blocs, the Enlightenment-and-its-critics narrative, the human-rights-as-Western-imperialism line, whatever). So: what does any adequate account of the phrase "Western values" have to be able to do?

I mean the full list. Which paradigm cases it has to get right -- rule of law, individual rights, free inquiry, democracy, but also the ones that make the label awkward, like the same tradition producing the Inquisition, chattel slavery, and the colonial civilizing mission. Which contrasts it has to sustain and which it has to refuse -- is the contrast with "Confucian" or "Islamic" values a real structural contrast or just rhetorical packaging? Which uses of the phrase it has to explain, including the ones where it's plainly doing argumentative work rather than describing (immigration debates, justifications for intervention, the Cold War "we won because of our values" line). And what it can't be allowed to do: collapse into "whatever values happen to be common in Western countries" (empirically shaky and clearly not what people mean), or into "the correct values" (which begs the question and makes the adjective redundant).

For each requirement give me the case or consideration behind it, and say which requirements are geniune and which are just the commitments of one camp. Then flag the pair you think can't be jointly satisfied. My suspicion is it's the distinctiveness requirement fighting the universality requirement -- you can say "these values are distinctive of the West" or "these values bind everyone," but the moment you say both you've either made the West the lucky custodian of universal truths (so why the label?) or turned the universality into a sales pitch. There's also the reflexivity thing that nags at me: self-criticism, the willingness to question your own tradition, gets claimed as a Western value, which seems to make the whole category self-serving -- is that a real problem for an account or just a rhetorical gotcha?

Tell me if I'm mislocating the fault line, and tell me which single requirement does the most to rule out candidate accounts. Skip the scorecard on particular authors, I'll do that in class.
