For a history-of-science paper I'm arguing for a reading of Kepler on which his astrology was not a leftover superstition but a considered position: the geometrical harmony of the heavens at the moment of a person's birth imprints a real but weak and easily overridden disposition on the soul, by way of the soul's own harmonic constitution, while the apparatus of houses and the predictive horoscopy of events he set aside as unfounded. I want to defend this as a coherent middle position rather than a halfway house, so treat the view as mine and tell me what would count against it. I'm especially interested in defeaters that arise from inside the position rather than only from modern physics: if the influence works by resemblance between the soul and the celestial proportions, it seems to follow that the same proportions produced by anything else — a chord, a drawn diagram, a numerical ratio — ought to do the same work, and that the effect should be indifferent to which planet occupies which position provided the angles match; I'd like to know which of these internal commitments are liabilities and which are genuinely strengths. Rank what you give me from fatal to merely awkward, indicate which defeaters a committed Keplerian would themselves concede and which they would dismiss as changing the subject, and tell me honestly whether the position has been insulated into unfalsifiability and whether that is a fault in a view of this kind.
