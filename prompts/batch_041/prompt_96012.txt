For the past two terms I have been running a graduate seminar on the concepts of health and disease, and I now need to write down my own position for the closing session. Before I do, I want an audit of whether the things I believe actually hang together; I have a nagging sense that two of them cannot both be right, and I would rather find that out now than in front of the students. Here is the set, as plainly as I can put it. (i) To call a condition a disease is already to evaluate it: the concept is normative through and through, and there is no value-free core of dysfunction underneath it that biology could hand us ready-made. (ii) For all that, disease verdicts are not matters of taste. A child born with a severed spinal cord has a disease, and would have had one in any century; and I take the removal of homosexuality from the diagnostic manual to have been the correction of an error rather than a shift in social fashion. (iii) What makes (ii) true, I believe, is that there are culture-independent facts about what human organs and mechanisms are for — their biological function — and that we discover these rather than stipulate them. (iv) I want a single account that covers bodily and mental disorder alike; I have no patience for a separate clause for psychiatry. (v) I also hold that the criterion for calling something a disease is ultimately what it is for medicine to do its job: the concept answers to medicine's aims, not to biology alone. (vi) And here are two verdicts I would defend over coffee afterwards: the man whose routine scan turns up a structural anomaly in his heart that will never trouble him has a disease; the man who is wretched about a body that is, by every biological measure, perfectly healthy does not, however real his suffering.

What I want from you is not a survey of the literature but an audit of this particular set. Tell me which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and which are doing no work at all. Rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give and what it would cost to give it, and finish by naming the single revision that would repair the most at the least expense. Please keep it under 600 words.
