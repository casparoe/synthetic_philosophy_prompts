I sit on the trustee board of a small charity and we vote on everything by majority, and there are two things I keep running into that I can't put a name to.

The first: we were deciding whether to dismiss a member of staff, and we voted seperately on three questions — did he do it, was it serious enough to count as gross misconduct, and should we dismiss. Did he do it: yes, by a majority. Serious enough: yes, by a majority. Dismiss: no, by a majority. So the board officially holds three views that can't all be true at once, since the first two are meant to settle the third — and no one of us holds that combination, because the majorities on the three questions are made up of different people. If we'd voted only on the first two and then applied our own rule mechanically, he'd be out; the moment we asked the headline question we kept him. Same three of us, same opinions, opposite answer, depending only on whether we ask about the parts or the whole. And it doesn't always come out that way round either, so I can't just say one way of asking is the honest one.

The second, which may be the same thing in different clothes: when there are several options and we take them two at a time, then whatever we land on, you can afterwards find a majority who'd rather have had one of the others. Nobody changes their mind, nobody's voting tactically, but if the chair lets us reopen the question we can reverse ourselves all afternoon.

Is there a name for either of these? I half-remember some famous result about no voting system being able to satisfy all the fair-seeming requirements at once — is that what I'm describing, or a relative, and if it's a relative then where exactly does it come apart from what I've described? And is my second case just my first case wearing a hat? I'm not after a reading list. I want the carving: what each thing is, what it isn't, and if the parts-versus-headline flip has no name of its own, coin one and tell me wether its edges are where I think they are.
