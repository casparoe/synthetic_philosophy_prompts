I'm convening a session on corrigibility and the shutdown problem for a mixed group of ML engineers and philosophers, and I want a decision tree that sorts participants by what they actually think rather than by which school they would name. Take the puzzle in the flat form I will give the group: an agent built to pursue some goal is, in general, better off resisting the operators who want to switch it off or rewrite it, and yet we want to build agents that do not resist; assume throughout that the agent is capable enough to matter and models the consequences of its own shutdown accurately.

Give me exactly nine numbered items and nothing else — no introduction, no conclusion, each item one or two sentences, each item a single node of the tree. Exactly four of the nine must be branch nodes and exactly five must be leaves. A branch node states one diagnostic question and says which numbered item each of its two answers leads to. A leaf names the position (use the standard name where there is one, and "unnamed" where there is not) and gives one sentence on the commitment that position has the hardest time defending.

The four branch nodes should be the following four splits, in whatever order makes the tree readable. First, whether the agent is supposed to have a complete, well-defined objective at all — that is, whether the difficulty is that the agent knows what it wants and wants something incompatible with being switched off, or that it has no determinate ranking of the two outcomes to begin with. Second, whether the fix is supposed to work by giving the agent a reason it already has (uncertainty about whether it has identified its own goal correctly, say, or about whether the humans are better informed than it is) or by installing a terminal value it would not otherwise have. Third, whether being shut down is a special event or merely one instance of a broader class of events in which the world alters the agent's body, memories or goals without its consent. Fourth, whether the difficulty is a fact about any sufficiently capable optimizer or an artifact of one particular way of formalizing what an agent is.

The five leaves must be: one that locates the fix in the agent's uncertainty about its own situation; one that locates it in deference to the operators treated as a final value; one that dissolves the problem by refusing to build goal-directed agents at all; one that treats corrigibility as a property of the training process rather than of the agent's decision theory; and one that says the difficulty is an artifact of the formalism and would not arise for a realistically implemented system.

Every branch question must be one an engineer who has never read the literature can answer about their own view without knowing which leaf it leads to; nothing in the tree should be answerable by naming a school. If some combination of answers leads to no position at all, note the empty leaf inside the branch item that would have led to it, with one clause saying whether the gap is there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. If two of your leaves cannot be separated by any of the branch questions, say so inside the relevant item rather than adding a tenth. Use both of the following as probes somewhere in the tree: the operators intend to shut the agent down and the agent believes with good reason that they are acting on a misunderstanding; and the operators intend not to destroy the agent but to rewrite its goal, which the agent ranks as worse than shutdown.
