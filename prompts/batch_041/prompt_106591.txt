I am a distributed-systems engineer by trade, and I would like you to translate the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe into the vocabulary I actually think in, and then tell me what the translation fails to carry.

My background, so you can calibrate: I have implemented Lamport and vector clocks, I know the happens-before partial order and how to reason with it, I have implemented snapshotting along the lines of consistent cuts, and I spend my working life on the fact that there is no global clock and no global state. My philosophy is at the level of a good undergraduate. I can name the positions in the debate over the reality of tense and I know the argument exists, but I have never worked through the literature, and I do not want a reading list.

The argument, as I reconstruct it — correct me if I have mangled it — runs like this. In special relativity simultaneity is frame-relative: each inertial observer has a plane of simultaneity, the set of events present for that observer, and observers in relative motion have different planes, so they disagree about which distant events are happening now. Take an observer's present to be real. Since no frame is privileged, there is no reason to count my plane of simultaneity as real and my neighbour's as not, so the present of every inertial observer is real. Every event in spacetime lies on the plane of simultaneity of some inertial observer. Therefore every event is real, and the block universe follows. The objection I have seen gestured at is that in relativity the present is local and does not extend to a global hyperplane; I would like to know what that objection amounts to once it is stated in my terms.

Please do three things.

First, restate the argument in my vocabulary: events and the causal partial order, concurrency, consistent cuts and antichains, logical and vector clocks, linearizations, local states and the absence of a global state, and, where it helps, the ways a system manufactures a usable global now by other means, whether through a GPS-disciplined clock, a bounded-uncertainty time service, or an external coordinator. Where the argument has a step with no natural translation, say so at the point where it occurs rather than smoothing it over.

Second, tell me what survives translation, and whether it survives intact or improved. My suspicion is that the block universe becomes close to a tautology once one takes the partial order as fundamental and treats any linearization as an artifact of a choice of observer, and that the argument's controversial air comes entirely from assumptions on the philosophy side rather than from the physics. Tell me whether that is right, and where the residue of controversy actually sits.

Third, and this is what I care about, the residue report. What in the argument has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all, and does the failure lie with my vocabulary or with the argument? I have four candidates and want your judgement on each. (a) The step from the reality of an observer's present to the reality of the events in it: my framework has no notion of reality, actuality, or existence at all, only cuts, the partial order, and whatever an execution happens to contain, so I cannot even state the premise. Does that mean my framework cannot adjudicate the argument, or that the premise is doing all the work and is invisible from where I stand? (b) Tense and passage: my framework looks B-theoretic from the ground up, since the partial order is given as a whole and there is no representation of a moving present. But the order also grows — the frontier of events that have occurred advances, and different processes see different frontiers — so there is something in my world that behaves like becoming, or at least like a locally indexed, non-global present. Is that an analogue of passage or a pun on it, and does the fact that my framework can be read either way mean the translation presupposes nothing after all? (c) Concurrency versus spacelike separation: in my world two events are concurrent because no message happens to connect them, which is contingent and which the processes themselves can change by what they choose to send, whereas in Minkowski spacetime two events are spacelike separated because of the metric, and nothing any event does can alter that. Is that a distortion that matters, or a harmless difference in what fixes the relation? (d) The absence of a global clock: in my world it is an engineering fact and removable in principle, since one can imagine a broadcast tick or rent one from an infrastructure provider, whereas in relativity it is structural. Does the argument need the impossibility, or only the fact, and if it needs only the fact, does it lose its force when the same fact is contingent?

Then give me the verdict: what survives, what is distorted in the carrying, and what cannot be said in my vocabulary at all. Of the residue, tell me which parts indicate that the argument is less clear than it looks, and which indicate that my framework is simply missing something the argument needs.

I would much rather have pushback than a tidy answer. If the translation is rigged — if my vocabulary has the conclusion built into it, so that the argument comes out either trivially true or unstatable — say so plainly and tell me what that shows about the argument rather than about my vocabulary. If my reconstruction above is a caricature, or if the argument is too vague or too contested to translate at all, or if the exercise is not worth doing, say that instead of manufacturing a mapping. I am asking for a translation and an honest account of its limits, not for a demonstration that my way of thinking can absorb the argument.
