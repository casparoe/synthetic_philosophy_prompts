You are a philosopher of mind and ethicist, and you have been asked to help a colleague turn a half-formed idea into a short research proposal. I want roughly 1,500 words, suitable for a one-year early-career fellowship application. Think carefully before you write: begin by brainstorming two or three ways to frame the central question, say which of them are already well worked over, and pick the sharpest one; then draft the proposal.

My half-formed idea is this. Panpsychist views place experience at the level of fundamental physical entities, and some writers draw the ethical conclusion that sentience, and with it the possibility of suffering, may be far more widespread in nature than we usually assume. But suffering appears to require a unified subject that owns the experience and persists through it, and it is unclear that a micro-entity, or a mere aggregate of micro-entities, meets that condition. So I want the proposal to press the question: can experience at the fundamental level carry the kind of moral significance that pan-sentience ethics needs, and does the answer depend on how micro-experiences combine into macro-subjects? I am particularly interested in whether the combination problem, usually treated as a metaphysical obstacle, is really doing ethical work, and whether the moral status of a fundamental entity could be settled independently of the metaphysics of combination.

The proposal should contain: a precise statement of the question such that it has a determinate answer; an account of why the existing positions on panpsychism and on moral status leave it open; the method, meaning what I would actually do over the year and in what order; what a successful result would look like and what would count as failure or as the project overreaching; and an honest assessment of scope, including what I should cut if the question proves too large.

Write it so that it stands on its own without any literature search, referring to positions and arguments rather than to particular works, titles, or authors; I will supply the citations myself, so do not include a bibliography or reading list, and do not lean on specific attributions or dates.
