heres the draft, minus the intro and the bit where i define terms:

**Whos owed what when you dont tip**

Tipping, queueing and jaywalking all get filed under "local customs", but i think theyre doing different jobs. Tipping in a place where servers wages are set on the assumption of tips is a price-shifting device: the norm is already priced into someones paycheck, so not tipping isnt just skipping a custom, its taking money off a particular person. A queue is a pure coordination device — the line is one of many possible solutions to "whos next" and its authority comes entirely from the fact that everyone is already in it. Jaywalking rules are a risk-allocation device: they hand out a scarce good (crossing time) and a bad (getting hit) between pedestrians and drivers.

Now, every convention does at least two jobs. It coordinates — tells you what to expect so you can act in concert with strangers — and it marks membership, telling you and everyone else whose team youre on. I want to claim obligations only attach to the first. Where a convention is load-bearing for someones interests (the server whose wage assumes the tip, the person behind you who has arranged their afternoon around the queue) you owe them compliance, and you owe it *to them*, not to the convention. Where it isnt load-bearing, the only thing your breach damages is your legibility as a member, and thats a social cost, not a moral one. So the woman who jaywalks at 2am on an empty street has done nothing wrong, and neither has the visitor who simply doesnt know the local rate.

Heres the bit i actually care about. A visitor gets a discount on compliance proportional to how plausibly her breach reads as ignorance rather than as a statement — nobody owes anyone the impossible task of knowing every local norm on arrival. But the discount is forfeited the moment she knows, and not because she has acquired new duties: its because the same act, once known, has stopped being an accident and started being a claim. Call it the ratchet. Knowledge doesnt add an obligation, it removes a defence. Which is why the visitor whos been told twice about tipping and still doesnt tip is in a totally different moral position from the one whos just landed, even though the act and the effect are identical. (A friend of mine from Osaka says leaving money on the table is basically an insult where shes from, which is a nice test case — the tourist who tips there isnt being rude, shes just legible as a tourist.)

So: "when in Rome" isnt a moral rule. Its prudence and courtesy, plus a genuine moral rule in the narrow cases where a breach lands on a specific person. What looks like a duty of respect for the culture is really two things in one coat — a duty not to free-ride on arrangements other people are sustaining, and a non-moral interest in not being read as contemptuous.

my worries, in order:

2. does the ratchet work, or is it sleight of hand? saying knowledge removes a defence without adding a duty feels like cheating, since if the act changes meaning when known, and meaning is what makes it wrong, then knowledge is adding something after all. i want a non-question-begging way to state it if one exists.
