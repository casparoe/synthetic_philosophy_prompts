Ok so I've been reading up on the historical Jesus stuff and I've landed on a view I want you to stress-test. Here it is: the toolkit historians use to figure out what Jesus actually said and did — the criteria of embarrassment, dissimilarity, multiple attestation, coherence, that whole crowd — can, if you apply them carefully and look for where several of them converge, actually recover a reliable core. Not every word, obviously, but enough to say who he was in outline. I'm not asking weather it's "proven." I'm asking what would count against it.

So play it straight and give me the map. What would have to be true for this to be false? Which of the criteria's assumptions could break, and what would the evidence have to look like for each break? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want you to be honest about what the view's own defenders would concede counts against them, not just what the sceptics say. In particular:

- Is the whole thing circular? The criteria seem to lean on our picture of first-century Judaism and of early Christian communities, and that picture is itself reconstructed partly from these same texts. Does that sink it or just make it harder?
- Dissimilarity in particular bugs me. It seems designed to strip away anything that looks too Jewish or too churchy, which leaves you with a Jesus who is neither, and that feels like it's smuggling in a conclusion. Fair hit or cheap shot?
- Even if the criteria work, do they get you "who he was," or just a pile of disconnected sayings? They feel atomistic to me — they certify bits, not a person. How big is that gap, and is it a problem for the view or just a limit on how much you get?
- What's the control-case test? If you ran these criteria on some figure we can independently check — a Socrates, a modern cult leader, whoever — and they handed you a badly distorted picture, would that count against the method, or would defenders just say the cases aren't analogous? Can the method even be tested this way, or is every proposed control case going to get waved off?

Also tell me which of these the defenders would actually accept as a defeater and which they'd dismiss out of hand. And at the end give me your read: is this position genuinely exposed, or has it been qualified into something that can't lose? If it can't lose, is that a problem for it as a historical claim, or just the price of doing business?

One more thing, a bit of a niche angle I want you to engage with: treat the criteria like a detector with a false-positive and a false-negative rate. What would it even mean to estimate those rates here, and does the fact that we can't cleanly do that undermine the whole project or just cap how confident we're allowed to be?
