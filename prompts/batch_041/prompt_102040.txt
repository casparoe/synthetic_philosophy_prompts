I'm running next month's session of a small rationality discussion group — mixed crowd, two of them have seen a bit of game theory, the rest are smart non-specialists — and I want to do superrationality, but built around the Platonia dilemma and the Luring Lottery rather than the Prisoner's Dilemma that everyone's already been bored by. Quick setup so you're with me: in Platonia, an eccentric trillionaire gets 20 strangers together and says that if exactly one of them sends him a telegram (reverse charges) by noon tomorrow, that one person gets a billion dollars, and if more than one sends one or nobody does, nobody gets anything, and they can't coordinate. The Luring Lottery was a real stunt a magazine ran in the early 80s: you entered by mailing a postcard with the number 1 on it, you were allowed to write any number of entries on the card, and the prize was a million dollars divided by the total number of entries, going to one randomly chosen entry. Hofstadter's superrational idea, roughly, is that in a symmetric puzzle the answer is the same for everyone, so you reason as if everyone picks the same strategy.

Don't explain it to me, I already get it — I want a plan for how to teach it. Act like a philosopher who's taught decision theory to non-specialists and thinks carefully about where people trip. Start by brainstorming the dependency order: what has to be in place before "superrational" even parses for them, and what depends on what. Then tell me which one example should carry the whole session — Platonia or the Lottery — and what it has to have and lack to do that job, plus why I'm right to think the PD is a trap here. Give me the misconceptions they'll bring or form and exactly where in the sequence to head each one off (I'm already worried about "superrational = being nice / just Kant" and "it's magical thinking where my choosing makes them choose"). What should I openly refuse to cover at this level and say so out loud? And finish with the one closing question that would show me they actually got it rather than just nodded along.
