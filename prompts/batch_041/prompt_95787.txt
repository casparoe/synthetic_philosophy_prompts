Write a joke about Bernard Williams's charge that utilitarianism leaves the agent with one thought too many — the man who saves his drowning wife rather than a drowning stranger is expected, on the utilitarian view, to add the further thought that this is what the impartial standpoint licenses, and the trouble is precisely that extra thought. The joke should turn on the structure of the objection rather than on a vague clash between love and moral theory: on the lateness or redundancy of the justifying thought, on the fact that the theory faults the agent's deliberation rather than his choice, and on how a committed utilitarian might try to absorb the charge by writing partiality into the rules. It should land for a reader who already knows the debate and could be on either side of it; a punchline that merely says utilitarians are cold does not count.
