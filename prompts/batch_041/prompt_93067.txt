I am a high school student on my school's debate team, and our regional resolution turns on whether it is ever legitimate to generalize about a nation's people -- the kind of claim my grandfather makes when he says that Germans are orderly, that Brazilians are warm, or that the Danes must be the happiest people alive. Reading around, I can find people who defend such claims and people who denounce them, but almost nobody states what a claim of this kind would actually have to accomplish in order to be defensible, and that gap is what I would like you to fill. Please write out the desiderata: the full specification that any adequate account of national character would have to satisfy -- where "adequate" means good enough to tell me, in a debate round, whether some particular generalization is one I can responsibly assert, one I must qualify, or one I should abandon altogether. I want this to hold whether the account ends up treating national character as a real, measurable feature of a population or concludes that the whole notion should be discarded, so please include the requirements that a debunking account would also have to meet.

For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that motivates it, so I can see why it is a genuine demand on the account and not just a preference of one side. I would like the list to work through at least the following tensions, though you should add whatever else you think belongs: the reference-class problem of who is even included in "the Germans" or "the Danes" when citizenship, language, ancestry, and residence come apart; the difference between a claim that a trait is distributed unevenly across a population and a claim that it belongs to the population's essence; the fact that nations change over time, so that any trait claim seems to need a date attached, and what could possibly unify a people's character across that change; whether a true statement about a national average can ever license an inference about any individual member, given that patterns holding for a whole can reverse inside its subgroups; the boundary between a generalization and a prejudice, and whether that boundary depends on the trait named, on who is speaking, or on how the claim gets used; and the suspicion that the surveys which rank countries on happiness or on personality traits may be measuring widely shared stereotypes about those countries rather than any feature of the people themselves. Please also flag any pair of these requirements that I should not expect to be satisfied together -- if the standard is internally unsatisfiable, that is exactly the result I most want to see -- and tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the positions people actually take on my resolution. I do not need reading recommendations or citations; I want the standard of adequacy itself. A table is fine if it keeps the requirements and their motivating cases side by side.
