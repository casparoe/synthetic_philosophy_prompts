Build the case on the following piece of ordinary reproductive biology, which I state up front so you needn't supply it: the cell a sperm fertilizes is a secondary oocyte, arrested in metaphase II, so its second meiotic division — and with it the haploid set it will hand on — is not completed until after sperm entry; the two sister chromatids of each chromosome in that cell are not identical to one another, since each is a recombinant product of crossing over; and which chromatid ends up in the ovum, rather than being extruded in the second polar body, is settled by segregation.

Constraints: no science fiction and no exotic technology — no cloning, no gene editing, no artificial gametes, no twinning, no chimerism; both worlds must contain exactly one resulting individual; and nothing may differ between the two worlds except the segregation. Give me the scenario in one paragraph.

Finally, tell me how confident you are in each of your claims and single out the point you are least sure of.
