Here's what I've got from the last several capital cases this board has handled (names changed):

- Avery: robbery-murder. The conviction rested on one jailhouse informant plus a confession he gave after roughly thirty hours in an interrogation room with no lawyer. No physical evidence. - Boone: a drive-by killing. The evidence was strong — an eyewitness plus Boone's own boastful texts. But Boone was 19, he was the follower, he handed the gun to the older guy and ran before the shots. Board recommended commutation.

- Dunn: killing of a police officer during a traffic stop. Ballistics were contested, so there was some doubt, but Dunn was the sole actor and had a prior violent conviction. Board refused.
