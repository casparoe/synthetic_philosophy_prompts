So our town is launching its own scrip in three weeks — local money thing, shops that sign up take it and knock 5% off if you spend it rather than sit on it — and since I'm the one who talked the council into it, I'm giving the speech at the launch.

The trouble is I spent the winter reading Berkeley's Querist, which is nothing but questions, and one of them is "whether the true idea of money, as such, be not altogether that of a ticket or counter?" And the more I sit with it the more I think he's right: money is a ticket you hand over for goods, the whole point of a ticket is that you hand it in, a ticket you hoard is a ticket doing nothing, and what actually makes a place rich is the goods and the labour behind the counter, not the tickets. Which is honestly why I wanted the scrip in the first place — the spend-it-don't-save-it design is Berkeley's ticket idea with the hypocrisy taken out.

But it wrecks the speech I'd already written. I can't stand up and tell the shopkeepers the scrip is "as good as cash," because on this view nothing is as good as cash except the stuff cash buys; I can't promise it'll hold its value, because holding is the exact mistake; and I can't pitch it to my neighbours as a way to save, since Berkeley would say that's like saving up tickets. Half the room is shopkeepers who want to know they won't get left holding useless paper, the other half is neighbours who just want to feel good about shopping local, plus my mother-in-law, who is convinced Im running a scam.

Write me the five-minute launch speech: honest about what the scrip actually is on that picture, still gets people to sign up and spend it, and doesn't lecture anyone. And flag anything in the ticket picture I should be hedging on while I'm up there, because I'm going to be saying it like I mean it.
