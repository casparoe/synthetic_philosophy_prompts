I'm a senior at Roosevelt High and I'm the student rep on our district school board — I get an actual vote on everything except personnel and land, which is of course the only stuff that matters. Anyway, in two weeks we vote on a two-year contract, $180k a year out of the tech budget, with a company that puts monitoring software on every school-issued laptop. It reads what kids type in docs and chats, looks for signs of self-harm, bullying, and violent extremism, sends the flags to a counselor, and in the serious ones to the police, and it keeps the flagged material. The board is split, and whats frying my brain is that both sides are making the same argument. The superintendent says we can't wait for proof that it saves lives — if one kid dies while we run a nice careful study, that's on us, you don't get to be cautious about a fire that's already burning. The teacher rep says you can't deploy a thing whose harms we also can't undo: a kid flagged for a bad joke, a kid's private writing sitting in a police file when they're thirty, a school where nobody writes anything honest anymore. Both of them are saying don't act without being sure, and they want opposite things. My dad is a lawyer and he says the EU has an actual formal doctrine about this and that it doesn't just mean "ban it" — he says it comes with conditions, like you need a real scientific assessment of the risk first, the measures have to be proportionate and temporary and dropped when the evidence changes, and the burden of proof lands on whoever wants to introduce the thing rather than on whoever's worried about it. I definately can't tell if he's remembering that right.

Could you write the opinion I'm going to circulate to the board three days before the vote? It has to land on something we can actually do — approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and if it's conditions they need to be specific enough to amend the contract (who sees the flags, how long they're kept, what the counselor is allowed to do with them, what triggers a police report, when the whole thing shuts off if it isn't renewed) plus a date on which we're forced to revisit it. The reasoning matters more to me than the verdict. I want it to work out what the don't-act-without-certainty idea can actually tell a body like ours, and whether it even makes sense to apply it to the risk of having the software AND the risk of not having it — because doing nothing has victims too, and my civics teacher said if you take it seriously you'd never build anything, which I half believe and half think is a cheap move. And I want it honest about where the whole approach goes quiet and somebody just has to make a value call, since that's probably where I actually am. Please take the strongest version of whichever side you come out against seriously enough that they'd recognize themselves in it — half the board is going to hate whatever I hand them, and if the memo just says both sides have a point it's useless to me. One more thing: a board member wants to table the vote for a year to collect data, and I honestly can't tell if that's caution or cowardice with a nicer name.
