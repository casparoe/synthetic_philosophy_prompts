Im arguing there's one idea underneath both. Liberty matters because it's the condition of individual self-development, and that same idea is what lets him countenance a lot of state action — co-ops, inheritance limits, some market interference — while giving up nothing he said about free speech or experiments in living. The economic stuff is just experiments in living applied to how work is organized.

Give me the objections I'll actually get, from which of them, in the words they'd use, ordered by how much damage they do to the argument, each with a two-minute reply and an honest note on where that reply is soft.
