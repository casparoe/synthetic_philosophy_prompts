I spent last week sitting in on a graduate seminar for a piece I'm writing on the small revival of interest in Aristotle's economics, and I came away with an argument that a student in the room constructed and that none of us could dismantle on the spot. He laid it out as follows. (1) Justice in exchange requires that the things exchanged be commensurable: if a builder and a shoemaker are to trade a house for a quantity of shoes, there must be some respect in which the house and the shoes are equal, or the exchange is a mugging dressed up as a contract. (2) The only candidate for that common measure is need; but need is not a measure at all, since it varies from person to person and hour to hour and admits of no unit. (3) So, to make exchange determinate, we introduce money, which Aristotle tells us exists by convention rather than by nature. (4) But if money's worth is conventional, then whatever rate the two parties settle on is by definition the just rate, since there is no independent fact of the matter for the bargain to get wrong. (5) Therefore, between consenting adults there can be no unjust exchange; a bad bargain is simply a bargain. (6) Aristotle plainly thinks otherwise, treating the petty trader and the money-lender as corrupt and holding that exchange can be unjust. (7) So one of these steps is false.

I would rather keep (6) than any of the others, but I want to know what I am paying for it. Tell me which step to reject, explain why that step looked so plausible in the first place, and then price the alternatives for me. In particular, if you keep (6), I need to understand what work money is actually doing in the argument: whether it supplies a measure of some real magnitude or merely a ratio between things, and whether calling its value conventional can mean anything other than calling it arbitrary. It would also help to know whether the flaw, if there is one, is a genuine gap in the reasoning or only a gap in this particular reconstruction of it -- because the student was adamant that he had done nothing but paraphrase.
