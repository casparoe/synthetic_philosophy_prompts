I'm a science journalist — mostly biotech and pharma stuff, but I've been trying to write a longer "ideas" piece about how you tell a healthy research field from a dying one, and I've been leaning on Lakatos's essay about research programmes. The draft, roughly, goes like this: a programme has a hard core its practitioners won't abandon, a protective belt of auxiliary assumptions they're allowed to tinker with, and a heuristic that tells them which tinkering is legitimate. A programme is progressive when the tinkering keeps generating new predictions that actually pan out, and degenerating when the belt just grows to explain away facts everyone already had. Lakatos himself was pretty relaxed about degenerating spells — he thought you should stick with a programme for a long while before giving up, since you can't always tell in the moment which way it's going. My essay's spin is that this test is close to useless in real time, because there's always someone promising the novel prediction is just around the corner, and I try to replace it with a distinction between what I'm calling rearguard and vanguard belt-modifications: rearguard ones only restore contact with old data (adding an epicycle, a free parameter, an extra field), while vanguard ones add contact with data you couldn't have gotten before. The diagnostic I propose isn't "is this programme progressive right now" but "is the ratio of rearguard to vanguard modifications getting worse over time" — a kind of belt-thickening index.

To make it concrete I use the dark matter vs. modified-gravity fight in cosmology. My read is that the dark matter programme keeps buying itself vanguard moves (structure formation predictions, the bullet-cluster stuff, direct-detection targets), whereas MOND's defenders mostly make rearguard moves — each new galaxy-scale anomaly gets a new tweak to the interpolation function or a new auxiliary field — but then I get nervous, because I can also see the reverse reading: every null result from a detector looks like dark matter's belt getting thicker for no new prediction, and MOND's successes at least post-dicted things nobody expected it to. I don't have a settled view and that's kind of the point of the piece.

What I want from you is comments, but I'd rather they be pointed than general. First, on accuracy: have I stated Lakatos's position fairly, or have I flattened something — in particular, is the progressive/degenerating distinction really as retrospective as I'm making it out to be, and does my rearguard/vanguard split actually add anything he didn't already have, or is it just his own distinction wearing a hat? Second, on the argument: is the belt-thickening index a real improvement or is it unfalsifiable hand-waving that a defender of any programme could game? Third, on the cosmology bit — is my dark matter/MOND application fair to both sides, and which reading do you think a working cosmologist would push back on hardest? And finally, the boring question: is this an interesting angle or have I just reinvented the demarcation problem with extra steps. Be blunt, I can take it, I've been workshopping this with a friend who does philosophy of physics and he keeps saying I'm smuggling in a falsificationist assumption I haven't earned, which I suspect is fair but I want to hear it from someone else before I rewrite.
