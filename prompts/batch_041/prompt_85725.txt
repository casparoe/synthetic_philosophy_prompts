My department runs a small interview podcast, and the next episode is a long-form conversation with a philosopher of work whose book manuscript I have been reading in draft. She defends what she calls a "practice-first" account of meaningful work, and I want my questions to land on the view itself rather than on the well-worn topic of automation.

Her position, as I understand it, has four planks. (1) Meaningful work is not a matter of the value of what is produced, nor of the worker's subjective satisfaction, but of the worker's standing as a co-author of the norms by which the work is judged: to work meaningfully is to have a say in what counts as doing the work well. (2) Automation characteristically strips out judgment rather than effort — it absorbs precisely those tasks whose standards are already fixed and legible — so it threatens meaning exactly where it succeeds, demoting co-authors to end-users of a process they no longer set. (3) The distributive question raised by automation is therefore not income but authority: she proposes an enforceable claim to co-govern some productive practice, which she calls a "right to a domain", and treats basic income as a distraction. (4) She insists this is not Luddism — automating drudgery is fine, because drudgery just is work whose standards the worker never authored and cannot contest.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions. They should press on the content of the view rather than invite her to restate it, and each should come with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give — the standard move she will reach for, and the question that makes that move costly. I would like at least one question each on:

- the solitary or hierarchically embedded craftsperson — a lone potter, or a surgeon in an operating theatre — cases where meaning seems present without co-authorship of the norms, or absent despite it;
- whether "co-authorship of norms" describes what workers actually care about, or whether the account quietly substitutes a political ideal for a psychological one (and what it says about a worker who wholeheartedly endorses standards she had no hand in setting);
- AI-assisted work, where the human is retained as a validator of a machine's judgment — in particular whether she accepts the apparent consequence that a clinician's work can become less meaningful while her patients do better;
- whether plank (4) can be held alongside plank (1), given that most waged work under a manager is work whose standards the worker did not author;
- what "co-governing a domain" could concretely mean for a production process that is globally distributed and increasingly machine-paced — and what becomes of the proposal if it turns out to be unenforceable.

No generic questions: nothing about how she came to the topic, and nothing whose answer is "that depends on your theory of meaning." I do not want the questions to presuppose that the view is either obviously correct or obviously unstable. If the framing I have given you is not a position anyone could actually hold, or if I have described it in a way that makes the request ill-posed, say so plainly rather than writing questions around it; and if some question I have asked for cannot be made genuinely pressing without knowing more of her view, tell me what you would need to know.
