A colleague and I are drafting a joint lecture on consciousness, and we disagree about a line she wants to put on a slide: that the hard problem is really a meta-problem, so the task is not to explain experience but to explain why anyone thinks there is a problem of experience in the first place. She reads the line as licensing the field to stop chasing experience and start accounting for the intuition; I suspect it is several claims wearing one coat, only one of which does the work she wants. Lay the readings out for me as a menu: the distinct precise theses that sentence could be making, ordered from the merely taxonomic through the deflationary to the flatly implausible, with a verdict on each — platitude, substantive and defensible, or false — and a note on what each would imply for whether the hard problem survives as a live research question. Then say which reading she must intend if the slide is to be more than a truism, and what further claim she would have to defend to get from that reading to the conclusion she wants.
