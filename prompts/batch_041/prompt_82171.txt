I've been poking at Copycat-style letter-string puzzles — the microdomain where you're shown something like 'abc is to abd' and asked what 'ijk' is to. I get the surface story: the program doesn't have its concepts fixed in advance, it builds them on the fly, and the same puzzle can come out as 'ijl' on one run and 'ijd' on another. What I dont have a grip on is what conceptual slippage actually is as a mechanism rather than a slogan. When a concept "slips" in a microdomain like that, what exactly is doing the slipping, what is it slipping toward, and why is that supposed to be more than just having a big pile of concepts plus a similarity metric over them? Second half of the question: what does the fluidity cost? If concepts are allowed to drift, what stops the whole thing from dissolving into "everything is a bit like everything else", and why isn't a plain probability distribution over possible continuations already the same thing as a fluid concept?
