Ok so my mate is an archaeologist and he's always going on about what actually counts as a society collapsing versus just changing into something else. Here's his track record as far as I can piece it together. Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean: yes, collapse — Hittite empire gone, Mycenaean palaces burnt, big cities abandoned, population way down, even writing disappears for a while. Western Roman Empire: yes, collapse, and he says this even though the eastern half carries on and plenty of people just keep living their lives. Norse Greenland colony: yes, collapse, it simply ends. Easter Island: yes, collapse, trees gone, the chiefly order comes apart. Tokugawa Japan turning into Meiji Japan: no, that's a transformation, nothing actually disappears except the shogunate. Ming to Qing: no, just a change of dynasty, the state is still there.

Now there's two new cases I want his verdict on. Classic Maya: the lowland cities get abandoned, the kings stop putting up monuments, the whole classic political order is finished by around 800-1000 CE, but there are still millions of Maya people living there today speaking Mayan languages. And the Black Death in 14th century Europe: something like a third to half of everyone dies, but the kingdoms, the church, the towns all just keep going.

What I actually want isn't the yes/no, it's the rule he seems to be running underneath. I think it's one of two: (a) collapse = a big lasting drop in population, or (b) collapse = the political order, the people who run things, actually disappearing. Both seem to fit everything he's said so far as far as I can tell. Which do you reckon he's really using, what does each one predict for the Maya and for the Black Death, and if I wanted to run one more case past him to definitively seperate the two rules, what should it be?
