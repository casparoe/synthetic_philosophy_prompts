I sit on my college's dining advisory committee and we vote next week on a proposal from the sustainability club. Right now the main dining hall serves beef and chicken at most meals. The club wants us to drop beef entirely and make chicken the default animal protein, because beef has a far bigger carbon footprint per serving. Most of the committee thinks this is a slam dunk and it probably passes unless I raise something.

My problem is I've been reading about animal welfare and I suspect the climate framing is steering us toward a decision that's actually worse for animals. As best I can work out, you get a lot more meals out of one cow than out of one chicken, so pound for pound chicken might mean many more individual animals raised and killed, even if the carbon math looks better. And the plant-forward stuff we'd nudge people toward isn't free either — crops get planted, harvested and sprayed, which kills a lot of field animals (mice, birds, insects), and once you let those count even a little, the whole comparison gets murky. So I'm not even sure "less meat" is the safe option.

I don't want to be the guy who torpedoes an obvious climate win over a philosophical tecnicality. But I also don't want us to vote for something that quietly increases the total amount of suffering in the world while we congratulate ourselves. Can you write the short memo I'll circulate before the meeting? I need an actual recommendation I can put to a vote — keep beef, drop it, or something in between like swapping the default or a partial phase-out — with a time frame and reasons the rest of the committee can actually push back on. Please take the club's side seriously rather than just dunking on it; if they're right I'd like to know that too, and where the numbers are genuinely uncertain, say so instead of pretending we know.
