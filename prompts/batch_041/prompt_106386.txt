I retired a couple of years ago after 35 years in a lab (neutron scattering, nothing glamorous), and I've spent the time since reading philosphy, which has been more humbling than I expected. Here's what I'm after.

Dennett's idea that the self is a center of narrative gravity keeps nagging at me, mostly because of the analogy with my old job. A center of mass is a fiction — nobody thinks there's a little pellet of stuff inside a hammer — but the hammer obeys it: shove the thing at that point and the whole hammer moves as if the mass lived there. And the point can sit in empty space, like the middle of a donut, with nothing there at all. The self is supposed to be the same kind of abstraction, a story the brain tells about itself, except for one asymmetry that strikes me as the seed of a joke: a center of mass never insists that it's real, and nothing has to tell it. A self is a story that exists only because the thing its supposed to explain keeps telling it, and the storyteller can't step outside the story to check whether it's true.

So write me a joke built on that difference — the fiction an object obeys without believing in it, versus the fiction that exists only because the thing it explains keeps insisting on it. It should be funny to someone who actually knows the Dennett picture, i.e. the punchline turns on that asymmetry rather than on a generic "the self is an illusion" gag, and it shouldn't need any particular paper or citation to land. Use the donut if it helps. Start with the joke itself, no preamble, and if a one-line tag after it helps the thing land, that's fine.
