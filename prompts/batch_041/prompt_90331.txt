Ok so here's a position I've basically talked myself into, and I want you to try to knock it down. The view: the single most important moral project available to us is using biotechnology to get rid of suffering — not just human suffering, but suffering across the whole tree of sentient life, wild animals very much included. And not just painkillers and therapy; actually re-engineering the machinery of negative experience itself, so the capacity for aversive states gets phased out over time and what's left is something like a gradient of wellbeing that never dips below zero. Call it a hedonic cleanup. I think if you take suffering as intrinsically bad and take the scale of it seriously — an enormous number of animals, a lot of it happening right now, most of it with no redeeming narrative — then this looks like the obvious priority, and everything else (poverty, injustice, even death) is downstream or at least smaller.

What I want from you: what would count against this? Real defeaters, not vibes. Which empirical findings, which conceptual objections, which possible outcomes of actually trying it should move me, sorted from fatal to merely awkward. A few things I already worry about, so tell me whether they're the right worries or whether I'm missing bigger ones:

- the backfire worry: if you make a species' life go better you might just get more of them, and if their lives are still net negative, total suffering goes up. Does that sink the project or is it a technical snag?
- the contrast worry: is a life with no capacity for suffering actually impoverished — missing striving, meaning, depth — or is that just sour grapes from people who've never tried it? What would have to be true about value for the no-suffering world to come out worse?
- the authenticity worry: engineered contentment seems fake in a way earned contentment doesn't. Is that a genuine defeater or an intuition pump?
- the nociception thing: you can in principle keep the flinch and lose the ouch — strip the felt badness while leaving the protective reflex intact. Does that make the project more tractable than it sounds, or does it show that once you've done it you've changed what the beings are, so the whole "same beings, less suffering" framing is a lie?

Also the honest meta-question: is this view even falsifiable, or have I built it so nothing counts against it? Is there any observation that would make me drop it, or is it the kind of view where suffering-is-bad does all the work and no evidence can touch it? If it's insulated, is that a bug, or just what any fundamental moral commitment looks like?

And separately from evidence — what would the people who hold this view themselves have to concede counts against them? Not what critics say, but where the view is actually exposed. Rank everything, and be willing to tell me it's bulletproof and that that's a problem.
