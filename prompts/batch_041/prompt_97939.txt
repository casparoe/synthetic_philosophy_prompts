Write a short story set in a society that has abolished the vocabulary of shared properties. Its members speak only of one thing resembling another, and their instruments can register resemblance only by comparing an object to a stored exemplar rather than to a kind: to call two things the same color is, for them, to say that each resembles the same stored sample. A student in this society notices that whenever two things resemble a common exemplar they must also resemble each other, and that this second resemblance cannot itself be checked against an exemplar without presupposing the very thing it was meant to explain. Write a short story that dramatizes this discovery through a concrete episode rather than a lecture, and let it take a genuine stand on whether the society's resemblance-talk has left out something real.
