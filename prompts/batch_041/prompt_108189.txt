Good's theorem on the value of information says that, for an expected-utility maximizer with a fixed prior and a fixed utility function over outcomes, the ex ante expected value of costless, perfectly reliable information is non-negative. I want your credence that the following stronger, philosophical claim is true: the non-negativity of the value of information is a constraint on practical rationality as such—so that any agent who assigns strictly negative ex ante value to costless, perfectly reliable information is thereby irrational, regardless of whether her preferences are representable by expected utility. Assume the agent's preferences over final outcomes are held fixed across information states, her beliefs are updated by conditionalization, and she chooses the act she most prefers given her beliefs.

Decompose your credence. At a minimum, give a rough probability for each of the following, with one line of justification, and then combine them:
(i) the probability that there is a coherent way to hold preferences over final outcomes fixed across information states without already presupposing an expected-utility representation;
(ii) the probability that non-expected-utility models—such as regret-based or ambiguity-averse models—can generate negative value of information even when the agent's preferences over final outcomes are fixed and she updates by conditionalization;
(iii) the probability that any such case should be classified as a failure of rationality rather than as a case where the decision problem itself changed (for example, the information altered the set of available acts or the agent's preferences; a narrative-utility agent who cares about how she came to know something is a useful test case, since it is unclear whether the narrative belongs to the final outcome or to the preference structure);
(iv) any other sub-question you think belongs.
Then tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, which term you are least confident in, and what argument or evidence would move it most.
