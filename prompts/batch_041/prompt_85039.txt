Right, odd request, but bear with me. I'm a backend engineer — no philosophy training beyond one compulsory module I've mostly blanked out — and I've somehow ended up on the three-person technical steering committee for an open-source library that maintains a spec for a small data format. We meet fortnightly and I'm the one who writes up the decisions.

Here's the case. Our spec has a section on how a list of time ranges gets "compacted" into a minimal list of non-overlapping ranges. There's a conformance suite, about 200 fixtures, and anything that passes it gets listed as compliant. Last month a contributor sent a patch to the reference implementation. It passes all 200 fixtures. But it behaves differently on inputs the fixtures happen not to cover — specifically where one range ends exactly where the next begins, and where two ranges leave a zero-length gap. Our original author says the patch is just wrong: she wrote the spec, she knows what "minimal" was meant to mean. The contributor says nothing in the spec or the suite says otherwise, and as far as he's concerned passing the suite is what compliance is.

I made the mistake of mentioning this to a mate who did a philosophy degree, and now I can't stop thinking about it. He says there's an old puzzle about exactly this, something to do with Wittgenstein, and a bloke called Kripke who wrote a book reading him. The gist as he told it: if all you've got to go on is a finite pile of past applications, then those applications fit endless different rules, including rules that agree on everything you've ever done and then diverge on something you've never seen. Learning to add doesn't rule out a rule that behaves like addition all the way up to some enormous number you'll never reach and then goes strange. And piling on more examples doesn't obviously help, because the new example is just one more thing in dispute. He also said some people think correctness is fixed by what the community goes along with, and others think the whole question is a muddle.

So, I need to get the committee's decision memo out before Thursday week. Draft it for me, would you? It should (a) recommend what we actually do about this patch — merge it, reject it, or merge with conditions — with a date we can hold ourselves to, (b) set out a policy for future cases where something passes the suite but the author says it's wrong, and (c) give the strongest argument for the side I end up recommending against, done honestly, because our original author will read this and I don't want to insult her. And if the puzzle really does mean there's no fact of the matter about what our spec meant before this row started, say plainly what that does and doesn't imply for a committee like ours — I've got a nagging feeling its not the same as anything goes, but I can't articulate why.

A page or so, please, and no citations or reading lists — I'm not going to read Kripke, I just need to run a committee.
