I'm building a little taxonomy of ways people try to make the presumption of independence rigorous — you know the move, a combinatorialist says "a random object wouldn't have any weird coincidences, so this one probably doesn't either" and then reasons as if its a proof. I've got seven candidate formalisations and I want them ranked into tiers by how much of the original intuition each one actually captures. Here they are, roughly as I've jotted them down:

1. Measure-theoretic / generic-case: fix a probability space over the class of objects, show the property holds with probability 1 (or 1 minus something tiny), and treat "probably" as licence to assume it of the particular object in front of you.
2. Incompressibility: assume the object has high Kolmogorov complexity, i.e. no short description, and derive the property from the fact that any structure in it would hand you a short description.
3. General position: assume no accidental algebraic or geometric coincidences — no unexpected linear dependence, no small collisions, nothing that holds only by fluke.
4. Defeasible defaults: treat "the overwhelming majority of objects in this class are like so" as a non-monotonic rule of inference, so you may infer the property unless something specifically defeats it.
5. Bayesian: put a prior over the class, condition on what you actually know about the object, read off the posterior.
6. Pseudorandomness / derandomisation: assume the object is indistinguishable from random to the relevant tests, or that a short seed generates it, and then reason as if it were genuinely random.
7. Deflationary: it isn't a claim at all but a research heuristic — a policy for where to spend your time — so asking whether it's "true" is a category error.

Sort these into tiers, from "basically the real thing" down to "a metaphor wearing a lab coat", with a sentence for each on where it lands and what it buys you that the others don't. Then collapse the whole list to two tiers and tell me where the cut falls and why. Finally, say which one you'd bet an actual proof on, and where each of the others quietly cheats — especially whether number 7 is doing anything or just refusing to play.

Please write it in British English — "randomised", "behaviour", "formalisation", that sort of thing.
