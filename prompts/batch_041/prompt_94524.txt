Every time the Fermi paradox comes up on here someone says "dark forest" and we all nod, and I've started to suspect we're nodding at a vibe. Before I accept any answer to the silence, I want the spec: what does an answer have to deliver to count as an answer? Give me the requirements, each with the consideration that forces it, mark which are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste, and flag any pair you think can't be satisfied together.

Things I'd want on the list, argue me out of them if there wrong: it has to explain the absence of probes and megastructures, not just the absence of radio chatter; it can't make us special, so no answer that needs Earth to be first, luckiest, or the only one that didn't get the memo; it has to hold across cosmic time rather than describe a phase we happen to be catching; it has to survive the fact that we've barely looked and only just started; and it can't require every civilization in the galaxy to independently land on the same strange policy forever.

That last one is where I keep getting stuck on the dark forest itself. It needs a first strike to be cheap, permanent and unpunishable, and it needs coordination between civilizations to be impossible forever -- but the civilizations doing the striking are supposed to be ones that solved much harder coordination problems at home in order to get off their planets at all. Is that a real tension, or is there a version of the view that gets around it? Then tell me which single requirement does the most to rule out the popular answers.
