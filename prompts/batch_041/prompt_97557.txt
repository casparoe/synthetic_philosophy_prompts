I'm a student in a game theory and social choice course, and our course reader includes a short sidebar comparing Nash's solution to the two-person bargaining problem with the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution. It runs as follows:

"Alongside Nash's solution, bargaining theory offers the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution. Nash's solution selects the feasible agreement that maximizes the product of the two players' gains over the disagreement point; the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution instead draws a straight line from the disagreement point to the ideal point — the pair consisting of each player's best attainable payoff — and takes the point where that line meets the Pareto frontier. It therefore asks each player to concede the same amount, and for this reason it is usually called the egalitarian solution: it gives both players the same payoff. The axiomatic contrast is what makes the pair interesting. Kalai and Smorodinsky proved that their solution is the unique one satisfying Pareto optimality, symmetry, scale invariance, and independence of irrelevant alternatives, while Nash's solution is singled out by a monotonicity requirement — the Nash payoff can never fall for either player when the set of feasible agreements grows."
