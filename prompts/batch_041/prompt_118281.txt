That distinction is the whole reason the thing is interesting and I feel like everyone flattens it.

Two different universal machines give you priors that differ only by a constant factor — the constant depends on how long it takes to simulate one machine on the other. So in the limit they agree, but at finite data the constant can completely swamp the difference, and there's no bound anywhere on how long "in the limit" takes. The scenario our prof put on the board was: suppose the true process is a short program in machine V and a long one in machine U. Then V's predictor beats U's for a very long time. But U's does catch up. Eventually. Make me a story where somebody has to live inside that "eventually."

So the question I want humming underneath it: is simplicity a fact about the world, or a fact about the notation you happened to pick?
