I'm a science journalist working up a long feature on the "is X a disease?" fights -- obesity, aging, burnout, the stuff that keeps getting added to and struck from the diagnostic manuals -- and every philosopher I call ends up walking me into the naturalism vs. normativism thing about health and disease. Is a disease a biological fact, some part failing at the job it was selected for measured against a species-typical baseline, or is it inescapably value-laden -- harm, unwantedness, social norms about what counts as a bad life?

I don't want the survey version. I want to know where the next three years of work in that debate are best spent. So what are the open questions, and how would you rank them by what actually matters? Defend the order: which ones are upstream of which, which are tractable, which look juicy but wouldn't move any real decision (what gets treated, what insurers pay for, what regulators approve), and what would change your ranking. And while your at it, tell me whether the move I keep hearing -- just engineer the concept to do whatever work we want it to, the way some people say we should with race -- is a genuine option here or a dodge, especially for the hard cases where the underlying quantity is a smooth continuum with no natural cut point (blood pressure, BMI, plain sadness).
