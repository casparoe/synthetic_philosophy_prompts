My roommate and I keep getting into it over this. She says "what if everyone did that?" like it's checkmate — most recently when I told her I wasn't going to vote this cycle, and she said to imagine everyone staying home. I've heard the same move from my mom about me not returning the shopping cart, and from a friend about buying stuff from a fast-fashion site. It's obviously supposed to be Kant's universalizability test in street clothes, but half the time it sounds like she just means "if everyone did it, the consequences would be bad," which is definately not the same thing, right?

I want a proper breakdown of the distinct things "what if everyone did that?" could actually be asserting. Like: (a) the prudential/Hobbesian warning that if everyone did it you personally would end up worse off; (b) the rule-consequentialist claim that the world where everyone does it is worse overall; (c) the Kantian move where you try to universalize your maxim and hit a contradiction; (d) something about fairness, cheating, or free-riding; (e) the universal-prescriptivism thing where you have to be able to endorse everyone acting on the same principle. And within the Kantian reading there's the further mess about whether the contradiction is a contradiction in conception or a contradiction in the will, and about how you're even supposed to describe the maxim in the first place — my maxim isn't "don't vote," it's "skip voting in this particular low-stakes election because I'm busy," and does that universalize fine? If the answer depends on how I word the maxim, that seems like a problem.

For each reading, tell me whether it's trivial or substantive, whether it's actually plausible, and whether it even gets her to "so you should vote." Then tell me which reading people usually have in mind when they deploy the line, because I suspect it's the vaguest one. Also, which of these readings survive the standard objections — the gap between "everyone doing it" and "everyone being permitted to do it," the act/omission thing, the fact that some maxims universalize cleanly and still feel wrong. Lay it out as a menu I can actually argue from, not a lecture.
