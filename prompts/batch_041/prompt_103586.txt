I'm running the discussion section for a 200-level "Minds, Brains and Machines" course and I've got the session on Dennett's four creatures — the Darwinian one that's just born knowing its tricks and can't learn a thing inside its own lifetime, the Skinnerian one that gets smarter the hard way, by getting burned, the Popperian one that can run the trial inside its own head and let the bad hypothesis die instead of itself, and the Gregorian one that thinks with tools, words, and other people's brains. The students will have done the reading but I really don't want to just redraw the ladder on the board again, because every time I do that they nod along and clearly don't feel it. What I want them to actually feel is two things they always miss: first, that it's not a ladder you climb off of — a Popperian is still a Skinnerian is still a Darwinian, the higher levels sit on top of the lower ones rather than replacing them — and second, that the jump to the Gregorian level isn't just "more cleverness", it's that part of the thinking gets moved out of the skull and onto stuff in the world, so the mind ends up partly on the table and not entirely inside anyone's head.

So heres the shape I want to try, and I'd like you to build it out into something I can actually run: one single task, played four times, where the ONLY thing that changes between rounds is what the creature is allowed to do about its mistakes. Round 1 nobody can learn — you commit to your rule before the round starts and live with it. Round 2 you only get to learn from feedback you actually earn by acting and paying for it. Round 3 you get a dry run, where you can discover you'd have been wrong without paying. Round 4 you're allowed to leave something behind on the table for whoever goes next, and I want that leftover thing to genuinely do some of the thinking rather than just record a result — the test is that the next player is measurably better off even though the person who made it has left the room. Give me the rules, the materials (cheap — index cards, a timer, a die, that kind of thing), how to split up ~16 students across one 75-minute block, a scoring scheme that doesn't need money, and a debrief that maps each round onto its creature and lands the nesting and offloading points without me just lecturing at them.

Also please be straight with me about two things: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (my guess is that round 3 collapses into pure guessing and that someone complains round 1 is just unfair), and which feature of the framework the game simply can't reproduce, so that I have to supply it by talking instead. Oh and — don't name the framework or the creatures to the students until after the debrief, the whole point is that they hit it themselves first.
