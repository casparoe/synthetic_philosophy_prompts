Wanna build a prompt around the socialist calculation debate. Heres the episode: Chile under Allende, 1971-73, Project Cybersyn. Stafford Beer, a British management cybernetician, gets brought in by a young Chilean official (Fernando Flores) to put telex machines in the nationalized factories, feed their production numbers into a statistical filter (Cyberstride) that flags anomalies -- absenteeism spiking, a factory not getting its inputs -- and display the whole thing in that famous operations room in Santiago, the screens, the sci-fi look. It got used to route supplies during the October 1972 truckers strike, and then the coup killed it. Beer also wanted a channel for ordinary people to signal how things were going, which never got off the drawing board.

Analyze this as a test of the calculation debate. Mises said rational allocation is impossible without market prices for capital goods; Hayek's later version says the real problem is the division of knowledge -- dispersed, tacit, local, never given to a single mind -- and that prices are a discovery procedure; Lange's answer was that a central board could set prices parametrically and adjust them by trial and error while managers followed simple rules. So: in Cybersyn, whos the planner, whats doing the work that prices do in Hayek's story, what knowledge actually flowed up those telex lines and what knowledge couldnt be pushed through them at all. Then the reverse direction: what does the episode show about the argument itself -- a point for Lange, a point for Hayek, or does it slide past the whole dispute because prices werent actually abolished in Chile and the thing sat on top of a still-marketish economy at a small scale? Be honest about what the case can settle and what it cant, flag where youre inferring past what you actually know about the history, and say what the calculation argument should look like after taking this seriously -- if anything changes at all.
