Construct a thought experiment in which an autonomous system causes a serious harm and, under a control-based theory of moral responsibility, no human being is responsible, while under an answerability or social-role theory, exactly one human being is responsible. The case must be realistic—a hospital, a workplace, or a public infrastructure setting rather than science fiction—and the system must not itself be a moral agent; the harm must result from the system’s exercise of discretion, not from a mechanical malfunction. The gap must not arise merely from unpredictability, opacity, or missing information; it must arise from a structural feature of how agency is distributed across designers, deployers, and operators, such as nested or divided control in which each person controls a proper part of the decision procedure but no person controls the whole. Keep the scenario to one paragraph. Then identify the structural feature doing the work; show why the control-based theory yields no responsible party and the answerability theory yields one; say who is responsible and for what; explain what happens to the gap if that feature is removed; and explain how a defender of the control-based theory would try to close the gap without abandoning the theory. The verdicts must follow from the theories rather than being stipulated by the scenario.
