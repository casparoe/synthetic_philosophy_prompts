Next month I'm running the session at our local rationality-adjacent reading group and I've landed on the sentience-is-a-matter-of-degree thing — the whole ladder from insects up (or down?) to thermostats, and what that does to moral status. I don't want you to explain the position to me, I want you to plan how I explain it. The room is about fifteen people, smart but not philosophers: two programmers, an ICU nurse, a guy who's read a lot of pop-sci and is now pretty sure octopuses are basically people, and one persistant heckler who keeps saying that if moral status comes in degrees then we can never say kicking a dog is *really* wrong, it's just, like, less optimal — and that this collapses into "nothing matters." I've got about seventy-five minutes and a whiteboard.

Here's the spine I want to try, tell me if it's a mistake. I found a blog comment a while back arguing you should split the question into two axes instead of one — (a) how bad the worst state a creature can be in actually is, hedonic intensity basically, and (b) how much of a creature there even is to have a state at all, like whether there's a "someone home" across time for the badness to accrue to — and I want to teach the whole session off those two axes. So: what has to come first, second, third, and why each piece depends on the one before it; what single carrying example should hold the hour together (I was going to reach for the bee that stops grooming after an injury, or the rat that's paralyzed but still has pain processing intact — tell me which and why, and what the example has to have and has to lack, e.g. it can't already smuggle in a theory of consciousness); the misconceptions people will walk in with *and* the ones they'll form live, in the order they'll hit them, and exactly where in the sequence I should head each one off; one question I can throw out near the end that would actually tell me whether they got the *degrees* part rather than just getting squeamish about bugs; and what I should flat-out refuse to settle at this level and say so out loud.
