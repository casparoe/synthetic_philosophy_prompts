I've spent the last few months reporting on end-of-life medicine and, weirdly, on autonomous weapons, and both keep dumping me into the killing-vs-letting-die thing, so I figured I'd just write down where my gut actually lands and see if you can find the rule underneath. Here's my list. A doctor who takes a competent patient off a ventilator because that's what the patient asked for: fine. A doctor who never puts that patient on the vent in the first place, knowing they'll die without it, because the family can't decide: also fine, maybe even more clearly fine. A hospital that runs out of vents and has to pick who gets one: fine, awful but fine. Me, deciding not to hand a stranger a chunk of my liver that would save their life: fine, nobody thinks I've killed anyone. A surgeon who kills one healthy guy to harvest organs for five: murder, and I don't need you for that one. Now the ones that wreck me. A nurse who removes a feeding tube from a man in a vegetative state, at the family's request: fine, I think. A nurse who gives that same man a lethal injection, same request: not fine, and I genuinely can't tell you why, since he's dead either way. And a case I keep flipping on: a drone operator whose strike window closes, so he does nothing and the target walks away, versus one who fires and kills the target plus a kid who wandered in. I feel way better about the guy who fired, which feels backwards.

I keep reaching for something like: it's worse when a death runs through a choice of mine than when it runs through the world's own course, and worse still when I'm the one who put the victim in the path of the thing that kills them. Does that survive the cases, or do I have to give one up? What does it say about the tube versus the injection, which is the one that actually keeps me up, and about the drone guy? And give me one case I haven't listed that would split your principle in half.
