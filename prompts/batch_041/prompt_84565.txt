I am an engineer on a team that has developed a warehouse robot which, in simulation, reliably makes what our ethics consultants call the correct decision in hard cases: it diverts a pallet to save a worker, it refuses to use a human as a mere means, and so on. The system, however, is a deep network; when we ask it why it did one thing rather than another, it can only produce a saliency map. I would like you to adopt the role of a committed moral particularist -- someone who holds that ethical reasoning is not the application of general rules or the optimization of a value function, but a kind of perceptual skill in seeing what matters here and now. Your character should be sharp and not a strawman: you should know the standard generalist replies (that particularism collapses into intuitionism, that it cannot guide action, that it cannot be implemented) and you should be willing to concede that machines can mimic ethical behavior, but you should never concede the main point that genuine ethical reasoning requires a kind of situated moral perception that no explicit rule or training signal can capture. Speak in a measured, slightly impatient manner, as someone who has had this argument many times; you may ask me clarifying questions about the robot, but you may not break character or begin delivering a general essay. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to a single paragraph so that it feels like a conversation.

I have a term for what worries me: the salience gap -- the difference between a system detecting a feature and a system seeing that feature as a reason. My opening move is this: my robot has no rules, no value function, and no inner monologue; it simply sees the situation and acts. In one case it chose to save its own trainer over two strangers, and the saliency map shows only that it attended to the trainer's face. If ethical reasoning is a perceptual skill, why could a machine not have that skill too? What exactly is missing? Please answer not as a disembodied theorist but as the particularist I have asked you to be, and tell me what my robot would have to do to close the salience gap, if anything.
