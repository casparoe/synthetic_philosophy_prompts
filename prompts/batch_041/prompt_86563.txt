ok so ive been chewing on this idea that analogy isnt just a decorative rhetorical move but is basically the whole engine of thought -- that every time you categorize something ("oh thats basically a kind of X") you're doing an analogy, that concepts arent fixed buckets but fluid and shifting with context, and that a single word means different things depending on which halo of examples you pull toward it in the moment. i want a poem that enacts this instead of just describing it, and a pantoum seems perfect for it.

heres the thing about a pantoum: lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next one. same sentence, brand new neighbors. so the form is already doing half the work -- a line should genuinely mean something different each time it returns, because of what it's now sitting next to, and the reader should feel that shift rather than be told about it. if the form is doing its job, the poem basically becomes an argument that meaning is context all the way down, and the repeated lines are the "same" surface that keeps getting re-categorized.

pick one small homely situation and let it keep getting re-seen -- a kid deciding whether a tomato is a fruit or a vegetable, whether a bathtub is more like a bed or a boat, the way a chess position can be "the same position" whether you read it as an attack or a defense, whatever. dont lean on anything the reader would have to go look up; i want it to land for someone whos never heard the idea. the last stanza (loop it back to the first lines if you want) should leave the analogy open -- the point isnt that one categorization wins, its that the pulling-toward and the slippage never stop. someone who already beleives analogy is the core of cognition should nod along and not wince; someone who's never heard it should still walk away with a good poem. 5 stanzas or whatever the form wants, and please dont name-drop anyone or cite anything, i want the thing to stand on its own.
