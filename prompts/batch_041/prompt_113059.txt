Okay, this is a small thing that's been eating at me for a couple of weeks and I can't tell if I'm being pedantic or if everyone else is being sloppy. I was at a reading group where we were arguing about whether it makes sense to say a digger wasp knows where to dig, or that the immune system solves the problem of a novel pathogen, and I said look, I'm not claiming the wasp is doing explicit inference, but there does seem to be a real difference between the wasp's behaviour and, say, a rock rolling downhill — the wasp's behaviour is tracking something about the world in a way that can be assessed as smart or stupid, and the rock's can't. A friend immediately said, oh, that's just a free-floating rationale, and everyone nodded like the conversation was over. Then the same thing happened a week later when I asked whether a chess engine can be said to have a reason to sac its bishop, and again when someone claimed that market prices contain information that no individual trader has. Free-floating rationale, free-floating rationale. I've now heard this phrase used as a mic-drop maybe five times and I genuinely can't tell whether it's doing work or just ending conversations.

So what I'd like, since I can't get the friend to slow down and spell it out: the precise claims that phrase could be making, layed out as a menu, from the trivial to the radical, with a verdict on each. Is it just the harmless point that we can describe a process in the vocabulary of reasons without anyone in particular doing the reasoning — a fact about how we talk? Is it the stronger claim that the rational description is the one that carves the behaviour at its joints, so the rationale is genuinely there in the world even though nobody holds it, and the wasp has a real reason the way it has a real metabolism? Is it the deflationary claim that reasons-talk is just a stance we adopt for predictive convenience, so the wasp neither has nor lacks reasons and the question is confused? Is it a metaethical claim, that there are real norms out there in nature with no subject for them to bind? Or something I'm not seeing. For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude, a substantive thesis that somebody actually holds and can defend, or just false, and what kind of person gets pushed toward it by what.

And then the part I actually care about: tell me which of those readings is the one my friend is reaching for when he uses it to shut down the wasp question, and whether that reading is true. Don't hand me six options and a shrug — plant a flag. If the honest answer is that the phrase equivocates between two of the readings and the mic-drop only lands because of the slide between them, say that and show me where the slide happens.
