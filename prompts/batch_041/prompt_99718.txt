Two of my friends have been argueing for a year about freedom and I want to force them to finally write the document they keep avoiding. N is a strict negative-liberty type: freedom just is the absence of interference, Berlin-style — nobody's blocking you, so you're free, end of story. R is a neo-republican: freedom is non-domination, Pettit-style — what matters isn't whether anyone actually interferes, it's whether you're living at the mercy of someone who could interfere arbitrarily. Write the joint statement of the adversarial collaboration between them.

I want it in four parts:

1. A numbered list of claims they can both actually sign — about what freedom is tracking, about the cases they already agree are unfree (chains, threats, coercion), about which of each other's alleged counterexamples they both agree fail, and about the methodology: are they doing conceptual analysis of one shared concept, or arguing about which concept we ought to care about in the first place?

2. A disagreements table, with the precise residual disagreements and each side's position written in wording that side would accept for itself. Pin down at least: (a) whether the thing R calls domination and N doesn't call interference — the unexercised capacity to interfere — is a genuine difference in someone's freedom or only in their security; (b) whether law and the state can be constitutively freedom-enabling (R) or are always a subtraction from freedom (N); (c) whether domination needs a dominator — can an impersonal structure, a market, a norm, or an algorithm dominate a person, or does domination require some agent who could have chosen otherwise?

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would actually move at least one of them — and for each crux a concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Use these, plus any you think are sharper: a slave whose master never once interferes and is genuinely fond of him; a worker under an at-will boss who happens to be kind and has never fired anyone; a person whose options are crushed by an impersonal market with no identifiable villain; a citizen under a law that could be enforced against her arbitrarily but never is. For each, say what N predicts and what R predicts about whether the person is free, and whether they'd even agree on what evidence would settle it.

4. Finally, tell me which part of this is a verbal dispute. My suspicion is that they don't disagree about any fact, but about whether "freedom" is the right label for what R is pointing at — that R's non-domination might really be a distinct good (dignity, security, standing, not being beneath anyone) rather than a rival analysis of freedom. If that's right, say so, and say what genuine disagreement is left over once the words are fixed. And note in passing whether the positive-liberty crowd, who think freedom is self-mastery, are even in the same conversation or just talking about something else entirely.

Don't let either of them off with a slogan, and don't let a claim into the agreed list just because it's vague enough to survive both sides. I want to see the formulations actually doing work.
