I wrote down where i've landed so i can present it next week, but i'm definately worried it's a total mess. Here's the list:

(1) I think composition is restricted. Some things make a bigger thing only when they're "on the same team" — when they're organized so they're all part of one ongoing process or working toward a shared function, not just sitting next to each other. (2) So cats, trees, and people are real composite objects. A cat isn't just cat-particles; it's a thing. (3) But tables and chairs aren't real composite objects. They're just atoms arranged tablewise. The only thing holding them together is that we use them for a purpose, and that's in our heads, not in the world. (4) A car is also an artifact, and i want to say it's not a real object, same as tables. Its parts are organized around one process though — engine makes the wheels go, battery powers the radio, etc. (5) I think a person can survive having all their atoms slowly replaced, as long as their memories and personality keep going. So a person isn't the same thing as the atoms they're made of. (6) I think the statue and the clay are the same object. There's just one lump of stuff there, and i don't believe in two objects occupying the exact same place at the same time. (7) I think there's no such thing as the sum of my left shoe and the moon. Just because you can name a collection doesn't mean it's a real object. (8) I also think there's no sharp line between a heap of sand and a sandcastle, or between atoms arranged tablewise and a table. But i do think there's a sharp line between alive and not alive.
