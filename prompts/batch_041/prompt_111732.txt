ok so I help run a meetup — the usual lw/ea crossover crowd, around 15 people, 90 minutes, and every few months I have to come up with something that isn't me re-drawing the importance/tractability/neglectedness triangle on a whiteboard while everyone nods. I want to try something where they walk into the point instead of being told it.

The point I keep failing to get across is that neglectedness isn't a fact about a cause, it's a fact about how many other people are already piling into it. Which makes the framework kind of self-defeating: hand the same I/T/N rubric to a room full of conscientious grantmakers and they'll all independently converge on the same tractable-and-neglected option, and by converging they un-neglect it, and every one of them was reasoning fine locally. It's a commons problem wearing a spreadsheet.

So design me a game, ~15 people, 90 min, where the rules produce that without me having to say it. I want: (a) people allocate some budget (points, tokens, whatever — no real money) across a menu of invented causes, (b) enough of them pick the same "obviously most neglected" one that its marginal value visibly craters, and (c) somewhere in there a small-sample thing where the cause that ranks best on its estimated numbers is best partly because its numbers are noisiest, so the top pick is systematically the most over-estimated one. I want at least one person to notice that before the debrief, not after.

Give me the rules, materials, a payoff scheme, a ~10 min debrief that maps what happened onto I/T/N (and is honest about which of the three the game actually models vs. just fakes), and the stuff that will definately go sideways the first time I run it. If there's room for a beat where someone realizes none of us can actually trace the long-run effects of any of these and we're all just trusting a proxy, put that in too. Don't make it need a projector.
