A few of us in my political philosophy reading group have a running competition to write the best joke about a theory, and this week I drew luck egalitarianism — specifically the brute luck / option luck split, where the idea is that you're owed redress for the bad stuff that just happens to you but not for the bad stuff you knowingly gambled on. The trouble is that almost every joke people write about it is just "lol egalitarians want to redistribute everything" or "equality is impossible anyway," which aren't really about the theory and would get a pity laugh from anyone who's actually read the stuff. I want one where the punchline genuinely turns on the content — on the fact that the whole view hinges on sorting outcomes into chosen and unchosen, or on how it treats someone who knowingly took a risk and lost, or on the awkwardness the compensation-for-brute-luck picture generates once you push on it.

Some ground rules: keep it short enough to actually tell out loud, it can be a one-liner or a little scene, and it should be at least roughly fair to the position, since the whole point of the game is that knowing the theory is what makes it land. Please don't make it depend on any particular philosopher's name or a specific paper, because half the group has read the secondary literature and half hasn't, and I don't want it to turn into a trivia contest. If it helps you can lean on the view's reputation for being kind of cold to people who screw up, but I'd rather the joke earn that than just assert it. Bonus points if it also lands as a real objection, but funny first — if it's not funny I can't use it.
