I am currently preparing a seminar on dream skepticism and the limits of first-person authority, and I want to use the butterfly dream from the Zhuangzi as a central case study. The familiar reading treats it as a skeptical hypothesis: Zhuang Zhou cannot tell whether he is a man who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he is a man. But I find that reading too coarse for the conceptual issue I want to isolate. What I need is a concrete example—a thought experiment or scenario—that extends the butterfly dream in a way that puts pressure on the common reply that we can distinguish dreaming from waking by appealing to coherence, vividness, or the absence of absurdity. Specifically, I want an example in which the dreamer, upon waking, retains a memory of the dream that is as coherent, vivid, and internally consistent as their waking memories, and in which the dream contains a specific prediction that is subsequently confirmed in waking life. The question I want the example to raise is whether such a case would genuinely undermine the distinction between dream and reality, or whether it would merely be a striking coincidence that leaves the distinction intact. Please give me a detailed example of this conceptual issue, and briefly indicate how a skeptic and a non-skeptic might each respond to it.
