I'm putting together a proposal for our humanities curriculum and I've ended up with a set of commitments that I'm not sure hang together, so I want a consistency audit before I commit to them in writing. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each? I genuinely don't know where the trouble is and I'd rather hear it now than from the committee. Rank the genuine ones by severity, and for each tell me the cheapest way to fix it and what I lose by fixing it that way. Give it back to me as a JSON object with these fields: "bottom_line" (a couple of sentences, the verdict up front); "tensions" (an array, one entry per pair you flag, each with "pair", "verdict" which is either "genuine" or "apparent", "severity" on a 1-5 scale, "why", "cheapest_fix", and "what_the_fix_costs"); "most_economical_single_revision"; and "residual_risk" for anything that stays unresolved even after the best fix. British English please, and don't pad it -- I'd rather have four entries I can act on than ten I have to sort through.
