I'm in a reading group that meets in the back room of a pub, and half of us haven't done the reading most weeks, but last week was famine and two of the more prepared members (Dev and Marta) went at each other for fourty minutes, and now they both want me to decide who won, because I'm the one who keeps the notes. So here's the fight, roughly in order. (1) Dev: none of these are natural disasters. The blight in Ireland was natural, sure, but the famine was made by relief policy and the poor law and the whole laissez-faire attitude; Bengal was an entitlement collapse, the grain was there but people couldn't lay claim to it; China was straight policy. So "natural disaster" is a category error and every famine is really a political crime committed by whoever was in charge and could have stopped it. (2) Marta: that's a good slogan that eats the facts. Bengal had a real shortfall — Bengal was importing a lot of rice from Burma, and once the Japanese took Burma in 1942 that supply was gone, plus the government pulled boats and rice stocks out of the coastal districts to deny them to a possible invasion — so the "there was plenty of food" line is a stretch, and some of the people who push food availability decline are serious historians, not apologists. (3) Dev: fine, so there was less food, but the deaths were still a political choice about who got to eat, which is what a crime is. And crime doesn't need a villain twirling a moustache; if you're told your policies are killing people by the million and you keep going and then cover it up, that's intent enough, and calling it a "disaster" is exactly how the responsible get off the hook. (4) Marta: that makes the thesis unfalsifiable. If every famine is a crime then "crime" isn't explaining anything, it's just a mood. Also Sen, who is Dev's guy, never said food supply doesn't matter, only that it isn't sufficient and that what kills people is the collapse of entitlements. Dev said Marta was doing the "it's complicated" move that never has to commit to anything, and then the pub closed.

Can you take it move by move — 1, 2, 3, 4, and the Sen point — and say who's right about each, and what both of them are missing? Wherever you land, tell me what would change your mind, since I have to post a summary in the group chat by Friday and I don't want to be confidently wrong in writing.
