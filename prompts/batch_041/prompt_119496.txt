Your a philosopher of mind, and I'd like you to think carefully about this one — maybe start by jotting down the different things people might mean by "the same thing", since I suspect that's where my confusion lives.

Context: I'm a software engineer, no formal philosophy training, and I keep running into a pattern in arguments about minds and machines that I don't have a name for.

The shape of it: someone says the mind isn't the meat, it's the pattern — what matters is what the thing does, not what it's made of, so a silicon system that did everything a brain does would be thinking too. Fine. Then someone says: ok, but a giant lookup table that returns the right answer to every possible question is also "doing the same thing", and obviously the table isn't conscious. So the first person adds a rider — it has to do it the right way, not by brute table lookup. And now the silicon-mind argument is dead too, because "the right way" is exactly the sort of thing they said didn't matter.

I see the same shape outside the mind stuff. My old team had a legacy service and a rewrite; the rewrite passed every test the legacy one passed, so management called them "the same service". The legacy one also had a race condition that only bit under load. When the rewrite didn't have it, half the team said the rewrite was a different (better) service and half said it was the same service with a bug fixed. And "an LLM is just autocomplete" is the same move aimed the other way — the "just" is doing the work of a criterion nobody has stated.

What I want a name for isn't either side of the consciousness question. It's the bind: the criterion for "same thing" is cashed out entirely in terms of what comes out — behaviour, outputs, results. To include the case you want, it has to be loose, and loose lets in the case you don't want. Tighten it to exclude that case and you've smuggled in facts about the inside — the history, the causal structure, how the result got produced — which is the thing the whole move was supposed to make irrelevant. I've never seen anyone notice they're stuck; they just quietly switch which criterion they're using.

Is there an established name for this bind? I assume functionalism and multiple realizability are in the neighbourhood, but as far as I can tell those name positions, not this. And is it just the old "where do you draw the line" problem in a costume, or is there something extra? If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it, and give me the boundary cases — in particular, when does the bind disappear (my guess: when something other than the disputed intuition fixes the level of description in advance)?

Tell me how confident you are in each claim, and single out the one point your least sure of. No reading list please, just the argument.
