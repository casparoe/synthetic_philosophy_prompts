There is a thesis I have come to hold and want tested rather than defended. It is that the resemblance between the way a sufficiently rich formal system can refer to its own syntax and the way a genome specifies the machinery that reads it is not a decorative analogy but a substantive structural claim: that self-replication and self-reference are two faces of a single phenomenon, and that the second is what makes the first possible. The picture that convinces me is typogenetics, Hofstadter's toy system in which strings over a four-letter alphabet are acted on by rules that cut, insert, and copy segments, so that certain arrangements of rules end up producing copies of the very strings that encode them. A system that, in the course of operating on letters, contrives to reproduce itself: that, to me, is the genetic code seen through Gödel's eyes, and the DNA–Gödel isomorphism is the claim I want examined. What would count against it? I do not mean the general complaint that analogies are not proofs. I want the specific observations, formal results, and conceptual distinctions that should lower my confidence, ordered by how much they should lower it. In particular: does the thesis have any content beyond the trivial observation that any system rich enough to be encoded can be diagonalized, and if so, what does it predict that a rival account of replication does not? What would an anti-Gödelian biology look like — what would we have to find or build, in autocatalytic networks, in replication without a template, in the origin of the genetic apparatus, for the self-reference story to look like something we imposed on biology rather than something we found in it? Which of these considerations would the thesis's own defenders concede, and which would they dismiss as a misunderstanding? And if it survives only by retreating to the claim that some description can always be found under which the loop is present, say so plainly, and tell me whether that retreat is a vice or the ordinary price of a claim pitched at this level of generality. I would be most moved by a system that demonstrably reproduces itself with nothing playing the role of a description that is simultaneously copied and interpreted; if you can construct one, say whether it defeats the thesis or merely marks its boundary. Please answer in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — write it as you would a letter to a colleague.
