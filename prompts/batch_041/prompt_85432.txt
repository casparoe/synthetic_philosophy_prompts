Construct a thought experiment to specification for a lecture on natural kinds whose point is to show that a single variable can prise three accounts apart. Introduce one category — invented if you wish, but precise enough that a reader can say what falls inside it and what does not — from an applied or interdisciplinary science, and make three verdicts fall out of the same stipulated facts rather than being asserted: the category is a natural kind on the homeostatic property cluster account associated with Boyd; it is not a natural kind on the essentialist account associated with Kripke and Putnam; and it fails the condition I stipulate here, that a category carves nature at its joints only if two competent research communities with different practical goals, working from the same data and neither deferring to the other, would fix the same boundaries for it. Constraints: no stock examples from the literature (no species, water, gold, jade or tigers), no science fiction, no resting the divergence on vagueness or measurement error, and a description that fits within a single paragraph. Then give the verdict and the reasoning for each of the three accounts, name the feature of the case that does the work, say what happens to each verdict when that feature is removed, and set out how a defender of the account that comes off worst would attack the case. A case that builds one account's favoured vocabulary into the definition of the category does not count: the verdicts must be generated, not stipulated. Answer in British English.
