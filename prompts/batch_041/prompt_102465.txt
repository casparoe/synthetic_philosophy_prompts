I'm a second-year PhD student in HPS, but I basically live in the quantum foundations group, and I keep bumping into a pattern I can't find a name for. It's definately slowing me down because I don't know if it's a known thing I should be citing or if it's actually just an unnamed mess.

Here's the pattern. Two people disagree about whether the quantum state is "real." One says: it's not a real physical thing, it's a representation of information, a bookkeeping device, whatever. The other says: no, it's real, it's what the theory is fundamentally about. But then you actually probe what each of them will and won't say about the physics and they agree on almost everything. The "it's not real" person still thinks there's a right and a wrong state assignment, still thinks the structure of the state is what grounds the probabilities, still won't let you just write down any state you like. The "it's real" person turns out to mean something closer to "you can't ignore it / it's doing explanatory work," not "it's a lump of stuff sitting somewhere in space." And if you ask either of them what "real" means they get a bit annoyed, because to each of them their own sense is the obvious one and the other person is just being sloppy.

So what I think is going on is that the disagreement is partly about which sense of "real" is in play. But it's not *merely* verbal either, and that's the part I can't place. Because which sense you pick seems to change how you set up the formalism, what counts as a satisfying explanation, which problems feel pressing. So it's a substantive disagreement that's somehow hostage to a definitional choice, and the participants can't see the hostage situation because each of them has naturalized their own sense of "real."

I know the obvious candidates and none of them quite fit. Chalmers-style merely verbal disputes make it sound like they should just define their terms and go home, which is clearly wrong here. The psi-ontic/psi-epistemic distinction is a position *in* the debate, not a name for this dynamic. Carnap on internal vs external questions is in the neighborhood but it's about a different kind of move. And I know there's a distinction people draw between reading the state as a genuine entity and reading it as something more like a law of nature, but again that seems like a side you can take, not a name for the phenomenon.

Is there an established term for this exact thing? And if there isn't, can you actually name it and then carve it for me, because the label is not what I need. I need to know what the phenomenon is, how it differs from a merely verbal dispute on one side and from an ordinary substantive disagreement on the other, and where the edges are. Like, is the whole realism/anti-realism fight in QM an instance of it, or is that something else? And is there a version of this that shows up outside physics, or is it special to cases where "real" is doing load-bearing work? A one-word answer won't help me, I need the boundaries.
