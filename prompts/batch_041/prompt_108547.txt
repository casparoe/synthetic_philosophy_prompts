About six months ago, after a dumb fight about who was going to quit their job first, my wife and I started keeping a shared spreadsheet where we log what we say we're going to do and what we actually do. Two things came out of it that I can't stop chewing on. The why column never settles — I've wrote "I wanted to go but I also didn't want to" about forty times, and not once have I written "I was wrong that I wanted to." Whatever I end up doing, it turns out I wanted something. And the only columns that ever predicted anything were the boring ones: how I slept, whether it rained, whether my brother was going to call. If you want to know what I'll do on Saturday, the wants column is useless.

So I feel like I'm keeping two records of the same person. One predicts and never mentions wants. The other is all wants and can't be wrong — it mostly says what someone is on the hook for. Meanwhile people keep telling me neuroscience will eventually replace all the belief-and-desire talk with something real, and I want to ask, replace what? Mine was never doing the job they think it's doing. Though maybe I'm just describing self-deception, which isn't news.

Is there a name for this — a way of talking about minds that has quietly stopped predicting anything and is really a way of assigning responsibility, while everyone still uses it as if it described stuff inside us? If there's an established concept I want to know how close it actually is, because everything I've run into either says the vocabulary is a bad theory (maybe, but mine isn't a theory) or a useful fiction (maybe, but it's not useful for predicting, it's useful for blaming). And if nothing fits, coin something and tell me where it stops applying — for instance, does it fall apart when want-talk does predict something, like when I can tell she's going to be hurt by a joke before she says a word?
