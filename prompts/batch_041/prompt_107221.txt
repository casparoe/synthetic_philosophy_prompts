I'm putting together a zine for my reading group and I want to close my bit with a poem about dialetheism -- the view that some contradictions are actually true, not just that our language ties itself in knots. I'd like a villanelle, spoken by someone who holds the view, to a friend who reckons it's just incoherent.

The two refrains should be the two halves of the contradiction itself: one refrain is a line that says the thing is false, the other is a line that says it's true, and the poem has to hold both without taking either back. Dont fuss over my wording, I care about the shape. What I really want the form to do is this: in a villanelle the refrains keep turning up separately and then finally land side by side in the closing quatrain, and thats exactly the dialetheist's move -- the two halves are both allowed to stand, and their meeting doesn't cancel either of them. So the ending should feel like a resolution that isnt one.

The bit I care most about, because everyone gets it wrong: the poem must not let the contradiction spill into everything. There's a standard inference whereby from a contradiction you can get any sentence at all -- that's what people mean by explosion -- and the whole point of the paraconsistent systems these people work with is that that inference fails. So somewhere in the poem there should be some completely unrelated, mundane thing (a kettle boiling, a bus running late, whatever) and the speaker should make clear that you dont get to that from the contradiction. If the poem ends up saying everything is true, it's failed. Relatedly, the speaker should be able to say that most apparent contradictions (a door being open and shut, that sort of thing) are just muddle or ambiguity and only some are the real thing -- the poem shouldnt flatten that distinction. And if you can work in, without forcing it, the thought that the liar isnt a one-off -- that the same shape turns up in other places too -- that would be a bonus.

Keep it to the standard 19 lines and dont load it with jargon; I want someone who already knows the debate to nod along and someone who doesn't to still get something from it.

After the poem, give me a short note: where it takes liberties with the actual logic or leans on a contested reading of it, how confident you are about each of those, and which one you're least sure about. Cheers.
