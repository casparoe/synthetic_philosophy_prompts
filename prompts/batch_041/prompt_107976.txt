I've been in a small reading group that's been grinding through the vagueness/sorites stuff for, no joke, about eight months, and I swear we're further apart now than when we started. The setup everyone agrees on: take a heap of sand, remove one grain at a time, and there's no single grain at which it stops being a heap — yet the million-grain pile is obviously a heap and the one-grain pile obviously isn't. One guy in the group is a committed epistemicist: he thinks there really is a sharp cut-off, some specific grain number, it's just that we can never know which one, and he says that's fine, it's just ordinary ignorance. Another friend thinks that's absurd and holds that borderline cases are genuinely borderline — no fact of the matter, the predicate simply isn't sharp, and calling something "true" just means "true on every admissible way of sharpening it up." A third person keeps insisting truth itself comes in degrees, wich the first two both treat as a non-starter. It's gotten heated and, honestly, a little boring in the way long arguments do.

What I want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. Is this a substantive disagreement, or are they mostly talking past each other? As a longtime reader of the rationalist blogs, my instinct is always to say "ah, this is just a dispute about words" and move on, but that move doesn't seem to actually dissolve anything here, and I want to know why. My own hunch — which I'd like you to test rather than just endorse — is that the epistemicist is really answering a question about the metaphysics of properties (is there a fact in the world about the grain count?), while the other two are answering a question about the semantics and logic of vague language (what should 'true' and 'definitely' do in a formal theory?), and that these are two different questions wearing one hat. But I'm not confident, because the epistemicist clearly takes himself to be making a claim about meaning too. And there's this wrinkle I can't get past: whatever you say about the first-order borderline, it seems to come back at the next level up — if there's a sharp cut-off, is there a sharp cut-off for where the cut-off starts becoming vague, and so on forever? Does that recursion just make all three positions equally stuck, or does it actually discriminate between them? So: tell me what kind of disagreement this really is, what each side is actually committed to once you spell it out, and what concretely would count as progress here — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or whether you think there's no progress to be had and why.
