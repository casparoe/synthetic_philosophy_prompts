For about a decade I have followed the decision-theory corners of the rationalist blogosphere, and for the last two years I have kept a private file on the pricing habits of one commenter there. She is a graduate student rather than a theorist, but she posts a maximum willingness-to-pay for whatever exotic game the thread has just invented, and she is consistent enough that I suspect a rule underneath. Her numbers, each for a single play, each assuming a bookmaker who can pay and collect any finite sum:

- A fair coin that pays $10 on heads: $5.
- A fair coin that pays $1,000 on heads: about $450.
- The St. Petersburg game (a fair coin tossed until the first heads; she receives $2^n if the first heads falls on toss n): about $5.
- The same game with the payoff capped at $1,024: about $7.
- The same game with the payoff capped at $10^6: about $9.
- The same game with the payoff capped at $10^9: about $12.
- The same doubling game with a payoff of 2^n/n rather than 2^n: about $4.
- A one-in-a-thousand chance at $100,000, and nothing otherwise: about $3.

She has never stated a rule. Pressed once, she said she prices a game by what she would actually walk away with, which I have not been able to turn into a formula. She has also mentioned, in a thread about stakes, that she keeps a couple of thousand dollars she could lose without it changing anything for her.

A new game is now circulating and she has not yet commented on it. A fair coin is tossed until the first heads. If the first heads falls on toss n and n is odd, she receives 2^n/n dollars; if n is even, she pays 2^n/n dollars. The bookmaker, again, can pay or collect any finite sum.

Give me a prediction I can post. Would she play this for nothing? For $1? For $100? For $10,000? If she would need to be paid, how much? More valuable to me than the number is the analysis: which rules are jointly consistent with the eight prices above -- expected value, expected utility with a concave utility function, ignoring outcomes whose probability falls below some threshold, pricing by the typical outcome rather than the average, capping the payoff at something that could actually be paid, or something I have not named -- and what each surviving rule predicts for this game. Tell me which rule you think she is running and how confident you are, then name the case you would put to her next that would separate the surviving rules, and what each possible verdict there would tell me about which one she is running.
