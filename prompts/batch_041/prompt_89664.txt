"Some of the biggest choices you'll ever make (having a kid, emigrating, becoming a vampire) are ones where you can't know what they'll be like until you've lived them, and living them changes who you are: your tastes and what you care about shift, so the you who evaluates the outcome isn't the you who chose it. She calls the first kind of ignorance epistemic transformation and the second personal transformation, and the cases that really bite are the ones where you get both, which she calls epistemically transformative experiences. Since expected value needs you to know what the outcome is like and to weigh it using your current preferences, the machinery can't get a grip. It's not that you're missing facts about what parenting is like, it's that the facts don't exist yet, because your future self is basically a different person. So the upshot is that these choices are arational, like picking an ice cream flavor: there's no fact of the matter about whether you should have the kid, and anyone selling you the one right big life decision is selling a myth. Her critics say this is overblown, you can read the research on how parents rate their lives, you can talk to parents, and she basically replies that those are other people's experiences and not yours, so it's still a leap in the dark."
