I've been going back and forth with a friend about this for weeks and I want to actually settle it. Here's my position: generations are real. Not in the "marketing departments invented them to sell you stuff" way -- I mean that Boomers, Gen X, Millenials, Gen Z and so on actually pick out something real in the social world, that there are genuine differences between people who came of age at different times that aren't just age effects or noise, and that "generation" is a legitimate kind to build explanations on, not just a convenient label we slap on birth years. My friend thinks it's astrology for people who read the Atlantic.

Play it straight and tell me what would count against my view. I want the actual defeaters -- arguments, findings, conceptual problems, whatever -- sorted by how decisive they'd really be if I were being honest with myself. A few things I'm half-aware of and want to know whether they're as bad as they sound: the age-period-cohort thing, where supposedly you can't seperate the three no matter how much data you have; the fact that the boundaries are arbitrary (twenty years? why twenty?); and the worry that the label creates the group it claims to describe instead of discovering it.

Also, and this matters to me: what would someone who holds my view actually concede counts against them, as opposed to what critics just shout? And what does my view predict that my friend's doesn't -- because if it predicts nothing extra, that seems like it should bother me.

Last thing, be honest about whether "generations are real" is the kind of claim that can't lose. If every counterexample just gets absorbed -- "well, that generation's different," "the boundaries are fuzzy, that's fine" -- is that fatal, or is fuzziness just what social kinds are like? If it's the latter, say so; don't soften it to spare my feelings.

For what it's worth, what would move me most is a case where knowing someone's generation tells you nothing you didn't already get from their age and the year, but I have a suspicion that's not even coherent, so correct me if I'm confused.

Answer in American English, please.
