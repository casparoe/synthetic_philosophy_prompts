"A pain has two parts: a sensory part that tells you where the hurt is and how intense it is, and an emotional part that makes it unpleasant. Pain asymbolia is the proof that these two parts can come apart. Patients with damage to the insula and the neighboring parietal cortex feel pain just as you and I do — the same intensity, in the same location — but it does not bother them in the least. They will smile and chat while a pin is pushed into their hand, and they will not pull away. What this shows is that the badness of pain has nothing to do with the sensation itself; it is entirely a matter of the emotional reaction that normally accompanies it. Asymbolia patients are therefore living proof that pain could in principle be felt without being bad, and it is not a fantasy to think that a drug which selectively silenced the emotional component would abolish suffering while leaving the protective sensory warning system intact."
