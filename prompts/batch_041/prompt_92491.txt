Im building a desiderata list for something Im writing on Wigner's unreasonable-effectiveness problem, and I keep catching myself arguing about candidate explanations before Ive said what an explanation would even have to do. So give me the requirements first: what any account of why mathematics fits the physical world has to deliver before I'll take it seriously, with the case behind each requirement. The cases I want on the list: the fit itself (Riemannian geometry sitting there ready-made for general relativity, Heisenberg's rules turning out to already be matrix algebra); the times the maths got somewhere before the physics did (antimatter falling out of Dirac's equation, the omega-minus falling out of the SU(3) classification and then actually turning up in 1964); the fizzles, where the maths got hyped and didn't deliver (catastrophe theory is my standing example, but tell me if thats a fair one); and the awkward fact that a lot of perfectly good mathematics never finds a use at all. Two more I think belong and want you to rule on: the account has to pin down what makes the effectiveness unreasonable rather than just effective, otherwise it's a feeling rather than a phenomenon; and it has to face the fact that a chunk of the fit is engineered, since we build the models to fit the data, so 'it fits' isn't automatically surprising.

Then tell me which of these are genuine requirements and which are just the prejudices of a camp (the pre-established-harmony people on one side, the maths-is-just-patterns-so-it's-a-tautology crowd on the other), which pair on the list can't be had together -- my suspicion is that not-being-trivial and being-naturalistic pull against each other, but you say -- and which single requirement does the most to rule out the popular answers. Skip the scorecard of individual accounts, or keep it to a line each.
