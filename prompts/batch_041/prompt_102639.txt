Write a short story of roughly two thousand words that dramatizes a question in the philosophy of representation without ever naming the question or its literature. The question is what fixes the content of a signal: the selection history that shaped it, the response machinery that consumes it now, or the internal state of the sender that produces it. No character may lecture on the topic, name a philosopher, or use the word teleosemantics; the disagreement has to be carried entirely by the situation, the dialogue, and the stakes.

Use the following premise and invent whatever else you need. On a tidally locked planet, a small xenobiology team studies a burrowing colonial organism, the vesper, which communicates by pulses of polarized light. One pulse is the crux; the team calls it the quench pulse. Three readings of it compete. On the first, the pulse is an alarm: it was selected because it made receivers withdraw from a predatory current, and that is what it means, whatever it does now. On the second, it is a recruitment signal: in the living lineage a different organ has taken the pulse over and triggers approach and feeding, while the ancestral alarm organ has atrophied, so the pulse now means food. On the third, it reports the sender: it is emitted only when the sender is in a particular internal state, so what it means is something like distress, whether or not any receiver is listening.

Two further elements should be built into the story. First, the team later finds a second population, structurally identical, that is not descended from the ancestral line at all — it arose through a separate process and has no selection history behind the pulse. Leave the question of whether its pulses mean anything genuinely open. Second, a collapse is coming, and the team can broadcast a quench pulse to steer the colony; the three readings predict three different movements, and the choice cannot be undone.

The readings must do real work rather than sit as labels. Let each one meet an awkward case: the alarm reading must confront a receiver that no longer flees; the recruitment reading must confront a response that has become harmful to the responder; the sender reading must confront a sender that pulses with no receiver anywhere near. Let a character coin a phrase for the way a signal's apparent meaning can drift from its origin, and let the others argue about whether the coinage names a real distinction or papers over one.

Do not resolve the question with a decisive discovery. End with the reader able to state what each reading predicts and to see why the evidence does not choose among them. Keep the prose plain and concrete, keep the biology internally consistent, and do not let the organism become a metaphor for consciousness. No citations, no footnotes, and no references to real works or researchers; the story must stand on its own.
