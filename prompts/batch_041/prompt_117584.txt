A committed reductionist and a committed emergentist about the special sciences have argued for years without ever agreeing on what they are disagreeing about. Draft the joint statement they could both sign. Have them first fix, and both endorse, one explicit criterion for what would count as reducing a higher-level regularity to lower-level dynamics — for instance, a mapping from macro-states to sets of micro-states, a derivation of the macro-regularity from the micro-dynamics, and an account of which micro-details the derivation discards and why discarding them is licensed. Then have them apply that criterion jointly to a case both accept: the phenomenon in which microscopically very different systems display identical macro-level scaling behavior, the standard instance being critical phenomena and the renormalization-group account of universality. Give me a numbered list of claims both can sign — about the criterion, about what this case does and does not establish, and about what would count as a decisive argument against each side — followed by a disagreements table: each remaining disagreement stated in wording its own side would accept, the crux beneath it, a case, argument, or calculation both sides agree bears on that crux, and each side's prediction about the outcome recorded in advance. Where a disagreement is merely verbal — one side speaking about ontology, the other about explanation or predictive compression — say which it is and why. Take a definite position on which cruxes are genuine and which are artifacts of phrasing, and defend it rather than surveying the options. Write in American English.
