I'm pulling together a draft for a small workshop on welfare and I'd like your comments before I circulate it — brutal ones, please. The topic is interpersonal utility comparisons, and I want to see whether I can save determinate cross-person comparisons without smuggling in a common cardinal scale, which has always struck me as a bit of a cheat.

Quick version of the argument. The ordinalist line is that since utility is only ordinal and each person's scale is defined up to its own arbitrary transformation, a claim like "A is worse off than B" has no factual content — all the content lives in within-person rankings. My reply: that only shows we lack a *total* common scale. It doesn't show we lack any determinate comparisons. So I distinguish two things. Call a comparison determinate when it holds under every admissable way of assigning preferences to the two people (admissible = consistent with everything they'd choose and report), and indeterminate when some admissible assignment reverses it. On that test, "the person being waterboarded is worse off than the person stuck in a dull meeting" comes out determinate, while "this comfortable life beats that other comfortable life" comes out indeterminate. Then I model each person's welfare as an element of a partial order rather than a point on a shared line, and say social evaluation only needs to rank alternatives where the comparisons it leans on are determinate; where they aren't, either option is fair game.

What I want from you: (1) Is the determinacy test principled or does it secretly just launder my intuitions — and can you hand me a case where it gives the wrong verdict? I'm nervous about adaptive preferences here: the contented slave's preferences may be perfectly consistent, so does my test declare her comparison to me indeterminate, and is that acceptable? (2) Does the partial order do any real work in social choice, or am I just relabeling the problem and getting the repugnant conclusions back? (3) If I instead ground determinacy in something objective — functionings, deprivation, take your pick — do I gain anything, or do I just swap one unpalatable commitment for another? Tell me which parts are load-bearing and which are decoration, and flag anything in the setup that's just plain wrong, since I've been staring at this too long to see it.
