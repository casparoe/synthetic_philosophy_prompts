im putting together the closing session of our dept's physics-and-philosophy evening, and the bit I've been handed is a "Gödel gets defrosted" panel: we pretend he's been thawed out and handed the last forty-odd years of work bearing on his rotating universe and on his argument that time is ideal, and we try to work out what he'd say. I'll be talking over a handout, so what I need back is a table, not an essay.

Here's the version of his position I'm working with, so we're on the same page (if you think I've mangled it, say so in a row, but build on it): the rotating universe he found in 1949 is an honest solution of Einstein's equations — swirling dust plus a cosmological constant tuned to a negative value to match the dust density, singularity-free, geodesically complete — and in it you can ride a closed timelike loop back into your own past. His philosophical point was not that our universe spins. It was that the bare existence of such a world shows that the split between here-and-now and there-at-the-same-time is not part of the furniture: no objective slice of the world is the present, so the lapse of time is something we contribute rather than something the world contains — time is ideal, and the four-dimensional block is the real thing. He also took the upshot to be a point for idealism about time rather than for a world of material stuff unfolding, which is what the title of the little philosophical piece he wrote for the Einstein volume that same year is gesturing at.

Things he'd be catching up on, roughly in order of how much trouble I think they make for him:

- Nobody thinks the cosmos actually rotates. We observe it to be very close to isotropic, and the cosmological constant we infer has the opposite sign from the one his solution needs. So his world is a possibility, not a description.
- Hawking's chronology protection conjecture: quantum effects might make causality violation impossible to create in the first place, so the closed timelike curves may never be physically realisable at all.
- The cosmic microwave background gives cosmology a natural rest frame and a cosmic time — a physically privileged global simultaneity after all, at least observationally.
- The A-theory is alive and well among philosophers of physics: presentists and neo-Lorentzians who keep a privileged present either by giving up the relativity of simultaneity or by denying that relativity gets the last word. And there's a standing complaint that his argument is verificationist — no detectable now, therefore no now.
- The problem of time in quantum gravity: canonical quantum gravity hands you a wavefunction of the universe with no external time in it whatsoever, and some people want to reconstruct time out of entanglement between subsystems.
- Causal-structure theory grew up around spacetimes like his: global hyperbolicity, chronology horizons, and a working practice of treating causality-violating solutions as pathologies to be excluded. His universe is the textbook specimen.
