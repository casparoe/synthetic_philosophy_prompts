I am preparing a long-form interview for a departmental podcast with a philosopher of computation who defends a position she calls feasibility realism. Her central claim is that the polynomial hierarchy — the stratification of computational problems by the resources required to solve them — is not merely a taxonomy of algorithmic difficulty but a constitutive constraint on what can count as a natural law, a rational inference, or an observable event for any physically realisable agent. She also holds that the P versus NP question is therefore not purely mathematical but metaphysical: if P = NP, she argues, the world would be unrecognisably different from the one we inhabit, and not merely more convenient.

Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the position. At least one question should address the distinction between mathematical intractability and physical impossibility; at least one should address whether the polynomial hierarchy is robust across reasonable models of computation, including quantum, analogue, and interactive-proof models; at least one should address what her view implies about the epistemology of mathematical discovery, especially the status of unproven lower bounds; and at least one should address the objection that she has simply renamed anthropocentrism as metaphysics. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Press on the points where the technical debate is sharpest: relativisation, natural-proofs barriers, average-case complexity, Kolmogorov complexity, and the difference between worst-case and typical-case hardness. Do not ask for reading lists, citations, or bibliographies. Please write your answer in British English.
