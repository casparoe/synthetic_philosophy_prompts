Bit of an odd one, and I'm not sure it's even a sensible question. I'm a software engineer and I've somehow ended up owning the "user insights" slice of our product roadmap. Our head of marketing is adamant that Gen Z are a genuinely different sort of person to Millennials — she reckons they were shaped by growing up online and that this gives them a set of values that stick for life, and she says the research backs it up — and she wants us to rebuild onboarding around generational segments. Boomers get one flow, Gen Z get another, Millennials and Gen X get their own, and so on down the line.

I was going along with it quite happily until a data scientist on the team said you can't actually pull out a clean "generation effect" from the effect of being a certain age and the effect of the year you happen to be measuring in, because all three things move together — if you know someone's age and you know the survey year, you already know their birth year, so there's nothing left to estimate. I sort of waved her off on the grounds that we've got millions of rows and surely enough data just washes a problem like that away, but now I'm not so sure she was wrong.

So what I want to know is: are generations actually real categories, the way blood types or chemical elements are real, or are they just lines somebody drew on a birth-year axis that we've all agreed to treat as things? And if it's the second one, does that mean the whole "Gen Z is like this, Boomers are like that" story is just a story with a scientific-sounding coat of paint? I genuinely can't tell whether I should be pushing back on this roadmap or just quietly building it. She's definately convinced and I don't want to be the bloke who derails the quarter over a philosophy question.
