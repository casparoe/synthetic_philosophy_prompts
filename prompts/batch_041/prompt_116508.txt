I'm in a reading group on philosophy of mind and we keep circling back to pleasure, and the thing that snags me is that the stuff we call pleasure doesnt seem to have anything in common -- a cold drink and a resolved chord and schadenfreude are all "pleasures" but I couldn't point to a shared ingredient. I don't want a reading list or citations, I just want to know what principle, if any, actually ties together my own verdicts on cases, so here they are. Drinking water when I'm really thirsty: pleasure, obviously. Scratching a mosquito bite until it's raw: also somehow pleasure, even though it's basically a mild pain and I'm a bit grossed out by it. The moment a chord in a song I love finally resolves: pleasure. Hearing that a coworker I dislike got passed over for a promotion: pleasure, but a squirming one I'd rather not have wanted in the first place. Listening to a sad song on repeat for an hour: I keep doing it and I want it to continue, but the song itself feels like grief, not delight. Same with pressing on a bruise -- I keep doing it, it hurts, and there's something satisfying in it. Relief when a headache finally fades: pleasure, or just the absence of the bad thing? An intense workout where my muscles burn: unpleasant while it's happening, but I seek it out and feel great afterward. And the pleasure of finally remembering a name that was on the tip of my tongue -- that one doesn't seem to involve any sensation at all, in my body or anywhere else.

My own tentative principle is that a pleasure is just any experience I want to continue, or want more of, while it's happening -- that's roughly what "enjoying" means to me. But the sad song and the workout seem to pull in opposite directions on that: the song I want to continue but it feels bad, the workout I don't want to continue but I keep seeking it. So does the principle actually survive all the cases above, or do some of them force me to give it up? If it doesn't work, what's the best principle that does, and which of my verdicts would it make me abandon -- do I have to say the sad song and the bruise aren't really pleasures, or that schadenfreude is, or that remembering the name isn't? Please also tell me what your preferred principle says about a case I didn't list: the pleasure of a hot bath versus the pleasure of finally cracking a crossword clue, since one feels like it's located in my body and the other doesn't seem to be anywhere in particular. And if the honest answer is that there's no single thing they all share, then say so plainly and tell me what the alternative picture is supposed to be.
