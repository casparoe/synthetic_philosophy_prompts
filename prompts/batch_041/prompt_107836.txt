I present a fortnightly podcast on the philosophy of physics — tiny audience, mostly grad students plus a few stubborn amateurs like me (I did a linguistics degree, so I'm out of my depth roughly every episode) — and next week's guest is a philosopher of physics who's spent the last few years defending a view she calls "shallow point realism". As I understand it: spacetime points are perfectly real and concrete, but two models that differ only by a diffeomorphism which leaves everything outside some region alone — say it's the identity everywhere up to and including a chosen spacelike slice, and only rearranges things to the future of that slice, in what people call the hole — are not two different ways the world might have been. She insists that isn't because points lack identities; it's because the relation between a theory and the world is many-to-one, so "the world" simply isn't the sort of thing a single model picks out, any more than a coordinate chart does. She pushes it further than most people do: she thinks "how many spacetime points are there?" is ill-posed in exactly the same way, and that the arguement's reputation as a threat to determinism is a misreading, since what it really threatens is a picture of how theories latch onto worlds. She mentioned in a seminar I sat in on that she's perfectly happy to be told her view is "just relationism with extra steps", and that she has an answer ready, so I don't want to burn a question on that without a follow-up attached.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions for her? I want them pressing on the actual machinery rather than having her restate the view. I'd like at least one on the determinism reading — why two models that agree on all the data on the slice and diverge only inside the hole aren't after all a case of the future being underdetermined by the past, and what she says to the physicist who replies that this makes the theory's content about the hole's interior evaporate (black hole interiors, say) — one on the relationism charge, and one pressing her on self-application: whether the disagreement between her and a naive point-realist is itself a verbal dispute, and if it is, whether that deflates the hole argument rather than dissolving it. My co-host is a combinatorialist who keeps insisting the whole thing is transparent once you draw it with two points and a swap, so something on what a finite toy model does and doesn't show would be good too. For each question, give me a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, and finish with a question about what would actually make her give the view up. Please write it all in British English, and don't worry about citing anyone — I just want questions I can ask out loud without sounding like I'm reading a bibliography.
