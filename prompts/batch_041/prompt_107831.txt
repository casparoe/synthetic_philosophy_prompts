So my flatmate and I spent basically all of last night arguing about contractualism and I'm still annoyed about it. He's a utilitarian (or plays one online), and his whole move was the aggregation objection: he says a view that only ever compares one person's complaint against another person's complaint, pairwise, is just dead — either it can't tell you to save the five, or it quietly smuggles in a consequentialist tie-breaker when the complaints come out "equal," and either way you've already lost the thing that made the view interesting. I don't think it's that simple, but I also couldn't say exactly where he's wrong, which is what's bugging me. He then got half-serious about a fix: he said fine, keep the individualist restriction for anyone with a really serious complaint, but let complaints get pooled among people who are all below some threshold of badness — so the worst off are protected one-by-one and the merely-badly-off get aggregated. I couldn't tell whether that's a genuine repair or just consequentialism with extra steps, and I don't think either of us knew. And then there's the thing I never know what to do with: are we supposed to be comparing the complaints people actually have, ex post, given how things shake out — or the complaints they'd have ex ante, under some uncertainty about who ends up who? A view where you can either blind one person in one eye or give some enormous number of people a permanent but mild scar seems to come out completely differently depending on which you pick, and I can't tell which one is the view.
