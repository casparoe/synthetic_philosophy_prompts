Ok so I'm a novelist, which means this isn't a hypothetical for me. I've got a draft where the prose is the best I've ever written, and the book also basically asks you to feel sorry for a man who does something unforgivable to his kid. My editor says the book is morally ugly and that that's my problem, not the book's. My best friend, who did a phil PhD and never lets me forget it, says the opposite: a book that gets you rooting for that guy is a worse book, full stop, not just a worse thing to read. We've been going in circles for weeks and I'd like an actual number instead of more vibes.

So: what's your credence that a work's moral defects are also, in themselves, artistic defects — i.e., that finding a novel morally repugnant in the right way gives you a reason to call it a less good novel? And I don't want the bare number, I want it decomposed. Heres what I think the sub-questions are, but add whatever you think belongs:

- Is "good as art" even one determinate property, or is artistic merit plural — this thing has great sentences and is worthless as a whole — in which case does my question even have a truth value?
- Does the moral flaw have to be endorsed or prescribed by the work, or does it count if the work merely depicts it, or depicts it and never condemns it? (My friend keeps sliding between these and I can't tell if it matters to him or he just hasn't noticed.)
- Where is the flaw located — in the work's perspective, in the act of making it (I lifted a real family's tragedy and never asked), or in the maker's private life? I want those pulled apart, because "separate the work from the artist" gets used to answer all three at once and I doubt one answer covers them.
- Can we ever be justified treating our current moral verdicts as stable enough to downgrade a work, given how often the verdicts flip? Books my grandparents would've burned are now on syllabi.

Give me a number for each term with one line of justification, then how you combine them, then tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to and which one you trust least. Last thing: tell me where the disagreement between my editor and my friend actually lives — which single sub-question, if we settled it, would make one of them fold?
