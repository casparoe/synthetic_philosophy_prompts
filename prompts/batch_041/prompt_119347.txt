My reading group's been going round the houses on the old Hart–Devlin thing for months now — legal moralism, whether the law can enforce a shared morality as such, whether only harm to others cuts it — and two of us have basically become the two camps. We keep talking past each other and I'm sick of it, so I want to force us to produce the document we should've written ages ago.

To stop it just being a rerun of a debate everyone already knows, I've drawn up a fake three-clause bill for us to fight over. It's not real, but it's realistic:

1. A ban on buying sexual services, justified not by harm to any particular buyer or seller but by the claim that the trade degrades the public standing of women and coarsens our shared life.
2. A ban on publicly glorifying terrorism — not incitement, just glorification — justified by social cohesion and security.
3. A ban on burning the national flag in public, justified by the claim that it outrages the ordinary person's feelings and weakens the sense of a shared political community.

Write the joint statement the two of us could both put our names to. What I want in it:

- A numbered section of agreed claims: about what the law is for, about each of the three clauses, about what harm does and doesn't cover, and about what each side is *not* claiming. It has to be neither vacuous nor slanted — anything one side could sign only by quietly meaning something else is cheating.
- A disagreements table. For each remaining split, a wording of each side's position that the other side would accept as a fair statement of it, and underneath it the crux — the sub-question such that settling it would actually move someone.
- For each crux, something we'd both agree bears on it (a case, an argument, a natural experiment like a jurisdiction that decriminalised the thing and didn't fall apart) plus what each side predicts about it, written down in advance.
- A section seperateing out what's genuinely a fight about words from what's a real split — e.g. whether we're actually disagreeing about what harm is or only about which things count as harms, and whether the legal moralist needs the majority's morality or just some shared morality.
- If one of the three clauses turns out to be something we can't split on, say so and say why; if we can, don't paper over it.

No citations or reading lists, I know the texts well enough — just the document. British English please. And don't hedge it all into mush: I'd definately rather have a sharp disagreement stated precisely than a bland formulation we could both sign while meaning different things.
