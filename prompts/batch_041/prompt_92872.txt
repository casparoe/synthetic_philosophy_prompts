I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, but I've somehow wangled an invite to present at a cognitive science / philosophy of mind reading group next month, and I need to know what they're gonna throw at me before I get up there.

Here's my thing. There's this thought experiment I keep chewing on — the "careenium." Picture a big machine packed with thousands of little magnetic balls, call them simmballs, all bouncing and jostling and sticking to each other. Out of all that churn you can read off a higher-level story: the careenium "remembers" things, "recognizes" things, its states track stuff out in the world. The question I want to push is whether that higher-level story is doing any real pushing — whether the symbols move the simmballs, or whether the simmballs just move each other and the whole symbol talk is a pretty gloss we paint on afterwards.

My answer, and it's the thesis I'm defending: the meanings are real causes, not decoration. When I say a character did X because he believed P, I'm not handing you a summary of ball-collisions in fancier language — the content is part of the causal story. I write novels, so I've got a stake here; if the meaning level is inert then so is everything I do.

Now the room. Mostly empirically minded cognitive science folks plus a few phil-of-mind people. Several are the "if you can't measure it you're just doing poetry" type. There's at least one eliminativist who thinks belief-talk is folk junk, and one or two who live and breathe the causal exclusion argument — they'll say once the simmballs are fixed the outcome is fixed, so my symbol-level causes get screened off, and I'm left with either overdetermination or epiphenomenalism. A couple of others are friendly to the "real patterns" line and might actually help me out.

Give me the Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll really get from THIS room, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way they'd phrase it, with a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is soft. And tell me the one I'm dreading and hoping nobody asks.
