Here's what the student wrote:

The leveling-down objection says that if equality is intrinsically valuable, then it can be better to make the better off worse even if it does not help the worse off. For example, suppose A has 10 and B has 100. Egalitarianism might prefer A=10, B=50 to A=10, B=100 because the gap is smaller. That seems wrong because the leveled-down outcome is Pareto inferior: B is worse off and A is no better off. Prioritarianism, by contrast, does not care about inequality as such; it cares about the absolute position of the worse off. So prioritarianism would not prefer the leveled-down outcome. In fact, prioritarianism just is maximin: it tells us to maximize the position of the worst off. So it avoids the leveling-down objection, but it still faces the objection that it might sacrifice too much for the sake of the worst off.
