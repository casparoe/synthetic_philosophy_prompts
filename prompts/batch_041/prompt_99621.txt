Hey, I need a hand with a writing bit for a philosophy blog. Write a deadpan product review — think Amazon or Trustpilot, first person, maybe 500-600 words — of a made-up product that promises to bind the parts of a proposition into one genuinely unified proposition rather than leaving them as a mere list of unconnected items. Call it something like Unity-Bond or Propositional Glue, your call.

The reviewer is a worn-out grad student who just wants to finally stick a subject, a relation and an object together into something that can actually be true or false. Every time he applies the stuff, he ends up with the parts plus the relation, but the relation itself just sits there beside them, not actually tying them together — so he buys a second product meant to attach the relation to the parts, and that one needs attaching too, and so on down. The whole review should be that spiral: order numbers, shipping delays, a mounting pile of tubes on his desk, customer support that keeps cheerfully selling him the next tier. The joke has to come from getting the actual arguement right — the thing that does the unifying can't itself stand in need of being unified by something further, on pain of never finishing — so that someone who teaches this stuff nods rather than winces, while a reader who's never heard of it can still follow the mess.

Keep it straight the whole way through. I definately don't want winking, no 'of course this is just a metaphor', no resolution where he finally figures it out or decides the parts were unified all along. Let the review end mid-spiral, still waiting on delivery.

And please write it as continuous prose — no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, just the review the way you'd actually sit down and type one out.
