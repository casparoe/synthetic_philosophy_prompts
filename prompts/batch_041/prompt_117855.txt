A friend of mine — call her V — has spent about a decade as the person our group chat asks when someone wants to know whether they have been manipulated. She has no training in philosophy, but she came up in the same corner of the internet I did, she reads the same blogs, and her rulings have been consistent enough for long enough that people quote them back at her. I have been keeping a list.

Her record, as best I can reconstruct it:

- A caller who impersonates a bank's fraud line to extract card details: manipulation.
- A door-to-door salesman who truthfully tells a homeowner that the rebate ends on Friday, and volunteers that he is mentioning it now rather than later because he would rather the deadline do the work than his arguments: manipulation.
- A colleague who conceals, until her notice has run out, that she is leaving, because she does not want it to derail a shared project: not manipulation.
- A doctor who, at the end of an unrelated appointment, volunteers that the patient's blood pressure numbers are bad, and explains what they mean: not manipulation.
- A colleague who forwards you a job listing with the note that you would be better at that job than at your current one: not manipulation.
- A shopping app that truthfully reports that thirty-seven people booked a given item in the last hour, in order to get you to book now: manipulation.
- A partner who says "it's fine, go" in a tone that makes it unmistakable that it is not fine: manipulation.
- A politician who tells only true anecdotes and never mentions base rates: manipulation.

Now something has happened to me that I cannot file. When I am drafting an angry email late at night, my partner takes the laptop out of my hands, opens a crossword, and starts reading clues aloud, and I stop. She told me what she was doing the first time it happened: it is not a trick she is hiding, she says, but a trick she is announcing, and arguing with me would be pointless, because I would win the argument and send the email anyway. It has worked six times.

Is that manipulation?

I am less interested in the verdict than in the rule V is actually running. So: set out the candidate rules that fit her whole record, say what each one predicts about my crossword case, tell me which rule you would bet on and why, and name the case — not necessarily one of these — that would best separate the rules you find hardest to choose between. If two of her rulings cannot both be right under any single rule, say which two, and what she would have to give up. And if you think her record underdetermines the answer, tell me which of her earlier rulings is doing the most work in your guess, since that is the one I would want to re-examine with her.
