I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm at the stage of a long piece where I need to pressure-test the conceptual machinery before I commit to it. The subject is the honor–dignity–victimhood trichotomy that keeps surfacing in commentary on campus politics, street violence, and online outrage: honor culture, where a slight demands a personal response; dignity culture, where grievances are routed to impersonal authorities and the law; and victimhood culture, where grievance is cultivated and broadcast as a claim on sympathy and resources. Before I write, I want to translate the thing wholesale into the vocabulary I actually think in — evolutionary game theory and institutional economics: costly signaling and credible commitment, reputational equilibria, self-help versus third-party enforcement, audience effects, and coalitional bargaining. So take the standard ranking argument — that dignity culture comes out morally superior to honor culture because it forbids private violence and treats persons as ends, and that victimhood culture comes out inferior to both because it rewards the manufacture of grievance — and restate it in those terms as faithfully as you can. I want honor rendered as a commitment device that makes retaliation credible where the state cannot, dignity as an institution that outsources enforcement to impersonal third parties, and victimhood as a bargaining strategy over sympathy and resources; and I want the translation to be honest, not a game-theoretic costume draped over a conclusion the economics never earned.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually need. Which of the moral claims survive the translation intact, which survive only as distortions, and which have no counterpart in the target vocabulary at all? My suspicion is that the notion of one culture being morally superior is precisely what fails to come through — game theory can tell me which equilibrium is stable or which is efficient under a given payoff structure, not which way of life is better — but I want you to say whether that residue is a defect of my vocabulary (a discipline that simply cannot talk about value) or a sign that the original claim was doing less work than it appeared to do. And if some fragment of the ranking does translate — say, "forbids private violence" becoming "minimizes costly self-help enforcement" — tell me whether the translated claim is still the same claim, or whether the translation has quietly swapped the subject out from under me.
