Ok, I'm building materials for a reading group on the lottery paradox and the preface paradox, and I keep noticing two camps that argue right past each other.

Camp one: rational belief is closed under conjunction and can't be inconsistent, so the two paradoxes are really showing us that belief isn't just high credence. On this view the lottery is a case where you shouldn't believe of each ticket that it loses even though its odds are as good as plenty of things you do believe, and the preface shows we need to separate belief from acceptance, or from assertion, before we can say the author's belief set is inconsistent at all.

Camp two: nah, closure is just false for rational belief. Put a fixed threshold on credence and the paradox falls straight out; the honest fix is to give up closure (or make belief graded), and the preface is a clean proof that a rational person's beliefs can hold a contradiction in exactly the way the author's do.

Please write the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want: a numbered list of claims both accept -- about what the two paradoxes share, what a belief has to do to generate them, and what neither side actually needs to deny; then the precise points of disagreement, each written in wording that side would accept as its own (dont sneak the closure-denier's framing into the closure-defender's column); then the cruxes, i.e. the sub-questions where settling one would move at least one side, and for each crux the case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance.

Two things I especially want. First, theres a version of the lottery where the tickets arent equally likely (some near-certain to lose, one near-certain to win) -- I suspect whether the paradox survives there is a real crux, so get both camps' predictions on it. Second, if you think part of the disagreement is just about the word 'belief' rather than anything substantive, say which part and why. And flag any claim that both sides would sign only because it's vacuous.
