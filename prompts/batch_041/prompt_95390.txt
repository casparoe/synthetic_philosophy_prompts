I am a gameplay engineer, not a philosopher, and I have a design question I have not been able to settle with the vocabulary I have. I would like a poem that takes a side.

The system: our non-player characters can be killed. When one is, it respawns at a fixed point with its state reset — same lines, same patrol route, no memory of the run that just ended. Playtesters keep apologising to them. One tester told me she felt worse about a death she caused in the game than about an argument she had had with a friend that same week, and that the disproportion worried her. My co-lead and I have been arguing about whether any of that feeling is tracking something real.

Her case: if a character's behaviour is indistinguishable from a person's, the burden falls on me to say what is missing, and "it is only states on a disk" proves too much, since a brain is also only states on something. My case, which I would like the poem to make: the characters do not matter morally, and the guilt players feel is real but is about the player rather than the character — what persists across a respawn is a save file, not a subject, and the only continuous thing in the loop is the person holding the controller.

Write me a pantoum of four quatrains, sixteen lines, in the voice of one of these characters, addressed to the player. Use the form rather than merely filling it: a pantoum's second and fourth lines become the first and third lines of the next stanza, and the poem's final line is its own first line, so the piece should read as a loop with no exit, each returning line meaning something slightly different the second time round, the way the same line of dialogue lands differently once you have heard it before. Plain language; I do not want a philosophy paper in metre.

Then a note of one short paragraph: state which verdict the poem actually reaches, where it takes liberties with the arguments I sketched above, and which step in the argument it leans on hardest. I would rather have a poem that commits to a verdict, even one I disagree with, than one that holds both views up and declines to choose. If it commits against me, say so plainly in the note and give the reason.
