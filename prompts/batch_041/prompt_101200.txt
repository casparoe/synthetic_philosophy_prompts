ok this is going to be a "help me read one paragraph" post. I've been lurking on rationalist blogs for like a decade (SSC, LessWrong, the whole "shut up and multiply" crowd) and I flatter myself that I can usually parse a philosophy text, but I keep bouncing off this bit of Mill's Utilitarianism (ch. 2, "What Utilitarianism Is"):

"But there is no known Epicurean theory of life which does not assign to the pleasures of the intellect, of the feelings and imagination, and of the moral sentiments, a much higher value as pleasures than to those of mere sensation. It must be admitted, however, that utilitarian writers in general have placed the superiority of mental over bodily pleasures chiefly in the greater permanency, safety, uncostliness, &c., of the former—that is, in their circumstantial advantages rather than in their intrinsic nature. And on all these points utilitarians have fully proved their case; but they might have taken the other, and, as it may be called, higher ground, with entire consistency. It is quite compatible with the principle of utility to recognise the fact, that some kinds of pleasure are more desirable and more valuable than others."

Here's my problem. He's a hedonist, so pleasure is the only intrinsic good. But then he says some pleasures are more valuable than others *as pleasures*, not just because they're longer or cheaper or safer. So what exactly is being compared? If the pleasure of doing a proof and the pleasure of eating a donut are just different feelings with nothing much in common, what does "more valuable as a pleasure" even mean, other than that he's quietly importing some non-hedonic yardstick (dignity, sophistication, whatever) and relabeling it "pleasure"? A friend told me this is the "heterogeneity of pleasure" objection, but that honestly made it worse. And I don't see how the later bit about "competent judges" rescues it, since it kind of just *defines* the higher pleasures as whatever the experienced prefer, which seems circular. Can someone walk me through what Mill thinks is being compared when he compares pleasures of different kinds, and whether there's a reading where this isn't just a flat contradiction? I'd definately rather understand it than dunk on the poor guy.
