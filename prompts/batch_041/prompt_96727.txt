I recently turned down a job whose prospects nobody could put a number on in favour of one that was modest but known, and I still cannot tell whether that was prudence or a bias I should be correcting. I want an instrument that sorts me, and the reading group I keep arguing with, by what we actually do with ambiguous odds rather than by which label we prefer.

Please write a decision tree, or a quiz with a scoring key, that leads from concrete choices to named positions on ambiguity. Eight nodes, each a case the reader can answer without knowing where it leads. The cases should include the two-urn and three-colour Ellsberg choices; an urn with thirty red balls and sixty that are black or yellow, where someone insists that black and yellow are exchangeable and that the apparent ambiguity is therefore an artifact of the description; the same bet offered by a party who knows the composition and profits when you lose; two bets at identical objective odds, one on a subject you know well and one on a subject you do not; a bet on a fair coin set against a bet on a coin whose bias was itself drawn from a known distribution; a loss-frame version of the urn choice; and a case where you already hold an ambiguous asset and must decide whether to pay a premium to exchange it for an unambiguous one of slightly lower expected value.

The leaves should be: Savage-style expected utility, on which the Ellsberg pattern is a mistake to be reasoned away; nonadditive-probability or Choquet expected utility; maxmin over a set of priors; smooth ambiguity, on which the aversion is risk aversion about second-order probabilities; incomplete preferences with a status-quo rule, on which no single number is ever needed; the view that the ambiguity is illusory because symmetry fixes the probabilities; and the view that avoidance is rational against adversarial or better-informed uncertainty but a bias everywhere else. Flag any leaves the tree cannot separate behaviourally, note which combinations of answers nothing occupies, and say of each gap whether it reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. No citations or reading list.
