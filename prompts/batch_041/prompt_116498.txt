ok so I just lost about two hours arguing with my mate Dan about dispositions — fragility, solubility, being irascible, that whole family — and I've come out the other side less sure of my own position than when I went in, so I want an outside read on whether the stuff I actually believe hangs together.

Here's roughly where I landed:

1. These things are real properties. When the glass breaks because I knocked it over, it broke *because* it was fragile — that's doing explanatory work, its not just a fancy way of saying "it broke."

2. To be fragile is to be the kind of thing that would break if you struck it — with the obvious patch that we mean *provided nothing stops the disposition from doing its thing*.

3. Wine glass wrapped in bubble wrap so that when you hit it, nothing happens. Still fragile. The wrapping didn't make the glass tough, it just stopped the breaking.

4. Wine glass with a wizard hovering over it who, the instant anything touches the glass, zaps it unbreakable. Still fragile, same reason — nothing about the glass itself changed, the wizard is doing all the work.

5. Steel ball, and a wizard who'll shatter it the moment you hit it. Not fragile. That's the wizard faking it, not the ball.

6. My mate Rob has never told a lie in his life, not out of any principle, but because his old man used to beat the hell out of him whenever he got caught lying. Dan says Rob's honest. I say no he isn't, he's just frightened — put a normal person in Rob's head and they'd lie like everyone else.

7. Dispositions have to be grounded in something about the thing itself — the fragility has to be the glass's microstructure, not some free-floating fact about the glass.

8. And that microstructure is just bits of stuff arranged in space. Theres nothing "would-ish" about it. A molecule doesn't contain a hypothetical.

Dan's big gotcha was that 4 and 5 are the same case — a wizard decides whether the thing breaks, so either both are fragile or neither is, and I'm just making it up as I go. I don't think he's right but I couldnt tell him why.

What I actually want: which of 1-8 pull against each other, how badly, and which ones only look like they conflict? And if something has to go, what's the cheapest thing to give up and what does it cost me? Don't just tell me Dan's right or wrong, tell me where the actual fault lines are and rank them.
