You are an expert on scalable oversight and adversarial approaches to AI safety, with a background in philosophy of science and the theory of argumentation. Please think carefully, and before answering, set out for yourself how the debate approach is supposed to secure its guarantee and what it actually claims about the judge. Then respond to my objection.

Here is the approach as I understand it. To oversee a system more capable than any human, you have two copies of it argue for opposing answers to a question, and a weaker judge — a human, or a smaller model — decides which side gave the better argument. The proposed guarantee is that the truth has the advantage, so the judge's verdict tracks the truth even though the judge could not have reached the answer on its own. I have also read the reply that the honest debater can always force the argument down to sub-claims small enough for the judge to check, so that honesty is supposed to be a winning strategy.

My objection is this. The judge is by hypothesis much less capable than the debaters, so it cannot check whether the arguments are sound. And a debater can use an obfuscated argument — one that may be valid, but whose steps are too complex for the judge to verify within any realistic budget. A dishonest debater will simply deploy one and win on appearance, and the reply about decomposing the argument does not help, because the dishonest debater can obfuscate at every level the judge asks about. So for the judge to pick out the truthful debater, the judge would already have to be able to work out the truth for itself, at the debaters' level — which is precisely what we lack. Doesn't it follow that debate can only work in the regime where it is not needed, and that for genuinely superhuman systems it reduces to a contest of who is more persuasive to a confused judge? I would like to know whether I am misconstruing what the approach actually claims, and if so, exactly where the misstep is.
