Ok, so I cover medicine and bioethics for a living, and somewhere over the last eight years I've ended up with a position on death that I suspect is held together with tape. I'd like an audit before I file the next one, because I don't want to be the guy contradicting himself in paragraph four and I'm not sure I'd notice.

(a) Death is a biological event. An organism is either alive or it isn't, and no legislature, priest or ethics committee gets a vote. When I write that someone died at 4pm, I mean something happened at 4pm.
(b) A human being is dead when the brain — the whole thing, not just the thinking part — has irreversably stopped working. That's the version I've defended in print: the patient on a ventilator with no brainstem reflexes and no ability to breathe on their own is a corpse being pumped full of air.
(c) Someone in a persistent vegetative state is not dead. The brainstem is still running the shop. I've never been able to take the version where it's enough that the thinking part is gone seriously; it always looks like the conclusion people wanted, dressed up as a criterion.
(d) Controlled donation after circulatory death — tube out, wait a few minutes after the heart stops, declare death, then recover the organs — I think thats good practice and I've said so. If I were on that table I'd want it.
(e) I don't think we should ever kill one patient to get their organs, and 'they were going to die anyway' doesn't get you out of it. The dead-donor rule isn't red tape.
(f) I also think hospitals should be able to accommodate families whose religion won't accept a brain-death determination, where there's some legal provision for it. I've interviewed those families. 'The law says he's dead' is not an answer to a person like that.
(g) For what it's worth, I'm a materialist. I think I'm my brain, or the goings-on in it, and when those stop there's nobody home.

A transplant surgeon I interviewed kept insisting that the load-bearing word in all of this is 'irreversible' and that it doesn't mean what I think it means. I've been chewing on that for a year.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, how bad is each one, and what's the cheapest thing I could give up? Rank them, please, rather than handing me a list of everything that rhymes. Some of these might just be the same commitment in different clothes, and I'd like to know which — I've got a nagging feeling that (b) and (c) are fighting even though they're both mine. And if the honest answer is that one of my favourites has to go, say which one and tell me what I lose.
