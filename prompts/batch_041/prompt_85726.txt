OK so I dont have any philosophy background, I just read a lot of blogs and argue with my roommate, and there's a pattern I keep running into that I can't find a word for. I want to know if it has a name, but honestly I mostly want someone to tell me exactly what the thing is, because I have a feeling I'm lumping together two or three different things.

Here's case one. My roommate genuinely doesn't care whether we get pizza or tacos. He'll say "either one, I'm happy." But if I say "I'll give you five bucks if we get tacos instead of pizza," he'll take it. And if the next day the deal runs the other way, he'll take that too. So over a couple of weeks I've paid him maybe $30 and we've eaten the exact same mix of pizzas and tacos we would have if I'd never opened my mouth. He's not confused and he's not lying about being indifferent -- he really doesn't care, he just also never turns down free money. He'll also take $5 to let me flip a coin for him when he can't decide, which I find funny, but maybe that's the same thing.

Case two is the one that actually bugs me. A different friend is trying to quit drinking. She pays her sister $20 to hold onto her debit card for the month. Two weeks in she pays her sister another $20 to get the card back early. Then she pays the sister $20 again to take it back for the rest of the month. Nothing about her situation changed between those payments, and she knew the whole time that she was going to get tempted in the middle. She says she doesnt regret any individual payment -- each one made sense at the moment. But she's out $60 and her drinking ends up exactly where it would have been.

I looked up "money pump" at some point and got the version where a person has preferences that go in a circle -- likes A better than B, B better than C, C better than A -- and a trader walks them around the circle taking a cut on every lap. That's not quite either of my cases. My roommates preferences don't go in a circle, he just doesn't care, and the thing only works because he'll accept a swap when there's a fee attached. And case two doesn't even involve swapping one thing for another, it's her against her own future self.

So: is there one phenomenon here or two, or three? Does the circle version have a requirement I'm missing -- do you actually have to prefer the thing that leaves you worse off, or does "I don't care" count as a preference too? Does it matter that in case one the money comes from me and I'm the one choosing what order to make the offers in? What if the fees were a penny instead of $5, or if my roommate got paranoid and refused to trade at all -- is he then being rational, or just lucky? And in case two, does it change anything that she saw it coming? Everyone tells her the answer is "commit harder," and I want to know whether that's a real fix or just changing the subject.

And the thing I actually want out of this: is the point of these examples that the person is being irrational, or that the setup is unfair? It feels like there's a hidden assumption somewhere about who gets to make the offers and in what order, and I can't put my finger on it. If the answer is "this is just the money pump, different flavor," fine, tell me that and tell me which part of the classic version I'm getting wrong. If it isn't, give me the right word and tell me where the edges are -- what's the smallest thing you could change about either case to make the problem go away. A bare label won't do it for me.
