Here's the argument I keep having with my friend Nadia (we're both in a phil of biology reading group, she's the developmental-systems person). I say DNA is a code: it gets transcribed, it gets translated, the whole apparatus is a coding system, that's not just a metaphor. She says the metaphor has gotten out of hand. Her version of the argument goes like this. A genuine code needs an arbitary mapping between the symbols and what they stand for — like Morse, or a cipher, where nothing about the dot-dash pattern forces it to mean S. Then she adds a second claim, which she calls causal parity: in development every cause contributes to the outcome, so singling out the DNA as the place where the information is, is arbitrary; the maternal cytoplasm, the temperature, the membrane, the ribosome are just as much part of the causal story, and if you're going to say the DNA sequence carries information about the protein, you might as well say a thermometer carries information about the temperature. Mere causal correlation, she says, dressed up in informational clothing.

So what I want from you is a comparison of the two ideas — "DNA as a code carrying information" vs. "DNA as one causal input among many with no special informational status" — and not a bland both-sides summary.
