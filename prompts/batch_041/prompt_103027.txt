Me and a couple of friends run a little suffering-focused ethics reading group — we're the sort of people who think Parfit and Benatar got a lot right and that most moral philosophy is too cheerful — and before the next session we're each supposed to circulate a short statement of our own view so the others can tear into it. Here's mine, more or less as I'd write it. (1) Suffering is the only thing that's intrinsically bad; pleasure and happiness are not intrinsically good at all — they can be instrumentally nice, but on their own they add no value to the world. (2) The reason to prevent suffering always beats the reason to create happiness, even when the happiness on the other side is enormous — a very heavy lexical-ish priority, not just a thumb on the scale. (3) What matters is total suffering in the world, present and future, and every being's suffering counts the same, no discounting for time or distance or species. (4) I am not a world-exploder. If someone offered to painlessly end all sentient life tomorrow, permanently preventing every future instance of suffering, I think it would be monstrous to take the deal, and I want to say it's wrong, not just that I lack the nerve. (5) I'm antinatalist — bringing a new person into existence isn't a benefit to that person, and there's something morally off about doing it on purpose.

I'm pretty sure those are my considered views, but when I say all five out loud together something feels off and I can't tell if it's a real inconsistency or just the general vibe of the package. So: audit it for me. Which of the five actually pull against each other and how badly, which only look like they conflict but are fine together, and for the genuine conflicts, what's the cheapest thing to give up and what does giving it up cost me? I care more about the ranking than the list — if only one has to go, tell me which and why. Also flag any of the five that's secretly doing no work, i.e. is just a restatement of another one dressed up. And one friend already shot back that (1) commits me to wanting everyone to be miserable, which I don't think follows at all — if that's just a misreading, say so and say exactly where it goes wrong, and if it isn't, then tell me that too.
