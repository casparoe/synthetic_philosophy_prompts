I've been following it for a while and both camps seem to be working from the same facts. The refuted camp points at the industrial revolution and the demographic transition — a couple of centuries where population grew and living standards rose anyway, which is the exact opposite of what Malthus said could last. The vindicated camp says the escape was a one-off, that his model is really a conditional about what happens when technology is held fixed, and that resource and climate limits are dragging the original mechanism back into view. A friend of mine reckons the whole dispute turns on whether a long-run prediction can even be tested on any human timescale — that "right but early" isn't a defence but a confession that the theory can't be falsified, since enough auxiliary assumptions can save anything.
