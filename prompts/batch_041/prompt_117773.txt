I am a retired natural scientist, an experimental physicist by training. Since retiring I have been reading philosophy of science, and I have arrived at a position I would like you to stress-test. Please act as a philosopher of science with expertise in the epistemology of contemporary physics, especially cosmology and quantum gravity. Think carefully before answering. Begin with a broad brainstorm of possible defeaters, then organize your final response.

The position I hold is a package of four claims. First, a physical theory should not be dismissed merely because it predicts a vast ensemble of regions or vacua that we cannot observe. Second, the string landscape, understood as the large space of vacuum states that string theory appears to allow, and eternal inflation, the idea that inflationary expansion continues forever in at least some regions, are at least coherent extensions of well-motivated physics; moreover, the absence of a viable competitor to string theory is itself a reason to take it seriously, even though that reason is non-empirical. Third, the anthropic explanation of the small but nonzero cosmological constant, which treats the observed value as a selection effect among many regions, counts as a genuine, if delicate, empirical success for this package. Fourth, therefore, the multiverse should be treated as a serious scientific hypothesis rather than a metaphysical speculation.

What would count against this position? I want a map of its exposure, not a general survey. Address each of the four claims separately, then the package as a whole. Which observations, formal results, methodological arguments, or developments in physics would undermine it? Which of these would be decisive and which merely awkward? What would defenders of the package concede as counting against them, as opposed to what only critics say? Has the position been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that a fatal problem or an acceptable feature of a research program? Where does it depend on controversial assumptions, for example about the measure problem in eternal inflation (the difficulty of extracting probabilities from an infinite ensemble), the distinction between a theory and a framework, the no-alternatives argument for theory confirmation (the claim that a theory gains support from the absence of viable rivals), the problem of unconceived alternatives (the worry that a theory may be replaced by a rival no one has yet imagined), the reliability of anthropic reasoning, or the swampland program, which tries to constrain which string vacua can be consistent with quantum gravity? For each defeater, say what the world or the physics would have to look like and how we could in principle come to know it. Finally, rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me whether the package is well insulated and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice.

Do not give me a reading list, citations, or a history of the debate. I want the arguments and the exposure.
