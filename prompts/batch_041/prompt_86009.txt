Ok, I'm in a second-year course on the historical Jesus — eight weeks of source criticism of the gospels — and my friend Ravi has built an argument I can't get out from under. I need to know which step is the bad one, because I don't want to pay the price of giving up the whole project.

1. Everything we have about Jesus reaches us through texts that were written, edited and copied by communities with a stake in what they contain.
2. So a criterion of authenticity has to answer a counterfactual: is this saying or deed more likely to be in our sources if Jesus actually said or did it, than if he didn't?
3. The standard criteria — dissimilarity, embarrassment, multiple attestation, coherence, Aramaic traces — all work the same way: they look for a feature the community would have been unlikely to invent.
4. But the thing that needs explaining isn't invention, it's survival. A saying is in our sources because someone transmitted it. Material that was awkward for a community is, by that very fact, material that community had a motive to drop or smooth over. So the awkwardness that makes invention unlikely also makes survival unlikely.
5. So each criterion, applied honestly, hands us the residue of the community's accidents rather than Jesus's life, and a Jesus assembled out of accidents is just as much an artifact of the community as one assembled out of its inventions.
6. Ravi's picture for it: all we have is what floated up from the wreck. What floats is what's buoyant, not what was typical of the ship. Every criterion we've got is a buoyancy test, and we're using it to rebuild the cargo.
7. And it generalizes. Socrates reaches us through Plato, who had an agenda; Alexander through court historians. Same reasoning, same result — we could know nothing about any of them.
8. But we clearly do know some things about figures like that. So one of 1–7 has to go.

I'd rather not sacrifice 8 — if 8 goes, the whole course goes, and I'd probaly still bet the baptism by John actually happened. So which step do I drop, why did it look fine in the first place, and what does dropping it cost me? I dont want a list of everything ever written on the criteria, I want the diagnosis. And tell me what would change your mind — which consideration would make you point at a different step, and where your own reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
