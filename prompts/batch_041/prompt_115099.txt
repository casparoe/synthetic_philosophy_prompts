I chair the policy comittee at our state corrections board and I need a short written opinion out of you before we vote on the 14th. Here's the mess, and I apologize in advance because it sounds like a thought experiment and it isn't. The legislature drafted our new two-tier early-release scheme with what I can only call privacy-minded overkill: the tier-two panel recieves the exact same anonymized file as the tier-one panel and is barred by statute from learning which tier it's sitting in, so from the inside the two decisions are indistinguishable, and whatever rule we adopt has to be one single rule applied at both. So the clean options are these two — release at both tiers, which means a lot of people walk out after nothing more than a paper screen, or detain at both tiers, which means we hold everybody to the outer limit. A colleague who used to teach decision theory says this is just the absent-minded driver problem in a suit and tie, and he's been insufferable about it: the driver can't tell the first intersection from the second, so the textbook instruction to maximize expected utility at the moment of choice tells him to keep going, and then to keep going again — exiting at the first is a 0, exiting at the second is a 4, never exiting is a 1, so he lands on the 1 when the right bit of randomization would have gotten him 1.33. His conclusion is that we should adopt always continue as our rule, and that the lottery that scores better is a mathematical curiosity with no place in a liberty decision.

I don't buy it and I don't fully disbelieve it, which is why I want the memo. Give me a concrete recommendation — the rule itself, how we would implement it, what we tell the panels, and a date by which the first cohort's files start moving under it — with reasoning a board member could actually argue with. Take my colleague's position seriously, including the part where he may be right that a rule which has us randomizing who goes home is arbitrary in a way no amount of arithmetic repairs, and take seriously my tier-two chair's threat that he will not follow a coin if his own read of the file says otherwise; if the honest answer is that we can't commit to the better rule, say so and tell me what the second-best rule is. Also say where the analogy to the driver breaks, because I suspect our panels can infer the tier from context cues even when the file is blind, and I want to know whether that rescues us or wrecks the whole frame. Then, in a short separate note to me rather than for the board, tell me how confident you are in the recommendation and which single step in the reasoning you're least sure of. American English, and keep the memo under about two pages.
