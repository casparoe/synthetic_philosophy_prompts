ok so a handful of us are working through the conditionals literature for a seminar and I've hit a wall. I get the shape of why the triviality results are supposed to be bad news -- something about a theory that says your credence in "if A then B" just is your credence in B given A, and then the whole thing collapsing once you let conditionals get embedded or compounded, so you end up with every conditional coming out trivial (equal to the consequent alone, or to the material conditional, or something like that). But that's the wrong direction for me. I don't want to be told which theory is broken; I want the full spec first. What does any theory of indicative conditionals actually have to be able to do before I bother judging one?

So give me the desiderata, and be specific about what forces each one. The kinds of things I keep half-noticing: it has to get the simple unembedded case right, so my credence in "if the next card is a heart it's red" tracks my conditional credence; it has to keep modus ponens and the other inferences I'd actually use in an argument; it has to have some account of conditionals nested inside conditionals or sitting inside a disjunction ("either it rained, or if the ground's wet the sprinklers are off" -- whatever that even means); it has to explain the difference between "if she didn't take the train, she drove" (said when I genuinely don't know how she travelled) and its counterfactual cousin, without just waving at the tense; and it has to say something about the case where two people each rationally accept "if A then B" and "if A then not-B" and neither of them seems to be making any mistake. For each requirement, tell me the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a nicety -- I want to see the case doing the work, not just the label.

What I most want is the part where the list eats itself. If some subset of these can't be had together -- and I have a feeling the probability one and the embedding one are the culprits, though I couldn't tell you exactly where the clash bites -- say precisely which pair or triple is jointly unsatisfiable and why, so that the list itself counts as a result and not just a checklist. Mark which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which are really just commitments of one camp (the folks who think conditionals have truth conditions vs. the ones who think they're conditional assertions or degrees of belief), and name the camp. Then tell me which single requirement, if I could only keep one, knocks out the most otherwise-attractive theories.

Give it to me as a table with these columns: requirement | the case or consideration that motivates it | status (non-negotiable, or camp preference -- and which camp) | conflicts with (which other requirement, if any).
