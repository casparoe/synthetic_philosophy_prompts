I keep running into the phrase "the monopoly of the legitimate use of physical force" — Max Weber's line about the state — and it seems to be doing incompatible jobs in the arguments where I meet it. In one discussion it was offered as a neutral description of how things happen to be; in another, as a definition that settles what does and does not count as a state; in a third, during a debate about defunding the police, as a moral indictment, on the grounds that any body laying claim to that monopoly is thereby an illegitimate wielder of violence. I want the sentence taken apart rather than explained. Lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making — descriptive, conceptual, and normative readings, plus the intermediate ones — and for each say whether it is a platitude, a substantive thesis that could be defended or contested, or false. Then say which reading each of those three interlocutors must be relying on for their point to go through, and whether it actually gives them what they want. I suspect the words "monopoly" and "legitimate" are each carrying more weight than the slogan admits, so please be explicit about the different things each could mean here — exclusive practice versus exclusive authorization, legal versus moral versus merely believed-in legitimacy — and about which combinations are coherent. Give me a menu I can take back to the argument. Please answer in American English.
