The puzzle is that the report seems to place the colour change before the second dot has even been presented, which is before the brain could have received information about the green colour. This has been taken to suggest that the brain might send information backwards in time, or that conscious experience is constructed retrospectively. Dennett distinguishes two ways of describing what happens. On the Orwellian interpretation, the subject genuinely has the experience of the red dot moving and then changing colour at the time it seems to happen; later, the memory of that experience is revised so that it fits the second stimulus. So the experience is accurate at the time, but the record is falsified — a bit like rewriting history after the fact. On the Stalinesque interpretation, by contrast, the brain waits until the second stimulus arrives and then stages the whole experience, including the colour change, as a finished product; there is no revision of an already conscious experience, because the experience only becomes conscious after the delay. So the Orwellian view says the experience is delayed, while the Stalinesque view says the memory is edited. Dennett's objection is that these two stories are empirically indistinguishable. All the evidence we have is the subject's retrospective report, and both stories can accommodate it equally well.
