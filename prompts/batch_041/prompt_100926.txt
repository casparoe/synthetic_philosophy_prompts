For a seminar on anthropic reasoning I need to hand in a short statement of my own position on the doomsday argument, and I would like a consistency audit before I commit to it. Here is the package.

1. Self-sampling. In reasoning about my place among observers I treat myself as though randomly sampled from the relevant class of observers. I accept the self-sampling assumption in that form.

2. Doomsday. I take the doomsday argument seriously and think it should move my credence toward human extinction arriving sooner rather than later. Given only that I find myself an ordinary human in the present generation, and no other indexical information, I should expect to be somewhere near the middle of the total number of humans who will ever live, and so should not expect a very long future for the species.

3. No self-indication. I reject the self-indication assumption, the principle that the bare fact of being an observer is itself evidence for hypotheses on which more observers exist. My reason is the presumptuous philosopher: told that either a small universe or a vastly larger one obtains, someone reasoning with the self-indication assumption could announce near-certainty in the larger one without looking out the window, purely because it contains more observers. That is the wrong kind of inference, so the principle is out.

4. Simulated and posthuman observers. I assign a non-negligible probability to our living in a simulation, and a weaker but real probability to the claim that most observers who will ever exist are posthuman or artificial rather than biological humans.

5. Reference class. I think the reference class for these arguments is observer-moments, not whole lives and not humans as a species, and that fixing the reference class is the substantive move in these debates rather than a technicality.

6. Impersonality. The correct anthropic conclusion should not depend on which observer-moment I happen to be. Whatever the reasoning tells me about the world, it should tell the same thing to any observer who knows the same facts about their position.

7. Sleeping Beauty. I am a thirder about Sleeping Beauty: on waking I take the probability of heads to be one third.

I suspect that something in here does not hang together, but I cannot see what, and I do not want to be told as a first move simply to drop the doomsday argument. Please audit the set. Which commitments are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to conflict, and which are jointly fine as they stand? Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. For each, state what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost; then say whether any single revision would remove the most tension at once, and which.
