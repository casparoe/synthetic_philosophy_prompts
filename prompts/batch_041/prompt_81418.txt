I'm building the take-home final for a 400-level applied ethics course — one essay question on insecticides and pest control, 25 marks, students get a week so they can go as long as they want. The unit's already done the obvious stuff (can insects feel pain, anthropocentrism vs biocentrism, speciesism), so I dont want a question anyone can pass by rehearsing that. What I want them stuck on is this: "insects matter morally, so we should spray less" looks like it falls straight out of caring about insects, but it doesnt, because the alternative to spraying isn't no harm to insects — it's a different pattern of harm. If low-input farming yields less per acre, it needs more land, and more land means more habitat converted, so you could end up killing more insects overall than the intensive sprayed version would (that's the land-sparing idea from conservation biology, and I want it applied honestly here, not name-dropped). So the insect-welfare premise, pushed through, might support more intensive agriculture rather than less. I also want them to have to deal with the resistance problem — every spraying decision makes the chemical less useful for everyone later, so individually rational choices sum to something nobody wants — without either hand-waving it away or collapsing into "so it doesn't matter." Write me the essay question, a model answer of around 600 words, and a marking scheme that names the specific moves a top answer has to make and the specific mistakes that should cost marks. And in the model answer, keep what's actually settled here separate from what's genuinely contested among people who work on it — I don't want a contested empirical or ethical claim handed to students as if it were the consensus.
