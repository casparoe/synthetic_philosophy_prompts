In October 2017 it was widely reported that Facebook's automatic translation feature had rendered an Arabic-language post — a photograph of a bulldozer near a border fence, captioned with an ordinary greeting — into English and into Hebrew as an incitement to attack, and that the poster, a Palestinian man, was arrested by Israeli police and held for some hours before being released; Facebook later apologised for the error. Treat this episode as a test case for Douglas Hofstadter's polemic against statistical machine translation: his claim that systems trained on large parallel corpora operate on surface regularities rather than on meaning, and that whatever practical utility they may have, they cannot be said to understand the texts they translate.

I want a two-directional analysis. First, map the theory onto the episode. What exactly does Hofstadter's account predict about a system of this kind, and does the error fit the prediction? I am especially interested in whether the failure is best described as semantic or as pragmatic: the original is a phatic, ritual act of greeting, and the mistranslation converts it into a directive. If the real content of the shallowness thesis is that the system has no situation model and no grasp of illocutionary force, then this case may be a cleaner confirmation than the stock examples about idioms and poetry — but say whether that reading is faithful to Hofstadter or a charitable reconstruction of him.

Second, say where the theory misdescribes the case or falls silent. The harm here did not flow from the translation alone but from an institution that treated machine output as usable evidence, and from a user who had no way of knowing the machine had failed. Does the shallowness thesis have anything to say about that, or does it need supplementing with an account of the epistemics of fluent output — the fact that a system's errors are not legible as errors, so that from the outside its confidence and a competent human's confidence look alike? Consider whether the right comparison class is a human with a phrasebook, who at least knows that they do not know. And consider whether a rare catastrophic failure is evidence for the thesis at all: could the thesis be true while this episode tells us nothing, or false while the episode still looks damning? Be explicit about whether the thesis is a claim about the system's inner workings or about what it may legitimately be credited with, since Hofstadter sometimes writes as though these amount to the same thing.
