Roughly where I've landed: (1) retributive desert is real and backward-looking — someone who did the thing deserves to suffer for it, and that's not just a convention; (2) conditional amnesty, full public disclosure with no prosecution, was the right call where a negotiated transition made trials impossible, and it wasn't merely the lesser evil; (3) what victims are owed is acknowledgment rather than information — a wrong that never gets publicly recorded as a wrong against a particular person stays a wrong in the present tense, it doesn't just become history; (4) forgiveness is a good thing, and a truth commission can stand in for the community's forgiveness or at least make it possible; (5) a state can owe an apology and reparations for what a previous regime did, including to citizens who weren't born yet; (6) none of this requires moral principles to be suspended in politics — I'm not a dirty-hands person, I just think morality demands something different in the political case.
