My friend and I spent most of a long dinner arguing about Samuel Scheffler's afterlife conjecture and reached an impasse that I would like you to resolve. As I understand the conjecture, the survival of humanity beyond our own deaths is not merely something we happen to want but a condition of our present lives striking us as meaningful: the prospect of the imminent end of the human world would, on this view, not just sadden us but drain our projects of their point. Scheffler distinguishes this collective survival of humanity after our individual deaths from the personal afterlife of religion, and his book takes the surprising fact that we care about the former as its central datum.

My friend drew a conclusion from this that I resist. If the conjecture is right, he says, then what matters is the continuation of human life as such, not the continuation of anything we in particular care about: a future humanity that had abandoned every project, value, and practice we now hold dear would still satisfy the condition, because our projects need a human world to be woven into, not successors who will carry them on. I replied that this must be wrong. A humanity that cared about nothing we care about would be no consolation at all, and on his reading the conjecture becomes either trivial (of course we like knowing there will be people) or false (it fails to explain why extinction would undermine the meaning of our lives rather than merely disappoint us).

Please explain the conceptual issue at stake here: what exactly the conjecture claims about the relation between the survival of humanity and the meaningfulness of our individual lives, and in particular whether it is the persistence of human life or the persistence of our values and projects that is doing the explanatory work. I want the argument, including the parts usually left implicit, such as why the survival of strangers we will never meet should bear on the value of what we do now, and whether the asymmetry between our indifference to the absence of past generations and our investment in future ones tells for or against the conjecture. I would like a committed answer rather than a survey of positions: tell me which of our two readings the conjecture actually supports, or whether the disjunction is a false one, and give your reasons. Assume I know the basic setup and take a definite line.
