Ok so I've been reading a bit of Simone Weil and her thing about political parties bugs me. Roughly as I understand her: a party is a kind of machine, and the members can all be decent, the cause can be just, and it still ends up eating the people it was supposed to serve — because the party turns into an end in itself, and everyone's sense of obligation quietly gets transfered from actual living people over to the group. I want a thought experiment that isolates that mechanism.

Build me a case: a party, or something party-shaped, that goes wrong in exactly the way Weil predicts. Constraints. All the members are genuinely decent — no cynics, no careerists, no infiltrators, no state repression, no charismatic monster at the top. If you can explain the outcome by pointing at a bad person or a bad intention, you've cheated. Keep it small and mundane, the kind of thing that could happen in a town, a school, a workplace, not a national movement. Setup in one paragraph. And the rot has to fall out of the party form itself rather than out of a bad goal — the goal should be something I'd sign off on.

Then walk me through which features of Weil's account are actually doing the work in your case. Be specific about which one is load-bearing: is it the obligation migrating from the person to the collective? the party needing rivals or enemies in order to exist at all? the group's love of itself? attention to the group replacing attention to whoever is actually suffering? the fact that it has to speak in the name of people who never chose it? Then the control condition: strip the party form out — turn it into a loose network of friends, no membership, no line, no name to defend — and tell me honestly whether the problem disolves or just relocates. Then steelman the other side. How does someone who thinks parties are just the price of getting anything done attack your case? Does Weil's objection really only bite on the totalitarian party, and not on, say, a boring tenants association? And if the honest answer is that any durable collective gets eaten the same way, say so, because then I want to know whether that's a claim about parties or a claim about people.

Don't quote her at me, I don't need the exact wording and I don't want you guessing at what she wrote in some particular essay — your own paraphrase of the view is fine.
