A small foundation has a budget of four researcher-years to spend on the philosophical implications of cross-cultural variation in folk morality. It accepts that much of the evidence base is WEIRD but is unsure whether that bears on moral objectivity. It is considering three agendas: treating variation as a defeater for realism; treating it as evidence about moral psychology that is largely orthogonal to metaethics; and shifting attention from cross-cultural variation to within-person variation across contexts, framing, and language, on the view that surface variation matters less if the underlying concepts are stable. Which open questions should it prioritise, and in what order? Defend the ordering: say which questions are upstream of which, how tractable they are for philosophers working without new fieldwork, and what argument or evidence would move a question up or down. Treat the three agendas as candidates rather than a fixed menu; I want a reasoned agenda, not a survey.
