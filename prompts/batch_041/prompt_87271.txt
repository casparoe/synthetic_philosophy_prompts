Me and a friend got into it for about three hours last night about innateness and the poverty of the stimulus, and by the end we were basicly just repeating ourselves, so I want an outside read on what actually happened.

Here's the shape of it. She's a nativist. Kids hear a finite, messy stream of speech, nobody ever hands them the rules, and they're never told "that sentence is ungrammatical" — no negative evidence. And yet every normal kid lands on basically the same grammar. So, she says, something has to be built in; the input can't be doing all the work by itself.

I came back with: the input isn't as poor as the old stories make out — child-directed speech is pretty well-formed, and there's indirect negative evidence and statistical preemption, plus all the distributional structure a modern learner can pull out. Connectionist models have learned a lot of this without any rule machinery being handed to them. So why keep positing domain-specific stuff?

And around there we stalled. She says a model that's already hand-shaped to the task just assumes the answer. I say the same thing back about calling the mechanism "innate" when it's really just some bias. She brings up Gold-style results about learning from positive data only, and the linking problem — how you get from distributional classes to syntactic categories. I bring up indirect evidence again. Repeat forever.

My hunch is we're not actually disagreeing about the same thing, but I can't tell which split it is. Is it a factual fight (how poor is the input, really)? A fight about what counts as evidence (does a working model settle acquisition, or just redescribe it)? A fight about what "innate" is even supposed to mean — domain-specific machinery vs a domain-general bias that's still innate? Or two different questions wearing one name: her "poverty" as a claim about the actual child's corpus, vs mine as a claim about what's learnable in principle from that corpus by any learner?

Diagnose it for me, with the actual positions in view, and tell me honestly what would count as progress here — a result, an experiment, a distinction — or whether the thing is just stuck for good. Don't just survey the field at me; I want the diagnosis and what would move it.
