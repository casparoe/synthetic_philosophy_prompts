I have been trying to state a position that I will call evidential cooperation in large worlds, and I would like you to serve as an expert in decision theory, game theory, and formal epistemology in order to map its exposure. The position is this: in a sufficiently large world — one containing many agents, many of whom are in situations relevantly similar to mine and whose reasoning is correlated with mine — I should treat my choice in a one-shot prisoner’s dilemma as evidence about what those other agents will choose. If I cooperate, that raises my credence that they cooperate; if I defect, that raises my credence that they defect. Because my payoff depends on their choices, and because this evidential correlation is not merely a causal channel, the evidential expected value of cooperating can exceed that of defecting even when no causal influence runs between us. More generally, the view says that the correct decision theory for large worlds treats logical and evidential correlations among agents as grounds for cooperation, not merely causal influence. I am not asking for a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I want the philosophical case against the view, developed from the inside.

Please begin by brainstorming the main defeaters, then rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and be explicit about which ones a committed defender of the view would concede and which they would dismiss as a misunderstanding. In particular, I want to know: (1) which assumptions about the world’s size, the degree of correlation among agents, and the nature of that correlation are load-bearing; (2) what would have to be true for the evidential correlation to vanish or to be screened off, and whether such conditions are realistic or merely logically possible; (3) whether the view collapses into ordinary evidential decision theory, causal decision theory, or functional decision theory under certain conditions, and whether that collapse is a problem; (4) which counterexamples or formal results — for example, cases in which the correlation is spurious, cases in which my action is evidence but not a reason, cases in which the large-world assumption fails, or cases like Newcomb’s problem and Parfit’s hitchhiker — would be decisive rather than merely awkward; and (5) whether the view risks anything at all, or whether it has been qualified into unfalsifiability. I am especially interested in whether the view can be distinguished from the simpler injunction to act as if one were cooperating with one’s copies without assuming a particular metaphysics of personal identity or a particular theory of logical correlation. Please write in American English and be precise about the distinctions among evidential, causal, and functional decision theories.
