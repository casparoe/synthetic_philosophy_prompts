I have no formal training in philosophy, but I keep returning to Plato and alcohol. My rough sense is that he did not simply forbid drinking: in the Laws he treats supervised drinking as a way to reveal and train a person's character, with a sober older person setting the pace, and he is suspicious of solitary or uncontrolled drinking. I am trying to work out what he would say about a modern proposal I read about. A city wants to decriminalize all recreational psychoactive drugs but permit their use only in licensed, supervised civic rooms, where a sober facilitator is present, group discussion is mandatory, and private use remains illegal. The stated goals are to reduce addiction, prevent overdose, and make character visible. Would Plato support this as a natural extension of his thinking about wine, or reject it because the substances are not wine and the setting is not a symposium? I am also interested in his likely reaction to two ideas that did not exist in his time: that addiction is a disease rather than a defect of character, and that harm reduction such as safe consumption sites and decriminalization is the humane response. Give me his probable verdicts on each, the arguments he would actually make, and where his own position would have to bend to accommodate the modern facts. Please do not just tell me he would disapprove of drunkenness; I want to know where his arguments point.
