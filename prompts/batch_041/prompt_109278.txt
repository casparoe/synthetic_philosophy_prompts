I'm in the middle of writing a short paper for a seminar on perception and I'd like your comments on the draft's central idea before I commit to it. The paper is about Molyneux's question — the one where a person blind from birth learns to tell a cube from a sphere by touch, then gains sight, and we ask whether they'd recognize the cube by sight alone, without touching it. I'm not interested in re-litigating the empiricist/nativist thing, everyone does that. My idea is that the whole debate is under-specified in a way that lets both sides be right about diffrent things. As it's usually posed, the newly sighted subject has to answer in the first instant, from a single frozen look, with no chance to move, squint, turn the object over, or compare it to anything — call that snapshot recognition. But nobody identifies objects that way in real life, and the interesting question is what happens once the subject can act: reach out, tilt the object, track its edges as they move — call that exploratory recognition. My claim is that the empiricist answer is probably correct for snapshot recognition and the nativist answer for exploratory recognition, and that a lot of the heat in the debate comes from people sliding between the two without noticing. I also think this is why the modern cases are so hard to interpret — people who get their sight back after surgery, and the ones who use camera-to-tongue or camera-to-vibration devices — since what gets measured in those settings is almost always some mix of the two, and usually mostly the snapshot part. I was arguing about this with a friend who works in vision science and she thinks I'm just overthinking a thought experiment that was never meant to be taken this literally, which might be the right answer, but I want to hear why from someone else.

Can you give me comments? I'm mainly worried about three things: (1) whether the snapshot/exploratory distinction is a real cut in nature or just a relabeling of the old immediate-vs-acquired distinction that everyone already knew about; (2) whether it's even coherent to say the subject sees the cube in the snapshot case if the identification is supposed to be withheld — i.e., whether I'm quietly assuming that perception and recognition are seperable, which is exactly what's at issue; and (3) whether the move makes the question empirically answerable at all, or whether I've just redescribed it in a way no experiment could settle. Also tell me if the whole thing is just a verbal dispute — if the answer to Molyneux's question depends entirely on how you draw the line around recognition, maybe the puzzle is a product of our vocabulary rather than a fact about minds, and I'd want to know that before I spend three weeks on it.
