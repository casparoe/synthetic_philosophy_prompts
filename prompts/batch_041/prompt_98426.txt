I'm giving a talk at a small workshop next month and I want a Q&A prep sheet, not a summary of my own argument. My thesis, roughly: 'merit' is a role-relative notion — what counts as merit for a position is fixed by the point of that position — so there is no single merit ranking that could order people across roles, which is exactly what meritocracy as a distributive ideal needs. Worse, I claim, every merit judgment smuggles in a substantive theory of what the role is *for*, and since nobody ever argues about that part, meritocracy just launders whatever roles already exist (a 'meritorious' CEO turns out to be someone good at being a CEO as CEOs currently are, and so on).

The room is mostly political philosophers — one luck-egalitarian bloc and one relational-egalitarian bloc — plus two sociologists of stratification who study how merit talk functions as legitimation, and one labor economist who only cares whether any of this has policy teeth. None of them are metaphysicians, and none of them care about conceptual analysis for its own sake; if I say "well, it's a conceptual point" I've lost them.

I want the objections I'll actualy get from this specific room, not the ones a Nozickian or a Sandel fan would throw at me. Order them by how much damage they do. Phrase each one in the vocabulary of whoever would raise it — the sociologists will say something like "this is descriptively inert," the relational egalitarian will tell me I've misdiagnosed the disease, the economist will ask what falls out for policy, etc. For each: a reply I can give in about a minute, and an honest note on where that reply is soft. Tell me what to concede up front so I'm not fighting on the wrong ground, and finish with the one objection I'm hoping nobody raises.
