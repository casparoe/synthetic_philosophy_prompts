So my reading group has been stuck on the identity of indiscernibles for like three sessions now and it's driving me up the wall. The setup is the usual one: Black's two spheres, two perfectly identical iron spheres alone in an otherwise empty universe, mirrored about the midpoint so that nothing anywhere in the world picks one out from the other. And here's the thing -- everyone at the table agrees about the *case*. Yeah, fine, it looks like a possible world containing two things that share every property. Then everyone draws a completely different lesson from it, and half the time the real disagreement turns out to be about stuff nobody said out loud: whether a property has to be pure and non-relational to count as a discernment, whether "I can clearly conceive it" is even weak evidence of possibility, whether the principle is supposed to be logically necessary or just true here, whether identity can be primitive rather than supervening on qualitative facts, that kind of thing. I want to make a decision tree I can throw up on my blog so people can actually find out which position they hold instead of which one they think they hold.

Could you build it for me? Start from a commitment someone could plausibly have without already knowing where it leads -- something like "I think the two-sphere world is possible" versus "I think it's impossible" -- and let each branch node be a single question about a case or a claim, phrased so a person can answer it without knowing which label it's heading toward. I want the questions to come apart over concrete cases, not over "do you accept the principle of identity of indiscernibles," which is exactly the question nobody can answer about themselves. Please use at least: the two-sphere world; a world with just one sphere; three spheres in a row; a world where the two spheres trade places (so is the resulting world the same world or a different one?); a pair of identical electrons; whether the principle should also hold for spacetime points in a symmetric empty space; whether {a} and {b} are discernible from each other; and the modal question of whether the principle holds in every world or only in ours. The distinctions I care about most are pure versus impure/relational properties, weak versus strong readings of the principle, whether identity is exhausted by qualitative facts or needs haecceities or bare particulars, and whether the thing is a truth of logic, a metaphysical necessity, or a contingent regularity.

For the leaves: I want at least strong PII, weak PII, contingent PII, haecceitism, bundle theory, and structuralism about spacetime, plus one sentence each on that position's most awkward commitment -- the thing its defenders have to swallow. Where the tree generates a combination of answers that no one seems to actually defend, don't just skip it; mark it as unoccupied and give me your best guess at why (incoherent, just unattractive, or honestly unexplored). And if there are leaves your tree can't cleanly separate, flag that instead of fudging it. Somewhere around 8-12 nodes and 6-8 leaves feels right. Keep the whole thing in American English, and don't worry about citing anyone -- I just want the instrument, not a bibliography.
