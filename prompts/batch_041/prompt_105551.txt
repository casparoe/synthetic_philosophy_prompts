In my philosophy club we read a dialogue in which an anteater converses with an ant colony that seems to have a mind, and the argument that kept recurring was that 'the whole is greater than the sum of its parts' — so, supposedly, the colony's mind is something over and above its ants. One member treated the phrase as settling the question; another said it is merely a reminder that we have not finished adding. I suspect the slogan is being asked to carry work that several quite different claims could do, and that only some of them would actually help. Would you lay out the precise claims the sentence could be making — what exactly is 'greater', and greater in what respect — arranged as a list running from the almost trivially true to the genuinely contentious, marking each as trivial, defensible, or false, and indicating which reading someone most likely has in mind when they use it to argue that a colony can have a mind? I would like a menu to take back to the group rather than a single verdict.
