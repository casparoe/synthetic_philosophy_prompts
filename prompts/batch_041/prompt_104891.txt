ok so I'm in this reading group that's been going through some of Robin Hanson's stuff on futarchy, and one of the guys there keeps making the same move over and over. He says futarchy is just corporate governance scaled up. Like, in a company the shareholders vote on what they want the company to do (roughly, they set the goal), and then the managers and the markets figure out the best way to actually get there, and nobody thinks that's insane. So, he says, citizens = shareholders, they vote on what the country should be aiming at, and the prediction markets = the managers, they bet on which policy actually delivers it. His punchline is that futarchy is obviously workable because corporations are the most successful organizations we've ever built and they already run on exactly this split between ends and means.

I want to push back but I don't want to just wave my hands. Can you take this seriously as a philosopher of social/political institutions and give me a proper audit? Start by brainstorming what the analogy is actually doing for him and what it would have to establish to earn the "obviously workable" conclusion, then lay out the correspondence explicitly (a table is fine -- what plays the role of the shareholder, the board, the manager, the price signal, the quarterly earnings, the hostile takeover, etc.), then tell me where the mapping breaks down.

The stuff I care about is: which disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion he's drawing, versus which are just decorative differences. Some things I'm already suspicious of -- corporations have a single, clean, measurable objective (profit/share price) and an owner who's a residual claimant, whereas the citizens' "what we want" is exactly the contested thing; shareholders can exit by selling but citizens mostly can't; and firms compete with each other in a way that disciplines managers, which nations don't. But I don't know how to weigh these or whether there's a version of the analogy that survives them.

So: where does it hold, where does it quietly import features the target doesn't have, and does "futarchy is corporate governance scaled up, therefore it's obviously workable" survive once the disanalogies are on the table? If it doesn't survive, is there a better analogy for what futarchy is actually like that would frame the real questions (about thin markets, manipulation, and who decides the metric) more honestly? Think it through carefully and don't just give me the standard pros and cons list -- I want the analogy itself dissected.
