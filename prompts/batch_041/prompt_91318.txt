ok so for my philosophy elective I have to write a short story that dramatizes a concept, and I want to do promises. Not the boring "keep your word" moral, the actual puzzle of why a promise has any grip on you at all.

Heres the idea I want the story built on, so first tell me if I've actually got it right and then write it: a promise cant just be a really strong intention, because I can change my intentions and nobody says I did something wrong. And it can't just be that the other person is counting on me, because they might be completely wrong about what I'll do and it's still wrong if I bail on them. What it seems to be instead is a joint thing — once I promise, it's ours and not mine anymore, so I can't unilaterally take it back. Only the person I promised can let me out, and thats why them saying "forget it, we're good" actually changes something instead of just being nice of them.

So build the story on that. I want the exits blocked: the person I promised is dead, or doesn't remember me anymore, or won't release me — so the "who can let me out" question is live and not just a technicality. Like 1200 words, third person, and the ending should turn on that release problem, not on the character just deciding to suck it up and be a good person.
