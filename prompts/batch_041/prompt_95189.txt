Max Black's two-sphere world seems to leave the identity of indiscernibles with no work to do in either direction. If the properties allowed to distinguish the spheres include impure, identity-involving ones — the property of being identical with that very sphere, or of being numerically different from it — then any two objects trivially differ, and the principle is vacuous. If only qualitative properties count, then the two spheres, which are stipulated to be exactly alike, refute the principle outright. Either way, the dispute seems to come down to a stipulation about which properties to count, with one side opting for triviality and the other for refutation, and no substantive metaphysical question at stake. Is that the right way to read the state of the debate, or is there a version of the principle that avoids both horns?
