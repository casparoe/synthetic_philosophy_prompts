My brother got held for 72 hours last month. Our dad died in March, and one night my brother told his therapist he didnt see the point of sticking around, and by morning he was in a ward with a locked door and it took me and my mom two days to get him out. I read that Szasz book while he was in there, the Myth of Mental Illness one, and I've been arguing with it ever since. Half the time I'm nodding along, half the time I want to throw it at the wall, and I cant tell if there's one rule behind my reactions or if I'm just being protective of my own family.

Heres where I actually come down, case by case.

- My brother. Grieving, said something dark on a bad night, spent three days locked up. Not an illness. That was a man in pain being handled by the state, not a sick person being treated.
- My uncle. Fifth of whiskey a day, liver's shot, two rounds of rehab, says he cant stop. His doctor writes disease on the chart. I dont buy it. He could stop. He doesnt want it badly enough. Not an illness.
- My coworker. Washes her hands till they bleed, checks the stove forty times before she can leave the house. She'd trade a finger to be rid of it. Ill. Obviously ill.
- My friend's mom. Convinced the neighbors are pumping gas through her vents, wont leave the house. Ill. Something in her is broken.
- My nephew's classmate. Eight years old, cant sit through math, gets a pill so he can. Not an illness. Thats a school with a problem, not a kid with a disease.
- A girl I knew in college. Anorexia, in and out of hospital, BMI down in the low teens. Ill. Not a choice, whatever anyone says.
- My neighbor. On disability for fatigue. Sleeps till noon, posts all day, perks right up when theres something he actually wants to do. I dont think hes ill, and I feel like a jerk for thinking it.
- Me, sort of. After the funeral my GP offered to put anxiety on the leave form so I could take a month off, and I took it, and the month helped. I wasnt ill. So why does the whole thing feel like a lie I told?
- A friend from work. Bad crash two years ago, still gets the shakes if shes behind a wheel. I say ill. But its the same shape as my brother — a normal person reacting to something awful — and I call one of them a disease and the other one not.

The two rules I keep reaching for each eat something I care about. Rule one, the Szasz one: an illness is a broken body, so if theres no lesion there's no illness, just trouble with living. That gets my uncle, my brother, my neighbor, the kid. It makes my coworker and my friend's mom not ill, and I'd bet my life they are. Rule two: its an illness when the reaction doesnt make sense as a response to the person's life, and not when it does. That gets my brother, my coworker, my friend's mom. But it makes my uncle's drinking an illness (makes perfect sense given how he grew up) and PTSD not one, and I dont want either of those.

So: is there a principle that gets all or most of this, or do I have to give one up? And say what it predicts for cases I havent listed — a gambler who's wrecked his life the way my uncle wrecked his liver, someone who's just flat miserable for years with nothing wrong, someone who's fine except she cant get on a bridge, and a whole family that shares one delusion so it never looks like a symptom to any of them. If I have to drop one of my verdicts, which one, and what am I paying?
