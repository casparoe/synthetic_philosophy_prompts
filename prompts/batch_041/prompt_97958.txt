I'm a backend engineer, never took a philosophy class, but I've been reading about Popper and this "poverty of historicism" business on and off, mostly because a friend of mine keeps insisting that if we understood history the way we understand physics we could predict what societies do next, and I keep feeling he's half right and half nuts. Last week I tried to write down the argument that I think actually kills his view, and now I can't tell whether I killed it or just shot myself in the foot, because the same reasoning seems to take out a bunch of things nobody doubts.

Here it is. (1) A law is basically a model: a generalization you can use to predict new cases and that would still have held in cases that never happened. (2) You only earn a model like that by training on many instances and then checking it on cases it hasn't seen -- in ML terms you need a train/test split, otherwise you're just overfitting. (3) History is one run: the sequence of human societies happened once, in one order, and we can't rerun it. (4) With a single run there's no held-out set and no rerun, so any generalization about how societies develop fits the one trajectory by construction and can never be validated against anything. (5) A model that can't be validated isn't a law, it's a story. (6) So there are no laws of history, my friend's whole project is impossible, and anyone claiming to forecast the future of society is selling curve-fitting. I *want* to believe (6) because I think his predictions are garbage, but (4) and (5) bug me, because by that logic there are no laws in cosmology or evolutionary biology or geology either -- those are also single runs -- and I don't think anyone wants to say the big bang or natural selection isn't law-governed. So somewhere in there the argument is rigged, but I honestly can't see where.

Where does it actually go wrong, and if I have to give up one of the premises to save the rest, which one is cheapest and what do I pay for giving it up? Please don't just tell me "history is complicated" -- I need the specific step that fails and why it looked fine when I wrote it.
