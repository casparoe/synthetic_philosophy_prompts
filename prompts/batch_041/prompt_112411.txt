A colleague has been pressing an argument on me for some weeks, and I have not been able to find its seam. He says it shows that "worse than" — and, by the same construction, any comparative we use to rank harms, lives, or policies — cannot be transitive. What I cannot accept is where he ends: that in the cases his argument covers there is no fact of the matter about which of two things is worse, so that our verdicts are projections rather than discoveries. Nor can I accept the intransitivity on its own, since clinical decisions, cost-benefit analysis, and the setting of damages all proceed by chaining verdicts of the form "A is worse than B, B is worse than C, therefore A is worse than C." Here is the argument as he gives it.

(1) Over a broad class of harms, comparison is determinate: of any two, exactly one is worse, or they are equally bad.

(2) Badness is fine-grained. There is no threshold of difference below which two harms count as equally bad; no difference of intensity or duration is too small to matter.

(3) Consider the series H0, H1, …, H30. H0 is one hour of the most intense pain a person can suffer. Each later member lasts a quarter longer than the one before and is a tenth less intense, so H30 lasts about a month and is roughly a twentieth as intense as H0. He stipulates that every verdict he assigns below is all-things-considered, not a verdict about intensity or duration taken alone.

(4) Each step is an improvement. A somewhat milder pain endured somewhat longer is the better bargain, and this holds at every stage of the series.

(5) No step can be singled out for rejection. To deny (4) at any point is to hold that the series reverses somewhere, so that some pair of adjacent members, one only somewhat longer and somewhat milder than the other, flips from "better" to "worse." By (2), the difference between such a pair cannot be waved away as negligible, so the reversal would be an arbitrary cut with nothing to justify it. There is no such cut, so (4) stands throughout.

(6) H30 is worse than H0. A month of unremitting, mild ache is worse than a single hour of the most intense pain. I find this verdict hard to resist.

(7) Hence "worse than" is intransitive: H0 is worse than H1, H1 worse than H2, …, H29 worse than H30, and H30 is worse than H0.

(8) A relation that can cycle in this way is not a ranking of anything. Where A is worse than B, B worse than C, and C worse than A, there is no order for the verdicts to track and no truth for them to state; they express attitudes rather than record anything.

(9) Nothing in the construction is peculiar to pain. Any two dimensions that trade off against one another — money against leisure, risk against severity, length of life against quality of life — generate the same cycle, so the conclusion is entirely general.

Every premise looks like common sense to me, and I cannot tell whether the chain breaks somewhere or holds and something must give. Tell me which step to reject, why it looked as good as it did, and what I pay for each repair. I would sooner keep transitivity, and keep (1), than keep the rest; but I want to know the price.
