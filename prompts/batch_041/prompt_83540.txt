I am circulating a short statement of my position ahead of a workshop on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness, and I would like a coherence audit of the position as a package before I commit to it on the record. I hold the following six claims, all of which I take to be constitutive of the view rather than optional trimmings:

1. There are no phenomenal properties. Nothing in the world answers to the description "an intrinsic, subjectively felt quality that outruns any functional or representational characterization"; talk of qualia, of phenomenal character, and of the felt quality of experience picks out no features of reality.
2. This is not eliminativism about experience. Pains, after-images, and the taste of coffee are real, and nothing in my view denies that we have them. What I reject is a theoretical gloss on them, not the phenomena.
3. The illusion is introspective and metarepresentational. We are not deceived by our senses, as in a hallucination; we are deceived by our own self-representation, which presents our mental states as having phenomenal properties that they do not have. The right analogy is a user interface: a simplified, action-guiding display that nothing in its design invites us to read as an inventory of the machinery beneath it.
4. Introspective representations have accuracy conditions like any others, and those conditions are fixed by the mechanisms that produce them, not by anything the subject imports from outside. So the illusion can be diagnosed from the inside, by asking what the introspective system is in the business of representing and whether anything answers to that.
5. The explanatory target is therefore why we think, and say, that we have phenomenal properties. A successful theory accounts for the reports without positing the properties reported: the reports are data, the properties are not.
6. The illusion is contingent on our representational architecture. Creatures differently built might represent their own states with no phenomenal gloss at all, and nothing in principle prevents our descendants from occupying that position.

I have been told that this package is unstable, but I cannot see where the joints are. Please audit it. Say which of the six stand in genuine tension with which others, and which pairs are only apparently in tension, and be explicit about why the apparent ones dissolve. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. For each genuine tension, identify the cheapest repair and say what it costs — what I would have to give up, weaken, or restate. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most at the least cost, and whether the resulting position is still recognizably illusionism or has quietly become a different view wearing the same name.
