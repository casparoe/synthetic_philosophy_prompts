The hundred-move version where the pot roughly doubles each pass: I think whoever takes at node one is being a dope, and if you asked me to predict what two students in a lab will actually do, I'd say they pass twenty or thirty times before somebody caves. But I'd also say taking is correct at the last node, and I can't see how that stops being true at 99, at 98... so apparently I think the prediction and the recommendation come apart here, and I'd like to know whether that's a coherent thing to think or just me wanting to have it both ways.

The chain store thing, ten towns, an incumbent who'd rather fight than share, an entrant who stays out only if she expects a fight: I think fighting in town one is right, and I think the "she folds in town ten, so she folds everywhere" line is clever and bad advice, even though I can't find the bad step in the induction.

Now change it so both players submit a program in advance, can't see each other's code, and the payoffs go to the programs. There I really do think a program that passes until node ninety is right, and I'd say the same if they're told up front that they're playing two copies of the same policy. So when nobody can react to anybody move by move, my verdict flips, and I don't know if that's a deep fact or a confusion on my part.
