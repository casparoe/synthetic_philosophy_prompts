(2) Now ask about that standard itself: is it good because of something further, or is it good just by being the standard?
(3) If something further makes it good, then it wasn't the ultimate standard after all, and the same question applies to whatever that further thing is. Regress, no stopping point, so nothing is ever ultimately good.
(4) If nothing further makes it good — it's good just by being the standard — then nothing rules out its having been the reverse. It's good for no reason at all. Arbitrary. Same shape as the horn where the gods happen to love some things, could just as easily have loved the opposite ones, and then the opposite ones would have been the pious ones.
(5) The standard either has something behind it or it doesn't, so those are the only two options.
(6) So every theory of value either makes value arbitrary or defers it forever, and deferring it forever is just as bad. Every theory makes value arbitrary.
(7) But then the Euthyphro dilemma isn't a problem for divine command theory in particular — it's a problem for everybody, welfare people and Kantians included. Which means it proves way too much and we should all give up on value, which is absurd.
