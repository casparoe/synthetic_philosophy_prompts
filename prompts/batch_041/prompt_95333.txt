ok so I'm a junior and I got put on our school's Student Conduct Circle, which is 3 students and 2 teachers, and we hear cases like once a week — 15 minutes each, we get about 10 minutes to read the writeup before — and we pick between a Repair Plan, a Formal Warning, or kicking it up to the principal for a suspension. The principal signs off but 90% of the time he just takes whatever we say, so basically we're 16 year olds putting stuff on other kids records. Its a lot.

And we keep fighting about the same thing. Someone does something bad and the first question somebody always asks is "well was it in his control?" because two years ago the district added this clause to the code that says no consequence if the behavior was fully outside the student's control. Then we argue for twenty minutes and never land anywhere.

My sister is a philosophy major and she told me about this guy P.F. Strawson and his essay "Freedom and Resentment." I read it twice and understood maybe 60% of it but the 60% was useful. What I got out of it: the real question isn't "did he have free will," its whether this kid is still someone we're in it with — somebody whose attitude toward us matters and who can be asked to answer for what he did — versus somebody we've stepped back from and started managing like a problem. She also said he draws a line between excusing somebody (this particular thing wasn't what it looked like, it was an accident, a mistake, he didn't know) and exempting them (this person isn't a fit subject for the demand at all, like a little kid or somebody who isn't himself). I definately think thats the argument we keep having without knowing it.

So can you turn that into a procedure we can actually run? The questions to ask in order, what counts as an answer to each, where it stops and says no consequence, where it hands off to the principal because its a values call and not a facts call. One page, runnable in 15 minutes by people who have read nothing, and mark the steps that are really just judgment calls dressed up as steps.

Stuff it has to handle:
- the kid who shoved someone in the hall while off his ADHD meds (there was a pharmacy shortage, so it wasn't exactly a choice, but it also wasn't like he was possessed)
- a kid who cussed out a teacher the same week his mom got deported, where everyone on the circle feels terrible for him
- the kid the whole circle has quietly given up on, where the honest feeling in the room is "thats just how he is," and I think we've been doing the managing thing with him for a year now
- the case where I'm friends with the kid, which will happen, and I want something I can actually do instead of just being told to recuse myself

Then run it on the first two. I want to see it give an answer I won't like, because I have a feeling its going to tell me the second kid still has to answer for it, and I already know thats going to be a horrible conversation.

Also be honest about what this cant do. I don't need a reading list or citations, I can't look anything up. And if the procedure is going to make us hand out more consequences than the control clause does, just say that, because thats going to be a real fight with the other students on the circle.
