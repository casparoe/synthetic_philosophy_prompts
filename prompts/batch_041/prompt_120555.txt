here's the idea. theres a result that says if everyone starts out with the same prior beliefs, and everyone knows everyone else is rational, and everyone knows that everyone knows it, and the market is already sitting in a nice efficient equilibrium with nobody trading for dumb reasons -- then NO trade happens. not "less trade", none, ever. and the reason is kind of funny: the second you make somebody an offer, they learn something from the mere fact that you wanted to trade. why would you be offering, unless you knew something they don't? so they update and say no thanks, and everyone reasons the same way, so the order book stays empty forever. (I think it's the Milgrom-Stokey theorem from 1982 but don't put any citation in, I'm not writing a bibliography.) in the real world trades happen for boring reasons instead: you need cash for rent, you want to hedge, you're an index fund on autopilot, or you just flat out disagree about the odds. and there's a second result I like even more, that if prices already contained all the available information, then nobody could make money by digging up information, so nobody would dig, so prices wouldn't contain all the information -- so the market being perfectly smart is sort of self-undermining. there's also some bargaining one where even two people who both want to trade can't get it done properly if each of them knows something the other doesn't.
