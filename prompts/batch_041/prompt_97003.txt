Hi, so I'm a junior and I'm in this philosophy club at my high school, theres about 14 of us, mostly juniors and seniors, and we meet Thursdays after school for an hour and a half. I got stuck running the session for our unit on memory and ethics. Our teacher handed us a thing about how some philosophers think there's a real duty to remember terrible things, especially terrible things done to your own people, and how other people like David Rieff think that constantly hauling the past back up can just keep a conflict alive forever, and honestly both feel right in different cases and I can't tell when each one applies. So instead of a lecture I want an activity where the tension actually happens to the group before anyone names it. The thing I keep noticing when I read about this is that the duty to remember feels way stronger when the people involved are your people (your family, your town, your lineage) and way weaker when it's strangers, and I want the game to make that asymmetry visible and then make people uncomfortable about it, and also give the forgetting side a real moment where remembering clearly costs something in the present.

Heres what I have so far, please build it out properly. Setting: 14 students, one 90-minute session, a normal classroom with a whiteboard and movable desks. Phase one: I hand out sealed role cards at the door, assigned randomly, no swapping, and I don't tell them what the cards are for. Everyone is a member of a town council in a made-up place called Marlowe Bend, and there was an atrocity there about eighty years ago, one community did something terrible to another, and I'll put the bare facts (date, who, roughly how many) on a one-page handout so nobody has to invent history or import real-world atrocities. The role cards give each person a connection: descendant of a victim family, descendant of a perpetrator family, a newcomer with no family link at all, a teenager whose parents say the town should stop dwelling on it, a local historian, a mayor up for reelection, and a few unaffiliated residents. Then the council has a fixed pot of 24 tokens and must spend them across four options: build and maintain a memorial museum (costs tokens every round), a scholarship fund for descendants of victims, a general town welfare fund that helps everyone, or leave it in the town reserve. They also have to agree on the exact wording of the memorial plaque, and I want a rule that the plaque needs a two-thirds majority so it can't be fudged. We run three rounds, and between rounds I read out a news item (first year is quiet, second year the memorial gets vandalized, third year there's a protest at the site), and I quietly raise the annual maintenance cost each round so that remembering keeps eating into the welfare fund. Then phase two: everyone trades cards with someone across the room, ideally landing on the opposite side from where they started, and we redo the same three rounds with the new roles, and I compare how the same people spent when it was their people versus when it wasn't. Debrief after that, and I want questions that map what happened onto the actual idea, like who felt the pull to fund the museum when they were a descendant versus when they were a newcomer, whether anyone changed their mind about the plaque wording, and what it would take for someone with no connection to feel the duty anyway. Also please be honest with me about which part of the real philosophical issue this just can't reproduce and that I'll have to explain myself at the end, I already suspect the game can show the asymmetry but not the actual argument that close relations are partly constituted by shared memory, and definitely can't reproduce what it's like for a real victim community.

And please tell me what will go wrong the first time I run it, because I know something will. My guess is people will refuse to play a perpetrator descendant, or they'll all just agree that obviously we should remember and the whole thing collapses into everyone nodding, or they'll treat the tokens like Monopoly money and stop caring. Also I'm worried the made-up atrocity will feel fake and they'll stop taking it seriously. Give me the full runnable thing, rules, materials, a timeline with minutes, the exact role cards, the news items, the debrief script, and a frank note on what the facilitator has to supply that the activity can't. No real money, candy or poker chips are fine. Also I don't want them hearing the names Margalit or Rieff until the very end.
