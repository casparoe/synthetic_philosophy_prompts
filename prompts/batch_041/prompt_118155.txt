A juror who privately thinks the defendant probably did it: I don't think she can just decide to believe him innocent, so I'm not even sure the ethics question arises there. A woman caring for a husband with a probably terminal illness who comes to believe he will recover: permissible, maybe admirable, though it runs against the medical evidence. A scientist who comes to believe a rival fabricated data on thin evidence because it would vindicate her own theory: not fine. A grieving parent who believes their dead child is watching over them: fine, or at least not blameworthy. Someone who adopts a conspiracy theory because it gives their life meaning, against the evidence: not fine. Is there one principle that gets all of these, or do I need several?
