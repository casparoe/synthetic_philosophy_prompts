I've fallen down the modern Stoicism rabbit hole lately — the popular books, the morning journaling, r/Stoicism, the whole routine — and something about how the scene handles the "dichotomy of control" has started to bug me in a way I can't quite put my finger on. The basic move everyone teaches is: take whatever's eating at you, sort it into "up to me" and "not up to me," and then let go of the second pile because it was never yours to carry. Fine, I get it. But my employer has turned this into an actual program. There's a worksheet you fill out, and there's a little dashboard in the wellness app that computes a "control ratio" — the percentage of your logged concerns that you classified as outside your control — and a higher ratio is treated as better, more Stoic, more resilient. Managers are encouraged to push people toward high ratios. Meanwhile the same employer's review rubric holds people accountable for "team culture," "collaboration," and "how the quarter went," none of which any single person fully controls, and nobody seems to notice the tension. I was venting about this to a friend who's doing a phil degree and she said "that's just a presupposition problem," which was not helpful.

So here's what I want. Treat this whole thing — the worksheet, the ratio, the way it's actually used — as a practice, and tell me what philosophical commitments it presupposes. Not what modern Stoicism says in the abstract, but what does this specific machinery only make sense under? I'm especially curious about three things. (a) Does it need the up-to-me / not-up-to-me division to be exhaustive and mutually exclusive, or would a more fine-grained picture — partial control, shared control, control over the process but not the outcome — break the metric? (b) Does it quietly assume that responsibility and blame track control one-for-one, and is that actually a Stoic commitment or a modern self-help accretion bolted on top? (c) Does "more concerns in the not-up-to-me pile = more progress" presuppose a substantive view about value and emotion — say, that caring about indifferent things is itself a defect, or that emotions are directly voluntary and so can be sorted away on command? Then separate what's strictly presupposed by the practice from what merely fits it comfortably; I suspect a couple of different theories could underwrite the same worksheet without anyone noticing. Tell me where it's genuinely underdetermined, and if the ratio metric does presuppose somthing false or incoherent, say what the program would have to look like if we kept the exercises but dropped that commitment. No citations or reading lists needed — just the reconstruction and your assessment.
