I'm a second-year doctoral student in a decision theory group, and I need to submit a dissertation prospectus by the end of the term. I have a half-formed idea that I'd like you to develop into a proposal. My group argues constantly about the common prior assumption—whether it is an innocent modeling idealization, an empirically false claim about how different people's beliefs actually relate, or a substantive requirement that rationality imposes on agents—and the debate always stalls because the participants never specify which of those three things they mean. The more I work through it, though, the more I suspect the common prior is not even the load-bearing assumption. The agreement theorem also requires the agents to share a state space: the same set of possible worlds, divided the same way into the same cells. That assumption is much harder to state cleanly, because the state space is typically just whatever the modeler happened to write down, so the agents' sharing it may be an artifact of how the model was constructed rather than a fact about the agents or a demand of rationality. What I want to propose, then, is roughly this: once the common state space assumption is separated from the common prior assumption, which of the two is actually doing the work in the agreement theorem, and does the theorem's philosophical significance survive if the common state space is treated as a modeling choice rather than as a premise about rationality?

Please turn this into a prospectus representing roughly ten to fifteen pages of work spread across a single year. It needs a precise statement of the question, sharp enough that it has a determinate answer; an account of why the existing treatments leave it open, framed in terms of positions and arguments rather than as a literature survey, since I will supply the citations myself; the method, meaning what I would actually do month by month and why that approach rather than simply cataloging what others have said; a clear sense of what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and an honest assessment of scope, including what I would have to set aside in order to finish within the year. Write it as continuous prose in the form of a letter to my advisor, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
