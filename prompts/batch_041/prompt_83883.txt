After twenty-eight years of reading X-ray diffraction films I retired, and I have spent the last two winters on philosophy of mind, working through Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach. His three-level account of messages is the most useful thing I have found in it, though it leaves me uneasy. As I understand the account, a message has a frame message, carried by its gross structural features, which announces that there is a message here at all; an outer message, carried implicitly by patterns within it, which tells the receiver how to decode; and an inner message, the content. Hofstadter's sharp point is that the outer message can never be spelled out: instructions for decoding it would themselves have to be part of the inner message, which cannot be read until the decoding mechanism has already been found, so the outer message must work as a set of triggers on machinery the receiver brings along. He goes on to suggest that an aperiodic crystal packaged inside a regular geometric form is a good candidate for an information-bearer, which describes a book and a DNA molecule equally well, and which is the sort of remark a crystallographer notices, given Schrödinger's old conjecture that the gene would turn out to be an aperiodic crystal.

Pushing the account, I arrived at the following, whose conclusion I cannot accept.

(1) The decoding mechanism for any message must come from the receiver's prior repertoire; the message itself cannot supply it.
(2) Therefore what a structure yields is fixed by the repertoire rather than by the structure.
(3) A repertoire is a fact about a receiver's constitution and history, and no repertoire is privileged over any other.
(4) For any structure and any content, some possible repertoire yields that content from that structure, since a code can always be stipulated and stipulating a code costs nothing.
(5) Therefore any structure carries any content, relative to some possible receiver.
(6) Therefore the difference between a diffraction film and a spilled cup of coffee, and the difference between a correct and an incorrect reading of either, is a difference between receivers rather than between objects.
(7) Therefore structural science has no subject matter; it is the projection of trained repertoires onto matter.

I have spent a career denying (6). When a student misindexes a film the student is simply wrong, and it is the crystal, not the student, that makes him wrong; and in my trade there is no sender at all, yet the film can still refuse a reading. I would rather keep (1) than (7), since (1) seems to me exactly right about every film I ever read. I suspect (4), but I cannot see how to deny it without denying (1) as well. Tell me which step fails, why it looked so plausible, and what the repair costs — in particular, whether after the repair meaning has any location left, or whether the receiver's contribution has merely been pushed further back.
