I'm on a small grant panel — four or five philosophers, three years, no money for experiments — and we're supposed to fund work on Dutch book arguments. I keep going back and forth about what's actually worth funding. My instinct is that whether probabilism is justified is the boring framing, and the live stuff is further in: whether a Dutch book shows incoherent credences are irrational or only that a bookie can take your money; how much weight is being carried by the assumptions that you'd take the bets at all and that utility is linear in money; whether the diachronic versions (for conditionalization, for reflection) do anything beyond smuggling in an extra premise; whether accuracy-first approaches make the whole apparatus redundant. Someone in the group says the real issue is that these are representation theorems about preference being quietly read as constraints on credence, and I cant tell if that's a substantive distinction or two descriptions of one thing. So: what should we fund, in what order, and why? I want the ordering defended — which questions are upstream of which, which are tractable in three years by philosophers rather than decision theorists, and what would move one up or down the list. Under 700 words, ordering first.
