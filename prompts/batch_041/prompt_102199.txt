You're a philosopher who works on the formal structure of value relations — transitivity, comparability, which axioms you'd actually want 'better than' to satisfy — and I want you to write me a dialogue. Two equally sharp people, neither a strawman: one insists that 'better than' has to be transitive, full stop, and treats any appearance to the contrary as a sign that our intuitions at some step are broken; the other thinks spectrum/sorites-style arguements show betterness genuinely isn't transitive in some domains and that we should just swallow that. They need to actually answer each other's moves, not trade monologues.

To keep it off the well-worn Parfit population track, have them work a spectrum I'll give you instead. Take a single person's life and line up lives like this: L1 is short but extremely good, and each life further down is a bit longer but a bit lower in quality, with the quality drops shrinking as you go, until Ln is enormously long and only just barely worth living. At every adjacent step it's tempting to say the longer one is at least as good — maybe even better — yet the two endpoints look nothing alike. Make them fight over whether the chain really forces intransitivity, whether the step-by-step verdict is just unreliable intuition, what happens if you deny transitivity only for 'better' and not for 'at least as good', and what all this does to the idea that betterness is a strict order at all.

Before you start writing, brainstorm a bit about the strongest version of each side's position so the dialogue has real teeth. End with a short paragraph where you commit — say definately which side you think wins and why, and flag anywhere the exchange leans on a contested reading of the spectrum. Casual tone is fine.
