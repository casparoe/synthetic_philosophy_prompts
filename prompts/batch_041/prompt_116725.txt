So I've been toying with a variant of the dark forest picture — you know the one, everyone stays quiet because anything loud gets shot — and I think the standard version buries the lede.

Here's my wrinkle. Silence is free. It costs nothing and it proves nothing. So a civilization that genuinely means no harm has exactly one way to credibly say so, and it's the one thing that guarantees it gets killed: light up. Broadcasting is a costly signal, and it's costly because it's suicide. Which means the honest ones are all dead and the survivors are all liars — so silence isn't just prudent, it's evidence of bad faith. The forest is dark because it's full of liars.

Second wrinkle, and this is the one I actually care about. Assume the hunters' strikes are visible. A kill is a muzzle flash, and a muzzle flash tells every third party in range where the shooter is. So visibility cuts both ways: loud gets you found, but loud also gets you witnessed, and nobody takes a shot that a dozen other hunters will see. A beacon buys you time. Not safety — time. Witnesses drift apart, the flash fades, and the patient hunter outlives the audience.

Okay. Write me a short story set in that galaxy.

Some parameters. I want it to be about a decision, not a battle — no armadas, no laser fights, none of that. Pick one point of view and stay in it: either the one who has to decide whether to go loud, or the one who recieves a broadcast and has to decide what to do about it. The second case is definately the richer one, I think, but your call.

Don't explain the physics to me and don't have anybody deliver a lecture on signaling or game theory. The philosophy has to be carried by the situation and by what the characters actually do and say — by what they're willing to trade and what they refuse to trade. And if you think my two wrinkles are incoherent, or that they quietly collapse back into the boring version of the dark forest, dramatize that objection inside the story rather than tacking on a disclaimer at the end. I'd much rather watch a character discover the problem than read a note about it.

No references to existing science fiction, please. Don't name-check anything, don't borrow anybody's aliens, don't rework a famous scene. I want your own invention, your own biology if you need one.

Somewhere between 900 and 1500 words, and write it in American English.
