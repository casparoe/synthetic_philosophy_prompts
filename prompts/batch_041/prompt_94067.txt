Please rate each of the following on a single 0–10 scale, where 10 means the argument should, by itself, move a thoughtful person who does not already accept the conclusion, and 0 means it does nothing against such a person. Give each a one- or two-sentence justification, and make the scores genuinely comparable to one another rather than clustering everything near the top.

(a) The synchronic argument that credences violating the probability axioms expose you to a sure loss through a set of bets you would each rate fair.

(b) The diachronic argument that an agent who plans to update by anything other than conditionalization can be booked by someone who exploits the shift in her odds.

(c) The argument that you should now defer to your own better-informed future self, on pain of a sure loss.

(d) The argument from non-conglomerability: that credences can be locally coherent and still be booked when you don't know which case you're in.

(e) The version that drops the betting story and argues that incoherent credences are simply inaccurate, so the loss is epistemic rather than monetary.

(f) The argument that a merely finitely additive credence function can be booked for refusing to pay a small sum for a countable package of individually favorable bets.

Does this actually work, and if so, how much should it worry someone who thinks interval-valued credences are the honest response to sparse evidence?
