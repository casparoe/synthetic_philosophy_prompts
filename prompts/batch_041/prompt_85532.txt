Ok here's a thing that bugs me. Imagine a club whose only rule about rules is: any rule, including this one, can be changed by a two-thirds vote. The members use it to raise the threshold to unanimity, then a year later 95% vote to drop it back to two-thirds. Someone insists the second vote is void — the unanimity rule now governs every change and was never validly repealled, so it's circular. I lean toward "no paradox, rules are just what people accept and act on," maybe 0.75. What's your credence that self-amendment of this kind can produce a genuine indeterminacy, a case where there's simply no fact of the matter about what the rules are, as opposed to a puzzle that dissolves once you stop treating rules like a priori propositions? Break it down: how much rides on whether validity has to bottom out in an unamendable basic norm rather than social acceptance, how much on whether the amendment rule can even reach itself, how much on whether a later valid act can reach back and void an earlier one, and how much on my bar ("no fact of the matter") being too strict. Then say which term you're least sure of and what would move it.
