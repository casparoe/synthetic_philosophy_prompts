I'm in a seminar on philosophy of mind and we just read Dennett's paper where he quines qualia using Chase and Sanborn, the two coffee tasters who both say the coffee tastes different but disagree about whether it's the coffee or their taste that changed. I get the negative argument—that the alleged intrinsic, ineffable, private, directly apprehensible properties don't survive scrutiny—but I'm not convinced the whole notion is incoherent. Anyways, my roommate and I came up with a variant we call the dimmer-switch case: two people have identical color discrimination, identical verbal reports, identical memory and behavior, but one reports that her visual experiences vary along a continuous vividness dimension she can adjust with a mental dial, while the other insists her experiences have no such dimension. We can't tell if this is just a difference in how they talk or a genuine difference in phenomenal character. Please argue for the position that qualia are a coherent posit, but only after granting Dennett everything he's entitled to: dont defend Cartesian inner objects, don't just assert that introspection reveals them, and don't lean on the what-it's-like locution as if it settles anything. Use Chase and Sanborn and our dimmer-switch variant to show what a defensible, non-Cartesian notion of qualia would have to look like, and say where Dennett's argument still bites. I want a real argument, not a survey. End with a line that begins Verdict: followed by either qualia are a coherent posit or qualia are not a coherent posit.
