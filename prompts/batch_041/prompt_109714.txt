I host a monthly interview series on epistemology, and next month's guest is a philosopher whose forthcoming book defends a two-register solution to the lottery and preface paradoxes. Her distinction is between conviction — the propositions a person will stake a decision on, treated as closed under conjunction and required to be consistent — and acceptance — the propositions she will take as premises in inquiry, neither closed nor required to be consistent. On her account, the lottery paradox demands conviction where only acceptance is warranted, while the preface paradox demands a consistency of acceptance that we have no reason to want. She holds that the asymmetry is principled because the two registers answer to different loss functions: conviction to the cost of error in action, acceptance to the cost of foreclosing a question.

Draft eight to ten interview questions that press on the substance of this position rather than letting her restate it. I want at least one question each on whether the two registers are anything more than a relabelled high/low threshold for belief; on whether the position collapses into a stakes-sensitive account of belief; on what happens when the stakes shift mid-inquiry and a proposition has to migrate from one register to the other; and on the most awkward case for her, the practitioner who accepts a proposition and must act on it. She is adept at meeting challenges with 'that is a question for the theory of assertion, not for the theory of belief', so order the questions so that this reply becomes progressively harder to give, and close with one about what would show the two-register picture to be unnecessary. Supply the follow-up you expect each question to need, and don't hedge: where the position faces a choice, say which way you think it should go and why, and identify the single point at which it is most likely to fail.
