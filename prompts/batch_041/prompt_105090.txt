I help run a small philosophy Discord — a few hundred people, mostly folks who read pop-philosophy and argue at 1am — and last month we did a session on hedonism that completely fell apart in a way I can't stop thinking about. The whole fight was basically: is pleasure one thing, or a pile of unrelated things we just slap the same word on? Someone said the pleasure of scratching a mosquito bite raw and the pleasure of finally getting a proof have literally nothing in common; someone else said no, there's a felt "yesss" quality in both and that's what makes them both pleasure; and a third person said the second person was just describing *liking*, not *feeling*, and we went around in circles for two hours. Every intro I look up says "hedonism says pleasure is the only good" and skips the exact part we care about, and I don't want to make everyone read a textbook anyway.

So I want to build a "what do you actually think pleasure is?" quiz for the server — like one of those personality quizzes but for views. 8 to 10 questions, each one a concrete case with 2 or 3 answer options, plus a scoring key that sorts people into the real positions on whether pleasures have anything in common. The thing I care about most: nobody should be able to game it by knowing the labels, so no question can just be "do you think pleasure is a feeling or an attitude?" It has to be cases, e.g. — is the good part of scratching an itch the scratching or the stopping? is the pleasure of a hot bath after freezing the same *kind* of thing as the pleasure of winning an argument you'd been losing for a week? what's going on in relief, or in a smell you like, or in savoring a memory, or in the weird pleasure of pressing on a bruise? I've been reading around and I think the live options are something like: (1) there's one special feeling-tone that every pleasure shares, and relief, intellectual pleasure, smell-pleasure are all cases of it; (2) pleasure isn't a feeling at all but a kind of liking/wanting attitude you take toward stuff; (3) pleasure is a way of perceiving or representing something as good; (4) pleasure is more like a command or impulse to keep going; (5) the deflationary one — no common core, "pleasure" is just a family/cluster word; and maybe (6) the view that pleasant and unpleasant aren't two ends of one scale but two separate things that can both be present at once. I also can't tell whether "bodily vs. higher" pleasures is a real fault line here or a red herring.

Please write the quiz and key. For each question give me the case, the options, and — in a separate section, not shown to quiz-takers — which answer pattern points where. Each position should get a short plain-English label, one sentence on what holding it commits you to, and its single most awkward case (the one the quiz-taker who lands there will immediately bring up). Where two positions can't really be separated by the cases I listed, say so instead of fudging it; where some combination of answers doesn't map onto any actual position, tell me that too and whether it's empty because it's incoherent or just because nobody's bothered to hold it. And if you think one of my six options above is secretly two different views, split it and tell me what splits it. Assume the quiz-takers are smart but haven't read any of this.
