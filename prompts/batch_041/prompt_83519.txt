I am taking a course on logical positivism, and we have just read Schlick's essay on the meaning of life. I am having trouble fitting it together with the rest of his philosophy. As I understand it, Schlick's central claim in the Vienna Circle is that a sentence has cognitive meaning only if it can be verified by experience or is true by definition. The question of what the meaning of life is does not seem to be either. It is not an empirical hypothesis, and it is not a tautology. So by his own criterion, shouldn't he have dismissed the question as meaningless and said nothing more about it? Yet in the essay he seems to treat the question as serious and even to give an answer, something about meaning being found in play, in youth, or in activities that are ends in themselves rather than in some goal beyond life. Is he simply being inconsistent, or does he think the meaning of life is a special kind of fact that we can verify in some unusual way? I would like to understand how he can hold on to the verificationist criterion and still write an essay answering the meaning-of-life question. What am I missing about what he means by meaning?
