Two theories of causation I cant get to sit still next to each other: the counterfactual-dependence one (c caused e just in case, had c not happened, e wouldnt have) and the interventionist one (c causes e if an intervention on c — an idealised wiggle that severs c's other incoming arrows and changes nothing else — makes e follow along, in a system of structural equations). Compare them for me, properly in the weeds rather than the potted textbook version. Where do they genuinely come apart, and which of the usual hard cases (pre-emption, overdetermination, double prevention) does each handle and each mangle? Then the bit I actually want: a colleague insists that interventionism is just the counterfactual theory with possible worlds swapped out for engineering jargon, and that an intervention is doing no work that the closest possible world wasnt already doing. Is he right, or is there a real difference? And does either view explain why we say the spark caused the fire and not the oxygen, or is that just a fact about us? British English please.
