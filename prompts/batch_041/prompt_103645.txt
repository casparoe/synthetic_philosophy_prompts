I am making a handout for a graduate seminar on machine cognition and want a taxonomy of the positions on whether large language models are agents, simulators, or something else. Build it as a decision tree from the logical space rather than from a list of labels: the response should be a numbered list of exactly five items, one per leaf, each a sentence or two naming the position and giving the single question that separates it from its nearest neighbour plus one sentence on that position's most awkward commitment, with no introduction or conclusion. Phrase every separating question so that someone can answer it without already knowing the labels — they should turn on concrete commitments, such as whether the model's outputs are caused by a persisting goal-directed system, whether they are better explained as sampling from a distribution over possible characters or agents, whether any system with the right functional profile counts regardless of its substrate, and whether the only genuine agents in the loop are the humans and the training process — and not on which word one prefers. Where two leaves cannot be separated by any answer to your questions, flag them rather than papering over the difference, and where the space leaves a combination unoccupied, note briefly whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
