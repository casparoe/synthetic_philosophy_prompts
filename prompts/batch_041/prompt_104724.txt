I run a small podcast about law, morality, and public life. I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have spent the last year thinking my way through the Hart–Devlin debate and the wider question of legal moralism. Next month my guest is a philosopher who defends a version of legal moralism that is not the standard one. Her claim is that the law may enforce certain moral norms not because the conduct they prohibit is wrong in itself, but because the norm is a shared resource — part of what she calls the moral commons — and that free-riding on it damages the collective capacity for self-rule. I want the interview to put pressure on that view rather than let her restate it. Please give me a sequence of around a dozen questions, each followed by a follow-up that anticipates the answer she is most likely to give. I need at least one question on how she separates a norm that is a genuine shared resource from one that is merely widespread or traditional; one on whether her position can avoid collapsing into the harm principle or into ordinary paternalism; one on what her view implies when the moral commons is unjust, exclusionary, or itself the product of domination; one on how the law should act when the relevant norm is seriously contested among citizens; and one final question about what evidence or argument could count against her view. The questions should press on the load-bearing parts of her position — especially the baseline against which free-riding is measured, and what she would say about a case where enforcing the norm would preserve the commons but impose serious costs on a dissenting minority. Please do not include generic questions about how she became interested in the topic, and do not give me a reading list or citations.
