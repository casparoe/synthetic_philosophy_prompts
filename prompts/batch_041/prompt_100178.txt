I have been reading about the duty to remember and the case for forgetting, and I keep encountering a distinction I cannot name. Consider two communities that suffered the same historical atrocity. The first commemorates it annually, teaches it in schools, and treats it as the center of its public identity; members who say they want to move on are accused of betraying the dead. The second acknowledges the atrocity in textbooks and museums but does not organize its identity around it; outsiders describe it as having forgotten, even though its members know the history perfectly well. The first is praised for fidelity, the second for healing. The puzzle is that the second community’s forgetting is not the opposite of memory; it is a change in the memory’s role—from a constitutive story that defines who we are to a bounded historical fact that we know but are not defined by. Is there an established name for this shift, or for the trap in which the very act of remembering is both the discharge of a duty owed to victims and the perpetuation of a wound that keeps the living captive? I know the debate is often framed as memory versus forgetting, and I know that some writers treat forgetting as a form of repair, but that framing seems too coarse. What I want is a concept that marks the difference between remembering an event and making the event constitutive of one’s identity, and that can explain why a community might be morally required to remember and also morally required to stop remembering in that way. If there is no established term, please coin one and define it, distinguishing it from the nearest concepts—collective memory, transitional justice, narrative identity, therapeutic forgetting, the right to be forgotten—and say where its boundaries lie. I am not asking for a bibliography or a reading list; I want the conceptual carving itself. Please answer in American English, and push back if this way of framing things is confused—for example, if the distinction I am after collapses, or if the duty to remember and the case for forgetting are not as opposed as I am making them sound.
