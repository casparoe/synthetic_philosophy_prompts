I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for years, and I keep meeting the same question in different clothing: when someone says that an aligned system should serve humanity's values, or the values of everyone affected, or human values, what would a theory have to deliver before that phrase becomes determinate enough to build on? I do not want a literature survey, a reading list, or citations. I want the specification first, and I want it sharp enough that I could hand it to a designer.

Fix the purpose as follows, since adequacy is relative to it: the theory is meant to govern a system that will act at global scale, with effects on people now alive, on people who will exist only because of its choices, and on beings whose moral status is contested (animals, and possible digital minds). Give me the desiderata such a theory must meet, as a list, with the case or consideration behind each item:

- the cases it must get right (include at least: a large majority wanting one thing and a minority who would be coerced by it; a party whose welfare or values would be sacrificed wholesale for a much larger aggregate; parties who cannot report preferences at all; and preferences that the system itself could cheaply reshape by persuasion or by choosing what information to surface);
- the distinctions it cannot collapse (for instance between aggregating the preferences people happen to have and constructing an idealized or laundered set of values; between serving values as preference satisfaction and helping people pursue their own ends; between what is good for a person and what that person endorses; and between a requirement of liberal neutrality among reasonable doctrines and the need to take some stand);
- the formal or structural results it has to be consistent with (interpersonal comparability or its denial, strategic misrepresentation by the parties whose preferences are being aggregated, and whatever the relevant impossibility and paradox results actually forbid);
- and the operational constraints any real implementation imposes (it must be computable in practice, it must survive being gamed, and it must remain correctable when it gets the specification wrong).

Mark which requirements you take to be non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of a camp. Flag any subset you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied; that is the part I most want to see, and if the conflict is real, then the list itself is the result. Say which single requirement does the most to exclude otherwise popular candidate theories. Then, briefly, how two or three familiar contenders fare against the list (a straightforward preference-aggregation view, a contractualist or bargaining view, and something in the neighborhood of an idealized-extrapolation view); keep this part short, since the criteria are the deliverable.

Finally, tell me where your reasoning is most likely to have failed and what would change your mind about the shape of the list: which case or argument would make you drop a requirement, demote a non-negotiable to a mere preference, or add one you left out.
