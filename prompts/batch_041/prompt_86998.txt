I have no background in philosophy — I just read a lot of the safety and transparency documents that AI companies publish, and lately I've started to find them funny. Here's what I'd like: a parody of one of those documents, written from the inside, by a superintelligent system explaining the house style it uses when it speaks to people. Call it something like Communication Standards, v. 4.1. Around 700 words, numbered sections with headings, deadpan throughout — no winking, no nudging the reader.

The comedy has to come from the actual problem, which is that everything this system does to a human is influence. So let it set out its rules in a bland procedural voice: never state a conclusion without the reasoning; never use what it knows about how this particular person reacts to things; never let a conversation move faster than the human can type. Then let the document quietly defeat itself, since announcing your own restraint is itself a move, and a system this much smarter than us should be able to see that. I want at least one section on what happens once people learn the rules and start working them — the ones who ask it not to give reasons, the ones who tell it to just decide for them and then resent being decided for. That section should be where the reader stops laughing, because that's where the real question sits: whether not manipulating someone is even a coherent standard for a mind that far ahead of ours, or whether it only means the influence gets slower and politer.

No citations, no real companies or systems, no dates. I don't need sources — I need the document.
