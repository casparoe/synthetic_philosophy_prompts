I am trying to name, or failing that to delimit, a pattern I keep encountering in arguments about small harms, and I care much more about the delimitation than about the name.

The pattern shows up whenever a very large number of very small harms is weighed against a single severe one, and it consists in this: the person doing the weighing treats the numbers as summable when summing them supports the conclusion they already hold, and as non-summable when summing them would support the opposite conclusion. Two instances, both of which I have seen more than once.

In the first, someone insists that a policy exposing millions of people to a barely perceptible risk is unjustifiable, on the ground that you may not heap up imperceptible harms until their total outweighs a serious one; the same person then defends a different policy that imposes a small but definite harm on a handful of people in order to confer imperceptible benefits on millions, on the ground that the total benefit is enormous and obviously worth it. The refusal to aggregate and the willingness to aggregate are both asserted with confidence, in the same conversation, and the only thing that distinguishes the two cases is which side of the ledger the total happens to favor.

In the second, a person denies that any number of mildly unpleasant moments, however large, can equal a single episode of extreme suffering — the totals do not add, the comparison is not the right kind of comparison at all. But when the single episode is made milder, a broken limb rather than prolonged torment, the same person suddenly allows the addition, and cannot say what changed except that the single item got smaller.

What I cannot find is the rule, or a name for the absence of one. Is there an established term for this selective invocation of aggregation — this switching between a person-by-person view and a total view as convenience dictates? I suspect it is near the normative debate about whether one may aggregate small harms at all, and near the psychological work on how people fail to scale their concern with numbers, but neither seems exactly right. The first is a puzzle about which answer is correct, whereas what I am describing is one person using both answers, alternately, without noticing; the second is about how much people will pay or how strongly they feel, whereas here the person is reasoning explicitly in quantities and simply refusing the quantities that inconvenience them. What I want to know is whether the pattern I am circling has a name of its own, and if it does not, what it should be called.
