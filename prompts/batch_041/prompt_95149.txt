I teach a senior seminar that ends with a unit on the careenium — Hofstadter's picture from I Am a Strange Loop, in case your memory of it is fuzzy, of a giant billiard table with enormous numbers of little magnetized balls careening around and clumping into stable higher-order structures. The clumps track regularities in the world outside the table and seem to interact with each other in ways you can describe perfectly well without ever mentioning an individual ball. Hofstadter's question is which level is really doing the shoving — the balls, or the clumps. He means it as a stand-in for the brain: neurons at the bottom, concepts/symbols at the top. Every year my students get stuck on whether the symbol level is doing any real causal work or is just a handy summary of what the neurons were already going to do anyway.

What I want from you: your best reconstruction of what Wilfrid Sellars would have said about this. The real historical Sellars — he died in the late eighties, long before any of this was written, so he never saw the careenium. Not a style pastiche (please skip the dashes-every-other-word thing, it gets tiresome), but his actual commitments brought to bear: the manifest vs. scientific image, the Myth of Jones, his insistence that the space of reasons is normative rather than a second causal order, the grain argument, whatever else you think is genuinely his. Would he say the symbols push the balls around? Would he call the question a category mistake, and if so, which category? Would he read the careenium as a threat to the manifest image or as exactly the kind of thing he was trying to build? And where would he dig in against the students who want to just shrug and call the symbols epiphenominal?

I'm after the machinery working, not a verdict, so: finish with a short seperate note distinguishing what's pretty settled among people who actually read him from what you're extrapolating into contested territory. Don't hand me a confident "Sellars would say X" if the honest answer is that his texts pull two ways — tell me where the pull is, and flag anywhere you're leaning on a particular reading rather than on something everyone agrees he held.
