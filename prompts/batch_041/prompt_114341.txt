I write about biology and medicine for a general readership, and I keep finding that quarrels among my sources are really quarrels about what it means to say that something has a function. Two accounts dominate. On the etiological account, a trait's function is the effect for which it was selected. On the causal-role account, a part's function is the contribution it makes to a capacity of the system that contains it. I would like the most important implications of choosing between them for the subjects I actually cover: the long-running dispute over how much of the genome is functional; the difference between classifying a condition as a disorder and classifying it as a variation; ecological talk of an ecosystem that is or is not functioning properly; and the awkward cases in which nothing was selected — a gene that engineers have inserted into a synthetic organism, or a feature that spread by drift. For each, say where the two accounts agree, where they force divergent conclusions, and which one a journalist should default to when the sources disagree; then tell me which single implication matters most for how I frame the piece.
