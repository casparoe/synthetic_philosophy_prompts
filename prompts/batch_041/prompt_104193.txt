My problem is the standard question. Everyone can recite the systems reply, everyone can recite Searle's comeback to it, and I get thirty identical essays. So I've built a variant, and I want the question built around it. Here's the case, and yes, I want it printed in the question stem:

The Bilingual Room. Everything is set up the usual way: the operator sits in the room, gets Chinese input, consults a rulebook of formal rules, and pushes out Chinese output. Only this time the operator is a fluent native speaker of Chinese. She's under strict instructions to produce every output by applying the rulebook mechanically, and she's being watched, so she does. It turns out the rulebook is a mediocre one — its rules mishandle a family of constructions, so the room's replies are smooth and fluent but systematically wrong in ways that make her wince, since she can see the mistakes. (a) Does the room understand Chinese? Does the operator? (b) Now suppose she ditches the rulebook and just answers from her own grasp of the language. The room's behaviour gets much better. Who understands now — the room, the operator, both, neither? (c) What do your answers to (a) and (b) do to the move where Searle says: fine, then let the man memorise the rulebook and the entire database, so that he just is the system — does he understand Chinese then, and is that a good reply to the systems reply?

That's the crux I'm after.
