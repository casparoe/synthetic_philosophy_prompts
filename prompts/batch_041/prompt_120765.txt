I'm a backend engineer, no formal philosophy background, but I fell down a rabbit hole recently reading about a clinical condition called pain asymbolia. Short version: people feel pain, they can point right at it and tell you how intense it is, but it just doesn't bother them. Some of them reportedly smile through it or leave their hand where it shouldn't be. What messed me up is it makes you ask what the bad part of pain actually is — the sensation, or the part where you mind it. Are those even seperate things or did we just assume they were the same thing because they usually come together?

I want a short story out of this, like 1500-2500 words, but with a twist I haven't seen anywhere and I want you to take it seriously. My character has a weird selective version of it: she keeps the full motivational pull of pain — she flinches, her hand yanks back off the stove, her body does all the self-preservation stuff automatically — but the "this is bad" part is just gone. No dread, no suffering, no "please make it stop." She dodges pain the way you'd dodge a frisbee. So on the outside she looks normal, she just isn't having a bad time.

The tension should come from two places. First, a treatment becomes available and she has to decide whether she wants it, and she can't tell if she's broken or if everyone else is just being dramatic about something she can't see the point of. Second, and this is the bit I actually care about, somewhere in there give me a scene where she's eating something good or listening to music and realizes she genuinely can't tell from the inside whether she's enjoying it or just not in pain. She's got no way to check. That's the scene I want to walk away from the story thinking about.

Constraints, since I know models love to go medical-drama on this: no disease-of-the-week plot, no wise doctor explaining the philosophy to her in a monologue, no real researchers or studies or papers named (I don't want citations, I just want the story). Contemporary setting, first person or close third, grounded, could be anyone. Keep the ending unresolved — she doesn't decide, and I don't want a reveal that she was secretly happy or secretly miserable the whole time. Just let her sit in not knowing. Bonus if there's a moment where somebody close to her is disturbed by how fine she is about something, and she can't tell if they're right to be.
