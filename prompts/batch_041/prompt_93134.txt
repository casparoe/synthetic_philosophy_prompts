ok so ive been reading about letterforms lately and theres a puzzle thats been bugging me, and I want you to write me a poem about it.

Heres the puzzle. Think of every 'a' youve ever seen. A serif one in a book, a fat sans-serif one on a sign, a kids crayon scrawl, the single-storey one that looks like a circle with a little tail (the way most people actually handwrite), and the two-storey one with the closed bowl at the bottom and the hook leaning over the top (the way its printed). They share basically no single feature. Some have a closed loop, some dont, some have a little foot or spur at the bottom right, some are all one stroke. And yet you never once confuse any of them for a 'd', or an 'o', or a 'q'. So what the hell is doing the work? Theres no definition, no essence, no one shape they all have in common, and yet the category holds rock solid.

My hunch (and I think theres some old AI project from decades ago, they tried to get a program to invent a whole alphabet in one consistent style, generate all 26 letters so theyd look like they belong to the same family) is that an 'a' isnt a shape at all, its a bundle of roles. A bowl that holds a counter, a stem that stands up on the right, an arc that leans over the top, a spur or terminal where a stroke stops. Any actual 'a' is just one way of filling those roles, and the style is all in how you fill them. Identity lives at the level of roles, style lives at the level of the filling. Thats the idea I want the poem to carry.

So: write me a sestina. Heres the gimmick. The six end-words should be the actual parts of the letter: bowl, stem, counter, arc, spur, terminal. Those six words are the roles, so they have to come back in every stanza but in the shifting positions the form forces on you, the same skeleton showing up in different flesh each time, everything around them changing. The form should be doing the theory, not just decorating it. Somewhere in there, probably the envoi, I want the single-storey vs two-storey thing to land, that we recognize both as 'a' even though one has a closed loop and one doesnt. And I want the poem to feel the weirdness of it, that we hold a category together with no definition underneath it and it just works.

Make it a real poem though, not a lecture with line breaks. If someone who knows nothing about typography reads it they should still get something out of it, and if someone who does shouldnt wince. American English please.
