Here's where I've landed on animals: (1) I'm a physicalist — no immaterial minds, ours included; (2) Descartes was basically right about animals: they don't think, they don't reason, and the yelping is all machinery; (3) I think the two tests he gives in the Discourse (no genuine language, no flexible response to novelty) are weak as tests, but the underlying move is strong — if a mechanism explains the whole of the behaviour, you've got no evidence of a mind in there; (4) I think a dog whose paw you stand on is in pain and it matters morally, and I think anyone who reads Descartes as leaving room for animals to have some kind of sensation is handing him an escape hatch he didn't want; (5) I think consciousness just is a certain kind of information processing, so anything doing that processing is conscious, no extra ingredient required; (6) but I also think the only reason I believe other people are conscious is that they're built out of the same stuff I am — build a silicon dog that behaves exactly like a dog and I'd say there's no reason at all to think it feels anything.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix in each case? Rank the tensions by how bad they are and tell me which single revision cleans up the most, and be specific about which one has to go.
