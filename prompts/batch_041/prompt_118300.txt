I have spent some time trying to find the right concept for a pattern of social punishment, and I have not found one that fits, so I want to describe the pattern precisely and ask what the nearest existing concepts are, and what this one should be called.

The pattern has five features that I cannot get any single concept to cover at once.

First, the norm that was violated is never actually produced. People who impose the sanction say it is obvious what was wrong, but no one can state the rule, say who holds it, or say when it was adopted; if pressed, they point back to the original act.

Second, no one imposed it. There is no body, office, vote, or procedure that can be identified as the source of the penalty, and therefore nothing to appeal to, no one to petition, and no one with the standing to lift it. It is not that the process was unfair; there is no process, and no defendant either.

Third, the sanction is not a harm added to a person but an association withdrawn from them. Invitations stop, a platform removes an account, a publisher declines to renew, colleagues stop answering. Its severity tracks how many people happened to see the original act rather than how much harm the act did, and it does not come in degrees: a person is either still inside the relevant community or outside it, with nothing resembling a sentence that could be served and completed.

Fourth, it does not end when anything is repaired. It ends when attention moves on, and because the record of the act is permanent and searchable, it can be revived years later without new conduct. The one thing demanded in exchange for readmission is a public statement of contrition, whose sincerity cannot be verified, so a statement that is too quick, too late, too polished, or too flat becomes a fresh offence.

Fifth, the people enforcing it are mostly strangers to the person being punished. It is not a community applying its own norms to its own members.

Two instances, to fix the shape. A speaker makes an offhand remark to a room of thirty people; someone films it; within a week two venues cancel, a platform suspends the account, and the person's employer announces that they have agreed to part ways. No charge is ever stated. In the second case, a mid-level manager who has been casually cruel to subordinates for years is finally named in a public thread after the internal process does nothing; within a month the manager is gone and cannot be hired. Here the norm was statable and the harm documented, and I think the outcome was on the whole good, which is exactly why I want the structural concept and not a verdict on whether the practice is good or bad.

I know the vernacular labels — 'cancelling', 'call-out', 'deplatforming' — and I do not think they are concepts; they are names for the controversy rather than for the structure. I also suspect the nearest neighbours are ostracism, outlawry, excommunication, vigilantism, and the general category of informal social sanction, but each of these seems to me to have a feature this lacks, and I want that checked rather than assumed. A bare name will not help me. What I need is the carving: whether there is an established concept that covers the pattern, which one fits best, exactly where each near neighbour diverges, and if nothing fits, a proposed term with a definition, its essential and accidental features, and its edges. In particular I want rulings on these cases: a consumer boycott of a company over a policy; a private decision not to associate with someone; a market refusing to deal with a person for purely commercial reasons; a formal disciplinary process that is merely harsh; and the state achieving the same effect through licensing or employment law. I also want to know whether the absence of a procedure is essential to the phenomenon or incidental, and whether the demand for sincere public contrition belongs to the same structure or is a separate one.

After your prose answer, append a JSON object with exactly these fields: "established_concept" (one of "yes", "partial", "no"); "best_candidate" (the name of the closest existing concept, or null); "candidate_concepts" (an array of objects, each with "name", "why_it_is_close", and "why_it_falls_short"); "coined_term" (a term if none of the existing concepts fit, otherwise null); "definition" (one or two sentences defining the phenomenon as you have carved it); "essential_features" (array of strings); "accidental_features" (array of strings); "boundary_cases" (an array of objects, each with "case", "verdict" — one of "instance", "not an instance", "unclear" — and "reason"); and "open_questions" (array of strings).
