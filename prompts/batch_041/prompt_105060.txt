I've been going down a rabbit hole about whether the stuff around us can be political on its own — not just how people use a thing, but the thing itself. You know the famous example, the bridges built too low on purpose so certain people couldn't get through. And then the other version, where some big centralized technology supposedly forces a certain kind of society whether anyone wants it or not. I get both, but they both feel like they smuggle the politics in through the front door — either somebody meant it, or the thing "needs" a certain society. Neither one scratches the itch for me.

What I want is a thought experiment that isolates the third thing, if there even is one: a case where some ordinary, boring artifact is political even though nobody designed it to be and it doesnt require any particular social setup — it's political some other way, and I want to see exactly which way. The hunch I keep circling is that it's about whats easy versus what's hard, like how a form makes you opt in vs opt out, or a door you pull vs a door you push — the artifact quietly makes one way of living the path of least resistance. But I could be wrong, so dont just confirm my hunch.

So please build me the case. Rules: it has to be something that actually exists or obviously could exist — a chair, a form, a lock, a schedule, a payment thing — no sci-fi, no magic, nothing that only works because of some future invention. One paragraph, plain language, ordinary setting. And hold everything else fixed — same designer, same users, same culture, same laws — so the only thing that changes is the artifact's design, and that change alone flips something political.

Then walk me through it:

- What each of the three views says about the case — the "it's just a tool, only use matters" view, the "politics by design" view, and the "it forces a society" view.
- Which specific feature of the artifact is doing the work, in one sentence.
- What happens if you tweak that feature or take it out — does the politics evaporate, or does it just move somewhere else?
- The strongest way someone who thinks artifacts are neutral would push back on the whole case, and whether that pushback survives.

And be honest if the case can't actually seperate the three views — if the politics keeps collapsing back into one of the first two, say so and tell me why, rather than fudging it.
