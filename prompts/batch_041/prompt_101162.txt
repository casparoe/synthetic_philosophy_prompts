Write a satirical in-universe document: the annual report of the Paradigm Transition Office at a fictional research university, addressed to a dean who has decided that the university must produce a paradigm shift within the current strategic-planning cycle. Treat the Office as existing in a world where paradigm incommensurability is an acknowledged fact of institutional life, and write the report deadpan and institutionally fluent: mission statement, scoring rubrics, pilot programs, key performance indicators, risk assessment, and a recommendation. It should never wink at the reader. Its comedy must arise from the collision between project-management categories and the actual structure of scientific change as described in the Kuhnian tradition: normal science as puzzle-solving within a paradigm; anomalies that resist assimilation; crisis and the proliferation of rival frameworks; revolution as a change in worldview rather than a straightforward inference; theory-laden observation; meaning change for central terms; and incommensurability that blocks any neutral, common measure for choosing between paradigms. The report should repeatedly try to operationalize these ideas and repeatedly fail in instructive ways: it should propose an incommensurability index, a conversion-velocity metric, a translation unit that reports high failure rates, a rubric for detecting pre-paradigmatic departments, and a plan to induce productive crises in underperforming units. The bureaucrats should not be stupid; they should be competent, literal-minded, and genuinely trying to manage something that resists management. The satire should cut both ways: it should mock the managerial capture of paradigm talk and also gently mock the temptation to use incommensurability as a shield against external criticism. The piece should be legible to a reader who has never read Kuhn and funny to someone who has. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or quotations from real texts. Do not name real living people; the dean, the Office, and its staff are invented. If you need historical examples, invent a fictional subfield and two rival paradigms rather than relying on real scientific episodes. About 1000 words.
