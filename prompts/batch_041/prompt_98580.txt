i'm a software engineer with zero philosophy training, but i keep reading about this decision theory idea called risk-weighted expected utility and i want a poem out of it. the way i understand it: you dont just multiply each outcome's value by its probability and add up. first you sort the outcomes from worst to best, and the weight you give the jump up to a better outcome isnt that outcome's own probability but a function of the chance of getting it or anything better -- so for a risk-averse chooser the bad outcomes end up carrying more weight than their real odds, and the worst case punches way above its probability.

so: a free-verse poem, maybe 20-30 lines, built around four outcomes with stated odds -- say 5%, 15%, 30%, 50% from worst to best -- except dont hand out lines in proportion to the odds. let the 5% worst case hog the poem and shortchange the 50%. the shape of the thing should make you feel the mismatch between the odds and the room the bad outcome takes up, and the turn should be the moment the speaker notices the odds never moved, only the weighting did. please dont let it drift into a poem about being careful or about anxiety being irrational; its about the shape of the weighting, not pessimism. someone who actually knows this theory should be able to point at it and say yeah, thats exactly it.
