ok so I'm in a small epistemology reading group and we keep banging into the dogmatism paradox and I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement is actually going on, not another proposed solution.

Rough shape of it, in case its useful: I know P. P is true. So anything that looks like evidence for not-P is misleading evidence. And you're allowed to ignore misleading evidence. So it seems like if I know P I'm entitled to dismiss, sight unseen, whatever shows up suggesting not-P — the photos, the texts, the whole envelope, without opening it. The scientist who knows the result is solid doesn't have to run the replication. Which is obviously the exact behaviour we call dogmatic and vicious. So either knowledge isn't what we thought or the argument has a hole somewhere.

The group has the usual shelf of fixes and none of them stick: that closure of justification under known entailment is the culprit; that there's a scope or tense thing where you may keep believing P but may not refuse to consider the new stuff; that you can't know in advance which bit is the misleading bit so the licence is empty; that it only works if you conflate having a defeater with there being one; that knowledge just isn't the sort of state that comes pre-armoured against future evidence; that it's a pseudo-problem from an equivocation on 'justified in ignoring'. And every fix gets the same comeback — fine, but you've just relocated it, or redefined your way out of it. A year now, no movement.

My hypothesis, and I'd like it attacked rather than agreed with: this isn't a disagreement about knowledge, it's a disagreement about what justification is for. One side treats it as a state that inherits the truth of what you know; the other treats it as a practice of responding to reasons as they arrive, and on that reading the paradox can't even get off the ground. If so, the two camps aren't giving rival answers to one question — they're answering different questions under one word. (There's a legal cousin of this: finality of judgment, res judicata, where we happily say the case is shut and no more evidence counts, and we don't call that vicious, we call it necessary. Maybe the real question is when closing the case is legitimate. Not sure if that helps or just changes the subject.)

What I want: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Verbal — different senses of 'justified', 'ignore', 'defeat'? Substantive but about two different questions wearing one name? A hidden formal question, like a scope ambiguity or a closure failure that settles it once stated right? Or bedrock — one side just can't feel the pull of the revisable-knowledge intuition and the other can't feel the closed-case intuition, and neither can argue for it?

And then, given the diagnosis: what would count as progress here? A distinction, a countermodel, a formal result, a decision to stop asking the question in that form? Or should we expect it to run forever, and why? And tell me straight whether my justification-as-state-versus-practice line is the real fault line or whether I've just invented a distinction to make the stalemate look respectable.
