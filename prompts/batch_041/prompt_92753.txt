I am a software engineer, and I spend a good part of my week staring at experiment dashboards, so Simpson's paradox reaches me less as a philosophical curiosity than as an argument I keep having with a product manager. I would like you to write a joke about it.

The joke has to turn on a distinction that most retellings of the paradox blur, and that I want the joke to respect. A reversal in the aggregate can come from a lurking variable, and in that case the disaggregated comparison is usually the one to trust. But a reversal can also appear in a ratio measure such as an odds ratio even when the treatment was assigned by a perfect coin flip and nothing is confounding anything, simply because that measure does not average up the way a difference does; in that second case neither the pooled number nor the stratum-by-stratum numbers is straightforwardly the truth. A joke that treats every reversal as a confounding story, or that takes always disaggregate as the moral, is precisely the joke I do not want.

Please also make the joke work for the neighbouring case in which two people analyse the same data with the same covariate and both are defensible, one comparing final values and one comparing change, so that the disagreement is not a blunder on either side but a difference in the question being asked. The punchline should trade on that structure, not on the idea that statisticians are indecisive or that numbers lie.

Invent the setting rather than borrowing one, and please stay away from the kidney-stone and admissions-office examples; everyone has heard them, and they would make the joke read like a recap of a lecture. A short exchange between two people is fine, but the humour should come from the shape of the paradox rather than from jargon or from a character being stupid. If the joke only lands on a contested reading of the paradox, add one sentence afterward saying which reading it assumes.
