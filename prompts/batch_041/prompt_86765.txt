Ok so I just spent like three hours argueing with my friend about Confucian role ethics and I think she won me over. The view I've landed on: there's no self that exists before its relations — you are your roles (mother, brother, neighbour, teacher, citizen), and the basic unit of ethics isn't the act of an autonomous individual but how well those role-relations go. Obligations come from the roles, not from a universal law and not from a contract.

What I want from you is what would count against this. Not the lazy "well that's not Kant" stuff — the real pressure points, ranked by how much they should actually move someone who holds it, and what the view predicts that its rivals don't. Three specific things she threw at me that I couldnt answer: (1) roles are handed to you by other people, often by institutions that are unjust — the family scapegoat, the kid expected to give up everything to care for a parent. Does a role generate real obligations just by existing? And if the view needs some outside standard for which roles count, hasnt it given up the whole "roles come first" claim? (2) If the self just is its roles, what happens when the roles dissolve — divorce, retirement, getting cut off, outliving everyone who had a role for you? Is the view committed to that being a kind of death, and is that a feature or a reductio? (3) Does it have anything to say about people who occupy no role in my life at all — a stranger on the other side of the planet, someone 300 years from now, an animal? If not, is that fatal or just a boundary the view can live with?

Also tell me what the view's own defenders would concede counts against it, not just what critics say. And be honest about whether it's even a distinct position or whether it quietly collapses into virtue ethics or particularism once you say what makes a role-relation a good one. If it turns out to be insulated — every counterexample met with "thats not a genuine role" — say so, and say whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me which one you'd find most persuasive if you were me.
