I am currently enrolled in an epistemology course, and for my final paper I want to read Christopher Nolan's Memento through the lottery and preface paradoxes. My thesis is that Leonard is a living preface paradox: each tattoo, note, and Polaroid is a chapter he rationally accepts, while his condition forces him to hold the preface belief that the whole system contains at least one error; the film's reverse chronology makes us experience the paradox from the inside, since every new piece of evidence seems justified before we see how it fits into a corrupted whole. I would like a reading that takes both the film and the paradoxes seriously: where does the lottery paradox illuminate his reasoning—each note is highly likely to be accurate, yet he cannot rationally accept the conjunction of all of them—and where does the preface paradox better capture his situation? Where does the film resist both analogies, especially in the final scene, where he chooses to believe a lie rather than merely tolerate an inconsistent set of beliefs? Does the film vindicate the preface paradox as a model of rational belief, or does it show that Leonard's condition is a pathology of acceptance that the philosophical discussion never considers? I do not need citations or a reading list; I need the reading itself.
