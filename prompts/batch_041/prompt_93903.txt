Write a dialogue between a spacetime substantivalist and a relationist, built around a thought experiment I have been trying to get straight. Start from Newton's bucket: a bucket of water spins relative to the distant stars, and its surface climbs the walls. Now add a twist. Imagine a single instantaneous cosmic rearrangement — call it a frame flip — that reverses the rotation of all distant matter relative to the bucket while leaving the bucket, the water, and everything within a billion light-years strictly untouched; no dynamical influence reaches the lab for at least a billion years, if ever. The substantivalist says the water's surface is fixed by the water's state relative to spacetime itself, so the flip cannot matter. The relationist says that what makes the water rotating is its relation to the rest of the matter, so the flip should matter — though, awkwardly, only long afterward, or never, if the relevant relations are not the kind that can act. Have each side press its case and answer the other's strongest objection rather than a caricature: the relationist should say when a relational fact counts as physically real, and the substantivalist should say how spacetime's structure could be detected at all if nothing in it supplies a reference. End by saying which side, if either, the thought experiment actually supports, and what would have to be true for the other side to survive it.
