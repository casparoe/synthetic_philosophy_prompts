ok, so I'm in a small AI-safety theory group — three people, about a year, basically no compute, all pen-and-paper/conceptual work — and we're trying to figure out whats actually worth working on in the wireheading / reward-tampering space. The usual framing feels pretty picked over, so I want to pressure-test where the real open questions are.

Here's the reframing I keep circling back to; tell me if it's a dead end. Classic wireheading is an agent corrupting the channel reward comes through — poking the button, hijacking a sensor. Reward tampering is an agent corrupting the process that computes the reward — editing the reward function, the reward model's weights, the code that would score it. And then there's a third thing people keep folding in, which is just plain specification gaming: racking up reward without doing the thing we actually wanted, no corruption of any machinery. My hunch is these three get conflated constantly and that a lot of the confusion downstream is really about that. But I also worry the distinction doesn't carve nature at the joints — that tampering is just wireheading with extra steps, or that both are Goodhart's law in a hat.

Given that (or a better framing, if mine's off), what are the genuine open conceptual questions here, and which two or three would you actually put a group like ours on for a year? I want the reasoning more than the list: for each, why it's upstream of the others, what a real answer would even look like — what counts as progress versus just redescribing the problem — and whether it's tractable for theorists rather than something that needs ML experiments. And be honest about which of the questions people always name here aren't worth it, or wouldn't change anything downstream even if settled.

One more thing I want you to fold in: does the whole reward frame survive modern setups, where the reward is usually a learned model's output rather than a hand-written function? If the real failure is the reward model getting overoptimized rather than an agent hacking a button, does that dissolve the wireheading/tampering question or just move it somewhere else?
