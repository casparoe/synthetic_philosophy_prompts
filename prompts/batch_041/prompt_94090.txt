The Cartesian theater is the picture of the mind as a place where experiences all come together and are witnessed by a single inner viewer; the multiple drafts model is the rival picture on which there is no such place, no single finish line, and no fact of the matter about exactly when an experience becomes conscious — only ongoing, parallel, revisable drafts out of which a self gets narrated. I would like the most important implications of the multiple drafts model for how I should treat my own memory and my own blameworthiness. I am a software engineer rather than a philosopher, and the analogy that makes the model vivid to me is a distributed system with no global clock and no single source of truth, whose apparently consistent state is really eventual consistency across replicated, sometimes conflicting logs. Please take that analogy seriously enough to say where it holds and where it misleads, and let the consequences for retrospective self-blame, for how much I should trust introspective reports, and for the supposed timing of conscious events be the center of your answer rather than an afterthought.
