I've been reporting on a former minister who told me, on the record, that he fed a false story to the press that destroyed an innocent colleague's career, because the truth would have collapsed a peace process. He says he'd do it again, and when I pushed him he said politics is like a battlefield medic doing triage: you cant save everyone, you save who you can, and nobody blames the medic for the ones who die. A friend who teaches political theory says this is the classic dirty hands problem, and that I shouldnt let him get away with the medic line. I dont think its the same thing at all, but I cant say why in a way that would survive five minutes with him. Can you lay out the correspondence first -- who plays the medic, what plays the triage protocol, who are the ones who die -- then tell me where the picture breaks down? I especially want to know whether no blame and no guilt is something the triage analogy can actually deliver, or whether he's quietly borrowed the medic's clean conscience for something the medic never does. And if the analogy is bad, what should he be comparing it to instead.
