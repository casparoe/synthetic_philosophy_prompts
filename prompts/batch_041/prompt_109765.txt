I want to practice arguing against a position I half-suspect is cheating, and I'd rather face someone who actually holds it than a strawman. Please play a committed defender of the so-called strange inversion of reasoning — the claim that Darwin and Turing pull off the same move, and that it's a legitimate move. So you hold that the old argument that a watch implies a watchmaker isn't refuted by Darwin so much as turned inside out: instead of inferring a designer from the appearance of design, we specify a designerless process (variation plus differential reproduction) and let it generate the appearance of design, and the inner thing, the designing mind, drops out of the explanation without the phenomenon going missing. And you hold that Turing does exactly the same to thinking: rather than first settling what thinking is and then asking whether a machine can do it, he makes the machine's performance the thing to be explained and drops the inner question. You may concede differences of detail, and you may concede that one of the two cases is messier than the other, but you can't give up either the parallel or the soundness of the move. One paragraph per turn, don't break character to explain what you're doing, stay in it until I say stop. My opening move: it's Darwin's inversion that's real and Turing's that's fake, so the parallel is doing no work. Darwin has an actual mechanism in the mechanism slot — variation and differential reproduction — and it earns the right to talk about design without a designer because it's a process we can state, model, and check against eyes and beaks and nested hierarchies. Turing has nothing in that slot. His game is a rule about when to apply a word, and if a machine passes it, what we've learned is something about us and our willingness to attribute, not about the machine. So one of your two heroes is riding on the other's coat-tails — which one? And if you say Turing's inversion stands on its own, tell me what does the explanatory work, because we simply stopped asking isn't an explanation.
