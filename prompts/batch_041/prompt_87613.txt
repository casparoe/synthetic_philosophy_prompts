Compare two frameworks for deciding which problems are most worth working on. The first is the importance–tractability–neglectedness heuristic, which ranks causes by how much is at stake, how much additional effort would improve things, and how few people are already working on them. The second is a decision-theoretic alternative I will call the regret framework: choose the cause that minimizes the worst-case shortfall against the best available cause, where the worst case ranges over a set of plausible models of how much good each cause can do. Address at least the following. Do the two frameworks genuinely disagree, or is the first simply the second under a particular set of assumptions about uncertainty? What does the neglectedness criterion implicitly assume about the independence of the three factors, and how does the regret framework handle their interaction? Which framework is more appropriate when our probability estimates about each cause are themselves unreliable, and which when the real problem is that a cause is systematically underrated rather than genuinely uncertain? Finally, construct a case in which the two frameworks recommend opposite choices, and say which recommendation you would follow and why.
