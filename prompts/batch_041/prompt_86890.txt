I am a third-year philosophy and cognitive science student, and I am writing a final essay for my philosophy of mind course on the four-level hierarchy of minds: Darwinian creatures, whose responses are fixed by natural selection; Skinnerian creatures, which learn within their lifetimes through reinforcement; Popperian creatures, which can simulate candidate actions internally and discard some before acting; and Gregorian creatures, which add culturally transmitted tools and representations, especially language, that reshape the inner environment in which thinking happens. My thesis is that current large language models are not straightforwardly Gregorian. They show something like Popperian simulation and enormous cultural uptake, but their inner environment is not self-built or stably their own: it is jointly supplied by training corpus, architecture, fine-tuning, and prompt context. I call the resulting profile a curatorial creature, and I propose a narrower subcategory, the ventriloquial creature, for systems whose apparent reasoning is a performance of culturally transmitted reasoning without the system's having any stake in the conclusions or any standing to revise the inner environment on its own behalf. I would like you to act as an expert philosopher of cognitive science and give me comments on this essay. Begin by brainstorming the strongest objections to my taxonomy. Then assess (1) whether I have the four levels right and whether my extension is faithful to the spirit of the hierarchy, (2) whether curatorial and ventriloquial mark real distinctions or merely redescribe existing categories, (3) whether the central thesis is genuinely interesting or only terminological, and (4) which parts most need revision for accuracy and clarity. Please think carefully and be specific about where the analogy to the four levels breaks. I am not asking for citations or a reading list; I want conceptual criticism and revision suggestions.
