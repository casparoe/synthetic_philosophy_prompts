I chair our county fatality review panel, which meets Friday to vote on a finding in the death of a seventeen-year-old held in juvenile detention who died of an asthma attack. Our statute requires us to record whether a death was caused by an act or omission of a county agency, and permits us to name individual employees whose conduct we find to be a cause; in practice the panel has drifted into recording whether a death was preventable, and last year we split badly over a draft finding that blamed the county board for failing to fund a program. The facts here: the boy complained of trouble breathing twice in the two hours before he collapsed; the shift supervisor had a rescue inhaler in her bag and told investigators she considered giving it but did not, because the medical policy is silent and she had been told never to dispense medication; 911 was called only after he collapsed. The medical examiner concludes he would probably have survived had he been given the inhaler or had 911 been called at the first complaint; the county's retained expert says the evidence is too thin to support that. No policy required either act of her. One member proposes we adopt what she calls a duty-baseline test — an omission counts as the cause only if the agency owed a duty, the conduct fell below it, and but for the required act the death would probably not have occurred — while another argues the duty element does all the work and the causal element is decorative, so that our findings are blame with a causal label, and he would enter no causation finding here. Write the opinion I will circulate before the vote: which test we should use for omissions, what finding to enter in this case and in exactly what words, and whether the report should name the supervisor. Engage the strongest point on the side you reject. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested position as the consensus. Lead with your recommendation in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble.
