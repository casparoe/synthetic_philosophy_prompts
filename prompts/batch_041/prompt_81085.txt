I'm the secretary of the Caxton Road Social Club — members' club, founded 1904, we own the building and the strip of land behind it that's a car park and a dead pond. I need you to draft the note I put in the papers for our special general meeting on the 14th. I'm the one who has to rule on whether the main motion is even admissable, and I'm definately not sure I know what I'm doing, so I want the reasoning written out properly before I stand up in front of ninety people.

The mess: our rules (about sixty of them, ring binder, last revised 1998) let any rule be changed at an SGM by two-thirds of those present and voting. Rule 41 then says the club must at all times remain a members' club and the back land can't be sold or mortgaged, and adds that Rule 41 itself can't be altered, repealed or suspended. And Rule 58 says the committee interprets the rules and its interpretation is final.

Deakin — property developer, member for years till he let his subs lapse — has 40-odd signatures on a motion that first deletes the bit of Rule 41 that stops Rule 41 being changed, and then, with that gone, lets the committee sell the back land to a housing developer. He reckons he's got well over two-thirds of the people who'll actually turn up. The committee's split 4-3 and the chair has told me the admissibility call is mine. If I rule it out he'll say the committee can't overrule the members, and if the members can overrule the committee then they can change Rule 58 as well — except that changing Rule 58 is itself a rule change, so who decides that one? Someone said it's like the Escher picture where each hand is drawing the other one, and now half the club's calling it the Escher problem, which is no help at all.

It's personal for me: if it goes through there's a new committee in October and I'm off it, and my dad's name is on the honours board in the bar. So I don't want a note that's just me keeping my seat. I want the argument to be right, and Deakin's best case set out fairly in it, so people can see I'm not simply blocking him.

So: the note. What I should recommend, what I should actually do and say on the night (SGM is Thursday the 14th, 7.30, back room), what to do if he pushes it anyway, and whether I should be the one ruling on it at all given the above. Plain reasoning only, please — no case law, nothing I'd have to look up. We had one solicitor's letter and it cost £400 to say it depends.
