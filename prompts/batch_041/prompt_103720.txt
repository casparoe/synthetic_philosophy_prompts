I'm a backend engineer at a mid-size company and I somehow ended up owning our people-analytics service -- a pile of ETL jobs that tag every employee with attributes for the HR dashboard. One of the tags is "generation" (Boomer, Gen X, Millennial, Gen Z), derived from birth year using a lookup table some vendor gave us years ago and nobody has touched since. Marketing has read access and segments customers off the same tag, and our L&D team runs a "managing a multigenerational workforce" module that the tag feeds directly.

Here's what's bugging me: nobody can tell me what the tag actually is. We have other cohort tags too -- people who joined in the same year, people who onboarded during the pandemic -- and everyone agrees those are just bookkeeping, filters you use to slice a chart. But the generation tag gets treated like a real property of the person, more like blood type than like a label we made up. A manager will say "Millennials need more feedback" the same way she'd say "this service is owned by the payments team." When I pointed out that if you shift the cutoffs by two years, half the labels change and literally nothing else in the data moves, the L&D lead said the boundaries are approximate but the generations themselves are real.

Meanwhile legal makes us strip age and birth year from anything touching hiring or promotion, so the tag never shows up there -- which is strange if the tag is basically a proxy for age anyway.

So my question is what this practice is assuming. Is "generation" being treated as a natural kind, the way "species" or "element" is, and if so what would actually have to be true for that to hold? Or is it a useful fiction, or a reference class, or a social construction -- I get the sense those are three different things and I want to know which one we're actually running. Which of those readings does our setup rule out and which does it merely fit? And is the whole thing internally consistent -- real enough to build a training module on, too radioactive to make a hiring call with? Tell me what would have to change if we dropped the "real" reading and treated it as a pure reporting convenience, and also which parts of what we do would be fine either way. I don't need cites, just tell me what we've definately committed ourselves to.
