Anyway I rewatched Amadeus (the 1984 Miloš Forman one, the one that won all the Oscars) for like the fourth time last month, and it landed differently this time, because I'd been reading about two ideas that seem to pull in opposite directions. On one side there's the American Dream, where the whole point is that if you're the best at something you should rise and be celebrated for it. On the other side there's Jantelagen — that Scandinavian thing, the "law of Jante," and the cousin of it people call tall poppy syndrome — where the ethic is basically that you're not supposed to think you're anybody special, and anyone who sticks up above the crowd gets cut back down to size. What got me is that the movie seems to run on the second ethic while acting like it admires the first. Mozart is the tall poppy, Salieri is the one holding the shears, and yet the film hands Salieri the narration, so we spend the whole thing inside the head of the guy doing the cutting. I don't want a plot summary or a list of themes. I want an actual reading.
