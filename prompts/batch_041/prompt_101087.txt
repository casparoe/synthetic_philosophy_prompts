ok so this keeps coming up in my jurisprudence reading group and i genuinely cant tell anymore if we're going in circles or onto something. here's the setup, keep it as abstract as you like: take any rule-system — a legal order, a game, a club constitution — that contains a rule telling you how to change the rules (call it the amendment rule), and suppose the amendment rule itself can be changed, using the very procedure it lays down. Toy version, two rules: R1 says any rule can be changed by a majority vote; R2 says no rule may be changed except by unanimous consent. A majority then votes to strike R2. Did it work? Did R1 authorize it? Now the nasty bit: suppose they then use the new majority-only regime to rewrite R1 itself. The authority of that second change traces back through a rule that only had authority because of a change that... you see where this is going.

Here's what bugs me. Everyone in the group agrees on the toy cases, agrees what a majority is, agrees on all the facts, and yet we've been stuck for months. One camp says there's a real self-reference paradox here, a cousin of the liar, and that a system can't coherently ground its own authority to change itself. Another camp says there's no paradox at all — that validity just bottoms out somewhere (in whatever people accept, in the originally stipulated rule, in some basic norm that isn't itself valid but is presupposed), so self-amendment is just ordinary rule-change with the meta-rule assumed rather than derived, no different from any other derivation that has to start from axioms. A third person in the group keeps insisting it's a verbal fight about the word "valid" — legal validity vs. logical derivability — and that the two camps are answering different questions under one word. I half think the third person is right, but I can't make it sharp enough to actually kill the arguement, and the other two think he's dodging.

What I want from you: a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, engaging what each side is really claiming (not a survey of legal theory — the actual moves, the ones that would matter), and then an honest answer about what, if anything, would count as progress. Would a distinction do it (say, between the rule being changed and its being validly changed, or between object-level and meta-level talk)? A formal result? Or is the question just ill-posed and we should stop? And tell me which of the three is closest to right and why — or that none of them is, and the real fault line is somewhere else. Also, briefly: does any of this change if R2 is written as flatly unamendable, or if the amendment rule claims it can only ever be changed by the procedure it currently specifies? Those two wrinkles are where our group usually starts shouting.
