I own a small chain of three coffee shops, and in a few weeks I will be away for six months, leaving a newly promoted general manager in charge. My instinct is to hand her a set of hard targets — hold waste under 4% of inventory at each store, lift same-store sales by 5%, keep the labor-cost ratio below 28% — because numerical thresholds of that kind are how I have always judged my own performance, and I want something objective to hold her to while I am gone. My business partner argues that any such numbers will simply be gamed: staff will stop comping drinks for regulars, shrink portions, let the pastry case run bare, and otherwise hit the metrics in ways that hollow out the very thing that makes the shops worth visiting. His alternative is to tell her, in effect, to run the shops the way she believes I would run them, and to trust her judgment in cases the numbers do not anticipate.

I am torn and would like your help thinking it through. Part of me suspects my partner is right about the numbers, but "do what you think I would want" sounds like a recipe for drift, inconsistency, and a manager who optimizes for pleasing me rather than for the shops themselves — and if she is skilled at predicting what I would approve of, is that not simply a subtler thing to game? On the other hand, I can see that a manager satisfying a fixed target I set is, in a sense, doing exactly what I asked even when the results disgust me, which strikes me as the worse failure of the two.

Please take a definite position: which mode of instruction should I give her, and why? I would rather have a firm recommendation with its reasoning than a survey of considerations, and I would like you to be candid about the strongest objection to the position you land on and to explain why you think it does not overturn it.
