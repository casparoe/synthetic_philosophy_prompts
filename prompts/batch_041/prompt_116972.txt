My sister is getting married next month, and I'm the maid of honor. She and her fiancé are both philosophy grad students, and they met in a seminar on the Euthyphro, so the toast is basically expected to do something with that. The problem is the room: half of it is their department, people who will notice if I dumb it down or get it wrong, and the other half is our very Catholic extended family plus his secular Jewish family, who have never heard of Euthyphro and will just want to cry and laugh. I'm definately not a philosopher, and I need a four-to-five-minute toast that is warm and funny and not a lecture, but where the Euthyphro actually shapes the whole thing rather than being a name-drop.

Can you write it? Please use the less standard version of the dilemma, not the one everyone knows about whether actions are good because God commands them or commanded because they're good. I want the version about God's own goodness: is God good by nature, in a way that's prior to God's will, or does God's will set the standard for what good even means when we say God is good? And if it's the second, can divine command theory even say God is good without going in a circle? I don't want the toast to resolve that. I want it to use the fact that it doesn't resolve to say something real about the couple, about promising to love someone without having a foundation that makes the promise guaranteed, and about how a shared life can be good without either person being the one who decides what good is. The philosophers should feel the argument is being treated honestly; everyone else should still be able to follow the emotional thread. No citations, no reading lists, no quotes from Plato or anybody else. Just the speech, and maybe a one-line note at the end about which part you think will land hardest with which half of the room.
