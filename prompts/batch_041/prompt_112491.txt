Our 140-member worker cooperative, which runs a bakery, a café, and a wholesale bread operation, is rewriting its community standards after a difficult year, and I need to circulate a written opinion before the board vote in three weeks. Attendance at general meetings has fallen below half; shift lateness and skipped task rotations have risen; two members left saying the place had become too rule-bound, while three others say it has become too permissive to function. The immediate triggers were a dispute over whether to sanction a member who missed three task rotations because of a childcare emergency, and a complaint about a political poster in the break room. A consultant has proposed a two-track rulebook. In what she calls coordination zones — scheduling, food safety, cash handling, task rotation, and meeting quorum — rules would be explicit, sanctions automatic, and exceptions rare. In what she calls expressive zones — speech, dress, political expression, food, and the decoration of shared spaces — rules would be minimal and enforced only against bad faith or persistent disregard. The board votes in three weeks. Please draft the written opinion I will circulate before that meeting: recommend whether to adopt the two-track rulebook, reject it, or adopt it with specific amendments; state the concrete implementation steps, who is responsible for enforcement, and a review date no later than twelve months after adoption; and give the reasoning the board can actually act on. The recommendation should be clear enough to vote on. The memo should engage seriously the strongest objection from each of the two opposing camps — one that wants a uniformly strict culture and one that wants a uniformly loose one — rather than dismissing either. Assume the board knows our history but not the academic literature on cultural tightness and looseness; the memo must stand on its own.
