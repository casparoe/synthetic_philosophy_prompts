For several years I have been collecting the verdicts of the instructor of a seminar I once sat in on, on questions of scientific priority, and I have reached the point where I can no longer tell what rule he is running. He credits Lavoisier with the discovery of oxygen and gives Priestley and Scheele nothing, on the ground that producing a gas is not the same as knowing what you have. He credits Chadwick with the neutron, and treats the earlier detection of the relevant radiation by Bothe and Becker and by the Joliot-Curies as background rather than discovery. He credits Mendel with the laws of inheritance rather than the men who reached them independently in 1900, and insists that the decades in which nobody read Mendel's paper are beside the point. And he awards the calculus to Newton and Leibniz jointly, calling the ensuing quarrel a disgrace, which I cannot tell whether to read as generosity toward a genuine multiple or as a rule that credit is shared whenever two people arrive independently. In every one of these cases the person he credits was both the first to arrive at the result and the first to make it public, so I cannot tell whether his rule is about who conceived the result, who published it, who demonstrated it, or who shares it.

Here is a case his record does not cover. By the usual telling, Gordon Gould worked out a workable design for the laser in late 1957 and had his notebook notarized, but said nothing publicly until a conference talk in 1959; Townes and Schawlow were the first to publish a design, in 1958; and Maiman built the first working device, in 1960. What would the professor say, and what in his record supports that prediction over the alternatives? I want the rules his earlier verdicts are consistent with, what each predicts about the laser, your best guess with your reasons for preferring it, and the case, real or invented, that would separate whichever rules his laser verdict leaves standing. Finally, he likes to say that when the same thing is found twice it was going to be found anyway, and I want to know whether his readiness to hand out credit to particular individuals can survive that thought or whether his practice and his rhetoric are simply in tension.
