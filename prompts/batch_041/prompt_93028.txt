I am a second-year undergraduate taking a module on practical rationality and the will, and I have been asked to run one 50-minute session for our seminar group of twelve. I would like an activity that makes the group feel the pull of hyperbolic discounting from the inside rather than simply hear the curve described: they should make a pair of real choices within the hour, in which the option they prefer at a distance loses to the smaller, sooner one once it is actually in front of them, with a genuine if small stake (a snack, not imaginary money). Include a way for them to bind themselves in advance, and design the running order so that it separates two things the group will otherwise run together — the participant whose preference genuinely reverses and who feels no conflict, and the participant who still judges the later option better and takes the earlier one anyway. Give me the rules, the timeline and the debrief: where each of those moments maps onto the distinction between time-inconsistent preference and weakness of will proper, whether what they experienced was acting against their own judgement at all, and what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it. End by telling me plainly which feature of weakness of will a compressed classroom version cannot reproduce, so that I know what I must supply myself.
