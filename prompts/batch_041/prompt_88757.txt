I'm running a session on consequentialist cluelessness and I want a dialogue, not an essay. Both speakers are card-carrying consequentialists -- no deontologist on a hobby horse, and no strawmen: they should answer each other's actual points rather than trade monologues. Call them A and B.

B thinks cluelessness sinks the whole thing: trace the causal chain out far enough and it runs into chaos, so any expected-value calculation past the short term is fiction dressed up as arithmetic. A agrees you can't trace the chain but says you dont have to -- all you need is the sign of the difference in expected value between two options, and there are cases where the sign is robust even when the magnitudes are anyone's guess. Make sure A's answer is a real one, a mechanism plus a case where he'd claim it works, not just "we do our best".

Have them distinguish causal cluelessness -- you can't trace the path from act to outcome -- from evaluative cluelessness -- even with the path in hand you couldn't rank the outcomes, because the values at the far end, or the standards for ranking them, may be unrecognisable. Let B argue that the second is the real threat, and let A say whether the sign-of-the-difference reply survives it. B should press with at least one concrete case where the short-run and long-run signs plausibly come apart, and A should either answer it or say why the case is rigged. No citations and no reading list -- they're arguing, not writing a lit review. End with where each of them actually lands, even if that's just "we've moved the bump under the rug".

Start with the dialogue itself, no scene-setting or preamble, and get the crux of their disagreement on the table within the first couple of exchanges.
