I'm definately not a philosopher or anything, just someone who got wierdly obsessed with a puzzle after a late-night argument with my mate. The puzzle is: could you have been born from a different sperm and egg? Not a different version of you in some vague sense — the exact you, same memories, same body, same everything, but the particular sperm and egg that made you were swapped out for another pair from your parents. My mate says obviously not, because you just are that combination, like a fire is that particular set of sparks. I say I'm not so sure: if the resulting person is identical in every way, what exactly is lost? We couldn't settle it, and it's been nagging me for weeks.

So I want you to write me a short story that stages this properly, not an essay. Set it a bit in the future, in a fertility clinic that has a machine — call it a conception replayer, or something better — that can take a person's DNA and show what they would have been if a different sperm had fertilised the egg, or a different egg had been used. The machine can only swap one gamete at a time, so the person it shows shares half your origin; there's a sperm-swap setting, an egg-swap setting, and a very expensive both-swap setting that produces someone who looks exactly like you but is, in origin terms, a total stranger. The story should follow someone who agrees to use it, maybe because they're curious or because they need an origin certificate for inheritance or marriage law. I want the philosophical question to feel urgent and personal: is the person in the machine them, or a stranger? If the machine reveals their original conception was actually a different pair than the one on their birth record, does that change who they are? Please don't have a lecturer explain the debate; let it come out through the characters, the clinic's forms, the legal stuff, the awkwardness. A bit of humour is fine. And don't end with a neat answer — I want the story to leave the reader feeling the pull of both sides.
