Two philosophers have argued past each other for years about individual duties in the face of collective harms. One holds that because your flight, your burger, or your vote is causally negligible — no single act shifts the aggregate, and the harm would occur without it — no individual duty to abstain follows. The other holds that negligibility is beside the point: the duty attaches to knowingly participating in, benefiting from, or being able to join a practice that does the harm.
