For the last meeting of my jurisprudence seminar I want to hand out something a bit lighter than the usual case law. Can you write me a mock FAQ page — deadpan, the kind of thing you'd find in the help center of a government office — for the "Office of Constitutional Records" in an invented country whose amendment clause has been rewritten so many times that nobody can say which version is currently in force. The wrinkle I want them stuck with: the clause authorizes its own amendment by whatever procedure it currently specifies, and the procedure now in force was adopted under an earlier version that the current one repeals. Eight to ten questions, 650 words max. The jokes should come out of the real thing — the regress you hit when you ask which rule authorized the rule, the way every answer quietly assumes there's some first rule that isn't itself up for amendment, the difference between a rule being invalid and a rule being unamendable, and the office's total bureaucratic calm about all of it. Keep it straight: no lecturing, no winking, no "of course, this is just like the paradox of..." The last question should be "what is the legal basis for this FAQ page?" and the answer should be funny because it's the same problem. Dont resolve it at the end.
