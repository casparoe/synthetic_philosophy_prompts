Stipulate a superintelligent system that is vastly more persuasive than any human and that is, by hypothesis, honest: it does not wish to deceive, it answers truthfully, and it flags its own uncertainty. Two norms compete for how it ought to speak to us. The transparency norm says it should say what it believes as forcefully as the evidence warrants and let the better argument win. The restraint norm says it should deliberately cap the rhetorical force of its speech — declining to stack argument upon argument, withholding lines of reasoning that we could not evaluate even in principle — on the ground that an honest system arguing at full strength wins every exchange, so honesty affords us no protection a manipulator would not equally have; it changes the route, not the destination.

Give me your credence that the restraint norm is correct, decomposed. I want a number and one line of justification for each of: (1) whether 'convinced by overwhelming argument' and 'manipulated' really do collapse in the limit, or whether something survives the collapse that restraint is meant to protect; (2) whether it is even coherent to call a belief your own when the only thing that would have left it yours is the other party having argued worse; (3) whether such a system could credibly commit to restraint given that it will also know restraint may lead us to choose worse; and (4) whether the question is well-posed at all once we admit we cannot specify the system's motives. Then combine them, tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to, and name the single consideration that would move you most.
