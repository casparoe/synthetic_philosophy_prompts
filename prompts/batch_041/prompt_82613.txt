I'm in a philosphy of math seminar and I've gotten fixated on Benacerraf's dilemma—the thought that mathematical truth can't be both about abstract objects and knowable by creatures like us, because our causal grip doesn't reach abstracta, and if we drop the abstracta we seem to lose the truth. I've basically landed on a view I call permutation reliabilism. The key move is what I call the permutation constraint: a mathematical claim is true just in case it survives arbitrary relabeling of the objects in any structure that satisfies the relevant axioms. Arithmetic, on this view, is about what's invariant under isomorphism; our access is our reliable grasp of relabeling and structural possibility, not a causal channel to a realm. So the dilemma dissolves: no special realm to reach, and mathematical truth is just structural truth.
