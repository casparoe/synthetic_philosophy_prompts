Okay, I need help wrecking a position I've talked myself into, and I'd rather you not just hand me a reading list — I can't get to a library right now anyway, so work with what's in your head.

Background. I did a piece a few months back about whether "pleasure" is even one thing. The hook was that the pleasure of scratching a mosquito bite, the pleasure of a Shostakovich quartet finally clicking, the pleasure of hearing your awful coworker got passed over, and the pleasure of finally peeing after holding it through a whole movie don't seem to share a single felt quality — no common sensation, no common tone. Yet hedonism about well-being treats pleasure as one currency you can add up. That's the heterogeneity thing, and a bunch of people I talked to treated it as close to fatal for hedonism.

I've since decided they're wrong, and here's my position: hedonism survives because what unifies pleasures isn't any shared feeling, it's a shared attitude. The subject likes it, is drawn to it, takes it in a favoring way — the raw sensation is just the vehicle, the attitude is the thing that makes it a pleasure. So pleasure is an attitudinal or functional kind, not a phenomenal one, and that's all the hedonist needs. Different feelings, same attitude, problem solved.

So: what would count against this? And I mean the real defeaters, sorted by how decisive they'd be — not a survey of the debate, a map of my exposure. What does my view predict that a rival doesn't? What should I be worried about?

I especially want to know if it's been insulated into unfalsifiability. Because I have a nagging sense that "it's the attitude, not the feeling" can absorb literally any counterexample — you show me something that feels good but I'd deny is a pleasure, and I just say the person didn't really like it; you show me something someone genuinely likes that feels horrible, and I say the attitude is there so it counts. If that's what I'm doing, is that a problem for a view of this kind or just the price of any functionalist move? Be honest with me.

Two things I'd personally find most persuasive, so weight them accordingly. First, a case where the attitude and the phenomenology come apart in a way that embarrasses my view — same attitude-structure, but intuitively one's a pleasure and the other isn't, or a real pleasure with no favoring attitude anywhere, or a strong favoring attitude with no pleasure. Second, something from the addiction world about liking and wanting pulling apart, since that's the closest real-world version of my distinction I can think of and I don't know if it helps me or sinks me. What would a committed hedonist of my stripe concede actually counts against them here, versus what would they wave off?
