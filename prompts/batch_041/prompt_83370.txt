I have come to accept the occasionalist account of causation associated with al-Ghazālī, and before I try to defend the resulting position I would like an audit of whether the things I hold actually hang together. My commitments are these.

(1) God alone is causally efficacious; created things, including human bodies and minds, are occasions on which God produces effects rather than sources of effects themselves. (2) The regularities we observe are contingent: that fire is followed by burning is grounded entirely in God's habitual action, and nothing in the fire requires it, so an exception is always possible. (3) Some things are nevertheless really good and others really evil, not merely commanded and forbidden; gratuitous cruelty is wrong whether or not anyone prohibits it. (4) Genuine necessities — the truths of logic and arithmetic — do not depend on anyone's will, God's included, and we can know them. (5) Nothing external to God's will constrains it: God has no obligations, acts on no reasons, and there is no standard of goodness or rationality that God's decrees must satisfy. (6) Our inductive and experimental claims are genuine knowledge of how things will go — that this drug will lower this patient's blood pressure is a fact about the future, not a report of past correlation.

Tell me which of these are actually in tension, which only appear to be, and what the cheapest way to restore consistency would be and what it would cost. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and do not simply declare the set consistent. Give me a one-sentence verdict first, then the breakdown; the whole answer should be under 550 words.
