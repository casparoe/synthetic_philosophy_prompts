ok, so me and some people on a discord I'm in have been arguing for like three weeks about the units of selection thing (gene's eye view vs group selection vs species selection vs whatever) and I've finally realised half the time we're not even disagreeing, we're just using the same words for different positions. So I want to make a "which view do you actually hold?" flowchart I can post there, that walks someone from stuff they can actually answer to a named position. Here's what I want out of it:

Every branch node has to be a concrete case or a specific claim, phrased so someone can answer it without already knowing what the labels are. No "do you think the gene is the unit of selection?" questions, because everyone says yes or no for totally different reasons and it tells you nothing.

Build the tree out of the logical space first — the questions and the combinations of answers they allow — and then slot the actual positions into that space, instead of making a family tree of famous people. If a combination of answers turns out empty, say so and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, or unattractive, or just nobody's bothered to defend it.

Cases I want used (some of these I made up, tell me if they're bad):
1. A gene that makes its carrier help unrelated neighbours at a cost to itself, in a patch where the neighbours happen to share the gene more often than chance. Does the patch do any work here, or is it all just relatedness?
2. Same trait, but the neighbours are unrelated and patches like this keep throwing off new patches. Group selection? And does it matter whether the patch is a real reproducing thing or just a temporary bucket?
3. A herd where every member benefits from shared predator watch, but the herd never reproduces as a herd. Same kind of thing as #2, or a different animal entirely?
4. A group where every member is dark, and each one is dark because being dark helps that one survive. Is the group selected for being dark, or is it just a pile of individuals who each got selected?
5. A chunk of DNA that spreads by cheating at meiosis and drags down the rest of the genome. Is that a unit of selection doing its own thing?
6. A clonal colony, members genetically identical, stuck together forever, never splitting. One individual? A group? Or a gene making copies of itself?
7. A species that sticks around a long time because it has a huge range, where nothing about the range seems to be for the species' benefit.
8. A human moral norm that spreads because groups that have it outlast groups that don't, even though it costs the individuals who follow it.

There's also an arguement someone made that I can't tell if it's a trick: that splitting fitness change into a within-group part and a between-group part (or into per-copy parts) is just algebra, and you can do the split however is convenient, so asking which level is "really" selected is like asking which coordinate system is really where something is. I'd definately want a leaf for that view if it's a real view, and a leaf for the view that says no, only one of those splits gets the causation right.

Must-have leaves (or tell me if any of these are actually the same as each other, or incoherent): pure gene's eye view; kin selection as gene-level selection with a relatedness term; classic group selection where the group's good is the thing being selected for; multilevel selection where group-level traits can be selected for in their own right; species selection; genes as rival agents fighting inside a genome; and the just-bookkeeping one.

Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't actually separate (I suspect kin selection vs gene's eye vs multilevel is a mess). Keep it to 8 branch questions and at most 8 leaves, and give each leaf one line on its most awkward commitment. The audience is people who've read a pop sci book or two and argue on the internet, so keep the leaf names recognisable but don't water down the questions. thanks
