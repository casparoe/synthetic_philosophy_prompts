A close friend has spent the last few months trying to convince me that an advanced civilization existed before recorded history and that the conventional account is a cover-up, and I have been trying to explain, without being dismissive, what is wrong with the reasoning. The usual answer — that the claims are unfalsifiable — does not fit. They make fairly definite predictions about what should turn up at particular sites, and those predictions keep failing. What actually holds the position together is a different move: each failure is absorbed by a further adjustment and, more strikingly, the very failure to find the expected evidence is treated as confirmation, on the grounds that a cover-up would naturally leave no trace. The view ends up compatible with any possible state of the record while still sounding empirical. I have also begun to notice a weaker version of the same move on the orthodox side, where every new anomaly is said to be already accounted for without the accounting ever being produced, which makes me doubt that what I am seeing is a vice peculiar to pseudoscience. Is there an established name for this structure, and for the general move rather than for the specific theory? I am not after a label to wave at my friend: I want to know what exactly the phenomenon is, how it differs from ordinary auxiliary hypotheses and from plain unfalsifiability, and where its edges are. If nothing established fits, please coin and define a term.
