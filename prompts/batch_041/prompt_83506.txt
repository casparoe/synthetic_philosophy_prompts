I'm making a handout for a small reading group on the Second Amendment, and I want a decision tree that walks someone from a starting commitment to a named position on the right to bear arms. My angle: I don't want it to just sort people by the usual labels ("pro-gun" vs "gun control"), I want it to sort them by what they think the right actually IS and who holds it. So build the tree out of the logical space, not out of a list of camps.

Structure I'm after: the first node should be whether the amendment protects a right individuals can assert against the government at all. After that, each branch node should be one question that seperates the positions, phrased so a person can answer it without already knowing where it leads. I want the branches to cover: (a) who the right-holder is -- the individual, the states, "the people" as a collective, or the organized militia; (b) what kind of Hohfeld incident it is -- a liberty, a claim-right, a power, or an immunity (gloss these in plain terms at each node, don't assume the reader knows Hohfeld); (c) what the right is FOR -- self-defense in the home, hunting and sport, keeping a "well-regulated militia" as a check on a standing army, or resisting a tyrannical government; and (d) whether the justification of the right fixes its scope or whether the scope can float free of the justification.

For the actual branch questions, use concrete cases, not abstractions. Something like: a city bans handguns in the home; a state requires a permit and a showing of need before you can carry in public; a ban on "assault weapons"; a federal ban on felons possessing guns; a private militia stockpiles arms; and a hypothetical where the federal government turns tyrannical. At least four of these should be branch nodes, and each answer option should be something a normal person could pick without knowing which leaf it leads to.

Leaves I need as named positions: the individual-right/self-defense reading; the individual-right/insurrectionist reading; the states'-right or collective reading; a militia-centered civic-republican reading; the reading where the amendment only limits Congress and protects no personal right; a "living constitution" reading where the right is real but broadly regulable; and a "the amendment is a dead letter and should be repealed" reading. If any two of those can't be seperated by your questions, say so instead of forcing it.

Also note any combinations of answers that nobody actually defends, and tell me whether the empty spot is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just never explored. Keep it to about a page and don't bother with citations, I know the case law.
