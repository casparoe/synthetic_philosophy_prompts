I recently took up a seat on the ethics panel of a public body that runs behavioural interventions, and I have been startled by how little the justifications put in front of us actually establish. The panel approves only schemes meant to work through a known cognitive bias — a default, a change in salience, a pre-commitment device, a friction placed in the way of an option — applied to a whole population, where seeking each person's consent is impractical, where a good many of those affected will never read the justification and would object if they did, and where the evidence for a scheme's effect size is thin and its effects uneven across the population. The panel is itself a choice architect, since its own procedures determine which schemes ever reach it. What I want from you is the specification such a theory would have to meet, not another defence or attack on nudging in general.

Set out the desiderata: the cases any adequate account must classify correctly, with the case behind each requirement; the distinctions it cannot collapse; and the findings from psychology, economics and law that it cannot contradict. The cases should include the ordinary hard ones (a helmet law, a ban on a recreational drug), the soft cases where the person is simply misinformed, interventions that steer a person's future self against her present preferences, and interventions aimed at people who are competent and dissent. Among the distinctions I care about: changing what is salient versus changing what is available; steering the means by which someone pursues her own ends versus substituting the panel's ends for hers; and paternalism proper versus preventing harm to others.

Then tell me which requirements are genuine and which are the prejudices of one camp, and flag any pair or set you think cannot be jointly satisfied. I have a suspicion about one such pair — that a requirement of full publicity sits badly with any intervention whose efficacy depends on the mechanism going unnoticed — but I want your judgement rather than my suspicion echoed back. Say also whether the account must apply to the panel itself. Finally, which single requirement does the most to exclude the leading positions, the libertarian-paternalist line and its autonomy-based critics, and why. Assume I know the basic vocabulary and do not need it explained. British English, please.
