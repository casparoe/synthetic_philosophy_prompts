I've got a CS degree and about fifteen years of writing code, zero formal philosophy training, and I keep bouncing off the problem of universals. I want to try it once more, but on my own turf.

Here's the debate as I roughly understand it, and I'm sure I'm mangling it: there's supposed to be a question about things like redness, or triangularity, or being a person. Are those things real, existing on their own over and above the individual red or triangular things? Or do they only exist in the things that have them? Or is there no such thing at all, just individual things plus the general words we use to lump them? And there's another option I half remember, where a property is just the resemblance among particulars, or where each particular has it's own individual redness and the general word picks out a family of those.

I want you to take that whole menu of positions and restate it, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually think in: types, classes, interfaces, nominal vs structural typing, typeclasses, prototypes, type erasure, value vs reference semantics, all of it. Then give me the residue report.

Some things already in my head that the translation has to deal with, because I'll be checking:

- TypeScript is structural: {x: number, y: number} written twice is one type, whatever you name it. Rust is nominal-ish: two structs with byte-identical fields are two different types.
- Java erases generics: at runtime List<String> and List<Integer> are both just List, and you can't ask an object what type parameter it was built with.
- Python classes are themselves objects, instances of a metaclass, and type is an instance of type. So a "type of a type" exists, and the chain is allowed to loop back on itself.
- Haskell typeclasses like Eq aren't types, they're constraints a type can satisfy; there's no value of type Eq.
- JS prototypes: no class object at all, just objects delegating to other objects down a chain.
- newtype / opaque types: two types with exactly the same members but a hard identity boundary between them.
- Void / Never: a type with no values at all.
- Python/Ruby at runtime: a value doesn't carry its type, only the interpreter's bookkeeping does, and you can reassign it.
