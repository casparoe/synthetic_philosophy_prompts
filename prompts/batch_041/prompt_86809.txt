Something has stalled in the long argument over whether atrocities can be ranked, and I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. The comparativists point out that we rank constantly — one massacre called worse than another, tribunals and aid and curricula ordered by severity — and that refusing to rank is itself a ranking, since treating the cases as equally grave is a comparative judgment too. The anti-comparativists answer that a ranking turns victims into entries in a ledger, that there is no common measure in which a murdered child and an eradicated community could be summed, and that the wish for a ranking betrays a moral failure rather than a cognitive one. Each side regards the other's central claim as either trivially true or a category mistake, and neither has budged in decades.

My hypothesis, which I want tested rather than assumed, is that at least two distinct gaps are doing the work. First, the comparativists defend ordinal comparability (this is worse than that) while the anti-comparativists attack cardinal commensurability (there is a common unit, so one is some multiple of the other); the two claims are independent, and each side hears the other as asserting what it is not. Second, the sides may be answering different questions under one name — one about whether comparison is possible or needed, the other about whether the act of comparing is permissible or what attitude it expresses toward the dead — so that a true comparison could still be an obscene one to make.

Diagnose this for me. Is the disagreement verbal, substantive but misframed, or substantive and merely hard? Where exactly do the sides part — over a matter of fact (does ranking help or harm?), over a standard of evidence or of moral reasoning, over which cases count as the data, or over starting intuitions neither can argue for? Is some hidden empirical or formal question doing the work? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a result about what comparison requires, an empirical finding, or a change of question — or should no progress be expected, and why?
