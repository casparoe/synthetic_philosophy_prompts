You are a philosopher of mind specializing in mental causation and the metaphysics of levels of description. Think carefully before you answer, and begin by sketching the space of possible views rather than by reciting the labels you already know.

The case: a careenium, a box of tiny magnetic marbles (simms) that bounce and occasionally clump into larger, slower-moving aggregates (simmballs), whose configurations and interactions are the things we read as symbols with content. The question is whether the symbolic level pushes the substrate around, i.e. whether the fact that a given simmball configuration means what it means makes any difference to what the simms do.

Write a decision tree that leads a reader from an initial commitment about physics to a named position on that question. Every node must be answerable by someone who does not yet know where it leads: whether physics is causally closed; whether the mental cause must be a distinct event alongside the physical cause or the same event under another description; whether the mental property, or only its physical realizer, is doing the work; whether multiple realizability is enough for the higher level to earn causal powers of its own; whether a state's causal powers may depend on facts outside the box; whether causation requires difference-making or only lawful accompaniment. Make the content-versus-realizer distinction one of the branch points: a reader who holds that the meaning does the work should land somewhere different from one who holds that only the realizer does.

Leaves should include epiphenomenalism in both its dualist and its physicalist forms, token and type identity theory, non-reductive physicalism with downward causation, supervenience-only physicalism, emergentism, interactionist dualism, Russellian monism or panpsychism, hylomorphism, eliminativism, and one leaf for the view that the question is ill-posed. Aim for nine to eleven leaves, and give each leaf one sentence naming its most awkward commitment. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate, and note any combination of answers that no one occupies, saying whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. No citations or reading lists.
