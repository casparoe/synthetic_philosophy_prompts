so I'm running the last session of a seminar on Kant's Perpetual Peace next week and I want to end with a poem instead of me summarizing the text again. can you write me a sestina. six stanzas plus the three-line envoi, and I want the six end-words to be the six preliminary articles, in order. I'll give you the words so you don't have to guess at the list: reservation, patrimony, army, debt, intervention, treachery. those are my glosses, not Kant's vocabulary, so use them as end-words and don't worry about matching his wording.

The part I actually care about: Kant has that footnote (I'm pretty sure its a footnote) where he splits the preliminary articles into the ones that are strict and the ones that are elastic -- some have to be complied with immediately, no exceptions, and the others he lets you put off, "in the course of time" is the phrase he uses for the armies one. I want the form to carry that distinction instead of announcing it. So the end-words belonging to the no-exceptions articles should come back letter-perfect in every stanza, and the end-words belonging to the postponable articles should be allowed to come back slightly changed each time -- same root, shifted sense or ending -- and the poem should make it obvious which is which and why, without a footnote of its own and without just telling me. If you're not sure of the numbering, work it out, thats part of the job.

Then the envoi, the three lines at the end, should be where the rotation stops and the poem says what all that preliminary clearing of the ground is for: the republican constitution, the federation of free states, and the stranger who arrives and is not to be treated as an enemy. It should feel like the poem finally sitting down.

Two things I dont want. Don't quote Kant at me, I want the poem doing the work, not the quotation. And don't put the Dutch innkeeper's signboard over the churchyard in it, every undergrad essay does that joke; if the poem has to answer the joke, let it answer it in the envoi without naming the sign.

A Kant person should be able to check the poem's structure against the text, and someone who's never read it should still get a poem. no title, I hate titles on things like this.
