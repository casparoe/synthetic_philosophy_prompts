The idealization is what makes the mathematics tractable; it is the price of treating knowledge as truth in a set of possible worlds, and for purposes of soundness and completeness proofs it is harmless bookkeeping. What it costs is the ability to describe agents who are less than perfectly logical: a mathematician who has not yet spotted the consequences of her own axioms, a database that does not 'know' what its own contents entail, a student who has not noticed that two of her commitments conflict.
