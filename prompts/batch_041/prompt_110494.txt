I am a retired experimental scientist—after a career in laboratory research—who has spent the last several years reading philosophy and social theory, and I keep returning to a question my old training did not prepare me for. In my former world, a cause was something you could intervene on, hold fixed, or randomize. Culture, by contrast, seems to be both inside and outside the causal order: it shapes what people take to be reasons, and it is transmitted through those reasons. Suppose Max Weber could be brought forward to read a representative body of contemporary work that treats culture as a cause—studies that use instrumental variables, natural experiments, and cross-cultural datasets to estimate the effects of religious belief on economic behavior, and theoretical models that treat cultural traits as inherited through transmission biases rather than as free-floating meanings. What would his considered reaction be?

I want a determinate reconstruction, not a survey and not a reading list. Specifically, address three questions. First, would he regard the causal-inference framework as vindicating, reframing, or misunderstanding his Protestant ethic argument? Second, would he accept that culture can be a cause in the same sense that a temperature, a selection pressure, or a policy intervention is a cause, or would he insist that meaning and value introduce a different kind of explanatory relation—one that is not merely a special case of interventionist causation? Third, how would he adjudicate between the modern ambition to identify cultural effects and his own emphasis on interpretive understanding, historical singularity, and the role of values in explanation? I am especially interested in the problem of explaining with values: whether invoking values explains behavior or merely redescribes it in a thicker vocabulary.

Please make the reconstruction as determinate as you can, flag where you are extrapolating beyond anything he actually said, and explain what in his intellectual commitments drives each prediction. I know the rough outline of his thesis; what I need is the philosophical anatomy of the disagreement, especially where the modern causal framework and the Weberian explanatory project come apart. Do not give me citations or a bibliography, and do not invent quotations from him; I want the argument.
