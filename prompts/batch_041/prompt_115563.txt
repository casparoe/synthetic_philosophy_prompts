A colleague of mine works on the ethics of intervening in nature, and I am trying to work out what rule she is actually running from her record of verdicts on particular cases. She supports disentangling a humpback whale from fishing gear and rehabilitating it, and supports a costly programme of feeding and veterinary care for a colony of bats whose roost was destroyed by a road. She opposes culling introduced foxes to protect a seabird colony, on the grounds that the ecological consequences are unpredictable and the cull would have to be repeated indefinitely. She opposes a vaccination programme against a naturally arising disease in a wild ungulate population, since no delivery mechanism could reach enough animals to matter. She also opposes releasing sterile males to suppress an abundant rodent species, for much the same reasons.

The proposal before her next month is to release a self-spreading vaccine against a naturally arising parasite that causes prolonged and severe illness in a wild mammal species. The vaccine has been well tested in captivity, its ecological effects are expected to be contained and monitorable, and it would spare an enormous number of animals a great deal of suffering.

Predict her verdict. More usefully, set out the candidate rules that fit her record equally well, say what each predicts about this proposal, give your best guess with your reasons for preferring it, and identify the case that would best separate whichever rules survive.
