Okay so I'm taking this metaphysics of social kinds course and I'm trying to pin down what I actually believe about which kinds are socially constructed, because my gut verdicts dont obviously hang together.

Money: constructed, obviously, but not arbitrary — you can't just declare pebbles to be currency and have it work, the thing has to do a job.
Race: constructed, strongly so, but I don't think that makes it any less real or less causally potent; the boundaries get drawn for social reasons and shift between places.
Gender: constructed too, but it feels different from race to me — the norms seem to constitute more of it, and it's the case where the classification loops back and changes the people being classified (prof called this the interactive/looping thing).
Quark: not constructed at all, even though the category is our invention in some sense. Same for electron.
Species: torn here. Different species concepts carve the tree differently and which one you pick depends on what you care about — but the lineages and the branching are there whether or not anyone cares. I lean not-constructed.
Teenager/childhood: constructed. There's no natural joint at 13 or at 19.
Weed: constructed, and this one bugs me — a weed is just a plant in the wrong place, so the kind is relative to a purpose, but the plant itself is as real as anything.

The pair I can't square: I say race is constructed because the boundaries are drawn by us and serve our interests, but I say species isn't constructed even though its boundaries are also drawn by us and also depend on our interests. What's the actual difference supposed to be?

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? My half-baked candidate: a kind is constructed when the world doesn't fix the boundaries and only we do. But that seems to make species constructed (which I don't want) and maybe quark constructed too. Test it against the cases, repair it, and tell me which of my verdicts has to go. Also say what the repaired principle predicts for a kind I haven't listed — I'll take either 'landslide' or 'deforestation', whichever turns out to be more interesting.
