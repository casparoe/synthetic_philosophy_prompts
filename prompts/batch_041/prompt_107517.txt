I need a joint statement from the two camps in the fakes/forgeries fight — the ones who've been arguing past each other for decades. Camp A, the empiricists: what matters is the experience the thing affords, so a molecule-for-molecule replica delivers the same aesthetic goods, and the massive premium on originals is snobbery, commodity fetishism, or plain market rent. Camp B, the contact view: the fact that this canvas was actually touched by that person, that it sits in the real causal chain of their decisions, is itself a good, and a copy however perfect is simply missing it. Write the document both sides could sign their names to.

Here's what I want in it.

A numbered section of agreed claims. That means: what a perfect copy does and doesn't reproduce, and how each side should describe the other's position without flinching; what we can say about the psychology of standing in front of the real thing; the point that deception is wrong on independent grounds, so that the interesting question is the non-deceptive replica and they should both agree to isolate it; what even counts as "the original" for prints, bronze casts, musical scores, and instruction-based or conceptual works where the artist sells a recipe; and a clean separation between three kinds of value at stake — the aesthetic value of the encounter, the value of the object as a historical trace, and the institutional/market value — plus which kind each of the cases below actually turns on.

Then the disagreements, each phrased the way its own side would want it phrased, no strawmen.

Then the cruxes, and I'd bet on at least these: whether relational-historical properties like derivative, ahead of its time, sincere, or innovative are genuine aesthetic properties or not; whether the contact value is final or merely instrumental; whether aesthetic value supervenes on perceptual properties; and whether the value of the original is the same kind of value as a wedding ring or a saint's relic, or something else entirely. For each crux give a case or test both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. They have to handle at least these cases: a molecularly identical painting made in the same year by an unknown contemporary, with no deception involved; a forgery discovered decades later that everyone including the experts agrees is more beautiful than the mediocre original it replaced; a work that's 80 percent restoration with the original composition intact; a genuine early work the artist later repudiated and denied making; a model trained on one artist's whole corpus that produces something better than anything they made; and a perfect digital scan that can be distributed infinitely at zero cost while the original still commands millions.

Finish with what each side would count as evidence against its own view, whether there's anything both agree would actually move the debate, and whether any part of this dispute is merely verbal — if so, say which part and why.

No citations please, I don't care who published what. Reconstruct the arguements from scratch. And please don't let the agreed-claims section go vacuous — I want claims that a hostile reader on either side would still have to accept.
