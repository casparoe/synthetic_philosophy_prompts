Hey, so I'm a fourth-year PhD student in science and technology studies and I've got a question I can't even figure out how to google, mostly because I dont know whether the thing I'm after has a name yet.

Here's the pattern. In arguments I keep watching a move where a belief gets treated as the floor you stand on, and then, a few minutes later, as just another claim sitting on the floor. Two examples from the last month. (1) In my reading group a guy defends a big claim about minds by saying 'come on, we all already agree the mind is just what the brain does, thats not even on the table here' -- so it's off the table, not up for discussion, doesn't need defending. Then later, when someone pushes on a claim that leans on it, the same guy says 'well, you'd have to actually give me evidence that the mind is just the brain, that's a substantive hypothesis.' Same belief both times, but once it's the floor and once it's a chair you can move around. (2) Online, in a policy fight, someone says the science here is settled, stop asking for more studies, you dont get to demand proof of the obvious; and then, to knock down one particular study, happily says the evidence is actually contested and every claim needs evidence. Again the same body of evidence slides from unquestionable to questionable depending on which way the argument is going.

What grabs me isn't the bad faith (though sure, sometimes it's bad faith). It's the structure. There seems to be a class of things that function as the un-argued floor, and the floor moves, and items get slid across the line -- sometimes on purpose, sometimes not -- and whether your demand for a justification is even legitimate depends entirely on which side of the line the thing is on, except nobody announces where the line is, and both sides will swear their demand is the neutral one.

I know this is near Wittgenstein's late notebooks, the bit about some things having to be taken for granted before doubting can even get off the ground. But as far as I can tell that picture is about the fact that there is a floor, not about the traffic across it -- who moves what, when, and what that does to the argument. Am I missing something? Is there an established concept for the sliding-and-exploiting part? The closest things I can come up with are 'moving the goalposts', 'special pleading', and 'motivated reasoning', but those are all about individual bad faith and dont capture the structural point that everyone leans on some un-argued floor. Also 'framing' and 'presupposition' feel too linguistic and too broad.

So: does it have a name? If it doesn't, coin one and tell me exactly what it covers and what it doesn't -- where the edges are, what it's not, and a couple of edge cases. I really dont just want a label, I want the carving. American English please.
