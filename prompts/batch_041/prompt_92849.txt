Until fairly recently I worked purely on the engineering side of machine learning, and I was fortunate enough never to have to think about what it means for a network to represent anything. That changed when I moved onto a small team that builds interpretability tooling. My job now includes drafting the technical claims that accompany what we ship, and I have found that I do not hold a stable view of the very thing we are selling.

The disagreement I keep colliding with takes roughly this shape. On one conception, a feature is represented when some comparatively simple function of the network's activations — a linear probe, a sparse decomposition, an attention head readout — can recover it reliably; representation is a matter of information being present and decodable. On the other conception, decodability is cheap and largely beside the point. What matters is whether the network uses the feature: whether intervening on the relevant structure changes behavior in a systematic, counterfactual-supporting way, and whether that structure composes with others instead of being a one-off correlation.

I would like you to compare these two conceptions as carefully as you can, and then tell me which one we should be anchoring our product claims to. Please do not stop at describing each side charitably. I want to know how each fares against the awkward cases I keep meeting in practice: properties that a probe recovers from a network that has never been trained on the relevant task; directions that steer outputs fluently but produce behavior that no one would call coherent or concept-guided; and features that the network plainly relies on but that no linear or sparse readout seems to expose. I would also like you to say whether the pair is exhaustive, or whether a third position — for instance, treating a representation as an abstraction relation that stands in a structure-preserving correspondence to some target system, rather than as either decodable content or a causal lever — genuinely dissolves the choice rather than just relabeling it.

Finally, and this matters for how I write the claims themselves: is there a fact of the matter here that our marketing language could be right or wrong about, or is talk of what a model represents irreducibly relative to the tool doing the asking?
