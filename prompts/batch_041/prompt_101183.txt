For seven years I have run a small neighborhood music archive that I started with my own savings; it now has a part-time assistant, a loyal membership, and a collection that a regional library has offered to absorb. I am deciding whether to hand it over and take a stable job or keep going alone. A level-headed friend tells me my thinking is muddled: the money and years already spent are gone whichever way I choose, so they should count for nothing, and I should simply compare the two futures on their merits. I half agree, yet something feels off. The archive as it stands — the contacts, the equipment, the goodwill — is not something I could have had without those years, so the past seems to be doing work in the present rather than merely lying behind me. I also promised two colleagues I would not walk away, and years ago I deliberately made myself into a person who finishes what he starts, precisely so that people would trust me with things worth finishing; but I notice that this same resolve is exactly what would let any project hold me hostage. And the library's offer might itself be a sign of how good the project really is, though I cannot tell whether that is evidence or wishful thinking. Can you sort out which of these considerations are legitimate and which are just dressed-up versions of not wanting to admit I have wasted my time — and is my friend's forward-looking rule the whole story?
