The Judy Benjamin problem is a classic puzzle about updating on conditional information. Judy is dropped into enemy territory and doesn't know if she's in Red or Blue territory. She starts with a prior: Red and Blue are equally likely, and if she's in Red, it's 50/50 whether she's in the headquarters area or not. Then she gets a message: the probability that you are in Red territory, given that you are in the headquarters area, is 1/3. Bayes' rule can't handle this, because she didn't learn a proposition outright; she learned a conditional probability. The standard fix is to take her prior probability distribution and find the new distribution that minimizes the Kullback-Leibler divergence from it subject to the constraint that P(Red|HQ) = 1/3. That gives a unique answer: her credence in Red should become 1/3, and her conditional probabilities P(HQ|Red) and P(HQ|Blue) should stay exactly where they were. The lesson is that updating on conditionals is just constrained optimization: you keep your old conditional beliefs fixed and only change the unconditional ones. And since dynamic coherence arguments show that any other update rule would leave you open to a Dutch book, the Judy Benjamin problem isn't a real problem for Bayesianism -- it's just a case where people got confused about how to apply Jeffrey conditionalization. The only interesting philosophical question is why the problem is still discussed when the math is settled.
