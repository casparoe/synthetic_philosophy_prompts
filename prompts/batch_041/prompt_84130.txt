I sit on the interpreting and translation services committee at a hospital trust, and on Thursday I have to table the committee's written opinion on our machine-translation pilot, so I'd like you to draft it. Everything you need is below, and its all internal anyway, so nothing here depends on being able to go and look things up.

The background. We serve a city where about ninety languages turn up in our clinics. We employ interpreters for eleven of them; for the rest we buy telephone interpreting, and out of hours the wait is often forty minutes and sometimes nobody picks up at all. Six months ago we began a pilot with an on-premise translation app — it sits on a trust laptop, it never touches the internet, and it was trained on a pile of legal, medical and public-service text plus a much larger general corpus. The vendor claims professional-standard output in forty languages and points to a score of 0.92 on their own evaluation metric.

What we're being asked to approve is an extension: keep it for appointment letters and discharge summaries, which nobody minds, and also allow it for consent conversations and safeguarding discussions whenever no human interpreter can be reached within thirty minutes.

The reason it's contentious. In March a registrar used it for a consent conversation in a language we don't cover. The patient said something along the lines of I suppose I'll have to; the app rendered that as a plain yes; the registrar ticked the box; and it only came out afterwards that she thought she'd been told she had no choice. Nobody was harmed, but by luck as much as anything.

Positions on the committee. Our lay chair says it doesn't understand a word of anything, it's a very good parrot, and we're being sold a fluency machine — he keeps going on about Hofstadter on Google Translate. Our digital lead says understanding is a red herring, since a thermometer doesn't understand heat either; the only question is whether the error rate is tolerable and whether the errors get caught. Our lead interpreter says the app is at its most confident exactly where it's most wrong: it turns hedges into assertions, flattens modality, and in languages that mark how you came to know something it simply picks a value and moves on. She adds that its mistakes are all of a piece, so asking the same system twice buys you nothing, whereas when she's unsure you can see it on her face and ask her to go again. And one of our governors noticed that the vendor's metric rewards output that reads fluently and naturally — which is precisely what the system is trained to produce — so a high score there is close to circular as evidence that the meaning survived the trip.

What I need from you: a written opinion the committee can actually vote on. Give me one definite recommendation — reject the extension, approve it as proposed, or approve it with conditions — and don't hand me a survey of the options and leave the choice to me; commit, and give your reasons. If it's conditions, name them so they're enforceable, say who owns each one and when it comes back to us. Take a position on the understanding question too, because it's doing a lot of work in this argument: if the digital lead is right that understanding is a red herring, say so plainly and tell me what follows for the policy; if he's wrong, say why. And be honest about the strongest point on the side you come down against — I'll have to answer it in the room. Roughly eight hundred words, readable aloud in ten minutes, then a JSON summary I can paste into the minutes, with exactly these fields:

- "recommendation": one of "reject the extension", "approve as proposed", "approve with conditions"
- "conditions": array of objects, each with "condition", "owner", "deadline"
- "review_date": date the committee should revisit this
- "position_on_understanding": one or two sentences
- "strongest_objection": the best argument against your recommendation
- "why_it_does_not_prevail": your answer to it
- "most_dangerous_failure_mode": the single failure you'd least want to see in practice
- "what_would_change_our_mind": the evidence or event that should reopen this
- "confidence": a number between 0 and 1
