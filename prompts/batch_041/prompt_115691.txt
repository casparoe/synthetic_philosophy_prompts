I'm putting together a session for a reading group on self-reference, and I want two designed cases rather than the usual Escher lithograph. So: build me a pair of scenarios, plain English, about a paragraph each, no math, no Escher, no nested fictions or dreams or mirrors, no self-referential sentences, and the tangle has to come out of ordinary institutional or social life.

Case A: a real tangled hierarchy that does have an inviolate level -- a level that has to stay fixed and outside the loop for the loop to run at all. Two extra constraints. First, the inviolate level can't be an agent (not the artist, the author, the programmer, God, whoever) -- make it a convention, a document, a rule, or a physical constraint. Second, someone inside the tangle should plausibly either misidentify it or insist there's no such level.

Case B: a case that looks like it has an inviolate level but doesn't -- the level that seems to stand outside is itself one of the things being looped through. Not just an infinite regress though; I dont want each level merely explained by the one above, the levels have to loop back on each other. And in both cases the loop has to actually run and produce something, not just be a paradox that breaks.

Then do the work: for each, name the levels and say where the loop closes, and for A say why that level can't be pulled inside the tangle without the whole thing collapsing. Finish with the honest part -- what single feature of A is doing the work (what I'd change to turn it into B, and vice versa), and how someone who thinks every tangle has to bottom out in an inviolate level would try to redescribe B as secretly having one, and whether that move is a discovery or a stipulation. If you think B is impossible and my spec is a cheat, say so and say why.
