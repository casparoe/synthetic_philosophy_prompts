Okay so I've been going down a Schopenhaur rabbit hole lately, and I keep coming back to his claim that music is special among the arts: it doesn't copy the Ideas the way painting or poetry do, it's a direct objectification of the Will itself (the Will being that blind, striving metaphysical reality behind all representation). I find this weirdly compelling, maybe because I play piano and there's something about a late Beethoven quartet that feels like it's getting at something deeper than any representation could. But I'm worried I'm just under it's spell. So here's what I want: if I take this position seriously -- music as the immediate voice of the Will, bypassing the Ideas -- what would count against it? I want the defeaters sorted by how much they should actually move me, from fatal to merely awkward, and I want to know which ones a committed Schopenhauerian would themselves have to concede rather than just dismissing as critics' misunderstandings.

And I don't just mean the standard stuff about music being about emotions or pure form. I mean the in-the-weeds challenges. Like, does the existence of program music, or music that seems to be about something very specific (a storm, a character, a political event), undermine the idea that it's Will and not representation? What about non-Western music that doesn't fit the Western tonal system he was theorizing from -- does the view secretly depend on a particular musical culture? Or the fact that music can be used as torture, or as a tool of nationalism, or as background noise in a supermarket -- does that fit with it being the pure Will? And what about cases where music seems to be about music itself, or where the composer's intention is completely absent (algorithmic composition, or a random generative process that happens to sound good)? I'd also like to know what the view predicts that its rivals (music as emotional expression, music as pure form, music as social practice) don't, and whether it's been qualified into unfalsifiability -- like, if every counterexample is just dismissed as not really music, or as a lower form, is that a problem for a view of this kind?

For what it's worth, the thing I'd personally find most persuasive against it is if there's a robust empirical finding that musical meaning is entirely culturally constructed and variable, because then the Will-objectification story starts to look like a projection. But I'm open to being told that wouldn't actually touch the metaphysical claim, or that it would only touch a psychological version of it. So yeah, give me the map of its exposure: what it risks, where it's vulnerable, and where it's just insulated by definitional moves.
