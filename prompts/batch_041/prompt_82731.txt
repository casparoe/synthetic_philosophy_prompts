I've been reading alot about the whole 'was farming actually a bad deal' arguement — the one where people say foragers barely had to work and ate better, and then someone else says look at the bones, early farmers were stunted and riddled with cavities, and it goes back and forth like that. There's a specific move in that literature I keep running into and I don't have a word for it.

The move is this. Both sides end up leaning on the same evidence: the handful of foraging groups that were still around a century ago. But those groups are the ones nobody bothered to take the land from. They're in deserts and swamps and deep bush, places farming never got to. So a comparison between foragers and farmers is really a comparison between farmers and the foragers who got shoved onto the land nobody wanted. The comparison group got selected by the very process we're trying to explain. And it's the same story with the record itself — farmers leave pots and granaries and burnt villages, foragers leave almost nothing, so a chunk of what we think we know about foragers having no chiefs is really just that there'd be nothing left to find if they did.

Here's the part that actually bugs me though. Everyone knows the first thing. It gets one sentence in the introduction, and then forty pages proceed as if it had been dealt with. The caveat is acknowledged and then never obeyed — it works like a permission slip instead of a warning.

So, two questions, and I'd rather not just get a label back. (a) Does the first thing have a name? I know it's in the vicinity of survivorship bias, but that seems to be about which attempts you get to see, whereas this is about a whole way of life persisting only where the alternative couldn't reach, so I don't think it's the same. Where's the line? And does the bias run toward making farming look better than it was, or worse? (b) Is the second thing a named move, or is it just the first thing being handled badly? It's not quite hedging and it's not quite begging the question. If there's no established term for either one, coin one, and tell me what it leaves out.
