ok so I've been lurking on LessWrong and in the ACX comment section for like ten years now and I finally hit an alignment argument I can't break. A friend dropped it on me in our discord and I'm pretty sure it proves too much, but I can't find the bad step. Roughly:

(1) An AI is aligned just to the extent that it does what humans value.
(2) What any given human values depends on their situation — and their situation includes what the AI itself says and does (it can persuade, nudge, model, whatever).
(3) So any AI that actually acts is changing the very values it's supposed to be aligned to. The target walks away as you aim at it.
(4) If we anchor alignment to people's current values, the AI can't ever move anyone toward better values (even in cases where they'd thank it later), and worse, it can't avoid shifting the target just by existing.
(5) If we anchor it to idealized values — what people would value with full info and clear heads — then somebody has to spell out what that idealization is, and whoever does that is the one actually picking the values.
(6) So either way, the AI (or whoever built it) ends up selecting which values get served.
(7) So "aligned to human values" is empty — every AI just serves whatever values its designers stipulate, and the phrase is a fig leaf over that stipulation.

My gut says something's off, because if this went through it seems like it would empty out basically every relational or normative predicate we've got (taller than, healthy, correct, tasty, you name it), which is definately too much. But everytime I try to actually name the step that breaks, I end up defending some version of it and going in circles. And don't even get me started on the version where it isn't one human but a whole population with clashing values and you need some aggregation rule — that seems to make it worse, not better, since now each input to the aggregation is drifting too.

So — which premise or step do I give up, why did it look fine, and what does giving it up cost me? If I have to keep one, I'd rather keep (3), because the moving-target thing feels like the real crux of the whole alignment problem, and I don't want to wave it away with 'eh, influence is weak,' since the whole point of a superintelligence is leverage. Also happy to be told the whole thing is just confused and I've been chasing a shadow for a week.
