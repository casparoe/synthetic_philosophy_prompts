My nephew recently had a corpus callosotomy for epilepsy that medication could not control, and I have spent the months since reading about what the operation does to the unity of experience. Two camps seem to talk past each other. The first holds that once the main bundle of fibres between the hemispheres is cut, what remains is not one subject of experience but two: each hemisphere supports its own stream of consciousness, and the apparently single person is largely an artifact of one hemisphere's habit of narrating behaviour it did not initiate. The second holds that there is still exactly one subject and one stream — the surgery merely restricts what parts of a single mind can share with one another, or the one stream switches between hemispheres rather than dividing. I want the document these two camps could sign together. Set out the claims both can accept: about what the experiments actually establish; about what unity of consciousness would have to consist in for either side to be right, including whether unity is all-or-nothing or a matter of degree; and about how much weight the patient's own reports can bear, given that the speaking hemisphere is one of the putative two. Then state, in wording each side would endorse for its own view, exactly where they still differ; give me the cruxes; and for each crux name a case, experiment, or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of the dispute is merely verbal — a disagreement about what 'unity' or 'one mind' is to mean — say which part, and why. A numbered list of agreed claims followed by a table of disagreements would suit me.
