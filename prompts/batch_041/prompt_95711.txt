My friend and I have been going at this for years and neither of us moves, so I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. I think sharing a passport gives you no reason at all to favor a compatriot over a stranger abroad — what counts is need, or the actual value of a relationship, and a state isn't a relationship. She thinks we may and should favor compatriots, because we're bound to them by a shared scheme of cooperation and a shared political project, and that it's absurd to treat the bloke down the street and someone on the other continent as equally my concern. We agree on every fact about who cooperates with whom and what the institutions actually do; the whole fight is whether any of that generates a reason. My hunch is that we're smashing two questions together — what distributive justice requires (does it stop at the border?) and whether partiality toward compatriots is permitted (may I favor my own?) — and that these come apart, so we may just be answering different questions under one heading. For her they really are independent: she'll happily sign up to full global distributive justice and still insist she owes her neighbour more. If my hunch is right, which question is each of us actually answering, and does the impasse dissolve once they're separated — or is there a genuine clash left underneath once you peel them apart? Give me a case that pries the two apart cleanly, and since I might just be flattering myself that the confusion is hers, tell me where this diagnosis is most likely to be wrong and what would make you drop it.
