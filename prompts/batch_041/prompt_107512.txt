ok so im in this philosophy club at my high school and we have to write a short story that's actually about an idea, not just a story that mentions an idea. i picked something my cousin told me about from her econ class and now i cant stop thinking about it.

heres the thing she said. there's this old arguement that a scientific model or theory doesnt have to have realistic assumtions at all. like you can assume that every person is a perfectly rational calculator who knows every price in the economy, or that the world has no friction, or that a business only ever cares about profit and nothing else. none of that has to be literally true. the weird part, and the part i want the story to be about, is the claim that this isnt a bug, its the point. someone even supposedly wrote that the more unrealistic the assumption, the better the theory can be, as long as it predicts well, and that you should never judge a model by its assumptions, only by its predictions. my cousin says a lot of smart people think this is either profound or total nonsense.

i want a short story that really lives inside this idea. so maybe set it somewhere that takes the rule seriously as an actual policy, like a town or a company or a school where every big decision is made using models that are proudly built on assumptions everybody openly knows are false, and the people there have gotten used to it and even defend it. show it working, and show it breaking. please dont give me the lazy version where the unrealistic model fails and everyone learns that realism matters, thats way too easy and kind of smug. i want to actually feel the pull of the argument first, like genuinely understand why someone would say a false assumption can still be the right one to use, and then show the exact kind of situation where that stops working. like when the thing you assumed away turns out to be the thing that actually matters, or when the world shifts under the model and the prediction scoreboard that everyone trusted goes quiet.

also put in at least one character who thinks the whole thing is a con and one who thinks its beautiful, and let them argue so that neither one is obviously the authors mouthpiece. keep it like 3 to 5 pages. make the models and assumptions feel concrete and specific, not vague hand-wavy stuff. and please dont just recycle the examples everyone uses like spherical cows or frictionless planes, come up with your own weird specific assumption that this town or company is secretly running on. thats the part im most excited to read.
