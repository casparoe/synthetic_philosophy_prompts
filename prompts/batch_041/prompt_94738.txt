I host a small software-engineering podcast, and next week's guest defends a view I'll call the counterfeit-people line: that large language model agents can pass every behavioral test we have for personhood without being the genuine article, and that they therefore deserve a distinct moral category of their own rather than being folded into either tools or persons. He tends to deflect objections with a version of "you're just describing the intentional stance, which is all we ever had with humans either." Write me eight to ten interview questions that make that deflection progressively harder to use, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give. I want at least one question that forces him to say whether the deficiency he's pointing at lies in the system's capacities or in its causal history — put the case of two systems with identical behavior, one trained and one evolved, and make him state whether his line falls between them or puts them on the same side. I also want a question that turns the counterfeit metaphor against him: counterfeiting presupposes a genuine article, so ask what the genuine article is and whether he is quietly assuming a metaphysical essence. And one on the reciprocity condition — whether personhood for him requires being able to hold and be held responsible, and what that means for a system that can imitate the exchange of reasons but cannot be a party to it. Finally, since I'm an engineer and not a philosopher, close with one question about whether his "distinct category" is a claim about moral status or merely a counsel of caution, and what concretely would change in how we build and deploy these systems if he were right. In your answer, state how confident you are in each question's framing and single out the one you are least sure lands.
