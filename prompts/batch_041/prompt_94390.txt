Im putting together a unit for a first-year seminar on the historicity question -- you know, did the Trojan War happen, was there a King Arthur, was there an Exodus -- and I want to hand out a decision tree instead of giving another lecture. Can you build it?

The audience is smart first-years with no background in archaeology or biblical studies, so every node has to be answerable without knowing the names of any schools or scholars. The tree should start from something they already accept -- that the Iliad, the Arthurian material, and the Exodus story were all written down long after the events they claim to describe and all contain plainly non-historical stuff -- and then branch by the questions that actually split people.

I want the branching to be about method and metaphysics, not about facts they'd have to look up: is 'was there a real event behind this?' an empirical question about evidence or a conceptual one about what could count as evidence; does a tradition's own claim to be handing down history count as testimony, or only as a symptom to be explained by other causes; does the burden of proof start on the side of the story or on the side of the skeptic; does a real event have to be causally upstream of the text, or is it enough that the text is somehow about something real; and is a real 'kernel' a single datable event or just a social situation -- a collapse, a migration -- that happens to generate alot of stories. Also worth branching on: is historicity all-or-nothing or a matter of degree, and does finding a kernel change what the text means.

Leaves should be named positions -- include the naive kernel-hunter, the minimalist who says the text can't serve as evidence at all, the euhemerist, the mythicist who says the story is the object and historicity is a category error, and the deflationist who thinks the whole question is a distraction -- plus whatever others the space needs. For each leaf, one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Two extras: mark any combinations the tree can't reach and say whether that's because the position is incoherent, unattractive, or just unoccupied; and flag any two leaves your tree can't actually separate with the questions you've got. Finally, a short note at the bottom saying which leaf each of the three cases most naturally falls into and where it strains the tree.
