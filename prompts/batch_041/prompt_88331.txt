Ok so I just got home from dinner with my friend Dana and we argued for like two and a half hours about methodological individualism and I'm still annoyed about it. Her parting shot was that if I'm right then everything she does is just storytelling with footnotes, and honestly that one landed.

Here's what I have to defend at the theory workshop on Thursday, and I need you to get me ready for the question period.

My thesis: the individualism-vs-holism fight is about the wrong axis. I've never actually seen a social-science explanation that works by positing a social entity that isn't made of people, so the ontological version of the dispute seems to me to be a fight about nothing. What really divides the camps is the modal status of the explanation. The holist wants a social-level explanation to generate expectations — given the structure (a fiscal squeeze, a legitimacy crisis, whatever), the outcome should have been foreseeable, at least probabilistically; otherwise, they say, the social level is doing no explanatory work and you might as well have written a novel. The individualist says the only expectation you're ever entitled to is conditional on individual-level states, and you can basically never specify those, so what a social explanation gives you is retrospective contrastive understanding: why this rather then that, given what we now know about who did what. And I want to say that's fine, it isn't a defect, and the demand for expectation-generating social explanation is physics envy plus a confusion between explaining and controlling.

The test case I use is the boring one: two places with the same structural profile, one boils over and one doesn't. The holist says the structure explains the one that boiled. I say it explains nothing until you can say what was actually different, and the moment you can say what was different you're back at the level of the people.

So the four things I'm committing to: (a) the dispute isn't ontological, it's about what an explanation licenses; (b) the individualist existence constraint is trivially true and does no work; (c) the holist expectation constraint is the only substantive holist claim left on the table and it's false; (d) both camps are right about the part that doesn't matter and wrong about the part that does.
