I write software for a living and have no formal training in philosophy, so I apologize in advance for any terms I bend out of shape. There is an idea I keep circling and cannot settle, and I would like you to stage the disagreement for me as a dialogue.

The idea is that a self-referential structure — two hands drawing each other, a program that prints its own source, a compiler that builds itself — only holds together because something stands outside the loop. Hofstadter calls this the inviolate level; the picture I carry around is Escher's hands, which appear to draw each other but were in fact drawn by Escher, who is not in the picture. I want a dialogue between two people who agree on the examples but not on what the examples show.

One of them argues that the inviolate level is a structural requirement: any tangled hierarchy that actually does work — that computes something, proves something, or produces a stable output — must be anchored by a level exempt from the tangling, and a loop with no such anchor either collapses into paradox or spins without producing anything. The other argues that the inviolate level is an artifact of description: a level is exempt only relative to some chosen encoding, and change the encoding and the privilege migrates. On this second view Escher's hands are just marks on paper, and Escher himself is one more thing inside the picture, no more outside it than the hands.

A few requirements for the dialogue:

- Keep both participants equally sharp. Neither is a strawman, and each should be able to restate the other's position in a form the other accepts before replying to it.
- Force them through at least one concrete case in detail — a self-hosting compiler and its bootstrap seed, a quine, a linter whose own rule definitions are subject to the linting it performs, or a build system that rebuilds itself. Let them disagree about whether the seed that starts the process is a genuine outside or merely the earliest inhabitant of the inside.
- Let one of them raise the objection that, without an inviolate level, the distinction between a system and its own description becomes arbitrary, and let the other either answer it or make plain what must be conceded in order to answer it.
- No slogans. Every move should answer the actual previous move rather than talking past it.
- Close with the two of them agreeing on a single narrow point and disagreeing about what that point implies.

Roughly 900 words.
