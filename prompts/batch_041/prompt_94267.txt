I have been working through the Judy Benjamin problem and have reached what feels to me like a fairly firm conclusion, which I would like you to assess. In the familiar setup, Judy parachutes into territory divided into red and blue regions, each containing a headquarters area and a second-company area, and then receives a message that constrains the conditional probability of her being in one sub-region given that she is in one of the two main regions. Because what she receives is a conditional rather than a categorical piece of information, and because Bayesian conditionalization can only take propositions as inputs, the example looks to me like a demonstration that the standard Bayesian updating apparatus cannot represent conditional evidence at all. That leaves, as far as I can see, two unattractive options: adopt a non-probabilistic logic of conditionals in the style of Stalnaker or Lewis, or patch the framework with an ad hoc distance-minimizing rule such as relative-entropy minimization. Which of these should I adopt, and what exactly do I give up by moving to a picture in which unconditional credences are no longer fundamental — for instance, by treating conditional probability as primitive in the way de Finetti does?
