Me and two friends have been running a reading group on emergence and mental causation, and we've gotten stuck on the careenium thought experiment — the one with a box of tiny magnetic marbles (simms) that clump into bigger moving clusters (simmballs), where the clusters seem to have their own dynamics and shove each other around in ways you couldn't read off any single marble. I'm one of the two people drafting this. Priya is a card-carrying deflationist: for her there is only simm-level causation; simmballs are real patterns, sure, but when we say a simmball pushed another simmball we're telling a convenient story that is fully implemented by simm-on-simm collisions, and the higher-level causal talk adds nothing but bookkeeping. I'm on the other side. I think the macro patterns do genuine causal work, and that in at least some cases the best explanation of why a particular simm went where it went runs through the simmball's persistence, its boundary, its trajectory — not through the micro-details, which are practically noise.

Please draft the joint statement we could both sign. We want: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims — about what the setup actually contains, what each of us means by a level, what the microphysics does and doesn't fix, and what we both accept as the thing needing explanation; (2) the residual disagreements, each written in a form its own side would sign off on, not a strawman of the other; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling one would actually move one of us — and for each crux, a case, argument, or possible observation that we both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If part of what looks like a disagreement is really just us using 'cause' or 'level' differently, say which part and why, and move it into the agreed section rather than leaving it in the table.

A few things I'd like you to work in if you can, since these are what we keep circling: whether a frozen snapshot of the whole arrangement — pattern intact, nothing moving — counts as having the same causal powers as the running version; whether a macro-level intervention that leaves the micro-details different on each run but reliably produces the same macro outcome counts as evidence for the macro level's causal standing; and whether the simmball-level story is merely more tractable for creatures like us (big and slow, can't track simms) rather than more true. Also be honest if you think one of us is confused about our own commitments — the document should be signable, but it doesn't have to pretend we're both totaly clear.

Keep the whole thing under 900 words. Numbered agreed-claims section first, then a table for the disagreements (columns: crux, neutral statement of the disagreement, what would bear on it, Priya's prediction, my prediction), and no more than a short paragraph of closing remarks.
