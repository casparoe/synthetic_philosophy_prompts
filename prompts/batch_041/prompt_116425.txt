I am advising a hospital trust that intends to replace its written patient-information leaflets with a purely pictorial system, for patients who read little and share no common written language. The premise I am working from — the part of Neurath's picture-language programme, developed in his Vienna work on pictorial statistics, that I take to be still live — is that such a system is a notation rather than a picture dictionary: a small stock of standard elements combined by rules, so that relations such as more than, part of, or before and after are shown rather than described, and can be read off without translation into any spoken language. Set out the most important implications of this premise: what the trust can and cannot ask the leaflets to carry, including the cases that resist picturing (negation, conditions, exceptions, degrees of confidence); what must be true of the notation if it is to bear that weight; what follows for how the notation would have to be taught and standardised; and where the premise overstates what a picture language can do.
