I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and I keep running into Gödel in places Gödel has no business being. Last month at an AI-safety workshop a guy said flat out that Gödel proved no system can prove its own consistency, and used that to declare "provably safe self-modifying AI" a non-starter. Then at a philosophy night someone trotted out the same theorm to say a human mind can't be a machine, because a machine is just a formal system and we can supposedly see the truth of its Gödel sentence. And an econ blogger I follow uses it to argue a central planner can never model the whole economy including the planner's own model. I have a strong feeling these three people are not making the same claim, and that at least one of them is dressing up something trivial or false as a deep theorem.

So what I want is a menu. Take the sentence "Gödel's incompleteness theorems show that..." and spell out the different precise claims it could be standing for — the actual formal results, the innocent-but-boring readings, the overreaches — and then for each one say whether it's true, trivial, or bunk, and which of my three characters was really leaning on it. I don't need the theorems re-explained from scratch, I need the disambiguation and the verdicts. Where a reading would actually get its user to their conclusion, say so; where they've smuggled in an extra premise to get there, name the premise. I'd also like to know which of the readings is the one people most often *think* they mean, versus the one they'd have to mean for the argument they're actually making.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble, no throat-clearing, and don't restate what I asked.
