I have no background in philosophy, but I've been keeping a notebook of a pattern that keeps catching my eye, and I'd like to know whether it has a name. It shows up whenever I compare two accounts of the same thing that are both wrong. My car broke down last year and two mechanics gave me two theories: one named three specific parts and described how the failure happened, and the eventual repair showed he had gotten two of the three right and the third badly wrong; the other said only that something was off in the electrics, which was true and told me nothing. I get the same feeling comparing weather forecasts for a trip -- the one that commits to specific rain on specific days and misses two of them seems more like a real forecast than the one that says the week will be changeable -- and listening to a doctor friend lay out rival explanations for a symptom. In each case I catch myself saying that one of the two wrong accounts is closer to the truth than the other, and it is invariably the one that stuck its neck out and therefore also got the most wrong. I've noticed the opposite instinct too: in an argument, the person who commits to least is the hardest to catch out, and that feels like an advantage of a completely different kind.

When I try to write the rule down, it comes apart. If closer to the truth just means more true parts and fewer false parts, then I ought to be able to move any account closer by bolting on an extra true but irrelevant remark, which is absurd; and worse, when I run that rule over two accounts that are both wrong, it seems to say that they can never be ranked against each other at all, since each one has true things the other lacks and errors of its own. Yet I'm confident I'm not confused about nothing. I really do think some wrong accounts are better than others, and not merely more useful or more likely to be right -- the vague account is the one I'd bet money on, and it's the one I'd throw out.

So I'd like you to act as a philosopher of science who works on how competing theories get compared and tell me whether this has an established name and an established treatment. Please think it through carefully before you answer, and begin by brainstorming the handful of concepts that come closest, then say for each how well it fits and where it falls short of what I've described. What I want is the carving, not the label: what exactly the relation is between the two wrong accounts, whether it turns on how much they say or on how much they resemble the truth, how it differs from their being roughly true or probably true or simply useful, and where the edges are -- two accounts pitched at different levels of detail, or about slightly different subjects, or one much longer than the other. If the natural way of defining it is known to break, say so plainly and tell me what has been put in its place. I don't need a reading list or citations; I can't check them anyway, and the idea is what I'm after.
