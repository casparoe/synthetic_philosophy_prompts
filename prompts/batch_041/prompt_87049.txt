I've been reading that short Simone Weil piece about abolishing political parties -- not the usual stuff about parties as coalitions or machines, the part where she's really talking about what happens to a person's thinking once they're inside one. I think I keep running into the thing she's pointing at, and I want to know whether it has a name, because 'groupthink' isnt it and I need something to call it in my own notes.

The pattern. I spent two years in a tenants union, and before that in a grad-student group that was nominally a reading group but was actually a campaign. In the union there was a question everyone would happily have discussed one-on-one: whether our particular landlord was the villain of the story or just the most visible link in a chain of them. In the other group it was whether the thing we were campaigning for was even the right ask. Nobody forbade either question. No vote, no email, no one told anyone to drop it. They just didn't get asked, and after a while it wasn't that I was avoiding them -- they stopped occuring to me. What strikes me is that the mechanism seems to run through something you'd normally count as a virtue: the group has to be able to say what it stands for, in public, as one thing. Once it's said that, any internal question that would change what it stands for has to be priced as a cost to the group rather than weighed as a question. So the person who raises it isn't being censored or pressured exactly; they're holding two things, one the group can act on and one it can't, and the second one goes slack. I gather Weil thinks this is essential to parties rather than a corruption of them, which is why she wants them abolished instead of reformed -- I don't know if I buy that, but it's the version of the claim I want to be able to state precisely.

So: is there an established concept for that specific mechanism, the way a group's need for one public position makes certain thoughts unavailable to its own members from the inside with nobody suppressing anything? I know Michels on oligarchy, but that's about leaders entrenching themselves, and this happens in groups with no leaders and no offices at all. Groupthink seems to be about decisions going wrong because everyone agrees, whereas here the whole point is the questions that never get to a decision. Is there a name, and does whatever it is capture Weil's version or is she after something stronger? If there's no name, I want a coinage with a definition that has edges -- what it requires, what it doesn't, and how I'd distinguish it from ordinary self-censorship, from an echo chamber, and from simply being outvoted and letting it go. Boundary cases please: does a church count, a company, a research programme? I don't care about the label as such, I care about the joint being cut in the right place. Skip citations, I don't need a reading list.

Last line: \answer{...} with the established term if there is one, and if there isn't, your best coinage in three words or fewer.
