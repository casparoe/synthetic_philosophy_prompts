Your task: construct a thought experiment to specification.

The case to build. A small, realistic community — on the order of a few dozen people, engaged in ordinary activity, no abstract masses, no perfect reasoners, no science-fictional devices — faces a repeated coordination problem with at least two equilibria, neither of them forced by the situation. Over time, one regularity comes to be followed by everyone. You must arrange the case so that the regularity that prevails is the less efficient of the two equilibria: the one that is easier to fall into or safer to expect, not the one that would be best for everyone. Nobody ever agreed, promised, or legislated anything; the regularity grew out of ordinary local interactions, with each person forming expectations by watching those around them.

The specification the case must meet. The prevailing regularity must count as a convention on the account associated with David Lewis. Paraphrasing that account, it must satisfy all of the following: (i) everyone in the relevant group conforms; (ii) everyone expects everyone else to conform; (iii) everyone prefers to conform provided the others do, because the problem is one of coordination; (iv) there is at least one other regularity that would have served the group's coordinating need just as well, so conformity is not compelled by the payoffs; and (v) conditions (i)–(iv) are common knowledge among the members. The case must not smuggle in optimality: nothing in your scenario may make the prevailing regularity the best available one.
