I'm a science journalist — econ models, climate models, the whole beat — and I'm writing a long feature about the fight over unrealistic assumptions. You know the drill: somebody builds a model where the agent lives forever and never gets confused, or a climate model can't resolve a cloud so it fudges it with a parameterization, and then somebody else says none of that is real, so what is the model telling us about anything? I keep meeting people on both sides who are completely sure of themselves, and I've started to think they're answering different questions without noticing. Somebody always brings up Friedman and the as-if thing and then we go around again.

So here's the deliverable. I want a decision tree / flowchart my readers can actually use to figure out where they land — a which-position-do-you-actually-hold thing, but about the assumptions question specifically, not about econ vs climate or left vs right. A few things I care about:

- Build it from the questions first, the labels second. Every branch node has to be a question a smart general reader could answer about their own instincts without already knowing which -ism it leads to. No node that basically asks whether you're an instrumentalist.
- 6 to 8 branch nodes, 7 to 10 leaves. Readership is a science-and-ideas magazine, so no jargon dumps, but you can assume they know what a model is.
- These leaves need to be in there somewhere: false assumptions are fine because the model is only ever a prediction machine; the assumptions are false but as a first pass, and you get nearer the truth by relaxing them one at a time; the assumptions are false because the model deliberately leaves things out and that omission is the whole point; the model isn't making claims about the world at all so the truth question never comes up; the model is a machine you assemble so the phenomenon happens, and the assumptions are just parts of the machine; and the assumptions have to be true of something — namely the little imaginary system the model describes — so it's that system, not the world, we're learning about.
- I want one branch that turns on whether the falseness is recoverable: can you tell me what the model would say if you fixed the assumption? I think that's the line between people who find the spherical cow scandalous and people who find it a Tuesday.
- And one branch on whether the model's success even needs an explanation, or whether asking why it works is a mistake.
- Each leaf gets one sentence on the most awkward commitment it's stuck with.
- Where two leaves come out identical on every question, flag it as a pair the tree can't separate instead of pretending.
- Where a combination of answers is empty — nobody actually holds it — say which combination, and whether it's empty because the view is incoherent, because it's merely unattractive, or because nobody's explored it.

Don't bother with who said what, I'll do attributions myself, I just need the sorting instrument. Also it's got to survive a reader who disagrees with me, so don't stack the branches toward one answer.
