I am a philosophy professor preparing a graduate seminar on the socialist calculation debate. For the seminar's simulation, I need a decision procedure that students can run as members of a regional planning board. The board serves a federation of worker cooperatives. It meets monthly, its decisions are binding for a year, and the region is under fiscal and energy pressure. The board already collects real-time production data, runs machine-learning demand forecasts, and operates a voluntary preference app; it is committed to democratic governance and to avoiding mass unemployment. For any good currently allocated by administrative direction, the board must decide whether to keep directing it, to create a market-like exchange for it, or to adopt a hybrid. The theoretical background is the debate over whether a planned economy can perform economic calculation without market prices, and the associated arguments about dispersed and tacit knowledge, incentives, and trial-and-error discovery. I do not want a literature review, citations, or a history of the debate. I want the procedure itself, translated for board members who have not read it. Please write a reusable procedure: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a decision, and where it must hand the question to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts. Make the procedure sensitive to the distinctions that actually matter in the debate: technical producibility versus opportunity cost, information that can be centralized versus knowledge that is tacit and local, the difference between a price that clears a market and a price that reflects social cost, and the role of trial and error. Then run the procedure on two proposals: (1) allocating a newly developed, highly specialized industrial catalyst among the federation's firms; (2) allocating irrigation water during a drought. For each, show the procedure's verdict, the answers that drive it, and the point at which judgment enters. At least one verdict should be one I will not like. After the applications, add a short section on what the procedure cannot decide. Then provide a table with columns: Step number; Question; Sufficient answer; If not satisfied; Residual judgment (yes/no). Then provide a JSON object with fields: procedure_name, central_recommendation, what_would_change_my_mind, where_reasoning_is_most_likely_to_fail, case_verdicts (an array with fields case_name, verdict, key_uncertainty). Finally, in plain prose, state what would change your mind about the procedure's central recommendation and where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Keep the whole answer under 1800 words.
