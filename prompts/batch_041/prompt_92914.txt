I'm in a comparative-politics seminar and we've spent the last three weeks circling the performance-legitimacy question — whether a government can earn a genuine right to rule by delivering growth, safety, low corruption and rising life expectancy rather than by being elected, and whether China and Singapore are the proof of concept or just two lucky cases. My roommate is Singaporean and we've been having this argument since September; by now I'm pretty sure we're talking past each other in a way that's fixable, and I'd rather produce something than win. I don't need reading lists or citations, just the document — I want to print it out and hand it to him.

So: write the joint statement that a serious defender of performance legitimacy and a serious defender of liberal/procedural legitimacy could both put their names to. Three parts. First, a numbered list of agreed claims, and make it non-trivial — a list that a lazy relativist couldn't also sign: what each side thinks legitimacy is *for*, what China and Singapore have and haven't actually achieved, what can and can't be inferred from satisfaction surveys and from the fact that people stay, what the order in "prosperity and order" costs and who pays for it (and whether the state's grip reflects an actual capacity to govern or just the absence of anyone willing to test it), and what the other side's position gets right that the cheap version of one's own position misses. Second, a disagreements table: the disputes that survive, with each side's position written in wording that side would accept — no strawmen. If the proceduralist's line is that a mandate you can't lose isn't a mandate, write that as a claim they'd actually sign, and give the performance side's best reply in the same spirit. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — including but not limited to how much of the performance is caused by the political system rather than by size, history, trade position and a security umbrella; whether stated approval under a regime that punishes dissent counts as evidence of anything, and if you can't ask people safely, what indirect measure (emigration, investment, tax evasion, private grumbling) both sides would accept as a proxy; whether the comparison class for "it works" should be the country's own past, its neighbours, or a counterfactual version of itself; and whether performance legitimacy is a *kind* of legitimacy or just a very good reason to comply. For each crux, name the evidence or case both sides would agree bears on it and write down each side's prediction in advance, so the disagreement is at least falsifiable-ish rather than a vibe. And if you decide part of their fight is verbal — one of them using legitimacy for what people beleive and the other for what's justified — say which part and why, but don't let that swallow the whole thing; I'm definately sure there's a real disagreement left once the words are fixed.
