In my decision theory course, we recently contrasted expected utility maximization with the Kelly criterion, which the lecturer called an instance of geometric rationality. As I understand it, the Kelly criterion tells you to maximize the expected logarithm of wealth in repeated bets where wealth compounds. But since maximizing expected log wealth is just expected utility maximization with a logarithmic utility function, the Kelly criterion is not a distinct theory of rationality at all—it is simply a special case of expected utility theory. So why do some decision theorists treat geometric rationality as a serious alternative to the standard Bayesian picture? My lecturer also mentioned that Kelly can be justified by the time-average growth rate of a single trajectory rather than by an ensemble average, but I do not see how that changes the point. I would like you to clarify what is actually at issue. Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
