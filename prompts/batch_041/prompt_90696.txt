I have been asked to present a paper at our comparative cognition reading group in three weeks, and I would rather spend the session defending my thesis than discovering it. My claim is that the familiar four-level scheme of minds — Darwinian creatures, fitted to their world by their genes; Skinnerian creatures, fitted by the consequences of their own behaviour; Popperian creatures, able to let a hypothesis die in their stead rather than die themselves; and Gregorian creatures, inheriting a scaffold of tools, notations, and practices — is almost always read as a ladder of intelligence, and should instead be read as a ladder of what is permitted to be selected. On my reading, the fourth level is not defined by language or by tool use but by a change in what gets transmitted: the environment itself becomes heritable, so that a creature inherits a share of its own intelligence rather than only its reflexes.

The room will not be kind. Present will be two or three comparative psychologists who work on corvids and cephalopods and who will say that I have merely redescribed their behavioural data at a coarser grain; one committed behaviourist who holds that the Skinnerian/Popperian boundary is a fiction, since operant behaviour is already selection by consequences and an inner hypothesis that dies in my stead is covert behaviour with a homunculus smuggled in; a philosopher of biology who will ask what is replicated at the third and fourth levels and will argue that where there is no heritable variation there is no selection, hence no distinct level; a postdoc working on cultural inheritance and niche construction who will say the fourth level is that literature with a new name; and two developmental psychologists who will ask what developmental evidence could ever distinguish a Popperian from a Gregorian child.

Draw up my preparation for the discussion. I want the objections this room will actually raise, in their idiom, in descending order of how much they would cost me; for each one, the reply I should give in two minutes and a note on where that reply is thinnest; what I should concede at the outset rather than have extracted from me; and the one question I am hoping nobody asks. Give me the arguments rather than the literature — I do not need citations, and I would rather not be handed a reading list I will not have time to use.
