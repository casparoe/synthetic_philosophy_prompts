I am the research director for a state law reform commission. Our legislature routinely refers bills that would criminalize conduct many citizens regard as seriously immoral but where the harm is disputed: consensual adult incest, assisted suicide for the terminally ill, public desecration of national symbols, the production and sale of violent pornography, and a proposal to criminalize the purchase of sexual services. We are not a court and we are not a philosophy seminar; we have to produce a defensible recommendation on each referral within a legislative session, usually with one staff attorney and me, and the recommendation has to be usable by legislators who have no background in the Hart-Devlin debate. I know the debate well enough—Hart's insistence on critical morality and the harm principle, Devlin's concern with social cohesion and the disintegration thesis, the distinction between enforcing morality as such and preventing concrete harm—but what I need from you is not a summary or a reading list. I need a reusable decision procedure that operationalizes the debate into questions a staff attorney can actually run in an afternoon, with the hard cases built in. Our chair has also proposed a two-track rule: no referral may be recommended for criminalization unless it passes a harm-to-others screen and a social-cohesion screen, and if the two screens conflict, the procedure must name a tie-breaker rather than averaging them or pretending the conflict is not there.

Please write that procedure. It should specify the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure terminates in a recommendation against criminalization, where it terminates in a recommendation for criminalization, and where it must stop and hand the matter to the legislature as a contested value choice rather than a factual or conceptual one. It should preserve the distinctions that matter—harm to others versus harm to self versus offense versus moral wrong as such; positive morality versus critical morality; the empirical claim about social disintegration versus the normative claim that society may enforce morality; the difference between a practice being widely condemned and its being harmful—and it should be explicit about which of these the procedure can operationalize and which it can only flag. It should also be honest about where the procedure will misfire: cases where the harm is speculative, where the moral condemnation is majoritarian but the conduct is not harmful, where a practice is harmful but the proposed criminalization would create more harm than it prevents, and where the very question is whether the community has standing to legislate morality at all. Finally, run the procedure on three referrals: consensual adult incest between siblings, assisted suicide for a competent terminally ill adult, and public desecration of a national flag. For each, show the steps, the recommendation, and the residual disagreement. At least one recommendation should be one I will not like; do not soften it to make the procedure look tidy. Do not cite cases, statutes, or books; I need the reasoning and the operational tests, not authority.
