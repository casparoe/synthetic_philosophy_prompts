I am a Year 12 pupil and the student representative on my school's curriculum committee. The English department runs a six-week unit on tragedy — three novels, a play and a film, all of them bleak — and the committee is reconsidering it after two parents complained that it is bad for pupils' mental health. One governor has put the objection in a form I cannot shake: nobody chooses sadness for its own sake, so a unit that reliably makes fifteen-year-olds sad must earn its place through some further benefit; and if that benefit is really comprehension skills or exam marks, then the sadness is doing no work and the texts could be swapped for cheerful ones at no cost. The department replies that the unit is the pupils' favourite of the year, and two pupils have asked to be excused from the bleakest sections while remaining in the rest.

Please draft the written opinion I will put to the committee when it meets next month. It should recommend plainly whether to keep the unit, change it or drop it, and if changed, how and by when; it should address the two pupils' request; and it should explain what is actually going on when we choose to read or watch something that saddens us, since the parents' complaint looks to me like a version of an old puzzle about why anyone seeks out sad art at all. It should take the governor's argument seriously rather than dismissing it as philistinism, and it should say what would genuinely justify dropping the unit — what would show that the sadness was harmful rather than worthwhile. I lean towards keeping the unit, but I would rather the opinion be honest than persuasive.
