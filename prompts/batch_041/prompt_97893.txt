ok so I'm a novelist, about a year into a book that keeps changeing shape on me. The premise: a small town votes to tear down its memorial to something awful that happened there, and the book follows the people who want it gone and the ones who think that's a betrayal. I figured the hard part would be plot. Turns out the hard part is that I don't actually know what I think, and every scene I write is secretly an argument I haven't finished having.

So I've been reading around the whole duty-to-remember versus case-for-forgetting thing — Margalit on why remembering is somehow owed to the dead, Rieff basically saying that collective memory is a trap that keeps old wounds festering, that forgetting can be a kind of health. I get the two poles. What I don't have is a sense of which questions in that territory are actually worth building a novel around versus which ones just sound deep. Like, is the live question whether there's a duty at all, or is it something further in — who the duty is owed to, whether a community can owe it the way a person can, whether forgetting can be an act of justice rather than cowardice, how you tell the difference between honoring something and being haunted by it, whether remembering is even the right word for what a town does with a monument.

Here's what I want from you. If you were me — one person, maybe two years of writing time, one book — which two or three questions in this area would you actually build around, and which would you drop even though they sound profound? And I want the reasoning, not a list: why one question is upstream of another, which ones have a real answer waiting at the bottom versus which just generate more talk, and which ones would change the whole book if I got them wrong versus which are decoration. Tell me what would make you move your own ranking — if I came back tomorrow and said I'd decided X, what would that shift?

One more thing: it's fiction, not a philosophy book, so the questions have to be ones that can live in scenes and fights between characters, not ones that only exist in a seminar. If a question can't be dramatized, say so — that's actually useful to me.
