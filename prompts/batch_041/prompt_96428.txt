Imagine that Frank Ramsey, who died in 1930, could be confronted with the Ellsberg paradox and the subsequent literature on ambiguity aversion. Ramsey had argued that degrees of belief are measurable by betting dispositions and had criticized Keynes for treating probability as an objective logical relation; his framework treats coherent degrees of belief as additive probabilities. The Ellsberg case presents choices that many people make and that violate expected utility: in the two-urn version, they prefer betting on a known fifty-fifty urn to betting on an urn with an unknown mix, even when the bet on the unknown urn is symmetric; in the three-color version, they prefer betting on red over black while also preferring betting on black-or-yellow over red-or-yellow. Later theorists have treated this as ambiguity aversion and have modeled it using sets of probabilities, non-additive probabilities, and related devices, while others have argued that such choices are irrational or that they reveal only incomplete preferences.

What would Ramsey's position have been? Would he have classified the typical Ellsberg pattern as a straightforward inconsistency that his betting operationalization exposes, as a rational response to missing information that his account cannot capture, or as evidence that his method of measuring partial belief by betting odds breaks down when the subject regards the odds themselves as indeterminate? Would he have regarded ambiguity aversion as a taste rather than a belief, and if so, would he have thought it has any normative authority? Would he have extended his own framework to accommodate non-additive or set-valued probabilities, or would he have insisted that a coherent agent must have a single additive subjective probability and that Ellsberg choices show only the limits of the agent's willingness to bet, not the limits of Bayesian rationality? How would he have responded to the suggestion that Keynes's distinction between the probability of a proposition and the weight of the evidence for it, which he criticized, anticipates the modern ambiguity literature? Give a reasoned reconstruction of Ramsey's likely view, not a survey of the modern literature, and be explicit about which parts of the reconstruction are speculative because the issue did not exist in his lifetime.
