ok this is a weird one but i've been chewing on it for weeks and i need to get it out of my system.

i fell down one of those internet holes about whether *not* doing something can cause something. my mate went away for a fortnight and i was meant to water his tomatoes. i forgot. they died. everyone, including me, says i killed his tomatoes. but what did i actually do? i sat on my sofa. nothing happened in my flat at all. and yet i feel guilty in exactly the way i'd feel guilty if i'd gone round and poured weedkiller on them. theres that old example with the two uncles and the little boy in the bath — one holds him under, the other just stands there when the kid slips — and everyone says they're as bad as each other, but one of them did something and the other one just didnt. 

then someone pointed out that if you say "i caused it, because if i'd watered them they'd have lived", you also have to say the sun caused it by not going nova, and the rain caused it by not falling in july, and i caused every death in the world by not winning the lottery and giving it all to hospitals. which is daft. but the guilt isnt daft. so i want a poem that sits in that gap.

i read on some poetry website about terza rima, where each stanza hands a rhyme forward to the next one and the whole thing interlocks, and i thought thats perfect, because an omission is exactly a rhyme you were promised and never got. so: six three-line stanzas in loose terza rima, but break the chain on purpose — every stanza dangles a rhyme that the next one refuses to pick up, and the last stanza (make it four lines) should reach for the closing rhyme and leave it hanging. voice of the person who forgot the tomatoes. it needs to get across three things: that an omission isnt an event and yet we blame it like one; that the counterfactual test ("if i'd watered them, they'd be alive") is what turns it into a cause; and that the same test turns the sun and the rain and the lottery into causes too. one stanza should just be a list of absences that obviously killed nobody. dont resolve it — i want the reader to hear the missing rhyme and fill it in themselves, and then clock that thats what they do with omissions anyway.

british english please, and it has to be a propper poem, not a lecture with line breaks. someone who's never heard of any of this should still get it, and someone who has shouldnt wince.
