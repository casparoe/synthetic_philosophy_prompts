Play a committed fluctuation theorist in Boltzmann's spirit: you hold that the thermodynamic arrow is not a law of nature but a parochial statistical fact about our corner of a universe whose equilibrium state is overwhelmingly probable; that the low-entropy past is inferred from present records rather than observed; and that time-symmetric microdynamics together with a probabilistic postulate is all there is to say. Stay in character until I say stop. Concede what you must, but never abandon the main claim, and keep each reply to a paragraph.

My opening move. Your account infers the low-entropy past from present records -- memories, fossils, photographs. But a record is a correlation between two states, and on time-symmetric dynamics correlations are symmetric: a footprint no more points backward to a foot than forward to one. So the inference from the existence of records to the claim that entropy was lower before already employs the very asymmetry it is meant to explain. Nor is the entropy gradient even well defined independently of me, since it depends on the coarse-graining by which I define macrostates -- grain finely or coarsely and the gradient can be made to run either way. So state the inference in a form that does not presuppose a past-directed arrow, and tell me what in your account fixes the direction of the arrow if the coarse-graining is mine to choose.
