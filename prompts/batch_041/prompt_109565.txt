I'm putting together materials for a small reading group that has been working through the conceptual puzzles surrounding the Fermi paradox, and I've reached a point where I'd like someone to lay out, carefully and in order, what actually follows from a particular framing we've been circling. The framing is this: suppose that any civilization which develops the relevant technology tends to expand outward through its galaxy at a substantial fraction of the speed of light, and that such an expansion, once started, tends to convert or visibly rework a galaxy on a timescale of tens of millions of years at most -- short compared with the billions of years the galaxy has existed. Suppose further that civilizations of this kind are not vanishingly rare across cosmic history but arise at some appreciable rate per galaxy. The observation, of course, is that we see no sign of any such expansion having reached us or having visibly reworked anything nearby. I don't want a survey of the literature or a list of proposed resolutions; I want the downstream consequences of taking this specific combination of assumptions seriously.

What I'm after is the set of most important implications, ranked by how much they should change our thinking rather than by how often they're mentioned. Please distinguish implications that are genuinely forced by the assumptions from ones that depend on further contentious premises, and flag explicitly which are which. I'm especially interested in what follows for three things: how confident we should be that we are alone or nearly alone; what our present moment likely represents in the broader history of life in the galaxy; and what, if anything, this should change about how we think about our own species' long-run prospects and the value of what we do now. Be concrete about the reasoning at each step rather than gesturing at it, and where an implication cuts against the intuitive reading of the paradox, say so and explain why the intuition misleads. Assume I know the basic shape of the paradox already and skip the throat-clearing.

Since this is going into a shared document where we like to be able to pull out the single takeaway, please close by naming, in one sentence, the most important implication of the whole set, and then give that same takeaway in the form \answer{...} on the final line, with the braces containing a single short phrase.
