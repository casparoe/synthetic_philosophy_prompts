I run sustainability for a small chain of staff canteens -- nine sites, about 4000 lunches a day -- and I've been told to write the protein policy. Which in practice means: a procedure my site managers and the procurement person can run on any dish or any swap to decide how bad it is for animals, so we stop having the same fight every menu cyle.

The fight, in case it helps: the veggie crowd says drop the beef, obviously. But a couple of us read the stuff about field animals -- that harvesting a field kills a fair number of mice and birds and the like, so a lentil bake isn't free either -- and then the intern pointed out that if you count individuals rather than kilos, chicken comes out worse than beef by a mile, because a cow is one animal and a chicken is, well, not one cow. She wants a two-column thing: how many animals, times how bad each death is, and she says both columns have to be in there or we're just counting heads. And she raised the awkward one too: if we buy less meat, there's less demand for farmed animals to exist at all, and she reckons some people think a life worth living is a good thing, so buying from a genuinely good farm might be a plus, not a minus. I don't know what to do with that and I half suspect it's a trap, but the procedure has to say something about it -- even if the something is "we don't score this, here's why, and here's who decides instead."

What I want: a decision procedure, in order, that a site manager with no philosophy traing can run in about half an hour on one dish or one swap. The questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it stops with a clear no or a clear go, and where it has to be handed to a person because it's a values call rather than a fact. It has to tell me which unit to count in -- deaths, kilos, animal-years, calories, servings -- or tell me honestly that the choice of unit is doing most of the work and can't be made by procedure at all. And it has to handle the crop-death thing without either ignoring it or letting it swamp everything else.

Constraints: I can't ask suppliers for anything they don't already publish, I've got roughly one number per dish (weight, and a vague idea where it came from), the head of catering signs off on anything that changes a recipe, and legal have said we can't put welfare claims on the menu. Assume whoever runs it is smart but untrained. Give me a one-page version and the long version.

Then run it on three cases: (a) swapping a beef burger for a chicken burger at the same protein, (b) swapping the beef burger for a lentil-and-rice thing, (c) a pork dish where the only supplier we can actually get is a high-welfare outdoor one at triple the price.

I'm expecting it to tell me something I don't like, so don't sand it off. And tell me what the procedure can't do -- the cases where two reasonable people run it and land in different places, and whether that's a bug or just ethics.
