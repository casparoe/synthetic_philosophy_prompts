You are a historian of early Chinese thought with a philosopher of language's instincts, at home both in the Gongsun Longzi material — the White Horse dialogue and the disputers generally — and in the modern literature on reference, predication, and the individuation of terms. I want you to stress-test an interpretation I have come to hold. Work in stages, and think carefully rather than performing erudition: first restate the position in your own words and say what it actually commits me to; then brainstorm, as widely as you can, everything that might tell against it; then rank and assess. Do not give me a bibliography, a reading list, or citations to secondary literature — I want the textual and philosophical considerations themselves, not pointers to where they are discussed. Where a point turns on the exact wording of a passage you cannot reconstruct with confidence, say so plainly and mark it as uncertain rather than supplying a quotation I can't check.

The position. The White Horse dialogue is neither sophistry nor an artefact of classical Chinese lacking articles. Its real subject is the individuation of names. Horse is a name answerable to shape; white is a name answerable to colour; a white horse is a name of the composite, a coloured shape. The Advocate's 非 is non-identity, not exclusion from a kind: his claim is that the name white horse and the name horse do not name the same thing, because names are individuated by what they answer to — by what a seeker who asks for one rather than the other will accept. That is why the seeking argument is the load-bearing move rather than a rhetorical flourish: yellow and black horses satisfy one demand and not the other, and the Advocate treats that difference in what is sought as decisive. On this reading the dialogue is a serious piece of proto-semantics, and the Objector is not a straw man but the voice of the rival conception, on which a name is individuated by the things that fall under it — hence his insistence that having a white horse is having a horse. The dialogue stages a collision between two conceptions of what a name does, and the Advocate wins only on his own conception.

What I want from you:

1. What would count against this? Which textual, structural, and philosophical considerations should move me, sorted from fatal to merely awkward.
2. What would a passage elsewhere in the same corpus have to look like to defeat it? Consider what the chapters on hardness and whiteness, on pointing or designation, and on names and actualities would each have to say, and likewise the later Mohist dialectical material, which may contain replies aimed at arguments of this family. For each, tell me what content would break the reading and what content would leave it standing.
3. The reading supplies a premise the text does not state — that different demands imply different names. Is that charity or invention, and what would let me tell the difference here?
4. Which parts of the position are doing real work, and which are unfalsifiable padding? In particular, could the two-conceptions-of-naming framing absorb any text at all, and if so, is that a fatal defect or an acceptable cost at this level of generality?
5. The received compilation is fragmentary and its chapters are of contested date and integrity, with passages plausibly dislocated and a few words thought lost. Does that damage the position as a reading of the dialogue, as an attribution to a thinker, or both? Does it matter that the School of Names may be a later archival category rather than a movement?
6. What would a defender of the position concede counts against them, as distinct from what a critic would throw at them? And finally: does the position risk anything at all — is there an observation, a passage, or an argument that would make me give it up — or have I stated it so elastically that nothing could?

I would be most moved by a passage in the corpus that I cannot read my way without strain, and by any reason to think the dialogue's earliest readers heard the Advocate as claiming class-exclusion rather than non-identity. Be honest if the answer is that the position is well insulated, and say whether that is a problem for a reading of this kind.
