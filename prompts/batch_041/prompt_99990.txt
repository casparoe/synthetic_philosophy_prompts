I'm in a small reading group that's been working through some decision theory and game theory stuff, and there's a kind of reasoning that keeps coming up that I can't put a name to, and it's bugging me. Here's the sort of thing. We were doing the two-strangers prisoner's dilemma — one shot, no communication, no future, the whole setup where the textbook answer is to defect. A friend of mine refuses to defect. Her argument is: "We're both sitting in the exact same situation with the same information, so if I can see that we'd both be better off cooperating, she can see it too. Whatever I do, she'll do, because we're the same kind of thing in the same spot. So I should cooperate." And honestly it doesn't feel like wishful thinking — it feels like it's actually tracking a reason, even though there's no causal connection between us at all.

Then a few weeks later, someone else made what I think might be a different version of the same move, arguing about a shared-house chore rota: "I'll do my bit, because if everyone in my position did what I'm about to do, we'd all be fine." That one feels less like a prediction about what the other people will actually do and more like a rule about what you could will everybody to do. So now I can't tell if these are the same idea wearing two outfits or two seperate ideas, and whether either of them has an established name. Is there a term for the first kind of reasoning — treating your own choice as bound up with what other people in the same boat will do, even with no causal link? And is that the same as the second, Kant-flavored one where the point is whether the rule could be followed by everyone rather than a guess about what they'll actually do?

Can you act like a philosopher of decision theory and work through this carefully with me? Maybe start by brainstorming the closest concepts you already know, then say which one genuinely fits the first case, which fits the second, and where the edges are — what's essential to each, what's incidental, and what the standard objections are. I really don't want just a label slapped on it; I want the carving, because I keep feeling like the interesting thing is exactly the bit that the labels don't quite cover.
