I'm a PhD student in philosophy of technology and I run a small reading-group podcast; next month's guest has been developing what she calls an "answerability" account of meaningful work. The gist of it: what makes an activity meaningful to the person doing it isn't the product, the skill it demands, or its social contribution, but that the worker is answerable to someone for how it goes -- someone could ask why you did it that way and you'd owe them an answer. So on her view the real threat from automation isn't task displacement, it's what she calls auditing automation, where a system assigns and appraises the work but no human is ever on the other end of the why. She'll say a warehouse where robots do the lifting but a foreman can still challenge you is better for meaning than one where you lift everything yourself while an algorithm times you and you never speak to a person. She also distinguishes being answerable to a supervisor from being answerable to the person who buys the thing, and thinks the gig economy's damage comes from replacing the first with a rating.

Could you draft eight to ten interview questions that don't just get her to restate all that? I want at least one on why answerability should beat the obvious rivals (craft, contribution, autonomy), one on whether she's forced to say that being micromanaged by a person is more meaningful than working alone unsupervised, and one on work nobody consumes -- shes committed to answerable-but-pointless beating meaningful-but-unanswerable, and I want to see how far she'll take it. Give each question a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. British English please, and none of the "what drew you to this" fluff.
