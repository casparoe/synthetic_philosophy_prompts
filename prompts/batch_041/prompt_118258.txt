I got into a long argument last night with a friend of mine -- he's a computational neuroscientist who also messes around with LLMs on the side -- about Searle's Chinese Room, and he thinks the whole thing is a failure of imagination, a fallacy of division dressed up as an intuition. His analogy, which he was extremely pleased with: no single neuron in your head understands English. A neuron doesn't understand anything, it just fires. And yet you understand English. So why is anyone supposed to be impressed that the guy in the room doesn't understand Chinese? The man is the neuron. The rulebook is the synaptic connections. The room is the brain. Zoom out far enough and there's your understanding, right there. Then he wheeled in the old bit about swapping your neurons out one at a time for silicon chips that do exactly the same job -- you'd never notice from the inside, he says -- which he took to prove it's the functional organisation that carries the understanding and not the meat, and therefore the room (and, he slipped in, a big enough language model) understands in pretty much the sense you do.

I didn't have a comeback in the moment but I don't buy it. Can you audit the analogy properly for me? I want the correspondence table first -- what plays the role of the neuron, the synapse, the brain, and the thing that understands -- then the places it breaks, sorted into the disanalogies that actually matter for what he's using it to show versus the ones that are just noise. Two things I'm suspicious of and want you to rule on: (a) a neuron has no point of view to lose, whereas the man in the room obviously does, and I can't tell if that's a real asymmetry or just sentimentality about neurons; and (b) in the brain case we know independently that the system-level understanding is real -- we're the brain, so to speak -- whereas in the room case the system-level understanding is exactly the thing in dispute, so I wonder if the analogy is quietly assuming what it's meant to prove. There's also some reply where the guy just memorises the entire rulebook and does the whole thing inside his head, and I never understood whether that kills the zoom-out move or whether it's a cheap trick. And at the end: does his conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, and if the neuron/brain picture is the wrong frame, whats the better analogy -- if there is one -- for what's going on when something pushes symbols around correctly and still, seemingly, gets nothing?
