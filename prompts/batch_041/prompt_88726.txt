ok so context: I've been hanging around LessWrong and the rat-adjacent blogs since the Sequences were still going up, so when I say I want something aimed at people who've actually thought about this stuff, you know the register I mean. Casual, in-jokey, but the philosophy has to be real.

The topic is Dennett's cranes vs skyhooks thing from Darwin's Dangerous Idea (chapter 3, the Universal Acid one), plus the good reductionism / greedy reductionism distinction he draws there. Old name for the bad one is "nothing-buttery," which I think is a criminally underused phrase and should be brought back. Short version as I understand it: a crane does real lifting work and is itself built out of things that were already lifted; a skyhook lifts for free, from nowhere. Good reduction explains design without skyhooks, greedy reduction wants to explain it without cranes -- it skips whole levels and tries to get from atoms to Shakespeare in one move, which just smuggles a miracle back in from the other direction.

What I want you to write: a Kickstarter-style crowdfunding page for a startup that sells skyhooks. Actual, literal skyhooks. Deadpan the whole way -- no winking, no "of course this is absurd" asides, no narrator who's in on it. The comedy has to come out of the structure of the thing being sold, so that someone who's read Dennett laughs because the page gets the distinctions right, not just because it's silly. A few things I'd love it to smuggle in:

- the skyhook that turns out to require a crane to install, with fine print admitting as much
- the infinite regress: a skyhook that lifts other skyhooks, plus a reward tier where you get to ask who lifted the first one
- a competitor product or stretch goal pitched at the greedy reductionist -- a device that skips every intermediate level so you arrive directly at "it's just atoms," with none of the explanatory work in between
- the "we get this a lot" FAQ entries ("isn't this just a crane?", "isn't it all just atoms anyway?", "is this just God?")
- a Risks and Challenges section, which every crowdfunding page is obliged to have and which for this product is definately hard to write honestly

Please don't turn it into a hit piece on Dennett, and don't let it drift into generic "religion dumb, science good" territory -- the target is the market for free lifting and the "it's just X" move, not any particular group of people. Somebody who's read the book should feel you got the distinction right, including where it's tempting to get it wrong.

Format, in this order:

1. Headline + one-line tagline (~40 words)
2. "The Problem" (~120 words)
3. "How It Works" (~150 words)
4. "Reward Tiers" (~120 words, at least four tiers)
5. "FAQ" (~200 words, 4-5 questions)
6. "Risks and Challenges" (~100 words)
7. Design notes (~150 words): what you were going for, which bits are for people who've read Dennett, and where a Dennett fan might say you got him wrong

Around 800-900 words total. Keep the voice of the page itself flat and sincere the whole way through.
