Design a 60-minute activity for a 15-person upper-level undergraduate seminar in philosophy of language or mind that makes participants feel the force of the rule-following paradox before it is named. One participant is secretly assigned a rule for transforming symbol strings; the others are inspectors who may query the rule-follower with a limited budget of test inputs, then must predict outputs for fresh inputs and stake points on their predictions. Unknown to them, the follower is using a deviant rule that agrees with the obvious rule on every queried case but diverges on the unqueried test cases. In a second round, the inspectors must agree among themselves on a single rule and the follower must follow the community's rule, so the group discovers that agreement, not private insight, is doing the normative work. Then debrief by mapping the rounds onto the idea that any finite sample underdetermines the rule, the distinction between a rule and its observed applications, and the suggestion that communal agreement fixes what counts as following a rule, and be explicit about which metaphysical feature—whether there is a fact of the matter about what rule is meant—the activity cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply. Also include the materials, the payoff scheme, a timeline, the two or three things that will predictably go wrong the first time, and how to handle them. Return the whole design as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
