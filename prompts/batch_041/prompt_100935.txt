I'm a graduate student TA-ing a seminar on epistemic rationality, and for the session on higher-order evidence I want to hand the students a decision tree instead of a reading. The starting point I want every branch to presuppose is a single case-shape, since I don't want us to get stuck arguing about whether such cases are even possible: an agent reasons competently about some first-order question, and then acquires evidence that bears on whether that first-order reasoning was any good without bearing on the subject matter directly. From there I want the tree to sort students into the live positions on what rationality requires of her, and every branch node has to be phrased as a question a student can answer without already knowing which position it drops them into — so no node may simply read "are you a conciliationist?" or "do you accept the enkratic requirement?" I'd like the branch questions to come from the logical space rather than from a list of names: whether higher-order evidence is genuine evidence about the first-order proposition or only about the agent's own reliability; whether the rational response is uniquely determined or merely bounded; whether it can ever be rational to hold a belief while regarding the holding of that very belief as irrational; whether the requirement is a requirement of rationality at all or only of some epistemic virtue like conscientiousness; and whether the standard of assessment is the agent's response in the moment or her dispositional, long-run track record. Build the tree first, note which answer-combinations are unoccupied, and briefly diagnose each empty cell — nobody is there because the view is incoherent, or merely unattractive, or simply unexplored — then place the named positions in the space you've built.
