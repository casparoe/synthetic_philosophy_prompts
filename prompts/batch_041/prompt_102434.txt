I teach a seminar on the morality of promising and every year I end up rebuilding the same handout from scratch, so I want you to do it properly this time. Give me the desiderata for a theory of promissory obligation -- not the theories, the spec: what any adequate account of why promises bind has to be able to do. Concretely, I want the cases it has to get right (the promise made with an ulterior motive, made really just to get someone off your back; the promise whose keeping now requires something the promisor couldn't possibly have anticipated; the promise to someone who has since died; the promise no one would be worse off for breaking because the other party has clean forgotten and won't show; the promise extracted by pressure that falls short of a threat; the promise to do something you shouldn't do; the promise nobody asked for), the distinctions it can't be allowed to collapse (obligation vs. a mere reason to act, the ground of the duty vs. its strength, being bound vs. being blameworthy for breaking it, and -- this is the one I actually care about -- promising vs. our other ways of changing what's owed: consenting, forgiving, commanding, resolving), and what it has to stay consistent with about the practice itself (that it's a human invention, that it's generally useful, that it survives even in cases where it isn't). For each item tell me the case or consideration that motivates it, and then tell me which of these are genuine desiderata and which are just the prejudices of one camp -- I've been reading too much stuff lately that quietly assumes its own answer and then rigs the list to fit.

I especially want you to flag any pair or subset I can't have together, because if two of these can't be jointly satisfied by any theory, that's the result I'm after, not a defect in the list. Same for the one requirement that does the most work excluding candidates -- Hume on one side, the Scanlonians on the other, they can't all pass. You can skip scoring the actual theories against the list, I do that bit in class; keep the weight on the criteria. And tell me how confident you are in what you're claiming here, and single out the one point you're least sure of -- I'd much rather have your hedges than a confident list I can't seperate from my own assumptions.
