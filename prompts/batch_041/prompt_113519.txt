I run a monthly discussion group of about eighteen adults, mostly engineers and scientists, none with formal training in philosophy, and I have no formal background in the subject myself. For a ninety-minute session I want to stage Nozick's entitlement theory so that the group feels the conflict between a historical conception of justice (what people came to hold through voluntary steps) and a patterned conception (some preferred distribution we keep restoring), rather than just hearing the distinction described. My rough plan is a small live economy: everyone begins with an equal endowment, one or two participants are given something genuinely desirable that others will want to buy, and all trades are voluntary. I then want to introduce a rule that resets everyone to equality at the end of each round, and let the group discover what that does to the seller's effort, to the buyers' sense of fairness, and to people who had already gained from earlier transfers.

Please design the full activity: rules, materials, round structure, roles, and a payoff scheme that needs no real money. Build in two wrinkles I care about. First, let some participants form a voluntary redistribution pact among themselves, so the group is forced to separate a pattern that is chosen from a pattern that is imposed. Second, include a small pool of initially unowned resources that participants may claim, so they run into the question of whether a claim leaves everyone else worse off. Then give me a debrief that maps each moment of the activity onto the historical-versus-patterned contrast and the claim that free exchanges will keep upsetting any favored pattern. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle each failure, and be candid about which part of the position the game cannot reproduce and that I will have to explain myself.
