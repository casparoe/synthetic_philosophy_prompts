I keep ending up on both sides of arguments about the precautionary principle and I'd like to figure out what I actually believe, because I suspect my gut is just inconsistent and I've been rationalizing after the fact.

Here's my verdicts on a pile of cases.

1. A chemical plant wants to start discharging a new effluent into a river. There's one decent study saying it screws with fish reproduction at concentrations you could plausibly get downstream. No replication, no proposed mechanism. I'd block the discharge until someone replicates it.

2. A new antibiotic for a resistant infection. The animal data has a hint it might cause a rare heart problem; nothing seen in humans. People are dying now and the alternatives are bad. I'd approve it and monitor closely.

These two feel structurally identical to me — plausible but unproven harm — and I go opposite ways. In 1 I let precaution block a profitable thing; in 2 I let the benefit override precaution. So something else is doing the work and I don't know what it is.

3. Gene-drive mosquitoes to crash a malaria vector. Huge benefit, but the ecological knock-ons are unknown and a release might be irreversible. I'd say don't release it, at least not without some kind of international sign-off. Note that here I'm using precaution to block something that would save a lot of lives, which is the reverse of case 2.

4. Large-scale stratospheric aerosol injection for cooling. I'd treat it as off the table for now. Some people tell me the precautionary move is actually to develop it, because of climate risk, and I can see the logic, but my gut says no.

5. A cheap new additive that would let food last twice as long in places where spoilage is a real cause of hunger. Some people worry about long-term effects nobody has studied. I'd allow it. So here I'm waving through a pretty big unknown.

My half-baked principle, which I'm not attached to: pick the option that avoids the worst plausible outcome, where plausible just means not vanishingly unlikely, unless that option would forgo a benefit that's already certain and large. I can already feel this bending under cases 1 and 5.

What I want: the principle (or a small set of them) that actually fits, stated precisely enough that I could apply it to a new case without asking you again. Tell me which clause each of my verdicts is propping up, what your principle says about cases I haven't listed, and — this is the important bit — which of my verdicts it forces me to give up. If my half-baked version can be repaired rather than thrown out, show me the repair and what it costs.

One new case to test it on: a city wants to add a low dose of lithium to the drinking water on the theory that it reduces suicide rates. The evidence is suggestive but thin, the effect size is contested, and you can't really opt out of your tap water. Where does your principle come down, and does that match or clash with case 3? I'm definately not going to be satisfied with "it depends."
