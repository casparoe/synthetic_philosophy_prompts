What I take from Quine's general picture of knowledge is that our beliefs don't get supported or revised one at a time: they hang together as a single connected body, none of them is immune to revision, and the sharp line between what is true by meaning and what is true by fact doesn't hold up. What I take from his remarks about morality is close to the opposite: that moral judgements are not answerable to observation the way scientific ones are, that there is no shared procedure for settling a moral disagreement the way there is for settling an empirical one, and that moral talk ends up looking less like findings and more like matters of taste, however serious.
