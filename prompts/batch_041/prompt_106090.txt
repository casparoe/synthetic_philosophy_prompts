I've been reading about the socialist calculation debate, and there's a pattern in the way the two sides argue that I can't put a name to, and the labels I already know don't quite fit.

Mises argued that a socialist economy cannot rationally allocate resources because it has no prices for capital goods. Lange answered with a central planning board that sets prices by trial and error, raising them when shortages appear and lowering them when surpluses do, while instructing factory managers to produce up to the point where marginal cost equals price. Once that model is on the table, the quarrel no longer seems to be about whether socialist planning can work at all; it becomes a quarrel about whether this hand-run version of the price mechanism will work well — whether it can track changing conditions, motivate managers, and so on. And then both sides treat the exchange as a win: one says the model shows prices are indispensable, since the planners had to imitate them; the other says it shows planning is feasible, since the planners can imitate them.

I want to know whether this structure has an established name, but the name matters less to me than the carving. It isn't just moving the goalposts, because nobody withdraws a claim — the thesis quietly slides from impossible in principle to impractical in practice, and each side reads the slide as vindication. It isn't a plain refutation either, since the impossibility thesis was never actually defeated by a working example. And it isn't quite motte-and-bailey, because the retreat is not to a modest core that was there all along; it is a relocation of the whole dispute onto a different question. So: what is this move exactly, how does it differ from goalpost-moving, from a proof-of-concept, and from the ordinary in-principle/in-practice distinction, and where are its edges — does the same shape appear, for instance, when a defender of markets concedes a role for state-provided public goods, or when someone defending machine understanding builds a system that mimics the disputed capacity? If no established term fits, please coin one and define it.
