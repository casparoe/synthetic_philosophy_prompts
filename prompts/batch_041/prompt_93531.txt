I sit on the data safety monitoring board for a phase 2 trial of an experimental drug for chronic low back pain. The idea behind it is that pain is basically an alarm, and this drug turns down the alarm rather than the fire — it's meant to blunt the affective sting, not the sensation. At the 12-week interim look the self-report numbers are excellent: pain intensity down, pain interference with daily life down, quality of life up. But the site coordinators notes are weird. Patients on the drug have mostly stopped doing things. One quit his job and says he's fine with it. Two asked for a higher dose because "it makes everything matter less." One wife told a coordinator her husband seems content but isnt really in there anymore. The patients themselves rate their lives as better, none of them wants off the drug, and the sponsor says the endpoint was pre-specified, the effect is large, and the rest is anecdote. Our charter only lets us weigh in on safety, not efficacy, and we meet Thursday.

Write the opinion I'll circulate before that meeting: a concrete recommendation with a time frame (continue as is, continue with a changed or added endpoint, or halt), reasons the sponsor and the regulator could actually respond to, and an honest statement of the sponsor's best case — because if the subjects are content and nobody has been harmed, it's not obvious what we'd be protecting them from. It would also help to say whether "stopped caring about his life" should be logged as an adverse event, since that's what decides whether this is even ours to rule on.
