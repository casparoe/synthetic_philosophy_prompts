A few years ago my older brother got sober, and ever since he keeps saying some version of: 'I know it looks like I gave something up, but you can't see it from where you're standing -- from in here the drinking life looks like nothing and this looks like everything.' My cousin says the same sort of thing about joining a monastery, and a friend of mine says it about having a kid, though she's less preachy about it. What bugs me is that I can't tell if this move is legitimate or if it's a trick that would work equally well for anybody. I can imagine someone giving the exact same speech about a gambling habit, or about getting pulled into a cult -- 'you can't judge from outside' -- and there the fact that they say it doesn't make it true. So it's not just 'you don't know what it's like until you try it'; that's ordinary ignorance and I get it. The part I keep tripping over is that what changes is what counts as a reason in the first place. Before, the reasons they'd offer me were reasons I could weigh against my own; after, they've got a whole different scale, and they use the new scale to say the change was the right call. That feels like it should be circular, and also like it's obviously how some good decisions actually work, and I can't hold both at once.

Is there a name for this? Either the general situation of having to choose something whose value you can only assess using standards you'd only have after the choice, or the narrower argumentative move where someone treats the fact that they can't be understood from outside as evidence that they were right. I assume there's some literature on decisions you can't fully evaluate in advance, and probably something in the addiction/recovery world about whether people's own testimony about their change is trustworthy, but I don't want just the label -- I want the edges. Is the legitimate case and the cult case the same phenomenon with different outcomes, or is there a real structural difference (reversibility? whether the new standpoint can be endorsed from the old one? whether the person could have been wrong in some checkable way)? And is there already a term for the suspicious version specifically -- the one where the new standpoint alot of the time guarantees its own correctness, so no possible experience could count against it? If the honest answer is that no single name covers all this, then give me the nearest few, tell me which fits best and what each one gets wrong, and coin something if you have to.
