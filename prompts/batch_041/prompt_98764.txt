I build knowledge graphs and semantic data models for a living: I spend my days turning messy real-world facts into relational rows and RDF-style triples, and I am fluent in schemas, primary and foreign keys, reification, n-ary relations, hyperedges, joins, and the difference between a typed node and an untyped one. A philosophy-inclined colleague recently told me that the thing I do every day is secretly haunted by something called Bradley's regress, and I would like to know whether that is a genuine problem or a philosopher's illusion. As I understand the worry: a proposition like the cat's being on the mat is supposed to be more than a mere collection of its parts -- the cat, the mat, and the relation of being-on -- because a heap of three things is not yet a claim about anything. Something therefore has to tie the relation to its two relata; but whatever does the tying is itself just one more item, which then needs its own tie to the items it ties, and so on without end. The unity of the proposition is supposed to be whatever stops this regress, and nobody seems to agree on what it is. Please act as an expert in both formal semantics and data/knowledge modeling, think carefully before you begin, and start by setting out for yourself exactly what the regress demands and what it would take to stop it.

Your task is to translate the regress, and the main positions on the unity of the proposition, into the vocabulary I actually use -- relations as tables or triple stores, rows as tuples, keys and foreign-key constraints, reification of a relation into a node, hyperedges, joins, and the schema-versus-instance distinction. Do this position by position rather than in one lump: the view that the relation is itself a genuine entity standing to its relata as they stand to one another; the view that predication is not an additional relation at all but something sui generis; the view that a proposition is unified by being an act or attitude of a mind rather than a structure in the world; and any other major line you think deserves a hearing. For each, say precisely how far it maps onto something I already do -- for instance, does the demand that the relation be tied to its relata correspond to a foreign-key constraint that a cell must point at an existing row, or to something the data model simply presupposes rather than enforces? -- where the translation has to be fudged, and whether anything in the philosophers' debate has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. My instinct is that this is a pseudo-problem: a row in a table is already a unit, and the database never has to tie it together because the tuple simply is one thing. I want you to tell me whether that instinct is right, or whether it is smuggling in exactly the assumption the regress is meant to expose -- that unity is stipulated rather than generated. Be honest about which side of the translation is doing the work, and if the residue shows that my framework cannot even state the worry, say whether that is a defect in my framework or a sign that the original worry was less substantial than it sounds. Please keep it tight and argued rather than padded: no literature review and no citations, just the translation and the residue, reasoned through carefully.
