I'm a backend engineer with zero philsophy training and I run a monthly lunch thing at my company -- whoever shows up, usually 10-14 people, 60 minutes in a confrence room, no prep expected, I bring sandwiches. Next month I want to do archives and power: how the historical record gets built, who ends up in it, and why the holes in it usually aren't anyone being evil. I don't want to give a talk. I want an activity where they feel it happen to them, and I'd rather they never hear the phrase "archival silence" until the debrief.

Here's the seed I want to build on. A silence in the record can get made at four seperate moments. (1) When the thing happens, and nobody with the power to write it down is there, or the only records of it come from people with an interest in it looking a certain way. (2) When the material gets collected and most of it gets thrown out, because you can't keep everything, and the throwing out follows somebody's idea of what counts as important. (3) When the document survives in the box but nobody can find it, because it's described in the wrong words or filed under a name nobody would ever think to search. (4) When later people decide what it all added up to, and the few scraps that made it through get read as the whole story. I want the rules, roles, and materials arranged so the group produces all four of those themselves, in an hour, on paper, at a table -- and I want them to hit number 3 especially, the one where the record is right there in front of you and still invisible.

Give me the rules, the roles, the materials (cheap, printable), the timeline, and then the debrief that maps what just happened back onto those four moments. Also tell me what will predictably go sideways the first time I run it and how to handle it live, and be straight with me about which of the four this format can't really do -- my guess is the first one, since we'll be handing people their documents as index cards instead of making them earn the power to have any.
