I'm a novelist finishing a comic novel set inside a failing research institute, and I need one in-world document from it: the mail-order catalog page, about 600 words, for the Institute's product line of Skyhooks. The distinction the whole joke rests on is the familiar one between cranes and skyhooks. A crane is a mechanism that does real explanatory lifting, but it is itself assembled out of simpler mechanisms, each of which was put in place by something simpler still, so that no part of the load is carried for free and the whole structure ultimately stands on the ground. A skyhook is the other thing: an explanation that lifts its load with nothing supporting it from below, mind-first or top-down, something for nothing.

The Institute sells skyhooks. Give me several models, written in the Institute's own promotional voice, and make each one satirize a real, recognizable bad explanatory move rather than an invented one — a device that accounts for consciousness by declaring it irreducible, a gadget that leaps straight from particle physics to romantic love while skipping every intervening level, and so on. Include at least one product that is really a crane wearing a skyhook's paint job, the item a greedy reductionist buys because he genuinely cannot tell the difference, and let the catalog copy itself betray that he can't.

Keep it deadpan throughout: no winking, no nudging aside to concede that the products are impossible. A reader who knows the crane-and-skyhook distinction should laugh and find the satire fair; a reader who doesn't should still enjoy a catalog of absurd products. Don't name any real living person, and don't quote from any actual text. American English.
