I keep running into the claim that moral compromise between people with different value systems is like trade: each side gives something up and both come out ahead, so there are gains from trade in morality just like in markets. Write an essay on whether that analogy actually holds up, and reach a verdict.

What I want you to really grapple with is that trade needs a common currency or at least an exchange rate -- indifference curves, a willingness to swap x units of one thing for y of another -- and it's not obvious what plays that role when we're talking about different values rather than different amounts of the same value. So: when two people with genuinely different value systems strike a comprimise, is there a real Pareto improvement, or are we just calling it a gain because both of them stopped fighting? Does it help to separate gains from trade from what I'd call gains from peace, the mutual benefit of not wrecking each other? And how much of the gain is actually produced by the compromise itself versus by the threat standing behind it? End with a conclusion please -- illuminating analogy, or smuggled-in commensurability?
