ok so ive ended up as the resident epiphenomenalist in my reading group — the view that experiences are caused by stuff in the brain but dont themselves cause anything — and someone handed me this argument that i think proves way too much but i genuinely cant find the loose thread. heres the reconstruction:

(1) Epiphenomenalism: a quale (the what-its-like of a pain, a red patch) is an effect of some physical brain event, and has no effects of its own.
(2) Every time i think or say "ow, that hurts," that thought or utterance is an event with a complete causal history.
(3) Given (1), that history contains only physical events. The hurting is not in it. Whatever caused me to think "ow," it was the neural goings-on, not the pain itself.
(4) A thought is about, and can be justified by, only what shows up in its causal history — to mean X, or to know X, X has to be part of what produced the thought.
(5) So my thought "ow, that hurts" is really about the neural state and justified by the neural state, not by the experience.
(6) Generalize: none of my beliefs about my own experience are about or justified by experience. A being with the same brain and no inner life at all would believe and say exactly the same things i do about its "expriences."
(7) But i obviously do know when im in pain, and my reports about my own experience are the one case where i have any kind of authority. If (6) holds i dont know what my inner life is like, or even whether there is one.
(8) So epiphenomenalism is false.

i would really like to keep both (1) and (7). which step do i give up, and whats the bill for giving it up? and if the answer is (4) — i have a suspicion it is, but tell me if im wrong — then i need to know what a story about meaning and justification has to look like so that a causally idle experience can still be what my thought is about, and what makes that thought knowledge rather than a lucky guess. does taking that route cost me the zombie intuition, or does it actually save it? id rather keep the view than keep the causal theory of content, but i want to know what im paying.
