I'm a philosophy professor and I've somehow ended up on the review board of a small foundation that funds and approves transmissions into space -- the kind of outfit that decides whether we beam anything out at all, and if so, what and where. We meet once a month, we get maybe ten proposals a year, and the stakes are irreversable enough that everyone at the table gets a little pompous about it. The board keeps arguing from vibes. I want to hand them an actual procedure.

Can you turn the dark forest line of reasoning into a decision procedure the board can run on a proposal? The questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it hands the decision back to a person because the question is about values rather than facts. Keep it usable by people who aren't physicists or game theorists -- they know the idea from the sci-fi novels, they don't know the math. Assume the board has already adopted one working assumption, which they call the quiet-sky premise: nobody seems to be broadcasting at us, and we can't tell whether that quiet means empty, cautious, or occupied.

The hard cases I need it to survive: a narrow one-time beam aimed at a specific nearby star with a detected planet; a permanent omnidirectional beacon announcing that we're here; and a proposal to send only a bare mathematical sequence with no hint that it came from us. Be honest about where the whole thing breaks down -- if the reasoning only bites under assumptions we can't check, say so, and say which steps are just dressed-up fear or hope. Then run the procedure on the first two cases, and tell me where it lands on an answer I won't like.

American English please, and don't pad it with reading lists or citations -- I can't look anything up from here and neither can you.
