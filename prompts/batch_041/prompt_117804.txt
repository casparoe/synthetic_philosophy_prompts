You are a philosopher of biology and of mind, with a special interest in explanations that attribute purposes, functions, or reasons to systems that do not have any, and in the question of when such explanations are illuminating and when they are merely flattering. I want a case analysis, not a survey. Before you write anything, think the case through on the page: begin by brainstorming the candidate rationales that might be at work in the episode below, the candidate bearers of those rationales — individual soldiers, units, pairs of opposing units, the institution of trench warfare itself — and the points at which the mapping is going to be strained. Then commit to an analysis. Be explicit about which claims belong to the theory and which are yours, and flag what the theory is silent about rather than improvising on its behalf.

The episode is the tacit restraint that settled over the quieter sectors of the Western Front in the First World War, the arrangement commonly called live and let live. In stretches of the line where neither side was preparing an attack, opposing infantry worked out, without ever negotiating it, a set of mutual forbearances: firing at fixed times and at known places, aiming high or at unimportant targets, leaving ration parties, working parties, and latrines alone, giving notice of coming shellfire by keeping to a schedule, and matching retaliation to the offence rather than exceeding it. There were no spokesmen and no agreement. The pattern varied from sector to sector, was largely invisible in official records, and collapsed when the tactical situation changed — most sharply when raiding became compulsory and when the artillery on either side began to prepare an offensive. The Christmas truce of 1914 is the famous emblem of the phenomenon, but it is the quieter, longer-running arrangement that I want analysed.

The theory I want you to work with is the notion of a free-floating rationale. The thought is that a behaviour can have a rationale — there is a reason why it works, a reason why it is stable, a reason why departing from it is costly — while no one entertains that reason. The rationale is real, but it floats free of any mind, so that the behaviour can be explained and even predicted by citing reasons without positing a reasoner. The paradigm case is a biological adaptation, where the reason for a trait is supplied by a selection history rather than by the organism's cognition. What I want to know is what becomes of the notion when it is moved to a case like this one.

Specifically: first, who or what is the bearer of the rationale here — the soldier, the unit, the facing pair of units, or the system of trench warfare? Is there a reasoner, and does the theory need one? Second, the rationale in this case is not genetic but strategic and cultural: it is sustained by expectations, by reciprocity, and by what happened to units that broke the pattern. Does the notion survive the transplant, or does it quietly borrow the credentials of its original home, where a selection history can be pointed to that makes the reason authoritative rather than merely predictive? Third, the soldiers evidently did sometimes give reasons for what they were doing, to each other and in letters home, and the restraint was often articulated as a rule about not killing men who were only doing what they were doing. Does that make the rationale unfree — merely under-articulated rather than free-floating — or does it show that the theory needs a category it does not have, namely a rationale that is followed without being entertained as such? Fourth, the theory as I have stated it runs together two things: the reason for a pattern (what explains why it was stable) and the reason the pattern is sensitive to (what would have to change for the behaviour to change). Which of these does the theory actually need, and which does the trench case supply? Fifth, and in both directions: what does the free-floating-rationale reading explain here that a rival does not — an account in terms of norms and reciprocity, or a plain rational-actor story in which each side restrains itself because restraint beats a shooting war it cannot win — and where does the lens distort the case? If the notion turns out to add nothing beyond the observation that this pattern had a function, say so plainly.

I want no citations and no literature review; argue from the case and from the theory as I have stated it, and tell me where the theory has to stop.
