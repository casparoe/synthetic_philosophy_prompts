I'm trying to get my position on consciousness into presentable shape and I'd like a consistency audit rather than a defence. Here is the package.

(1) Illusionism: there are no phenomenal properties. The inner glow, the ineffable what-it's-likeness, is not a feature of anything; it is an artefact of the way introspection represents our own sensory states, and no residue of it survives into the physical story.

(2) The artefact has a determinate content, and that content is what does the explanatory work: introspection represents sensory states as simple, intrinsic and unstructured, concealing their relational and functional organisation. The felt inexplicability of consciousness — why this brain state rather than another, why anything at all — is a downstream effect of that representational simplification rather than a datum pointing to a further fact.

(3) The real problem is the meta-problem: we need a tractable empirical account of why creatures like us are disposed to judge that we are phenomenally conscious. I take cognitive science to be able to supply one, and I take supplying it to be the main task.

(4) First-person reports remain indispensable evidence. A science of consciousness should treat what subjects say about their experience as data about their mental lives, not as noise to be explained away.

(5) The illusion is cognitively inescapable. Even a fully convinced illusionist will go on representing herself as phenomenally conscious, and no amount of theory will make the seemings stop.

(6) Illusionism should be ethically consequential. I want to use it to argue that the apparent mystery of phenomenal feel must not be allowed to block moral concern for insects, fish and AI systems; what matters morally is the presence of valenced representational states, not the presence of a felt quality.

I suspect some of these pull against each other and I can't tell which, or how badly. Please tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, then rank the genuine ones by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, saying for each what would have to give. I'd also like to know which single revision repairs the most and what it costs me elsewhere in the package. One request: don't tell me the set is consistent on the grounds that illusionism has a stock reply to each familiar objection. I want to know where the replies cost more than the view can pay, and where two of these commitments are each independently reasonable but cannot both be motivated in the way I have motivated them.
