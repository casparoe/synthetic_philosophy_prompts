I've been assembling my own position on al-Ghazālī's occasionalism and want a consistency audit before I commit it to paper. Here is the package. (1) Created things have no causal efficacy at all: fire does not burn and bread does not nourish; God is the sole agent, and what we call causal regularities are only the habitual order in which God acts. (2) What settles (1) for me is that at the moment of any event matters could equally have gone otherwise, and a thing that has no will cannot prefer one of two indistinguishable alternatives; only a particularizing will can determine which of them occurs. (3) God's will is eternal and unchanging, and the world is temporally originated: God determined that it should begin when it did, rather than from eternity. (4) Miracles therefore require no special interruption of nature; talk of violating a law of nature was always a misdescription. (5) Empirical and inductive knowledge — medicine, astronomy, the ordinary expectation that the sun will rise — is genuinely reliable, and I take our confidence in it to be knowledge rather than mere custom.

Tell me which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how badly, ordered by severity rather than as a flat list. For each real tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost me, and then say which single revision would clean up the most.
