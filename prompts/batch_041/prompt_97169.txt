Write a poem in exactly five numbered stanzas, with no introduction or conclusion; each stanza should be a single sentence broken across lines. The speaker is the auditor of a hedonic ledger in a society that has adopted lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: pleasures are minted as mild units and pains as severe units, and the ledger's one unbreakable rule is that no column of mild units, however tall, can cancel a single severe unit. Let the first stanza establish the exchange rate, the second record the first severe entry, the third and fourth pile up mild pleasures in ever larger quantities, and the fifth give the auditor's verdict. Make the form carry the argument: use a refrain that states the threshold rule, let it hold unchanged while the pleasures accumulate, and break or refuse it only in the final stanza, so that a reader who accepts the ledger and a reader who believes pleasure and pain are commensurable can each hear their position in the poem. Keep the diction concrete -- ledgers, columns, mint marks, audits -- and do not resolve the tension between the two readers. Do not cite or name real philosophers or works.
