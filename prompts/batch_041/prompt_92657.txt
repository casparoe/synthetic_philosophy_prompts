I retired from atmospheric science a couple of years ago and have been catching up on moral philosophy, mostly by argueing with my old colleagues and with my nephew who's doing a PhD in it. Here's what's tying me in knots. I spent thirty years telling people that the extra tonne of CO2 from one more flight is a drop in the bucket and yet that the bucket is the whole problem, and now I can't decide whether that's a confession that my own individual flying never harmed anyone, or a confession that I was using the wrong unit of analysis all along. Same story with a burger, same story with a vote. The nephew says there's a name for my worry, something about inefficacy, but every time I push him he gives me a different answer, and half the answers seem to be about what kind of thing a "we" is, which is not a question I feel equipped to answer. What I'd like from you is a decision tree — or a quiz with a scoring key, whichever fits the material better — that takes a person from the plain facts about these cases to an actual named position, so I can work out which one I've been holding all these years without knowing it.

Build it from the questions that genuinely do the separating, phrased so somebody can answer them without already knowing which position they lead to. I think the first split has to be whether a single token action ever changes the outcome at all; if you answer never, the next split should be whether that's even the relevant question, because some of what I've read seems to say the act can be wrong for reasons that have nothing to do with the outcome. After that, branch on: whether the individual contribution is ever necessary for the harm; whether it can be sufficient; whether the harm arrives in lumps (you cross a threshold) or comes in degrees all the way down; whether the right unit of assessment is the act, the rule, or the practice; whether a "we" can carry an obligation that doesn't distribute to any of its members; and whether the honest upshot is that the whole site of obligation should shift from private consumption to political action. Use four test cases and keep them concrete: one more long-haul flight; one more beef burger from a butcher who has already ordered the meat; a vote in a seat that's safe by a mile; and a case I keep inventing to annoy the nephew — a hundred walkers crossing a fragile alpine meadow that dies on the thousandth footstep, where the group lays down a thousand and one footsteps in total, so no single walker's step was necessary for the meadow's death. I want the leaves to carry the name of the position where there is one and "unnamed" where there isn't; if two leaves come out behaviourally identical on my four cases, please say so rather than pretending the tree separates them; and tell me which combinations of answers nobody seems to occupy, and whether that's because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply hasn't been explored. Keep it to something like a dozen nodes, so I can draw it on the back of an envelope and pin it above my desk.
