I've been messing with a little double-pendulum sim on my laptop and it's thrown up something I don't have a word for. If I nudge the starting angle by a millionth of a degree the two runs seperate after a minute or so, which I expected — fine, butterfly effect, I get it. But it's how they come apart that I can't get over. The diverged run isn't garbage. The energy is right, it still swings the way a double pendulum swings, every instant of it is a perfectly possible state of the same system. It's the same movie with the timing knocked out — flips happen at the wrong moment, the whole thing's out of phase — but nothing in it couldn't have happened. So the sim isn't exactly wrong. It's just describing a differnt afternoon than the one I had. And when I try to say how long my forecast is good for, it depends entirely on which question I'm asking. How much energy in ten minutes: good forever. When does it flip next: useless after about a minute. What fraction of the time is it upside down: fine again, comes back quick. One error, three totally different verdicts, and no single number that measures the "predictability" of the thing. And if I show someone the bad run and say the model's off, they can't point to anything in it that couldn't have happened. There's nothing there to falsify. It's a real trajectory of the same equations, just not mine.

So, is there a name for this? Not the butterfly effect — I know that one. I mean (a) one and the same error being fatal for one question and harmless for another, so that "predictability" isn't one quantity at all, and (b) a chaotic forecast being flatly wrong while nothing in it is impossible, so the usual "does it match the data" test doesn't seem to bite the way it does elsewhere. If the parts have names I want them; if the combination doesn't, say so and then have a go at naming it yourself, with the edges — where does it stop being true? My sim is a closed box, no noise, nothing pushing it. Does any of this survive a system that's getting shoved around from outside, or one where I can only ever measure the starting state to twelve digits? And does it matter that the thing is deterministic? Everyone says chaos isn't randomness and I believe them, but from where I'm sitting the practical upshot looks the same, and I can't tell if that's a real distinction or just a way of talking.
