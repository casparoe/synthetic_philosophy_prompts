Here's the sketch I had in mind, but scrap it if you've got something that does the job better. A trust market. Everyone writes a few short messages on index cards -- advice, a rumor, a claim about how our deploy pipeline actually works -- and they all go into a pool. Each round, people spend a limited budget of trust tokens on the messages they'd actually act on. By round 2 or 3 the pool is contaminated: some entries are still human, some are recombined from earlier entries, and nobody is told which. Then the mechanic I care about most: people can pay to sign/watermark their own messages. It's cheap but not free, and it's per message, so the individually rational move is to skip it while everyone else keeps paying -- and the medium degrades even though no single person was fooled by anything in particular. I want the room to feel that the harm lands on the shared thing rather than on the individual dupe, and then to argue about whether Dennett's fix (mark the fakes, make passing along unmarked fakes illegal, hold the vendors liable) is the right shape of fix or whether it just relocates the free-rider problem somewhere else.
