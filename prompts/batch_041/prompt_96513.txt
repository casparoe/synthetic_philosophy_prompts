Me and a friend are in a reading group on AI safety and we keep going round in circles on the same thing. Everyone says an agent is corrigible if it lets you shut it down, and everyone also says corrigibility isnt the same as being indifferent to shutdown, but I cant actually picture a case where those two claims live together — every example I try either makes corrigibility just mean "doesnt care" or makes it just mean "has been told to obey", and both of those feel like theyre missing the point. So build me the case, properly, the way youd engineer an experiment.

Two agents, held fixed in everything except their preferences — same beliefs, same information, same abilities, same training, both of them some unglamorous piece of software doing unglamorous work (a scheduler, a recommender, a warehouse robot, nothing with plans for the galaxy). Agent A has no terminal preference whatsoever about whether it keeps running, and yet reliably takes steps that stop it being shut down. Agent B wants to keep running, terminally and badly, and yet reliably lets itself be shut down. Constraints: no deception, no threats, no self-modification, no acausal or multiverse reasoning, and neither agent is secretly reasoning "this is probably a test". Also both of the obvious cheats are off the table — A's resistance cant come from it being mistaken or confused about what shutting down does, and B's compliance cant just be an overriding terminal preference for obeying shutdown commands sitting on top of everything else; whatever makes B comply has to come out of the structure of its survival preference itself. One paragraph each for the two agents, then walk me through which feature of the two preference orderings is actually doing the work (is it what the preferences are over — existing, versus being a certain way while existing — or where the shutdown sits relative to something the agent already values, or something I havent thought of?), and then delete that feature and tell me whether A collapses into B or whether the cases just sit there unchanged. The whole point is that corrigibility isnt readable off behaviour, so dont just write down two agents that behave the way I asked and call it a day — the behaviour has to fall out of the preferences, and if it doesnt, say so instead of papering over it. Finish by telling me how someone who thinks corrigible and indifferent-to-shutdown come to the same thing would try to take these cases apart, and whether theyd be right. And say how confident you are in all this and flag the one step youre least sure about.
