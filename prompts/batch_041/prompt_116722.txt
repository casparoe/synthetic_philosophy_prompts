I have been arguing with a friend who works in machine learning about whether mental states can be multiply realized, and I would like a careful audit of the analogy he keeps falling back on rather than a verdict. His view is that the mind is to the brain as software is to hardware: the mental states are the program, the neurons are the substrate, and just as the same program can run on silicon, on a mechanical calculator, or on a trained network, the same belief or the same pain could be realized in a brain, in a computer, or in something no one has built yet. From this he draws two conclusions -- that psychology is autonomous from neuroscience, in the sense that a complete theory of mental states could be written without ever mentioning neurons, and that any two systems implementing the same program would thereby share a mental life. I find the picture compelling and I also suspect it is doing more argumentative work than it can bear, and I want to know exactly where the load is being carried by the analogy itself.

Please lay out the correspondence explicitly -- what is supposed to map onto the program, onto the hardware, onto the implementation relation, onto the input-output profile, onto whoever or whatever writes the specification -- and then tell me where the mapping breaks down. I am especially interested in the disanalogies that bear on his two conclusions: that ordinary software has a specification authored by someone with a purpose in mind, that it is individuated by its syntax rather than by any subject matter it is about, that the very notion of the same program is relative to a chosen level of description and an implementation mapping, and that running a program is not by itself sufficient for understanding or for there being anything it is like to be the system. Are these fatal to the autonomy claim or beside the point? Separate the disanalogies that matter for his argument from the ones that are merely picturesque, tell me whether the conclusions survive once they are on the table or whether the analogy was carrying them the whole time, and if some better analogy covers the part of the picture that does survive, give me that as well.
