I'm in a political theory seminar and we've spent three weeks on the problem of dirty hands, and I want to close the last session with a poem instead of another summary. Heres what I care about. Not the easy version, where the politician faces a hard call and it turns out okay, and not the cynic's version where politics is just dirty and thats that. The version that interests me is the one where the act really is wrong — wrong in the sense that if a private person did it wed call it a crime, not wrong just in the sense that it made some people angry — and yet in the circumstances it's the thing that has to be done, and doing it does not get you off the hook. You come out the other side owing something you cant repay. And then the part I keep getting stuck on: the demand that you acknowledge it, that you not launder it into a policy win or a necessary sacrifice, that you carry the guilt visibly rather than privately. Is that acknowledgment a duty or a trap? Because the person who makes a display of their guilt is also, in some way, still profiting from the deed — its the same act, one more time, only now it buys them a conscience.

So: a poem, in the voice of the actor, ten or fifteen years on, speaking to someone who wasnt there — a junior colleague, a journalist, a grandchild, you pick, but pick one and stay with them, the choice should show. Form: a pantoum, four or five quatrains — second and fourth lines of each stanza come back as the first and third of the next, and the last stanza folds the opening lines back in so the poem ends on the line it began with. Thats the whole reason for the form: a line that sounds like a defence the first time it comes around should sound like something else the next time — a boast, a confession, a plea youd make to a tribunal — and I dont want you to tell me that the two readings differ, I want the repetition to do it. Rhyme if it helps you, but the returns matter more than the rhyme. Let the other person interrupt somewhere, with the obvious objection: if it was really wrong, what does your feeling bad about it change? and let the answer be worse than either of them wants.

Accuracy matters more than beauty here, so: no vague regret, no hard choices in general, no naming any philosopher and no using the phrase dirty hands in the poem, and no epigraph. The circumstances should be specific — a peace bought by letting a killer walk, a siege ended by killing the wrong people, a deal with someone youd have prosecuted last year, something like that, one of them, concretely. A political theorist should be able to read the poem and see their own argument in it, and someone who has never heard of any of this should still get a good poem.
