I keep arguing with a friend who says 'qualia' is a word for nothing, and I want to pin down what I actually think, so here are my gut verdicts on when my experience of something has changed and when only my take on it has.

Black coffee: used to be an ordeal, now it's the best part of the morning, and the bitterness itself is milder. Experience changed. My nan's fruitcake: used to make me gag, now I eat it and feel warm, but the taste is exactly as cloying as it always was, I just love it now because of her. Experience same, attitude changed. A song I've heard 200 times: I can suddenly pick out a bassline I never noticed, and I'd say I now hear something I literally wasn't hearing before, even though the sound hitting my ears is identical. Two years after a bad cold wrecked my smell, everything smells faintly of burnt plastic underneath; my beliefs and preferences are untouched, I just want to smell normally again. Experience changed, and this is the one I'd stake anything on. Same wine poured from an $8 bottle and an $80 one: it tastes richer when I think it's dear, and I still want to say the experience changed, though I know nothing in the glass did. Learning a painting is a forgery: it looks flatter and more gaudy afterwards. Experience changed, same as the wine.

My working guess is that it's a real change of experience when I can't undo it by reminding myself of the facts, and only an attitude change when I can. But that says the wine case is an attitude change, and the fruitcake one is an experience change, which is the reverse of what I said, so the guess is probably junk.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? What would it say about the glasses that supposedly let colourblind people see red and green for the first time? And if the principle ends up being about my judgments, memory and attention rather than some inner property of the experience, say so plainly -- is my friend right that qualia is an incoherent posit? Dont hedge: give me a definite position, and tell me which of my verdicts you'd overturn.
