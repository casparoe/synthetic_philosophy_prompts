I'm a third-year PhD student in decision theory, and my working vocabulary is the one you'd expect: preference relations over lotteries, utility representations, continuity and Archimedean-style axioms, lexicographic orders, incomplete preferences, and the various ways a preference structure can fail to admit a real-valued representation at all. Over coffee after a seminar on social choice, a friend in the philosophy department laid out a position she calls lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism, and I have been unable to work out how much of it I can actually say in my own language. As I understand her, the view has three parts: pleasure and pain are not measured on a common scale, so the question of how much enjoyment would justify a given amount of suffering has no answer; the reduction of suffering is nevertheless lexically prior to the promotion of enjoyment, at least up to some threshold beyond which ordinary trade-offs become admissible again; and all of this is a claim about what is good rather than a policy about what to do. I am reasonably confident I have her view right, and I am equally confident that when I try to restate it I am quietly replacing it with something else.

What I would like from you is a translation of that position into my vocabulary, done as faithfully as you can manage, followed by a residue report. Go component by component — the incommensurability of pleasure and pain, the lexical priority, the threshold, and the distinction between the axiology and the decision rule — and for each one tell me what carries over cleanly, what survives only if I distort it, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Be explicit about which piece of formal machinery you are reaching for at each step, and about where that machinery forces a choice she never made. The threshold in particular seems to me to admit at least three treatments: a very steep but continuous trade-off in a single real-valued utility function, a genuine lexical break that no real-valued representation can capture, or something that only a non-Archimedean or otherwise non-standard construction can carry. I would like to know what each of those treatments quietly gives up, and whether the choice among them is a substantive disagreement about value or merely a modelling convenience. Then give me your verdict on the residue: where the translation fails, is that because my vocabulary is too coarse to express a distinction she is genuinely drawing, or because the original claim was doing less work than it sounded like it was doing?
