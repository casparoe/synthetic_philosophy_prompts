I've been reading Anscombe's moral and political essays for years, and I want to check whether I've actually internalized the shape of her judgments rather than just the slogans. Here is the track record I've assembled from her writings and public interventions. She held that Truman's decision to bomb Hiroshima and Nagasaki was murder, and she treated the congratulatory attitude toward him as a symptom of moral confusion. She held that the deliberate area bombing of German cities was murder. She did not think that a soldier who kills an enemy combatant in battle is a murderer, nor that a commander who bombs a munitions factory is a murderer merely because he foresees that some civilians nearby will die. She thought that killing a hostage to force a concession is murder, and that a threat to kill the innocent as a means of deterrence is itself wrong, even if the threat is never carried out. She also thought that a person may use lethal force against an unjust attacker in self-defense, and that this is not murder, even if the attacker's death is foreseen. Now consider a case I've been chewing on. A military commander has two options. Option A: launch a cyber operation that will permanently disable a hospital's power grid in an enemy city, causing the deaths of many patients who depend on life support. The commander's aim is not to kill those patients; his aim is to force the city's surrender by demonstrating that the enemy government cannot protect its own civilians. He knows that the hospital deaths are the mechanism by which the surrender will be forced. Option B: bomb a military command bunker, which will kill the enemy leadership but will also foreseeably kill a number of civilian staffers who work in the same building. The bunker strike would not end the war as quickly, and more soldiers would die in the continued fighting. What would Anscombe say about each option? I'm less interested in the verdicts than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, and which further case would best tell them apart. I'm trying to build a decision procedure that would let me predict her on new cases, and I'd like you to stress-test it.
