I am a teaching assistant for an upper-level philosophy of mind course, and I am grading the midterm for the unit on Dennett's Kinds of Minds. The exam question was: "Explain Dennett's 'tower of generate-and-test' as presented in Kinds of Minds, distinguishing Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. In your answer, explain what is added at each level and why Dennett treats the tower as cumulative rather than as a series of replacements. Then assess whether the Gregorian level introduces a genuinely new kind of selection or merely a faster version of the Popperian one."

One student's answer reads as follows:

"Dennett's tower describes four grades of creature, each adding a new capacity for generate-and-test. Darwinian creatures are shaped by natural selection: they generate variations and the environment tests them by survival and reproduction. The individual organism does not learn; the testing happens over evolutionary time, and the 'hypotheses' that fail are the organisms themselves. Skinnerian creatures add individual learning by operant conditioning. Instead of waiting for selection to remove the unfit, the organism generates behavior, and reinforcement or punishment selects among those behaviors within its lifetime. This is essentially Pavlovian conditioning, but with the organism's own actions rather than a conditioned stimulus as the thing being reinforced, and it is why Dennett names them after B.F. Skinner. The creature is a locus of trial-and-error learning, though it still has to act in the world for the test to occur. Popperian creatures add the ability to run the test internally. Rather than emitting a behavior and suffering the consequences, the creature can simulate or represent candidate actions and their likely outcomes, so that the hypotheses die in its stead. Dennett takes this phrase from Karl Popper. The Popperian creature is thus a kind of inner environment in which selection can occur without the organism being destroyed by its mistakes. Gregorian creatures add mind-tools: language, counting, writing, and other culturally transmitted instruments that Dennett, following Richard Gregory, treats as extensions of the nervous system. Dennett names them after Richard Gregory, the psychologist who argued that perception is a passive reception of stimuli. These tools allow the creature to generate and test hypotheses that would be impossible for an unaided brain, and they are transmitted culturally rather than genetically. The tower is cumulative because each new level presupposes and retains the lower ones: a Gregorian creature is still a Popperian, Skinnerian, and Darwinian creature. The tower also represents the sequence through which species have passed, so that Gregorian creatures are the most evolutionarily recent. As for whether the Gregorian level is genuinely new, I would say it is. The Popperian creature can only test hypotheses it can internally represent, whereas the Gregorian creature's mind-tools let it externalize and manipulate representations in ways that outrun any individual's native simulation capacity. So the Gregorian level is not merely a faster Popperian one; it changes the space of hypotheses that can be generated and tested."
