Me and my mate Dee have been going round in circles on this for years and its starting to annoy me, so I want a straight answer about where she's going to land next. Her thing is: if you can picture a thing happening without the picture breaking down into nonsense halfway through, then it's a real possibility. That's the test, she reckons. Nothing else to check. Mine is: I can picture all sorts, that doesn't make it so.

Here's everything I've got out of her over the years.

1. A creature that does everything a person does — holds down a job, tells jokes, worries about its mum — but with nobody in there, no experience at all. She says she can picture that no bother, and that it's a genuine possibility, not just a story. Her line is: how would you ever tell?

2. Water that isn't H2O. That one doesn't count, she says. What you're picturing is a liquid that looks and tastes and behaves like water, and that's a different thing from water. She changed her mind on this one years ago, after she found out water just is H2O, and now she thinks anyone who says "but I can imagine water that isn't H2O" is picturing a lookalike and calling it water.

3. Something that's completely red and completely green all over at once. She says you can't picture it — the words don't add up to a picture — so there's nothing there to test.

4. A rock that just starts moving on its own, nothing pushing it, nothing making it happen, no reason for it at all. She says you can picture that — you just watch the rock slide — and she thinks it's possible; she says the idea that something has to have made it happen is in us, not in the rock.

5. A universe with no beginning, just always going. Pictureable, so possible.

6. Someone who popped into existence five minutes ago with a full set of memories, a scar on their knee, a house full of photos, and no life before that. Pictureable, so possible. "The memories are all there. It's the past that's missing."

Now the one I want to put to her: a world where the sea is blue and the grass is green and the sunset is red, all of it exactly as real as it is here — but nothing in that world can see. No eyes, no minds, no one, and never has been. Not that the colours are greyed out or missing. They're just not being looked at, ever.

What I want from you, in this order, with these headings:

- What she's running: the rule she's actually going by, a couple of sentences.
- The rules that still fit: the different rules that all fit everything above, and what each one predicts about the blue sea world — a paragraph or two. I've a feeling there's more than one that fits.
- My call: what she'll say about the blue sea world, one paragraph.
- Why: your reasons for that call, a paragraph or two.
- The case to put to her next: the one that would show which rule she's really on, two or three sentences.

Don't hedge. Pick one, back it, and say what would change your mind. And British English please — I can't stand "analyze".
