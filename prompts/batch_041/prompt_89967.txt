A theory that arranges minds into ascending grades of cognitive sophistication has been proposed in several forms, and before I weigh any particular version of it I want a specification of what any such theory must deliver. The family of views I have in mind orders creatures roughly as follows: those whose responses are fixed at birth and shaped only by natural selection; those that adjust their behaviour through reinforcement and extinction; those that can model a situation internally and test a course of action against the model before committing to it; and those that think with tools, symbols, and information inherited from others rather than learned afresh from their own experience. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of this tower must satisfy.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. I want at least these: the kinds of creature the taxonomy must classify correctly, including one organism that appears to straddle two grades at once and one collective or artefactual system — a colony, a firm, a trained artificial agent — whose placement the theory must settle; the distinctions it must not collapse, for instance between the capacity to learn and the capacity to represent, between the locus of a grade in an individual and its locus in a lineage, and between the four grades as stages of a single life and as modes that one organism can occupy in different domains simultaneously; and the results from biology and from the study of learning it must not contradict, in particular the continuity of evolutionary descent and the fact that reinforcement and internal modelling are not mutually exclusive mechanisms.

Then sort the list. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are parochial demands of one camp rather than genuine conditions of adequacy. Where some subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied — I suspect the demand for a single ascending order sits badly with the straddling organism and with the collective case — say so explicitly and say which member of the inconsistent set you would give up, since that is the part I most want to see. Finally, identify the single requirement that does the most to rule out otherwise attractive theories, and say briefly how the four-grade tower fares against the full list. Keep the weight on the criteria and the cases behind them; a short verdict on the tower itself is enough.
