I just got a plot at the community garden a few blocks from me, after two years on the waitlist, and now that I'm on the inside I can't stop noticing how the whole thing is actually run. There's an annual fee per plot. There's a mandatory work day every spring and fall, and you loose your plot if you skip two in a row. There's a rule that you can't let your bed go to seed or let the weeds creep into the paths. There's a waiting list so long that people treat their plots like heirlooms and hand them down to friends. And there's a shed fund that everyone is asked to chip into, but nobody is ever charged for, and nobody ever checks. The people who run it keep telling me the work days are the whole point, because if everyone just pays the fee and never shows up, the place falls apart. Which is definately true, but I'm not sure it's the same problem as the one the weeding rule is about.

What I want from you is the theory of the commons that's baked into how this thing actually works -- not the one they'd recite if you asked them, the one the rules commit them to. Specifically: which failure mode is each rule aimed at? I can see two different ones floating around. One is people taking more than their share of the thing itself -- the good soil, the water, the shared tools, the one sunny corner. The other is people not putting in what the place needs to keep existing -- the work days, the shed fund, the fence repair. Are those actually two different problems with different fixes, and does this garden's rulebook treat them as if they were the same one? Because the fee, the waiting list and the don't-let-it-go-to-seed rule look like they're doing one kind of work, and the work days and the shed fund look like they're doing another, and I honestly can't tell if that's a coherent package or just a pile of habits that happen to have grown up next to each other.

Also tell me what the rules don't settle. My guess is that most of them would be fine under a picture where people are basically self-interested and the rules just change the payoffs, and also under a picture where the rules exist mainly to teach and reinforce a norm of membership -- and I want to know which rules are actually evidence for one of those pictures versus which ones just fit both. And where is the whole arrangement underdetermined? Are there rival stories about what's going on that would license exactly the same rulebook?

One more thing, because I have to argue about this at the next general meeting: when they say "if everyone just pays the fee the place falls apart," who is "everyone"? Me, my household, my plot, the whole garden? The rules seem to switch between those without warning, and I suspect that's doing more work than anyone realizes. So: if you had to swap the implicit picture for the other one -- if we decided the real problem was never overuse but always upkeep, or the reverse -- which rules would have to go, which would have to change shape, and which would stay exactly as they are because they were doing something else entirely the whole time?
