I've spent the last two weeks going back and forth with a friend from my jurisprudence seminar about why promises bind, and she's a thoroughgoing consequentialist — she thinks the only reason to keep a promise is that breaking it usually makes things worse down the line, and that if you could secretly break one and make everyone better off you should just do it. I keep telling her that can't be the whole story, but I've realized I've never actually written my own view down, so before I go back at her I want a consistency check. Here's roughly where I've landed, and I'm handing it over raw so you can tell me whether it's a package or just a pile of hunches I happen to like: (a) what makes a promise bind is the promisor's own act of undertaking the obligation — giving your word does it by itself, rather than being a prediction or a warning about consequences; (b) the obligation is owed to the promisee specifically, not to the world or to yourself, so if the promisee lets you off the hook it's gone, and only they can do that; (c) whether the promisee actually relied on you, or would even be hurt by a breach, doesn't change whether you're bound — you can wrong someone by breaking a promise they never find out about, and you can still be bound even when breaking would do more good overall; (d) a promise obtained by coercion or serious deception isn't binding at all — not "you have an excuse" but "no promise was ever made"; (e) there's nothing magic in the words themselves, and a promise only exists where there's the social practice of promising and the promisee understood that you were putting yourself on the hook; and (f) you can't release yourself from your own promise, only someone else can, which I take to show the whole thing is relational rather than a matter of your private integrity.

So what I want from you is an audit rather than a lecture. Which of these actually pull against each other, and how badly? I'd like them ordered by how severe the tension is instead of a flat list of "these are all in tension," and for any pair that only looks conflicted, say why it isn't — I'd rather you discriminate than flag everything. For each real conflict, tell me the cheapest thing to give up or modify and what that would cost me, and if there's one revision that cleans up the most at once, say which. A lot of my friends find the "you can wrong someone who never finds out" bit the most counterintuitive, but I'm not asking you to defend it, just to see if it survives contact with the rest.

One more thing: be honest about which parts of this are things people who actually work on promising agree on versus where I'm just staking out a side in a live dispute. I don't want to walk into seminar presenting some contested position as if it were the obvious consensus view, so please seperate the settled bits from the genuinely up-for-grabs ones as you go.
