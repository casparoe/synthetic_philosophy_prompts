1. I teach a 90-minute session on consciousness to about 16 undergrads and I want a runnable game where they end up feeling the force of the split-brain challenge -- that the "one experiencer" feeling might be something the brain builds rather than something it finds. Don't let me name split-brain or commissurotomy until the debrief.

2. The setup: groups of four become a single "agent" -- two of them get different restricted views of what's going on (each sees only their own feed/cards), exactly one of them is allowed to talk, exactly one is allowed to act, and they can only pass notes through a tiny channel with a strict word limit. Give me the rules, the materials (index cards, envelopes, whatever's cheap), and a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money but is juicy enough that they actually want to win.

3. The crux: arrange it so the person who acts is set off by a cue that only the silent, non-talking side can see, and then the talking person has to explain out loud, to the room, why they did it. I want them confidently making up a reason for an action they had zero access to, so build in a step where the explanation gets demanded before anyone is allowed to compare notes.

4. Debrief that maps what happened onto the idea: the narrator/interpreter that supplies reasons after the fact, confabulation, why the felt unity never wobbles from the inside, and the difference between unity of access and unity of agency. Also tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time -- someone refuses to confabulate and just says "I don't know why," someone declares "we're just acting," someone breaks the channel rule -- and what I should do in each case.

5. And be honest about what the game can't reproduce: no arrangement of four undergrads gets anywhere near the actual first-person feel of a patient whose hemispheres have been seperated, and the real patients mostly don't feel divided at all, so I'll have to supply that part myself. Say which bit that is and how to land it without turning the last ten minutes into a lecture.
