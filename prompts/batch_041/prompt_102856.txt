I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of mind, and I want to audit my own position before a job talk, where I expect to be pressed on the empirical literature about introspection. Here is roughly where I've landed. I would like you to tell me whether the package hangs together.

(1) Introspection — our capacity to attend to and report on our own current mental states — is a reliable source of evidence in ordinary conditions. Not infallible, but its track record is good enough that first-person reports should be treated as data in cognitive science rather than as mere verbal behavior to be explained away.

(2) There is a kind of first-person authority that is not a matter of reliability at all. When I sincerely and reflectively avow that I believe that p, I am not issuing a fallible prediction about an inner state that could be checked against independent evidence; the avowal expresses, and arguably helps constitute, the belief.

(3) The confabulation findings — people's confident reports about why they chose or judged as they did, which come apart from the actual manipulations — show that we have essentially no reliable introspective access to the causes of our judgments, preferences, and behavior.

(4) I accept the transparency of belief: we do not come to know our own beliefs by turning inward and inspecting a mental object; we come to know them by looking outward, at the world as we take it to be, and reading off what we are thereby committed to.

(5) I also think our access to occurrent experience is best modeled on perception: there is a distinctive, non-inferential channel through which experiences are presented to us, and it is this channel that underwrites whatever epistemic standing introspection has.

(6) Finally, I think the content of introspective reports is heavily shaped by the concepts the introspector has available and by the culturally transmitted theory of mind she has absorbed, so that two people with different conceptual repertoires can introspect what is, in some sense, the same state and produce reports between which there may be no fact of the matter as to which is more accurate.

Please tell me which of these are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and which single commitment I should weaken or drop to buy the most consistency for the least cost. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and for each say what repairing it would cost me. I would also like you to be explicit about which of these claims are settled among people who work on introspection and which are genuinely contested — I do not want a contested position presented to me as if it were the consensus.
