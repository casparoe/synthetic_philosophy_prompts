My seminar keeps tossing around the idea that the self is a center of narrative gravity, and last week someone used it to shut down a question about whether someone with advanced dementia is still the same person — the story frays, so the self frays, case closed. I've also heard it used to mean there's no self at all, and to mean the self is just whatever story you happen to believe about yourself. Those can't all be the same claim. I've heard people say the self is a fiction, but also that fictions can be real patterns, and I cant tell if that's a real distinction or a dodge. Can you seperate out the different precise claims that one-liner could be making — from the boringly true to the genuinely radical — and for each say whether it's plausible, whether it's a claim about what exists or just about how we talk, and whether it would actually support the move my seminar made about dementia? I want a menu I can bring back, not one verdict. Also tell me which reading people probably mean when they repeat it, and which reading a card-carrying materialist could still accept without blinking.
