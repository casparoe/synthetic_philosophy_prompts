Write a deadpan mock judicial opinion — about 650 words — from an invented tribunal (say, the Standing Committee on the Recognition of Persons) ruling on a petition that a single ant colony, nicknamed Aunt Hillary, be recognized as one legal person, with standing to sue and to be compensated for damage to its nest. Counsel for the colony argues that the colony's foraging, division of labor, and chemical signaling realize the same functional organization as a mind, so that on any substrate-neutral criterion of mentality the colony has beliefs, preferences, and something like a point of view; counsel for the ministry replies that whatever is happening is either a mere aggregate of ants or, if it is a mind at all, then thousands of minds, none of which is the colony. Have the committee take both arguments seriously and reason honestly to whatever conclusion the functionalist criterion actually forces, including the awkward ones: whether the colony is one subject or many, whether something can be composed of persons without itself being one, whether the same reasoning obliges the committee to recognize a market, a city, or a corporation as a person, and what the colony could be owed if it can genuinely be wronged. Keep the register flat and bureaucratic; the humor has to come from the tribunal following the logic rather than from mocking it, and a philosopher who works on functionalism or collective mentality should find the reasoning fair rather than a strawman. Do not break the frame or wink at the reader. Then add a section titled Notes on the satire (about 150 words) explaining the philosophical points the piece was built to expose and the choices you made in writing it.
