I host a small interview podcast that grew out of a reading group; the audience is rationalist-adjacent autodidacts who know Berlin's distinction between negative and positive liberty and the rough shape of the republican revival, and not much past that. Next month's guest is a political theorist who has spent about a decade arguing that freedom as non-domination is better understood as answerability than as control: a power is non-arbitrary, on his view, when those subject to it can compel its holder to give an account and have that account challenged, even if they cannot remove him or veto what he does — the capacity to depose, he says, is a liberal fetish rather than a requirement of freedom. He also has a signature move, the claim that freedom is a status rather than a level, which he reaches for whenever an objection turns probabilistic.

Draft eight to ten questions that get past restatement. I want at least one each on: the master who neither interferes nor threatens to, and how his account explains that unfreedom without falling back on what might happen, given that freedom is supposed to be a status; what 'arbitrary' does in his account that 'interference' could not do, and whether specifying it requires a substantive theory of the good — or a claim about the quality of the ruler's will — that Berlin would have called a relapse into positive liberty; the state as the largest potential dominator, where the remedy for private domination is an agent whose only check is that it must explain itself; and whether an answerable but irremovable power is a check at all or merely a request. For each question, give the answer he is most likely to give and the follow-up that answer invites, and close with one that makes the status-not-a-level move hard to use a third time in a single sitting.
