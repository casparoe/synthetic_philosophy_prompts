ok so here's roughly where I've landed on how science works and I want to know if it's a coherant package before I put it in a paper. (1) Theories are never appraised on their own — you appraise whole research programmes, and what makes one good is that it keeps predicting new stuff instead of just absorbing whatever comes along after the fact. (2) The central commitments of a programme are held by decision, not by evidence; you can't refute them, you can only decide to stop defending them. (3) So there's no instant rationality — you can't tell at a given moment whether a programme is on its way out, since it might always recover later; the verdict is retrospective. (4) Still, I want to be able to say about some programmes running right now, in physics and in economics, that they're degenerating and that the people keeping them alive are being irrational. (5) And I think a theory only counts as scientific if it forbids some possible observations.

Which of these actually fight each other, which pairs only look like they do, and can you rank the real tensions by how bad they are? I'm fairly sure one of them has to go but I honestly don't know which, and for each real tension I'd like the cheapest repair and what it costs me.
