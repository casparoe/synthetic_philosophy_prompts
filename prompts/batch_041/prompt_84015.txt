I'm a systems security engineer. I spend my days with access control models -- ACLs, capability systems, ambient authority, least privilege, revocation, sandboxing, audit trails -- and I have no philosophical training. Restate the republican or neo-Roman account of freedom as non-domination, and its contrast with freedom as mere non-interference, entirely in that vocabulary. What is the security analogue of the master who could interfere but never does, of interference that is arbitrary as opposed to authorized, and of the requirement that a free person be able to contest the terms on which others may interfere? Then give me the residue report. Which republican claims translate cleanly into statements about permissions, capabilities, and revocation; which survive only with distortion; and which cannot be expressed at all? I'm especially interested in whether the translation quietly presupposes the very thing republicans want to explain -- some notion of legitimate authorization -- and if so, whether that is a limitation of my vocabulary or a sign that their appeal to arbitrariness is doing less work than it appears to. Note also where republicans themselves disagree about whether non-domination reduces to non-interference plus reliable protections, and say what that disagreement looks like once it is recast. End by telling me whether anything survives the round trip that would change how I actually design systems, or whether I should treat the whole thing as a metaphor.
