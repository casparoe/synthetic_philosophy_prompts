I write a science-and-ideas column and I am building a reader quiz on sphexishness: the suspicion that much apparently flexible, reason-guided behavior is really a fixed routine that only looks adaptive because the environment usually cooperates. Please write a decision tree, not a prose essay, that takes a reader from four or five concrete cases to a named position about free will and agency. Use cases such as a wasp that keeps re-inspecting its burrow after the cricket is moved, a language model that produces a novel plan when its first tool call fails, a person who reliably keeps a promise only when a calendar reminder makes it salient, and a person who under fatigue reverts to a habit she explicitly disavows. Each internal node should be a diagnostic question answerable without knowing the labels: does the flexibility survive interruption, does the agent represent the interruption as a reason, is the stability supplied from inside or by external scaffolding, and does the agent's own endorsement of the routine matter? Leaves should be positions such as hard sphexism, compatibilist local flexibility, inner-model agency, scaffolded agency, libertarian agent causation, and one leaf for the view that the question is ill-posed. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and note any empty combinations, saying whether the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Keep the whole thing under a page, and do not cite any literature; I want the sorting to turn on the cases, not on names.
