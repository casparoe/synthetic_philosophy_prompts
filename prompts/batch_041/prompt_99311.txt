ok weird one. I've been roped into writing the closing piece for my dept's phil of science pub night — the theme is the demarcaton problem, everyone's read Popper and Lakatos, and by the end of the night everyone's a little too pleased with themselves for deciding science vs pseudoscience is just a social boundary. I want to land the evening with a poem instead of a summary.

Can you write me a sestina about falsification? The form is the whole reason I'm asking: six end-words that keep getting shuffled but never actually leave the poem is exactly how a theory survives being tested — the same handful of rescue moves comming back in a new order every time. So make the six end-words do that job. Each one should be one of the dodges, roughly in the family of again / exception / core / ad hoc / save / still (swap in your own if they're better), and in the envoi I want all six crammed together, since that's the pile-up where every defense fires at once.

The arc I'm after: the first couple of stanzas build the clean falsificationist picture — bold guess, risky prediction, one black swan and the whole thing is dead. Then the turn is where that picture cracks, because the counterexample never lands on the theory itself, only on the bundle of theory-plus-auxiliaries, so you can always walk it back: blame a background assumption, quietly redefine a term, or just call the anomaly a monster and carve out an exception (that last one's Lakatos, if it helps). The close should sit on the uncomfortable bit — if any theory can be rescued this way, the razor that was meant to cut science from pseudoscience cuts nothing, and yet we obviously do draw lines somewhere, just maybe not the ones Popper drew. Don't resolve it. Leave it uncomfortable.

A couple of asks so it doesn't come out generic: don't name-drop stuff you can't actually put to work, and if you can fold the asymmetry into the refrain logic — you can never verify a universal claim, one counterexample seems to kill it, but the killing blow never quite lands on the target — do it. Someone who's read Popper and Lakatos should nod along, and someone who hasn't should still get a real poem out of it. i'm not precious about hitting all 39 lines; if a looser sestina or a 6x6 with a short envoi serves the poem better, fine, but keep the permutation logic doing the heavy lifting. And if the last line can come back to the first wearing a slightly different coat, that's the ending I want.
