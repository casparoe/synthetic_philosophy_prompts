I'm a second-year PhD student (philosophy of mind, though my project has drifted toward the experimental side of things), and I got dragged into an arguement at the pub last night that I completely failed to referee. Two people in my cohort, J and M, went at it over whether first-person reports about your own mind count as evidence at all. Roughly:

J: the confabulation stuff settles it. Split-brain patients invent explanations for actions that were driven by the other hemisphere. In the classic studies people give you sincere reasons for a choice they were manipulated into making. In the choice-blindness setups they defend a preference they never expressed. If the report can be produced by whatever the person happens to be handed, it isn't reporting anything — introspection is a storyteller, not an instrument.

M: that shows introspection is bad at one job, reporting the causes of what you did. Nobody serious ever claimed it was a causal read-out. Its job is reporting what's happening now, the current experience, and none of that work bears on it. Also, to call the patient's answer a confabulation you need to know the right answer, and that comes from the experimenter's third-person data. Where there's no third-person signature to check against, the charge can't even get set up.

J: the same trouble shows up for current experience. Change blindness — people are certain they saw it change. And in the choice-blindness cases the person reports the swapped option with reasons attached. That's a report about what's in front of them being generated by the setup.

M: two different mistakes there. The subject is being asked about the new item, so a report about that isn't a misreport of the earlier state. And "generated by the setup" is true of every measurement anyone has ever taken, including the ones you'd trust. Being caused isn't the same as being unreliable.

J: fine, then tell me what would count as introspection failing about experience. If nothing could, you're not making a claim, you're holding up a shield.

M: lots of things would count. If reports about current experience turned out to be driven by what people expect to experience rather than by the experience, that'd be a failure. Your studies just don't show that.

J: I think they do show it, you're redescribing them.

Neither would budge, after wich they both turned to me. Can you go through it move by move and say who's right at each step, and what each of them is missing? I'm not after which side to join — I want to know which of the actual claims hold up and which don't.
