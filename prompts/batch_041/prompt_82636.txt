My thesis: the cluelessness problem for consequentialism isn't really about our ignorance of consequences — it's about the shape of the expected-value sum. For most real actions the EV gets dominated by a tail of tiny-probability, enormous-magnitude outcomes (far-future effects, effects on possible people, butterfly-effect ripple stuff), so the sum is unstable and you can flip its sign just by changing what you count as part of the probability space. So I claim act-consequentialism needs a principled way to exclude or discount those tails, and I sketch one (roughly: restrict the relevant consequences to a causally-close domain, not an epistemic one).
