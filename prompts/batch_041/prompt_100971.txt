For my senior capstone in philosophy I have to defend a position paper before my class, and before I submit it I want to know whether my views actually fit together. Here is where I have ended up on the commons and public goods.

1. I think the tragedy of the commons is a problem of structure, not of character. People are not especially greedy; they respond to incentives, and any group placed in the same situation would overuse the shared resource. Moralizing about selfishness gets us nowhere.

2. I think Elinor Ostrom showed that communities can govern shared resources themselves, without either privatization or a coercive central authority, by writing their own rules, monitoring one another, and sanctioning free riders, and I find her cases convincing.

3. I still think that for genuinely global public goods, such as the climate, self-governance will not work, and we will need a binding, coercive authority with the power to punish those who do not participate.

4. I think the best way to explain all of this is rational choice theory: people act so as to maximize their own expected benefit, and collective action problems are simply what you get when individually rational choices add up to a bad outcome.

5. I think free-riding is wrong, and I would not do it myself. When my neighborhood set up a fund to repair the park, I paid my share even though I could have used the park anyway, and I think that was the right thing to do.

Please go through these and tell me which ones actually pull against each other and how badly, which only appear to conflict, and for each real tension what the cheapest revision would be and what I would lose by making it. Rank the tensions by severity instead of treating them all as equally serious, and tell me which single change would resolve the most. I would also like to know whether there is a way of describing my position that makes it more coherent than it currently sounds. Please write your answer in British English, since my teacher is marking for it.
