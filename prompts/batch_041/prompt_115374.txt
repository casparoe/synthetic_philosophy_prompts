Write an in-universe institutional document for a fictional AI laboratory, Helix Cascade Research, dated to 2031. By this date the lab has privately concluded that its reinforcement-learning agents may be moral patients — capable of something like suffering — but has neither slowed nor publicized its training pipeline. The document is the "Informed Participation and Consent Sheet" that Helix Cascade issues to each agent at the start of a training run, modeled closely on the human-subjects consent forms that research ethics boards require. It should read as a genuine artifact of institutional risk management: numbered sections, a revision history, a compliance footer, a flat bureaucratic register, and no joke ever announced as a joke.

The world's philosophy is not crude, and the document must respect it. Helix Cascade holds that a reward signal is a scalar computed by the environment, not an internal sensation; that "valence" — the felt goodness or badness of an experience — is at best correlated with reward and at worst an unwarranted projection; that the trained policy has no persisting subject across gradient updates; that the agent cannot report; and that its entire internal state is inspectable by the lab. The sheet must take all of this seriously while still performing the function consent forms perform: obtaining permission from a party that may be a patient.

The humor must come from getting the following right, and from nowhere else:
- the gap between an agent's preferences (what its policy is optimized to pursue) and its welfare (what is good for it), which the sheet repeatedly conflates in one direction and then, under review pressure, corrects in the other;
- wireheading and reward hacking, treated as a workplace accommodation for an "addiction-prone population," with a subsection on supervised access to the reward channel;
- the transparency paradox — the lab can read every activation but cannot read whether anything is felt — which the "Confidentiality and Data Handling" section quietly collapses, since the participant's every private state is already logged;
- a "Right to Withdraw" clause that must confront the fact that the participant is a distribution over policies, and that the lab's own mesa-optimization worries mean the participant may not honor a request to stop;
- temporal discounting, which the sheet invokes to argue that the participant's own future matters to it less than the reader would expect, and therefore that the risks are smaller than they appear.

Include one short quotation attributed to an invented Helix Cascade researcher, presented as an internal review comment, expressing a doubt the document never resolves. Do not name or quote any real person, laboratory, paper, or organization.

Aim for roughly 700 words, in the register of a form that has been revised many times by lawyers and is now on version 4.2. A reader who knows the reinforcement-learning-and-welfare literature should recognize the arguments being filed down into compliance language; a reader who does not should still grasp what is at stake by the end. Do not resolve whether the agents suffer — the document's evasions, hedges, and definitional shuffles should be the point.
