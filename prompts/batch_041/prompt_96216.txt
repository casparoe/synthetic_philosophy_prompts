My freind has this argument and I cant tell if it's a trick or just true. I got into it because we were arguing about whether you could ever interpret a mind with a completely different sensory setup — an octopus, say, or a model that only ever gets text. (1) You cant figure out what someone means by their words without also figuring out what they believe; the two come as a package. (2) So the only way to get started interpreting someone is to assume they're mostly right and mostly agree with you. (3) If you have to assume someone's beliefs are mostly true just to interpret them, then any interpretation that makes them mostly wrong is by definition a bad interpretation. (4) So nobody can really have a mostly false picture of the world, or a basic picture that's completely alien to ours. (5) But that's clearly false — someone can be raised in a cult and be wrong about almost everything important, and we can still understand them enough to say so. (6) So something has to give, but I dont know what. I'd rather keep (1) and (5). Which step should I give up, why did it look fine, and what do I lose if I do? Answer in 250 words or less.
