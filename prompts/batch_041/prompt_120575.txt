I keep running into a move in politics that I dont have a word for, and I want to know if it already has one. The shape of it: somebody in power wants something done that they know is ugly -- a dirty war, a mass deportation, closing the local hospital -- and they don't do it themselves, and they don't just order a subordinate to do it either. They build a structure. A committee with a mandate, an arm's-length agency, a procurement process, a proxy militia, an "independent" review. Then the thing happens, and every single person involved can say, honestly and with a straight face, that they didn't do it: the minister didn't decide, the committee decided; the committee only applied the criteria it was handed; the general only sold the weapons to local forces who made their own choices. And the outcome is exactly what the minister wanted. That last bit is what nags at me. It isn't that responsibility got lost by accident -- the loss of responsibility was the point of the design, or at least a foreseeable and welcome side effect of it.

I know the dirty hands thing (Walzer), the politician who does wrong and carries the guilt. This is the inverse: nobody carries anything, and the guy in the suit is explaining that the process was independent. I also know the ordinary phenomenon where lots of people contribute to a harm and nobody feels responsible -- but that's usually described as something that happens to us, not something somebody builds on purpose.

So does the deliberate version have a name? If there's a literature, what's it called, and does it treat the designed diffusion as a distinct wrong or just as buck-passing with better PR? If nothing fits I'd like a term plus a definition, and mostly I want the edges drawn: how does it differ from (a) dirty hands, (b) the accidental kind of diffusion, (c) just lying about what you did, (d) delegating work you'd have been happy to do yourself. Does it matter whether the person actually believes their own story? I think the interesting case is the one where they do believe it, or half believe it. And is there a version where the architecture is legitimate and necessary -- a genuinely independent regulator, a real arms-length body -- so that the same structure is sometimes just how decent government works and sometimes a machine for laundering guilt? I'd like to be able to tell those two apart.
