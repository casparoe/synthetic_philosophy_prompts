I write about science and ideas for a magazine, and I'm putting together a long feature on teleosemantics — the whole programme of explaining what it is for a thought or a signal to be about something in terms of what it was selected to do. I've got a hunch I can't shake, and it's definately got under my skin, so I'd like you to stress-test it for me.

Here's the thing. Classic teleosemantics leans hard on selection history: a state means what it means because of what it did for our ancestors, or because of what some mechanism was designed to do. But a lot of the representational systems we're now building — big trained models, and honestly plenty of biology too — weren't shaped by that kind of selection over generations. They were shaped by a training run, a reward signal, a few weeks of gradient descent. I've started calling this the training-versus-selection gap, and I can't decide whether it breaks teleosemantics or just forces it to generalise.

So here's what I actually want from you. Within the naturalising-content programme, which open questions matter most right now, and how would you prioritise them? I don't want a reading list or a who-said-what — I haven't got the time to chase citations and it's not the point of the piece. I want the reasoning: for each question, why is it upstream of the others, how tractable is it for someone with a few years and a philosophy background rather than a lab, and what would move it up or down the ranking. And be honest with me about whether my training-versus-selection gap is a genuine crux or just me pattern-matching onto whatever's fashionable this season.
