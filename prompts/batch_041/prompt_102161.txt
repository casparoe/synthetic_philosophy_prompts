I have no philosophical training, but I have been working through the occasionalist picture of causation on my own: the view, associated with al-Ghazālī, that the things we call causes do not really bring their effects about, and that what looks like a necessary connection is only the regular order in which some power greater than either of them keeps pairing one event with another. Before I decide what to make of it, I want the full specification, each requirement stated together with the case or puzzle that forces it. Which regularities a theory must count as causal and which it must not; why we single out one antecedent as the cause when several were equally necessary; why the relation runs one way and not the other, so that altering the cause alters the effect while altering the effect leaves the cause alone; what it must say about prevention and omission, about an effect that would have occurred anyway, and about a correlation produced by a hidden common factor; and whether it owes an account of the felt necessity of a causal link or may dismiss that feeling as a mere habit of mind. Then tell me which of these requirements al-Ghazālī's view can meet, which it cannot, and -- the part I care about most -- whether any two of them cannot be satisfied together, so that the list itself becomes a result. A table is fine.
