I've been argueing with a friend about whether it's ever wrong not to have a kid, and I've realized my gut verdicts dont fit any neat theory. I'm hoping you can help me find the principle behind them, or tell me which ones I have to give up. Here's what I think, going case by case. A couple can have a child who will be happy and loved; they decide not to. I don't think they've done anything wrong. A couple can have a child who will be miserable -- chronic pain, no relief, early death. They decide to have it anyway. I think thats clearly wrong. A couple can have a child whose life will be just barely worth living, more good than bad but only slightly. They decide to have it. I feel uneasy, maybe it's wrong, but I can't say it's as wrong as the miserable-child case. A couple can have a child who will be happy, but only if they don't have a different child who would also be happy, just a bit less so. They pick the happier one. I don't think that's wrong. A couple can have a child who will be happy, but that child will also cause some real harm to others -- not huge, but not trivial. I think they should not have it, or at least it's a strong strike against. A person can create a sentient AI that will be happy and not harmed. I don't think there's a duty to create it, but if the AI would be miserable, creating it is wrong. A couple can have a child who will be happy, but the pregnancy will cause the mother significant temporary suffering. I think it's permissible, not required. A couple can have a child who will be happy, but they'll have to give up their own life projects. I think either choice is permissible. A couple can have a child who will probably be happy, but there's a small chance it will be miserable. I think it's permissible if the odds are good enough, but not if the misery would be severe. And a person can either create a child who will be happy or use the same resources to help existing miserable people. I think helping the existing people is at least as good, maybe better.

Now here's the one that's really bugging me. Suppose you can create a person whose life is good, but only by also creating a second person whose life is bad -- a kind of package deal. I think creating the package is wrong, even if the first person's good outweighs the second person's bad. But that seems to conflict with the first case, where I said not creating a happy person is fine. Maybe the difference is that in the package case you're actively causing the bad life, whereas in the first case you're just leaving a possible happy person uncreated. My own tentative principle is what I've been calling the creation ledger: when you create a person, any suffering in their life counts as a cost you must justify, while any happiness counts as a bonus that doesn't create a requirement. That gets the first two cases, but I don't know if it gets the barely-worth-living case or the package case, and it sounds too crude. My friend says the asymmetry is just a bias -- we're loss-averse about suffering, or we can't compare existence to non-existence, so we treat the two sides differently. If she's right, which of my verdicts should I drop? If she's wrong, what's the precise form of the asymmetry? I'd like a small set of candidate principles, each stated precisely, with the case that motivates each clause, what each says about cases I haven't listed (for instance, creating a person who will be happy but will exist for only a second, or creating a person who will be happy but will never know it, or creating a person who will be happy but whose existence prevents many other happy people from existing), and an honest statement of which of my verdicts the principle can't accommodate. I'm not looking for a reading list or citations -- just the principle and the reasoning.
