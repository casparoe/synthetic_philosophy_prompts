For a novel I am drafting, I need a scene that works as a thought experiment — an intuition pump that makes a reader feel a harsh moral judgment is warranted, so that I can later dismantle the pump in front of them. Build me the case to order. The pump must push the reader to the verdict that a character is fully blameworthy for a harm he foresaw but did not intend, and it must draw at least as much blame in that version as in a version where the harm was his actual aim. Everything a responsibility theorist would call relevant stays fixed — the act, the foresight, the outcome, the character's indifference to the harm. The only variation permitted is how the narrator frames what the character was after. Constraints: a domestic or workplace setting, no trolleys, no science fiction, one paragraph, and the two versions must be the same passage with a single clause changed. Then run the diagnostics: say what each framing invites the reader to infer, name the one feature doing the pumping, and tell me what becomes of the effect if I strip that feature out while holding the act and its consequences identical. Close with the strongest case a reader could make that the blame is warranted rather than an artifact of my framing.
