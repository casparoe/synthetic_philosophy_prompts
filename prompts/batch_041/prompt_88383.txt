ok so I've been mainlining philosophy of medicine stuff lately and I want a consistency check on my position before I say any of it out loud in seminar. Here's where I've landed: (1) disease is value-laden, nothing is a disease in a purely biological sense, calling something a disease always smuggles in a norm about how a body ought to work; (2) I'm not a relativist about it though — a heart that can't pump is malfunctioning whether or not anyone has norms about it, thats an objective fact about biology; (3) the treatment/enhancement distinction is definately bogus, statins and vaccines both just shove a body further from its statistically typical state, so no biological line can be drawn there, only a conventional one; (4) my working heuristic is that something counts as a disease roughly to the degree that medicine can do something about it — no intervention, no disease, so 'disease' tracks the clinic rather than nature; (5) I'm hostile to psychiatric nosology, DSM categories mostly look like bureaucratic artifacts to me, but I also think schizophrenia is a real brain disease, as real as diabetes.

So: which of these actually fight each other, ranked by how bad, which pairs only look like they conflict, and for the real ones what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me? And if one of them is secretly doing all the load-bearing work and should just be dropped, say so.
