I'm taking a metaphysics seminar on dispositions this term and I've managed to build an argument that I think proves too much, and now I can't find where it goes wrong. Quick background in case it matters: a fink is something that shows up at the moment of the trigger and stops the basis of a disposition from doing its work (the sorcerer who makes the glass tough exactly as it's struck, so it doesn't break), and a mask is something that blocks the manifestation without touching the disposition or its basis at all (the fragile glass packed in foam — still fragile, just not breaking). We spent two weeks on these, wich is why I'm annoyed at myself: I was supposed to write a short paper saying they don't really threaten the idea that fragility, solubility and the like are intrinsic properties of the things that have them. Instead I wrote this. (1) A property is intrinsic to a thing just in case, necessarily, both whether the thing has the property and whether the property manifests are settled by how the thing is in itself, with no help from anything outside it. (2) Take a glass with molecular structure M, and suppose its fragility is identical with M, or anyway nothing over and above M. (3) Then M alone settles that the glass is fragile. (4) But imagine a demon who stands ready to prevent that glass from ever breaking, no matter how or when its struck — the glass keeps M throughout, and it is never going to break. Then whether the fragility manifests is plainly not settled by how the glass is in itself. (5) So by (1) the fragility is not intrinsic to the glass. (6) Nothing in this turned on the glass or on fragility: for any disposition and any basis, we can rig the surroundings so that the manifestation never comes off, and in fact the same reasoning reaches any property at all, since whether a thing's mass ever shows up depends on whether something pushes it, and whether its shape ever shows up depends on light or on a hand. (7) So no property is intrinsic, and the intrinsic/extrinsic distinction marks nothing. That's absurd, so something has to give.
