I'm TAing a discussion section on the Epicurean argument about death, and I want to get the students past "well, you'd just miss out on stuff." So I've wrote up five different things you could say a person is deprived of when they die:

1. the life they would in fact have gone on to live, if the thing that killed them hadnt happened;
2. the life of a statisically typical person in their position;
3. the life their own plans and projects were aimed at;
4. their life as it actually was, just extended at the same level;
5. nothing at all — death isn't bad by comparison with a life you never got, it's bad because it cuts off a life that was already going fine.

Rank these from the one that best answers the objection that you can't compare a real life with a life that never happened, to the one that answers it worst. Give me exactly five numbered items, best first, one or two sentences each, no intro or conclusion.
