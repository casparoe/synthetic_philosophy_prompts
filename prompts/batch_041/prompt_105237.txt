So my brother-in-law and I got into this thing over Christmas that's still bugging me. We were arguing about coffee — I said the fancy single-origin pourover place near me is better than the chain, he agreed, then he said the chain is better than the gas-station stuff, no argument, and then he goes "so the pourover is better than the gas station." And I said yeah obviously. Then he got this grin and said "then watch this," and started doing this thing where you only ever compare two cups that are a hair apart. Cup A vs cup B, genuinely can't tell which is better; B vs C, a hair apart; and so on down and down, and if every step is "at least as good," he slides all the way from amazing espresso to warm brown water without ever catching a single step where it gets worse. He thinks this proves "better than" isn't even transitive and everyone's just pretending it is. I think it proves he's cheating somewhere but I can't put my finger on where, and I don't have any philosophy training — I did one semester of econ — so I'm probably missing something.

Anyway I want to see this done as comedy, because I feel like if a good satirist did it I'd finally spot the trick. Can you write me a parody of one of those snooty consumer-magazine "The 10 Best Cups of Coffee in [City], Ranked" columns — deadpan the whole way, with the little star ratings and a "Verdict" box at the end — where the critic does exactly my brother-in-law's slide, one hair-thin comparison at a time, everything totally reasonable at each step, and by the end is earnestly crowning a cup of hot water with one sad instant granule as #1 and telling readers to skip the espresso bar. I want the jokes to come from the actual machinery being right rather than the critic being an idiot — so each small step has to be individually plausible, the assumption that if A beats B and B beats C then A beats C has to be doing real work somewhere, and the ending should be something a person who's read about this stuff laughs at instead of wincing. Around 600-700 words. No real cafes or brands — make them up.

One ask on top of that: after the column, stick a short straight note in your own voice (not the critic's) telling me what's actually settled here versus what people who study this genuinely fight about — like whether the fix is to give up transitivity, or to say the tiny steps are where the error lives, or whether "better than" is just too vague/incomplete for the question to have a clean answer — and be honest that these are live disagreements rather than one obvious fix. Don't hand me one camp's line as if it's the consensus.
