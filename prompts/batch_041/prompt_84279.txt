I'm giving a colloquium paper in about three weeks and I want to spend the remaining time on Q&A prep rather than tinkering with the paper. Can you help?

The paper is on the self. Thesis, roughly: everyone in this fight treats "the narrative self" as one thing and it's at least two. First there's narrativity — how story-shaped a stretch of life is, how much it hangs together, how much of it the person can actually tell. Then there's narrative ownership — whose story it is, who has standing to revise it, who answers for it. The Dennett-style center-of-gravity picture is a picture of narrativity: it explains why we attribute a self the way we attribute a center of gravity to a physical body, as an abstraction that earns its keep by letting us predict. Fine, I mostly buy it. But it's silent on ownership, and ownership is where the philosophy actually lives. My claim is that ownership is not a further cognitive achievement layered on top of narrativity. It's a normative status — closer to being a legal person, or to being on the hook for something, than to being a well-told story. You have a self, in the full sense, when you're treated as answerable for your own narrative, and that's a status conferred in practice rather than a fact discovered in the head. Consequence I care most about: selfhood comes in degrees and can be lost without any corresponding loss of cognitive machinery, and without the narrative getting any less coherent.

The room. Departmental colloquium, so mostly analytic philosophers of mind, and a solid contingent of Parfit people — reductionists who think we should stop asking about identity and start asking what matters in survival, and who get twitchy around anything that sounds like the self is a further entity. Plus two from the med school: a psychiatrist who runs a dissociative-disorders clinic, and a neuropsychologist who mostly sees frontotemporal dementia. They're coming because the topic is load-bearing for them and they dont care about the metaphysics. And there's definately one emeritus who always asks about animals and infants, so assume that's coming.

What I want is a prep sheet, not a defense of the thesis. Sections, in this order:
