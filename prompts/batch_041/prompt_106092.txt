Consider a claim I will call the correlation thesis: there can exist two agents sharing no causal contact of any kind — no common cause in their histories, no exchange of signals, no shared random seed, and no simulation relation between them — such that each can nonetheless reliably anticipate the other's choices, solely because their decision procedures are logically or algorithmically correlated, and such that this correlation can sustain a mutually advantageous exchange, an "acausal trade," even when neither agent can confirm that the other exists.

Please give me your credence that the correlation thesis is true, understood as the claim that at least one physically realizable pair of agents satisfies the conditions just stated, and decompose the number rather than asserting it. Specifically, I want:

(a) your probability that a correct decision theory should treat an agent's choice as correlated with the choices of any agent running a sufficiently similar decision procedure (the logical-correlation premise), with one sentence on what that probability chiefly rests on;

(b) your probability that, conditional on (a), there can be two agents with no common cause whose decision procedures are similar enough to ground such correlation — noting whether mere instantiation of the same abstract algorithm on physically disjoint hardware suffices, or whether something thicker is required;

(c) your probability that, conditional on (a) and (b), the resulting exchange can be mutually beneficial when the agents' value systems may be incommensurable, or whether "benefit" here presupposes a common utility scale, so that trade is undefined rather than merely difficult;

(d) your probability that the arrangement is self-enforcing, that is, stable without any commitment device, verification mechanism, or shared enforcement.

Then combine the factors into a single credence, state the arithmetic you used, and identify which factor the final number is most sensitive to, along with the single argument or piece of evidence that would move it most. Finally, address the boundary question directly: if the two agents share a random seed or a common cosmic event, does that reintroduce causal contact and thereby trivialize the thesis, and how much of your credence survives once that case is excluded?
