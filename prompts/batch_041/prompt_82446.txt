My prof said the honest way in is to just write down what you actually think about a bunch of cases and then dig for the principle underneath, so I did that on the bus and now I'm worried my verdicts don't fit together at all. Here's what I've got. Zombies (a physical duplicate of me down to the last neuron, but no inner life whatsoever): sure, I can sort of "conceive" it, but I don't think that gives me any real reason to believe such a thing is possible — my hunch is that whatever I'm doing when I "conceive" it is just a side effect of not knowing enough about how brains make experience. Inverted spectrum (a twin wired up identically to me but who sees red where I see green): same deal, I can tell that story in about four seconds and I don't think it counts for anything. Water that turns out not to be H2O, or a world where the clear drinkable stuff in the lakes is some other compound: no, that's not conceiving at all, that's me failing to hold onto what "water" means, and it's not evidence of anything. A partless, immaterial thing that thinks (a Cartesian ego sort of deal): I think I can really imagine that, and I think it's possible, and the fact that I can imagine it is part of why I think so. A world containing facts that nobody could ever in principle know — say a fact about the distant past that left literally no trace: I can conceive that and I think it's a real possibility. And the five-minute world (everything is particle-for-particle identical to here, but it just popped into being five minutes ago with all the fossils and memories and coffee stains already in place): I go back and forth, but I lean yes, that's genuinely possible, and conceivability is doing some work for me there.

Here's what's bugging me. The zombie and the five-minute world feel like exactly the same move — take the actual world and change one thing while every observable stays fixed — and I'm giving them opposite verdicts. Same with the partless thinker vs the XYZ water: both are cases where my "conceiving" seems to depend on my ignorance of something (brains, chemistry), but I let one count and not the other. My friend in the seminar said the fix is that conceivability only counts when (i) you can actually imagine the scenario as a thing rather than just parse a sentence about it, and (ii) nothing in the scenario contradicts something you already know.
