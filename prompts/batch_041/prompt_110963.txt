I teach a metaphysics unit on material constitution, and I keep running into a pattern in seminar discussion that I have no name for and am no longer sure has one. Take the standard case: a lump of clay is moulded into a statue, so that the statue and the lump occupy exactly the same place, come into and go out of existence at different times, and one of them can survive being squashed while the other cannot. The discussion reliably runs as follows. The students agree about every fact anyone could check — the clay, the shape, the history, what would happen under various counterfactual manipulations. Then one of them says that there are plainly two things here, and another says that there is plainly one thing described in two ways, and within minutes each is accusing the other of cheating. The pluralist is said to multiply objects in order to save a description; the monist is said to let the word 'thing' legislate about the world. What strikes me is that neither party experiences the exchange as terminological. Each takes the other to have got something wrong about the world, and yet neither can produce a fact that the other denies. I know the general vicinity — there is a literature on disputes that turn out to be about words rather than facts, and another on whether there is a fact of the matter about how many things there are — but those are positions about what is going on in such an exchange, and what I am after is a name for the shape of the exchange itself: the standoff that survives complete agreement on the facts, that does not feel terminological to its participants, and that no further investigation appears able to settle. Does it have an established name? If it does not, coin one — but I care far more about the boundaries than about the label. What exactly must be true of a dispute for it to be an instance? How does it differ from a dispute that is merely verbal, and from a dispute about which vocabulary we ought to adopt? Can it be one-sided — one party simply confused about the facts, the other not — without collapsing back into an ordinary disagreement? And what becomes of the pattern when the subject matter is not artefacts but, say, whether a virus counts as alive, or whether a species name picks out a real kind? I would rather have a careful map of the nearest existing concepts, with an account of where each falls short, than a single borrowed label.
