I've been trying to work out this liberty business on my own and I think I've nearly got it, but something isn't clicking. As far as I can tell there's three of them. Negative liberty is when nobody's actually stopping you from doing what you want. Positive liberty is when you've genuinely got what it takes to do it — the money, the health, the education, that sort of thing. And then this third one, the "republican" one, that people keep mentioning, which seems to me to be just those two stapled together. You need to not be interfered with and you need the means, so it's definately not a third thing at all, it's just the honest version of the other two.

But someone told me the whole point of it is that a slave with a kind master isn't free even if the master never once interferes, and I can't see how that works. If he never interferes, what's the slave actually missing out on? Isn't "domination" just interference that might happen later, which seems a funny thing to call unfreedom when it hasn't happened yet? Or is the slave simply failing on the positive count, because he's got no means?

Two cases made me more confused rather than less. A tenant whose landlord has never once bothered her, but could turf her out tomorrow for no reason. And a tax I hate, brought in by a government I voted for and can vote out. The first feels like the slave case and the second feels like being interfered with but somehow not unfree, and I can't line either of them up with my three boxes.

So what I'd like is: which of the three is actually right, and where exactly my picture is going wrong. Write it out as plain prose please, the way you'd explain it to me in an email — no headings, bullet points, bold or tables, I find all that harder to follow than a paragraph. And don't worry about giving me a reading list, I'm not going to read it.
