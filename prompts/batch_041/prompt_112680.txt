I serve on the grants committee of a small local foundation. This cycle we have a single unrestricted award of about twenty thousand pounds to give away, and two finalists, and I have to cast the deciding vote at our meeting next week.

The first finalist, whom I will call M, is a woman in her late fifties who has spent most of her adult life moving between insecure, badly paid work and spells of poor health. The award would pay for a retraining course and a year of breathing room, and it is clear to all of us that it would move her from a precarious, pinched existence to something stable and genuinely good. The second finalist, J, comes from a comfortable background and has independent means. He has been trying for years to finish a demanding artistic project, and the award would let him complete it. Completing it matters enormously to him; he has told us it is the one thing in his life he cares about, and I believe him.

We can fund only one. Our chair argues that we should simply fund the person who would benefit more, and that in this case that is plainly J, because the difference the award makes to his life, measured by how much he wants the thing it buys, is larger than the difference it makes to M's. A second member objects that the chair is talking nonsense: benefits to two different people cannot be added up or weighed against each other at all, there is no fact of the matter about who would benefit more, and the only defensible rule is to help the person who is worse off, which means M. A third member proposes that we settle it by imagining ourselves behind a veil, ignorant of which of the two lives we would end up leading, and choosing the allocation we would prefer from that position. I find all three proposals unsatisfying, but I cannot put my finger on what any of them gets wrong, and I am not confident that my discomfort is anything more than squeamishness.

Could you help me understand what is actually at stake in this disagreement, whether the three proposals are even answering the same question, and how I should think about casting my vote? I am less interested in a verdict than in knowing which distinctions I am currently blurring and what each of them would commit me to.
