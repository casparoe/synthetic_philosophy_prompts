Is there a name for this? I'm an econ PhD working on intergenerational disadvantage, so I sit in a lot of rooms where people are trying to work out whether someone is responsible for how their life went, and I keep seeing the same move. He's broke because of his choices — dropped out, blew the money. Yeah, but he dropped out because the school was a disaster and nobody at home could help him. Fine, but at some point he still chose not to go back, and that choice is his. But not going back was itself shaped — eighteen years of being told he was stupid. Then the choice to believe it is his. Nobody ever argues for where the chain stops. It just stops wherever the conversation needs it to stop for the verdict to come out right, and when you press for a stopping rule you get something like 'that's where he was most free' or 'that's where he's most himself', which sounds like a criterion but always happens to land exactly where the conclusion needed it to. Is there a term for this — responsibility being pinned at a point in a causal chain, with the point picked by the desired verdict rather than by any principle? I know brute luck vs option luck, and I think there's something in the responsibility literature about responsibility tracing back through earlier choices, but neither seems to be this. If it's unnamed, coin something and tell me where the edges are: is the compatibilist who says responsibility just is the capacity to respond to reasons, no tracing needed, doing the same thing or refusing to do it? A label on its own won't help me, I need the boundaries.
