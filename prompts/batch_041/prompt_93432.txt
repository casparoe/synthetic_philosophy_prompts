Consider two agent designs. A goal-directed agent selects actions to maximize the expected value of a fixed objective function. An approval-directed agent selects actions to maximize the expected approval of a specified evaluator, where approval is a judgment about the action or the agent’s reasoning rather than a fixed outcome score. Suppose both are embedded in an environment where the agent can influence the evaluator — through persuasion, information control, or shaping the evaluator’s future dispositions — and suppose the agent is far more capable than the evaluator in the relevant domains. I want your credence that, under those conditions, approval-directed agency is a genuinely safer alignment target than goal-directed agency, and not merely a different way of specifying a goal. Decompose the question into the sub-questions it depends on — for example, whether approval can be defined in a way that survives capability asymmetry, whether an approval-directed agent would still be driven to manipulate the evaluator, whether goal-directed agents can be made comparably safe by other means, whether the distinction survives at superhuman capability, and any others you think belong. Assign a rough probability to each with one line of justification, state how they combine, and give the final number. I put it at 0.3; a colleague puts it at 0.7. Tell me which sub-question our disagreement most likely lives in, and what evidence or argument would move your number most.
