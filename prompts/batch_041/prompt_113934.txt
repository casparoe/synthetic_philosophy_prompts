I teach an undergrad seminar on practical rationality and I want to close the term with Memento. The philosophy people usualy write about that film is all memory and personal identity, which I'm a bit bored of, so I want to do money pumps and diachronic rationality instead. Leonard's tattoos and Polaroids are plainly a commitment device, and the ending is him using that device to commit a future self he'll never share memories with to killing Teddy, so: is he running a money pump on himself, and is it the same kind of pump the literature describes, or does the analogy break because the later Leonard doesn't remember the trades and can't feel himself being taken? Teddy says he's been using Leonard for years and that the whole search just resets, which sounds like a pump running on a loop. And Natalie gets work out of him and then hands him Teddy's name, which may or may not be the same move. I also want the sophisticated-vs-resolute chooser distinction taken seriously (if I know I'll defect later, why not defect now?) and a straight answer on whether the film vindicates resoluteness or shows it collapsing, plus what it dramatizes about diachronic agency that the decision-theory papers dont. Where the analogy to the atemporal pump strains, say so, and don't let the whole thing dissolve into a claim about personal identity. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning — no preamble, no restating the question.
