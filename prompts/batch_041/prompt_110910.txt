I'm a novelist — literary fiction, mostly — and a metaphysics question keeps messing with how I write backstory. Take the view that a person's origin is essential to them: the particular sperm and egg that made you aren't just how you got here, they're part of what you are, so a world with a different sperm and egg contains a different person, not you. The rival view says that's wrong: you could have come from a different sperm and egg, because what makes you you is some later fact — psychological continuity, a pattern, whatever — not the source. I know the debate well enogh to get into trouble, but I think in drafts, canon, origin stories, prequels, retcons, alternate-universe fanfic, character arcs, and the weird feeling when a character in draft 2 is 'the same character' with a completely different backstory. You're a philosopher who also knows how novels actually get made: think carefully, and start by brainstorming the best mapping you can between the two views and the novelist's toolkit. Then translate both views into that vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and give me the residue report — what maps cleanly, what only maps with distortion, what has no counterpart at all, and whether the leftover is a defect in the novelist's vocabulary or a sign the philosophers are fudging something. Don't hedge: take a definate line on which translation is better and why. British English, please.
