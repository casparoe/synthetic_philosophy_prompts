Write a dialogue between two colleagues about whether the genetic code is a code in more than a metaphorical sense, and whether anything turns on how that question is answered. The first is a molecular biologist who takes the language literally: the codon-to-amino-acid mapping is arbitrary and varies across lineages, so the genome is a text and the cell reads it, and the charge of metaphor is a philosopher's scruple. The second is a philosopher of biology who holds that "code," "information," and "program" are at best useful shorthand — that nothing in the chemistry is about anything, that the arbitrariness of the mapping is just the contingency of any evolved mechanism, and that "coding for" invites a picture of the genome as a set of self-interpreting instructions. Let each press the other on the strongest version of the disagreement, and make them work through one concrete case, for instance a bacterium engineered so that a codon now specifies a different amino acid: is that a new code, the same code with a modified decoder, or a distinction without a difference? Neither party should be a strawman or concede cheaply, and neither should talk past the other's actual arguments. End with one sentence from each saying what would change their mind. No citations or reading lists — argue from first principles. At most 900 words.
