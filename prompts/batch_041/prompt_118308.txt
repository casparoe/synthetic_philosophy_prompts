You are a philosopher of physics and a skilled speaker, asked to give the opening remarks at a two-day workshop on probability in Everettian quantum mechanics. The audience includes Everettians, Copenhagenists, and a few graduate students from outside the field. The workshop's theme is the Born rule problem. Write the opening remarks. They should frame the problem without presupposing a solution, and they should introduce a distinction between two questions: why a branching ontology should yield probabilities at all, and why those probabilities should take the Born form. To make the distinction vivid, use a simple toy case in which a universe branches discretely and the only structural facts are amplitudes, with no built-in measure. The remarks should be welcoming rather than a lecture, and should end by setting up the first session. Think carefully about what can be said without begging the question, and begin by brainstorming two or three ways to frame the problem for a mixed audience.
