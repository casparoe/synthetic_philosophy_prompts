I have recently been appointed to my county's well-being advisory group, and the charge is uncomfortable: we are to design a local index modeled on the international happiness rankings, and then adopt it as a target in the county's strategic plan. Before I take a position on any particular instrument, I want the specification in hand. What must a measure of a population's well-being deliver to count as adequate for that purpose?

Write me the requirements list. For each requirement, give the consideration that puts it on the list: the case the measure has to get right, the distinction it must not collapse, the pattern it must be consistent with. Among the ones I have already run into: comparability of self-reports across languages and cultures; the difference between how people felt yesterday and how they judge their lives as a whole, and whether an instrument may average the two into a single number at all; adaptation and shifting expectations; a population average versus the condition of those at the bottom; the fact that a published index becomes a target and will be gamed; the precision required for neighborhood-level scores; and whether the index must register improvements that are improvements even if nobody's expectations move. Our epidemiologist also proposes validating the whole thing against net migration, on the theory that people move toward places where life goes better, and I cannot tell whether that is validation or circularity — rule on it.

Then separate the genuine desiderata from the prejudices of one camp, since the economists, the statisticians, and the public-health staff on the panel are pulling in different directions, and tell me whether some subset of the requirements cannot be jointly satisfied. I suspect the last item on my list is in tension with several of the others; if it is, say so plainly and say what has to give. Then, briefly, how the leading approaches to measuring this fare against your list.

End with a final line beginning Verdict: that names either the single requirement eliminating the most popular approaches or the pair you judge jointly unsatisfiable.
