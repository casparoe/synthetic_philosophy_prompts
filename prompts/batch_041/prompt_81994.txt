Me and another RA in the sleep lab I work in keep getting into this argument and I want an outside read on it. My advisor's line is that dreams aren't experiences at all — nobody actually experiences anything while they're asleep, the brain just stitches a story together out of scraps as you start waking up, so a dream report is basically a memory of something that never happened. He likes to point out that when your alarm goes off you often find it was already inside the dream, as if the story got written after the fact to fit.

I think he's wrong, mostly because of two things we see constantly. First the lucid dreamers: they agree on a signal ahead of time (look left, right, left) and then during verified REM they do it, on cue, from inside the dream. Second, the guys with REM sleep behavior disorder who punch and kick and yell stuff that lines up with what they report when we wake them. He says neither of those shows that anyone is experiencing anything, it just shows the eye muscles and the motor system are doing things. That feels like a dodge to me but I can't say exactly why.

So who's got the better case, and what is the disagreement actually about — is it a dispute about facts we could settle with more data, or about what the word experience is even allowed to cover? I want to get this straight before I write the intro to my thesis. Bottom line first please, then the reasoning, no need to cite anything, keep the whole thing under 300 words.
