A teacher announces on Sunday: "There will be an exam on one of the next five days, and you will not be able to know in advance which day it will be on." The student reasons that the exam cannot fall on Friday, since if it had not occurred by Thursday night they would know it was Friday and it would be no surprise; the same step eliminates Thursday, and so on back to Monday, so there will be no exam. The exam is then held on Wednesday, to the student's surprise.

Consider this diagnosis: the backward induction is a valid reductio of the premise that the student knows the announcement, and what it establishes is that the announcement cannot be known in advance — not that it is false or that no exam will occur. The student's mistake is to run a reductio and then retain its intermediate conclusion.

Give me your credence that this diagnosis is the correct resolution of the paradox, decomposed. For each term below, give a number and one line of justification, then show how they combine: (a) how much rides on analyzing "surprise" in terms of knowledge rather than justified belief or merely low credence; (b) how much on whether the induction survives as valid once the knowledge premise is dropped, or whether the Friday step secretly assumes that the exam must occur; (c) how much on whether an announcement that is partly about the hearer's own epistemic state can function as a premise in the hearer's reasoning at all; (d) whether the diagnosis is substantive or merely restates the puzzle, which I want as a separate number. Then name the term you are least confident in and say which argument or piece of evidence would move your overall number most. Please answer in American English.
