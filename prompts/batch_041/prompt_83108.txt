im writing a short position piece on Molyneux's question for a seminar and i want to check the package before i present it, because i have the feeling that one bit of it is doing work the rest won't let it do and i can't see which bit. here's what i hold:

1. the answer is no — someone blind from birth who's been taught by touch to tell a cube from a sphere, and then gets his sight, wouldn't be able to pick out the cube just by looking.
2. and the reason is the empiricist one: you can't have the concept of a cube unless experience handed it to you. there's no innate stock of shape concepts, whatever else may be innate.
3. but i don't think experience is conceptual all the way down. i think there's a way a cube looks to you before you have the foggiest idea what a cube is. the content is there; the classification isn't.
4. i've been splitting the question in two: whether the cube *looks* cubical to him, and whether he can *tell* that it's the cube. i take Molyneux's question to be the second one, and i think the people who get worked up about innateness are usually answering the first.
5. i also think the modality boundaries the question assumes are less solid than they look. the camera-on-the-tongue, camera-on-the-back stuff, where blind people learn to navigate and even to reach for things, makes me doubt there's a determinate thing called the visual idea of a sphere for the newly sighted man to hold up against his tactual idea.
6. on the clinical cases — the people who had cataracts removed after decades of blindness, the children in the sight-restoration work — i think they're the best evidence we have about any of this, and also that they can't really answer the question as it was originally put.

which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix for each real clash? rank the real ones by how much i'd lose by giving something up, and tell me if there's a single revision that clears up more than one of them at once. alot rides on this and i'd rather not find out in the seminar that its incoherent.
