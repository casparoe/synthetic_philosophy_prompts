I'm a novelist at work on a book whose protagonist has staked her career on the zombie argument, and I've read enough of the literature to see that the fight between the zombie lovers and the zombie skeptics has not budged in decades: the same intuitions are exchanged, the same replies recited, and each side treats the other's central move as question-begging. I want a diagnosis of the impasse, not a summary of it. On one side: if a being physically identical to me yet wholly dark inside is genuinely conceivable, then such a being is possible, and physicalism is false; the skeptic's complaint that the conceiving is merely apparent misses the point, since the burden is on her to explain the appearance away. On the other: the appearance of a gap is precisely what a physical system equipped with the concepts it uses to represent its own states should generate -- the phenomenal concept strategy -- so the seeming coherence of the zombie is an artifact of our self-conception rather than evidence of a possible world, and the whole apparatus of silicon isomorphs and fading and dancing qualia is a hall of mirrors. Is this a verbal quarrel about the words consciousness or conceivability, a substantive disagreement about whether conceivability can ever be a guide to possibility, or two parties answering different questions -- one about worlds, one about concepts -- under a single heading? Is some hidden empirical or formal question doing the work? And given your diagnosis, what, if anything, would count as progress: a distinction, a result, a change of subject -- or nothing, because the dispute finally rests on intuitions neither side can argue for? Be concrete about what each side actually commits to, and tell me where my own suspicion -- that they are not really disagreeing about the mind at all -- is right, and where it is too neat.
