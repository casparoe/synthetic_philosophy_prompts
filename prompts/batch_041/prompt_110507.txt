My roommate read the Three-Body books over the summer and hasn't been the same since. Any time SETI or aliens comes up he says the same thing, that the universe is a dark forest, and treats it like it settles everything. His actual one-liner, which he repeats constantly, is 'everybody out there is hiding, and the ones who aren't are already dead.' The annoying part is he uses that single line for two totally diffrent jobs without noticing. Job one: it's his explanation for the Fermi paradox, why the sky is silent. Job two: it's his argument that we should never broadcast, and honestly that if we ever did catch a signal we should think hard before answering it. I want to argue back but I cant, because I think that line is secretly like five different claims, some of them maybe fine, some of them doing all the work.

So can you lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making, from the boring to the strong? Like — is it just 'space is dangerous and strangers can't be trusted,' which is basically the security dilemma from international relations except with no police, no trade and no history? Is it a game-theory claim that hide-and-shoot is the unique stable equilibrium, and if so what exactly has to be true for that — that a first strike always works, that you always see them before they see you, that nobody can make a credible promise, that there's nothing worth gaining from contact? Is it a prediction about what's actually out there, i.e. civilizations are common but quiet? Or is it sneakier than that, treating the silence as evidence for the theory when the silence is equally consistent with nobody being home, or everybody being home and loud but too far away, or us just being early?

For each reading I want a verdict: trivial, substantive and actually defensible, or false/overreach. And then the part I actually care about — which reading does job one need, which does job two need, and does one reading really do both, because my hunch is he's smuggling the second one in on the first. Lay it out as a list, I'm bringing it to dinner. And please don't just tell me it's unfalsifiable, I know that much.

One more thing, he also recites these axioms from the book, he calls it cosmic sociology: there are tons of civilizations out there, they always want to survive, they always expand, but the total matter in the universe is fixed. Plus two extra assumptions he names, the 'chain of suspicion' and the 'technological explosion.' If those are doing any of the work, use them in the menu and say so.
