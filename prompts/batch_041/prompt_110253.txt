Assume that every mental state is realized by some physical state of the brain, and that the physical realizer, once present, is by itself sufficient to bring about whatever bodily movement follows. I want to understand why this package is widely taken to leave the mental state with no causal work of its own to do, and why the problem is supposed to be sharper for someone who denies that mental properties are identical with physical ones than for someone who simply identifies them.

Please make the reasoning explicit rather than gesturing at the causal closure of the physical. In particular: distinguish the work done by closure from the work done by the sufficiency of the realizer, and say whether the argument needs both or whether either one alone does the damage. Explain the difference between the claim that the mental is nothing over and above the physical and the claim that it makes no causal difference, and identify the additional principle that is supposed to carry you from the first to the second. Then say whether the argument requires treating the mental state and its realizer as two distinct sufficient causes competing to produce a single effect, so that exclusion is really a verdict about overdetermination, or whether it can be run when they are one event under two descriptions, in which case the competition has to be reconstructed rather than assumed.

To test how much of the argument concerns the mind specifically and how much concerns higher-level causation in general, take a case with no mentality in it. A tornado causes a roof to collapse, yet a tornado is just a pattern in a collection of air molecules, and those molecules are already sufficient for the collapse. Run the exclusion reasoning on this case. If it yields the conclusion that the tornado does no causal work, say what that reveals about the original argument; if it does not, state precisely which premise fails here that was supposed to hold in the mental case. Finish by identifying the best available escape route for the non-reductive physicalist and what it costs.
