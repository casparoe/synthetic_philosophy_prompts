I host a small decision-theory podcast — maybe 300 listeners, mostly grad students and a couple of stubborn economists — and next month's guest is a philosopher who's spent the last few years arguing that decision theory should be stated over whole policies or plans rather than act by act, and who uses the absent-minded driver as her flagship case. I know the setup cold (two exits, first exit pays 0, second pays $4, sailing past the second pays $1, and he can't tell the exits apart, so his only strategy is a single randomization probability used at whichever exit he happens to be at), so please don't burn questions having her restate it.
