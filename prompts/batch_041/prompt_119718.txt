I've been reading the mechanistic anomaly detection material for a while now, and there's a step I can't get past. The pitch is that you flag a model when it produces the right output for the wrong reason, when the mechanism behind the behavior isn't the one you'd expect, but that presupposes that "same mechanism" names a definite equivalence class, and I don't see any of our tools supplying one. Sparse autoencoder features are identified only up to rotation and rescaling; circuits get individuated relative to a task and a choice of interchange interventions; and two networks that agree on every input we can test are behaviorally indistinguishable by construction. So the criterion looks like it has to be either whatever our instruments happen to resolve, in which case the whole program is out-of-distribution detection under a different name, or something we simply stipulate, in which case it is arbitrary. Given that this seems to be treated as a bookkeeping detail rather than the central difficulty, what is the principled account of mechanism identity that I'm missing?
