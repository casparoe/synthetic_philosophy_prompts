I'm a second-year philosophy PhD — action theory, mostly, though I've been reading my way into the free will literature for a chapter — and there's a shape of argument I keep running into that I don't have a name for.

Roughly: someone argues that nobody is ever really responsible for what they do, because being responsible for an action would require being responsible for the self that produced it, which would require being responsible for the self that produced that self, and so on. I say fine, but that only tells us about a very demanding notion of responsibility — the one where you're the ultimate author of yourself — and I don't think that's what the word is doing when I say my flatmate is responsible for leaving the window open in a hailstorm. They say the thin thing isn't responsibility, it's just a system for allocating blame. I say the blame-allocating thing is the only one worth the name. Round in circles for an hour, neither of us saying anything false, both of us getting a bit cross, which suggests somethings at stake. (I know this particular argument has a name and an owner. That isn't what I'm after.)

What I've realised is that we aren't disagreeing about any fact. We're disagreeing about which of two things the word should be tracking, and every argument either of us makes only gets off the ground if you've already settled that question our way. You can't dissolve it with 'right, two senses, use subscripts', because we each think the other person's sense is the one doing the real work. I've seen the same shape in arguments about whether machines can think — one side means inner experience, the other means information processing, and each side's arguemnt only works on its own reading — so it isn't a quirk of the free will debate.

Is there an established name for this? Not ordinary ambiguity: I know what that is, and this is worse, because the disagreement about which sense is the correct one is the whole disagreement. I have a vague sense that there's something in the literature about words whose proper use is itself up for grabs, and something about disputes that are really about our concepts rather than about the world, but I can't find the thread and I may be making both up. If there's a term, what is it, and how does it differ from the two or three nearest ones? If there isn't, coin one, with edges: it should cover this case and the machine-thinking case, and it shouldn't cover two economists who mean different things by 'recession' and both know it.
