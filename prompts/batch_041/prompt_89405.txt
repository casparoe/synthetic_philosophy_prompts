im putting together a handout for a population ethics reading group (grad students, mostly consequentialists) and the centrepiece is a 'which view about wronging future people do you actually hold?' quiz, because last time half the room called themselves person-affecting and it turned out they meant four or five incompatible things. so i want to sort them by what they actually say about cases, not by which label they like.

ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options. every question has to be answerable by someone who's never heard a single label — so no 'do you accept the person-affecting restriction', instead give the case and ask whether the agents did something wrong, and if so whether anyone in particular was wronged, or whether it was wrong in some way that isn't owed to anybody. avoid questions where one of the answers is obviously the humane one.

cases i want in there, though you can swap in your own where a case is doing too little work: (1) a pregnancy where the mother is warned the medication she's taking will cause permanent hearing loss in the child, and the child later says she's glad she was born; (2) a couple who know the child they're about to conceive will have a life that is barely worth living, and the only alternative is not conceiving at all; (3) an energy policy that will leave the world much poorer for whoever is around in two centuries, where adopting the policy also changes who ends up existing; (4) embryo selection where the only embryo that will implant carries a mild impairment, and the resulting person is glad to be alive; (5) a couple who conceive a child specifically so that she can donate tissue to a sick sibling; (6) a choice between a world with many people whose lives are slightly above zero and a world with fewer people whose lives are very good; (7) a case where nobody is worse off than they would have been under any alternative, but the future is worse in a way that isn't anyone's loss.

the scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these positions: totalism, the average view, a critical-level / threshold view, the narrow person-affecting restriction, the wide (conditional) person-affecting versions, a non-comparative account of harm on which being in a bad state is what harms you, the view that coming into existence can't harm or benefit anyone at all, and the asymmetry view on which procreation is never required and at worst presumptively wrong. give them names where there's a name and mark them unnamed where there isn't.

two more things i need from you. flag any combination of answers that corresponds to a position nobody actually defends, and say briefly whether that's because the view is incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. and tell me which of your leaves the quiz can't separate — if two positions give the same answers across all ten questions, say so rather than pretending one question does the work. also make sure a couple of the questions are ones where people who share a label will still disagree with each other, otherwise the quiz is just a label detector.
