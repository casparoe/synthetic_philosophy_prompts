I am a PhD student in formal epistemology working on resource-bounded agency and the problem of logical omniscience, and I am preparing a satirical piece for a departmental humor panel. Please write a mock employee handbook—roughly 900–1100 words, in numbered sections, with a short preamble and a signature block—for a fictional firm, Deductive Dynamics, whose compliance and HR departments have adopted the ideal agent of classical epistemic logic as their literal model of an employee. The deadpan target is the idealization itself: closure under entailment, knowledge of all validities, consistency requirements, and the absence of any distinction between explicit and implicit belief. The humor should come from the formal content, not from generic corporate absurdity. Include, for instance: an onboarding clause presuming that every new hire has already derived every consequence of the handbook; a provision that ignorance of an entailment is no defense; an awareness audit; a contradiction clause under which one inconsistent timesheet entitles the firm to every conclusion, including the employee’s promotion; a reasonable-accommodation form for bounded agents that is itself available only to agents who do not need it; and a grievance procedure for an employee who believes a conjunction but not one of its conjuncts. A brief memo from the Office of Deductive Compliance would help, as would a dissenting footnote from a staff logician who proposes one of the standard repairs—awareness logic, impossible worlds, or resource-bounded reasoning—and is overruled on the ground that the proposed repair is not itself deductively closed. The piece should be funny to someone who knows the problem of logical omniscience and legible to someone who does not. Do not cite any works or use any real names, and do not break the deadpan with a wink to the reader.
