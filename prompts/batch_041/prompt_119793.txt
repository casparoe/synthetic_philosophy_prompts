I'm on the board of our housing co-op (24 units, we run the place ourselves, no property manager) and we've gotten into a stupid fight about how we decide things, and I think I've embarassed myself by citing something I don't actually understand. My proposal was that we stop trying to get everyone to agree on everything and just put things to a vote, and specifically that more voters is always better, because I read about this result where if each person is even a little bit more likely than not to get the answer right, and they each make up their own mind instead of copying each other, then the more people you add the closer you get to being certain that the majority has the right answer. The write-up I read used a jury as the example and said something like a jury of several hundred would be right essentially every single time, and the moral was that majority rule is a truth-finding machine, which sounded amazing to me. So my plan was: every decision goes to a vote, everybody votes, and we should want as many people voting as possible, because a couple hundred people basically can't be wrong. Someone on the board shot back that the very same guy (Condorcet? some French guy from the 1700s, I definately read the whole thing but I'm blanking) also proved that majority voting is incoherent -- you can set it up so that most of us prefer fixing the roof to the new fence, most prefer the fence to repaving the lot, and most prefer repaving the lot to fixing the roof, so there's no majority will at all, it just goes around in a circle. He says the crowd thing is pop science and the circle thing is the actual result.

So which is it? I feel like I'm missing something, because both of these are supposed to be about the same thing, adding up what most people think, and they can't both be right about whether majority rule finds the truth. Is one of them debunked, or did he change his mind later? And if the circle thing is real, does it go away if we switch to ranked choice voting? Three of us want ranked choice anyway, and if that fixes the circles I'll just vote yes and stop worrying about it. What I actually need to know is whether I should keep pushing the vote-on-everything-everyone-votes plan or whether I've built the whole thing on a misreading. For what it's worth, on the roof question none of us knows anything about roofs -- we all just read the same two quotes from the same contractor, and we all trust the one guy on the board who used to do construction -- so I don't know if that changes the math or not.
