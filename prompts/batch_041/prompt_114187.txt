my reading group has been doing Parfit all term and i got stuck with the teletransporter chapter, so next week its my turn to present. you know the setup — you lie down, the scanner reads you, you get vaporised, and a copy assembles on mars with all your memories and your lip still nicked from shaving that morning. everyone in the group already knows the setup cold, so i want to close the session with a poem instead of a recap.

can you write me a pantoum (the form where the 2nd and 4th lines of each stanza come back as the 1st and 3rd of the next, and the last stanza loops back to the first line), in the voice of the copy that wakes up on mars. i picked the pantoum on purpose: it keeps half-carrying its own material forward and then closes the loop, which is more or less the shape of parfits point — you can list everything that carries over (the memories, the temper, the sore tooth, the way it flinches at its fathers voice) and there's simply nothing left over that would settle whether this is the same person or a new one. so i want it to land on the actual conclusion and not the horror-movie version: not 'oh no, im a copy', but 'whether im a copy turns out not to be a determinate question, and the part i actually cared about is the part that got carried across, and it comes in degrees anyway'. maybe five or six stanzas.

the bit im fussy about: let it notice that it remembers lying down in the scanner, the countdown, the cold of the plate, and then waking up on mars — and that it has no way of telling whether the thing that got switched off was it, or whether it was the one who walked in. it remembers its own death as a blank it cant find the edges of. and let it treat its memories of the original's life as memories in every way that matters even though they aren't strictly its own — as the ordinary case, not a horror. thats the move i want it to make without announcing it.

should hold up for the two people in the group who've read parfit properly, and still land for the ones who only came for the snacks.
