Can you write a dialogue between two people arguing about whether eating beef is better or worse than a vegan diet, once you factor in crop cultivation and wild animal suffering? Person A is a utilitarian-ish animal welfare type who's arrived at the surprising conclusion that pasture-raised beef is the low-harm option — their reasoning being that you get a lot of calories per cow, that chicken is a disaster by comparison (dozens of birds a year for one person), and that harvesting crops for a vegan diet kills a ton of field animals per hectare — mice, voles, rabbits, insects — so the vegan isn't obviously ahead. Person B is a vegan who's read all the same stuff and pushes back hard, definately not a strawman: they should make the marginal-vs-average point (cattle are eating feed crops, so those deaths are on the beef eater's tab too, and skipping the beef doesn't stop the harvest but does change how much of it happens), the replaceability point (field mice are r-strategists, populations bounce back, so it's not clear a death costs a life that wouldn't otherwise exist), and the point that a pasture isn't death-free either — grazing, haying, predators, insects. Let them really go back and forth on the accounting: deaths per calorie vs. suffering per calorie; why a chicken's life might be worse than a cow's even if you count one death each; whether a quick death in a thresher is actually better or worse than starving or being taken by a predator; and whether the field mouse at least got some life-years out of the deal, so the metric should be net welfare per hectare rather than a body count. Also have them get into whether insect deaths should count, how much the uncertainty about insect sentience should move the verdict, and whether that uncertainty cuts toward eating beef or toward not counting crop deaths at all. Neither should be handed the better arguments — both competent, both landing somewhere honest, even if it's just a sharper disagreement. Don't worry about citing sources or dumping stats, I care about the arguement itself. And no lecturing from either side; they should respond to each other's actual points.
