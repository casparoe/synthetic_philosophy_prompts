I teach a 400-level seminar on free will and moral responsibility, and every year I watch a room full of otherwise sharp students collapse the whole debate into compatibilist vs. incompatibilist within ten minutes of the first meeting, then spend the rest of term defending whichever label they grabbed before they understood the argument. So I want a decision tree I can hand out after we've done the Frankfurt-style cases — one that sorts them by which step of the argument they actually bail out at, not by which -ism they like. Take the standard setup: Jones does the thing on his own, for his own reasons, and a hidden intervener would have made sure he did it anyway had he shown any sign of waivering. Root the tree at "is Jones blameworthy for what he does?" and build down through the questions that do the real seperating: whether we're reading the case as deterministic or as leaving room for indeterminism in the actual sequence; whether the residual "he could still have decided differently" possibility counts as a genuine alternative or as a mere flicker; whether "could have done otherwise" is a categorical claim, a conditional one, or a matter of disposition; whether the requirement in play is a requirement on free action or on responsible action; and whether the blame is pinned to the act, the intention, or the character behind it. Two nodes I especially want in there, because I think they're where the real splits hide: one on whether the intervener has to be external to the agent (some of my students insist that a sufficiently settled character just is the intervener, and I want the tree to show what that move costs them), and one on whether the intervener would have caused the action in the counterfactual branch or merely blocked the alternative — I keep suspecting those get treated as one case when they shouldn't be. I've also been cooking up a variant I'd like the tree to handle: same story, except there's no manipulator at all, just a standing disposition of Jones's own — a deep cowardice, say — that would have stopped him backing out. Some students say that's still a Frankfurt case, others say it's just a compatibilist picture of character in disguise, and I want the tree to force a verdict.

Leaves should be named positions where a name exists and "nobody, as far as I know" where it doesn't: leeway incompatibilism, source incompatibilism, semi-compatibilism, the conditional-analysis compatibilist, the responsibility-without-freedom view, the view that the whole case is incoherent (either it begs the question or it leaves a flicker), and the attributability-only line that Jones is answerable for the act but not accountable for it. One sentence per leaf on the commitment that view has the hardest time explaining away. Aim for eight to ten nodes — every node has to be a question they can answer in plain English without knowing where it leads, so no node that just asks them to pick a school. And please flag any leaves the tree can't actually pull apart; I'd rather have two positions in one bucket with a note than a fake fork. If some combination of answers is a spot no real position occupies, say so and tell me whether that's becuase the view would be incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. Don't bother citing anyone, I know the literature.
