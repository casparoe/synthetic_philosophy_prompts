ok, so I'm sitting in on a metaphysics seminar this term and they've asked everyone to write down where they stand before we get picked apart. here's my package, and I'd like a straight audit of it before I hand it in.

- No universals. I don't think properties are repeatable things that lots of objects somehow share. Whatever properties are, they're not something over and above the particular things that have them.
- Objective resemblance. Some things really are more alike than others, and that's a fact about them, not about our words or our sorting habits. When two things resemble each other they resemble each other because of how each of them is -- I don't want to say they're alike by both latching onto some one thing.
- Laws aren't regularities. A generalization is a law only if the one property actually necessitates the other; a true, exceptionless generalization that merely happens to hold isn't a law.
- No brute necessities. If something is necessary there's an explanation of why it's necessary. 'It just is' is not an answer.
- Naturalism. Everything that exists is either what our best physics posits or built out of it. No abstract objects, no extra realm.

I've got a nagging feeling that one or two of these are living off credit the others can't extend, but I honestly can't see where. What I want: which of these are genuinly pulling against each other, ranked by how bad it is, and which pairs only look tense and are actually fine (please don't just flag everything as a problem, that's useless to me). For each real conflict, the cheapest fix and what that fix costs -- i.e. which other thing in the list I have to give up to get it. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and whether I'm kidding myself that it's all just loose wording on my part. No reading list, just the audit.
