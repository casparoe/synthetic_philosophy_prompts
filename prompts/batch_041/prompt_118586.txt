After thirty-five years as a physical chemist, I retired and began reading philosophy in earnest, and I have spent the last several months circling al-Ghazālī's seventeenth discussion in the Incoherence of the Philosophers. I had always assumed that the regularities I spent my career measuring were necessary in some sense—that when flame meets cotton, the burning must follow. Al-Ghazālī denies the must while granting the follows, and I cannot dismiss the move as pre-scientific: he concedes that the burning never fails to accompany the contact, and denies only that the connection is necessary, holding that what we call laws of nature are better described as the customary course of divine action, so that the burning is produced by God, either immediately or through some intermediary. On this view an interruption of the usual sequence would be no contradiction, only a departure from custom, and the sciences are not refuted but reinterpreted.

Write me a villanelle that keeps both halves of the position in view at once. I find that whenever I try to explain it aloud I collapse it into one of two errors: either fire does not really burn anything, which is not what al-Ghazālī says, or of course fire burns things, which quietly grants the necessity he denies. Let the two refrains be the description and the denial respectively—one line recording the regularity as I would once have recorded it in the lab, one line refusing the necessity of that sequence or naming what actually produces the effect. The obsessive return of the form should do the argumentative work: the description must keep coming back, as the regularity does, and the denial must keep coming back alongside it, so that in the closing quatrain, when the two lines finally stand together, the reader can hear that they were never rivals. Please let the notion of custom or habit carry its full weight rather than sounding like a demotion, and let the poem register without resolving whether an intermediary in the chain of production changes anything essential or merely relocates the same puzzle. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows the debate will recognize the position rather than the name-drop, and clear enough that someone who does not will still have a poem.
