I spent most of last night arguing with a close friend from my graduate seminar in normative ethics, and we ended at an impasse I can't resolve alone. She holds that the problem usually called the paradox of deontology is fatal to any non-consequentialist ethics of constraint: the prohibition on killing one innocent to save five (and the whole family of agent-centered restrictions) is supposed to be grounded in the equal and inviolable worth of each person, yet the restriction itself requires us to let five be killed rather than kill one, and so it seems to license more of the very violation it is meant to honor. Her conclusion is that any view that keeps the restriction must be leaning on something else — either a smuggled-in concern with maximizing the good, or a self-regarding concern with the agent's own clean hands that cannot be squared with equal respect for all persons. I think this is too fast. I suspect the argument trades on an aggregationist assumption about what it means for a consideration to ground a constraint, or on a confusion between the level at which we justify a rule and the level at which an agent deliberates under it, and I've been trying to say that constraints might be grounded in a distinctive wrong done to the victim that is not a homogeneous bad to be summed. But when she presses me I cannot tell whether I have a real distinction or only a redescription.

I'd like your own credence, as a number, in this proposition: there exists a defensible, non-consequentialist account of agent-centered constraints — one on which the prohibition on killing one to save five is justified without collapsing into either a disguised appeal to maximizing the good or an unjustifiable privileging of the agent's own agency, and one that a reasonable person could accept once it is fully spelled out. Give the bottom line first, in a sentence or two, then a decomposition as a table with one row per sub-question, each row carrying a rough probability and a single line of justification: whether the paradox is a genuine tension or a level-confusion that dissolves once the justification of rules is separated from deliberation under them; whether the wrong done to the victim is a distinctive kind of violation rather than a quantity of harm, and whether that is enough to block the summing move; whether the agent-relative reasons invoked to defend prerogatives can do the same work for restrictions without becoming merely self-regarding; whether a contractualist or Kantian grounding sidesteps the problem rather than relocating it; and whether the appearance of paradox depends on an assumption that any consideration grounding a constraint must ground proportionally more reason the more of it there is.

Then give me the combination, name the single sub-question my number is most sensitive to, and tell me what argument or thought experiment would move it most. Since my friend sits at about 0.15 and I'm at about 0.7, also tell me which of those two numbers is closer to yours and which sub-question our disagreement most likely lives in. Keep the whole thing to 900 words or fewer, and don't hedge with it-depends-on-your-metaethics — I want the numbers and the load-bearing terms.
