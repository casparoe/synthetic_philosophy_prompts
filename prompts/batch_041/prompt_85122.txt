I'm in a history seminar on the First World War and we just spent two weeks on the Fischer controversy, the whole fight over how much of the blame for starting the war belongs to Germany, and I keep getting stuck on something that I'm pretty sure isn't really a history question. What I can't stop noticing is that everybody in the debate, Fischer's critics included, is perfectly happy to say that Germany wanted the war, or that Germany felt encircled, or that Germany miscalculated, and it's never clear who or what Germany is supposed to be in those sentences. Sometimes it's the Kaiser and two or three ministers in a room. Sometimes it's the general staff and the whole officer culture. Sometimes it's a mood that had taken hold of the educated middle class. Sometimes it's just the state as a legal person that signs treaties. And the switch seems to track the verdict the speaker wants. If they want a conviction, Germany shrinks down to the handful of men who actually made the calls. If they want an acquittal, Germany swells out into a system, the alliances, the mobilization timetables, the arms race, that nobody in particular chose, and then, conveniently, a system can't be guilty of anything. Both moves feel legitimate from the inside. What bothers me is that this wierd ambiguity never gets flagged. It just quietly does the work.

Is there a name for that move? Not for the Fischer debate itself, I mean the shape of it, treating a collective as one agent with intentions and a will when you want to praise or condemn it, and then dissolving it back into a crowd of seperate people when the condemnation starts to cost you something, flipping whichever way suits you. I've seen the same shape in arguments about the banks and the 2008 crash, and about the West, and about the police. A friend said it's just scapegoating, but that doesn't seem right, because scapegoating is about blame landing on the wrong target, whereas here the point is that the target keeps changing shape so it never quite lands at all. I've also heard the phrase collective responsibility thrown around, but as far as I can tell that's a different question, that one is about whether a group can bear responsibility at all, whereas what I'm after is the opportunistic flip between the group and its members.

So does this have a name, and if it doesn't, can you name it and mark out its edges? The label isn't really what I'm after. I want to know what the thing actually is and what it isn't, whether it's a fallacy or just an ordinary feature of how we talk about big events, and, honestly, since we're here, whether a nation can be responsible for a war in the first place, or whether that's a sentence we should just stop using. I have a nagging feeling that the two questions are really the same question, but I can't see how yet.
