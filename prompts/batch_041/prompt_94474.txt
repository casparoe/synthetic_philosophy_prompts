Ok so I'm a 4th year in cog sci, and I've been going back and forth with my superviser about colour phi for like two months, and I think I've argued myself into something stupid. Tell me which step to give up and what it costs.

Setup is the standard one: blue dot up top, red dot below, ~150ms apart. People report a single dot travelling down that switches from blue to red about halfway along the path — i.e. they report the change as happening before the second dot was ever on the screen. Fine, that's the phenomenon, it's been replicated a million times.

Here's the argument. I built most of it myself, which is probably the problem.

(1) At the time subjects report the colour change as occurring, the second stimulus has not yet been presented. (Empirical, not up for grabs.)
(2) So the content blue-to-red-at-midpath can't have been caused by the second stimulus at the time it's reported as occurring. It's fixed by information arriving later.
(3) Any state whose content is fixed by information arriving later than the time that state is experienced as occurring can't be a faithful record of what it was like at that time. It's either (a) a construction built only after the fact and stamped with an earlier time, or (b) an earlier state that got overwritten. (My supervisor calls these the Stalinesque and Orwellian options, which I gather is not original.)
(4) Under (a) there was no experience of red at the midpoint; under (b) there was, but no trace of it survives. And nothing can distinguish (a) from (b), since the only access anyone has to the midpoint is via states of one of those two kinds. So there is no fact about what it was like at the midpoint.
(5) Nothing here is special to phi. Every perceptual content is fixed at least partly by information arriving after stimulus onset — latency, postdiction, flash-lag, backward masking are all the same shape.
(6) So for any experience at any time, there's no fact about what it was like then — only facts about what the system ends up reporting or storing.
(7) So first-person reports about the timing of experience are never evidence about experience, only about the reporting machinery.
(8) And if a subject's own reports aren't evidence about her experience, nothing else is either. What it was like at t is doing no work.
(9) So eliminativism about the temporal location of experience — and since an experience that occurs at no time isn't an occurrence, about experience.

I'd like to keep (1), (2) and (5); (5) especially seems airtight, I can't see how perception could possibly work otherwise. (9) is obviously nuts, I'm not giving up on there being something it's like to be me right now. But when I try to say which step I actually don't believe, I end up either denying something empirical or biting a bullet about my own present experience, and I can't tell which is worse. My superviser says this is Dennett with the serial numbers filed off and I should just swallow (4) and stop being squeamish, which doesn't help, because swallowing (4) about one midpoint doesn't obviously commit me to (6) about everything.

Which step do I reject, why did it look fine before I started poking at it, and what am I paying? If the move is to keep (4) but block (6), say what extra premise does the blocking and whether it's independently motivated or just bolted on.
