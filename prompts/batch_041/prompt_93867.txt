Write an essay on whether karma can serve as a theory of moral desert — whether the claim that a person suffers because of their own past actions can do the work that desert is meant to do, rather than merely describing a causal order that happens to run through human lives.

To keep the question sharp, work with a distinction I'll stipulate. On the forensic reading, karma is a ledger: wrongs create debts, and the debts must eventually be paid by the one who incurred them; the picture presupposes something like a bookkeeper, or at least a book. On the naturalistic reading, karma is simply a law — actions produce like effects in the life of the agent, with no ledger, no judge, and no intention behind the arrangement as a whole. The naturalistic reading is the more defensible one for anyone who wants karma without the cosmology, and its defender owes us a thesis of roughly this shape: an instance of suffering is deserved when it is traceable to the sufferer's own wrongful action through the right kind of causal chain. Call that the traceability thesis. Your essay should decide whether the traceability thesis can be made good.

Address at least these four pressure points, in whatever order serves your argument. First, whether traceability is the right relation for desert at all: desert seems to require that the outcome obtain because of the action, in a sense that mere causal descent from it may not secure. Second, the problem of persons. Consider someone born with a painful and disabling condition that the naturalistic theory traces to an act of cruelty in a previous life — a life she cannot remember, lived by a character with none of her present attachments, tastes, or commitments. Ask whether she deserves the condition, and then ask whether the naturalistic theorist can answer without quietly reinstating the very self whose persistence the ledger reading seemed to require. Third, calibration: a law of nature has no reason to make the magnitude of suffering match the magnitude of the wrong, and if the reply is that the moral quality of the act itself fixes the magnitude, say what that could mean for a law that is not the work of any mind. Fourth, the forward-looking retreat: some will say karma is best understood not as a claim about desert but as a device that makes responsibility intelligible and reform attractive. Decide whether that is a rescue or a change of subject.

Reach a definite verdict; do not end by reporting that each reading has its costs. Say which of the following you accept, and defend it: the naturalistic reading can ground desert; it cannot, and only something like the forensic reading could; or no karmic theory grounds desert, and karma is better understood as something other than a theory of desert. Argue from the positions themselves rather than from what authorities have said about them, and state plainly what you take to be the strongest objection to your verdict and why it does not move you.
