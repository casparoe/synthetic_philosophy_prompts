I teach an upper-level epistemology seminar — 14 students, one 75-minute session — and I want them to feel the dogmatism paradox from the inside before anyone says the word dogmatism. I have the skeleton of an activity and I need you to make it actually runnable.

Setup: everyone gets a sealed source card stating a factual claim plus where the claim came from (you saw it happen / a reliable instrument read it off / you worked it out yourself). About half the cards are secretly backed by a truth sheet I keep, half aren't, and nothing on the card tells the player which kind they're holding. Everyone starts with 10 chips and stakes some on their claim.

Then I announce the house rule: your card is a case of knowledge, so any evidence that turns up against your claim is by definition misleading evidence, and you may discard contrary evidence for free — no chips lost, no cost, no justification needed. Then I deal counter-evidence cards over three rounds, some of them genuinely decisive, some junk.

What I need from you: a scoring scheme where discarding really is the smart play for someone who knows and really is ruinous for someone who merely believes, and where the two are indistinguishable from the inside. Chips, not money. Before I reveal the truth sheet, each table has to write down, in one sentence, the general principle that licenses their discards — I want them to generate that sentence themselves. Then a debrief that maps each moment of the game onto the paradox: the factivity of knowledge, the closure step that lets you infer from "if I know, contrary evidence is misleading" to "I know that contrary evidence is misleading," and why the conclusion (ignore future evidence against anything you now know) is both absurd and hard to locate a bad step in.

Also: what predictably goes wrong the first time I run this, and how do I handle it? My guess is some students will refuse to play and argue the rule is rigged, and others will smell the trick early and just update every time. And be honest with me about what the activity can't reproduce — I suspect it can't reproduce the agent's own perspective, since I've stipulated the know/believe split with an oracle that real life doesn't hand out, and it probably can't make the closure step feel questionable, since the game makes discarding feel perfectly licensed. Say where the facilitator has to do the work instead.
