I have been reading about Hofstadter's careenium: a frictionless table covered with countless tiny magnetic marbles, which he calls simms, that collide and clump into larger, semi-stable clusters, simmballs, which shift when the walls flex in response to the outside world, so that the clusters can be read as symbols standing for events in the environment. Hofstadter asks who is shoving whom around inside the careenium, and answers that it is the simmballs that shove one another about, at their own symbolic level. I cannot see how that answer can be coherent. If every sim moves only as the laws of mechanics dictate, then the microphysics already fixes every collision, and the simmballs can contribute no push of their own; so his talk of symbols shoving things around must be either a covert dualism that smuggles in a higher-level force, or else a mere redescription of what the simms were doing anyway — in which case the symbolic level is causally inert and the belief that my thoughts move my body is a comfortable illusion. Which of the two is it, and if neither, what precisely is the third option? Assume that I accept the microphysics in full and have no patience for souls. Answer in British English, and end with a final line beginning 'Verdict:' that states in a short phrase where my reasoning goes wrong.
