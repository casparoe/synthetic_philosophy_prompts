My old boss is retiring after forty years as a psychiatrist, and I've been asked to give the toast at her dinner. Here's the awkward part. In the late 80s she wrote the criteria for a diagnosis that's now in the manual. The first version said the patients avoid social contact; the current version says they're worn out by the extra work of being readable to other people, and that rewrite came from the patients, not from the clinicians. So the room splits three ways: the clinicians who think she identified a real thing, a table of people who carry the label and think she invented them, and her husband and kids, who mostly remember the conferences. Write the toast, about six minutes. I want it to take Hacking's looping effects seriously -- the category changes the people in it, and they change the category back -- and to actually use that rather than just name it. It can't call what she did a discovery, and it can't shrug into "it's all socially constructed", because that's just the other half of the room. And it has to be warm enough that her family isn't sitting through a seminar. Definately no citations.
