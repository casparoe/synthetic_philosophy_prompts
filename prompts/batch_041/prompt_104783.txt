I am trying to state my view of the architecture of mind cleanly enough that someone can examine it, and what I want back is an audit rather than an exposition. The view is built on a layering of four grades of cognitive capacity, which I will name for convenience:

- Darwinian creatures: their competence is fixed by natural selection; they cannot learn within their lifetimes.
- Skinnerian creatures: they are reshaped by reinforcement, so their competence is tuned by the actual consequences of their own behaviour.
- Popperian creatures: they can run a model of the world internally and let hypotheses die in their stead, so their behaviour is guided by anticipated consequences rather than actual ones.
- Gregorian creatures: they inherit thinking tools — words, notations, procedures — from others, so their competence is amplified by artefacts they did not invent.

On top of that taxonomy I hold the following commitments.

(1) The four grades mark differences in kind, not merely differences of degree: each grade adds a capacity that the grade below it cannot simulate.

(2) Nothing in the picture is teleological. Every grade, including the top one, is selection — variation and selective retention, run over bodies, over behaviours, over inner models, or over culturally transmitted tools. There is no little person inside directing the show.

(3) A Popperian creature's behaviour is reason-sensitive in a way a Skinnerian creature's is not: we explain what it does by citing its reasons, and those reasons can themselves be assessed as better or worse.

(4) Reasons are genuine causes. Explaining an action by the agent's reasons is not a redescription of its reinforcement history; it is a different and non-redundant kind of explanation.

(5) Praise and blame attach at the Popperian grade, because that is where a creature can let a hypothesis die instead of itself, and so can be said to have done otherwise.

(6) As an empirical matter, operant conditioning is a far weaker explanation of adult human behaviour than its partisans supposed; what actually does the work is the inherited stock of thinking tools — the Gregorian layer — and that stock is social before it is individual.

I would like to know whether this package hangs together. Tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to conflict and why they are in fact compatible, and, for each genuine tension, how severe it is and what would have to give in order to resolve it. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging, and finish by naming the single revision that would repair the most at the least cost, together with what that revision would cost me.
