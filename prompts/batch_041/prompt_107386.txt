My friend and I have just spent two days arguing about who should get to vote on our community land trust's proposed sale of its last open parcel, and I want your read on where she will come down. She sits on the board, and her record on past membership questions is consistent: she voted to let renters and live-in caregivers vote, and also owner-occupiers who were behind on their ground-lease payments, but she voted against letting a former member who moved two blocks away yet still attends every meeting, a neighboring shopkeeper whose delivery access the sale would destroy, and a co-founder who now lives out of state and merely fundraises for the trust. Throughout the argument she kept saying the line is not who is touched but who is governed — that the demos is whoever the trust's decisions bind, not whoever they affect — while I kept insisting that a decision that can ruin a neighbor's livelihood ought to give that neighbor a say. The board now faces a new case: a household just outside the trust's boundary whose garden plot is leased from the trust and whose members are bound by the trust's bylaws, including its maintenance and dispute-resolution rules, but who do not live on the land. Predict how she will vote, identify the rule she is actually running, give the other rules that fit her past votes just as well, say which of them the new case separates, and name the further case I should put to her to force the issue.
