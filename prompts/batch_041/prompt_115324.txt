That's the colour phi phenomenon, and the article says philosophers are fond of it because it looks like the brain working backwards in time, furnishing the past with information it couldn't possibly have had. It then says Daniel Dennett cleared this up by distinguishing two possible stories. The Orwellian one: you have the experience in real time, but your memory of it is quietly rewritten afterwards, so the record no longer matches what happened. The Stalinesque one: the brain sits on the input, waits for the second dot, and then manufactures a single edited experience and serves it up to you as though it had unfolded in real time. According to the article, Dennett showed the Stalinesque story is the true one — the brain really does build a fake experience after the fact, which is why the effect has that faint flavour of precognition — and that the filled-in colour is 'epiphenomenal', a decorative overlay on the brain's real business. The closing section ropes in Libet's readiness-potential experiments and concludes that if the brain can doctor experience like this, consciousness is essentially a story the brain tells itself about itself after the fact, so the self and free will are illusions.
