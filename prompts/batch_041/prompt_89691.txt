I chair the use-of-force review board of a mid-sized city police department and need to circulate a written opinion before Thursday's vote. The case before us is an officer who shot and killed a man who turned out to be holding a phone; the officer's report says he saw a gun. Our policy asks whether the officer's belief was reasonable given the circumstances as he perceived them, and the board is split over what that means when the perception itself may have been shaped by the officer's expectations. One member argues that because what a person expects and believes can change what he literally sees, an officer's report of a gun is evidence about his own state rather than about the world, and a mistaken perception of this kind is therefore a failure of judgment for which he is answerable; another argues that if expectation shapes perception, the officer had no more control over seeing a gun than over seeing a color, so the error is a malfunction to be corrected by training rather than blamed. Draft the opinion I will circulate: it should recommend a concrete rule for how the board should treat an officer's report of a perceived threat when the object turns out not to be a threat, including whether the rule should treat an officer whose expectation was itself reasonable differently from one whose expectation was not, say how the rule applies to this case and what disposition it implies, and give the strongest version of the argument on the side you reject its due.
