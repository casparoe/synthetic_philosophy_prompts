Write a self-help column of about 600 words in the deadpan voice of a wellness coach who has absorbed the free energy principle and now markets it as a complete philosophy of living. Use the format of a weekly advice column: a short opening pitch, then two or three reader questions with the coach's answers.

The comedy must come from taking the machinery seriously rather than inventing it. The coach treats the minimization of prediction error as the highest good, and therefore recommends — with total sincerity and no sense of the irony — a sensory-deprivation "Dark Room Protocol," while treating any reader who raises the familiar objection (if surprise is the thing to avoid, the dark room wins) as someone whose own generative model is simply malfunctioning. The self is a Markov blanket to be defended against intrusion; active inference means you receive the outcomes you predict, so the prescription for a failing marriage or a dead-end job is to predict better; "attention" is precision weighting; and everything from a bacterium to the coach's own subscription newsletter is one and the same process of minimizing free energy. Include a skeptical reader's letter pressing the charge that a framework which explains every possible behavior thereby explains none, and have the coach absorb the objection cheerfully as further confirmation of the theory.

The result should be funny to a reader who knows the technical debates — the dark-room problem, the smuggled-in prior preferences that quietly reintroduce teleology, the unfalsifiability worry — and legible to a reader who does not, and it should never wink.
