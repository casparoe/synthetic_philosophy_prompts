I belong to a small reading group on biological signaling — mostly biologists and a couple of philosophers of biology — and every year we close out the term with a short "signaling jokes" slot, and every year we get the same peacock gag, in which the tail is supposed to be funny merely because it is expensive. I would like one that is actually built on the theory rather than on the well-worn observation that some animals are wasteful. Here is the machinery I want the joke to turn on, since it is exactly the part people get wrong and I would like the humor to live there rather than in the slogans.

The handicap principle is almost always paraphrased as "the signal is honest because it is costly," and that paraphrase, taken literally, is false. A trait that is expensive in the same way for every bearer — a fixed tax of calories, say — carries no information about quality, because the low-quality individual pays no more for it than the high-quality one does; either both can afford it or neither can, and nothing is revealed. What does the informational work is differential cost: the display has to be more affordable for a high-quality bearer than for a low-quality one, so that only the former can run it at a profit. A second distinction I would like somewhere in the background is the contrast between a signal and an index — an index being a trait that cannot be faked at all, because it is causally bound up with the quality it advertises (a body size, the pitch of a call fixed by the length of the vocal tract), as against a signal proper, which could in principle be faked and is kept honest only by the differential cost. With that in mind, write me a joke.

It can be about animals, about humans, or about the theorists themselves; a one-liner or a little scene, whichever serves. The test I intend to apply is this: the joke should be funny to someone who actually knows the material, and it should stop being funny — or stop making sense — if you swap the handicap principle out for some other account of honest signaling. In other words, I want the punchline to turn on the real logic, whether that is the differential-cost condition or the fakeable-signal-versus-unfakeable-index line, and not on the bare fact that some displays are costly. A short setup would help me see that it is doing genuine work; and if it helps you get started, feel free to seed it with a species of your own invention whose display misfires in some way the theory would predict.
