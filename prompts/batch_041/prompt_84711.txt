Two friends are standing at the mailbox with the entry forms for one of those luring lotteries: a hundred thousand people got invited, the prize is $100,000 split evenly among everyone who enters, and it costs $2 to enter (you mail in the form with a cheque). If nobody enters, nobody gets anything. Write a dialogue between them.

One of them is a superrationalist. Her line: the question isnt what should I do, its what should everyone who reasons the way I do do — and if all of us enter we each get $1 and pay $2, so none of us should. The lure is the fantasy that you'll be the one clever person who enters alone. The other thinks that's logic-shaped nonsense: 'everyone who reasons like me' isn't a group, it's a way of assuming strangers will do what you want; the only choice correlated with mine is mine; and plenty of people will just ignore the letter, in which case entering is a great deal. (The mailing list is obviously half philosophy department, half sweepstakes, so neither of them knows which kind of pool they're in.)

Make it a real fight between two smart people who are actually listening to each other. He should have a real answer to the symmetry argument — something like, correlation needs a common cause, and a common cause doesn't give my cheque any power over anyone else's — and she should have a real answer to that. Let them go at the thing neither of them can settle: how many people, if any, actually reason the way she does, and whether that number changes the answer or just changes who the sucker is. Neither of them can remember who invented any of this, so no names and no citing papers. Have them do the arithmetic out loud at least once. Around 700-800 words.
