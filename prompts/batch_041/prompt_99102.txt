Write a dialogue between two Bayesians who are stuck in a Judy Benjamin-style situation and disagree about how to update on a conditional. The setup: you've been dropped somewhere in a territory divided into Blue and Red. Red is split into Headquarters and Second Company. Your prior is P(Blue)=1/2, P(Red)=1/2, and within Red you think HQ and Second Company are equally likely. A radio operator tells you: "I can't tell you whether you're in Blue or Red, but if you're in Red, you're in Headquarters." The first Bayesian says teh right update is to minimize KL divergence from your prior subject to the new conditional probability P(HQ|Red)=1, which gives Blue=2/3, Red HQ=1/3. The second Bayesian says that's wrong because it treats the conditional as a pure constraint and ignores the fact that the operator's utterance is itself evidence: you need to model how likely he is to say that given Blue vs Red vs Red HQ vs Red Second Company, and if his likelihood is flat across those states, you just conditionalize on the utterance and get Blue=1/2, Red HQ=1/2. Have them actually argue this out, working through the numbers and responding to each other's points. Neither should be a strawman. Then have one of them bring up a twist: what if the operator adds "and if you were in Blue, I would have said something different"? Does that change the update? The dialogue should end with them identifying exactly what further fact about the operator's reliability or reporting policy would settle the disagreement, or agreeing that the dispute is really about how to model the informant. Keep it natural, like two philosophers talking in a bar. Don't cite any papers, just reason.
