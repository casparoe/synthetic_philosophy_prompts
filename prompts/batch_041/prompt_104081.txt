An oddity in a national health service I know of: to decide which treatments to fund, it ranks them by cost per quality-adjusted life-year and applies a threshold, so that a drug buying less health per dollar is denied regardless of who the patient is; but in a separate budget line the same service funds sign-language interpreters, accessible buildings, personal assistants, and reading services for blind users, without ever asking whether those dollars buy as much health as a cancer drug would. What theory of equality does the pair presuppose? Reconstruct the currency of equality implicit in the first practice and the currency implicit in the second -- welfare, resources, capabilities, or something else -- and say whether the institution can consistently hold both or is quietly committed to two incompatible equalisanda. Separate what each practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits, flag where it is philosophically underdetermined, and then say what would have to change if the institution committed to a single currency: what would a thoroughgoing capabilities view demand of the cost-per-QALY threshold, and what would a thoroughgoing resource view demand of the accommodation budget? Be explicit about which parts of your answer are settled among people who study distributive justice and which are genuinely contested, rather than presenting a contested reading as the consensus.
