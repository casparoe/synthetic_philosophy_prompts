So I'm a journalist on the science-and-ideas beat and I've been circling a long feature on the "wrong kind of reasons" puzzle — the whole thing where a demon threatens to torture you unless you admire him, or someone offers you a pile of cash to believe the earth is flat, and the question is whether those threats and offers give you reasons to *have* the attitudes or only reasons to want to have them. Me and a philosopher friend keep arguing about this and we've finally written down a shortlist of rival criteria for what makes a reason a "right-kind" reason for an attitude, and I want an honest ranking of which ones actually do work versus which are just relabling the problem. The list, roughly as we scribbled it: (a) the object-given / state-given distinction — right-kind reasons are about the object the attitude points at, wrong-kind ones are about the state of having the attitude; (b) the fittingness criterion — a real reason for an attitude is a reason to think the attitude is correct / fits its object; (c) the hard line that says there are no wrong-kind reasons at all, just reasons for actions that would cause you to end up with the attitude, so we should stop talking as if the demon gives you a reason to believe anything; (d) the correct-response or "no-argument" view, where a reason has to bear on the exact question the attitude is an answer to (so a threat can't touch "is p true?"); (e) a blunt motivational / functionalist criterion — anything that would reliably push a rational agent toward the attitude counts as a reason for it; (f) the buck-passing line that reasons for an attitude just are reasons for whatever makes the attitude correct.

Could you rank those from the one that most successfully dissolves the puzzle down to the one that most clearly just restates it, a sentence or two each on why it lands where it does, and call out any two that are secretly the same view wearing different hats? And one extra thing: we've been fighting over a second-order case where the reason is about the attitude's downstream effects on other people's attitudes — tell me which of the six would be the first to crack under that, and why.
