ok so there's this thought experiment Hofstadter called the careenium and I keep going back and forth on it. Picture a bowl full of tiny magnetic marbles (he calls them simms) bouncing around; sometimes they clump into clusters (simmballs) that have their own dynamics and shove each other around in ways you'd never guess from watching individual marbles. Everyone asks who's pushing whom — do the simms push the simmballs, or the simmballs push the simms? — and the standard answer is "both, obviously," which I find totally unsatisfying.

What I actually want explained, intuitively rather than formally: I get that the marble-level story is causally complete — know every marble's position and velocity and you can predict everything, including which clusters form and where they drift, without ever mentioning a simmball. I also get that the simmball-level story is the one that explains anything (why the cluster drifted left, why two of them merged). But I cant see how both can be true at once. If the marbles already fix the cluster's motion, in what sense did the cluster do any pushing? And if the cluster really pushed, doesn't that mean the marble story left something out?

So walk me through the ways of making this precise. Is simmball-level causation real and irreducible, or a convenient fiction / just a compressed way of describing marble facts, or something in between that needs a theory of what "cause" even means before you can state it — and if it's the third, what does a broadly interventionist account (causes = things you can wiggle to change the effect) end up saying about the careenium? Also here's the wrinkle that I think breaks a lot of the pat answers: imagine a careenium where you can carve the very same marble motions into two different sets of clusters — one description sees a few big simmballs, another sees many small ones — and both predict equally well and both "explain" the drift. Do both cluster stories have an equal claim to be the real causal story? If not, what picks the winner? If yes, doesn't that make "the simmballs push the simms" cheap, something any old description can earn?

Keep it grounded — I care about the general shape of the problem (same shape as "do thoughts push neurons around"), not just the toy.
