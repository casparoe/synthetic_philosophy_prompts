Me and my mate Dan got into a five hour argument last night about knowledge and I'm still annoyed about it. He's been reading Nozick and now he's a total convert to the tracking theory — the one where you only know something if your belief would've been different had the thing been false, plus something about how you'd still believe it if it were true. Fine, whatever. But then he says this means closure fails: he knows he's got hands, he knows the table in front of him is real, but he does not know that he isn't a brain in a vat being fed all of this, and he doesn't know he isn't dreaming, and he reckons that's just correct. Not a bug, a feature. The price of a good theory.

I said that's obviously broken. He said I only think that because I haven't thought about it properly, and that if you can't know the boring consequences of the stuff you know then knowledge isn't good for anything. Then he gave me this one: you can know every single thing that has to be true for your flight to be safe — the wing's attached, the pilot slept, the fuel is the right kind — and still not know the flight is safe, because the big claim isn't tracking anything. That one smells wrong to me but I couldn't say why. And he says it gets worse, because knowing things is useless for telling other people too: I know something, I tell my sister, she believes me because she trusts me, but her belief isn't tracking anything either, so she doesn't know it. Same with me remembering it next week. He thinks all of this is fine and consistent. By the end I honestly couldn't tell if he was winding me up.

Write it out as a dialogue between the two of us — him defending the tracking view, me pushing back — so I can see who's actually got the better of it. I dont want him as a strawman and I don't want to win by default; we should both be sharp and actually answer each other's points instead of talking past each other. Get into the weeds. The flight thing, the telling-my-sister thing, the remembering thing, whether saying you know you've got hands while denying you know you're not a vat-brain is something a sane person can say out loud, and whether closure is the kind of thing you can just give up or whether giving it up means you were never using the word knowledge the way the rest of us use it. And please dont just rehash the standard brain-in-a-vat material, I've read that a hundred times — I want the weird downstream stuff.
