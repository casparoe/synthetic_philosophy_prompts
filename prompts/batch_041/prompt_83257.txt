I'm a high school student on my school's philosophy club newsletter, and for our parody issue I'm writing a deadpan internal memo from a fictional 1950s research unit—say, the Machine Intelligence Evaluation Section—in which a junior administrator processes the director's request to procure and certify a telepathy-proof room for the imitation game. The humor should come from the actual content of Turing's discussion of extra-sensory perception in his 1950 paper: he treats telepathy as a serious objection to the test, since if an interrogator could read minds or a human competitor could transmit answers without any physical channel, then a machine's performance would no longer be a measure of the machine. The memo should take that stipulation literally and bureaucratically: what counts as a telepathy-proof room, how you would test whether a room is telepathy-proof without already having a telepath, what the placebo condition looks like, what happens if the room is only mostly proof, and who signs off on the residual risk. It should be funny to someone who knows the paper and readable to someone who doesn't, and it should never wink at the reader. About 600–700 words, formatted as a memo with a subject line, numbered points, and a signature block. Please paraphrase the paper's ideas rather than quoting it directly.
