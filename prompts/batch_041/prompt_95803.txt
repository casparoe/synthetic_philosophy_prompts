my freind has been reading one of those big-ideas pop-philosophy books and he's now convinced that self-deception is impossible. his version of the chapter goes roughly: 'Self-deception is an incoherent idea. To deceive yourself you'd have to know the truth and hide it from yourself at the same time, which means beleiving P and not-P at once — logically impossible. The only escape is to split the mind into two agents, a liar and a dupe, but that's just parking a little person inside your head and it explains nothing. So self-deception doesn't exist; what we call it is really wishful thinking or motivated reasoning, a bias rather than deception. And since the deception part is doing no work, we should drop the whole notion.'

I don't know the literature at all, but this feels too tidy to me. Can you go through it claim by claim — what would someone who actually works on self-deception nod along with, what would they push back on, and what's just mistaken? And separately: does his last move, 'so it's just bias, not deception,' actually follow from the rest, or is he smuggling in a whole extra view and calling it a conclusion? A concrete case we argued about: a guy who is clearly dying but keeps cheerfully planning his next decade. I want to know whether calling that self-deception is a folk illusion, or whether there's a respectable way to keep the term.
