I have no physics training, but I've been reading popular accounts of Maxwell's demon for a couple of years, and something about them doesn't add up. They all agree that the demon cannot beat the second law, and then each one locates the failure somewhere different: the demon has to pay for measuring the molecule; the demon has to pay for forgetting what it measured; the second law was never more than a statement about our ignorance, so there was never a violation to explain in the first place; the demon is not a legitimate thought experiment at all, since any real device doing the sorting would carry its own entropy and heat up. Each account is written as though the others don't exist. So is this one debate, or several sharing a name? I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict: whether these are really different questions under one heading, one about a physical machine and one about the meaning of entropy and one about what knowledge is; whether a hidden calculation or experiment would settle it; or whether it is a clash of starting intuitions that no argument can reach. My own guess, which I'd like you to test rather than assume, is that the whole dispute turns on whether entropy belongs to the world or to our description of the world, so that the question of where the demon fails is not a single question with a single answer. A friend sharpens this with an objection I can't answer: a steam turbine already separates fast molecules from slow ones, so why isn't the demon just a very good turbine, and if it is, what exactly has it done wrong? Tell me what would count as progress here — a distinction, a calculation, an experiment — or whether the question is ill-posed and the apparent stall is a sign of that.
