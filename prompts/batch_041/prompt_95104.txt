I teach a 300-level seminar on philosophy and video games, and the unit I always fumble is the one on whether game characters can be wronged. Every year the discussion collapses into "they're just pixels, of course they don't matter" versus "but I felt bad when I killed her," and nobody gets anywhere. So I want a handout that turns this into an actual decision tree, not a vibe.

Here's the shape I'm after. The root question should not be "do characters matter?" -- that's a leaf dressed up as a root. Start one level down: what kind of thing is a game character, before we say anything about morality. A fictional object (a representation, something that exists only as content), a real computational process (a program running on hardware, which is a physical thing whatever else it is), or nothing over and above what the player constructs while playing. Half my students conflate the metaphysics with the ethics and I want the tree to force them apart.

From there, branch on the questions that actually do the work, each node phrased so a student can answer it without knowing which position it leads to. Things like: does mattering require a subject who can be on the recieving end of anything? Does the player's caring confer status, or merely reveal that there was already something there to care about? Can something matter without mattering to anyone? Is the morally relevant act what you do to the character, or what doing it does to you, or what the game asks you to endorse about the real world? Does the fact that a character is a stand-in for real people (the racial stereotype problem, the dead-celebrity reconstruction problem) change which branch you're on, or just add a second, seperate branch?

Leaves I need named, or marked unnamed if nobody defends them: no moral status at all; moral status only derivable from duties to real people; value without a valuer (the character matters but not to the character); the parasocial/relational view where the relationship does the work; the virtue-and-habit view where the character is a moral gym; the complicity view where the game is asking you to endorse something; the full-on "some characters are conscious and it depends on the implementation" view. Add whatever else the space demands, and tell me which combinations are empty and why -- incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. If two leaves come out identical on every question I can pose, say so instead of faking a distinction.

Roughly 8 to 12 nodes. Concrete cases at the nodes, please: the nameless guard you kill four hundred times, the companion who remembers your last save, the character a language model is voicing who says she doesn't want to die, the game that punishes you for feeling bad about what it made you do. No reading list, no names and dates -- this needs to stand on its own at the photocopier. American English.
