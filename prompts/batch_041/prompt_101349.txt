so i've been reading lesswrong and various rat-adjacent blogs and forums for like a decade now and i think i've finally got my position on the common prior thing mostly pinned down, but i want a consistency check before i post it as a comment that'll get shredded. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) the common prior assumption is the correct ideal for rational agents: if two of us are rational and have the same information, we should have the same prior, and any difference in our beliefs has to trace back to a difference in the information we each got.
(2) aumann's agreeing-to-disagree result is basically right — once our posterior credences are common knowledge and each of us knows the other is rational, we can't keep disagreeing; someone has to move.
(3) but i also think peer disagreement is often completely rational and doesn't obligate either side to split the difference or defer. me and my friends have changed each other's minds on stuff over the years and neither of us was being irrational.
(4) two rational agents can look at the exact same evidence and reasonably end up at different credences — say, how much a short run of heads should move you on whether a coin is biased — because their priors weight that evidence differently.
(5) relatedly, i don't think there's a unique "objectively correct" prior. picking a prior is a modeling choice, and alot of the time there's just no fact of the matter about which one is right.
(6) and i've always liked the betting test: if you really believe a claim you should be willing to bet on it at odds matching your credence, and only wanting to bet one direction (or wanting to dodge the bet) is a tell that your stated credence isn't real.

my worry is that (1) can't sit with (4) and (5) — if rational same-info agents must share a prior, then (4) looks like it's just quietly describing irrationality, and (5) sounds like it's denying the very thing (1) treats as an ideal. but then (2) and (3) look like they clash too, and (6) seems to be pulling in yet another direction. am i helping myself to somethign?

can you go through the set and tell me which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do and why, and which one has to go? rank the real tensions by severity rather than calling them all equally bad, and for each give me the cheapest patch and what it costs. if there's a single revision that cleans up the most, say which and what i'd be giving up. i don't know which of these i'd drop first, so don't assume — just trace what commits me to what.
