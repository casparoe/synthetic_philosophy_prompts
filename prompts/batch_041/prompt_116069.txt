Explain the conceptual issue that arises for the distinction between epistemic bubbles and echo chambers once one asks whether these are properties of a person's information environment or of the norms by which that person apportions credence. Start from two cases that pull in opposite directions: (a) an agent whose sources are topically broad but who discounts testimony from anyone outside a single trusted lineage, and (b) an agent whose sources are narrow but who treats every source as corrigible and would revise given sufficient evidence. Say which case, if either, is a bubble and which a chamber, and show why the intuitive verdict can flip depending on whether you locate the defect in the structure of the network, in the agent's second-order attitude toward their own beliefs, or in the social cost of the arrangement. Then take up the wrinkle that the standard remedies come apart: exposure corrects exclusion but can deepen trust-capture, while repairing trust can leave the informational deficit untouched. What does that asymmetry imply about whether bubble and chamber are two species of one failure or two independent failure modes that happen to co-occur? Reconstruct the concepts from first principles rather than summarizing the literature, and do not bother with citations.
