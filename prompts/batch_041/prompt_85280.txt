I'm the research officer at a mid-sized trade union federation -- about 90,000 members spread across retail, logistics, care work and a couple of NHS trusts -- and I keep getting the same request from branch secretaries. The employer announces some automation thing (self-checkouts and an app that writes the rota, autonomous trolleys in the warehouse plus the scanners that time your pick rate, an AI triage tool in A&E), and they want to know what to do about it. Right now the answer we give is either "we oppose it" or "we probably can't stop it, so let's talk redeployment", and neither of those is really an argument. Our education officer keeps saying we should be doing this with Marx, and she's got something, but when I read the machinery chapter of Capital volume one, or the bit of the Grundrisse everyone calls the fragment on machines, I can see the shape of the analysis and I still can't hand anything useful to a branch seceretary who has three days and no theory. So what I want from you is a decision procedure they can actually run on a specific employer proposal.

Something like: the questions to ask, in order, what a satisfying answer to each one looks like, where the procedure stops and says "there's nothing specifically to fight here" or "yes, this is the thing to fight", and where it hands the decision back to the members because it's a question of what they're willing to trade rather than a question of fact. I'd like the Marxian distinctions to survive the trip into the checklist rather than getting flattened into "technology is bad" -- in particular the machinery-versus-its-capitalist-use distinction, the argument about whether displaced workers get compensated by the new jobs that cheaper goods are supposed to create, the reserve army, the difference between capital just taking over a craft as it finds it and actually reshaping the labour process to suit itself, and the general intellect point that the same development which deskills everyone also concentrates the knowledge in a shared, socially produced form that could in principle be run on differnt terms. And honestly I want it to be able to tell me when the members' instinct to just oppose the machine is theoretically wrong, because I'd rather hear that from you than from the employer's consultant.

Then please run it end to end on three cases: a big supermarket chain rolling out more self-checkouts and replacing the paper rota with an app that allocates shifts and break times; a distribution centre introducing autonomous mobile robots and scanner-paced picking, where the employer's line is that nobody is being made redundant and everyone is being "upskilled" into robot supervision; and an NHS trust piloting an AI tool that sorts A&E arrivals, which the trust says will free up experienced nurses for a new "virtual ward". At least one of those should come out in a way my members won't like. Be explicit about which steps are doing real work and which are Marx-flavoured decoration, and about what the procedure can't do -- I'm not expecting it to tell me what the wage claim should be. British English, please, and if it helps, write it as a page a branch secretary could print off and take into a meeting.
