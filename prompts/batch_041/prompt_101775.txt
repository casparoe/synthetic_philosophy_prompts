You are advising the board of a small foundation that can commit roughly six researcher-years to conceptual work on moral trade — the class of situations in which two agents who hold divergent moral views each do something the other approves of, so that each is better off by her own lights even though each regards some of the other's values as mistaken. The board cannot agree on where the money should go. Some members want work on whether such trades are permissible in the first place, and specifically on whether a person who trades away a piece of her own conduct is corrupted or made complicit by the exchange; others want work on whether individual trades can be aggregated at all, and what an equilibrium would even mean when there is no common moral currency in which the trades are denominated; one member keeps pressing for a formal treatment in which the traders are uncertain which of them is right and trade partly as a hedge against that uncertainty. They have asked you for a ranked research agenda rather than a survey. Which open questions in the theory of moral trade most deserve those six researcher-years, in what order, and why? For each, make clear what puts it upstream of the others, how tractable it is for people doing conceptual and formal work rather than empirical work, and what argument or result over the next few years would move it up or down the ranking. Do not supply citations or a reading list.

Return the agenda as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion.
