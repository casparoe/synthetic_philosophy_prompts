I'm writing a term paper for a seminar on metaphysics and ethics about whether anything in fundamental physics could be a subject of suffering, and what I need is a decision tree rather than a list of positions. Start from the commitment that consciousness is either fundamental or emergent, and make every branch node a question a reader can answer without already knowing which view it lands them in — the node should be the claim that separates its children, not a label. The nodes I want are the ones that actually do the separating: whether fundamental entities have experience or only something short of it; whether experience needs a self, memory, or boundary to be valenced at all, so that there could be pain with no one in pain; whether micro-experiences combine into a subject or merely aggregate; whether moral standing tracks being a subject, having interests, or having the right functional organization; and whether micro-minds are in principle inaccessible, and if so whether that argues for precaution or for disregard. Anchor the leaves in cases: an electron, two entangled particles, a rock, a bacterium, a split-brain patient, someone under anaesthesia, the universe taken as a whole. Seven to nine leaves, named where a position exists and 'unnamed' where none does, each with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I'd like three views included even if they end up as unoccupied branches: that panpsychism adds subjects but no sufferers, because valence requires a self; that the micro-level already contains suffering, so physics is a moral catastrophe; and that moral predicates simply do not apply below the level of organisms. Flag any leaves the tree cannot separate, and any combination of answers nobody occupies, with your diagnosis of whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
