My mum died last year and left me and my brother a half-share in the family farm, which has turned into a proper mess: he wants to sell, I want to keep it going, and the tax bill is the thing that decides it. At the same time I've somehow ended up as the token non-expert on a cross-party group looking at inheritance tax reform, and we've got about 18 months and a tiny budget. So I'm trying to work out which questions in the philosophical debate about inheritance, dynastic wealth, and equal opportunity actually matter for what we recommend, and which are just interesting puzzles.

Could you give me an agenda, not a survey and definately not a reading list or citations? I want to know which open questions are most worth our time, in what order, and why. Some candidates I keep tripping over: whether the unit of justice is the individual or the family; whether inheritance is a gift, a transfer, or a form of co-ownership; whether equal opportunity requires equal starting points or only fair competition; whether inherited advantage in kind — schooling, networks, expectations — should count as inheritance at all, or only cash and capital; whether the real problem with dynastic wealth is inequality, political power, or the dead hand; and whether a tax should target the estate or the recipient. I'm also curious about a weirder idea I heard: a tax whose rate rises with how long the wealth has been inherited rather than how big it is — so old money gets taxed harder than a first-generation fortune. Is that a serious line, and what would it need to get right?

Please prioritise for us: we're not trying to solve everything, we're trying to pick two or three questions where some conceptual work would actually change what a policy could look like. For each, tell me why it's upstream of the others (or not), how tractable it is for a small group with more time than expertise, whether it's neglected, and what evidence or argument over the next year would move it up or down the list. Also tell me which questions I should stop worrying about because their answer wouldn't change much downstream. I'd rather have a defended ranking with the trade-offs spelled out than a list of everything anyone has ever written about it.
