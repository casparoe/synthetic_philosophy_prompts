I am writing the final essay paper for an upper-level undergraduate course in aesthetics (closed book, ninety minutes, one question answered in full). The last unit of the course concerns authenticity, forgery, and the value of originals, and I need a question that cannot be answered by reciting the standard opposition between the view that a work's aesthetic value supervenes on what can be perceived in it and the view that the history of its production enters into its aesthetic properties. My students can reproduce that opposition without effort. What they cannot do is say what would have to be true for either side to be right.

I have therefore been teaching a four-way distinction between loci of value, and I want the question to force students to work with it rather than gesture at it:

- vehicle: the value of the perceptible surface itself;
- achievement: the value of the making, i.e. of what the maker had to solve;
- standing: the value of the object's position in a causal and institutional history — its provenance, its place in a corpus, what it enables downstream;
- encounter: the value of the experience available to a viewer who is situated in a particular way, including what that viewer knows.

The crux I want the question to turn on is this: "perceptually indistinguishable" is an incomplete premise until one says indistinguishable to whom, and in what state of information; and once that is specified, the surface-supervenience view turns out to be a claim about an experience available only to someone who does not know what he is looking at — an experience that, after the discovery, is available to no one. I want the question to make students see that this is a consequence of the position rather than a debating trick against it, and to require them to say whether it is a cost.

Please build the question around three stipulated cases, which the question itself should state:

(a) a perfect copy — a copy of an existing painting, indistinguishable from it by any means available now or later;
(b) a forger's new work in the style of a painter who never made it, likewise undetectable, certified by the leading connoisseurs of its day and received into the corpus as a masterpiece before the deception is exposed;
(c) the surviving original of a famous work whose attribution is subsequently downgraded — the object is unchanged, only what is known about it changes.

The question should require, for each case, a verdict on which of the four loci of value survive and which do not, and should then press on the asymmetry between (a) and (c): in (a) the object differs and the knowledge is held fixed; in (c) the knowledge changes and the object is held fixed. It should also require students to say what evidence could settle the matter, or why none could. Note that (b) is not a copy of anything, so it must be handled separately from (a); I want to see whether students notice that a forgery of a work that never existed has no displaced original against which to be compared.

Constraints: the question must not require students to name any author, work, or movement, and the model answer must contain no citations — the argument has to be carried by the cases. Aim for roughly 600 words of model answer, and make the marking scheme name specific moves rather than general virtues, with the total at 20 marks.

Give me the whole deliverable as a single JSON object with the following fields, and no prose outside it:

- "question": the full text of the essay question exactly as it will appear on the paper, including the three cases and the instruction about what the answer must do;
- "crux": one or two sentences, for my own calibration, naming the crux the question is built around;
- "model_answer": approximately 600 words;
- "marking_scheme": an array of objects, one per required move, each with "move" (a short name), "marks" (an integer; the marks must sum to 20), "full_credit" (what a full-credit treatment of the move contains), "partial_credit" (what earns some but not all of the marks), and "typical_failure" (the specific mistake that costs marks here);
- "band_descriptors": an object with "first", "upper_second", "lower_second" and "third", each a one-sentence description of where that boundary falls.
