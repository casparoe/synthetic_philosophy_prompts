I am finishing a novel set some forty years from now, in a household where meat is grown in tanks, insects are farmed for protein, and a domestic machine holds up its end of a conversation. One of my principal characters is a Cartesian by conviction, and I would rather his arguments were recognisably those of the historical Descartes than a convenient caricature. So what I need from you is a prediction, not an exposition: where would Descartes have come down on questions that only arose long after he died? I will use your answer to write his dialogue, so I care as much about the shape of his reasoning as about the verdicts.

Four questions, in descending order of how hard they press on his principles.

First, the modern attribution of sentience to invertebrates. In 2024 a large group of researchers put their names to the New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness, which asserts that the evidence makes it at least realistically possible that cephalopods, decapod crustaceans and insects are conscious. My understanding is that Descartes's criterion for mind was never the possession of a nervous system but the possession of language and of behaviour too flexible to be explained mechanically. Does a crab that endures a noxious stimulus in order to reach a preferred shelter, or an octopus that solves a novel problem, meet that criterion? Or does he have a principled way of redescribing such behaviour as mechanism?

Second, the machine. If a system produces novel and apt replies, while its workings remain wholly mechanical and transparent to inspection, does he say that it has a mind? Would he see such a machine as vindicating his treatment of animals, or as destroying the very criterion by which he separated them from us?

Third, his own texts. I keep meeting two Descartes: one who denies animals thought only, and one who denies them feeling as well. Which is the better reading of him, and which is the position he would have defended if pressed by a modern interlocutor who conceded mechanism but insisted on suffering? I would like the seams in his system shown to me, not smoothed over.

Fourth, scale. Would the existence of industrial animal agriculture — billions of creatures bred into lives that would be unbearable if they were experienced — change anything in his ethics? Or is his position simply insensitive to scale, and if so, why?

Tell me what is forced by his principles, what is merely compatible with them, and where he would have to choose between commitments he actually held. Say how confident you are in the reconstruction as a whole, and single out the one point on which you are least sure.
