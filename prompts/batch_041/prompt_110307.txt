I'm in a seminar on Plato's later political thought and we've been spending a lot of time on the second book of the Laws, where the Athenian stranger defends drinking parties as a way of testing and training character -- the idea being that wine loosens shame so you find out what someone is really like, that wine is a kind of medicine for the crabbedness of old age, that the young shouldn't touch it at all until they're older, and that somebody sober has to be in charge of the whole affair. Meanwhile my roommate, who has never read a word of Plato, keeps saying "in vino veritas" whenever somebody at a party says something too honest, as if that settled it. I want a menu of readings, not a verdict on which one is right.

So: what are all the distinct precise claims that one-liner could actually be making, from the totally trivial to the fairly radical? Is it just that alcohol lowers inhibition and so people blurt things they were already thinking? Is it that alcohol gives you access to a deeper self that's normally hidden? Is it a claim about reliability, that drunk speech is more trustworthy than sober speech? Is it about the drinker being unable to keep a social mask up? Is it about the listener's obligations rather than the speaker's state -- that you should treat what a drunk person says as true? For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude, a serious empirical claim, a romantic myth, or just false, and which ones people probably mean when they deploy it to win an argument. Then say which of those readings Plato could sign on to in the Laws and which he'd reject. My suspicion is that he's committed to something much weirder and more specific than the slogan -- not that wine reveals a pre-existing truth, but that it strips away shame so you can see what someone's habits have made of them, and that the whole point is pedagogical rather than epistemic. But lay the readings out as a numbered list so I can see where that fits, and say where his insistence on sober commanders over the older drinkers cuts against the slogan's flattering picture of drunk honesty. Please write it in American English.
