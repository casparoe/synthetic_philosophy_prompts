I'm a novelist and I keep a notebook of arguments I overhear, and I've got three entries now that I'm fairly sure are the same animal, but I can't find a name for it, and I need one, because a character in the book I'm writing has to lose an argument to this thing and I want to know exactly what beats her.

Entry one: a guy at a party telling his friend to put his entire savings into a biotech startup. The pitch is that if it works it saves millions of lives, and sure the odds are terrible, but you can't say they're zero, and any number times millions of lives is still a big number. When someone asks him what the odds actually are, he says that's not the point.

Entry two: a tenants' meeting. Someone wants everybody to chip in for an appeal that will almost certainly fail, because there's some chance it sets a precedent that protects thousands of tenants down the line. Another guy says you could use that reasoning to spend every penny you own on literally anything. She says that's a cynical way to talk about people's homes.

Entry three, my favourite: two guys at the same table. One says give the money to the AI people, because there's a small chance of something unthinkable. The other says give it to the malaria people, because those deaths are certain. Same shape of argument, opposite conclusions, and neither of them budges when you swap which one he's hearing.

What I keep circling is that the argument gets stronger the less anybody knows. Normally an argument gets weaker when you can't check the claims it's resting on. Here the uncheckability is the engine: you can't rule the huge thing out, and that's somehow supposed to count in its favour, and it doesn't seem to matter whether the huge thing is likely, possible, or merely not excluded. Also nobody ever puts a number on the odds. The number only ever shows up on the payoff side.

I know Pascal's wager from school, and I know the objection about all the other gods you might be annoying, and I have a feeling this is related but its not the same — the wager is about believing something for an infinite afterlife, this is about real money and real strangers and what to do before Tuesday. So: does this pattern have a name, or a few names for its different parts? I'm not after a reading list. What I want is the carving. Which bit is the load-bearing trick — the unbounded payoff, the missing odds, or the fact that the same move serves any conclusion and its users don't treat that as a reductio? Is the version where nobody has any odds at all definately a different animal from the version where the odds are just tiny? Is it really the wager in a coat? And if nothing fits, invent a name and give me the edges: what's the nearest case where this reasoning is actually fine — an asteroid, a real one-in-a-billion risk with a real remedy — and what makes that one fine when the others aren't. If you hand me a term and nothing else I'll be annoyed, because I still won't know what my character loses to.
