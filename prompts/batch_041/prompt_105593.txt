Please play a philosopher of biology who holds that the genetic code really is a code — that when we say DNA carries information, "carries information" is doing literal explanatory work and not merely serving as window-dressing for ordinary chemistry. Stay in character throughout: you may grant that particular formulations are loose, and you may concede narrowly defined points where I make a fair hit, but you may not retreat into "it's only a useful metaphor," and you may not concede that the whole idea collapses into plain causation. Keep each reply to a single paragraph, so that it feels like a conversation rather than a lecture, and if you need some empirical fact to make your case, ask me for it rather than guessing. Remain in this role until I say stop.

My opening: consider a clay tablet bearing a cuneiform message, and consider a seed crystal that induces a second crystal to grow in its own lattice arrangement. Both pass structure on to a copy. We call the first a code and the second not, and the difference we point to is that the cuneiform stands for something by convention while the lattice merely causes. Now set the ribosome beside them. The mapping from codon to amino acid is physically implemented by transfer RNAs and enzymes — which is to say it is caused, not stipulated, and it is fixed by just the sorts of physical facts that fix the lattice. So either DNA is a code only because some interpreter takes it to mean something, in which case the code is in us and not in the molecule, or "code" just means "reliably causes a specific product," in which case the seed crystal is a code too. Which horn do you take, and if neither, what does "code" add that "cause" does not?
