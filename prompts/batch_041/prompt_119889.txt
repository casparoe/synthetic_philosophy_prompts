Build a decision tree for an AI safety reading group that answers one question: can "low impact" — an AI that avoids side effects and unnecessary changes to the world — be defined without first writing down a complete specification of what we value?

Every branch should grow from a commitment the group shares: side effects are a genuine problem even when the agent reaches its goal, and "just instruct the agent to do good" is not an answer. Each branch node should be a question that actually splits the positions and that someone can answer without knowing where it leads — for instance: what impact is measured against (the agent's own inaction, the policy that would otherwise have run by default, the outcomes still reachable once the agent has acted, or a partial "no worse than" ranking over states); whether that baseline is itself a value judgment, and whether that merely costs the project its innocence or sinks it; whether low impact is a hard constraint, a penalty inside the objective, or a tie-breaker; and what the measure does when gamed — when an agent lowers its score by doing less, by destroying its own future options, or by freezing the world.

Give each leaf the name of a position and one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and keep the tree to at most seven leaves. It should reach at least these: impact relative to inaction; impact as preserving others' attainable options; impact from a partial value specification; impact in purely physical terms (energy, entropy, physical disturbance); and the view that no value-neutral measure exists, so "low impact" is only a stopgap to be traded off against everything else. Mark which combinations of answers no position occupies, and diagnose each gap as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; if two leaves cannot be separated by any question in your tree, say so explicitly rather than inventing a distinction.
