I want your credence on a specific interpretive claim about Machiavelli, and I want the number built from its parts rather than asserted.

The claim: Machiavelli's doctrine of virtù is internally consistent — that is, there is a single coherent reading on which both of the following hold at once: (i) fortune governs a large share of human affairs, and (ii) virtù is a genuine and reasonably reliable counterweight to fortune, without covertly redefining either term.

The pressure comes from a familiar corner of the text. The prince who succeeds is described as the one whose mode of proceeding matches the quality of the times, and men are said to fail when their nature and the times come apart; but a nature is not something one chooses or revises at will, so which man succeeds can look as though it were settled by fortune before virtù gets to operate at all. Add the praise of impetuosity in the fortune chapter — the bold and the young are said to fare better — and it is unclear whether that is a technique available to virtù or a wager that merely pays off when the times happen to be propitious.

My own number is about 0.55 that the claim is true.

What I want:

1. Your credence, stated as a number.

2. The decomposition: the sub-questions the number depends on, each with a rough probability and one line of justification — for instance, whether virtù is best read as a single capacity or as a family of distinct traits; whether the requirement that a prince match his times is a constraint on virtù or merely a redescription of it; whether the impetuosity advice is offered as a reliable method or as a gamble; whether the addressee of the advice is the new prince whose virtù consists in founding or re-founding an order rather than in fitting one; and whatever else you think belongs. Then show how the parts combine into your number.

3. The term your number is most sensitive to, and the specific evidence or argument that would move it most — a passage, a distinction, or a case.

4. Whether you and I are most likely disagreeing about a reading of the text or about what consistency even demands of a political theory. If you think my 0.55 rests on an error I should be able to see, say so plainly.

5. Finally, separate what is settled in the scholarly discussion from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus. In particular, tell me whether the existence of the tension is itself disputed or whether it is agreed on and only its severity is in question.
