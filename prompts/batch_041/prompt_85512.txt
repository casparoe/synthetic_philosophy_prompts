I have been trying to find the line between manipulating someone and legitimately persuading them, and every test I try either lets in things that are plainly manipulation or rules out things that plainly are not. What I want from you is a matched pair of cases, not a general discussion of the distinction.

Both cases should be the same kind of thing: one adult friend trying to change another adult friend's mind about a serious decision (a job, a move, an engagement — your choice). In both, the person doing the influencing has nothing to gain, tells no lies, makes no threats, offers no rewards, and plants no false beliefs. Hold everything else fixed as well: the same closeness between them, the same stakes, and in both cases the person ends up making a decision that is, on the merits, a good one for her. The only thing that should differ is the channel the influence runs through.

In the first case, it should run entirely through the other person's thinking. Only true information and genuine arguments pass between them, and if you asked her afterwards to defend her decision, she would give back exactly the reasoning she actually went through and stand by it. It should still be a clear case of manipulation.

In the second, nothing should run through her thinking at all. The influencing friend never argues and never supplies a fact that bears on the decision; she works instead on mood, timing, or surroundings, deliberately, knowing how it will land. It should not be a case of manipulation.

Then, for each case, give the verdict of each of these four tests and say why it lands that way: (1) that the influence did not pass through the person's own weighing of reasons; (2) that the influencer concealed what she was doing; (3) that she played on a weakness or soft spot she knew was there; (4) that she steered the person away from what she had reason to do. Say whether any single test gets both cases right, and if none does, which case each test gets wrong. Then tell me which one feature of the first case is doing the work, and whether removing it would stop the case from being manipulation. The verdicts have to come out of the details you build in; do not simply announce that a case is manipulation and then dress it up. And finally: what would someone have to believe about people in general in order to insist that the second case is not even a candidate for manipulation?
