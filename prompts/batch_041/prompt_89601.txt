I'm a TA for a second-year metaphysics unit and I've somehow been put in charge of the end-of-term film night, and I've decided to screen The Prestige (Nolan, 2006) as a way into the identity of indiscernibles. I want to know whether that's actually a good plan or whether I'll end up using the film as a fancy illustration and teaching them nothing. The bit I keep circling: Angier steps into Tesla's machine and it leaves him standing there and puts a second one of him somewhere else in the theatre -- same body, same memories, same scars, same certainty that he's the one who walked in -- and then one of the two drops through the trap and drowns in the tank, night after night, until there's a warehouse of tanks with the same man floating in them, and at the end he dies admitting he no longer knows which one he is. That's Max Black's two iron spheres, near enough, except Black's spheres just sit there being identical and Nolan's duplicates are immediately in different places and then, fatally, headed for different fates. So is the film dramatising Black's point (numerical distinctness with no qualitative difference whatsoever, and the principle is simply false) or does it quietly hand the game to the other side, since the moment one of them is on stage and the other is at the back of the hall they've got positional and relational properties to tell them apart -- and Black's spheres arguably do too, each being two miles from the other?

There's a second thread I'd want in the reading, because the Borden brothers run the argument backwards. They're genuinly discernible -- one loves Sarah, the other loves Olivia, one loses two fingers in the pistol trick and they amputate the other's matching fingers so the trick keeps working -- and yet they live as a single person, so that being one person starts to look like a legal and social achievement rather than a fact about qualities. So the film gives us two-who-are-treated-as-one and one-who-becomes-two, and I'd like you to say what that chiasmus does to the metaphysics. A few things I want you to actually take a position on: does the film need the weak reading of the principle (relational properties allowed to individuate) or the strong one (intrinsic qualities only), and does it know which it's using? Does anything in it support the thought that persons are a special case -- that the spheres have no point of view and the Angiers do, and that his grip on being the one in the tank might be a kind of property that individuates without being a quality anyone else could check? Does he need something like a haecceity, a bare thisness, and is that a cost or just a label for what we already knew? And where, if anywhere, does the film cheat -- is the drowning business a way of making the copies discernible after the fact, which would contaminate the thought experiment rather than settle it?

I'm not after a plot summary, and please don't quote dialogue at me, I've seen it four times and I'm not asking for sources. What I want is criticism: which scenes to put up for discussion, where the film earns the philosophy and where it trades on a confusion between being indistinguishable and being identical, and whether it adds anything to the Black debate that the argument on its own doesn't -- my hunch is that it's something about whether the question 'which one is me?' has an answer at all, and whether losing the answer is a loss or a relief.
