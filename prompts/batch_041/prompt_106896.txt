Write a joke about the preface paradox. It should be funny to someone who actually knows the paradox, so the punchline has to turn on the real structure of the thing — an author who believes every sentence in her book, and also believes that at least one of them is false, and can't keep both without giving up something about belief or justification — not on some generic bit about philosophers and books.

Here's a premise I like, use it or improve on it: she finally tracks down the one sentence that's actually wrong and cuts it. Body's clean now. But the preface still says what it always said, namely that at least one sentence in this book is false. And now the only sentence left that could be the false one is the preface itself (the preface counts as a sentence in the book, don't let that loophole off the hook). So the preface is true exactly if it's false. She fixed her one error and accidently installed the liar paradox in her own front matter. Something like that — make it land.

If you can get the lottery paradox in there too, great: the temptation being that her fallibility looks like a lottery, one error guaranteed, every sentence equally likely to be the culprit, so just cut one at random and be done with it. Which is exactly the wrong picture, since nothing in a book is a winning ticket by design. But if that only makes it worse, drop it and keep it about the preface. A few sentences is fine, it doesn't need to be long, and please don't make it a metafictional bit about the joke itself.
