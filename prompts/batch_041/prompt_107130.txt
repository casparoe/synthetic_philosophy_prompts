I'm running a 90-minute session next month for a mixed group of non-philosophers -- mostly early-career nurses, a couple of social workers, plus one lawyer who thinks he already knows the answer -- on where persuasion ends and manipulation begins. Dont explain the distinction to me; I know roughly what I think it is. What I need is a plan for teaching it.

Start with the order: what has to land before anything else does, and what depends on what? My instinct is to open with a definition and I suspect thats exactly wrong. Then the carrying example. I want to build the whole session on this one case: a GP's office whose reminder software texts patients about their statins using wording that gives them a small jolt of dread about their heart -- every word true, nobody coerced, the patient is grateful and takes the pills. Is that the right carrier? If it is, tell me what it has to have and what it has to lack to do the work (I worry it quietly imports deception and turns the whole thing into a lecture about lying). If it isn't, what should replace it, and why.

The misconceptions I'm expecting, roughly in the order I think they'll surface: manipulation means lying or tricking; true information can't manipulate you; it's all about the persuader's bad intent rather than the mechanism; if you could have resisted, it wasn't manipulation; and 'nudge' is just the polite word for it. For each, tell me where in the sequence I head it off and what I say there -- and add the ones I've missed.

I'm also tempted to hand them one real distinction to chew on, something like: manipulation that works by short-circuiting someone's reasoning, versus manipulation that works straight through their reasoning by rigging the inputs (what's salient, what the options look like, what gets framed as normal). If that's a good spine for the session, build the sequence around it; if it's going to muddy things at this level, say so and give me something better.

Finally: one question I can ask at the end that would actually show whether they've got the distinction rather than just memorized my examples, and what I should openly refuse to settle -- and say out loud that I'm refusing -- so they dont walk out thinking there's a clean test I'm withholding.
