My uncle's stepping down after thirty years running a small fishing co-op on the coast, and I've got to give the toast at his send-off dinner. Half the room is co-op people who've been on the water thier whole lives and know exactly what's happening to the stock; the other half is family who mostly know him as a nice guy who likes boats. The thing I want to get across, because he'd want it said out loud: the fishery isn't dying because anyone's greedy. It's that any boat that holds back just feeds the next boat, so restraint gets punished one boat at a time and only pays off if everybody does it at once -- and the quotas and gear limits they wrote to fix it just pushed the same race up a level, into who can game the rules. He's spent thirty years explaining this to people, and the brutal part is that everybody gets it and nothing changes. Write me a four-minute toast that's warm, that doesn't invent a villain or promise a happy ending, and that says the true thing about the trap without lecturing the family or turning into a funeral for the co-op. No hero stuff either -- he'd hate that.
