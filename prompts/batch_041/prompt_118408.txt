doing a film night next week, we landed on Gattaca, and I want to do a reading of it through what heritability actually means rather than the pop version. The whole world of the film runs on the idea that your genome is a report card on your future -- valids, in-valids, the profile decides the life -- and I want a reading that takes that seriously as drama while pushing on whether any of it follows from anything. Here's the stuff I want it held against, and I'm confident about all of it: heritability is a ratio of variance in a population at a given time, not a property of an individual or of a trait; it can't be exported to a different population or a different environment; equalize everyone's environment and heritability goes *up*, not down, which is the opposite of what most people intuit; and a highly heritable trait can still be enormously responsive to environment, so heritability tells you nothing about whether a trait can be changed. So: does Gattaca's world need a determinism that heritability can't give it? Where does Vincent's arc, especially the swimming-out-into-the-ocean contest with his brother, dramatize the range-of-reaction idea better than the film knows, and where does the ending sell out by making it willpower beating genes? Which scenes would you actually put in front of a group? Honest version please, including where the film is right that the pop version is a swamp. No need for cites.
