I'm a retired atmospheric chemist — thirty-odd years in the lab, now doing a part-time MA in philosophy because I got tired of only arguing about science with people who already agreed with me. A friend in tech keeps banging on about futarchy, and I'm definately intrested enough to want to run a session on it, but I don't want to just read out the idea and have everyone nod. The core idea, as I understand it, is that you vote on the metric you want to maximise, then a prediction market decides which policy will do best on that metric. I want people to feel the force of that, and also the weirdness of it: the difference between a market that forecasts an outcome and a conditional or decision market that helps decide one, and the way the choice of metric quietly becomes the real centre of power. They should come away seeing that the market doesn't settle values, it relocates them, and that a decision market changes the incentives of the people trading in it.

I run a monthly philosophy of social science reading group in the village hall. About 14 regulars, mixed backgrounds — a couple of economists, some teachers, a few retired engineers like me, one person who used to work in local government. I've got two hours, including a break, and no real money; tokens or points are fine. I'd rather not lecture at the start. I want them to run into the problem themselves and only hear the name futarchy at the end, in the debrief. Could you design a runnable game or structured deliberation that does that? Give me the rules, materials, roles, a payoff scheme that doesn't need cash, a timeline, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the parts of the idea — the values vote, the belief market, the metric choice, the oracle problem, manipulation, liquidity, all that. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest about which feature of futarchy the activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself. No need for reading lists or citations; I just want the thing to work on the night.
