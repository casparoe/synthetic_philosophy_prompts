Two years of argument in my interpretability group have not moved either side, and I want a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a survey. The dispute always takes the same shape. A model produces an anomalous output — an odd completion, a failure on a task it otherwise handles — and someone locates a set of components whose ablation removes the anomaly. The next question is whether this is the same mechanism as the one responsible for the model's ordinary performance on that task, or a different mechanism that has displaced it; and when a similar structure appears in a second training run, whether that is the same mechanism again. We have no criterion, so we talk past one another. One of us individuates a mechanism by its parts; a second by the causal structure those parts realize, so that mechanisms with different parts can still be the same; a third by function or input–output profile; I have argued for individuation by intervention profile, counting two mechanisms as the same only when the same interventions on their components produce the same changes in behavior. These criteria agree on the easy cases and diverge exactly on the cases we care about, and no experiment has settled anything, because every experiment is designed under one criterion or another. My hypothesis is that this is not a verbal dispute: that the criteria are not rival answers to one question but answers to different questions, and that our habit of saying the same mechanism without qualification is what makes the impasse look substantive. Test that hypothesis against the positions as I have described them. Which part of the disagreement, if any, is merely terminological? What is each party actually committed to, and does any criterion beg the question? If the disagreement survives disambiguation, is what remains an empirical question — whether features really recur across seeds and checkpoints — a disagreement about what an explanation of anomalous behavior ought to deliver, or an incompatibility among criteria answerable to different explanatory aims? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, an experiment, a formal criterion, or the recognition that no single notion of mechanism identity is needed? I do not need references; I want the argument.
