ok so I'm building a fake newspaper for a worldbuilding project and I need one proper Onion-style news report from it — deadpan all the way through, no winking at the reader, no jokes that are just "look how silly this is". About 750 words, headline and dateline, at least two quoted officials and one ordinary punter who's been caught by the thing. British spelling please.

The world: the country has decided to take Dutch book arguments completely at face value and legislate them. If incoherent credences really do leave you open to a guaranteed loss, then the state is going to guarantee it. So there's a regulator — invent a boring name for it, something with "Authority" or "Bureau" in it — with a licensed corps of arbitrageurs who are legally entitled to make book against citizens, and the whole of civic life is quietly rearranged so that the assumptions the argument actually needs really do hold. That last bit is where the jokes live, so please get the mechanics right rather than drifting into generic dystopia:

- the arbitrageurs have effectively unlimited credit and can set any stake they like, since the argument only bites if someone can bet you into the ground
- money is linear in utility for everybody, because the country runs on a utility-denominated scrip and has abolished diminishing marginal returns (the Chancellor's office will explain that this was a purely technical reform)
- once you've announced your odds, betting is compulsory — nobody escapes by simply declining to play
- you have to lodge a schedule of your conditional bets in advance, because the diachronic versions of the argument only work if the bookie can count on you to follow through on the plan
- there's a state machine that tells you, instintly, every logical consequence of everything you believe, since otherwise you can hardly be blamed for not noticing that two of your credences contradict each other
- and one official keeps having to say, in quotes, that none of this has anything to do with truth — coherence is a purely practical business of not being exploitable

Other threads you could weave in, if they fit: a clause about countable additivity that was quietly repealed after the draftsmen couldn't make the books work for it (I think the whole apparatus only really delivers finite additivity — check me on that and cut the joke if I'm wrong); a dissident faction who insist the fines are a distraction, because the real failing of incoherent credences is that they're accuracy-dominated rather than exploitable, and who want the regulator run on entirely different lines; a citizen with bounded, saturating utility who literally cannot be made to lose enough to care and whom the Authority finds deeply irritating; and an arbitrageur who is himself an epistemic shambles and doesn't care, since nothing in the argument requires the bookie to be rational.

No real names of philosophers, no citations, nothing in quote marks that's lifted from an actual text — invent the officials, the regulator, the dissident faction, the punter. Assume the reader already knows what a Dutch book is and don't stop to explain it.
