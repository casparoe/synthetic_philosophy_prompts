I am a high school student working on a project for my school's philosophy club, and I have run into a pattern that I cannot find a name for. The rule I have been using for fuzzy news is this: when something makes me, say, 80% confident that a claim is true, I do not treat the claim as settled; I set my confidence in the claim to 80% and keep my remaining opinions in whatever proportions they had before, so that the fuzzy item of news disturbs nothing except the claim it is about. My friend and I worked through a small case. A coin is either fair or double-headed, fifty-fifty to start. The first item of fuzzy news is a quick glimpse that leaves me 80% confident the coin is fair; the second is a fuzzy look at a toss of the coin that leaves me 90% confident it landed heads. Taking the news in that order, I end up 70% confident that the coin is fair. Taking the same two items in the opposite order, I end up 80% confident. (The two orders also disagree about the coin's next toss, 65% against 60%; I checked both orders twice on a spreadsheet because I assumed I had made an arithmetic slip.)

That seems wrong to me, and I cannot work out how wrong. When an item of news is certain, order never matters, since ruling out possibilities is order-independent, and when two items are about the same feature, order matters only in the boring sense that the later item overwrites the earlier one. Here the two items are about different features, neither overwrites the other, and yet my final answer depends on the sequence in which I happened to receive them. It is not that one order feels more correct; it is that I can find no reason to prefer either, which makes me suspect the rule itself rather than my arithmetic.

So: is there an established name for this? I suspect the pattern is known, but I would not be satisfied with a bare label and a pointer to the literature. What I want is to see how the thing I have noticed is carved up: whether the phenomenon is the order-dependence itself or the deeper fact that fuzzy news cannot be stated as a fact about the world, so that the same two items taken in two orders are not really the same evidence at all; whether the trouble disappears if I refuse to update on fuzzy news, and what I am supposed to do instead; and whether there is a boundary — a kind of item that is uncertain yet still order-independent — or whether order-dependence is simply the price of admitting fuzzy news. Please open your reply with your bottom line, that is, whether there is a name and which candidate fits best, and then give the reasoning; no preamble, and please do not restate my question.
