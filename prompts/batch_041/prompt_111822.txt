"Plain majority first. On G, Ava and Ben say yes and Cleo says no, so G is yes. On V, Ava and Cleo say yes and Ben says no, so V is yes. On B, Ben and Cleo say yes and Ava says no, so B is yes. On M everyone says no, so M is no. So the board officially says the gym is free, there are enough volunteers, the budget covers it, and there is no movie night. Those four answers can't all be true at once, because the setup says if G, V and B all hold then M holds, and the board says M is false. So the group is inconsistent even though none of the members is: each of them says no to M because each of them says no to one of the three conditions, and each of them is being perfectly logical about it. Nobody is confused and nobody is voting strategically, which is the creepy part.

This is the doctrinal paradox, some people call it the discursive dilemma. What's happening is a kind of cycling, the same thing as Condorcet's paradox where majority rule can put A above B, B above C and C above A. Here the majority is for each of the three reasons and against the thing the three reasons add up to, so there's no way to line them up into one coherent view. That's why people say a group can't really have a view of its own: the majority answer on the parts and the majority answer on the whole can pull in different directions, so there's no fact of the matter about what the board really thinks, only about which question you asked it.

Now the reason-first rule. The majority said yes on G, V and B, and no on M, and the rule needs both, so M comes out no. Same as what every member said about M, so no movie night.

The reason-first rule is basically the premise-based fix, and I think that's the point of it. The premise-based fix says you decide the reasons first and then read the conclusion off the reasons instead of voting on the conclusion directly. That's what makes the board's answers consistent, because the conclusion is just whatever follows from the reasons the board accepted. The other standard fix, the conclusion-based one, is to only count the votes on M and ignore the reasons, which also gives no movie night, but it leaves you with no record of why the board decided what it decided, and it never checks the reasons against the conclusion at all."
