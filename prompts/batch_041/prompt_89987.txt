My Myth, Memory and History seminar has set me the task of writing the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the two camps that have been talking past each other over when a legend becomes history: the historicists, who hold that the Trojan War, King Arthur and the Exodus traditions each preserve at least some recoverable historical events, and the compositionists, who hold that all three are literary constructions whose relation to any past happenings is at most thematic and cannot be recovered case by case. Our convenor has proposed that both camps drop the question of whether it happened and adopt instead a narrower test: whether the tradition contains at least one singular causal claim whose truth is required to explain some independent feature of the tradition -- a place-name, a cult site, or an awkward detail no composer had reason to invent -- and has asked whether that test settles the dispute or merely relocates it.

Draft the document the two camps could jointly sign. Open with a numbered section of claims both accept, covering what counts as evidence here, where the burden of proof lies, and what the three cases share as against where they differ. Then state the remaining disagreements precisely, each in wording that the side it belongs to would accept as its own. Set out the cruxes -- I take one to be whether the question concerns event-tokens or event-types, another whether a tradition's kernel is a claim about reference or about causation, and a third whether the standard of proof should be legal or scientific -- and for each give a test, case or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Finish by identifying which parts of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal. Assume I know the three traditions but have no library to hand, so let the argument turn on the material itself rather than on citations.
