Two friends are going back and forth about that lamp puzzle where you flip a lamp on and off at half a minute, then three quarters of a minute, then seven eighths, and so on, so by the one-minute mark it's been flipped infinitely many times. One of them thinks this shows the whole idea of finishing an infinite series of actions is incoherent: the description tells you exactly what happens at every earlier moment, but there's no fact of the matter about whether the lamp ends up on or off, so the setup can't be describing a real possibility. The other thinks that's a trick: the description simply doesn't specify the final state, and there's nothing paradoxical about a description that leaves something open, so the puzzle shows nothing at all.

Write the dialogue between them, and keep them both sharp — the second one shouldn't be an idiot and neither should the first. I'd rather they test each other with variations than just restate their positions: for instance a lamp that goes to half brightness, then a quarter, then an eighth (where the answer seems obvious), a lamp that's also wired to a switch that only gets flipped three times, or a real bulb whose brightness cant change instantaneously. Make them respond to each other's actual points.
