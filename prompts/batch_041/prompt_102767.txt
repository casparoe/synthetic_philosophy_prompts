A comparison I keep running into on the blogs and in the comment threads — usually offered as a way of making illusionism about phenomenal consciousness sound obvious — is between conscious experience and the desktop interface of a computer. The icons, the trash can, the cursor gliding across the screen: all of that is a user-level presentation, the hardware underneath has nothing corresponding to any of it, and the presentation is not a lie told to anyone, just how one level of description compresses what is really going on. The people who deploy the comparison use it to license a conclusion, namely that there is no hard problem left over, because the interface is all there is at the level where we live and nothing is hiding behind it. I would like an audit rather than a summary. Begin with the correspondence table — what plays the role of the hardware, the interface, the user, the cursor, the icon, and the designer, if anything does — then tell me where the mapping breaks, with particular attention to whether an illusion can be an illusion without a subject to whom things seem so, and to where the "user" sits relative to the machine. Then commit. Does the analogy actually carry the no-hard-problem conclusion, or was that conclusion riding on features of desktops — a genuine level beneath, an audience for whom the interface is drawn — that the target does not have? Give me your own verdict, with reasons, and say what a better comparison would be if this one will not do.
