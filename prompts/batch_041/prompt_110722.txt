I'm a second-year PhD student and I sit in on a social ontology reading group where we've just done Lewis on convention, and I keep noticing that my gut about what counts as a convention doesnt match the official machinery (regularity; everyone expects everyone else to conform; everyone prefers to conform given that the others do; common knowledge of all that; and — the clause I always forget — the thing has to be arbitrary, i.e. some other regularity would've solved the same problem just as well). So I want to just dump my verdicts on a pile of cases and have you find the principle behind them, even if it means one of the verdicts has to go.

Where I land: driving on the right — convention. Shaking hands instead of bowing — convention. 'Dog' meaning dog in English — convention. Dollars as money — convention. QWERTY — convention, and the fact that a different layout would've been fine is exactly why it feels conventional to me. Wearing a seatbelt — not a convention; I'd wear it whoever else did or didn't, so it's just a thing we all happen to do. Putting the bins out on Tuesday because that's when the truck comes — not a convention, same flavour of reason: nobody's choice depends on anybody else's. Everyone crossing the common by the one dry path in the wet season — not a convention, there's no alternative so where's the arbitrariness. Now the trouble. (a) A village where everyone fasts on the same arbitrary day each year and everyone knows everyone else does, but each person's reason for fasting is personal — they'd fast whether or not anyone else did. I want to call that a convention. (b) A lab where everyone uses the same clumsy in-house acronym for a machine, a word nobody would've coined but that everyone uses because it's the shared term — convention, I say. (c) A town where everyone privately dislikes a custom but assumes everyone else loves it, so everyone keeps it up — I also want to call that a convention, though I'm told the common-knowledge clause kills it. And (d), my actual sticking point: two monasteries both fast on an arbitrary day; in the first the monks fast because that's the done thing, in the second each monk hit on the same day by private revelation and would fast alone on a desert island. I keep flip-flopping on whether the second has a convention or just a coincidence of habits.

So — is there one principle that gets all of this, maybe Lewis's with the coordination clause loosened or relocated, or maybe something else entirely? If it takes an ugly disjunction to save every verdict, is there a cleaner principle that just overturns one or two of mine, and which would you give up? And whatever you settle on, what does it say about a case I havent listed: a regularity everyone follows and expects everyone else to follow, that isn't arbitrary because it's the only thing that works?
