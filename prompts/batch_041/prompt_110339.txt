In spectrum arguments against the transitivity of 'better than,' a chain of options is presented in which every adjacent comparison seems defensible on its own, yet the chain as a whole yields an intransitive — sometimes cyclic — pattern, as when the last option strikes us as better than the first. Argue for the position that this intransitivity is an artifact of sliding, within a single chain, between different granularities of comparison, and that 'better than' is transitive once each comparison is indexed to a fixed granularity. In doing so, specify what fixes a granularity, show how the spectrum case is handled under it, and answer two objections: that the appeal to granularity is ad hoc, and that relativizing betterness to a granularity collapses it into mere incommensurability. Argue on the merits; no survey of the literature is needed.
