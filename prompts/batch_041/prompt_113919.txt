I'm a PhD student in metaphysics, currently drafting the modality chapter of my dissertation, and before I build an argument on top of my commitments I want an audit of whether they actually hang together. I don't need a survey of the available positions; I need to know which parts of my own package are pulling against each other. Here is where I've landed.

1. Individuals are world-bound: nothing exists at more than one possible world. When we say of a thing that it could have been otherwise, what makes that true is that some counterpart of it — a distinct individual, standing in the relevant relation to it — is otherwise, not that the thing itself has the property in question at another world.

2. Haecceitism: there are pairs of possible worlds that agree on every qualitative fact, down to the last distribution of properties and relations, and differ only over which individuals occupy which roles. What separates such worlds is nothing over and above the individuals themselves; I don't want to posit primitive thisnesses or any further entity to do that work.

3. Robust realism about de re modality: claims about what I could have done and about what is essential to me have objective, determinate truth values that don't shift with how we happen to describe or compare things. I take it as a datum, for instance, that I could not have had different parents.

4. Names rigidly designate: a name picks out the same individual with respect to every world at which that individual exists.

5. Endurantism: a material object is wholly present at each moment at which it exists; it persists by being strictly identical through time rather than by having distinct temporal parts at distinct times.

Treat these five as a package. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each genuine tension? For each one you identify, give me the cheapest repair and tell me what it costs me elsewhere in the package. Then say which single revision would clear up the most, and whether the residue is something I could reasonably live with.
