Explain the tension between content externalism and the explanatory role of content, and assess the narrow-content strategy for relieving it.

The tension, as I understand it: two thinkers can be molecule-for-molecule duplicates whose environments differ only in the hidden microstructure of some substance both have been interacting with all their lives. On the externalist view they differ in what they believe — one believes that the stuff she drinks is water, the other that it is twater — even though nothing about their bodies or brains differs, and each does exactly what the other does. If a difference in belief corresponds to no difference in the thinker's behaviour or internal states, it is hard to see what the belief is explaining, and the causal work seems to be done entirely by the shared internal state.

Set out how the distinction between narrow and wide content is supposed to help: what a narrow content is supposed to be, how it is fixed by the thinker's intrinsic properties, and how wide content is recovered once the environment is supplied. Then judge whether the strategy can meet two demands at once. Narrow content must be shareable — it is meant to be precisely what the duplicates have in common, so it cannot be individuated by anything environmental. It must also be attributable — it must be the sort of thing that can be stated, or at least that can appear in an explanation, rather than an ineffable sense of how things are or a bare functional role. Say whether anything satisfies both demands, and, if nothing does, what that shows about the original tension: whether externalism really does cost us the explanatory role of content, or whether the requirement that content be shareable was the error. Along the way, say whether the premise that the duplicates' behaviour is itself duplicated is secure, since an externalist may deny it, and whether the parallel worry about self-knowledge calls for the same device or a different one.

I am not after a survey of positions; I want the structure of the problem and a verdict.
