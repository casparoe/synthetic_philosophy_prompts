I'm a 4th-year PhD student in philosophy and I'm making a handout for a seminar session on Nietzsche's diet-and-health material — the stretches of Ecce Homo where he holds forth on nutrition, place and climate as if they were philosophical questions, plus the letters where he's obbsessed with what he's eating. Class discussion always collapses into 'it's just hypochondria' vs 'it's just a metaphor for his values', and both are lazy. I want a proper taxonomy of the positions you could actually hold about what those passages are doing, built as a tree where each branch is labeled with the question that splits it. The distinctions I care about: (i) whether the physiological claims are meant literally and are load-bearing for the philosophy — i.e. the critique of morality is supposed to rest on facts about nerves, digestion, and decadence as a bodily condition — or whether they're scenery; (ii) whether health is a state he thinks he's failed to attain or a regimen/activity he's prescribing; (iii) whether the advice is meant for anyone or only for people of his own type; (iv) whether the passages are read biographically and medically (a record of decline) or as deliberate self-fashioning. Candidate readings I want placed as leaves, though add any I've missed: philosophy-as-a-way-of-life (the regimen just is the doctrine in practice); naturalist grounding (his critique of morality is downstream of physiology); persona-performance (in Ecce Homo the food talk isn't about food at all); symptom (read it with a doctor's eye, not a philosopher's). Give it to me as exactly five numbered items, one per branch question, each item one or two sentences: the question, what each answer commits you to, and which leaves come out — no intro, no wrap-up. Phrase the branch questions so someone can answer without already knowing which reading it leads to (concrete conditionals are good: 'if his stomach had been fine, would the books be the same?'). Where a leaf comes out empty, say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and if two positions land on the same branch just say the tree can't seperate them.
