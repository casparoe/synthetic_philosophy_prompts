I do risk assessments for a state environmental health agency — maybe thirty a year, each one ends in a written finding that either triggers a drinking-water advisory or doesn't. The science is always thin: three rodent studies that disagree, one epi study with a confidence interval you could drive a truck through, or nothign but a structural analog and a QSAR model. Right now we do what everyone does: pick a central estimate, divide by an uncertainty factor of 10 or 100 or 1000, and write it up as though it were a number we know. A colleague in our biostat group keeps telling me we should be doing imprecise probabilities instead — carry a whole set of distributions, report a lower and upper bound on the risk, then apply a decision rule, maximin or Hurwicz with some optimism index, or maximality, or E-admissibility. I understand maybe half of that. She also mentioned something called dilation, where conditioning on an event that's meant to be independent of the thing you care about makes your interval wider rather than narrower, which if true sounds like it wrecks the whole approach.

So: give me a procedure I can actually run in a day, that my board (non-technical) and our counsel (who needs a yes/no and a single number in the record) can live with. Questions in order, what counts as good enough to move to the next one, where it stops, where it hands the decision to a human because the disagreement isn't about facts anymore. I need to know which steps are real analysis and which are values wearing a lab coat, and I need it to say so out loud when the interval straddles our threshold and the rule I pick is what decides the case. Also tell me how to get from an interval to the one number the record requires without lying about it.

Then run it on two cases: (a) the three-rodent-studies contaminant, and (b) the structural-analog-only contaminant, where I personally think the risk is negligible and I suspect the procedure is going to tell me I'm not entitled to that view.

Procedure in prose first, then a JSON object with this shape:

{"procedure": [{"step": 1, "question": "", "sufficient_answer": "", "if_not_met": "continue | stop | escalate", "fact_or_value": "", "hidden_judgment": true}], "worked_cases": [{"case": "", "bounds": {"lower": "", "upper": ""}, "verdict": "", "rule_that_flips_it": "", "who_signs": "", "outcome_analyst_wont_like": ""}], "cannot_do": [""]}

One entry per step in the procedure array, in order; one entry per case; and in cannot_do, the things this procedure will not settle no matter how carefully it's run.
