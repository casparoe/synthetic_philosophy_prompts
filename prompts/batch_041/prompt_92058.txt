I'm a software engineer with no philosophy training, and I'd like a probability rather than a survey. Here is the claim I want a number on: for any given physical system — a thermostat, a vending-machine mechanism that sorts genuine coins from slugs, a language model — there is a fact of the matter, independent of any observer or designer, about whether its internal states genuinely represent or are about something, as opposed to having their aboutness conferred entirely by how we interpret or use the system. (This is the issue behind the coin-sorter that only counts as detecting coins because of the role we assign it, and behind Searle's Chinese room, where a symbol-shuffler is said to have at most borrowed intentionality.) Give me your credence that this claim is true — that original, interpreter-independent intentionality is real — and show the derivation: a rough probability and one line of justification for each sub-question the number depends on (for instance, whether intentionality is a real feature of the world or an interpretation-relative ascription; whether the question is even well-posed; what we should conclude from actual systems of the kind above), and then how those combine. Tell me which sub-question the final number is most sensitive to, and what single argument or piece of evidence would move it most. My own gut number is about 0.3, and I'd like to know whether that's naive.
