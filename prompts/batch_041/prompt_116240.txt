I'm a second-year PhD student working at the boundary of machine learning and safety, and I've been asked to introduce the iterated amplification proposal at a reading group next week. I think I have the machinery right: the loop alternates between an amplification step, in which a human works with the current model to break a hard question into subquestions small enough that the human can answer them reliably, and a distillation step, in which the model is trained to reproduce the outcome of that amplified process so that it can answer the original question on its own, after which the improved model is used to amplify the next, harder round. What I can't get past is an objection that seems to me too obvious to be the intended reading, and I'd like to know where it fails.

Distillation trains a student to match a teacher, and a student cannot in general exceed its teacher on the distribution it is trained on; that, as I understand it, is the point of distillation — you accept some loss of capability in exchange for cheap inference. But on this description the teacher at every round is simply the previous round's model, since the amplified answers are produced by a human working with that model, and the only thing that changes from round to round is how much of the labour the model does. So the sequence of models looks monotone non-increasing in capability to me: each round's target is generated by a system no stronger than the one before it, and every error the student makes is carried forward into the next round's decompositions, where it can only compound. Alignment looks worse under the same reading rather than better, because the student acquires its dispositions by imitation, and the dispositions it is most likely to inherit uncritically are precisely the ones the overseer cannot detect — the hard cases are hard for the overseer too, so imitation should propagate the initial model's hidden tendencies rather than filter them out. The upshot, as I see it, is that iterated amplification launders the first model's competence and its flaws through a series of copies, diluting both with each round. I suspect I'm misreading something structural, but I can't locate it. Concretely: which step is supposed to introduce capability that was not already present, which step is supposed to secure alignment, and are those the same step or different ones? And what about the loop makes it something other than the copy chain I've described?
