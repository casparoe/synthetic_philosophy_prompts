I'm putting together a handout for a grad reading group on Moore's paradox, and I want a decision tree rather than another list of -isms. Start from the plain sentence "it's raining but I don't believe it's raining" and make every branch node a question someone could answer without already knowing which position it lands them in -- is the oddness in what the sentence means or only in the act of saying it; is it the belief that's inconsistent or the assertion; does the speaker have to know their own mind for the sentence to come out wrong; does it matter that the second half is about the speaker rather than about someone else. Leaves should be named positions, and I want these among them: the "this sentence has no use, it isn't a move in any language-game" line, the pure pragmatic line where the sentence is perfectly meaningful but unassertable, the expressivist line where asserting just is expressing belief, the doxastic-logic line where believing a conjunction requires believing each conjunct, and at least one leaf holding that a speaker simply cant be mistaken about what they believe. Two things I want the tree to do that most writ-ups dont: (a) run the same questions over the knowledge version ("it's raining but I don't know it's raining"), the past-tense and third-person versions, and a case where a language model prints "p, but I don't believe p" -- for each, say whether the absurdity survives and which leaf that pushes you toward; and (b) mark any combinations of answers nobody occupies and say whether that's because the combo is incoherent or just unexplored. Also flag any leaves the tree can't pull apart. Keep it to like 8-10 leaves.
