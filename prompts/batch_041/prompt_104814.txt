Write a short joke whose punchline turns on the actual content of Aumann's agreement theorem — the result that two agents with a common prior, who are both rational and have common knowledge of each other's posteriors and rationality, cannot agree to disagree. The humor should come from the theorem's real machinery (the common prior, the common-knowledge condition, the rationality assumption) rather than from the phrase "agree to disagree" or from a generic joke about stubborn economists. It is fine for the setup to spell out the assumptions and for the payoff to turn on how spectacularly they fail to hold in an ordinary dispute. A few lines is enough, and someone who actually knows the theorem should recognize the joke as being about the theorem rather than merely name-dropping it.
