Bit of background so you know who's asking. Thirty-one years in aquaculture, mostly Atlantic salmon on the smolt side, then the last decade on a shrimp farm, and I retired four years ago and have done almost nothing since but read philosophy, which my wife finds extremely funny. I've gotten myself into a small reading group and next month is my session — the topic is whether fish and crustaceans suffer, and what that should mean for how we farm them. I want to open with a poem I can read out loud. I have never written one and I'm not intrested in becoming a poet, so this is a one-off favour.

The form is the point here, so let me be precise about it. I want one sentence as a refrain, at the end of every stanza, and each time it comes back exactly one word has changed. It starts out certain and ends up somewhere certainty can't be argued back from. Something like: it doesn't feel anything / it probably doesn't feel anything / does it feel anything / I don't know if it feels anything. The animal is identical in all four versions. Only my sentence about it moves. Thats the whole poem as far as I'm concerned. Six stanzas, three to five lines each, no rhyme, no meter, plain words — it should sound like a man talking, not like a poet.

One idea I'd like in there, because it's what I keep chewing on: there's a difference between a nervous system that registers damage and a nervous system that minds about it. I spent my whole working life around animals that had the first thing and I was sure that was all they had. The way I put it to myself is that a smoke alarm going off is not a house on fire — the alarm works fine, nothing is burning, nobody's home to care. I'm no longer confident I know where the line between those two is, or that there is a line, and I'd rather the poem not pretend I do.

If you can get this in without forcing it, I'd also like the other thing I noticed late in my career: the law needs a cutoff. A list, in or out, this species counts and that one doesn't. And I spent thirty years looking at a gradient, not a list. Somewhere a regulator has to draw a line through a continuum and then defend the spot they picked, and it will be arbitrary wherever they put it. If that won't fit, drop it, don't wedge it in.

Things not to do. Don't give the prawn a monologue, don't let any of the animals talk, don't tell me what to conclude. My group argued for two hours and didn't land anywhere, and the not-landing is the honest place to be, so let the poem stop there. And no sermons about the industry — I know exactly what it looked like, I was there, I'm not looking to be scolded.

Last thing, since I'm the one leading and people will want something to hold onto: after the poem, give me a small JSON object instead of prose, with these fields —

form: the constraint I asked for, in one line.
refrain_walk: every version of the refrain in order, one per stanza.
device: what the changing refrain is doing to the argument.
alarm_vs_fire: one sentence on how the poem treats the registering-versus-minding distinction.
line_vs_gradient: one sentence on where the law-versus-biology tension shows up, or null if it didn't fit.
unsettled: what the poem deliberately refuses to settle.
shaky: anything a specialist would object to, and why.
