I have been trying to find a name for a kind of category that keeps turning up in the applied sciences, and I cannot place it on the usual map that runs from natural kinds at one end to socially constructed kinds at the other. I would like to know whether it already has a name, and if it does not, whether it deserves one.

The category has three features at once. First, there is a genuine division in the world: things on one side of the line really do resemble one another in ways that support prediction, so that a generalization which holds of one member usually holds of the rest. Second, the boundary between the sides is not discovered but stipulated. Something varies continuously, a number is chosen, and above that number one belongs to the category while below it one does not. Third, the number is not chosen at random; it is chosen with an eye to what follows from belonging, and it is revised when those consequences change. The boundary is thus at once an administrative instrument and a working part of the science.

Two instances. In medicine, a quantity that varies continuously across a population is given a threshold, and the threshold decides who is diagnosed and who is treated. When a treatment becomes cheaper, or a trial shows benefit at a lower level, the threshold is moved, and a large number of people who were healthy last year are patients this year, though not one of their measurements has changed. In field ecology, a classification manual stipulates where one vegetation type ends and the next begins; a rival manual draws the lines elsewhere, and yet either set of classes will predict how a plot responds to fire or drought, so a model that uses the wrong boundaries makes real errors.

What I cannot classify is the combination. These categories are not merely conventional, since they support inductions and figure in causal explanations. They are not natural kinds in the usual sense, since their boundaries are not discovered, and since moving a boundary changes which generalizations come out true, which suggests that the boundary is part of the machinery rather than a mark on something that was already there. Nor are they simply vague predicates, because the boundary is not fuzzy but exact and deliberately placed.

If there is a term of art for this, I would like to know which of the nearby terms covers it and where the others fall short. If there is none, I would like one coined and defined, with its edges drawn: does the phenomenon essentially involve the revision of the boundary, or can a boundary be fixed once and for all and still count; does it require that the boundary have practical consequences, or is it enough that it be stipulated; and does it matter that the boundary is set by people acting within an institution rather than by the science alone? I am not much interested in the label itself. I want to know what exactly is being asserted when someone says that one of these categories is real, and whether that assertion survives a change in where the line is drawn.
