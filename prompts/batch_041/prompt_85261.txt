Ok so I have no background in this stuff at all, I just read alot and argue with people online, but I've been chewing on something for about a year and I want an actual number out of you.

Here's what I keep noticing. Some diagnostic-ish labels spread like crazy and some just kind of fade out. And I don't think the difference is how real or how serious the underlying condition is. I think its which way the label pushes. I've started calling them pull-labels and push-labels.

A pull-label is one where getting it feels like a compliment or an upgrade — highly sensitive, gifted, empath, introvert, that kind of thing. People go looking for it. A push-label is one where getting it feels like an accusation or a defect — alcoholic, narcissist, hoarder, hypochondriac. People flinch away and fight it.

My claim, and I want you to score it: whether a label is a pull or a push predicts the direction of its trend (rising or falling, over a 20-30 year window, in the US and UK and similar countries, for labels that show up in the DSM or in pop psychology) better than how solid or how severe the underlying condition is. And the mechanism is the part I actually care about — the pull ones grow partly because people bend themselves to fit them. They read the checklist, they recognize themselves, they start describing their inner life in the label's words, and then they match it more. So the label makes more of the thing it names. The push ones shrink, but only until they come with a payout — money, meds, accommodations, sympathy — at which point they flip and start spreading like a pull one.

I'd put myself at about 0.6. Give me your number, and give me the decomposition, not a vibe. The terms I'd guess are in there: how much of any rise is just better detection and less stigma, how much is the criteria getting looser, how much is a genuine increase in whatever's underneath, how much is the label not even naming one thing so "prevalence" is mush, and then the looping part where the label reshapes the people. Add whatever I'm missing, and tell me if any of those are secretly the same term counted twice — I suspect the better-detection one and the looping one bleed into each other and I can't tell how much.

Then the combination, and which single term the whole number is most sensitive to. Also be honest if this is just the old stigma-plus-secondary-gain idea with new names. If pull/push is just repackaging something obvious, say so, that should drop my number.

Last thing: what would actually settle this? Not a book, an observation. What would I have to see to think I'm wrong?
