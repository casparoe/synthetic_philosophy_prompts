I'm in a seminar on corruption and development and something from last week is still bugging me. One of the readings basically said that in a lot of places the whole thing runs on gift-giving and personal obligation — you get a post, you're expected to bring your relatives in; a friend in the ministry sorts out your permit and down the line you sort out his cousin's — and that calling any of that "corruption" is just importing Western standards and moralizing at people for how their society actually works. Okay, I can sort of see the point of that. But then the next reading was all about measuring corruption across countries, ranking governments, argueing about which indices are better, and nobody in the room seemed to notice the tension. Those two things can't both be right. If the local norms say the gift is just how you get things done, then there's no fact of the matter about whether it's corrupt; but if there is a fact of the matter, it has to be about whether public money or public power ended up serving private ends, and that's true or false no matter what anybody's culture says about it. My gut says it's the second one, honestly. A spade is a spade even if everyone in town calls it a fork.

So who's wrong here — the anthropologists, the index people, or me? And if it's me, I want to know exactly where the mistake is, because right now it just looks like a flat contradiction and I can't find the seam.
