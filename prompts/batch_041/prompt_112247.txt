I write a weekly science-and-ideas column and I'm trying to build one of those interactive flowcharts readers can click through — answer a few questions, it tells you where you land. Topic: when is a person dead. Reason I want it: my last few columns on organ donation got a pile of mail saying some version of "this is all just a definitional trick," and I'd like to actually take that seriously instead of quoting one bioethicist and moving on.

So, a decision tree. Every branch point has to be a question my readers can answer without knowing which camp it leads to. No nodes like "do you accept whole-brain death?" — thats the answer, not the question. Start from something concrete, like: here's someone on a ventilator, no brain activity at all, heart still going; are they dead, or dying, or alive? Then split on whatever actually seperates people, and label each split with the thing doing the separating.

Leaves I want to be able to land on, at minimum: whole-brain death (the version most US states use), the version where only the brainstem has to go (roughly how the UK code runs), higher-brain or cortical death, the view that death is when circulation stops for good, the view that dying is a biological process with no single moment in it at all, and the one that says the definition of death is a policy choice rather than a fact about biology — that we decide who counts as dead based on what we need, rather than discovering it. Also, if it's coherent, a leaf for people who think the current rules are a mess and keep using them anyway. You tell me whether that one's a real position or just a mood.

Cases I'd like you to build nodes out of: the ventilator patient with no brain activity but a beating heart; a patient with a dead cortex but a working brainstem who breathes on their own; someone in a permanent vegetative state; a donation-after-cardiac-death case where the heart stops, five minutes go by, and then the heart starts up again on its own; a pregnant woman kept on machines for months after being declared brain dead. And the one I care most about, which is a hypothetical I'm going to float in the column: suppose next year a machine shows up that can reliably bring back integrated brain function 40 minutes after circulation stops, in some patients. Who was dead before the machine existed? Does the answer change, or did we just find out we were wrong? That needs to be a node, not a sidebar.

Also flag any leaves the tree can't separate — pairs of positions where the same answers to all my questions land you on both, so I don't oversell how discriminating the thing is. And note the branches that are empty: combinations of answers nobody actually defends, with a one-line guess at why (incoherent, unattractive, or nobody's bothered yet).

Aim for 8-10 nodes and keep the language plain. My readers are smart but they're not philosophers, and they can't be left guessing what "irreversibility" is supposed to mean here. If a distinction only makes sense to someone who's already read the literature, either spell it out in the node or cut it.
