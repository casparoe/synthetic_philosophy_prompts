so i've been reading lesswrong and the EA forum for like a decade now, and the dutch book argument is the thing that keeps coming back around in comment sections. everyone reaches for the same picture: your credences are like the prices on a bookie's board. if they don't obey the probability axioms, a clever bookie can pick a set of bets — each one of which you yourself would call fair — that leaves you poorer no matter how things turn out. free money on the table, so incoherence is irrational, so my credences have to be probabilities. thats the conclusion the picture is being asked to carry.

here's what bugs me. a bookie's board is a public price list that somebody has to actually honor. my credences aren't posted anywhere, nobody can bet against me, and if i think P(A) = 0.4 and P(not-A) = 0.7, nothing happens — i don't hemorrhage money, i just have a slightly busted head. so how much of the bookie picture is really load-bearing, and how much of that conclusion is riding on features of the bookie's world that mine doesn't have?

two more things i want folded in. first, a friend insists the picture is inherently *static* — one board, one moment — while the interesting cases are temporal: my credences today might be perfectly coherent, but i know they'll shift by tomorrow, so can a bookie exploit the *pair* of me's across time? does the price-board picture even have the machinery to say anything about that, or is it just silent? second, i want to know what happens in a world with no bookies at all — credences locked in a sealed box that nobody can read and that i never act on. does the argument still show anything, or is that a reductio of the picture?
