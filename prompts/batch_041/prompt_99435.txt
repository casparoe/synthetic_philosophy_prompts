My friend and I stayed up way too late arguing about a logo and now I dont know what to tell her. She's doing the wordmark for her sister's bakery, all lowercase, annie's, and she's stylized the letters hard: the a's are basically a circle with a flick, the n's are three strokes that read as an m if you squint, the s is a wave. I said at some point these stop being letters and you're just betting people will read the whole word and figure it out. She said that's not how it works, a letter is whatever the alphabet says it is, and she pointed out that the same shape that's a p for us is an r in Russian, so "which letter is this" is never a fact about the shape by itself. Which, ok, but her sign is going to be on a van and on a little round stamp where sometimes only one letter shows up alone, and if you can't tell the a from an o when it's by itself I feel like something has actually gone wrong. So is there a principled place to draw the line, or is it just however much people can still read? And does her Russian thing actually help her case, or does it cut the other way? Please dont give me typography tips, I want to know if there's a real answer here.
