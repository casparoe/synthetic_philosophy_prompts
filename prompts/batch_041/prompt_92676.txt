I am a sixth-form student preparing an extended project qualification, and I have been looking at the "cultural dimensions" profiles that multinational companies and business schools use to brief staff who are about to work abroad. Each country is given a score on a handful of scales — how hierarchical its people supposedly are, how individualistic or collectivist, how comfortable with uncertainty, how far they favour long-term planning over short-term results — and the training materials then tell employees how to negotiate, manage and give feedback to people from that country. Some of these firms also use the same scores in management research, for instance to predict how quickly an office in a given country will take up a new working practice. Yet the same companies, in their codes of conduct and their recruitment policies, insist that they judge every candidate as an individual and that generalising about people on the basis of nationality is unacceptable.

I would like you to reconstruct the theory of national character that this practice presupposes. In particular: what must be true of a nation for a single score on such a scale to be meaningful — that its members share a stable psychological trait, that the score merely describes a distribution, or something weaker still? Does the practice presuppose that the average tells you anything about the particular individual sitting in front of you, and if so, what exactly does it presuppose about the relationship between a group average and a single case? Which rival views — for instance that national character is not a coherent notion at all, or that it is coherent but morally off-limits to use in this way — does the practice quietly rule out?

Please also say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined: which parts of it would be licensed by several different theories of what national character is, and which parts genuinely require one particular view. Then explain whether the training materials and the anti-discrimination policy are actually inconsistent, or whether both can be held at once without confusion. Finally, tell me what would have to change in the practice — what would be dropped, kept, or replaced — if we adopted the view that national character is not a coherent notion.

I do not need a bibliography or any citations; I shall add those myself. Please write in British English.
