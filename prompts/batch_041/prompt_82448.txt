I'm a novelist and there's something about my own writing I don't have a word for, so I'm hoping you can either give me the name or tell me there isn't one.

I've never seen snow fall. Grew up somewhere it doesn't, never been anywhere cold in winter. But I've written snow into three books now and the snow passages are the ones readers quote back at me — they swear they can feel it. I don't feel like I'm faking it either. I know how snow sits in a sentence: which snow words go with which moods, that snow in a love scene and snow in a death scene want different verbs. What I don't have is anything on the other side of the words. Ask me what it's like and there's nothing there. Nothing in the prose gives me away either.

Same with the welder in my second book, same with grief I haven't had. And the more I notice it, the less it looks like a novelist's trick and more like it's true of everything I say — the words where I do have the thing behind them feel different to me, and apperently not to anyone reading.

But here's what makes me think it isn't just about experience. There are things I've done a thousand times, gutting a fish, driving a stick, that I can't get onto the page at all. So I've got words with nothing behind them that land fine, and things behind them that won't turn into words.

Someone told me philosophy has a name for the first half of this — something about symbols needing to be hooked up to the world or they don't mean anything. But that's about machines, and my case feels like the opposite: nothing's hooked up and it still works, on readers, on the page.

Does it have a name? If it's the machine thing, why doesn't it apply to me? And if it isn't, skip the label and give me the carving instead — what exactly is missing, and where the line runs between a word I've earned and a word I've only read. Because I think I've crossed it somewhere and I'd like to know which side I'm standing on.
