My friend Dana and I got into it for like two hours last night about the arrow of time — we were supposed to be watching a movie, but no, we had to do this instead — and I walked away feeling like I won every exchange, which honestly makes me suspicious that I was just talking past her. Before I go back and declare victory, I want an audit of my actual position, because I'm presenting it at a reading group next week and I'd rather find the hole now than in front of everyone. Here's roughly everything I believe about this, in order of how confident I am:

(1) Time is more like space than like a river. There's no objective present moment and no literal passage or flow; tense is a feature of how we talk and think, not of the world, and the whole block is just sitting there.

(2) But the past/future difference is not a projection, a convention, or something we impose. It's a real, objective feature of the world, not just in our heads.

(3) That feature is at bottom thermodynamic. The future is just the direction of entropy increase. As far as I can tell, that's the only asymmetry there is.

(4) The fundamental laws don't distinguish past from future at all — run any of them backward and they're still satisfied.

(5) The reason entropy was ever low in the first place (the universe started in a ludicrously special low-entropy state — and gravity makes this worse, since clumping seems to raise entropy rather than lower it) is a brute contingent fact. It has no explanation, and demanding one is a category mistake.

(6) Entropy is always relative to a coarse-graining, to a choice of which macrovariables you track, and the choice of the "right" ones is at least partly a matter of what's useful for creatures like us, not something nature hands you on a platter.

(7) Memory, records, causation, the fact that we can affect the future but not the past, the felt sense that time passes — all of that is downstream of the entropy gradient, not an independent source of the arrow.

Dana's line was basically that if (1) is true then (2) is just me smuggling a present moment back in through the side door, and that the whole package collapses into "we remember the past because we remember the past." I don't think she's right, but I couldn't say cleanly why, and I have a nagging feeling that two of these are quietly doing the same job and that one of them is cheating — I just can't tell which.

So go through them and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way. Rank the real ones by how bad they are, say for each what would have to give and what it would cost me to give it, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most mess with the least collateral damage. Write it in American English, and don't just hand me the standard story about entropy and boundary conditions — I already know that story, I want to know where it leaks.
