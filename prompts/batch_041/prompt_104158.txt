My vocabulary, so you can calibrate how much you may presuppose: generative models and latent variables; hypothesis classes and model selection; minimum description length and compression-based accounts of learning; predictive coding and variational free energy; bias-variance tradeoffs; and, from an undergraduate degree in physics, coarse-graining, effective field theories, and the renormalization group.

Here is my first pass, offered so that you can correct it rather than restate it: the physical stance is the base-level simulator; the design stance is a coarse-grained model whose states are functional roles rather than microphysical states; the intentional stance is that same model with its latent variables interpreted as propositional attitudes, that is, a model whose hypothesis class is restricted to systems with beliefs and desires. The real-patterns thesis then becomes the claim that the intentional description earns its ontological keep when it compresses better than the alternatives. I suspect this is too clean.

Spend a few sentences first working out what each stance is doing in Dennett's own setting, then translate each of the three stances and the real-patterns thesis into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, without smuggling in my framework's assumptions and calling the result a translation. And once the pattern talk is made precise, does Dennett's realism about patterns reduce to the claim that some coarse-grainings are objectively privileged — a claim that, if so, my renormalization-group vocabulary can state more sharply than his can?

Think carefully and lay out your reasoning before concluding. Answer in American English.
