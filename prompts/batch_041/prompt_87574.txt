I am a theoretical condensed-matter physicist — a postdoc, if it matters — who uses effective field theory and the renormalization group daily and who has read perhaps three papers in the philosophy of science, all on scientific realism. I would like you to translate the structural realist position in philosophy of science into the vocabulary I actually work in, as faithfully as you can manage, and then tell me what did not survive the translation.

The position as I understand it, and I would like you to correct my understanding wherever the translation forces you to: the structural realist holds that when a successful theory is replaced by a better one, what is carried over is not its inventory of entities and properties but its structure — typically its equations, or their mathematical form — and that this carry-over is the best explanation of why the successor theory works in the predecessor's domain; the discarded parts (the ether, phlogiston, the crystalline spheres) are the ontology, and the retained parts are the relations. Against this stands the pessimistic meta-induction, which observes that our present ontology will look like the ether eventually, and the suspicion that structure itself has never been made precise.

Restate this in my vocabulary: the Wilsonian picture of coarse-graining; effective field theories with their cutoffs and operator expansions; universality classes and fixed points; relevant and irrelevant operators; the distinction between what a regularization scheme makes of a theory and what is scheme-independent; the role of symmetry and anomaly in determining what a coarse-graining map can and cannot wash out. I want the translation to be systematic rather than impressionistic — take the structural realist's central claims one at a time and say what each becomes, if anything, when it is asserted by someone whose notion of a theory is an effective action and whose notion of a real feature is something that does not depend on the cutoff.

Then the residue report, which is the part I care about. Which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart at all in what I do? I am particularly interested in whether the epistemic and the ontic versions of the view come apart in my vocabulary: a working physicist has a clear sense of what everything in a universality class shares, but not obviously a sense of structure existing, as opposed to structure being what the models happen to share. And can the claim that relations are prior to their relata be said at all in terms of correlation functions, order parameters, and symmetry groups, which seem always to be relations among some degrees of freedom one has already posited? Tell me also whether the structural realist's choice of what to call the retained structure survives contact with the fact that equations are themselves scheme-dependent, so that the invariant content of a theory looks smaller and more abstract than the equations — perhaps what survives is the fixed-point data and the anomalies rather than the Lagrangian.

Stress-test the translation on at least one case where the continuity story strains rather than supports the view. I have in mind the cosmological constant, where the structure we are most confident of — general relativity plus quantum field theory, coarse-grained — yields a vacuum energy that misses observation by an enormous factor; or quantum gravity, where background independence suggests that the coarse-graining framework I am offering you as the target vocabulary may not itself survive as the vocabulary. Say whether the strain indicates a defect in my vocabulary or something the structural realist claim was quietly assuming.

Finally, keep the translation honest. Do not read the target framework's assumptions — that irrelevant operators do not matter, that the cutoff is an artifact, that only scheme-independent quantities are real — back into the original position and call that a translation. Where the only way to make something come out is to use a word of mine in a way a physicist would not, flag it. And when the translation is done, give me your view on the residue: is what is left over a sign that my vocabulary is missing something a physicist ought to have, or a sign that the original claim was less clear than it looked?
