I convene a working group on explanation in the social sciences. Two of its members have been circling each other for two years over a single question — can culture be a cause? — and have finally agreed to stop arguing and write down where they actually stand. One holds that values and religious commitments are among the genuine causes of economic development, and treats Weber's thesis about ascetic Protestantism and the rise of a methodical, capital-accumulating conduct of life as the paradigm of a cultural explanation that earns its keep. The other holds that where culture correlates with development it is a downstream marker, that the causal work is done by institutions, incentives, and material conditions, and that appeals to values are either unfalsifiable or a restatement of the behaviour they were meant to explain. They have asked me to commission the document they should have produced instead of another year of seminar skirmishing.

Write that document: the joint statement both could put their names to. Include, in this order:

1. A numbered set of agreed claims — what the dispute is and is not about; how each side will use the words cause, culture, and value, and which disambiguations both accept; what Weber's thesis asserts and what both sides agree it does not assert (paraphrase throughout; no quotations, no citations); what could count in principle as a cultural cause; and what the existing evidence does and does not settle.

2. A table of the residual disagreements, each side's position stated in wording that side would accept as its own.

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — each with the case, natural experiment, or argument both sides agree bears on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance. Work these through, describing each so that it stands on its own: whether the operative mechanism is specific to the Calvinist doctrine of double predestination (which would predict a gap between Calvinist and Lutheran regions, and whose absence would leave the Protestant-capitalist correlation intact but the mechanism dead); whether a confession persists in a population that migrates and then lives under institutions it did not choose, or fades within a generation or two; whether conduct tracks the confession a region currently has or the one it was made to abandon under a ruler-imposed reversal; and whether individual differences in values can be assumed to aggregate to a macro-level outcome at all, given that prices, selection, and strategic interaction can absorb or invert them.

4. A closing note saying which parts of the dispute you judge to be merely verbal, and why.

I want a committed document, not a survey. Where a formulation has to be chosen, choose one and give your reason; where the argument or evidence plainly favours one side on some sub-question, say so rather than manufacturing symmetry; and end with your own judgment about which crux is load-bearing. No bibliography.

Keep it under 900 words.
