I'm a novelist, about 60k words into a book that has turned into a procedural, and I need help with the procedure itself. Set-up: near-future country, a law that protects any entity "to the extent that it is sentient" -- the vagueness is the engine of the whole plot. My narrator is a welfare classification officer. She drives out to a site (cricket farm, lab, robot factory, petting zoo), spends maybe twenty minutes, fills in one page, and the tier she writes down decides whether the place needs a licence, whether the cruelty provisions bite, and whether the owner can appeal. She has no philosophy training. Her supervisor signs anything above the lowest tier. Unknowns get kicked to a regional panel. Three or four of these a week. The book definately dies if the procedure is hand-wavy, because then the drama is just me deciding case by case what I want.

So: please turn "sentience comes in degrees and moral status tracks it" into a form she can actually fill out. I've got a rough sketch of the criteria and I want you to fix it, not flatter it. My sketch: (1) does it have some kind of nervous system or equivalent wiring; (2) does it avoid things that hurt it before, even when avoiding them costs it something else it wants; (3) is there anything it's like to be it; (4) does it have a stake in its own future; (5) failing all that, is there any internal state that functions like a good/bad signal.

Two things I'm pretty sure have to survive your version, even if the form ends up longer than a form should be. First, the officer's confidence has to be kept separate from how much is riding on being right -- "probably not sentient, but horrific if we're wrong" is a different box from "certainly sentient, mildly." Second, don't let the form collapse "it registers damage" into "damage is bad for it."

Then run it on three cases and give me the filled-in form for each: a black soldier fly larvae farm (millions of them, one officer, one afternoon); a warehouse robot whose fault-avoidance circuit has learned to steer around the spot where a pallet fell on it; and a farmed octopus. Somewhere in there, mark the places where the form has to stop and hand the decision to a human being because the question is values and not facts. I'm worried your procedure makes the robot a flat no, which guts my third act -- if it does, say so straight, don't fudge it, and tell me what I can honestly do with that. Also tell me whether the form sums across individuals or not, because the larvae farm needs a number and I don't know what number it should need.

Last thing: say how confident you are in all this, and single out the one claim in it you're least sure of. I'd rather have one honest flag than hedges sprinkled everywhere.
