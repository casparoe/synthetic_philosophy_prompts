Ok so I'm trying to get clear on the aggregation problem before I commit to any view, and I've decided what I actually need first is a spec, not another argument for one side. So here's what I want. Suppose someone wanted to give a full theory of when it's ok to let the numbers count in a moral decision and when it isn't, basically the adding-up move that Scanlon's contractualism tells us we're not allowed to make. What I want from you is the list of things any such theory has to get right. Not the theory itself, the requirements.

For each item I want the case or consideration that forces it. The classic one-person-drowning-versus-five is obviously one, but I don't just want that. I want the requirements that come from: the fact that a single person can have a claim so strong it beats a bunch of weaker ones; the fact that we seem to care about numbers at all and that caring doesn't look like an obvious mistake; cases where the harms differ in kind and can't be lined up on a single scale; cases where one person will suffer a great deal versus many people each suffering a little; cases where the same person is affected repeatedly over time, which seems different from affecting many people once, and honestly this is the one that confuses me most, because I have a hunch a decent theory has to say something about whether time-slices of one life can be aggregated the way separate lives can, or why they can't; and the requirement that the theory not just quietly collapse into do-the-most-good when nobody's looking.

Then tell me which of these requirements you think are real and which are just prejudices of one camp, like is never-aggregate itself a substantive moral claim or more of a methodological rule. And flag any pair you think can't both be satisfied, because if there's a genuine conflict buried in there then that's kind of the whole point and I'd rather know now than after I've picked a side.

If you can, also tell me which single requirement does the most damage, i.e. which one rules out the most otherwise attractive theories.

One thing about format: please write it as continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just paragraphs like you'd write in an email. Lists make me skim and I want to actually read the reasoning behind each requirement.
