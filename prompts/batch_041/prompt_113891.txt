Help me sort out the relation between the hole argument in general relativity and Leibniz's older shift argument against absolute space. As I understand it both work the same basic way: generate two worlds that a substantivalist seems forced to count as genuinely distinct even though nothing observable differs — one by translating or rotating the whole material world relative to absolute space and time, the other by applying a diffeomorphism that is the identity everywhere outside some bounded 'hole' and different inside it. Are these really the same argument wearring different clothes, or is the local character of the hole (identity outside, non-identity inside) doing essential work that the global shifts can't do? I'm especially interested in whether the hole version raises a problem about determinism — same data on a slice, different futures — that the global Leibniz shifts simply don't raise, and whether that means the two arguments are pressing on different premises, say the Leibniz shifts needing absolute space as a background container while the hole argument instead trades on identifying the metric field with spacetime itself. So: which of the two is harder for a substantivalist to defuse, and should a relationalist who shrugs at the Leibniz shifts be equally relaxed about the hole argument, or does the hole argument embarrass her too?
