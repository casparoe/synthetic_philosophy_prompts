I would like a position argued rather than a survey. Risk-weighted expected utility weakens or drops the independence axiom and instead lets a risk function r reweight how much each probability interval of a prospect contributes to its value. The claim I want defended is that r is not an independent parameter of practical rationality at all — it is a component of the agent's utility function, and the theory only looks like a distinct rival to expected utility because it launders a taste about uncertainty into the aggregating machinery. Let one case carry the argument: an agent, before facing an otherwise ordinary gamble with a fixed and known prize, is offered a pill that will rewrite her risk function — she now weights the tails heavily and would, after the pill, weight them lightly. Ask what risk-weighted expected utility can say about whether she may coherently prefer taking the pill, given that r enters the evaluation of the prospect rather than appearing among its outcomes, and let the answer fix the general moral. Then meet the obvious reply — that relocating r into the utility function makes "risk-averse" and "dislikes uncertainty" the same predicate and leaves the theory with no empirical content — and say which reading a decision theorist should adopt, and what would move you off it.
