I sit on a labor tribunal that hears complaints under a statutory prohibition on relations of domination in employment. The statute leaves the phrase undefined, so the content of the prohibition has been built case by case, and the parties now appearing before us are citing our four decided cases against each other.

In Kestrel Basin, the employer owned every house, the only store, and the only payroll within ninety miles. It had never evicted or fired anyone for complaining; grievances went to a company committee whose findings the owner was free to reject, and the workers' charter could be amended by the owner at will. We found a relation of domination and ordered the charter converted into a binding agreement with independent arbitration.

In Ardent Logistics, comparable work was performed in a metropolitan area with dozens of competing employers, under a sectoral collective agreement and binding arbitration that the employer had no power to alter unilaterally. We found no domination, even though the supervisors there held at least as much formal authority to dismiss as the Kestrel Basin managers held.

In the Dalal matter, a live-in caregiver's residence permit was tied to her employer, so that leaving meant removal from the country. The family was generous and treated her well, but she had no enforceable contract and no forum in which to contest anything. Domination.

In the Whitcomb matter, adjunct instructors were paid by the course, were free to teach at several institutions in a dense market, and were covered by a systemwide agreement with arbitration that the administration could not change on its own. No domination.

The case now before us concerns Northline, a freight-dispatch platform. Drivers own their trucks and can work for competing brokers or take direct contracts; most do, and demand for their services is strong. Northline sets load prices and assigns routes through a proprietary model, and its terms permit it to change the rate formula and to deactivate an account at any time. Deactivations are issued by the model. An internal review team exists, but it corrects only data-entry errors, does not disclose the criteria applied, and has reversed only a small fraction of the appeals brought to it. Drivers' counsel argues that this is Kestrel Basin with an app; the platform argues that it is Ardent Logistics with a different interface.

I have to write the tribunal's opinion. Recommend a holding, name the closest of the four precedents, and set out the two or three rules of decision that fit all four of our earlier cases equally well. Say what each rule predicts about Northline, which rule we should adopt and why, and what further case would separate the rules cleanly. Lead with the holding in one sentence, write in American English, and keep the whole answer under 650 words. Do not cite any literature, statute, or decision beyond the ones I have described; where a general principle is doing work in your reasoning, state it in your own words. In no more than two sentences at the end, say whether these four precedents commit us to a conception of liberty that goes beyond simple non-interference, and then tell me which consideration would move you to the opposite holding and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
