I teach a 300-level seminar on science, technology, and society -- 18 students, one 80-minute session, movable chairs. I want them to feel a race to the bottom in their gut before I ever put a name on it. Not the grazing-commons story where the pasture gets eaten; something closer to the case where there's no shared resource at all, just everyone paying more and more to stay in exactly the same place, with the damage landing on people who aren't in the room.

Design me a game. Here's what I want built into it, and I'm open on how. Each team can, every round, pay something to improve its standing, but the standings are positional -- what you're buying is the ranking, so if everyone buys, nobody has moved and everybody's poorer. Layer a second thing on top of that: every escalation also nudges up a risk that's shared and correlated rather than independent, so one bad draw can hit all of them at once, and the individually sensible expected-value arithmetic adds up to an outcome none of them would have chosen. Put one team under a charter that forbids it from escalating, and let me watch what the structure does to that team, because I want the debrief to be about selection pressure and not about anybody's character. Then give them a phase where they can talk, negotiate, sign whatever they want, and watch the agreements come apart for reasons we can actually name; then a phase where they're allowed to merge, buy each other out, or hand power to a referee -- so they see that the exits exist and what each one costs.

I want rules and materials, a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money (or, if it needs a little, tell me how much and how you'd cap it -- I dont want a student in tears over forty bucks), a minute-by-minute timeline, and a debrief that maps each moment of the game onto the idea, with clear places for me to hang the standard vocabulary afterward. Nothing up front: no lecture, no name on the board until the last ten minutes.

Predict what goes wrong the first time I run it, including the student who simply refuses to bid, because I know thats coming and I have no idea what to do with them. And be honest about what the activity can't reproduce. My suspicion is that it can't give them the part that makes the real cases so hard -- the losses are invisible, delayed, and mostly fall on people who never show up -- but tell me what else is missing and what I'll have to supply myself.
