We tried Nomic in my legal philosophy reading group last month — ten of us, mixed law and philosophy grad students, 90 minutes — and it was a bust. Everyone just argued about whether the rules permitted the thing being argued about, we never really played a turn, and I dont think anyone felt the thing I wanted them to feel. So I want a redo, but tighter.

The thing I want them to feel: you write down a rule to stop some other rule from being changed, then you notice that the protection can be taken apart by the very procedure it was supposed to constrain — so you protect the protection, and the protection of the protection, and it never bottoms out; and at the bottom there's nothing inside the system that authorizes it, just the fact that everyone is treating it as the bottom. Plus the sneaky move where you change who's allowed to vote before you change the voting rule. Plus the specific squirm of a rule changing and then having to ask whether someone's move last turn was legal when they made it.

So: design me a runnable 90-minute game, actual moves and small stakes, so the group has a reason to want the rules to come out a certain way and the paradoxes arrive as consequences of them trying to win rather than as topics of conversation. What I need:

- Printable materials: the starting charter (write the actual rule text out — short, no jargon, nothing that reads like a textbook or a legal document), a handful of special cards, tokens or some way of keeping score.
- Roles. I want one person whose only job is to answer "is this move legal?" using only what's written, and someone keeping a record of what got enacted and when. Tell me whether I should play or just facilitate.
- The charter should include a clause protected by a unanimity requirement, a clause about who gets to vote on what, and at least one card that lets a player do something retroactive. Should the unanimity clause protect itself too? Tell me what happens either way — I want to know which version actually produces the regress and which one just produces a boring standoff, and how to recover if we get the standoff.
- Playable by people who've read nothing for the session. The words validity, regress and rule of recognition shouldn't appear in anything the players see.
- A 15-minute debrief that maps specific moments from our run onto: entrenchment not terminating, the difference between a rule being valid and a rule being accepted by the people who have to enforce it, and the fact that the bottom of the system is an act rather than a rule.
- Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time (my bet: someone declares the whole charter void, someone else refuses to accept a vote they lost, and a quiet majority just stop caring). And be honest about what the game can't reproduce — my suspicion is it can't reproduce the slow thing where a rule of recognition gets built out of habit and convergent practice over generations rather than by a founding vote, and that I'll have to seperate that out and just tell them.

It also has to be fun enough that they don't feel like they're doing homework.
