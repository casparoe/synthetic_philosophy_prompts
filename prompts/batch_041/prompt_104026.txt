i'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training at all, but i sit in on a monthly reading group some friends run out of a cafe, and next month's session is on whether what you believe or expect can actually change what you see, versus just changing what you'd say or judge. what i can't get past is the arrowhead thing, where the two lines have those little fins on the ends, and you measure them, you know they're the same length, you'd bet your rent on it, and they still dont look the same. one friend says that's the whole ballgame, it proves the beliefs never get in, the eye is sealed off from the head. the other one says that's just a weird lab trick and that when a radiologist reads a scan or a forager walks a hillside they genuinely see more, they're not just infering it off the same old pixels. i want to close our session with a short poem that lays that fork out without picking a side.

what i'm picturing: five quatrains, and every one of them ends on the same line, word for word, except the last quatrain, where exactly one word in that line gets swapped. that repeated line should be a plain report of what's in front of the eyes, the bare look of the thing, and the three lines leading into it should shift from stanza to stanza to carry whatever's going on in the observer's head, what they know, what they're hoping for, what they've been staring at all week. so the same report keeps coming back out of a different person, and the reader has to decide whether it's the same experience each time or just the same words for a different one. in the first couple of stanzas the belief shouldn't touch the seeing at all, somewhere in the middle it should go genuinely unclear, and that swapped word in the last stanza is the thing i want left hanging, i dont want it resolved as either real penetration or wishful seeing, and i dont want a moral tacked on either. ground it in a scene rather than in abstractions. the case i know best is my own, i've spent two months drawing boxes around solar panels in satellite photos for a training set and i swear i now see them on rooftops i'm not even looking at, but a radiologist or someone hunting mushrooms would work too, whatever you can actually make breathe.

one more thing, please dont use the technical name for the phenomenon anywhere in the poem and dont explain the theory inside it. it should just be a poem about looking at things that happens to be exactly right about this, so that someone who's read the literature recognises the two cases sitting side by side, and someone who hasn't just reads a good poem about eyes. plain verse is all i want back, no headings, no bullet points, no notes or commentary after it, no explanation of what you did, just the poem.
