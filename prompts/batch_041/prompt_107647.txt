So my friend Dana and I spent most of last night arguing about whether interpretability research actually discovers anything about what's going on inside a network, or whether it just builds tools that predict and control behavior. She kept calling me a realist like it was a diagnosis. Her line was that when I say a model "represents" a concept, all I've actually earned is that some direction in activation space predicts the behavior, and the rest is me smuggling in my own vocabulary and then acting like the network agreed with me.

Which would be fine, except I run a small interview series (audio only, like 45 minutes) and next week's guest is a researcher who has built her whole career on Dana's side of that fight. Her signature move is: every interpretability result is an instrument, and that's fine. She thinks "does the network really represent this feature" is a question with no possible evidence behind it, and that the practical stuff -- monitors, feature edits, steering -- doesn't need an answer to it. She's very good on mic. She has a smooth reply for everything, and I dont want to sit there nodding for 45 minutes while she does the smooth reply nine times.

Can you write me 9 interview questions? I want them aimed at the actual joints of her position, not at getting her to restate it. Specific things I need covered, at least one question each:

- What exactly is the inference from "a probe decodes X from the activations" to "the network represents X" supposed to be, and where is it supposed to break?
- The intervention test: find a direction, intervene on it, get a specific and predictable behavior change, including off-distribution. Is passing that test supposed to be sufficient for representation, or only necessary? What does it rule out that decoding alone doesn't?
- The thing Dana said that I actually couldn't answer: the same features keep getting rediscovered across different methods, different random seeds, and even different models. She says that's too much coincidence to be us inventing things. I want the guest to have to say whether rediscovery is evidence of anything and why.
- Underdetermination: you can get two different decompositions of the same network that both pass the intervention test. So what would "the network's own decomposition" even be? I want her pressed on whether that's a coherent thing to want or a fantasy about a privileged vocabulary.
- Whether "represents X as X" needs a theory of content at all. Dana says this is just the old problem of intentionality and that interpretability people wave it away by saying they're mechanists, not philosophers. Is she right?
- Stakes: if features are instruments, what happens to the safety pitch that a monitor or a feature edit will transfer to another model, or to a fine-tune of the same model? Does instrumentalism undercut that pitch or actually support it?
- One that asks what would change her mind, phrased so that "nothing, the question is confused" isn't a free pass.
