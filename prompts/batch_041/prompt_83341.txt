I am preparing a position paper for an interdisciplinary workshop on value alignment, and I want a consistency audit of my own commitments before I circulate it. You are a philosopher with expertise in AI alignment, social choice theory, and the theory of idealized preferences. Please treat the following as my considered position:

(1) The proper target of an aligned system is coherent extrapolated volition: what we would jointly endorse under full relevant information, unlimited deliberation, and mutual accommodation, rather than what we actually prefer now.
(2) The ideal is constructivist: no substantive standard of the good exists prior to the ideal deliberative procedure; the procedure constitutes the ideal.
(3) Idealization must correct for adaptive and endogenous preferences, but only by returning agents to their own standpoints under improved conditions, never by substituting the system's or designer's values. Call this the no-ventriloquism constraint.
(4) There is a unanimity lock: if every actual person prefers A to B, the ideal cannot rank B above A, even if ideal deliberation would reverse that preference.
(5) Where ideal deliberation fails to converge, a tie-breaking aggregation rule is needed; that rule should itself be selected under idealized conditions.
(6) CEV is a single, unified object that can be represented as a utility function and used directly for action.

I do not know which of these is the weak link, and I am not antecedently attached to any one of them. Please tell me which commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each tension is. Rank the tensions rather than listing them as equally bad; for each, give the cheapest revision and what it would cost; then identify the single revision that would resolve the most. Start by brainstorming the possible fault lines before committing to a ranking, and think carefully about the difference between a procedural constraint and a substantive one. If the package cannot be audited as posed without further stipulation, or if one of my formulations is confused, say so plainly rather than manufacturing a tidy answer.
