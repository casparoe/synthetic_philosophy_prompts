I would like you to tell me whether the following pattern has an established name, and if it does not, whether it deserves one and how it should be defined.

I keep running into the same shape in unrelated places, and I have not been able to match it cleanly to anything I already know. Two instances will make the shape clearer than an abstract statement would.

The first is a scientific field I have followed for some years. Slow, careful work — long replications, honestly reported negative results, modest claims — is steadily outcompeted by fast work that cuts methodological corners and reports more striking findings. Each individual decision is defensible on its own, and no one is breaking an agreement, because there never was one to begin with. Over time the corners become ordinary, the journals and hiring committees adjust to the faster tempo, and the field's shared sense of what counts as a solid result quietly loosens. What strikes me most is what happens to the people. Those who remain and rise are increasingly the ones who are temperamentally untroubled by the looser standard, so that after a decade the field could not restore the old standard by vote even if the question were put to it, because the people who would have voted for it have been filtered out.

The second is the attention economy of a large online platform. Creators who want to make restrained, careful work find that it is outcompeted by creators who optimize hard for engagement, and each round of optimization raises the audience's threshold for what registers as engaging at all. The creators who survive are the ones willing to go further; the ones who disliked the direction leave, and the culture of the platform's successful creators drifts toward the very dispositions that keep the competition running.

Several features seem to me to be doing the work, and none of the familiar stories I know quite accommodates all of them at once. First, there is no defection: nothing is violated because nothing was agreed. Second, what gets used up is not really a fixed stock of some rivalrous good but a shared standard or norm, and the competition degrades the standard itself, so there is no external yardstick by which to say how far down we have gone — the measure moves with the race. Third, the competition does not merely lock everyone into a bad equilibrium that they would collectively prefer to escape; it also changes who the participants are, selecting for people whose preferences make the equilibrium self-sustaining. Fourth, and consequently, the obvious remedy — getting everyone to agree to stop — is undermined in advance, since the constituency that would have to agree has been replaced by one that would not.

I am aware of the usual examples of individually sensible choices adding up to a collectively bad result, and of the general idea that competition can leave everyone worse off than they would be under coordination. But those accounts seem to assume a fixed preference over outcomes and a fixed thing being consumed, whereas the pattern I am describing seems to turn on the standard being consumed and the participants' preferences both shifting under the competition.

What I want is not a bare label. If an established concept covers this, I want to know which parts of my description it captures and which it leaves out, how it differs from its nearest neighbors, and which of those neighbors fits best. If nothing established fits, I want you to propose a term and define it, and then draw its boundaries: what would count as an instance, what would look similar but not qualify, and where the edges of the concept fall.

Please answer in American English.
