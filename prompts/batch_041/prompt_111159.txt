I've been chewing on this whole Moloch / multipolar trap framing that everyone in my corner of the internet keeps going on about, and I think I might be missing something, because the more I look at it the more it seems like it's just a collective action problem wearing a scary mask. Here's my reasoning. Collective action problems are basically the most studied topic in all of social science, and the big takeaway from Ostrom's commons work is that real communities solve them all the time without any top-down enforcer — clear boundaries, repeated interaction, cheap monitoring, graduated sanctions, people writing their own rules. So if a race to the bottom is just a commons dilemma, the practical answer is "go find your institution," which feels way too easy given how much existential weight people hang on Moloch. So what am I getting wrong? Is the Moloch framing just "sometimes coordination is hard," with extra vibes?

To make it concrete: my lab has drifted into everyone working weekends, no ones happy about it, everyone says they'd prefer to stop, but nobody stops, because the lab across the hall does it and the PI is vaguely aware of who's around on Saturday. My instinct is to say, fine, commons problem, get everyone in a room, agree on norms, monitor each other. But a friend says that's not what a multipolar trap is, and that if the norm doesn't stick it's because the whole configuration is being selected for — labs that slack get outcompeted for grants and grad students and quietly disappear, so there's no "everyone in a room" that includes the actual relevant players. I dont really understand the distinction he's drawing. Is he right that some of these things are structurally different from commons problems, and if so what exactly is the difference — is it about monitoring, about the payoff being positional rather than about a shared stock, about players being replaced rather than persuaded, about third parties who can't sit at the table? And does any of that change what I should actually do, or advise other people to do, about the big versions of these races? Happy to be told my Ostrom analogy is doing way too much work, I'd just like to know where exactly it breaks.
