So my brother-in-law and I got into it at Thanksgiving — he's the history guy in the family, I never went to college, I just read a lot — and the fight was supposedly about who started the Cold War. Except it wasn't really about the Cold War, because we kept agreeing on every actual fact and still not agreeing about who started it, and at some point I realized we weren't even using the word the same way. I've been chewing on it for a few weeks since, and now I see the same shape in much smaller fights, so I figured I'd ask someone who might know if this thing has a name.

Here's the shape, as best I can lay it out. Take two neighbors with a feud that ends with somebody calling the cops. Ask who started it and you get two complete, honest, accurate stories, and in each one the teller is reacting to something the other one did. And the reaction is doing real work there: whatever happened first only becomes a 'start' once the other person treats it as one, so the person who moves second gets to pick, after the fact, where the beginning was. Second thing, the answer slides with the window you choose — go back far enough and everybody's a starter, go back a week and it's just whoever did the last thing, and nobody says which window they're using. Third, the thing that gets called the start is almost never the slow part, it's the tellable part — the shot, the insult, the slammed door — not the ten years of small stuff that made the door slam pretty much inevitable. And fourth, the question gets asked like it's factual but it lands as a verdict, because whoever started it is the bad guy; that's how people use it, even the same people who'd happily admit the one who threw the first punch isn't always the one at fault (somebody can throw a punch that was coming anyway, or a first move that was honestly justified). With the Cold War it's all of that with more paperwork and a longer window.

So: is there a name for this? Not for one piece of it but for the whole package — the beginning getting assigned retroactively by the response, the sliding window, the start going to whoever's act is most narratable, and a factual-sounding question smuggling in a verdict. I know some things that are close but I don't think they're it. I've heard that in law you split cause-in-fact from proximate cause, and that picking which cause is 'the real one' is a judgment call, but that seems built for one event with a victim, not for two sides telling two true stories. And I know about questions with a bad assumption baked in, but that's not it either, because this question can be answered honestly from both sides and still be the wrong question. If there's no name, make one up and define it, and tell me what it does and doesn't cover — is it different when there's a genuine first move with no prior history at all? Is it different when one side is way more powerful and the 'response' was wildly out of proportion? And then the part I actually care about: is 'who started it' ever the right question, or should it always be swapped for something else, and if so, swapped for what? Keep it under 600 words and lead with your verdict — name, no name, or here's a new name — before you start explaining, because I'll read the first line and then get distracted.
