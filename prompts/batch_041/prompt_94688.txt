Last night a friend and I argued for the better part of three hours about whether any of our beliefs is ever actually justified, and we ended exactly where these arguments always end: she insisted the chain of reasons must bottom out in something that needs no support, I insisted it can only turn back on itself, and a third friend who had been listening quietly claimed it simply runs on forever and that this is not a defect. I would like to send the three of us a single poem that sets the three answers side by side and lets each one have its own shape, so that the form of the verse does the arguing for me.

Write that poem, in three parts, one for each position: the view that justification terminates in something basic and self-standing; the view that it consists in a system's members lending one another support; and the view that it is an endless, non-repeating chain of reasons whose length is no objection. Give each part a distinct form and let the form carry the position rather than merely describe it. The first part should be able to come to rest, ending on a self-sufficient couplet that would stand on its own if everything before it were struck out. The second should return to its own beginning, so that its closing line is its opening line, or at least ends on its opening word, and the reader feels the circle close. The third should refuse to close: each line should hand a word or phrase to the next, and the final line should be left plainly unfinished, reading as the first link of a further turn rather than a conclusion. Rhyme and meter are yours to choose, but the three parts should feel like three different machines, and someone who knows this territory should be able to tell which part is which without being told.

The poem should be accurate enough that a specialist would nod rather than wince, and clear enough that a reader who has never encountered the problem would still extract the three positions from the verse alone. It should not crown a winner; if it leans at all, let it lean toward whatever the reader already brings. Aim for roughly forty to seventy lines in total, and give it a title that does not give the game away.
