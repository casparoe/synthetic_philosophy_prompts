I am a retired physicist who has recently begun reading Schopenhauer, and I have been struck by his claim that music is not, like the other arts, a copy of the Platonic Ideas but a direct copy of the Will itself — that the composer reveals the inner essence of the world in a way that bypasses conceptual representation altogether. What interests me philosophically is not the claim's romantic appeal but its precise architecture: that the bass voice corresponds to the lowest grades of the will's objectification (the inorganic), that harmony and the intermediate voices correspond to the plant and animal grades, and that melody corresponds to the highest grade — the striving, deliberating human subject. I find this both thrilling and suspicious, given my scientific habits of mind. Please write a short story about a character — perhaps a scientist, perhaps a musician, perhaps both — who has a genuine and unsettling encounter with this Schopenhauerian claim: that music does not represent anything, yet somehow expresses the metaphysical ground of everything. The story should not simply illustrate the doctrine but should dramatize the tension between the claim and a more skeptical, analytic mindset.
