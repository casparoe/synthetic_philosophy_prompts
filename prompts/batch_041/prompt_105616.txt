A relative of mine who teaches economics dismisses the degrowth movement with an analogy: an economy that stops growing is like a bicycle that stops moving — it does not merely stand still, it topples over — so growth is a precondition of stability, and the only serious climate policy is to keep the economy growing while taxing carbon to make that growth cleaner. The picture is persuasive in the moment and I suspect it is carrying more weight than it can bear. Set out the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of the rider, the pedals, the forward motion, and the balance — then say where the analogy holds, where it breaks down, what it quietly assumes about why economies grow and what happens when they do not, and finally whether the conclusion it was used to support, that degrowth is a fantasy and carbon-taxed growth is the only option, survives once the disanalogies are on the table. Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
