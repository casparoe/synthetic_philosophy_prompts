Next month I'm presenting a paper at our work-in-progress seminar and I'd rather prepare for the Q&A than just hope it goes well. My thesis is that personhood is a standing you hold rather than a property you have: relations of recognition are constitutive of the status itself, not merely causally necessary for the capacities, so a creature with all the right psychological capacities but no community holding it in the relevant way isnt a person in the full sense. I'm drawing on ubuntu — you become a person through other people — but I'm arguing it's a general claim about personhood and not a regional curiosity. I also split a floor from a ceiling: the floor is equal basic standing, binary, nobody is below it; the ceiling is thick personhood, earned through participation, comes in degrees, and can be lost. Two bits of the paper I expect to cause trouble. One, I lean on an island case — a human raised entirely alone, or the last survivor after a plague — and claim what's missing there isn't capacities, its uptake. Two, I argue from ordinary usage that "person" sometimes functions as an honorific in a way "human" never does.

The audience is mostly analytic. Two Parfitians who think personal identity is psychological continuity and doesn't much matter. One Kantian working on dignity and respect who gets twitchy whenever personhood is described as gradable. One liberal political philosopher who thinks any graded status is a ladder to atrocity. A bioethics grad student who mainly wants to know what this does to capacity and consent. And an anthropologist who comes to these and is sceptical of "African philosophy" as a category at all.

Give me the prep sheet: the objections this particular room will actually raise, in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of those people would phrase it, each with a two-minute reply, a note on where the reply is soft, and what I should concede up front. Tell me which one I should actually be scared of, and which single reply has to land for the paper to survive. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
