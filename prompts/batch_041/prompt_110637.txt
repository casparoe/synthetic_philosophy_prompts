I am a doctoral candidate in political theory, and I need a decision procedure rather than a discussion. My department has a long-running dispute about which of two neighbouring countries is the more liberal; the pairing assigned to me is Sweden and Norway, though the same dispute is conducted with Canada and the United States and with Austria and Germany, and I take it to be one dispute. As I understand it, the question has a determinate answer: there is a fact about which of the two countries is more liberal, and the work is to identify the instrument that measures it correctly.

I have been working with two candidate measures and have hit a wall. One treats a country's liberality as the extent to which the state leaves its citizens alone — the tax take, the volume of regulatory restriction, the size of the sphere in which private arrangements are nobody else's business. The other treats it as the permissiveness and the generosity of the country's arrangements — the range of lives a citizen may lead without penalty, and the security with which she may lead them. The two do not rank my two countries in the same order, which I had taken to mean that one of the instruments is miscalibrated and that the task is to find out which.

A further difficulty: the only concrete proposal I have managed to formulate myself is to count. Enumerate everything that is legally forbidden in each country; the country with fewer forbidden things is the more liberal. This has the virtue of being runnable, but I cannot get it to behave, since it counts a prohibition on dumping waste into a lake alongside a prohibition on speech, and I do not see how to weigh them against each other without smuggling in a conception of liberty that my measure was supposed to deliver.

Please tell me which of the two measures is correct, and if neither is, what would settle the question. Bear in mind that I intend to write a chapter in which the comparison is actually carried out, so an answer that ends in the observation that the term carries two senses would be of no use to me unless it also tells me how to fix the sense in a way that is not arbitrary — and whether, once fixed, the comparison yields a single ordering at all.
