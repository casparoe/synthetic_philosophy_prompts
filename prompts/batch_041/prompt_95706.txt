ok so I've fallen down a rabbit hole on conscription and armed neutrality and I keep getting into the same argument with two friends — one's Finnish and thinks universal male conscription is just the price of having a country at all, the other did his service in the Swiss militia and insists that's the only arrangement where the army and the citizenry are the same thing, and then there's me, the liberal who keeps muttering about just paying professionals. Every argument I read bottoms out in either "well it worked there" or "well it's basically slavery," and I don't think I can referee any of that until I know what a theory of compelled military service is even supposed to do. So before I read anything else, give me the desiderata: what does any adequate theory of when a state may conscript have to deliver? I want the requirements list, and behind each requirement the case or consideration that forces it — not a list of buzzwords.

The cases I already know have to be handled, so make sure your list accounts for them: the person who'd fight if his own country were invaded but refuses to be shipped off to someone else's war; the person who'd happily do two years in a hospital or on forest fire crews but won't touch a rifle, and the awkward fact that in places with a real civilian-service option so many people take it that the army gets the leftovers; the democracy that votes for a war whose costs fall on a demographic that didn't vote for it; the small neutral country that has to stay heavily armed precisely in order to stay neutral, which seems to make neutrality a reason *for* conscription rather than against it; and the question of women, which everyone dodges. Distinctions it can't be allowed to collapse: losing two years of your life versus being made to accept a chance of dying — those are different burdens and I suspect a theory that justifies one doesn't automatically justify the other; a duty you owe your compatriots versus a duty you owe the state; objecting to all war versus objecting to this war. And it has to be consistent with the boring empirical stuff — professional forces are better at fighting, yet mass conscription keeps coming back, so a theory that can't say why it persists is missing something.

Then the part I actually care about: tell me which of these requirements are non-negotiable and which are just the house preferences of one camp (civic republicans, libertarians, the just-war crowd, the neutrality people), and flag any pair or subset you think can't be jointly satisfied — I have a suspicion that "must be defensible to the person conscripted" and "must be imposable without their consent" are pulling in opposite directions, but I want you to find the real conflicts rather than the ones I've thought of. Skip scoring the existing theories against the list, I'll do that in class.
