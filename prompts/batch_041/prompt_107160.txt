Last night my roommate and I got into a stupidly long arguement out on our porch, all because of a bug zapper I'd just ordered off the internet. She saw the box and got weirdly upset about it — said I was about to electrocute hundreds of bugs a night and that I should be ashamed. I laughed it off, and then she said something I haven't been able to shake. She said: forget whether any single bug matters, look at the numbers. There are something like ten quadrillion ants crawling around on Earth at any given moment, plus who even knows how many other insects, and even if the odds that one of them can actually suffer are minuscule — one in a million, one in a billion, whatever — you multiply a tiny probability by an astronomically huge count and you get a total amount of possible suffering bigger than every human war and plague combined. So by her math, zapping moths or spraying my kitchen is one of the most morally serious things I do all week, way more serious than anything I do for other people.

My other friend thinks thats nonsense. His line is that you can't just multiply when you don't actually know the probability — nobody has any real idea whether bugs feel anything, so "one in a billion" is a number she made up, and if you're allowed to invent tiny probabilities and pair them with huge headcounts, you can justify caring about literally anything. He also says bug nervous systems are so different from ours that "pain" probably isn't even the right word for whatever they've got going on. And honestly, whenever I try to defend her side, I end up sounding like I'm arguing we should all be paralyzed by guilt over stepping on a beetle.

I can't tell who's right and it's driving me a little crazy, especially because it's about bugs, of all things. Can you walk me through it? Is her multiplication actually valid, or is he right that it's a trick? And more practically — should I cancel the zapper, stop squishing ants on the sidewalk, actually care about this stuff? I don't have any philosophy background, so please spell out the reasoning in plain steps and tell me where each of them is going wrong, if they are.
