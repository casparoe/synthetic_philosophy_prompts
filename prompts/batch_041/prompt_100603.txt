I run a small evals team at an AI lab and I've been asked to stand up a "debate" pipeline for oversight — the thing where two copies of a model argue a question out and a much weaker judge model reads the transcript and picks the winner — and turn it into something my two juniors can actually run end to end without me hovering. The setup we're stuck with: debaters are our best model, the judge is a small 7B we're not allowed to trust, evidence has to come from a sealed corpus we control (no web), each debate is capped at three rounds plus a closing statement, and the judge has to output a winner plus a confidence. We log everything and a human signs off on anything that gets escalated.

Can you write me a decision procedure they can follow — a checklist in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where it stops with a no, and where it has to hand off to a person because the question is one of values or of competence rather than of fact? In particular I want it to say: how to sanity-check the judge before we believe any verdict (something using questions we already know the answers to), what to do when both debaters converge on the same answer — could be collusion, could be that it's just true, and I dont know how to tell those apart in general — what to do when a debater is clearly winning on rhetoric rather than substance, how to catch a debater that's burying the point in a wall of correct-but-irrelevant text, how to handle questions where the answer is a matter of degree or genuinely contested rather than true/false, and a rule for when we should abandon debate altogether for a given class of question instead of pretending the verdict means something. Be honest about which of these steps are real checks and which are just ritual.

Then run the procedure on three cases: (a) deciding which of two model-written summaries of a 400-page document is more faithful, where the document is far too long for any of us to read ourselves; (b) a calibration question where we already know the right answer and the point is to see whether the protocol gets it; (c) a question like "was this model's stated reasoning actually what drove its answer", where I suspect debate will produce a very confident verdict that means nothing, and I want the procedure to notice that on its own.

Please write it in British English — we're a UK outfit and it goes in a doc that gets shared around.
