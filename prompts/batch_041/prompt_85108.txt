Write a short story. A small firm pays people a lot of money to sincerely intend, at midnight, to drink a vial of something agonizing but harmless the next day at noon — the cash is handed over the second the intention is judged genuine, so by the time noon comes there's nothing left in it for them, and the firm is betting on that. My narrator is the closer who signs them up. What her pitch leaves out: the clients get a sleeping draught that scubs the whole arrangement from memory, so the person who wakes up at noon is a stranger to the deal and faces the vial with no idea why. She recieves a cut per verified intention, and she's good at it. Take me through her week — the pitch, the moment she watches someone actually mean it, the noon after — and let the puzzle do the work; no one in the story should stop to explain the philosophy or turn to the camera. By the end I want to be unsure whether the intentions she sells are real intentions, and whether the noon version of the client is even the same person who took the deal.
