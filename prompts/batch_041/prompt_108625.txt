I read alot of history and there's a shape of explanation I keep running into that I've never found a word for, and it nags at me.

It goes like this. A historian tells you the outcome turned on something tiny — a message that arrived a day late, a scout who took the wrong road, a fog that lifted an hour early — and says that if that one thing had gone the other way, the whole thing comes out different. Fine. But the exact same tiny mishaps show up in stories where nothing turns on them. The message is late, the fog rolls in, and the army just wins anyway. So a small thing is decisive sometimes and completely swallowed other times, and the difference seems to be whether the situation was already sitting right on the edge, a hair's breadth from going either way. Which you can only see afterward, once you know which way it went. Beforehand nobody can tell you which of the fifty small things is going to turn out to be the load-bearing one.

The part I actually want named is that last asymmetry. You can always assemble a satisfying chain — the whole nail, shoe, horse, rider, battle, kingdom thing — but a chain of the same shape would have worked just as well for the opposite outcome, and there's no test that would have told you in advance which little events were going to matter. I've also noticed the load-bearing ones tend to be timing things, a delay or a head start, rather than big material differences. And the same writer will be a hard determinist about the slow stuff — geography, demography, trade — and then casually let a migraine decide a war.

I know people call this the butterfly effect, but thats not what I mean; the butterfly thing just says small causes can have big effects, which is true of everything and tells you nothing about when a small cause is decisive and when it gets absorbed. I dont think it's just that we tell tidier stories once we know how it ended, either, since the poisedness feels like a real feature of the situation and not only a trick of looking back.

Is there a name for this — either the whole pattern or just the hindsight half? And if there isn't, coin one, but I don't want just a label: tell me where the edges are, what it rules out that butterfly-effect talk and inevitable-outcome talk both let in, and whether the poisedness is definately a real property of the world or something we only get to attribute after the fact.
