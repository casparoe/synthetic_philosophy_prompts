"Hacking's central idea is that psychiatric categories are interactive kinds, unlike the indifferent kinds studied by the natural sciences. When you classify someone as having, say, borderline personality disorder, the label changes how they see themselves and how they act, so the diagnosis partly produces the very thing it diagnoses — a self-fulfilling prophecy. That's the looping effect, and it's why Hacking says human kinds are a moving target: the DSM gets rewritten every decade or so and the disorders mutate along with it, which is why he thinks there can never be a genuine science of mental illness the way there is of chemistry. He contrasts this with natural kinds like gold, which don't care how we classify them. His historical cases — multiple personality in the 1990s, fugue in 1890s France — are illnesses that existed mainly because doctors went looking for them; he calls these transient mental illnesses and says they need a niche to flourish, like an organism does."
