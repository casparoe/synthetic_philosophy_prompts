Write a poem about the hole argument in the philosophy of spacetime. Take the standard setup: begin with a model of a spacetime theory, apply a diffeomorphism that is the identity everywhere outside some open region — the hole — and not the identity within it, and you arrive at a second model with exactly the same observable content; the substantivalist, for whom the manifold's points are themselves real, must count the two as distinct possibilities and therefore concede that the theory fails to determine what happens inside the hole, whereas the relationist may treat them as one possibility differently labelled. I want the poem's form to do that work rather than merely report it: build it on a repetition that recurs with a difference that makes no difference — a refrain, a duplicated stanza, a fixed scheme of line-endings — so that the reader feels the pull of 'here are two poems' and the counter-pull of 'here is one poem read twice', with the turn falling where the substantivalist has to insist that the difference is real after all. Put it in the voice of someone holding the substantivalist line and beginning to be uneasy about it, keep the technicalities honest (the hole a genuine open region, the map the identity outside it, indeterminism the thing at stake), and make the repetition literal enough that a reader could actually perform the swap and find the sense unchanged. Around twenty to thirty lines, in whatever metre or free verse best suits the repetition you choose.
