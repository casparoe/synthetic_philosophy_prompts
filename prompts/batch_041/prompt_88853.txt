I run a fortnightly reading group — a few ML engineers, a couple of philosophers, an economist who turns everything into a game — and next session is my turn to do HCH and factored cognition. What I actually want people to walk out with isnt the engineering story (here's an architecture where the system breaks a task into sub-tasks and subcontracts them out) but the philosophical one: that HCH is offered as an idealization of what deliberation is, a fixed point where one agent's hard judgement gets cashed out as a whole tree of sub-judgements, each one made by a copy of that same agent, copies all the way down, until the leaves are simple enough that somebody just sees the answer. Factored cognition is the piece that says the decomposition can be clean, that a judgement can be split into parts that dont have to be made all at once by one head.

Dont explain it to me — plan how I explain it. What has to be in place before the fixed-point idea makes any sense at all, and in what order. What single example should carry the whole session: I was going to use a person deciding whether to take a job in another city, but I keep thinking there's a better one, maybe something like a novelist who cant decide how a chapter ends and ends up consulting imagined versions of herself who've each read only part of the book — tell me whether that one earns its place or whether I should stick with the obvious case, and what the carrying example needs to have and lack either way. Then the misconceptions this crowd will bring and exactly where in the sequence I head each off: I'm betting on one engineer hearing the whole thing as a concrete proposal for building a system, someone else saying it's just divide and conquer with extra steps, the economist insisting the base case is doing all the real work and the recursion is decoration, and at least one person quietly assuming the copies are all running in parallel and never talking back up. Also one question I can put to the room at the end that tells me they've got the idealization and not just the flowchart, and the two or three things I should openly refuse to go into at this level and say so.
