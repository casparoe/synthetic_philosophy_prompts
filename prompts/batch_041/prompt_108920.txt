Here's my gloss so we're on the same page, since I read it in translation and I'm paraphrasing rather than quoting: his claim that a white horse is not a horse seems to rest on horse naming something general — the kind, the shape any horse has — while white horse names something narrower and determinate, so the two expressions don't pick out the same thing and the sentence comes out true on one reading. The deeper move is that a determinate instance or a compound term doesn't just inherit the general name; the general and the specific aren't interchangeable, and naming has to register that. My seminar also told me the disputers went in for hardness and whiteness coming apart, and for the one and the many, and for names versus actualities, so I'm assuming that machinery is on the table.

Now the collision. On the LLM side there's a picture I find compelling: the model itself isn't an agent at all, it's a simulator that generates continuations, and some of those continuations describe agents, so when it books a flight or plays a character what we have is a simulated agent rather than the model acting. My hypothesis, which you should feel free to wreck, is that Gongsun Long would find this embarassingly congenial and say roughly: the agent-name belongs to the kind, the thing in front of you is one particular generation with a lot of extra determinations hanging off it, so of course the simulated agent isn't an agent, the same way the white horse isn't a horse. But I suspect I'm pattern-matching a slogan onto a text I half understand.
