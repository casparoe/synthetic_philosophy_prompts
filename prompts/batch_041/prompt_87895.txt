I have been reading about information cascades and want to check the logic before relying on the idea. My understanding is that when a cascade goes wrong, the cause is a failure of reasoning: at some point people stop updating properly and treat the accumulating run of earlier choices as stronger evidence than it deserves, or else they simply follow the crowd. On that view, rational herding must be a loose way of speaking, and the remedy is better probabilistic training. What makes me doubt the diagnosis is that the inference itself looks sound — if each person really does draw the correct conclusion from the choices of those before them, the group should end up at least as well informed as the best-informed individual in it, and the only room for error would be a slip in the arithmetic. Could you sort out where the failure actually lies, and say whether the herder's inference is as sound as it looks?
