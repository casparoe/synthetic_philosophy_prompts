A few months ago I started selling little cartoon mascot designs I made for local sports clubs, and last week I found out a big apparel brand is printing basically my mascot on hoodies. When I complained, a friend shrugged and said you can't own an idea, only the particular drawing of it, and anyway copying is how culture works, and if I wanted to profit I should've kept it to myself. I think thats nuts but I can't quite say why, since he's right that me having an idea doesn't stop anyone else from having it too.

Write a dialogue between two people hashing this out — one who thinks creative work is a real kind of property you can genuinely be robbed of, and one who thinks "intellectual property" is just a misleading name for temporary government-granted monopolies, useful at best but not property in any honest sense. Make them both sharp and let them actually answer each other; I don't want them talking past each other. Bonus if the sceptic pulls the move that ordinary property is also just a bundle of legal relations, so the real-property-vs-fake-property line gets messy. Don't end it on "well, both have a point" — have one of them land a decisive blow, and tell me straight which one you think won and why. I'd rather you commit than hedge.
