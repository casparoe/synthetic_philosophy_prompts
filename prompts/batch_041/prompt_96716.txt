I am a PhD student in philosophy of machine learning, and I have drafted an essay on a failure mode for AI safety via debate that I would like you to comment on. The essay's target is the gap between what a debate protocol can guarantee and what a judge can actually verify. My central distinction is between two kinds of obfuscation. Rhetorical obfuscation makes a bad argument look good, and it is the kind the debate literature typically has in mind. Structural obfuscation, by contrast, makes a good argument unavailable to the judge: the honest debater's case may be sound, but the judge cannot check it without solving a problem outside its complexity class, while the dishonest debater can offer a short, misleading certificate that the judge can check. I argue that debate can be robust against the first but not the second, and that this is not merely a point about judge bias or argument length. I then propose a protocol fix: require each debater to submit a verification key, understood as a short certificate that the judge can check in polynomial time, so that the burden shifts from the judge's competence to the protocol's design. My worry is that this fix either collapses into asking the judge to solve the original problem or gives the dishonest debater a new surface for obfuscation by choosing which verification key to submit. I would like blunt, specific comments on four things: whether the distinction between rhetorical and structural obfuscation is real or just a restatement of the verification gap; whether the verification-key proposal is coherent or trivial; whether there is an obvious counterargument I have missed; and whether the central claim is interesting enough to pursue or is simply a known point about computational complexity. I am not looking for citations or reading suggestions; I want conceptual and argumentative feedback.
