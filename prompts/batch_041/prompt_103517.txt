An origin essentialist — someone who holds that no organism could have had a different sperm and egg — and an origin contingentist — someone who holds either that a person could have come from different gametes or that the question trades on a mistaken account of individuation — have argued for years without converging. Draft the joint statement they could both sign. To keep it from collapsing into a restatement of familiar positions, both parties must treat two cases as central, neither of which has a stock answer: first, the case in which a person develops from gametes s and e in one world, while an organism with an identical genome and an identical subsequent life develops from different gametes in another; second, the case of a single organism formed by the fusion of two embryos, so that it has two origins at once. The document should have the following sections, in order: (1) Shared commitments, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, covering what "same individual" can mean, what the modal intuitions do and do not establish, and what the two cases leave open (roughly 200 words); (2) Points of disagreement, a table with one row per live issue and each side's position stated in wording that side would accept (four to six rows); (3) Cruxes and advance predictions, two to four sub-questions whose settlement would move at least one side, each paired with the argument or case both sides agree bears on it and each side's prediction about how it comes out (roughly 300 words); (4) Verbal residue, a short section identifying which parts of the dispute are merely about words, naming the words and explaining why (roughly 100 words).
