I teach high-school biology, and I also read a lot of pop-science stuff in the evenings, and for the last couple of years something about the way people talk about genes has been bugging me and I can't find a name for it, which makes it hard to argue with anyone about it.

Here's what I keep noticing. Instance one: height. In any given rich country, if you do the twin studies, height comes out as something like 80% genetic, and that's how people say it — 'height is mostly genetic'. But the average height in those same countries went up a good bit over the last century or so; my grandma was tiny and my students are giants. Everyone agrees that was food and disease and general living conditions, not genes. So the same trait is mostly genetic and also clearly shifted a lot when the world around it changed. Instance two: a colleague told me that if you compare kids in places where pretty much everyone gets decent food and schooling against kids in places where some do and some really don't, the 'genetic' percentage comes out higher in the more equal places. I might be mangling what she said, but if it's right it's the opposite of what I'd have guessed, since you'd think a level playing field would make the environment matter less, not the genes. And she added that if you made everyone's environment exactly identical, the genetic share would go to 100% — not because genes got stronger but because there'd be nothing left for them to differ against. I don't know if that's a deep point or a trick with words.

So what I want to know is: is there a name for this? Not for the number itself — I know the word heritability, that's not what I'm after — but for the thing that happens when a number that's really about the spread of differences within one particular group of people at one particular time gets picked up and used as a fact about the trait itself, or about what can't be changed. And is there a name for the flip I described, where making environments more alike makes a trait look more genetic? If neither has a name, then tell me which of the existing ideas comes closest and where each one falls short of what I'm describing — the name isn't really the point. I want to be able to say exactly what the mistake is, and also when it isn't a mistake, because surely there are cases where a high heritability really does tell you that changing the environment won't do much. And please don't give me a reading list, I just want the idea.
