I spent thirty years in a physiology lab — frog muscle to start, then a long stretch on vision — and hung it up a couple of years ago. Since then I've been reading alot of philosophy on my own and sitting in on a reading group, so I know just enough to be dangerous, which is why this has been nagging at me.

Last week the group got onto machine consciousness and a friend of mine (computer scientist, very no-nonsense) shut the whole thing down with a line she's clearly fond of: 'consciousness is an illusion anyway.' Heads nodded, we went back to the wine. But the more I chew on it the less I'm sure what she actually said. I'm a vision guy — I spent years on optical illusions, I know exactly what it means for a percept to be illusory. When I try to run that notion on consciousness itself my head spins.

So what I want is a menu, not a lecture. Lay out the seperate precise claims that one sentence could be making, from the boring to the wild, and for each give me a verdict — platitude, real and defensible, or just false. Something like: does she mean only that particular contents of experience can be unreliable (sure, fine, who cares)? Or that introspection systematically misrepresents what's going on, so experience isn't really the way it seems to us from the inside? Or the flat-footed reading that nobody is actually conscious, there's no inner life at all — is that a position anybody definately holds, or is it the strawman the other side always accuses them of? Or maybe it's not about minds at all and is really a claim that our concept of consciousness is a muddle that should be dissolved rather than answered?

And the part I actually care about: which of these readings would license the move she made — using it to say we can stop worrying whether the machine is conscious? It feels like only the flat-footed nobody's-home reading does that work, and that's the one that sounds craziest, so if the sensible reading is a softer one then her one-liner was doing something else entirely and I want to be able to say what.

One more hunch to check while you're at it: I half suspect she's blending two different slogans — the consciousness one and the 'the self is an illusion' one. If those are getting crossed, untangle them for me too.
