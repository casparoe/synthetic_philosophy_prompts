3. He later withdrew that verdict: the theory is difficult to test, but testable.
4. The theory of natural selection is not a universal law of nature but a historical statement about one particular sequence of events; the universal laws it contains are trivial.

6. Organisms are active problem-solvers rather than passive recipients of instruction; the environment does not instruct, it selects.
7. His propensity interpretation of probability: chances are objective, single-case, dispositional properties of a physical set-up, not frequencies and not degrees of belief, and he wanted them so that the world could be genuinely indeterministic and open to novelty.
