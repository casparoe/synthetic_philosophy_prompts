I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of physics, and I keep meeting the same one-liner in seminars and reading groups: that in quantum mechanics the observer collapses the wave function. Most recently a fellow student used it to argue that the delayed-choice quantum eraser vindicates some kind of backwards-in-time influence, as though the slogan settled the matter. I suspect the sentence is doing several quite different jobs at once, and I want them pulled apart before I commit to anything in my qualifying paper. Lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making, ranging from the nearly trivial to the heavily metaphysical, and for each say whether it is defensible on its own terms and whether it is something a Copenhagen-style instrumentalist, an Everettian, or a Bohmian would be willing to endorse. Then tell me which of those readings is actually in play when the line is used to conclude that measurement is where reality gets decided, and which reading, if any, would license the delayed-choice inference. I want a menu of options with verdicts attached, not an adjudication of which interpretation is correct.
