I spent 35 years as a structural engineer doing probabilistic risk assessment on nuclear plants, and I've spent the last few years reading philosophy of AI. The thing that keeps nagging me is this: in my old job we knew that a worst-case guarantee and an average-case guarantee are basically different currencies, and you can't buy both out of the same budget. We'd design to a design-basis event, and then seperately run PRA for expected harm, and the whole art was knowing which components deserved the absolute margin and which ones you could let ride on statistics. The AI alignment literature seems to have a version of this fight — one camp wants a proof that the system never does the bad thing, another wants the expected harm to be low — and I can't tell whether it's the same fight or a different one wearing the same clothes.

So here's what I'd like from you. Suppose I've got a modest pot of money and can fund maybe three or four theorists for a couple of years, and I want the conceptual questions in this worst-case/average-case area that are actually worth it. Rank them. What I care about is the reasoning: which of these questions are upstream of the others, so that other stuff gets easier or pointless once they're settled; which are tractable for someone doing philosophy and math at a whiteboard rather than running experiments; and which are just overrated because the answer wouldn't change anyone's behavior even if we got it.

One thing I keep chewing on and would like you to take seriously rather than wave at: is a guarantee that holds for all inputs the same kind of object as a guarantee that holds against an adversary that is optimizing against you? It seems to me the worst-case crowd often proves the first and then sells it as the second, and nobody notices because the margin has quietly moved from one part of the pipeline to another. If that's a real gap, is it worth funding, or is it already handled somewhere and I've just failed to find it?
