I'm trying to specify what a system that learns what people want from what they do may legitimately infer, and my hand-labelled verdicts on cases don't come from any one rule I can state. When a colleague always takes the stairs, even carrying heavy bags, I take her choices as evidence that she prefers the stairs to the elevator. When a man orders the same dish every visit but insists he has no particular liking for it, I still think his choices reveal a preference he would deny. But when someone drinks a bitter tea he says he dislikes, because he believes it is good for his stomach, I do not infer that he likes the taste; I infer only what he wants given what he believes. When a person with agoraphobia never leaves the house, I resist saying she prefers staying in, even though staying in is what she repeatedly does, and I would call the behavior a symptom rather than a preference. When a colleague attends a weekly gathering out of habit and family expectation, and would rather be asleep, I treat his attendance as weak evidence at best. Two cases I cannot reconcile: a man who says he wants to stop drinking and does not, and a man who hands over his wallet at knifepoint. Both contradict what the person says he wants, and my instinct is that the second tells me nothing about his preferences while the first tells me something important — though by what right, I cannot say. My rough rule has been that a behavior is evidence of a preference when it is the person's own, repeated, and performed with full knowledge of the options, but that rule makes the akratic case and the case of the dish he denies liking come out alike, and I feel they should not.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? State it precisely, say which case motivates each clause, and give its verdicts on cases I have not listed — choices made when the stakes are trivial, choices that reverse when the options are presented in a different order, and choices that change once the person learns more about the options. Tell me which of my verdicts I must give up, and whether the principle is meant as a claim about what people actually want or about what we are entitled to infer.
