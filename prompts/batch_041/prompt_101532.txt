I'm putting together a handout for a grad reading group on animal and AI ethics, and the session is on whether sentience and moral status come in degrees — like, is there a real slope running from an insect down to a thermostat, or is the slope just in our heads, an artefact of how unsure we are?

What I want from you is a taxonomy, as a tree, of the positions on this. Every branch node should be labelled with the question that splits it from its siblings, and every leaf should be a named position (or 'unnamed' if nobody really defends it) plus one sentence on that position's most awkward commitment. The nodes have to be phrased as questions somebody could answer without already knowing which leaf they land on — so no node that just asks 'do you think moral status is graded?'. Use concrete cases to force the answers: a honeybee that can be trained to a colour, a mouse, a clam, a slime mould, an anaesthetised human, a very simple artificial agent with a reward signal, a large language model that says it's suffering, and the thermostat.

I want the following positions to show up as leaves somewhere: a flat threshold view where nothing counts until it counts fully; a smooth scalar view where status is proportional to how much of the relevant thing you have; a threshold-plus-gradient view where crossing the line buys an inviolable floor and only degrees above that get traded off; the view that the gradient is epistemic rather than metaphysical (there's a fact of the matter, we're just uncertain, and partial belief is not partial sentience); the view that status tracks welfare or interests rather than sentience; the view that it tracks agency or self-consciousness rather than feeling; and the attributionist/deflationary option where status is something we assign for our own practical purposes. Anything else the logical space forces should also be in there, and please mark the combinations that come out empty and say briefly whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Two things I especially care about. First, I want the degree-of-property vs degree-of-confidence distinction to be one of the branch nodes, not a footnote. Second, if there's vagueness in whether a thing is conscious, is it then half-conscious, or is that a category error — I want that on the tree too. And flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually seperate; my suspicion is the smooth scalar view and the epistemic view collapse on some of the cases, but tell me if I'm wrong.

Aim for 8 to 12 leaves. It's a handout, so keep each leaf to a sentence or two, no citations needed.
