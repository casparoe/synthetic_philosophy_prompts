I am a novelist, and for three years now I have been writing a historical novel set in the last decades of a Bronze Age palace economy. The book is going badly for a reason I did not anticipate: my problem is no longer what happened but what to call it. I am hoping you can tell me whether the thing I keep bumping into already has a name, or whether I should coin one — and, if I coin one, where its edges are.

The pattern is this. Every account I read tells me that a society collapsed at a particular moment. When I follow the evidence down to the ground, the moment is always the moment at which a certain kind of trace stops: the storerooms are emptied, the palace archives go silent, the script falls out of use, the monumental building halts, the tribute rolls end. The evidence does not show that the people stopped. The villages are still inhabited; the pottery continues with small changes; the fields are still worked. I find the same shape elsewhere. On Easter Island the quarrying and raising of the statues stops, and everything then turns on what became of the population — a question on which the specialists still disagree. In the Classic Maya lowlands the carved monuments cease to be dated and the royal centres empty, while the countryside does not simply empty with them. So the word collapse appears to attach itself to the layer that leaves durable, legible, datable traces — the palace, the state, the monument, the archive — and then to treat the silence of that layer as the silence of the society. What is mourned, when I read closely, is the end of a particular kind of political and economic complexity; what is described is the end of a people.

Is there a name for that slippage? I have half-found candidates, and none of them quite fits. Collapse of complexity seems to name the event rather than the inference. Dark age seems to name a gap in our evidence rather than a change in the world, though it may be closer than I think. Resilience names the opposite outcome and quietly assumes the answer. And decline and fall is a genre, not a concept. What I am after is a term for the specific move — reading the disappearance of a society's self-documenting apparatus as the disappearance of the society — and I want to know how it differs from ordinary survivorship bias, which is about the evidence we happen to keep, and from the substantive claim that a complex society really can lose its complexity.

If no such term exists, please coin one and define it, and tell me what it does and does not cover. I need its boundaries. Does it apply when the population really did fall, as some argue it did on Easter Island? Does it apply when the successor order is poorer and smaller but continuous, as in the Maya case? Does it apply to a society that raised no monuments and kept no archives in the first place, so that there is no layer whose silence could be mistaken for death? I would rather have a sharp term with hard edges, and cases where it clearly fails, than a broad one that flatters every collapse story I might want to tell.
