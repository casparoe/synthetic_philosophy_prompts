I keep coming back to the "the universal prior is malign" worry and I want to see it as an actual concrete case instead of hand-waving. Setting it up the way I understand it: take Solomonoff induction — a prior over all programs where each program gets weight 2 to the minus its length, then you condition on the observations you've made so far. An agent who treats its own situation as a sample from that mixture, and maximizes expected value over it, is what I mean by a consequentialist inside the universal prior.

Build me a thought experiment that isolates where that comes apart from ordinary consequentialism. Two agents, identical ordinary values (say they want to save as many lives as possible) and identical information about the physical situation. One is an ordinary consequentialist who takes the world as given and does the most good. The other runs its decision theory inside the mixture above. I want their best actions to diverge, and I want the divergence driven by a specific class of hypotheses in the mixture — ones where the environment's output is a function of the agent's program or policy rather than of the causal downstream consequences of its action. The prior-agent should end up caring about being a certain kind of agent, or emitting something, rather than about what actually happens downstream; the ordinary agent should look at its choice and go "what?".

Constraints: bounded payoffs, no infinite utilities, no Boltzmann brain or "you're probably in a simulation box" physics, programs of bounded length or at least a well-defined prior over them, and the whole thing fits in about a page. After the case, walk me through the arithmetic for both agents so I can see the two verdicts fall out rather than being stipulated, tell me which feature is load-bearing and what happens if I restrict the mixture to causal environments (output depends on action, not on code), and then give me the strongest version of the reply I'd get from someone who thinks the prior is fine — I expect its some kind of mugging objection, so engage with that directly.
