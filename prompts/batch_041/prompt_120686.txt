Ok so I've been going down a rabbit hole on this illusionism thing about consciousness and I want to turn it into something. As I understand the view (and I have zero philosophy training, so forgive me), the claim isn't "we're all zombies" or "nobody feels anything." It's that the raw-feel part — the inner glow, the thing people mean when they say there's something it's like — is something your brain's self-model says is there and isn't. The model of you comes with a field marked "experience: private, ineffable, not physical," and the model is working perfectly, it's just that nothing in the world answers to that field. The pain report is real. The glow it's reporting on isn't. And you can never catch it from the inside, because the catching is done by the same model.

Here's what I want: a satirical internal deprecation notice, in exactly the style we send at work when we kill an API. The bit is that the team that owns the self-model has announced it's sunsetting phenomenal consciousness in the next major release and everybody downstream has to migrate.

Some things it needs:

- Header stuff: version number, sunset date, owning team, a ticket number, a "impact: all customers, all environments" line.
- A "what's changing" section that's actually correct about the view. What's being removed (the phenomenal properties, the glow, the raw feels). What's staying (the reports, the access, the behaviour, the discrimination — you'll still flinch, you'll still say "ow"). And why it's happening: the field was never populated, downstream systems were reading their own schema and mistaking it for the world.
- A migration table, old phrasing to new phrasing. Pick the ones where the change is actually subtle and not just a find-and-replace.
- A compatibility shim paragraph: first-person reports keep working, they just get parsed as claims about the model rather than about a glow, and callers who depend on the old parsing won't notice anything. Which is the point.
- An FAQ, and this is the part I care about most. It has to include the question a normal person asks immediately, roughly "if it's an illusion, who's being fooled?" and the answer has to be the one a real illusionist would give, not a punchline. Same treatment for "then why does it feel like something?" and "aren't you just renaming the thing and calling it explained?" The answers should be deadpan, defensible, and slightly chilling. Nobody in the document admits the view is silly and nobody winks at the reader.
- A "known issues / won't fix" section for the stuff the team has decided to leave broken forever. That's where the real cost of the view should live. Not "users are confused" — something that actually hurts.
- One quote at the end from a customer who is furious that the migration broke something they cared about.

Around 700 words. No citations, no name-dropping, I don't know the literature and neither do you. Write it like an internal doc for engineers who half-remember the all-hands. Deadpan the whole way. And it has to be funny to someone who actually knows this debate — if the illusionsit reading it gets mad, I've done it wrong; they should recognise their own view and wince a little at the last section. My girlfriend, who has never heard of any of this, should still be able to follow it.
