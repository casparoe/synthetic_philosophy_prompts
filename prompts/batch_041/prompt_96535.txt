I'm teaching a senior seminar in philosophy of mind, mixed phil and cogsci crowd, and I have to write the final this week. The unit on the timing of experience went ok, they read the Dennett stuff about Orwellian vs Stalinesque revisions and the colour phi phenomenon, and I want the essay question to make them actually *do* something with it instead of reciting "no fact of the matter, Cartesian theatre, the end". So my plan is to hand them a short results blurb on the exam sheet and make them referee it. Here's the blurb I've drafted:

"Participants viewed two flashes in succession: a red dot at position A, then 100 ms later a green dot at position B. At the usual spacings, participants reported seeing a single dot travel from A to B, changing colour about halfway along the path. In the probe condition, a small gray ring was flashed at the midpoint of the path 30 ms after the first flash; afterwards participants were asked whether the travelling dot had been red or green at the moment the ring appeared. In the clean condition 85% answered 'red', which lines up with the reported change point. In the load condition participants also performed a concurrent digit-retention task: the motion report was unaffected, but only 55% now answered 'red', and the reported colour change drifted further along the path. The authors conclude that the load selectively impaired retention of the earlier, genuinely experienced red content, and hence that the colour phi phenomenon is Orwellian -- the red experience occurs and is later overwritten -- rather than Stalinesque, with the whole episode assembled and back-dated from the start."

So what I need from you: the essay question itself, a model answer of about 700-750 words, and a marking scheme. The question has to be doable by hand in 45 minutes, and it should force them to (a) say what the two hypotheses actually disagree about, given that the authors themselves aren't claiming any disagreement about what the brain does, (b) assess whether the load result really breaks the tie, and (c) say whether the tie can be broken in principle at all, and what that does to the question "when did she experience the colour change?". 20 marks, five must-hit points in the scheme, marks for each, and for each point a one-line note on the way candidates typically blow it. Please also flag the specific mistakes that should cost marks -- e.g. I keep getting answers that just assert the undecidability verdict without ever touching the data, or that think a better temporal resolution would settle it. The tie cant be broken just by naming Dennett's diagnosis, they have to earn it by working through the load result.
