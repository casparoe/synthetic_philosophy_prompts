Treat the following distinction as a stipulation I am imposing on the discussion, not as a report of any existing taxonomy.

A harm caused by an autonomous system can fail to be attributable to any person in two structurally different ways. There is a *desert gap* when the harm occurs and no human being is an appropriate target of blame for it: the harm was not foreseeable, or no individual's contribution was sufficient, or the behaviour was not under anyone's control at the moment that mattered. There is a *standing gap* when some human being does meet the conditions for being an appropriate target of blame, but no one is in a position to hold her to account — because those who would do the holding are implicated in the same chain of decisions, benefit from the same arrangement, or would have acted as she did.

Argue for the following position: the cases that matter are standing gaps rather than desert gaps, and recognising this relocates the problem. Remedies aimed at the desert gap — a human kept in the loop, no-fault liability for the operator, stricter certification of the system before deployment — presuppose an accountability relation that the standing gap shows to be absent, and so cannot close it. What is needed is a way of restoring accountability that does not depend on first locating a blameless party to bear it.

In the course of the argument:

1. Construct one concrete setting — a hospital ward, a trading desk, a content-moderation pipeline, a delivery fleet, or the like — in which the two gaps come apart in the direction your thesis needs, and demonstrate the divergence rather than asserting it.
2. Explain why the standing gap is not simply the desert gap seen from further away: say precisely what is missing, and why more evidence or more patient causal tracing would not supply it.
3. State the strongest objection to your position — for instance, that standing is a matter of interpersonal morality and cannot bear the weight of an institutional conclusion — and answer it.
4. Say what would count against the position: what would have to be true of an actual case for the desert gap, rather than the standing gap, to be the one that matters.

Argue from the structure of the cases themselves. Do not appeal to particular statutes, court decisions, or named theories, and do not attempt to survey a literature.

End with a single line of the form \answer{...} containing, in no more than fifteen words, the position you have defended.
