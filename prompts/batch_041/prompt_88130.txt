I'm giving a paper at our department's ethics-and-policy workshop next Thursday and I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a pep talk. Here's what I'm defending, and then who'll be in the room.

The paper is about whether pleasure and pain are commensurable, and my move is that the whole debate conflates two seperate questions. Question one: can you put pleasures and pains on a single cardinal scale at all, i.e. assign each episode a real-valued magnitude? Question two: even granting that, is there a fixed exchange rate at which some quantity of pleasure balances some quantity of pain? I say yes to the first and no to the second. So I'm a commensurabilist about magnitudes and a lexical-threshold person about exchange: below a certain severity of suffering, finite pleasure can outweigh a finite pain, but above that severity no amount of pleasure buys it off. The wrinkle that people trip on is that I think the threshold is agent-relative -- it's indexed to the individual's own hedonic range, their baseline and their ceiling, not a single number you can read off a scale and apply across people or across species. I get that this makes it sound like I'm sneaking relativism in the back door, and I have a story for why it isn't, but it's the soft spot and I know it.

The room: mostly normative ethicists, several of them Kantians who think welfare-talk about persons is close to a category error, and one Aristotelian who's convinced "pleasure" as a single measurable quantity is a fiction cooked up by economists. There's one decision theorist who will go straight for the cardinal-scale claim and won't let me wave at psychophysics. There's a health economist who builds QALYs and DALYs for a living and will want to know what happens to my threshold at the level of measurement and what it does to willingness-to-pay, which she'll point out is supposed to be continuous. And there's a grad student who works on invertebrate sentience and will ask, not unfairly, how you even locate the threshold for a shrimp.

Give me the prep sheet. The eight or so objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that particular person would phrase it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is thin. Tell me what I should concede up front so I'm not fighting on ground I don't need. Tell me which single objection, if I lose it, sinks the paper. And include the question I'm praying nobody asks -- you know the one.
