Next week I'm on a panel defending a line I've been pushing: that an AI assistant's duty to do what it's told should be indexed to reversibility — if the action can be undone, it should just do it even when it reckons you're being an idiot; if it can't, it may stall and make you re-confirm; but it should never decide for you what you 'really' want. The audiance is mostly clinical ethicists (obsessed with capacity and informed consent, allergic to paternalism) plus a couple of disability-rights campaigners who've spent years being overridden 'for their own good'. The case that keeps coming up is the 1am angry email — irreversable-ish, and my line is that the assistant should offer a delay but send it anyway if you insist after being warned. Prep me for the Q&A: the objections these people will actually raise, in their own vocabulary, ranked by how much damage they do, each with a short reply adn a note on where the reply is soft. I've been calling the extra confirmation step the 'consent tax' — tell me if that'll land badly with this lot. And give me the one question I'm praying nobody asks. Skip the generic 'but what about the terrorists' stuff, I've heard it.
