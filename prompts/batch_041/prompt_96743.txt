I'm putting my position together for a grad seminar and I want a consistency check before I say any of it out loud. So for this one, be a philosopher of moral epistemology -- someone who really knows the testimony literature -- and actually think it through instead of pattern-matching to the usual debate. Start by spelling out what each commitment below commits me to, then audit.

Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Moral realism. There are mind-independent moral facts and at least some of them are knowable.
(2) Testimony is a basic source of knowledge, not a second-class one. I can know the bridge is safe because a reliable engineer told me so, without checking the calculations myself, and I don't need independent evidence for every bit of testimony I accept.
(3) But you can't get moral knowledge from moral testimony. If I believe X is wrong only because someone I trust says so, and I can't see the reasons for myself, then at best I have a true belief. I don't know it, and I can't hand it on to anyone else as knowledge.
(4) Testimony about the non-moral facts is a different story. If someone tells me a practice is cruel, or that a policy will leave people destitute, I can come to know that it's wrong, because the cruelty and the destitution are things I can be told and the moral principle is mine.
(5) Deferring is often the right thing and not a defect. I've sat on an ethics committee and gone along with the clinical ethicist's read on a hard case, and I think that was correct -- a novice who does what a wiser person says isn't doing anything wrong, thats humility. Most of us inherited our moral views wholesale from our upbringing anyway, and I don't think most people are epistemic failures because of it.
(6) What has moral worth is understanding. Acting on reasons you've taken on loan isn't acting for the right reasons, which is why (3) matters to me in the first place.

I don't know which of these are actually pulling against each other, and I'm not looking for advice on which one to give up -- I want to know which pairs genuinely can't both stay, which ones only look like they clash, and what the cheapest repair is for each real one.

Items 1-3: the real tensions, worst first, each saying which commitment has to bend and what that costs. Item 4: the pair or pairs that look like tensions but dissolve, and why. Item 5: the single revision that would clean up the most, and what it leaves unexplained.
