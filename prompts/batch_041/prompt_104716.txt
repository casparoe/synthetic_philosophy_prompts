I'm putting together the midterm for a 300-level course I'm TAing on Aristotle's ethics, and the unit on money and exchange has turned into a mess — half the students think Aristotle is doing a proto-just-price theory and the other half think he's just doing sociology of the agora, and neither group can say anything about why he's so hostile to interest. What I want for the exam is one essay question, answerable in about 50 minutes, that forces them to actually connect the Nicomachean Ethics account of exchange to the Politics material on acquisition, rather than paraphrasing either one. The hook I want it built around is this: in NE V.5 Aristotle says 'demand has come to be conventionally represented by money; this is why money is called nomisma (customary currency), because it does not exist by nature but by custom (nomos), and can be altered and rendered useless at will' — and yet in the Politics he treats lending at interest as the most unnatural form of acquisition, on the grounds that money is for exchange and interest makes money breed from money. So if the token is pure nomos and can be voided at will, where does the naturalness of money's function come from? That's the crux I want the question to land on, and I'd also like the commensurabilty problem dragged in — his own admission that things so different can't be strictly commensurable, so that demand furnishes only a 'sufficiently accurate' practical measure. I don't want a question that can be answered by reciting the three kinds of exchange or the household/chrematistike distinction as labels; they have to say what work 'nature' is doing in the argument, and whether the commensurability problem undermines or actually supports the usury verdict.

So please write: (1) the exam question itself, with a short rubric note saying what it's testing; (2) a model answer of roughly 600–700 words, the kind of thing a strong student would produce in 50 minutes, not a polished paper; (3) a marking scheme out of 25, broken into must-hit points with marks attached, plus a short list of the specific moves that separate an A from a B (e.g. whether they can say what would have to be true of demand for money to be more than a convention), and a one-line note for each on how students typically fluff it. Assume they've read NE V and Politics I and have the texts in front of them, so no need for the model answer to quote anything beyond the passage I gave; and please don't let it smuggle in modern marginalist or Marxist readings — I want it to stay inside Aristotle's own vocabulary (chreia, nomos, ergon, oikonomia) as far as possible, and if it has to step outside that vocabulary, say so explicitly.
