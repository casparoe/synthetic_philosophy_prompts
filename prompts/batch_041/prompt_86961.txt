I sit on my city's planning commission, so I spend two evenings a month watching people fight about words, and there's one pattern I can't find a name for.

Our code lets the commission act on 'blighted' property, and in hearings the word does two jobs I can't get it to do one at a time. Ask someone what makes a block blighted and you get a description: boarded windows, code violations, delinquent taxes. Ask them why a block shouldn't have boarded windows and you get a value claim: nobody should have to live next to that. Press on the description and they'll answer with the value; press on the value and they retreat to the description. And that isn't sloppiness, the argument needs it. Stated as pure description -- this parcel generates forty thousand dollars in tax revenue -- there's no reason to do anything about it. Stated as pure value -- this parcel ought to be generating more -- they've just asserted the conclusion. The word only works while it stays between the two.

Same shape with 'underutilized' (a parking lot is underutilized compared to what?) and 'highest and best use', which shows up in every consultant report we get. And the neighbors do it just as hard -- 'neighborhood character', 'overdevelopment' -- so it isn't one side being sneaky. Somebody at a hearing told me it's just the naturalistic fallacy, but that seems to be about inferring an ought from an is, and here nothing is being inferred; the word is somehow both at once. Someone else said 'loaded language', but that's for dressing up a claim you could state plainly, and I don't think you can state these plainly.

So: does this have a name, or a literature? I'd rather have the carving then the label. What is actually going on when a word has to stay ambiguous in order to do its work? How is it different from the naturalistic fallacy and from loaded language? And is it ever legitimate -- my hunch is that some of these words are doing honest work and some are begging the question, and I can't tell you what seperates them, which is the part I actually care about.
