So I got hit by a car in March, I'm in a chair now, and the whole thing has turned me into someone who reads political philosophy at 2am instead of sleeping. I want to know whether my gut reactions add up to a view about equality of what — welfare, resources, capabilities, whatever — because right now they seem to point in different directions and its driving me nuts.

Here's where I land. My neighbour and I make about the same; she walks, I need something like $30k a year for aides, a chair, a modified van. Equal incomes, and I'm definately worse off — that feels like a real inequality, not just bad luck. Someone told me the technical name for this is that we have different 'conversion factors' for turning money into a life; is that the right frame or a red herring? Then there's a woman in my support group who's been blind since birth and genuinely reports being as happy as anyone; I still think something's been taken from her even though her welfare looks fine. But when a guy tells me he's miserable because he can't afford to keep his boat, I don't feel like the rest of us owe him a boat. And a friend who's broke and happy on cheap hobbies — nothing unfair there as far as I can see. The one that really gets me: I'm pretty happy, honestly, maybe more than before the accident, but I'm stuck in my flat because the tube isn't accessible. The stuckness is the injustice there, not my mood. Except some days the pain itself is the bad part, and no ramp fixes that.

So: is there one principle — welfare, resources, capabilities, or a hybrid — that gives all of these verdicts, or do I have to drop one? And what does it say about a case I haven't thought of, like someone with constant pain who says they're thriving? Spell the principle out, don't just name it, and tell me which of my verdicts it can't keep.
