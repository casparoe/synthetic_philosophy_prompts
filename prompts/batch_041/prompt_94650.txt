I'm on a search committee that's redesigning our history-of-philosophy core, and I keep going in circles on the Frege thing — the diary he kept late in life, with the ugly stuff about Jews and democracy in it. People trot it out in two opposite directions: either it's irrelevant because logic is logic, or it's the smoking gun proving his whole Platonist project was authoritarian from the start. Neither feels right to me, and I suspect both are dodging the actual hard question. So: if you had to sort out which open questions here genuinely matter most — the ones that would change how we read the Foundations of Arithmetic or teach "Sense and Reference" if we settled them — how would you rank them, and why? I want an ordering with reasons, not a survey. For each one, tell me whether it's upstream of the others, whether it's tractable for an ordinary philosopher who can't read German and has no archive access, and what would move it up or down your list. And a colleague keeps insisting the honest move is to treat a philosopher's work like a tool — the tool works or it doesn't, regardless of the guy who made it — and that everything else is just virtue-signaling. Is that one of the top questions or a distraction? Work it into the ranking. Keep it under 600 words, lead with your number one, and please actually commit to an order instead of hedging every step.
