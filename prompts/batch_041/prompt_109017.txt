I'm moderating the Q&A after our department's grad conference keynote, and the speaker has a line on the teletransporter that I'd like to see her have to work for in public. She agrees with Parfit that personal identity isn't what matters, but she thinks he was too quick to let the teletransporter off the hook: on her account what matters is the continuation of the very same causal process that's been generating your mental life, so the person assembled in the receiving booth from the scan is a duplicate who happens to remember being her, not her. She'll happily say you survive gradual neuron-by-neuron replacement, and she thinks the degree to which something matters to you comes in degrees, not all-or-nothing.

Draft eight or nine questions, each with a short follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give. Its a casual keynote Q&A so keep them askable out loud, but I want at least one question each on: (1) what the causal-continuity constraint is actually doing -- if the causal chain runs through the radio signal in the teletransporter case, why doesn't it run through the blueprint in the vat case, and what does she say about the version where the scanner fails to destroy you and you end up sitting in a room arguing with your own replica for an hour; (2) the division case, where the process forks into two -- if what matters comes in degrees, was that one loss of what matters or two, and can she make the arithmetic come out; (3) the self-concern objection -- why is it rational to care more about a future person just because the connection to her is causal and continuous, once identity has been taken off the table. Close with one question about what would actually change her mind.
