I have just joined the panel that reviews benefit sanctions in my region, and the chair has asked me to draft the reasoning for next week's case in a way that stays faithful to our own record. Four past decisions, in brief: a claimant who missed a mandatory interview because the bus she depends on was cancelled and who telephoned the same day was not sanctioned; a claimant who missed two interviews in the fortnight after his mother died and did not answer our letters was sanctioned; a claimant who attended everything and made the required number of job applications, but applied only for posts he was plainly unqualified for and told his adviser he was going through the motions, was sanctioned; and a claimant whose medical certificate we accepted as genuine, but who had told his adviser he would not take a job while the benefit paid as much as it did, was sanctioned.

Next week's case is a woman whose employer has unilaterally cut her hours, so that her earnings now fall below the threshold we have recently begun using to decide whether someone is moving towards work. She has missed nothing, continues to apply for additional work, and her adviser reports that she does everything asked of her. Tell me how the panel will rule if it stays consistent with itself, which rules its record is compatible with — I suspect there is more than one — what each of those rules predicts here, which I should bet on and why, which of the four decisions is the closest precedent, and what single further case would force the panel to choose between the competing rules. If the record genuinely does not settle it, say so, and tell me what we would be committing ourselves to either way.
