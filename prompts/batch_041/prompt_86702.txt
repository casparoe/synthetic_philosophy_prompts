Write me a sustained deadpan satire: an excerpt from the employee handbook of a fictional mid-sized company (invent a boring name — Northwind Regional Logistics, Meridian Facilities Group, whatever) that has recently decided to stop treating "going above and beyond" as optional. The whole gag is that the company has tried to make supererogation mandatory, and the handbook has no idea that this is self-defeating. Voice: HR, cheerful, procedural, no winking, no nudging the reader. The document should never once admit the joke. Around 700 words, laid out as numbered clauses or handbook sections.

Stuff I want in there, roughly:
- a welcome note from the CEO announcing the new Moral Excellence Framework
- a table of the three performance tiers and what happens to you if you land in tier one
- an FAQ entry answering "Am I really expected to run into the burning building?" with a carefully hedged yes
- the Hero of the Quarter award, now carrying a clause that makes accepting it compulsory
- a separate grievance procedure for employees who believe they've performed a supererogatory act, in which the burden falls on them to prove the act wasn't required of them — which, given the rest of the document, they can never do
- a short boxed note from the in-house ethicist, a genuine demandingness maximalist who thinks this is all excellent news, plus one dissenting line from the union rep buried in the footnotes
- a sidebar on the "Saints Track" promotion ladder, definately distinct from the Heroes Track

The humor has to come from the actual structure of the concept, so someone who's read the supererogation literature laughs rather than winces. In particular I want the document to keep bumping into the fact that praise seems to presuppose you could have done less, and then to keep solving that problem the wrong way: raise the bar, reward people for clearing it, notice nobody's clearing it, raise it again. Somewhere in there the ethicist should cheerfully concede that no employee has performed a supererogatory act at this company since 2019, and treat that as a success.

Don't mention any real philosophers or papers. Keep it legible to someone who's never heard the word supererogation, but don't stop to explain the word either.
