I teach an upper-level seminar on rationality and choice and I am drafting the final exam. I want a single essay question on the sunk cost fallacy that cannot be answered by reciting that sunk costs are irrelevant to rational choice, nor by summarizing the standard experiments. To force genuine reasoning rather than recall, the question should introduce a term the students must apply: a cost-contingent continuation policy, meaning any rule that makes continuing with a project conditional on how much has already been spent on it (for example, keep funding as long as cumulative spending exceeds some threshold). The question should ask students to separate three things: (a) cost-contingent continuation that is simply the fallacy of treating the past expenditure as itself a reason to continue; (b) cost-contingent continuation that is rational because the policy was chosen in advance as a commitment device that changes the agent's own later incentives or those of other parties; and (c) cost-contingent continuation that is rational as a heuristic when the agent is uncertain about the structure of the decision problem. It should require them to state what would have to be true for (b) or (c) to be genuine rather than a rationalization, and to construct a case in which a cost-contingent policy is rational even though the past expenditure is, strictly, causally and informationally irrelevant to the future payoffs. Please write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that distinguishes an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain and the specific mistakes that should cost marks.
