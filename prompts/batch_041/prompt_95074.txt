Write a poem for a philosophy of mind seminar on personal identity and mind uploading. The poem should be about the gradual replacement thought experiment—the case in which a person's mind is uploaded by replacing neurons one by one with functionally identical silicon chips—but it must not mention Theseus, his ship, or any named theory. Instead, the form itself should enact the replacement. Use a sestina if you can, or a form of your own devising that achieves the same effect: begin with a set of six recurring end-words that stand for the matter of the self (for example, memory, intention, perception, mood, habit, and voice), and then, as the poem progresses, replace each end-word with a functionally equivalent but different word, so that by the final stanza the poem is built from entirely new words while preserving the original pattern. The reader should feel both the continuity of the pattern and the strangeness of the substitution, and the poem should leave open whether the person survives. It should be accurate enough that someone who knows the debate recognizes the positions at play—without the poem ever stating them—and good enough that someone who doesn't knows it is about a self coming apart and holding together at once. Write in American English.
