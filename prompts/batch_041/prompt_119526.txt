The same university that will tell you, if you push it, that 'generations' are a marketing fiction will turn around and design its orientation week around what Gen Z supposedly needs, and I dont know whether that's incoherent or just pragmatic. So take the practice -- sorting people into birth cohorts and then explaining their behaviour by which cohort they're in -- and tell me what it presupposes about kinds. Does it need cohorts to be real kinds, with shared traits that the birth year somehow stamped in? Or is it satisfied with something weaker: arbitrary labels laid over a continuous age gradient, or a story on which the thing doing the real explanatory work is the historical period everyone lived through, not the year you were born? When someone says Gen Z is more anxious than Millennials, what would have to be true for that to be a fact about the generation rather than about being twenty-two, or about what the 2010s were like, and does the practice of talking this way quietly rule those readings out? And where is it underdetermined -- could I keep doing exactly what I do now while believing cohorts are pure convention? Reconstruct the implicit theory, tell me which parts the practice actually needs and which it merely fits well with, and say what would change if the whole generational pattern turned out to be a period effect.
