Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between an etiological (selected-effects) theorist of biological function and a causal-role (systemic) theorist. The collaboration is organized around a synthetic-biology case: a laboratory lineage of bacteria in which a newly introduced metabolic cycle is at first retained only because it buffers a toxic intermediate, and after many generations of laboratory evolution it becomes required for cell division, although it was never selected for in the wild and has no natural evolutionary history. The statement should contain a numbered agreed-claims section, covering what both sides will say about function versus mere effect, malfunction, novelty, and the explanatory aims of function talk; a disagreements table in which each side's position is phrased so that side would accept the wording; and the cruxes, including whether function ascriptions attach to trait types or tokens, whether a selection history is necessary for a function, and whether present causal contribution is sufficient. For each crux, identify a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record what each side predicts about that case. If any part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why. No citations or reading lists.
