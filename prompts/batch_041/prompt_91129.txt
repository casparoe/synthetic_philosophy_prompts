Give me an example of a case where Jeffrey conditionalization and ordinary conditionalization on the underlying observation come apart in a way that actually matters — not just a relabeling of the same math.

Some context on why I care. A friend of mine has been arguing that "uncertain evidence" is a fake category. His line is that whenever you're tempted to say "I didn't learn that the sweater is green, I only learned that it probably is, so I need to shift my credence in green by some amount and hold the conditional probabilities rigid," there's definately a finer event you actually observed — the candlelit retinal image, the doctor's exact words, the feeling of the fabric — and if you condition on that instead you get the same answer or a better one. So Jeffrey conditionalization is either redundant or wrong. I mostly buy it, but it feels like there should be cases where the observation is tangled up with the hypothesis in a way that blocks the trick. The example I keep circling back to is me stumbling around at night trying to figure out whether I'm wearing my glasses: the bluriness is evidence for "no glasses," but conditional on the glasses being on, that same bluriness is evidence about the room. Something similar seems to happen when the reliability of the evidence source depends on which hypothesis is true, or when the "evidence" is partly about my own mental state.

What I want from you: one concrete example, ideally with actual numbers in a small probability table, where the choice between (a) Jeffrey conditionalizing on the uncertain evidence and (b) conditioning on the finer observation event either gives different credences, or where the finer event just isn't available in any non-cheating way. Bonus if you can say something about two uncertain updates in a row and whether the order matters — I have a vague memory that they commute only in special cases, and I'd like to know if that's the kind of thing that can get you Dutch-booked.

Lead with the example in your first sentence — just the case itself, no preamble and no restating of my question — then give the numbers and the reasoning.
