I teach philosophy of science, and 'not even wrong' has become a small scourge of my working life: colleagues now use it to dispatch string theory and the multiverse in a single breath, though no two of them mean quite the same thing by it. In one recent exchange it was made to say that string theory yields no predictions whatsoever; in another, that it is not even a well-defined theory; in a third, merely that it is not worth a serious physicist's time. I would like the phrase taken apart rather than brandished. Set out the distinct precise claims 'not even wrong' can be making about a theory of this kind, ordered from the nearly definitional or trivially true, through the substantive and genuinely contestable, to the probably false. For each reading, say what would have to hold for the charge to be warranted, whether it would sustain the further conclusion that the theory cannot be confirmed by any means at all -- including by the non-empirical considerations, such as unification or explanatory scope, on which its defenders rely -- and which of the three uses above it most plausibly underwrites. Give me a menu of readings, each with a verdict, rather than an essay in defense of one.
