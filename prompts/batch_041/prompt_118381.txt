I'm a novelist, about two-thirds through a book set in a housing co-op — fourteen adults in a converted motel, shared kitchen, one washer that eats socks — and the whole plot turns on the fact that their bylaws can be amended by the members, including the rules about how you amend the rules. I've been reading about self-amending rule systems for a month now and I keep hitting the same wall: the paradoxes are gorgeous on the page in a logic paper and dead on arrival in fiction, because the second a character says "ah but that rule refers to itself," the scene turns into a lecture.

So: write me a short story, 1500-2200 words, that stages at least three of the following and never explains them out loud. Let the reader feel the vertigo first and work out why after.

- a rule that lowers the bar for future amendments, and is then amended using the lower bar it just created, so nobody can say whether the change was legal
- an entrenched rule that says certain rules cant be touched, repealed through the very procedure it forbids
- a rule that applies to every rule in the book, which therefore applies to itself, and is self-undermining (or self-validating) as a result
- the bootstrap problem at the founding: how did the very first rule get adopted, if the rule for adopting rules arrived later
- two amendment procedures that each claim the final say, where each one's claim is valid under itself and void under the other
- a rule declaring the interpreter's reading of the bylaws final, sitting alongside a rule saying no interpretation may be immune from review
- a rule whose content is a welfare test — amendments count only if they leave the members better off — plus the fight over whether the test is applied before or after the amendment takes effect

Somewhere in there I want one rule printed in the story word for word, so a careful reader could stop and go "hang on." And I want one scene where two people are both right on the paperwork — not one sensible person versus one crank, but two defensible readings of the same sentence, with something real riding on it (a marriage, a job, a kid, somebody's dog). No lawyers, no courtroom; these are amateurs with day jobs and a shared laundry room. And please don't end by settling which reading was correct. I want the story to end with the rulebook still winning.
