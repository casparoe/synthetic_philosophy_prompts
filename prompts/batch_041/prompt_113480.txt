I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I want to commission a joke rather than a paper. The audience is people who have spent an unreasonable amount of time on a particular trade-off, so let me state the trade-off explicitly instead of assuming you know which version I have in mind.

The case: a chooser must pick between two outcomes. Option one is a single person being tortured for fifty years. Option two is a staggering number of people — 3^^^3 in Knuth's up-arrow notation, a power tower of roughly 7.6 trillion threes — each getting a single dust speck in the eye, for a moment, with no lasting harm. Some people say they would take the specks at any N, however large, because no pile of trivial annoyances can be weighed against a lifetime of agony. Others say that once N is large enough the arithmetic has to win, and that a refusal that survives every possible N isn't moral seriousness but a refusal to count. What I find interesting is that the disagreement isn't really about how big the numbers are: the refusers typically refuse at every N, which is a claim about whether harms of different kinds can be added up at all.

Write one joke that turns on that disagreement. The punchline has to come out of the actual structure of the case — what it is to aggregate harms that are individually beneath notice, or what it is to refuse to aggregate them — not out of generic material about utilitarians being monsters or philosophers being out of touch; someone who knows the case well should find it funny for the right reason. Set the joke where this kind of trade-off actually gets made, casually, by someone with no philosophical vocabulary — a support desk, a product team, an insurance office, a city agency; pick one, make it concrete, and keep the whole thing under 150 words so I can tell it out loud. Don't let anyone in the joke say "utilitarian" or "the greater good"; the humor has to come from the situation itself.

Give me one joke, not three, and commit to it: no menu of options, no hedging about which version is funnier. Afterward, in two or three sentences, tell me where the joke takes liberties with the case or leans on a contested reading of it, so I know which side of the disagreement I'm laughing at.
