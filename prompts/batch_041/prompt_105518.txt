Bostrom's simulation argument is commonly summarized as showing that we are probably living in a simulation. If that is right, the far future that longtermists care about — vast numbers of lives spread across cosmic timescales — would itself most likely be simulated rather than real, and the astronomically large stakes that are supposed to license acting on tiny probabilities would attach to a simulated world rather than to the actual one. Doesn't the simulation argument therefore substantially dampen fanaticism about the far future? I would like to know whether that conclusion actually follows, or whether I have misunderstood what the argument establishes and what would follow from it.
