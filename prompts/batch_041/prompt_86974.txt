A colleague of mine works on distributive justice, and we have been working through pairs of distributions over two groups of equal size. On a common scale of well-being, her verdicts so far are these:

- both groups at 50, versus one group at 50 and the other at 90: the second is better, and she offers this as her reason for rejecting egalitarianism;
- 10 and 50, versus 11 and 50: the second is better;
- 10 and 50, versus 10 and 60: the second is better;
- 10 and 50, versus 14 and 45: the second is better;
- 30 and 60, versus 40 and 50: the second is better.

She calls her position a priority view rather than an egalitarian one. The case she must now rule on is 20 and 45, versus 21 and 30: the worse-off group gains a single unit, and the better-off group loses fifteen.

I am less interested in the verdict than in the rules that fit her five verdicts equally well, what each of those rules implies about the new case, which rule you would bet she is running and why, and which further case would separate the surviving rules most cleanly.
