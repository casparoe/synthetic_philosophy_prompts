Imagine you are grading a final exam in a mid-level seminar on probability and evidence. The exam question and a student's answer appear below. Grade the answer and provide the specific comments that justify the grade. The student is generally strong, so be careful to separate what is correct from what is subtly wrong; a general impression should not substitute for a point-by-point assessment.

The question: Explain the difference between strict conditionalization and Jeffrey conditionalization. What is the role of the evidential partition, and what constraints must it satisfy? Then consider an agent who receives a reading from an instrument that she regards as 80 percent reliable. She does not learn the reading with certainty. Does Jeffrey conditionalization apply to her situation? If so, how should she update? If not, why not?

The student's answer: Strict conditionalization updates a prior probability P to P_new(A) = P(A | E) when E is learned with certainty. Jeffrey conditionalization generalizes this to cases where the agent does not learn any proposition with certainty but instead acquires new probabilities for the members of an evidential partition. If the partition is {E_i}, the rule is P_new(A) = Σ_i P_old(A | E_i) P_new(E_i). The P_new(E_i) are the agent's new credences in the partition cells after the experience. The rule is sometimes called probability kinematics because it holds the conditional probabilities P_old(A | E_i) fixed while the probabilities of the partition cells change. It reduces to strict conditionalization when P_new(E_j) = 1 for some j. The evidential partition must consist of mutually exclusive and exhaustive propositions, and its members must also be probabilistically independent of A; otherwise the weighted average would double-count or omit possibilities.

In the instrument case, the possible readings form an evidential partition. Since the instrument is 80 percent reliable, the agent's new credence that the reading is correct is 0.8, and her new credence that it is incorrect is 0.2. These are the P_new(E_i). She should therefore conditionalize on each possible reading and then average the results using these new credences. She must not conditionalize on the proposition that the instrument is 80 percent reliable, because that proposition is part of her background knowledge and stays fixed. The case shows why Jeffrey conditionalization is more general than strict conditionalization: it can handle uncertain evidence, whereas strict conditionalization cannot. One limitation is that the rule requires definite new probabilities for the partition cells; if the agent has only an interval or is unsure how confident to be, the rule does not directly apply.

Assign a letter grade and give the comments that justify it, identifying any errors and explaining why they matter.
