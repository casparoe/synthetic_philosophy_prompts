Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps that have argued past each other for decades over whether objective chance has any distinctive authority over rational credence.

The deferrers hold that chance is by its very nature a guide to credence: if an agent knows that the objective chance of A is x, and the remainder of her evidence is admissible with respect to A — that is, it tells her nothing about A over and above the chance hypothesis itself — then her credence in A must be x. On this view the chance-credence link, of which the Principal Principle is the canonical statement, is constitutive of the concept of chance rather than a defeasible rule of thumb, and a credence that departs from a known chance is irrational even when the agent's other evidence supports it.

The skeptics hold that chance facts are ordinary facts about the world's patterns, and that a pattern's being chancy confers no authority on it over credence. Credence answers to the agent's total evidence and to the aim of accuracy; chance enters as an exceptionally good indicator of what the evidence supports, and nothing more. On this view the chance-credence link is a derived and defeasible regularity at best, and there are possible cases in which an agent who knows the chance rationally declines to match it.

The document must also treat two items of shared agenda, on which neither camp has a stock answer.

The Two Levels case. The history of a world admits of a fine-grained and of a coarse-grained description. At each level there is a function that satisfies the platitudes for objective chance at that level: it is non-epistemic; it is the probability function of the best explanation of the regularities described at that level; it constrains the long-run frequencies of the events described at that level; and it is the sort of thing an agent would reasonably treat as a guide to credences about those events. A proposition H is expressible at both levels. An agent whose evidence carries no information about the world's history at either level knows that the fine-grained chance of H is 0.5 and that the coarse-grained chance of H is 0.9. No camp may dismiss the case as incoherent by fiat; a camp that takes it to be impossible must say which stipulation fails and why.

The principle LRD. For any level, any proposition H expressible at that level, and any agent who knows the chance of H at that level to be x and whose other evidence is admissible with respect to H: the agent's credence in H must be x.

The document should contain, in this order: a numbered section of claims both camps can sign; a disagreements table, one row per surviving dispute, giving each camp's own formulation of its position in wording that camp would accept and each camp's formulation of what it takes the other side to be denying; a section giving each camp's verdict on the Two Levels case and on LRD as stated, including whether the camp accepts LRD, the least amendment it would make if it does not, what it says the agent's credence in H should be, and on what grounds; a crux section giving, for each crux, the sub-question at issue, the argument, case, or formal result that both camps agree bears on it, and each camp's prediction about how it comes out, recorded in advance; and a closing section on which disputes, if any, are merely verbal, and on what would actually move each camp. State the camps' positions in their own terms rather than by reference to named philosophers, and do not pad the agreed-claims section with formulations that a camp could sign only because they are vacuous.
