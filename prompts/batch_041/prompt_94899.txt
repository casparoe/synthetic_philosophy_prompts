I've talked myself into a fairly specific reading of Wittgenstein's notes on certainty and I want to know how exposed it is. The reading: the certainties that make doubt possible in the first place aren't beliefs and aren't knowledge -- they're more like a standing way of acting, habits, a background that shows itself in what we do rather than in anything we hold. So the Moore-style ones (there's a hand here, the world wasn't created five minutes ago with all our memories intact) aren't items in the belief box at all. And the further claim I want to bolt on: on this reading, someone who doubts a hinge isn't making an epistemic mistake, they're making a category mistake -- the doubt doesn't get a foothold in the practice, so it isn't even false, it just has no place to stand. I like it because it dissolves the skeptic instead of refuting him.

What would count against this? I want the honest map, not the standard defensive moves. In particular:

- What would actually show that hinges are beliefs, or knowledge, after all? I keep hearing they must be propositional because they can be negated and embedded in conditionals, but I don't know how much that carries, and I'd like to know if there's a cheaper version of the objection that does more work.
- Does anything show hinge-certainty can be rationally revised in a way that's more than conversion? A physicist who slowly comes to see a whole framework was a mistake, someone argued out of a certainty over years -- if that happens, is the reading dead, or can it absorb the case without cheating?
- I made up a distinction myself and want it stress-tested: global hinges (the past is determinate, other minds, memory is broadly reliable) versus local hinges belonging to one practice (this instrument is calibrated, this is how you count these things). I've been assuming only the global ones are non-epistemic in the strong sense. Is that doing any work, or does it collapse -- either because the local ones turn out to be just as non-negotiable, or because the global ones turn out to be answerable to evidence?
- The thing that actually worries me: hinges get taught. Kids are raised into certainties, and some of those certainties are terrible -- whole communities where a conspiracy story or a suspicion of outsiders functions exactly like a hinge. If hinges can be better and worse, and if raising someone into one is a kind of rational influence, doesn't that mean hinges are answerable to somthing, which would break the non-epistemic reading? Or can I say "worse hinge" without conceding it's a belief that could be assessed?

Then sort the defeaters: which would a defender of this reading themselves concede actually counts against them, which would they wave away, and which if any would be fatal. And the meta-question: has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability -- is there anything that couldn't be described as "that's just a different practice"? If so, is that a problem for a view of this kind, or is the demand for falsifiability the wrong demand here? And if the reading turns out to be well insulated, tell me what the rival reading (hinges are just very deeply held beliefs, with all the usual epistemic machinery attached) does better, and what I'd be giving up by staying where I am.
