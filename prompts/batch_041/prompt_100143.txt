Put on your philosopher-comedian hat for this one, and think carefully before you write — brainstorm a few setups silently first, then write up the best one.

I want a joke about the wrong kind of reasons problem, and the punchline has to actually turn on the content rather than just gesture at it. The content being: some consideration can give you a reason to have an attitude — a belief, an admiration, a fear, a laugh — that makes the attitude good for you to be in, without being the kind of reason that makes the attitude correct or fitting, and it gets especially juicy when the consideration would stop working the moment you took it as your reason. So, for instance: a company rolls out a 'sincerity bonus' — five hundred bucks if your admiration for the CEO is judged genuine on the polygraph — and the HR memo has to explain that the money can't be why you admire him, on pain of disqualification. Or an insurer who only pays out if the asteroid really does hit, so now you have a reason to believe it's coming, but not the kind of reason that could make it true. Pick one of those, or something better, but keep it that concrete, and write the bit: setup plus punchline. A couple of lines of dialogue is fine if the joke needs it. Someone who's actually read the literature should laugh rather than wince, and dont let it collapse into 'haha threats aren't evidence' — the good stuff is in the asymmetry between what makes an attitude correct and what makes it pay.
