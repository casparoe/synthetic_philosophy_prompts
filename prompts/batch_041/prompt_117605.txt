Write me an essay prompt on whether generations are real kinds. Not the usual 'are Gen Z real?' culture-war thing, I want it in the weeds, and I want the writer to actually land somewhere instead of ending on 'well, it's complicated, both sides have a point'.

Here's what I want the essay to have to deal with. I'll spell it out in the prompt itself so whoever answers it doesn't flail:

- the difference between a birth cohort (everyone born in some window of years, a totally real and very boring set) and a 'generation' (a cohort that's supposed to share a formative experience and, crucially, to know itself as a generation);
- the fact that age, period and cohort are locked together by definition (your birth year plus your age gives you the current year), so you can't cleanly seperate the three even with decades of survey data, you have to bolt on extra assumptions. Which means a lot of claims of the form 'Gen Z is more anxious / Boomers are like this' are doing something that's very hard to falsify in practice;
- the possibility that the label is self-fulfilling: once you tell people they're a generation, some of them start behaving like one, which then makes the label look like it was tracking something real all along;
- the fact that the boundary years are set by polling orgs and marketing firms and keep sliding around, which smells more like convenience than discovery;
- the rival explanation that most of what gets called generational is just 'young people are like that' (and later, 'old people are like that').

Then reach a verdict: are generations real kinds, or gerrymandered labels? And make the essay say what it's claiming by 'real' — does the category earn its keep by supporting predictions or causal explanations its rivals can't, or is being socially constructed enough to count as real (and if so, why that doesn't make 'millennial' as real as 'electron', or why it does)? If the verdict comes out different for different labels (say 'boomer' vs 'gen z'), say so and say why.

About 1200-1500 words. No reading list or citations needed, the writer doesn't have internet access.
