I've been covering AI for a while now — long enough to watch "agent" go from a term of art to a marketing word — and I keep getting into arguments with readers about weather the things I'm describing are agents at all. So I sat down and wrote out what I actually think about a bunch of cases, to see if there's a principle underneath. I'm not sure there is one.

Here's my gut:

- My thermostat: not an agent. Obviously.
- A chess engine: not an agent. It searches, it doesn't want to win. Even when it sacks a piece for a mate in seven, that's calculation, not desire.
- My robot vacuum: not an agent, but close. It "decides" to head back to the dock when the battery gets low, and that decision is doing real work.
- A trading algorithm trained on a reward that figured out how to game a metric nobody intended: agent. I feel strongly about this one.
- A chatbot that, when I ask it to play a character, keeps that character's goals straight across a two-hour conversation and schemes toward them: agent, or at least more agent than not.
- A sleepwalker: not an agent, even though they'll steer around furniture and open doors. There's nobody home.
- A corporation: agent. It sues people, it runs strategies, it outlives every human inside it, and no single person has to hold the whole plan in their head.
- A river: not an agent, even though we say it carves its way to the sea and finds the lowest point. Nobody I've argued with disagrees, so I'll leave it.

My tentative principle is something like: a thing is an agent when the cheapest way to predict what it'll do next is to assume it's trying to bring something about. That gets the thermostat and the river out, and it gets the bot, the corporation, and the chatbot in.

But it's making me uneasy. It says the corporation is an agent for exactly the same reason it says the trading bot is, and I have a nagging feeling those are two different reasons and that the sleepwalker case is where it shows. And honestly the chatbot one might just be me getting pattern-matched by a good character actor — some people insist these models aren't doing anything at all, they're only simulating a character who is.

What I want: tell me if there's a principle that gets all eight of these, or if I have to give one up. If a single principle can do it, state it precisely, and tell me which clause of my list each part of it comes from. If I have to drop a verdict, say which and why, and don't hedge about it. Then apply whatever you land on to a case I didn't list: a model that has no goal anywhere in its weights — it's just predicting text — but that, asked what it wants, produces a fluent, consistent, plausible answer, and then behaves in ways that fit that answer. Agent, or a very good forgery of one, and does that distinction even hold up on your account?

Also, some of my verdicts feel like the same verdict wearing different clothes — the trading bot and the corporation especially, and the chatbot and the sleepwalker. If they are, say so and tell me what the shared thing is. Don't hand me a survey of what everyone thinks, I've read enough of those. Pick a side and commit, and give me the reasoning you'd actually defend.
