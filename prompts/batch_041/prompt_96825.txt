Right, I've been slowly talked round to Strawson's line on the reactive attitudes — the Freedom and Resentment thing. Roughly: moral responsibility isn't some extra fact about an agent sitting behind our practices of blaming, resenting, being hurt, apologising; it just is the fittingness of those attitudes, taken from inside the engaged way of looking at someone that we slip into whenever we deal with them as a person rather than as something to be managed. The detached, causal, objective way of looking at people isn't a rival truth about them, it's just another attitude we can adopt, and the demand to justify resentment from outside the practice is basically incoherent — or at least not the kind of thing that could be answered.

I want you to try and break it. Not with the lazy determinism objection, I know that one's supposed to bounce off. I mean: which cases, which intuitions, which results from elsewhere would actually put pressure on the claim that responsibility just is the practice? Sort them from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me which ones Strawsonians themselves would concede rather than just what critics say. I also want an honest verdict on whether the view is insulated — if every apparent counterexample gets absorbed with 'well then our attitudes would be different', is that a strength for a view about a practice, or is it unfalsifiable in a way that should bother me? And what does it predict that a straight realist account, or a straight hard-determinist one, doesn't? If nothing, say so.

Things I keep chewing on that you should adress:

- the blamer who can't feel it. Someone with flat affect, or on the right meds, who goes through all the motions of holding people responsible but feels none of it. Is she holding them responsible, or just saying the words? Strawson seems to let in people who are only half inside the participant attitude — does that make responsibility a fact about what a particular person happens to be able to feel?
- the induced case. If I could reliably make you feel genuine, full-blooded resentment toward someone who did nothing at all — a drug, a bit of wiring — would that be a warranted reactive attitude with no responsibility behind it? If yes, then the attitudes look like they're tracking something they don't constitute.
- outsourced blame. In a modern legal system the resentment is mostly felt by nobody; it's an institution issuing censure on the victim's behalf, and most of us never form the attitude at all. If the practice has migrated out of individuals into bureaucracies, does the Strawsonian grounding still hold, or has the thing that was supposed to constitute responsibility quietly gone missing?
- ritual blame. A community that performs all the blaming practices, sincerely, but where as far as anyone can tell nobody ever actually feels the attitudes. Are they holding each other responsible?
- and the boring one I've never been satisfied on: what the view says about blaming versus merely disapproving, and excusing versus exempting. If it can't mark those differences without smuggling in something like desert, that's a problem.

Also tell me whether any of these pushes the view toward collapsing into one of its rivals — an expressivist story about responsibility talk, or a straight error theory with a story about why the practice survives.
