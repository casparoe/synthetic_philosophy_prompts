I have no training in philosophy — I buy and sell cut stones for a living — but a friend and I have spent a few years arguing about when a run of observations really supports a general claim, and I've kept track of where she comes out. On a thousand emeralds, all green, she says the evidence supports "every emerald is green," and she'll act on it. On the same thousand she says the evidence gives no support at all to "every emerald is green if it is first looked at before 2050 and blue after," even though the observations fit that claim exactly as well. She says a run of black ravens supports "every raven is black." She says those same emeralds give no support to "every emerald is green unless it was a gift from my grandmother, in which case blue." And she says that if every coin I have drawn from a jar is a quarter, that supports "every coin in this jar is a quarter" — but that this is bookkeeping about one jar, not a rule of nature the way "every emerald is green" is, since she expects the emerald claim to keep working on emeralds nobody has looked at yet.

Here is the case we fought about last week. Suppose we had examined a thousand emeralds, all green, and suppose it also turned out that every one of them weighed a prime number of carats, that all of them were found before 2050, and that none of them was a gift from my grandmother. Would she say that evidence supports "every emerald is green"? Would it support "every emerald is green if it weighs a prime number of carats and blue otherwise"? And would she treat that last claim the way she treats the before-2050 claim, or differently?

Predict what she will say on each point, then tell me which rules fit her record so far, what each of those rules predicts about the prime-carat claim, which rule you would bet she is actually running and why, and what single further case would settle it. I care more about the rule than about the verdicts. Please answer in American English.
