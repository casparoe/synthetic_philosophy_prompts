Ok so I've been going down a rabbit hole after argueing with a friend about this, and I want to see it played out propperly instead of me just gesturing at intuitions.

Set up a dialogue between two people arguing about whether you could have come from a different sperm and a different egg than the ones that actually made you. One of them says no — your origin is part of who you are, so any world where a different sperm fertilized a different egg contains a different person, not you. The other says yes — the actual cells are more like the starting materials, and what makes the resulting person you is something else (psychological continuity, the particular organism that grew out of the pregnancy, the soul, whatever), so a different sperm and egg could still have produced you.

Ground rules: both of them are sharp, neither is a strawman, and they have to actually answer each other's points rather than trade speeches. And I don't want the generic version everyone trots out. Push it into the details, specifically:

- Get them to fight about what the "origin" even is. Is the essential bit the two gametes themselves? The whole event of fertilization, including when and where it happened? The biological parents? The particular zygote? Make them notice these come apart: with IVF, a frozen embryo can be implanted years later, in a different place and a different body. Same zygote, different time and place and womb. Does the essentialist say that's still you? Most people feel it obviously is — so what does that do to their theory? And if the timing and place don't matter but the cells do, why exactly the cells?

- Bring in a clone or an identical twin. Same nuclear DNA as you, different origin. Is the clone you? If they say no, is it because of the origin, or because it's a separate organism with its own life and its own future? Press on whether those are really two different reasons or the same reason wearing a hat.

- Have the pro-different-sperm person give their best case that the intuition about the classic example (the one about someone not being able to have had different parents) is doing too much work, and have the essentialist reply to it rather than brushing it off.

- Somewhere in there they should hit the worry that this whole thing might be a verbal dispute: if the "other person" in the different-sperm world is exactly like you in every way that could ever matter to you, in what sense is calling them "not you" more than a choice about how to use a word?

- End with each of them naming the single case they'd most want the other to explain, and a one-line honest verdict on which of them has the harder job.

Keep it readable — no jargon dumps, no citing famous philosophers at each other, just two smart people talking it through. Maybe 1000-1500 words.
