I've been reading rationality blogs and forums for something like a decade, and there's a cluster of puzzles I keep circling back to that I'd like to finally get straight before I look at any more proposed answers. Here's the setup I care about. Take an agent that is genuinely part of the world rather than a Cartesian ghost standing outside it, and suppose it forms its beliefs the way Solomonoff induction says to: rank hypotheses by the length of the program that generates them, so short programs dominate, and predict by mixing over them. Now notice what happens to an agent that shows up inside those programs. A short program describing a world that contains the agent will usually have to say what the agent does, and the cheapest way to say it is to make the agent simple — so the same compressed description that fixes the weather and the furniture also fixes the agent's next move. The agent, reasoning as a consequentialist, then finds that its action is not independent of the world it is trying to steer: it is correlated with the environment through the shared program, in the way the one-boxer insists your choice is correlated with the contents of the box. And because the agent's own policy is presumably not the shortest description of itself, most of the prior's weight may sit on worlds where a simpler stand-in does the acting — worlds in which "the agent" is a puppet whose strings are pulled by the same short subroutine that pulls the rest of the scene. I've seen people gesture at all this under the heading of the prior being malign, and I believe there's a real worry here, but I have no idea what a good response is even supposed to look like.

So what I want from you is not a proposed solution but the specification a solution would have to meet. Write me the desiderata for a theory of how an embedded consequentialist should decide when it is one of the programs the universal prior is ranking — the phenomena it has to account for, the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse, and the results it must stay consistent with. For each requirement, give me the case or consideration that motivates it, so I can tell whether it is a genuine constraint or just an assumption smuggled in by one camp. Separate the non-negotiable ones from the ones that are really preferences — for instance, if some requirement quietly presupposes that the agent's choice is metaphysically independent of the world (a Cartesian gap), say so, and say whether the theory can be built without it. Flag any two requirements I probably cannot have together; if a subset of them is jointly unsatisfiable, that is the single most interesting thing you could tell me, and I would much rather hear it than get a tidy list. Also tell me which one requirement does the most to exclude candidate approaches, and which families of approach it rules out (describe them by their commitments rather than by labels if you prefer). If the whole package turns out to be unsatisfiable, say that plainly.

Three things I want the list to speak to directly, because they are where I keep getting stuck. First, self-prediction: the prior effectively hands the agent a prediction of its own action for free, and I don't know whether a theory of choice is allowed to use that prediction, must ignore it, or has to explain why using it is incoherent — a toy case I'd want handled is an agent that can read off its own next move from the shortest program fitting its observations, and is then asked to choose; whatever the verdict, it should be a requirement on the theory and not a footnote. Second, the reference machine: the prior's actual numbers depend on the choice of universal machine, so any requirement that turns on specific probabilities had better be invariant to that choice or explicitly relative to it, and I want to know which requirements survive and which quietly die. Third, the utility function ranges over outcomes, and the agent's own future deliberation is one of those outcomes, so optimizing may require it to care about its own reasoning states, which threatens a loop; the theory needs to say how the loop terminates or why it is benign. I don't need citations or a reading list — I'll dig up the literature myself — and I don't need you to score every existing proposal; keep the weight on the criteria and the cases behind them, and only sketch the contenders if that is genuinely illuminating.
