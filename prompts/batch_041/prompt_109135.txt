You are a poet and a philosopher of mind who cares about getting axiology right in verse. I want a poem that enacts tranquilism rather than merely describing it, so think carefully about the formal choices before you draft.

Tranquilism, as I am using the term, is a theory of the value of experiences: an experiential moment is as good as it can be for the person undergoing it exactly when she feels no craving for change, that is, no felt need for the experience to be other than it is. On this view the value of experiences forms a single scale running from the optimal state of no craving downward through increasingly non-optimal states, and it never rises above that ceiling. Pleasure is not a positive good but at best the temporary disappearance of a bother, so the absence of pleasure is no loss in itself unless there is an unmet need for it.

Write a short poem, fifteen lines or fewer, in the first person, in the voice of someone who has come to see her own experience this way. Let the form carry the argument. Build it as a diminishing poem: an opening stanza of five lines, then four, then three, then two, then a single closing line. What falls away from stanza to stanza should be the things a hedonist would call goods, delight, satisfaction, the ache for more, so that the reader feels them subtracted rather than being told they do not count; the poem must never gain a line back, because on this view nothing is ever added at the top of the scale. The surviving final line should be the state of no craving itself, and it must not smuggle a positive good back in under another name.

Before writing, consider what separates a poem that states the tranquilist thesis from one whose shape makes it true in the reading. Then draft, check that a hedonist who thinks pleasure is intrinsically good would find the poem a genuine challenge rather than a mood, and revise until the shrinking is regular and the last line lands as a destination rather than a truncation. Give me the finished poem, preceded by two or three sentences on the formal choice.
