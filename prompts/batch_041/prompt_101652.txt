I'm in a weekly reading group on AI safety, and we've been spending a lot of time on inner alignment and goal misgeneralisation — the thought that a system can be trained to behave well across its training distribution and then pursue something quite different once that distribution shifts. Last week we were arguing about whether inner alignment is genuinely a distinct failure mode from outer alignment, or whether it is just specification failure under another name, and one of the more senior members cut the discussion short with a line I have now heard from him several times: the model is just doing exactly what we trained it to do. He treats this as settling the matter — if the behaviour is what the training process produced, he says, then there is nothing here beyond the familiar problem of specifying the objective correctly, and any talk of the model having its own goals is mystification.

I don't think that can be right as it stands, but I can't articulate why, and I suspect the slogan is doing several jobs at once. Could you lay out, as a menu rather than a single favoured interpretation, the distinct precise claims that this line might be making? For each reading I'd like it stated as sharply as possible — what it commits you to about the training process, about the model's internal states, and about what the system's goal is taken to be — followed by a verdict: which readings are near-trivial or definitional, which are substantive and defensible, which are simply false, and which the phrase is most naturally heard as asserting when someone deploys it in this kind of argument. Finally, and this is what I actually need for the next session, tell me which of these readings, if any, would genuinely license his conclusion that inner alignment collapses into outer alignment, and which of them are quietly smuggling in something the surface grammar of the slogan does not support. Lay it out so that I can take the menu to the meeting and point at the row that applies.
