I have been asked to run a ninety-minute session for a continuing-education history of science seminar on Kepler's views on astrology. The audience knows the Copernican story and has heard of Kepler's laws, but they arrive with two assumptions I need to dismantle: that astrology in the early modern period was simply the sun-sign horoscopy they see in newspapers, and that a serious astronomer like Kepler must have been either a cynical courtier or a credulous mystic. I want to teach a more precise thesis: that Kepler defended a reformed astrology while attacking the astrology of his contemporaries, and that the distinction he drew was between the astrological aspect as a geometrical configuration that acts on the Earth's soul—by which I mean a kind of world-soul or formative principle, not a literal ghost—and the horoscopic apparatus of signs, houses, and predictions of particular events. Plan the explanation for me. What has to come first, and what depends on what? I am inclined to begin with the Aristotelian-Ptolemaic picture of celestial influence and Kepler's physical astronomy, then introduce his harmonic geometry, then his theory of aspects, then his polemic against conventional horoscopy, but I want you to tell me if that order is wrong or if something must be moved.

What single example should carry the session? I was considering the Jupiter-Saturn conjunction as an aspect, or the casting of a nativity, but I want the example to make the distinction vivid without requiring the audience to learn technical astrology. Tell me what the example must have and lack. What misconceptions will they form—for instance, that Kepler was a proto-skeptic, that his astrology was merely metaphorical, that the Earth's soul is a mystical entity rather than a physical principle, that aspects are causal forces like gravity—and at exactly which point in the sequence I should head each off. What should I refuse to explain at this level, and what is the one question I can ask at the end to see whether they have understood Kepler's position rather than just memorized a paradox? Finally, say how confident you are in this teaching plan and identify the point in it you are least sure of.
