I'm not an academic or anything, I just read a lot and got a bit obsessed with this after an arguement down the pub. A mate told me history is basically physics with worse PR, and I went off and read Popper's thing about the poverty of historicism to see if that held up. As far as I can tell his point is that you can't have laws of history the way you have laws of physics: societies are these sprawling wholes, not things you can generalise about cleanly; the trends people point to (population, technology, capital piling up, whatever) aren't laws, they can bend or stall; and the big one, any prediction about what people will come to know changes what they do, so the forecast ends up part of the thing it's forecasting. And then the people who claim to have found the laws of history always seem to be predicting the outcome they wanted anyway, and can never be shown to be wrong, which they act like is a strength.

What I want is a parody brochure for a company that has gone and done exactly the thing Popper said you can't do — a consultancy selling governments and investors "scientific" forecasts of the inevitable course of history. Like the "our approach" page of a slick firm's website, or a printed prospectus with a few short sections and a pricing table at the end. Deadpan, no winking at the reader, and the jokes have to come from getting the actual critique right, so someone who's read Popper laughs instead of wincing. It should trade on the real features of the thing being mocked: unconditional prophecies dressed up as hard science, holistic talk about "society as a whole", trends treated as laws, the refusal to ever be falsified presented as rigour, the conspiracy theory of society flogged as a premium add-on, and piecemeal reform sneered at as tinkering while "planning the whole of society" is the enterprise tier. The self-fulfilling prophecy thing should be sold as a feature — they should be genuinely proud that their forecasts bring about the future they describe. Maybe work in a line from a "concerned" in-house historian or a testimonial from a satisfied client.

About 700-800 words, and please write it in British English, spellings and all. Don't name Popper or turn it into an essay about him — it should read as the brochure itself, from inside the firm, with nobody stepping outside to explain the joke. If you've read the book you should recognise your own objections being sent up; if you haven't, it should still work as a bit of corporate nonsense.
