Phd student in philosophy of mind, and I've been chewing on an argument I built that I can't decide is a real refutation of epiphenomenalism or a slieght of hand. It goes:

(1) Right now I know I'm seeing words on this screen — I know something about my own experience.
(2) To know something, my belief has to be reliably connected to the fact.
(3) That connection is only reliable if the fact makes a difference to the belief — if my experience had been different, the belief would have been different too.
(4) So my experience has to make a difference to my belief about it.
(5) Epiphenomenalism says experiences make no difference to anything, beliefs included.
(6) So epiphenomenalism is false.

Each step looks fine on its own and I can't swallow (6). I'd most like to keep (5) and still keep some real sense in which I know what I'm experiencing, so something in (2)–(3) has to give, but that feels expensive. My gut says the culprit is either (3), where I think I'm sneaking in a difference-making condition on knowledge that isn't really needed, or (1), where I think I might be overclaiming what "know" amounts to here. I genuinely can't tell which, and the two have very different price tags.
