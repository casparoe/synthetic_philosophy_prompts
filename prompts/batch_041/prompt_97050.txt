I retired a few years back from a physiology lab — thirty-odd years of nerve-muscle prep work, mostly, which teaches you a certain humility about systems you think you understand — and since then I've been reading philosophy seriously for the first time. My wife says I've gone from knowing a lot about a little to knowing a little about a lot. The current obsession is Thomas Szasz and his famous claim that mental illness is a myth. I've been arguing about it with a friend who's a clinical psychologist, and he thinks Szasz is obviously, definately wrong, whereas I think Szasz is onto something about how the manual-makers decide what counts as a disease, and we go around in circles because he says "read the diagnostic criteria" and I say "who voted on those criteria, and by what standard?" In the lab we'd settle whether a preparation was sick or just odd with an assay. Here there's no assay, and I can't stop noticing that.

So here's what I'd like you to write for me: a mock entry for the next edition of the big diagnostic manual — the DSM-style thing — with the criteria A, B, C, the duration requirements, the specifiers, the prevalence figures, the differential diagnosis, the culture-related section, all of it. Only the disorder it describes is believing that mental illness is a myth, i.e. the entry pathologizes the person who keeps making Szasz's argument. Deadpan, no winking, formatted like the real thing, maybe 700–800 words, with a footnote or two in that dry house style. The humor should come from getting the genre exactly right: the way "lack of insight" does double duty as symptom and as diagnostic requirement, the way resistance to treatment is built into the criteria so that accepting the diagnosis is the cure, the footnote noting the category was added by a vote of the membership, the prevalence estimate that quietly counts everyone who disputes it as a case, the functional-impairment clause that only bites when the patient is doing fine. I want someone who knows the Szasz and Popper arguments to laugh rather than wince.

Two things, though. I don't want this to be a cheap shot at psychiatry, so give it an honest differential-diagnosis section that concedes the obvious point — some people really are ill and really do need help, and no cleverness about nosology changes that — and then let the entry show why the concession doesn't get the manual out of the bind. And please don't put a real living person in it as a named patient or a named villain; invent the case. No citations or reading list needed, I'm not going to check anything, I just want to see whether the form can be made to eat itself.
