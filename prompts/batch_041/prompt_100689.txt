I am a fourth-year PhD student in formal epistemology and philosophy of science, and I have been asked to serve as the graduate student member on my university hospital's Algorithmic Clinical Decision Support Review Board. The board must vote next week on whether to allow a vendor's sepsis-prediction model into the intensive care unit, and I have been asked to draft the decision memo that will frame the discussion. My dissertation concerns the formalization of heuristic arguments, so the board asked me to take the lead on the methodological part. The model's central statistical assumption is that the laboratory and vital-sign features are conditionally independent given a latent patient state. The vendor's validation report concedes that several pairs are mildly correlated in the training data, but argues that those correlations are unstable, that the residual dependence matrix is algorithmically incompressible—no short program reconstructs it better than chance—and that this constitutes a heuristic argument for treating independence as the only non-arbitrary default. The clinical team favors the model because it outperforms the current early-warning score in backtesting, and the vendor maintains that demanding a fully specified joint distribution would be scientifically unreasonable and would block deployment indefinitely. I need a short written opinion addressed to the board that recommends a concrete course of action—approve, reject, or approve with specific conditions—with a clear time frame and with conditions the vendor could actually meet. The memo should not be a literature review and should not cite sources; the board needs the argument itself.

It should engage seriously with the strongest case for the vendor's position, including the thought that the presumption of independence is a legitimate methodological default when dependence cannot be reliably estimated, and then say where that case fails or what it would take to make it succeed. I would also like it to distinguish between the descriptive question of whether the residual correlations are real and the normative question of what a hospital should do under uncertainty about them, and to say explicitly which parts of the recommendation are judgments of value rather than statistical facts. Finally, please make the recommendation actionable: if you recommend conditional approval, specify what monitoring, revalidation, or transparency obligations should attach, and what would trigger suspension.
