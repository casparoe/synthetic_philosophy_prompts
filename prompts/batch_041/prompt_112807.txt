Ok so in my decision theory seminar we did the absent-minded driver — the guy driving home who has to choose exit or continue at each of two intersections but can't tell which intersection he's at when he chooses. Payoffs: exit at the first = 0, exit at the second = 4, continue through both = 1. I wrote up this argument and the conclusion is obviously wrong but I can't find the bad step:

(1) At the second intersection, exiting beats continuing (4 > 1), so a rational driver exits there.
(2) At the first intersection, continuing beats exiting (continuing is what sets up the 4), so a rational driver continues there.
(3) A rational driver takes the locally best action at each intersection.
(4) So a rational driver continues at the first and exits at the second, and collects 4.
(5) But the driver's information is the same at both intersections, so no strategy he can actually follow gets him 4 — he can only exit twice (0) or continue twice (1).
(6) So the rational driver is both guaranteed 4 and unable to get it.

Which step is bad, and what does dropping it cost me — do I have to give up "act on your information", or "maximize expected utility", or is it something else entirely? And does your answer change if the driver can sometimes, but not always, tell the intersections apart?
