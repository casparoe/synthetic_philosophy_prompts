I hold a two-year research fellowship in philosophy. My training is in formal epistemology; I am comfortable with the standard decision-theoretic machinery, but I am not an economist and I will be working alone. I now have to commit to a single programme in the foundations of risk-weighted expected utility (REU), and what I want from you is an agenda rather than a survey: no literature review, no reading list, just a defended ranking of the open questions with the reasoning exposed.

For clarity about the framework I have in mind: the value of a lottery is a weighted sum of the utilities of its outcomes, but the weight on an outcome is not its probability; it is a rank-dependent weight, built from a function r — the risk function — applied to the probability that the lottery yields something at least as good as that outcome. Where r is the identity, the representation collapses into ordinary expected utility; where it is not, its shape encodes the agent's attitude to risk. Assume I already know the Allais-style motivation, the standard expected-utility axioms, and the textbook objections, and do not spend words re-explaining them. I want the questions that are visible only from inside the framework.

Assume I can produce one substantial paper and one follow-up. For each question you rank, tell me why it is upstream of the others or not, whether it is tractable for a single philosopher working alone, whether it is neglected mainly because it is unfashionable rather than because it is settled, and what would actually change downstream if it were answered. The following are the ones I keep running into and cannot rank myself:

- that the representation is formally rank-dependent, and so a close cousin of the rank-dependent and cumulative-prospect-theory models in economics and psychology: is the claim that r is an attitude to risk rather than a distortion of belief doing real work, or is the philosophical content carried entirely by the formalism?
- whether r must be a single global transformation of the relevant probabilities, or whether a plausible theory needs the risk attitude to vary with the kind of stake at issue, and what that would do to the axiomatisation.
- whether the framework can be extended from risk to ambiguity without collapsing into expected utility with respect to a non-additive capacity, and whether anything of philosophical interest survives the collapse.
- whether the departures from expected utility force revisions to the standard results about the value of information, the option value of delay, and dynamic consistency.
- whether risk attitudes can be aggregated across persons, and what REU implies for the ex ante / ex post dispute in normative economics.
- whether REU is best read as a theory of instrumental rationality that permits risk attitudes, or as a theory on which chances are themselves objects of value; I have been playing with the second reading and cannot tell whether it is coherent or merely a restatement of the first.

Tell me where each of these belongs, add the ones I have missed that outrank them, and say what would have to happen in the next year or so for the top of the list to change. If the honest answer is that one of these is not a real question, or that the foundations are in worse shape than the framework's proponents allow, I would rather hear that than be handed a list on which everything is equally worth doing.
