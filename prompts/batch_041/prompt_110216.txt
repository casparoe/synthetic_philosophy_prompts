Ok so I'm a retired chemist — 35 years of reaction kinetics, never took a philosophy course in my life — and I've spend the last year reading my way into the philosophy of psychiatry, mostly around Ian Hacking's line that classifying people can change the people being classified. Everyone nods along to that. I want a number.

Here's the claim I want your credence on: that at least one diagnostic category in current routine clinical use is a case of what I'll call thick looping — where the category has changed not just how people talk about themselves but the clinical picture itself: age of onset, the mix of symptoms, the typical course, the ratio of mild to severe cases. Not that patients now have a word for it, but that the phenomenon has drifted.

Give me the decomposition, not a vibe. The sub-questions as I see them:

1. Is the thin/thick distinction even coherent, or does it quietly assume a report-independent real phenotype sitting underneath, which is exactly what the interactive-kind idea denies? If it's incoherent, say so and put a number on that instead.
2. Assuming it's coherent, how likely is it that some category actually shows drift, and that the drift isn't a measurement artifact — changed criteria, lower thresholds, better screening, cohort effects, doctors getting better at spotting it?
3. Given drift, how likely is the classification doing the causal work, rather than the culture around it: media, patient communities, insurers, drugs?
4. The moderator I actually care about, because it's the part that smells like a real hypothesis to me: my hunch is this should be much stronger for categories whose criteria are heavy on subjective report — mood, pain, dissociation, anything where the patient has to tell you what's going on — than for categories built on observable signs. Does that hunch survive? What's your credence in it?

Combine 2 through 4 into one number for the main claim, tell me which term you're least sure of and what evidence would move it most, and — since you can't look anything up — if any of the four is unanswerable without data you don't have, say so and tell me what data would settle it.
