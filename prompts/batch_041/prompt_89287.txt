My two closest friends and I have been arguing for months about whether getting someone deplatformed is really a punishment, and we keep sliding past each other, so I sat down and tried to break it into the different mechanisms that could be doing the work. I'd like you to rank them from most to least plausable as the *main* thing happening when a person gets mass-unfollowed, dropped by their publisher, quietly uninvited from a conference, that kind of thing -- and for each one tell me whether it actually counts as punishment in the strict sense (hard treatment imposed on someone plus an authoritative expression of censure) or whether calling it that is a category error. Here's my list: (a) incapacitation, you just remove their reach, like locking somebody up; (b) deterrence, you make an example so the next person thinks twice; (c) denunciation, the point is the collective "this is beyond the pale" and the target's misery is a side effect nobody cares about; (d) norm-constitution, the sanction isn't applying a rule that already existed, it's what makes the norm exist at all; (e) pure coordination, nobody is punishing anybody, everyone is just updating about what will get them in trouble.

The thing I suspect might be the actual crux, and I'd like you to tell me if I'm off: none of (a) through (e) requires the sanctioner to have any standing or authority, and punishment as it's normally theorised is administered by somebody who has the right to do it. So either informal punishment is a coherent category and the authority condition is overrated, or else a lot of what gets called cancel culture isn't punishment at all but something else we don't have a good word for yet. I should warn you I made this list up myself in about twenty minutes, so it may be a mess -- if the categories overlap, or if the hard-treatment-plus-censure test is doing illegitimate work here, or if the mechanism actually doing the heavy lifting isn't on my list at all, just say so instead of politely ranking my garbage.
