For years I have watched the literature on the epistemic regress problem run in place. Foundationalists who terminate the regress in beliefs justified without inference, coherentists who replace the chain with holistic mutual support, and infinitists who insist on a non-repeating chain of reasons that the subject can produce at every link — each has a well-rehearsed answer to the others' standing objection, and I cannot recall a recent exchange in which any position actually moved. I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict, and I have a hypothesis I would like tested rather than assumed: that all three sides accept a presupposition none of them argues for, namely that justification is a relation among propositions, so that a belief is justified only if some further proposition supports it and the sole remaining question is the shape of the resulting chain. If that is what does the work, the interesting division may not be three-way at all; it may fall between everyone who accepts the presupposition and the views that reject it — for instance the view that inferring is something a subject does rather than a further proposition she endorses, or the externalist view that drops the requirement that the supporting reasons be available to the believer.

So: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one about the word 'justification', or three parties answering different questions under a single heading — and if the last, which questions? Is a hidden formal or empirical issue doing the work, about what an infinite chain could be or about what a subject can be said to possess? Do the sides differ over what a reason is, over which cases count as data, or over starting intuitions that no one can argue for? Say where my hypothesis illuminates the impasse and where it flattens real disagreements — I expect an infinitist to object that I have redescribed her position as precisely the thing she denies. Then tell me what would count as progress on your diagnosis (a distinction, a result, a change of question), or argue that none should be expected.
