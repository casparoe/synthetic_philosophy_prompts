I have no academic background in philosophy, but I have spent a long time arguing with a friend about whether an individual can be blamed for things like flying, eating meat, or voting when their own act makes no difference on its own. We keep talking past each other, so I want us to produce the joint statement we could both sign after a proper adversarial collaboration. Treat the two camps as, on one side, the view that individual acts in these mass-scale cases are morally insignificant because they are causally inefficacious, and on the other, the view that individuals remain responsible as participants in a harmful collective pattern even when their own act is not a but-for cause. Set out the claims both sides can accept, about causation, moral responsibility, and what changes if enough others act similarly; then state the remaining disagreements in wording each side would accept as a fair statement of its own position. Identify the cruxes, including at least one about whether the relevant unit of assessment is the act, the pattern, or the agent's commitment; one about whether making a difference and making a contribution come apart; and one about whether responsibility here is backward-looking blame or forward-looking commitment. For each crux, give a case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record what each side predicts about it, using both threshold cases, where harm appears only above a certain number of acts, and non-threshold cases, where harm accumulates continuously. Please distinguish climate, meat-eating and voting if the answers come apart, and say plainly if any part of the dispute is merely verbal. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and write in British English. No citations are needed.
