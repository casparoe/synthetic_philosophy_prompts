My girlfriend and I have been having the same fight for about a month now and I want an outside read before one of us says something we can't take back.

She isn't preachy about honesty, but she has a very sharp nose for it, and she's been handing down rulings on this kind of thing for as long as I've known her — dinner, group chat, the news, whatever comes up. Here's the track record, as best I can put it back together.

Her coworker told a client "we ran the test and it came back clean." They had not run the test. Her ruling: lying, obviously, and she'd never work with him again.

A company we both hate ran an ad saying its supplement was "clinically studied." Technically true — it had been in one study. The study showed it did nothing. Her ruling: she was down on it hard, and she didn't care at all that every individual word was true. She said that was the whole trick.

Our friend Tom told us a restaurant closes at 11. It closes at 10. He honestly thought 11. Her ruling: not a lie, he was just wrong, let it go.

Tom again, same dinner: he'd told his mum he'd come over Sunday and then didn't, because he'd forgot he said it. Her ruling: not a lie, flaky, but not a lie.

And once I asked her about the case where someone's hiding in your house and the guy at the door asks whether they're in there. She said she'd lie to him without a second's hesitation. Which is fine, but it doesn't help me, because it doesn't tell me whether she thinks what you do to the murderer is the same kind of thing as what you do to your boss.

Anyway. Last Thursday my manager asked me, in front of two other people, whether I'd warned the client about the schedule slip. I said "she and I talk most weeks." Which is true — we do. I had not warned her about the slip. I knew he'd hear it as a yes, and he did; he nodded and moved on. There's no email, no note, nothing anyone could ever find. I said one true sentence and let him finish it for me.

She says that's a lie. I say it isn't, and honestly I don't think I did anything wrong, though I notice I wouldn't say that out loud.

So what's she going to rule, and is she right? What I actually want to know is what rule she's been running all along, because I can see at least three that fit everything above and they don't agree here: she's against deceiving people whatever the words are, or she's against saying false things specifically and this doesn't count because I didn't, or she's against traps and this is the worst one yet because it's the one nobody can ever catch. Or something else I'm not seeing. And what would I have to put to her to tell them apart — whats the case where those come apart and I could actually get a straight answer out of her?
