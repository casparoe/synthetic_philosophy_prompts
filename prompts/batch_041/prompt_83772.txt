My reading group has spent the last year watching two of its members argue past each other about mind uploading — one is a comp-sci postdoc who thinks you're just a pattern, so the substrate is irrelevant and uploading is fine; the other is a neuro-adjacent grad student who thinks you are the ongoing biological life of your brain, so a silicon swap kills you even if nobody, including the person, could ever tell. I've been asked to write the document they should have written a year ago instead of going another round. So: draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between those two positions. I want (1) the agreed claims — about the imagined technology itself, about what's observable or reportable from the inside, about what neither side is claiming, and about what would actually change someone's mind; (2) the genuine disagreements, each stated in wording that its own side would sign off on; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions where settling one would actually move somebody, and for each crux a concrete case, argument or result both of them would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down before we run it; and (4) if part of the fight is really about what the word "survival" should be doing, say which part and why, instead of pretending it's all substantive. Number the agreed claims, then put the disagreements and the predictions in a table.

Please use these cases rather than the usual teletransporter stuff, we've worn those out. (a) The chip case: neuron by neuron, person awake the whole time, each neuron scanned, destroyed, replaced by a silicon chip stipulated to be functionally identical in every respect; she notices nothing and behaves the same. (b) The stem-cell case: exactly the same procedure, except each neuron is replaced by a fresh neuron grown from her own stem cells — so the tissue stays biological, but not one original cell survives. (c) The parallel case: nothing gets replaced; they copy the whole brain onto a chip array, run the array alongside the biological brain for a week (both are conscious, both insist they're her), and only then switch the biological one off. (d) The round trip: after the all-chip stage they go back the other way, replacing each chip with a stem-cell-grown neuron until she's biological again. If some of the cruxes can't be tested with cases like these, say so and say what they'd need instead, and flag anywhere the two sides are just using the same words for different things.

Keep it as a document we could actually hand to both of them, not a scorecard of who's winning.
