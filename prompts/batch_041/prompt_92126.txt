Please decompose the question and give me a rough probability for each term, with a line of justification for each: (i) whether a fourth relation — call it parity, or whatever you prefer — is even conceptually coherent, or whether 'neither better nor worse nor equal' is merely the negation of a comparison rather than a comparison in its own right; (ii) whether the small-improvement argument establishes anything, that is, whether the fact that a tiny improvement to A still leaves A not better than B shows that A and B are not equal, rather than merely that our judgements are imprecise; (iii) whether any of the standard candidate cases — a career against a family, one child's needs against another's, liberty against security — survives the deflationary explanation that we simply do not know the weights; (iv) whether the phenomenology of hard choices is evidence for incommensurability or only evidence about our psychology, and in particular whether a test one of us keeps proposing has any force: that incommensurability is confirmed when an agent who chooses A and later learns everything relevant about B still cannot reasonably regard the choice as a mistake, whereas under mere ignorance of weights full information would license regret; and (v) whether the whole question is hostage to a metaethical backdrop, so that a realist and a constructivist should answer differently. Tell me how those terms combine into your number, which term you are least confident in, and what evidence or argument would shift it most.
