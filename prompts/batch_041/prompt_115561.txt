My mate Ravi has been running this one on everyone in the group chat and it's doing my head in, because I cant find the seam and I'm starting to worry there isnt one. It's about that auction game where you bid for a dollar bill: highest bidder pays what they bid and gets the dollar, and the second highest bidder also pays what they bid and gets nothing. Bids go up in 10 cent steps and you cant take a bid back.

Here's his argument.

(1) Say I've bid 60 and the other guy has bid 70. If I stop here I'm second highest, so I pay 60 and get nothing. I'm down 60.
(2) If I bid 80 and he stops, I pay 80 and collect the dollar. I'm up 20.
(3) So one more bid isnt a bit better than stopping. It's 80 cents better. Thats the entire difference between a loss and a win.
(4) And watch what that 80 cents does as the numbers get big. At 60 and 70: stopping is -60, winning is +20. At $6 and $6.10: stopping is -$6.00, winning is -$5.20. At $600 and $600.10: stopping is -$600, winning is -$599.20. One more bid beats stopping by 80 cents, every time, no matter how deep in you are.
(5) And dont come at him with "he might not stop". He says put a number on it: one more bid is still the better bet as long as the other guy folds more than one time in five, and a guy who has already bid 70 folds more often than that.
(6) So there's no level where it flips. At every point in the game, the best move available is one more bid.
(7) Same shape everywhere else: two firms undercutting each other, two countries each waiting for the other to give up, two brothers paying lawyers over the same house. Quitting is always eating a loss you could have turned into a win.
(8) So "cut your losses" is a myth and escalation is just what rationality looks like from the inside.
(9) Except two people who both believe (6) walk away having paid two grand for a dollar bill, and I'm supposed to say they were being rational the whole way down?

I'd rather keep (4) than anything else on that list. The arithmetic looks bulletproof to me and I've checked it twice. So where's the break, and what do I have to pay to fix it?
