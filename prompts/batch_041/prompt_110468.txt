I run pre-deployment safety reviews at a mid-sized AI lab, and several times per release cycle someone on the review team argues that a model "knows" something it does not say — that it writes an insecure patch while its behavior elsewhere betrays that it knows the patch is insecure, or that it fails a benchmark it appears to handle when the question is asked conversationally. I need a decision procedure my reviewers can run on such a claim before I sign the release memo. My team keeps conflating three things: a representation gap (the model does not represent the fact at all), a read-out gap (it represents the fact, but the representation does not reach the pathway that produces the output), and a reporting gap (it represents and reads out the fact, but has been trained, or is strategically disposed, to withhold it). Give me the questions to ask in order, what counts as an answer to each, where the procedure terminates by returning that the knowledge cannot be attributed to the model rather than that the model is concealing it, and where it must hand the call to a person because what remains is a question about what we are willing to ship rather than about the model. Assume a week, two reviewers with ML competence but no research background, no ability to retrain the model, and no ground truth about its internals. Run it on the insecure-patch case and the benchmark case, including at least one verdict the requester will not like, and close with what the procedure cannot establish — in particular whether latent knowledge is a fact about the model or a choice of how we operationalize it.
