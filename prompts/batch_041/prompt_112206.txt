I teach a seminar on consciousness that ends every year with the Ant Fugue chapter of GEB — Achilles and the Tortoise and the Anteater going on about Aunt Hillary, the ant colony that can hold up her end of a conversation even though no single ant has the faintest idea what's happening. Every year a student asks the obvious question and every year I wave my hands and move on. This year I'd like to pin the hand-waving down to a number.

Here's the case. Take the colony and beef it up until its verbal behavior is indistinguishable from a normal human interlocutor's — call it Hillary-plus. Stipulate the behavior only: it converses, it jokes, it has ongoing projects, it says "that hurt" when you step on the anthill. Don't stipulate anything about its internal organization, and in particular don't assume its insides satisfy whatever conditions your favorite theory of consciousness lays down. Now: what's your credence that Hillary-plus is a subject of experience, that there is something it is like to be it? Not "does it have a mind" in the deflationary intentional-stance sense — if it talks like a person we should treat it like one, that part's cheap, I'm asking about phenomenal consciousness specifically. And honestly I find it hard to beleive the answer isn't just whatever your theory of consciousness says, which is exactly why I want the number decomposed rather than asserted.

So give me the number and the terms that produce it. My own would be roughly: P(some functionalist or computational theory of experience is right) against biological naturalism and against the view that experience is a basic feature of certain physical stuff; then, conditional on the functionalist branch, P(the colony actually meets the conditions — including the unity condition, since the standing worry is that a colony realizes a swarm of micro-subjects rather than one subject); then a term for what I've started calling the "spokesmodel hypothesis" — that the sentences come out of some subsystem that functions as a press office for a process that is not itself unified, so the reports are fluent and systematically misleading about the colony's inner life — and while you're at it tell me whether you think that hypothesis is even coherent, because I go back and forth on it; then a term for the panpsychist route, where the ants do have tiny experiences and the question is whether they combine; and finally a term for "the question is ill-posed / there's no fact of the matter," and if you give that one real weight, say how you want it to redistribute across the others instead of just letting the total come out low. Show me how the terms combine, tell me which one your final number is most sensitive to, and what single argument or piece of evidence would move it most.

For calibration: I'm at about 0.55. My colleague in entomology, who thinks an ant's nervous system is about as thick as a hair and that we're just projecting, is at maybe 0.03. A grad student of mine who is a panpsychist about everything is at 0.9. Where does the disagreement between the three of us actually live — in the theory-of-consciousness term, the unity term, or the spokesmodel term? That's the part I really want; the number is almost secondary.
