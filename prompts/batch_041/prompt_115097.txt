I sit on the animal care committee at the small marine centre in my town — I'm the community member, I run a hardware shop, no science background at all, I just got asked to be on it. We're rewriting our animal welfare policy and we've been stuck on one question for two months: which animals it actually covers. Right now the policy says vertebrates, full stop, which nobody ever thought about because it was inherited from the old trustees.

The reason it's come up is the new octopus exhibit. Our director says obviously the octopus gets full protections, same as the otters. Somebody then said fine, but an octopus is further from us than a crab is, so if the octopus is in, the crabs in our touch pool are in too. Somebody else said if crabs are in, why not the bees we want for the pollinator garden, and if bees then basically anything with a nervous system, and then we're writing a policy for the whole tank room and the budget meeting is going to be fun. Last time we met, two of our most reasonable people ended up genuinely annoyed with each other, which I think means we're not really disagreeing about the animals, we're disagreeing about how to decide.

Here's where I've got to by myself, and I'd like to know if it holds up. I've been reading alot about this and I don't think 'conscious' is a switch that's either on or off. It seems more like it comes in pieces — does the animal have sensors that detect damage, do those signals get integrated somewhere instead of just tripping a reflex, does the animal trade off avoiding harm against other things it wants, does pain relief change how it behaves — and different animals seem to have different numbers of those pieces. So I don't think the question 'is a crab conscious' doesnt have a yes or no answer, and I don't think 'we don't know' is a good enough place to stop either, because we still have to decide on Thursday.

My idea, which I came up with driving home and might be nonsense: don't draw a line, use a ladder. Top rung gets everything we give the otters — the octopus goes here. Bottom rung gets nothing — clams, mussels. Middle rung is for the ones we honestly can't call, like the crabs and maybe the bees, and for those we do the things that cost us almost nothing and skip the things that cost us a lot, and we write down our reasons so it doesn't look like we're just doing whatever's cheapest. What worries me is that 'sort of protected' sounds like a fudge, and I can already hear one of our trustees saying a policy you can't enforce is worse than no policy. And the sharper version of that worry is: if we protect the octopus and boil the crab, aren't we just protecting the animals we find impressive and dressing it up?

Can you write the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. I need it to land on something specific — which animals go on which rung, what the middle rung actually requires in practice, and what we do about the ones we're genuinely unsure about — plus the reasoning, in a form the director can use and the trustee who thinks this is all sentimentality can actually respond to rather than just be told he's wrong. And please be straight with me about the strongest argument against the ladder, including the possibility that it's just a way of dodging the hard call. If you think it's a bad idea, give me the alternative I should put to a vote instead.
