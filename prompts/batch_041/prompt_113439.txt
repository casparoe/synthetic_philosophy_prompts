My brother-in-law ran a dollar auction at his 40th birthday and its been bugging me for a month.

The rules: he auctions off a $1 bill. Highest bidder pays what they bid and gets the dollar. Second-highest bidder also pays what they bid and gets nothing. Bids go up in 5 cent steps. It finished at $8.10 and $8.05, so he took in $16.15 for a dollar bill, and two grown men weren't speaking to each other by the time the cake came out.

Everyone keeps telling me they got caught by the sunk cost fallacy. I don't think that's what happened, and I've built an argument about it that I can't break. Set it up at the end: top bid $8.10, I'm sitting second at $8.05.

(1) A choice is only irrational if some other option was available to you at the time that would have left you better off.
(2) At every point in that auction, each of the two bidders has exactly two options: bid again, or stop.
(3) Stopping isn't free. The second-highest bidder really does pay. My $8.05 isn't gone the way a spent $8.05 is gone — it's a live bill I'm still going to have to hand over. This is the one place where the usual sunk cost story doesn't seem to fit.
(4) So my options are: stop and pay $8.05 for nothing, or bid $8.15, take the top spot, and pay $8.15 for a dollar — down $7.15 instead of down $8.05. Bidding again is 90 cents better than stopping. And it's like that at every stage, for both of us, all the way up.
(5) So at no point does either bidder have a better option available than the one he takes. Stopping is always the worse of the two. Notice the guy who finally stopped is the one who, at that exact moment, made the worse choice.
(6) By (1), if there's never a better option available, then nobody ever did anything irrational.
(7) So the dollar auction doesn't show a fallacy at all. It shows that "throwing good money after bad" is a description of how it looks from outside the table, not a real mistake anybody makes.
(8) But he cleared $15.15 off two people who knew the rules before they sat down, and I'm fairly sure a mistake happened somewhere in that room.

My wife says the answer is just "dont play." But they all knew that going in and played anyway, so that can't be the whole answer, and whatever the real answer is I want to know what it says about the way people talk about wars you can't leave because of the ones you've already lost.

So which step goes, and what does giving it up cost me? I'd rather keep (1) and (8) than (5) — (5) feels like arithmetic, and (1) feels like it's just what the word "irrational" means. But if you tell me to drop (1), I want to know what's left of the word.
