My friend and I burned like two and a half hours last night on that beetle in a box thing from Wittgenstein — the one where everyone has a box with something in it they call a beetle, nobody can look in anyone else's box, and the point is supposed to be that the beetle ends up doing no work at all in the language-game: the word would mean whatever it means even if the boxes were empty. (I'm paraphrasing, I don't have the book in front of me.)

Here's what she did with it. She says it proves my headache is nothing over and above my headache-behaviour — if the inner thing drops out, then the inner thing is irrelevant to what pain is, so we might as well be behaviourists and stop talking about the felt quality. Then she pushed it further: even if the box really has a beetle in it, and even if her beetle and my beetle were exactly alike, it wouldn't matter. Meaning is public all the way down, so someone alone on a desert island from birth couldn't have any language at all, not even words for sensations. She also says it kills the inverted spectrum question — the "what if her beetle is green and mine is black" thing — stone dead, no discussion needed.

I said the analogy shows something about the word and she's using it to show something about the thing, and thats not the same move. I also said a box with a window in it is a completely different case, and she's smuggling the sealed-box version in as if it were the general one. She said a window changes nothing, because the beetle still isn't what makes the word work, and that I'm just attached to the idea that there's something in there.
