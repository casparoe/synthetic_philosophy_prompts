I am a retired experimental physiologist — thirty years on the neural control of breathing and, toward the end, on the autonomic accompaniments of attention — and since I closed my lab I have been trying to teach myself philosophy of mind, largely by reading and by arguing with a former colleague who has gone into cognitive neuroscience. Our recurring fight is about the distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, and about whether global workspace theory — or any account of that general shape, in which a state becomes conscious by being broadcast widely enough to guide report, memory, and the flexible control of action — is a theory of the first thing only, or of both at once. I have invented a criterion for my own convenience, and I should like you to treat it as stipulated rather than defended: call a state access-conscious when its content is broadcast in that way, and call it phenomenally conscious when, in addition, the system's being in that state is itself among the contents the workspace can carry and re-broadcast — a re-entrant self-model condition, if that is not too grand a name for it. My suspicion is that the broadcast story is what the evidence actually supports, and that the re-entrant condition is quietly imported to make the story feel complete; but I cannot tell whether that is a real gap or merely my residual behaviourist upbringing.

Please act as a philosopher of mind with a firm grasp of the empirical work on consciousness, and begin by putting my proposition in its sharpest form. What I want to know is your credence that any system whose states satisfy a well-specified global-workspace characterization of the kind I sketched is thereby phenomenally conscious, with no further fact required. I do not want a survey of positions; I want a number and its derivation. Before you assign anything, brainstorm the sub-questions the number depends on — at minimum: whether the access/phenomenal distinction marks a genuine division or is an artefact of our report-based methods; whether a broadcast/workspace account correctly characterizes the class of states we care about; whether my re-entrant condition is doing necessary work or is already implicit in the architecture; whether facts of that functional kind could ever suffice for phenomenality; and whether the phrase phenomenal consciousness has a determinate extension for the systems at issue at all. Give each sub-question a rough probability, one line of justification, say how the terms combine into the final figure, and then tell me which term the answer is most sensitive to, what argument or piece of evidence would move it most, and what — if anything — would count as settling the matter. For calibration: my own credence is about 0.35, my colleague's is about 0.8, and I would rather learn where our disagreement actually lives than be told that we are talking past each other.

Two practical constraints. I am working offline at the moment, so please give no citations, no reading list, and no references to particular papers or authors; if you need a distinction, state it. And please keep every term defined tightly enough that the numbers attached to it could in principle be checked by someone who disagreed with you — a decomposition whose terms are vague is worth less to me than a single honest number.
