I run a small wedding photography business in a town with four other established photographers, and the five of us have built something I can't name. Five years ago we all delivered edited galleries in four to six weeks and charged accordingly. Then one of us started promising ten-day turnaround. Within a year all five of us were promising five days, plus a free engagement shoot, plus unlimited revisions, at prices that haven't moved. Nobody is making more money than before; we're each working twice the hours for the same income, and couples aren't happier, they just expect more. In the spring we met and agreed to go back to three-week turnarounds and drop the free shoots. Two of them broke it within a month, quietly. Meanwhile new photographers keep arriving in town who don't know any of this history, and they simply undercut whatever the going rate is. I've done the arithmetic: if I alone go back to three weeks, I lose the next six bookings to the others; if all five of us did it, we'd all make the same money in half the hours. I like these people, and I've taken my own turn at the shortcuts, so I don't think anyone here is a villain. It looks like five reasonable people, each doing what's best for themselves, have assembled a machine that grinds all of us, and none of us can switch it off by being good. Should I hold the line I agreed to, or am I just volunteering to be the one who loses? Is there a sensible move here that isn't defecting? And if the only way out is to change the rules somehow, what does that actually look like when none of us has authority over the others and we can't stop new people from joining?
