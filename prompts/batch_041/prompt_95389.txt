I have been trying to reconstruct and defend a broadly Neurathian position on scientific language, and I want a consistency audit before I commit it to paper. My commitments are as follows.

(1) Scientific inquiry should aim at a unified physicalistic language in which every special science can be expressed without remainder.
(2) The ideal form of that language is a picture language: its elementary signs record observable states of affairs rather than private experiences.
(3) There is no non-linguistic given that such signs copy; whatever objectivity they have comes from their place in a coherent, revisable system of statements.
(4) Protocol statements are not indubitable foundations. Any of them can be rejected if keeping it forces unacceptable revisions elsewhere, and the image of rebuilding a ship at sea is the right one: we revise our conceptual scheme from within, with no dry dock.
(5) The unity of science is a regulative ideal of translation and coordination, not a metaphysical thesis about levels of reality.
(6) The same pictorial conventions should serve expert scientific recording and public civic education; a picture language that only specialists can read fails a central purpose.
(7) A picture language must be able to represent logical relations, not just observable scenes, because scientific inference and the comparison of rival hypotheses need to be expressed in it.
(8) The program is compatible with scientific realism: the physicalistic thing-language can track real structures even if we never reach a final vocabulary, and there is a pragmatic fact of the matter about which protocol language is better, not a correspondence fact.

I suspect these cannot all be held together, but I do not know which are pulling against which, and I have no settled view about which of them is most expendable. Please audit the set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and which are independent? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to be given up or reformulated to restore consistency and what that revision would cost. I am especially interested in whether the semantic, epistemic, and political strands can be combined, or whether one has to be subordinated to another. Do not tell me what Neurath himself would have said unless it bears directly on the consistency question. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this material, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
