I host a small podcast on philosophy of mind and cognitive science, and next month's guest is a philosopher who has spent her career defending a Gödelian argument for the non-mechanical character of human understanding while insisting that her version is not the Lucas–Penrose argument, which she regards as refuted. Her reformulation runs roughly as follows. The lesson of the incompleteness theorems, she says, is not that for any formal system there is some truth it cannot reach; it is that any system rich enough to model itself must, in representing itself as reliable, adopt a commitment to its own soundness that it cannot derive from within. She calls this the trust gap. On her account the gap is not a defect but the formal signature of self-awareness, and what it marks is the difference between a genuine strange loop — a self-model that is causally load-bearing in the system's own transitions — and a merely encoded self-description of the kind Gödel numbering supplies. She reads Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach as advancing a claim of precisely this kind rather than trading in metaphor.

Draft eight to ten interview questions that force her to develop this position instead of restating the familiar debate. I want at least one question on each of the following, and for every question a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give:

- how her formulation escapes the equivocation objection, namely that the reason we can see the unprovable sentence is true is only that we assume the system's consistency, so the gap seems to be about our assumptions rather than about minds;
- the standard retort that a machine can simply add the unprovable sentence as a new axiom and carry on, and whether the resulting regress makes the trust gap trivial and universal rather than special to minds;
- whether the self-trust commitment is empirical or transcendental, and if it is something a system can rationally adopt, why a machine could not be built with a dedicated trust module that adopts it;
- what exactly it means for a self-model to be causally load-bearing, and how in practice one would distinguish a system whose self-model does work from one that merely encodes a description of itself;
- whether any existing artifact — a self-monitoring controller, a proof assistant, a compiler that reasons about its own output — already meets her condition, and if not, what the nearest miss reveals;
- why the trust gap should be expected to yield awareness rather than simply a distinctive kind of incompleteness, since incompleteness of some sort is cheap;
- what an inconsistent yet plainly self-aware mind would do to her premises, and whether she is willing to say that consistency is required for awareness;
- whether the argument overgeneralizes, given that any sufficiently rich system exhibits such gaps, and what then separates minds from thermostats and rock formations;
- whether she is making a claim about minds or only about our theories of them, and what would be lost if the latter reading were correct;
- and a closing question about what evidence or argument would actually change her mind.

Please do not ask her to recite the standard Gödelian case against mechanism, to recommend reading, or to cite sources; the questions should stand on their own for a listener with no library at hand.
