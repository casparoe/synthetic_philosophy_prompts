I am a Bayesian about credence, and I have been trying to work out what I actually think about updating on uncertain evidence, going by my gut on cases.

1. A glance at a thermometer. Before looking, I am spread over 15–25 °C. I glance at the display and would say I am 70% sure it read 20.0 and 30% sure it read 22.0. My verdict: my new credence for each temperature should be the 0.7/0.3 mixture of what I would have believed had I been certain the display read 20.0 and what I would have believed had I been certain it read 22.0.
2. A remembered bird. I seem to remember a bird at the feeder yesterday, and memories of that kind are right about 85% of the time. My verdict: my credence that a bird was there should not simply become 0.85; if birds at my feeder are rare, the remembered sighting should leave me well short of 0.85.
3. Two readings. I take two readings from a jittery scale, 20 and 22, each with a known error distribution, and my uncertainty about one reading is independent of the other given the true value. My verdict: the order in which I take the readings should not matter; my final credence about the true value should come out the same either way.
4. Dim light. In poor light my credence that a wall is red rises, but I could not name any proposition I have become certain of — not even "it looks red to me", since I am unsure how it looks. My verdict: my credences can legitimately change without my becoming certain of anything.

The rule I have been assuming is Jeffrey conditionalization: choose a partition, set my new probabilities for its cells to my confidence in them, and hold fixed all conditional probabilities given those cells. It fits the first case by construction, and I cannot tell whether it fits the rest. The first two verdicts look to me like the same shape — a confidence figure attached to an uncertain proposition — yet they pull in opposite directions, which is what worries me.

Is there an update rule that gets all four verdicts, or close to all four? State it precisely, say which case motivates each clause, and tell me which verdicts I would have to give up to keep the rest.
