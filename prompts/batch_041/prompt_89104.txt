I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, and I've been using an exercise my therapist gave me, one I've since seen in Stoic-flavored self-help books and meditation apps — you sort whatever is bothering you into two lists, things you control and things you don't, then work on letting go of the second list. Our team even ran a lunchtime session on it. What I want to know is what that exercise quietly assumes. It treats the line between the lists as something I can draw in advance and as stable, and it treats the difference between the columns as a difference in whose power a thing is. But the tradition behind it seems to hold that the only thing genuinely mine is my own judgment or assent, not my health, my job, or other people's opinions, and also that everything that happens, my own judgments included, is fated or caused by things outside me; those claims can't all mean the same thing by control. Please reconstruct the picture of agency, causation, and the self that the exercise presupposes, and which rival pictures it rules out; separate what it strictly needs from what it merely comes bundled with; say whether the exercise and the stricter doctrine are consistent, and whether the exercise would survive that doctrine being true; and tell me where the practice is underdetermined, so that a purely therapeutic rationale would license it just as well as a metaphysical one. Then tell me what the exercise would have to become if the strict reading turned out to be right.
