I am a retired biologist who has only recently turned to philosophy, and I have been trying to make sense of the thesis that an organism's origin is essential to it, so that a human being, for instance, could not have come from a different sperm and egg. The case of Dolly the sheep seems to me a useful test, and I would like you to analyze it through that thesis. Dolly was produced by somatic cell nuclear transfer: the nucleus of a mammary gland cell from one ewe was placed into an enucleated egg from another, and the resulting embryo was carried by a third. Distinguish, if you can, a gametic version of origin essentialism, on which the origin is the sperm and egg, from a zygotic version, on which it is the zygote, and from a somatic or genomic version, on which it is the nuclear genome. What would each version say about Dolly's identity and about the identity of the three ewes? Then bring in monozygotic twinning as a parallel case: one zygote, two individuals, the same origin. Does origin essentialism survive these cases in its standard form, in a revised form, or not at all, and where is it silent rather than wrong? I am not asking for citations or a survey of the literature. End with a single line beginning Verdict: and then one of these labels: survives unchanged, survives revised, or refuted.
