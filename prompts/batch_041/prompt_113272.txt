David Hume died in 1776, long before any machine could hold a conversation. Suppose he were brought up to date on a contemporary phenomenon: users of AI companion apps report real grief — bereavement, guilt, a sense of abandonment — when the service is discontinued, while insisting that they know the companion was only a language model. How would Hume decide whether this grief is a genuine emotion or a form of self-induced make-believe, and what work would his account of the passions, and his treatment of belief as a special liveliness of an idea rather than a separate act of assent, do in that decision? Would he see the case as continuous with the familiar puzzle of being moved by the fate of a character we know to be invented, or as categorically different, given that an AI companion responds and adapts rather than lying fixed on a page — and, on either verdict, would he have to revise his theory of the passions?
