My uncle has a relapsing illness where he goes through spells where he's really not himself, and a few years back he wrote a living will that basically says: if I ever say I've changed my mind while I'm in a spell, don't believe me, the well me wrote this and the well me outranks the sick me. The thing that gets me is he's been on the other side of it too — he once tore up an older directive, and at the time he was dead certain that the guy who wrote it was a stranger he had nothing in common with. So the two of them aren't cleanly seperate people, they keep swapping places, and each one thinks the other is the imposter.

What I keep circling is whether a directive is more like a note the earlier self leaves that has to be obeyed, or just the earlier self's best guess about what the later self will want — because if it's only a guess, then a later self who really disagrees should just win, and the whole point of writing it down early collapses.

Can you write me a short story that makes me feel the weirdness of this? I don't want a courtroom thing about pulling the plug. I want it to turn on who counts as the author of a life, and on the fact that the earlier self only gets to boss the later self around if you think of a person as one thing stretched over time. Maybe the narrator is the later self, reading a letter the earlier self left for exactly this situation. And please don't end by telling me which one was right — I want to still be chewing on it.
