I'm an undergraduate currently taking a seminar on political theory, and there's a pattern I keep running into that I don't have a word for. The course keeps returning to the distinction between left and right; I can see that it tracks something, but I can't get clear on what, and I've begun to suspect I'm running several different things together. I'd like to know whether the pattern has an established name — and I'd want more than the name. What the thing is exactly, what it isn't, and where its edges are.

Three observations, in increasing order of how much they bother me.

First: a controversy opens up over something with no obvious connection to the standing disputes — whether a particular line of research should be funded, how much of a new medical technology should be permitted, what a school should do about some new device. Within a term, everyone I know has a position and can predict everyone else's, before any argument has been made. My own position tends to arrive before my reasons do: I notice where I stand and then go looking for the argument. This isn't just my friends being predictable; the same thing happens to me with questions I have genuinely never thought about.

Second: when I ask people who agree on everything to explain why, the answers don't match. One says the point is never to be subject to a power you didn't agree to; another says the point is what happens to the worst off. Neither would switch sides if the other's reason were refuted, and neither treats the agreement as a coincidence. The same person gives different reasons for different questions, so no single principle seems to be doing the work. And the side that wants one thing tightly controlled in one domain wants the next thing left alone in the next; when I ask why, people say the domains are simply different, but can't say what the difference is except that this is where the side has ended up.

Third, the mirror image: two people I know agree on every concrete question I've put to them — the same verdicts on the same controversies — but each would join a different side if a fight broke out, and each treats the other as an opponent.

So: what makes a side a side? The labels plainly refer to something, but whatever it is seems to be doing less work than the sides themselves. Are these three observations one phenomenon or three? Is there a term for the process by which a previously unclaimed question gets a side, as distinct from the state of people's answers lining up? What are the nearest established concepts, and where exactly do they fail to fit? If nothing fits, coin something and define it — and tell me what it excludes: what would have to be true for the pattern to come apart, or for a question to stay unclaimed.
