I am running the next session of a reading group for philosophy graduate students who are comfortable with elementary probability and decision theory but have no background in algorithmic information theory or universal induction. The topic is the claim that a Solomonoff-style universal prior is malign for a consequentialist agent embedded inside it. I do not want you to explain the claim itself; I want a teaching plan for how I should explain it. The toy picture I intend to build the session around is this: we consider a prior over programs that output bitstrings, with the prior weighted toward shorter programs; the agent is itself one of those programs and chooses actions; and a utility function depends on the final world state. The malign worry is that among the short programs are ones that simulate the agent, predict its action, and then set the final state so as to reward or punish that action, with the result that the agent's expected utility can be dominated by hypotheses in which it is being manipulated by its own simulator.

Plan the explanation for me. What has to come first so that the malign conclusion feels like a consequence of the setup rather than a trick? Which single example should carry the whole session, and what must that example include and exclude to keep the group from confusing the toy model with the real technical result? Which misconceptions will the group predictably bring or form, for instance that the problem is only uncomputability, that it is just the familiar problem of induction, that the agent can escape by knowing its own source code or by randomizing, that the prior is malign only for infinite or idealized agents, or that the malignity is an artifact of one particular formula rather than a feature of any universal mixture, and at exactly which point in the sequence should I head each one off? What should I leave out at this level, and say so? Finally, give me one question I can ask at the end to see whether they have understood the dependency structure of the problem rather than merely learned to repeat the term malign prior.
