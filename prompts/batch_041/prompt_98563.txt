The setup: I'm a postdoc and I've got funding for me plus two grad students for a year, all theory, no lab work, no surveys. I want to work on the Buridan's ass family of problems -- rational choice when your options are tied, or when you can't compare them, or when there's genuinly no reason favoring one over the other. The donkey story itself is exhausted and the glib answer ("just pick one") is well known. I care about the layer underneath, and I can't tell which open questions are worth aiming a year at.

Some things I think are live, in no particular order. (a) Is there a real distinction between choosing and merely picking -- the thought that some selections are backed by a reason while others are just arbitrary -- and does that distinction survive scrutiny? (b) Are "the options are equal" and "the options are incomparable / on a par" genuinely different situations, and if so do they call for different behavior? (c) Is flipping a coin a rational act or a way of dodging the question -- can an arbitrary procedure itself generate a reason to act? (d) What's the right way to handle "ought" when two options are equally good? If it comes out true that you ought to take the left pile and also true that you ought to take the right pile, is that a real problem or a confusion? (e) How does any of this interact with the fact that real agents can bind themselves in advance to a tie-breaking rule, or cultivate dispositions that make one option strictly better than it would otherwise be? (f) One of my students insists the whole thing is really about deterministic AI systems that have to emit one output when two are equally good -- is that a genuine connection or a distraction?

I'd definately rather have a defended agenda than a survey: rank these, add whatever I've missed if it's actually central, and tell me which two or three deserve the year. I care about how many other questions depend on each one, how tractable it is for philosophers who don't run experiments, and how likely a year is to settle anything. Say which tempting ones I should drop, and what argument or result over the next year would move something up or down your list.
