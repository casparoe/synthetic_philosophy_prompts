Alright, take a side and actually defend it. There's this old line of thought that you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg -- that the particular pair of gametes that actually fused to make you is essential to you, so any world where a different sperm or a different egg was involved contains a different person, not you. I want you to argue that this is false: that some person could have come from a different sperm, a different egg, or both.

Please dont just recycle the usual modal-intuition stuff. Build the case out of the cases where the ordinary picture already looks shaky. For starters, we've got mice with two mothers and, more recently, mice with two fathers, and there's serious work on making gametes out of ordinary body cells -- so "one sperm plus one egg" may just be a parochial fact about how our species happens to reproduce. Then there's monozygotic twinning, where one starting zygote turns into two people; if the same starting material can yield either one person or two, how essential can the starting material be? And chimerism, where two embryos fuse into a single person carrying two genomes and, in some sense, two origins. Use whichever of these actually helps you and say why the others don't.

Two extras I want. First, seperate out whether it's the gametes that are supposed to be essential or the zygote (the fused cell) -- I suspect the interesting action is there, and I want to see you pick a line and commit to it. Second, give me the strongest objection to your own conclusion and say whether you'd bite the bullet or not. And a last thing: is there any practical upshot here at all -- does it touch how we should think about donor conception, cloning, or the "it had to be this particular child" feeling? Be honest if the answer is that it doesn't.
