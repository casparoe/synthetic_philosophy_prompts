Two of my graduate students have spent a term arguing about uncertain evidence and neither has moved an inch. I want the document they should have produced instead: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the two positions below.

The first position, probability kinematics, holds that sometimes an agent's evidence is not a proposition she comes to be certain of but a redistribution of credence across a partition she already possesses. In that case the correct update is the rule usually called Jeffrey conditionalization: hold fixed the conditional probability of each proposition given each cell of the partition (the rigidity assumption), and set the new credence in any proposition to the average of the old conditional credences, weighted by the new credences in the cells.

The second position, conditionalization-only, holds that there is exactly one rational update rule: conditionalize on a proposition the agent becomes certain of. Allegedly uncertain evidence is handled by enlarging the space — introducing a signal variable whose value the agent does come to know, and conditionalizing on it. On this view Jeffrey's rule is a derived shortcut, valid only when such an enlargement is available and correctly specified, and it never enlarges the agent's repertoire of rational responses to experience.

Write the statement both camps could sign. It should contain:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section: what both accept about what evidence is, about what a rational update must preserve, about how the two rules are related as mathematical objects, and about the status of the rigidity assumption within each framework.
2. The residual disagreements, each formulated in wording its own side would accept, in a table with columns for the claim, each side's formulation, and whether the disagreement is factual, normative, or merely verbal.
3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. My own suspicions, to be confirmed or rejected: that one crux is whether the enlargement maneuver genuinely reduces Jeffrey conditionalization to conditionalization or merely relocates the rigidity assumption to the finer partition; that a second is whether the order-sensitivity of successive Jeffrey shifts on different partitions is a defect of the rule or a correct verdict about two genuinely different epistemic situations; and that a third is whether the introspection variant of the maneuver — conditionalizing on a proposition about one's own experience, or about one's own credences — is coherent at all. For each crux, give the concrete case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, record each side's prediction about that case in advance, and for at least one crux state what each side would accept as defeating its own position.
4. A short section on the old-evidence problem, since it is where the two frameworks appear to give visibly different advice; tell me whether it is a crux or a distraction.

Keep the cases concrete — a diagnostic test whose reading is graded rather than decisive, a perceptual experience that redistributes credence without settling any proposition, two tests that can be run in either order — and keep the mathematics honest: where a claim about rigidity or commutation holds only under a condition, state the condition.

No survey of the literature and no bibliography. Close by committing: which parts of the residual disagreement you judge to be verbal, which part is load-bearing, which side the balance of the argument favours on that load-bearing crux, and what would change your mind.
