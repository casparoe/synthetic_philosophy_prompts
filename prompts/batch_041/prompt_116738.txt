The regress argument strikes me as settling the dispute among foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism. If a belief is justified only when the believer can give a reason for it, and each reason must itself be justified by a further reason, then the chain of reasons must either stop at some unjustified stopping point, circle back on itself, or run on forever; and since the first two are plainly unsatisfactory, infinitism is the only serious contender. But no finite mind can actually possess an infinite chain of reasons, so it looks as though nothing is ever justified. Should epistemologists therefore abandon the notion of justification in favor of weaker ones such as entitlement or reasonableness, or is there a way to rescue it?
