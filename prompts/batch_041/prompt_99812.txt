Two accounts of why originals matter can be distinguished. The evidentiary account holds that an original is valuable as the most reliable record of the artist's act: it is the primary document of the choices that produced the work, and its value is fundamentally epistemic. On this account the value is in principle transferable — if some other source preserved everything the original tells us about the artist's choices and technique, nothing of value would be lost. The contact account holds that an original is valuable as the terminus of the artist's agency: it is the object the artist actually selected and made, and its value is relational rather than informational. On this account no amount of information substitutes for the object itself, because what is valued is precisely the causal connection between the work and the act that produced it, not any fact that could be learned from it.

Please compare these two accounts, addressing the following in order:

1. Which account better explains the persistent intuition that even a perceptually perfect forgery — one that preserves every visible property and whose creation we can fully document — is worth less than the original? Explain the mechanism by which your chosen account generates that intuition, and say whether the other account can generate it as well.

2. Consider the case of the self-forgery: the original artist, decades later, deliberately produces a copy indistinguishable from the original, intending it as a reproduction rather than as a new work. Does either account entail that this object has less value than the original? If your preferred account does not, say what that reveals about it; if it does, say whether that result is acceptable.

3. Are the two accounts genuinely distinct, or does the contact account collapse into the evidentiary account once we ask what "contact" is supposed to add beyond the facts it makes available? Argue for your answer rather than merely asserting it.

4. Which account is more vulnerable to the charge that it merely dresses up a taste for authenticity as a theory of value?
