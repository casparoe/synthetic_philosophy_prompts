Someone pitched me on the hedonistic imperative recently — the idea that we should use biotech to abolish suffering outright rather than just make life a bit nicer, and not only for humans. The part I keep chewing on is the suggestion that pain can be split into an informational side (where it hurts, how bad it is, what to do about it) and an awful side, and that we could keep the first while engineering away the second. Please describe the most important implications of taking that seriously, especially for the things I'd normally assume are worth suffering for: grief after a death, the sting of failing at something that matters, the sense that some pain is meaningful or earned. And does the whole picture change if the tech turns out to be uneven — say it dulls physical pain beautifully but barely touches loneliness, shame or depression?
