Next month I have to open a session of a small decision-theory reading group on Kelly betting and geometric rationality, and the format is that each of us circulates a statement of where we have landed so that the others can attack the position rather than the paper. I have been told that the useful thing to hand over is not a summary but a list of my own commitments, so here is mine, and what I want back is an audit of whether it hangs together rather than an exposition of the theory. (i) I take expected utility maximization to be the correct general account of rational choice under risk: an agent is rational just in case she maximizes expected utility with respect to her credences and a utility function over outcomes. (ii) Whenever I can choose how much to stake and can reinvest what I win, I stake the full Kelly fraction — the fraction that maximizes expected log wealth — and I regard this as required by rationality, not merely as good advice for someone whose utility happens to be logarithmic. (iii) The reason Kelly is right is the growth-rate argument: it maximizes the asymptotic growth rate of wealth, and over a long sequence of bets it is the time-average growth rate, not the ensemble average, that governs what actually happens to a single agent who keeps playing. (iv) I am a Bayesian, and I hold my probability estimates to be uncertain: in any real bet I do not suppose myself to have an exact credence about my edge. (v) I will not take a bet that could leave me ruined. (vi) When I have a concrete target — I need to turn five thousand dollars into fifty thousand by a fixed date — I should size my stakes so as to maximize the probability of reaching it, even where that means staking more than Kelly prescribes.

Go through the set and tell me which commitments actually pull against each other, which pairs only appear to, and how severe each genuine conflict is, ordered from worst to least. For each real tension, say what would have to give, what giving it up would cost me, and whether the conflict is between the commitments themselves or merely between the way I have phrased them; for each apparent tension, say precisely what has to be made explicit for the appearance to disappear. I would rather be told plainly that some of these are irreconcilable than be reassured that they can all be made to fit, so do not smooth anything over on my behalf. Finally, if there is a single revision that would clear up the most while leaving my position recognizably mine, tell me which commitment to drop or amend, and what I would still be entitled to keep.
