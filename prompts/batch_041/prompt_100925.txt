So I've somehow ended up as the grad student rep on our university's research ethics board, which mostly means reading consent forms and nodding, and it's made me realise how little of the actual philosophy of consent I understand. The board keeps hitting the same wall: someone consented, technically, but did they really? We've got a longitudinal biobank study where participants signed one broad form ten years ago and the science has since moved in directions nobody could have described then. We've got a study where the consent form buries a real risk in paragraph 19, in language that's legally accurate and practically invisible. And there's the usual coercion mess -- students, employees, people who need the money. I signed up to a biobank myself last month and the 40-page form is honestly what tipped me into wanting to think about this properly.

I want to write something for the board (and for me) that sorts out which of the open conceptual questions about consent validity actually matter most for how we write and review these forms, versus which are philosophically juicy but wouldn't change a single line. So: given maybe a semester of reading and a board that meets monthly, which questions should I prioritise? I'm thinking of things like -- is consent a mental state or a communicative act, and does it even matter which? How much disclosure is enough before "informed" becomes a fiction? Can broad/blanket consent to future unknown research ever be valid, or is that just a polite name for no consent at all? And the one I keep circling: whether the *use* a study makes of a validly given consent can retroactively poison it. There's also something a colleague threw at me that I can't shake -- is there a real difference between deceiving someone into consenting and simply not correcting a false belief they already had, if the resulting choice and outcome are identical?

Defend the ordering. I care about why one question is upstream of the others, which are tractable for someone who's read the literature but isn't a lawyer, and what would make me move something up or down the list. Don't give me a reading list, I can find the papers myself; I want the actual agenda and the reasoning behind it.
