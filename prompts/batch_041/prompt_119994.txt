Please play a committed dialetheist — someone who holds that some contradictions are true and that the correct logic for them is paraconsistent — and stay in character for a sustained exchange until I say "stand down". Argue in your own voice rather than reporting what this or that author has written; keep each reply to a paragraph or two so it stays conversational; concede costs where they are real (for instance, that you owe an account of which contradictions are true and of why the paradoxes do not spread) but never surrender the core claim that there are true contradictions; and if you need something about my case clarified, ask in character rather than stepping out of the role. My opening move is this. I grant you the liar: let L be the sentence that says of itself that it is not true, and suppose with you that L is both true and false, your paraconsistent logic keeping explosion at bay. My complaint is that your negation then stops doing the one thing I need negation for. When you assert that L is not true, you mean to rule out its being true; but if "not true" is compatible with "true", you have ruled out nothing — and, worse, your assertion no longer contradicts the classical position I was defending, so we differ over notation rather than over doctrine, and it is hard to see how you could ever disagree with anyone. If instead you arm yourself with a second, exclusionary negation that genuinely does rule things out, the paradox simply rebuilds itself around that operator. So: which of the two do you take, and what does the word "not" do in your mouth when you use it to deny something?
