Tolstoy's treatise on art, completed in 1897, sets aside beauty as the defining mark of art and treats art instead as the deliberate communication of feeling — judged by the sincerity of the artist, the individuality of the feeling conveyed, and the clarity with which it reaches an audience. It was written in a world of oil paintings, novels, opera houses and conservatoires. I want you to reconstruct, as carefully as the evidence allows, what he would have made of three things that postdate his death in 1910 by several decades.

First, works whose substance is a set of written instructions, so that whoever conceives the work need never touch or supervise its making, and whoever does make it may feel nothing at all about it — the instruction-based wall drawings and comparable pieces of the 1960s and after are the familiar case.

Second, images, music and prose generated by a system from a short verbal prompt: the person writing the prompt may feel something keenly, the system that renders the output has no inner life whatever, and the result may nonetheless move a very large audience — which is precisely the effect his theory treats as the mark of art.

Third, the present institutional setting: museums, auction houses and the market, algorithmic recommendation deciding what millions of people encounter, and production aimed at a global audience rather than at a community.

For each, give your best reconstruction of his verdict and of the reasoning he would offer in his own terms, rather than a generic portrait of a stern moralist disapproving. Where his criteria commit him to a conclusion he would himself have found absurd or repellent — for instance if his account of infectiousness as the mark of art obliges him to count machine output as art — say so plainly rather than harmonising it away. Indicate where his framework could be amended to accommodate the case and where amendment would amount to abandoning the theory.

Be explicit about the epistemic status of each claim: distinguish what the texts commit him to, where knowledgeable readers genuinely disagree about how to take him (how literally to read his dismissals of Beethoven, Wagner and Shakespeare, for instance), and where you are extrapolating. Do not present a contested reading as the consensus; where the evidence underdetermines a prediction, set out the competing readings and your reasons for preferring one. Answer in British English.
