I run a small grad seminar on social epistemology and I want to spend one 90-minute session (16 people) on a game instead of a lecture. The target is Condorcet's jury theorem, but not the version everyone has already seen on a slide — majority of independent, better-than-chance voters converges on the truth. I want them to feel three things in sequence, and I want the rules to do the work, not me. First the aggregation miracle: add more voters, get more accuracy, seemingly for free. Second, the mirror-image result that almost never gets taught: when individual competence dips just below chance, adding voters doesn't merely stop helping, the majority becomes *reliably wrong* and the bigger the crowd the more confident it is in being wrong. Third, the assumption that actually dies in the wild — independence — because the moment jurors deliberate and influence each other their errors correlate, and a talking jury can be strictly worse than one that just ballots its private signal. If you can also work in the shared-cause point (that a common source of error caps accuracy no matter how many people you add) without crowding the session, great, but don't force it.

So design me the thing: cheap materials (I have no budget and I'm not putting real money on the line), exact setup, a token/points payoff scheme, the sequence of rounds with the parameters already chosen so I'm not deriving numbers live, a 90-minute timeline, and a debrief that maps each moment onto the formal assumptions — independence, sincerity, homogeneous competence above 1/2 — so they can see which assumption did what work. The reveal should come out of what happens to them, not out of my mouth. Also be straight with me about what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to recover, and which part of the theorem the game simply can't reproduce so I have to supply it verbally — the actual limiting result, or genuine large-n independence, or whatever you think the activity can't fake. Assume they're sharp but have never seen the math; I don't want them to need any notation to play.
