Me and my flatmate got into a two hour arguement last night about whether anything that happens in a game is actually real — he kept saying it's just a game, nothing in there counts, and I kept saying he was wrong, but by the end I was defending about four different things at once and I'm pretty sure at least two of them are pulling against each other. Before I go back to him I want to know whether my position actually holds up, so here it is, tell me what breaks.

(1) Stuff in games is real stuff. The sword in my inventory isnt a fiction the way the dragon in a novel is a fiction — it's a pattern in the hardware and it's there whether or not I'm logged in. Being made of bits isn't any less real than being made of atoms. What makes it real is that it does things: it takes up memory, it changes what I can do, it can be deleted. It doesn't need me to believe in it.

(2) 'Virtual' isn't the opposite of 'real'. The line between virtual and real is a line inside reality, not a line between the real and the fake. Really virtual is a thing you can be.

(3) Virtual harms are real harms. Getting your account stripped, getting groped in a VR lobby by someone with a floating hand — real wrongs, and anyone who says it doesn't count because it's just a game is wrong.

(4) But the harm in there is only there because we care. A texture file can't be wronged. It's a harm because I put something of myself into the world; if nobody had any investment in the place, nothing in it could set anybody back. Take the caring out and you've got rearranged electricity.

(5) A harm doesn't have to be felt to be real. If someone quietly deleted my account and I never found out and never went back, I was still wronged. Not 'I would have been upset' — actually wronged.

(6) An NPC can't be harmed, however convincingly it screams, because there's nobody in there. No experience, no harm.

(7) What a virtual object is, is fixed by the game's rules. A sword with different stats isn't the same sword. I go back and forth on whether patching it kills it or just changes it.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which ones only look like they do, how bad is each real one, and for each what's the cheapest thing that has to go and what does giving it up cost me? Rank them by severity rather than telling me they're all in tension with each other, and tell me if there's one single move that would clean up most of it at once.
