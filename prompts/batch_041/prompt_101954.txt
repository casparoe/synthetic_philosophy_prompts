I write about philosophy and science for a magazine, and I've been stuck for a few weeks on a fight that just won't die: the Peirce people vs the James people on truth. Both sides call themselves pragmatists, both roll their eyes when someone says pragmatism just means "whatever works," and yet after a century of arguing they still seem to mean different things by the word truth and to be talking past each other. Peirce, as I understand him, ties truth to inquiry in the long run -- the opinion the community of investigators is fated to land on -- while James ties it to what it's good for us to believe, what pays off, what gets verified in the course of living. The Peirceans think the Jamesians don't really care whether anything is true; the Jamesians think the Peirceans are chasing a ghost they can never actually reach. It's alot of heat and, from where I sit, not much light.

What I want is the document these two camps should have written together instead of yelling. Write me the joint statement a committed Peircean and a committed Jamesian could both put their names to after a genuine adversarial collaboration. I want the claims they can actually agree on -- about truth, about what inquiry is for, about what the word truth is doing in each of their systems, about what neither of them is claiming. Then I want the precise points where they still part, each one worded the way that side would accept it as its own position, not the way its opponent would caricature it. Then the cruxes -- the sub-questions where settling the thing would actually move somebody. I suspect one crux is whether truth is tied to actual verification or to an ideal limit that no one ever reaches, and another is whether part of this dispute is even about a fact or just about how two people are using one word; but you tell me.

For each crux I want a case or argument that both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Let me throw in a few test cases I keep wondering about, use them if they help: a community of inquirers that gets wiped out by an asteroid before it ever converges -- was anything true for them, or only true-in-the-limit-that-never-came? A belief that's indispensable for acting but that nobody could in principle ever check. And a scientific claim that was useful and verified for a long time and then turned out false -- did it stop being true, or was it never true, and does either of them have to say something embarrasing here? Format it as a numbered section of agreed claims, then a disagreements table with the cruxes and the predictions. And please don't just hand me the textbook contrast -- the whole point is formulations that aren't vacuous on one side or slanted toward the other, and an honest call on which parts of this are real disagreement and which are just two guys using "true" for different jobs.
