I'm in a slow reading group working through Strawson's "Freedom and Resentment," and we've gotten stuck on something I can't stop chewing on. Strawson's move, as I read it, is that questions like "is she really responsible?" get whatever sense they have from inside the practice of reactive attitudes -- resentment, gratitude, indignation, hurt feelings -- and that the demand for some further fact that would either underwrite or undermine the whole practice is confused. What I actually want from you is a number, not a survey of positions: your credence that the response-dependent reading is right, i.e. that there is no fact about a person's responsibility over and above the fact that our reactive attitudes toward them are, in the relevant sense, warranted or fitting. Then decompose it.

Here's the wrinkle that's making me doubt the whole thing. My friend Priya thinks the view dies the moment you imagine a community that can flip its reactive dispositions on and off with a pill -- say a reversible modulator that suppresses resentment and indignation for a day without changing anything about how the person will behave tomorrow or how anyone will treat them afterward. She says if responsibility were really constituted by our attitudes, then swallowing a pill would change the moral facts, which is absurd, so responsibility must be tracking something independent of us. I think that's too quick: Strawson can just say the pill changes whether the attitudes are warranted in the sense that matters, and anyway we don't think a pill that stopped you loving your kids would show that love isn't real. So please break the question into the sub-questions you think it actually turns on -- whether the reactive attitudes are conceptually prior to the concept of responsibility or the other way around, whether the "objective attitude" can be adopted wholesale without cost, whether the psychopath and manipulated-agent cases show the practice answers to something outside itself, whether fittingness of an attitude is itself a real property or just a way of talking, and whatever else belongs -- give each a rough probability with one line of justification, show how they combine into your final number, and tell me which single term your least sure about and what evidence would move it most. Also tell me where Priya and I most likely actually disagree, since I suspect it isn't the pill. Put the credence on the last line as \answer{0.xx}.
