Ok so I've been trying to figure out my actual views on when a bunch of stuff adds up to a thing, and my gut keeps contradicting itself.

Cases and gut verdicts:

- Heap of sand on the beach: not a thing, just grains sitting next to each other.
- My bicycle: a thing.
- My two hands pressed together: not a thing.
- The cells of my body: a thing (me, obviously).
- A freshly dead body: still a thing.
- A candle flame: a thing, even though none of its 'parts' survive a second.
- A whirlpool: also a thing.
- The disassembled bike's parts in a pile: not a thing.
- A cloud: yeah, a thing, though barely.
- Everything in my room right now, taken together: definately not a thing.

My best guess at the principle is something like: there's a composite exactly when the parts are causally unified, i.e. mutually maintaining or transmitting structure. That gets the bike, the flame, the whirlpool, the cloud, me, and the corpse. But the hands are transmitting force too, and I don't count them. And the heap vs the cloud bugs me — both are loose aggregates, so why is one a thing and not the other? Maybe the cloud is a self-maintaining convection pattern and the heap isn't, but then my principle is a disjunction (rigid cohesion OR self-maintaining dynamics OR life) and that feels like cheating.

So: is there a principle that gets all ten, or close, and what does it say about a slime mold and a forest? And which of my verdicts does it make me give up? I don't want a principle that's just 'it's a thing if we find it useful to call it one' unless you can show me that's the only one left standing.
