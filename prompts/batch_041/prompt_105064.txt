My brother says nudges are totally fine because they don't take anything off the table — moving the fruit to eye level in the cafeteria doesn't stop me from buying the brownie, it just makes the good choice easier, so it respects your preferences instead of overriding them. But I thought the whole point of revealed prefernce was that you read off what someone prefers from what they actually choose. So if the new layout makes me grab the apple when I used to grab the brownie, then by revealed preference my preference just changed, and saying the nudge "helped me act on my real preference" is circular — you're assuming a preference the choice doesn't show. So which is it: does revealed preference make the nudge defense vacuous, or am I mixing up two different senses of "preference"? I feel like I'm missing something, because the nudge people seem to think its obvious and I can't tell if they're smuggling in a notion of preference that choice-based theory can't even see.
