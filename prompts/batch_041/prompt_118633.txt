I'm a software engineer on the trust-and-safety tooling team at a mid-sized social platform — I write the rules engine, not the philosophy, and I have no formal background in any of this. Our community standards are literally code: a queue of contract moderators works through reported posts, several thousand a day, roughly sixty seconds per decision, with an appeal to a staff lead and a monthly audit of a random sample. Last quarter our policy lead pasted a single sentence into the standards document — the paradox-of-tolerance line, the one that amounts to saying that a community which tolerates the people who want to drive others out will not stay tolerant for long — and it has since become the moderators' magic phrase: anything that makes some group feel unwelcome gets suppressed, and when anyone pushes back, the answer is that the paradox of tolerance requires it. I think the slogan is eating the argument, and I want a procedure instead of a slogan. Three candidate rules are on the table and I genuinely do not know which to adopt. Rule A, the slogan: suppress any post that advocates excluding a group, whether from the platform or from public life. Rule B, the reciprocity rule: suppress a post only if the position it advocates, if it prevailed, would remove the ability of the people it targets to participate on the same terms they now have. Rule C, the argument-first rule: suppression is a last resort, legitimate only where the view has already been answered by argument in public and is gaining ground anyway, or where the post is functioning as an instrument for pushing people out rather than as a position offered for debate. One thing I keep tripping over: the slogan was written about a society and its laws, and we are not a state — we are a private forum with an exit door and a competitor one click away — and I cannot tell whether that makes our bar for suppression higher or lower.

Turn whichever of these rules survives into a decision procedure our moderators can actually run: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with allow and where it stops with suppress, and where it hands off to a human because the question is one of values rather than facts. Three things it has to survive. First, the self-application case: our rule is a rule about suppressing things, so sooner or later the procedure will be pointed at a post arguing that the rule is itself an act of intolerance and ought to be repealed, and I need to know whether it says to suppress that, and if it does, whether that is a reductio of the rule or just a bullet we bite. Second, the moderators cannot see intent — they see a post and a report, not a person — so any step that requires judging what the poster really meant or really wanted will be run on vibes; if the procedure needs such a step, mark it as the place it will misfire. Third, I want the procedure to be honest about the counterfactual it leans on: "would this view, if it became the norm, destroy the norm of tolerance?" is a prediction about a world we will never see, and I would rather have it flagged as a guess than dressed up as a finding. Do not give me a survey of what philosophers have said about tolerance, and no reading list or citations — I cannot check them and I do not want the scholarship, I want the procedure. Take a definite position: pick among my three rules or replace them with a fourth, tell me which you picked and why, and commit to a verdict on every case rather than laying out the options for me to choose from.

Run it on five reports and give me a call on each rather than a discussion: (1) a post arguing that a particular religious group should be barred by law from holding public office; (2) a post calling for every user who belongs to a particular political movement to be banned from the platform; (3) a post arguing that our no-group-exclusion rule is itself intolerant and should be repealed; (4) a post from members of a stigmatized group using contemptuous language about the majority; (5) not a post but a pattern — forty near-identical posts from different accounts, each individually deniable, all naming the same person's employer. At least one of your calls should be one I will not like; I expect it is case 3, and I would rather have a procedure that is wrong in an identifiable way than one that hedges. Then give me a brief structured summary in a single JSON object, placed after the prose, with exactly these fields: 'steps', an array of objects with 'n' (integer), 'question', 'sufficient_answer', 'if_no', 'kind' ('fact', 'value', or 'prediction'), and 'owner' ('moderator', 'lead', or 'policy'); 'verdicts', an array with 'case', 'call' ('allow', 'allow_with_label', 'suppress', 'escalate', or 'pattern_escalate'), 'deciding_step', 'confidence' ('high', 'medium', or 'low'), 'strongest_objection', and 'asker_will_dislike' (true or false); 'self_application', an object with 'suppresses_own_critics' ('yes', 'no', or 'only_if'), 'why', and 'what_changed'; 'misfires', an array of strings, each a situation where the procedure returns the wrong answer or none; and 'sacrificed', an array of strings naming the distinctions you dropped in order to make this runnable in sixty seconds. Keep the prose under about nine hundred words so that the schema is the part I can skim.
