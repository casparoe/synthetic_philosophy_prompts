A friend of mine runs a small reading group that's split down the middle and it's getting tense. One of us is a cliodynamics guy — you know the type: agrarian empires go through these long secular cycles, population grows until it outruns the land, real wages fall, the number of people groomed for elite positions grows way faster than the number of positions actually available, the state's finances strain, and then you get a stretch of instability, civil war, breakdown, and a reset. He'll tell you Ibn Khaldun got there first and that the same machinery is running in the modern US; he also points out that he called the current mess back in 2010, in print, before it happend. The other one is a historian who thinks this is astrology with error bars: the model's knobs get turned until the past fits, the cases arent independent because every polity is in contact with every other one, "elite overproduction" ends up measured by whatever proxy happens to be lying around, and if you smooth any long series of violent events you can find a cycle in it.

They've finally agreed to try an adversarial collaboration and I've been deputized to write it up. So draft the joint statement they could both put their names to.

I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both accept — what's been established, what hasn't, what the framework is actually committed to, what quantification in history can and can't buy you, and what the cycle claim would have to amount to for it to be a real prediction rather than a redescription of the past; (2) the residual disagreements in a table, each position phrased the way its own side would want it phrased; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling the thing would actually move someone. I'd guess at least these are in there: whether retrodiction counts for anything; whether there's any test that is genuinely out-of-sample rather than back-fitted; whether the cases are independent enough for the statistics to mean anything; whether the key variables can be measured without peeking at the outcome they're supposed to explain; and whether a heavy-tailed process with no characteristic timescale could spit out the same patterns — but use your judgement, add or drop what you think.

For each crux, give me a test, dataset, or concrete case that both of them would accept as genuinely bearing on it, and write down what each side predicts about it ahead of time. If they'd end up disagreeing about what "predict" even means, or if part of this fight is really just about words, say which part and why.
