I am a litigator who, after watching a family member be worked over by a high-pressure sales operation, has spent the past year handling undue-influence and fiduciary-fraud cases; the question of how a vastly more persuasive intelligence should speak to a human has therefore stopped being abstract for me. I know the vocabulary of fiduciary duty, informed consent, capacity, undue influence, unconscionability, duress, anchoring, framing, BATNA, reservation price, principal-agent problems, and professional responsibility reasonably well, and I know almost nothing technical about AI. Restate the debate over how superintelligent AI systems should talk to humans in that vocabulary. Treat the main positions as: (1) the disclosure view, on which such a system must announce its persuasive advantage and refuse to exploit it; (2) the paternalist view, on which it may use that advantage for the user's own good when the user consents or lacks capacity; (3) the Socratic view, on which it may ask questions but not assert or steer; (4) the fiduciary view, on which it owes loyalty and candor to the user's reflective ends; and (5) the negotiation view, on which the exchange is a bargaining problem in which the system should seek integrative solutions rather than manufacture consent. Use, among other tools, the distinction between procedural and substantive unconscionability, the therapeutic-privilege exception to informed consent, and the negotiator's concepts of BATNA, anchoring, and reactive devaluation. For each position, give the translation, then the residue report: what carries over cleanly, what has to be distorted to fit, and what cannot be said at all in my vocabulary. I especially want to know whether concepts like epistemic autonomy, manipulation, and the difference between persuading someone and bypassing their agency have any genuine counterparts in fiduciary or negotiation theory, or whether they are doing work my vocabulary cannot capture. If any of the five positions is not a stable position but a slogan, say so as part of the residue report. No citations or reading lists; work from the concepts. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
