Me and my flatmate got into a five-hour arguement last night, and it started because I said you can know something without being able to give a reason for it — she's doing her PhD on scepticism and thinks that's just cheating — and by about 2am I wasn't sure I still believed any of it. So before I go back and get flattened again I want a proper audit of where I've actually landed. Here's my profile, roughly. First, I'm fully signed up to the hinge picture from On Certainty: propositions such as that I have two hands, that the earth has been around for a very long time, that my memory isn't systematically lying to me aren't things I know and aren't things I doubt, they're the frame that makes knowing and doubting possible in the first place, and trying to doubt them wouldn't be a more rigorous version of inquiry, it would be the end of inquiry. Second, I don't think hinges are arbitrary or just picked — they're held fast because they're woven through everything we do; you can't get on with counting, or buying bread, or doing physics without them, and that's where their grip comes from. Third, I'm also a completely straightforward realist about them: that the earth has been around for ages is just true, true because of how the world is, not because of us or our practices or what's convenient. Fourth, I think some hinges are local — a friend of mine was raised to treat the river as an ancestor and I really don't want to say she's being unreasonable or epistemically deficient, it's just not my frame — and at the same time I want to say there are hinges nobody can opt out of, like there being other minds, and I thought that was supposed to be the whole point of the view. Fifth, I think the hinge picture is the right answer to the sceptic, that the whole Cartesian performance is a mistake, even though I'll happily admit you can't argue a determined sceptic out of it, because there's no shared ground left to argue from. And sixth, a concrete case that keeps nagging at me: when I read about people who think illness is caused by bad air or moral failing rather than germs, I want to say flat out that they're wrong, not that they're working within a different frame — but when I imagine a community that's lost a hinge and picked up a different one, I don't want to call that a mistake at all, just a different form of life.
