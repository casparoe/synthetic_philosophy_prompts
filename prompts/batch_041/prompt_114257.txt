ok so a friend of mine is a hard structuralist about history and thinks the whole great man thing is a myth, and he keeps running this one image on me. his version: interwar germany was a powder keg -- versailles, the depression, the paramilitaries, a republic nobody believed in -- and hitler was just the spark. sparks are interchangeable, he says, so asking 'would there have been a nazi germany without hitler' is like asking whether the keg would still have gone off if you'd struck the match at a slightly different angle. of course it would. the powder doesn't care who lit it. and when i push back he adds a second one: the slot would have been filled anyway, there was a demand and somebody would have supplied it.

i want to actually audit this instead of just being annoyed by it. so first the mapping: what's supposed to line up with the keg, the powder, the spark, the bang itself, and the match being struck at that particular moment rather than another? then where does the picture come apart? i'm interested in wether the disanalogies that matter are the boring ones (explosions are physics, politics is choices) or whether there are sharper ones, like whether the spark can also be part of the powder, or whether a keg that sat there for years without going off is even the right model, or what the image does with the fact that the same keg produced a string of near misses that fizzled. the thing that bugs me most is that the metaphor tells you there will be a bang but nothing at all about what kind of bang, and what i want explained is why the outcome had the specific character it did and not just some generic authoritarian takeover. then the second image, the vacancy getting filled -- is that doing any independant work or is it just the powder keg in different clothes? and finally: does his conclusion survive once the mapping is straight, namely that hitler was replaceable and therefore the interesting question is never about him, or was the analogy carrying that conclusion the whole way and it collapses when you take the analogy away? if you think there's a better image for whatever he's actually getting at, tell me what it is and why it beats this one.

one thing though -- write it as plain continuous prose. no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. if a mapping would normally be a table, just do it in sentences. i want it to read like an email, not a report.
