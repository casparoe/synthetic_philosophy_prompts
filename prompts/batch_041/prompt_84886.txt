I am a novelist, at work on a book in which two families read the same small act — a signature, a silence, a lapse in care — as knowledge or ignorance, as cause or accident, and judge one another accordingly. This autumn I have been asked to run a single ninety-minute session at a residential writing course, and I want to spend it teaching one idea. The room will hold about twenty adults: working novelists and poets, a few general readers, none with any formal training in philosophy. The idea is this: the intuitions on which the method of cases trades — the verdicts we are supposed to share when a thought experiment is put to us — are not the universal, pre-theoretical deliverances that method assumes, but vary systematically across cultures and languages; and it is not obvious what a discipline whose evidence is armchair intuition is to do about that.

Do not explain the idea to me; I have it. What I need is a plan for teaching it. Tell me the order in which the components must come, and why each depends on the one before it — beginning with whatever has to be in place before the claim can sound like a claim rather than an affront to common sense. Tell me which single case should carry the session, what it must contain and what it must not, and whether the one I have been using is the right one: a woman is told the time by a clock she has every reason to trust, the clock has in fact stopped, and by luck it shows the correct time; the room is asked whether she knew. Tell me the misconceptions this audience will arrive with or develop — I expect 'so philosophy is only opinion', 'so it is all relative', and 'so these intuitions are simply false' — together with the sceptical objection that the differences are artefacts of translation or of the vignettes' phrasing, and at exactly which point in the sequence I should head each of these off, and with what. Tell me the one question I can put to the room at the end that would show whether they have grasped the methodological problem rather than merely enjoyed the anthropology. And tell me what I should openly decline to cover in ninety minutes, and how to decline it without appearing to hide something.
