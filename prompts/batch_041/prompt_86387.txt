I want a credence, with the reasoning laid out, on a question in the units-of-selection debate. Take this proposition, which I have stated so that it has a truth value:

P. At least one trait in nature cannot be causally explained without citing selection among groups as a cause — that is, there is a case in which intervening on a group-level variable (group composition, colony or deme productivity, the distribution of helpers) shifts the frequency of a heritable trait, and no intervention on individual-level variables within the group does the same work, so that the group-level term is eliminable from a predictive model but not from a correct causal one.

Stipulate that the algebra is not the issue: multilevel gene-frequency accounting, inclusive-fitness or neighbour-modulated formulations, and contextual-analysis regressions can be made numerically to agree wherever they are all defined, so the equivalence of the equations settles nothing by itself. Read "cause" in the interventionist sense just given, and read a re-description as mere bookkeeping if it keeps the predictions while dropping every term that refers to the group as such. Argue from the concepts; I don't want citations, reading lists, or names to lean on.

Answer as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, with no preamble and no closing summary. Item 1 is your overall credence in P, as a number, together with one sentence on how you combined the terms and one naming which term carries the most weight and what would move it. Items 2 through 5 are the sub-questions that number rests on: whether the interventionist reading of cause is the right one for evolutionary biology, or whether causal claims there are always relative to a model and a set of interests; whether any real group-level variable can satisfy the manipulability condition, given that group composition is itself constituted by the behaviour of individuals; whether the equivalence results show that every group-level causal claim has a matching individual-level one and so is always eliminable in principle; and whether the population structures that would make P non-vacuously true — nonlinear fitness interactions, heritable group-level variance, limited dispersal — are common enough in nature to matter. For each of items 2 through 5, give a credence and one line of justification.
