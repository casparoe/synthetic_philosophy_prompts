I am a high school student in an ethics and technology club, and we have been discussing longtermism and the difference between existential risk and suffering risk. I think I understand existential risk well enough: it is the risk that humanity goes extinct or that our long-term potential is permanently destroyed. But suffering risk, or s-risk, is confusing me in a way I cannot resolve. The way I have heard it described, an s-risk is the risk that the future contains astronomical suffering. My first thought is that this is just the risk that sentient beings suffer, which is guaranteed in any future with sentient beings. So either the term is picking out something much narrower, perhaps suffering of a certain intensity or scale, or people who worry about s-risks are committed to the idea that any suffering at all is an unacceptable outcome, which seems like a very extreme position. I also wonder whether, if you really wanted to minimize suffering, you would have to prefer human extinction, since no future beings means no future suffering. I do not think that is what s-risk researchers mean, but I cannot see where my reasoning goes wrong.

Could you diagnose my confusion and explain what an s-risk actually is, why it is not just the risk of any suffering, and whether the extinction implication follows? Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
