So my ethics reading group has been deadlocked for months over the spectrum arguments, and I want to force the two loudest people in it to actually produce something together instead of sniping. On one side is someone I'll call the transitivist: she thinks "better than" is transitive no matter what, so any spectrum that seems to show otherwise has to contain a false step, a vague boundary, or an equivocation somewhere, and she's fine paying that price. On the other side is the intransitivist: he thinks the spectrum is a real counterexample, that the transitivity axiom is the thing that has to go, and that this is a surprising result about the structure of value rather than a reductio of the premise we'd like to keep. They've been talking past each other for about a year and I suspect a fair bit of it is a word fight.

Can you draft the joint statement the two of them could both actually sign? What I'm after is: first, a numbered list of claims both accept -- about what a spectrum argument is and isn't, about the specific ladder we keep dragging out (a chain of lives or worlds where each rung is only marginally better than the next along one dimension while markedly worse along another, running from something genuinely wonderful down to a life barely worth living), about what such an arguement can and can't establish on its own, about the relationship between "better than" and "at least as good as" (and which of the two a transitivity failure would have to show up in), and about whether the relevant failure would have to be an actual cycle or could just be a gap where the two options aren't comparable at all. Then a disagreements table: for each remaining difference, the claim written in wording that each side would accept as a fair statement of its own position, the crux underneath it, and for each crux a concrete test or case that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance -- for instance, what a money pump built from a particular triple off the ladder is supposed to demonstrate, whether it should budge either of them, and whether "parity" or incommensurability is a genuine third relation or just a fancy name for our ignorance about which option is better. And if you decide part of their dispute is merely verbal, tell me which part and why.
