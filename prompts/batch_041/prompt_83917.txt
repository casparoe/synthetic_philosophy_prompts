I am presenting a paper at a performance studies colloquium, and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The room is mostly theater historians and performance theorists for whom Rancière's attack on the passive spectator, in The Emancipated Spectator, has long since settled the question, plus two philosophers of art and one historian of eighteenth-century French theatrical institutions who knows the Genevan quarrel of the 1750s far better than I do. My thesis is that Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert has been misread as moralism about what plays depict, when the argument is really about the structure of the spectator's position — bodies that cannot intervene, feeling discharged onto fictional persons rather than fellow citizens, each spectator alone in a crowd — and that on this reading his proposal of civic festivals, in which the spectators themselves become the spectacle, is the argument's test case rather than an embarrassing appendix. My closing section transposes the claim to contemporary spectatorship. Give me the objections I will actually get from this room, phrased the way each of these three kinds of interlocutor would phrase them and ordered by how much damage they do, what I should concede up front, what I should hold, where each reply of mine is soft, and the one question I am hoping nobody asks.
