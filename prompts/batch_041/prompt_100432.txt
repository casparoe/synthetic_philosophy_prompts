I cover science and ideas for a general-interest publication, and I keep running into the same puzzle on my own beat: how my sources' fields decide what counts as a result. Across much of biomedical and social research, a finding becomes a finding only once a null-hypothesis significance test crosses the p < 0.05 line. Journals, funders, ethics boards, and the press offices I deal with all treat that threshold as the boundary between there is an effect and there is no effect, and the replication crisis is now widely described as a story about what that threshold rewards. Yet those same fields simultaneously push preregistration, effect sizes, confidence intervals, and, in some quarters, Bayesian alternatives. I would like you to reconstruct the philosophical commitments this practice presupposes.

Specifically: what conception of evidence does treating a single p-value as the line between a real effect and a null one presuppose? The practice seems to draw on two traditions at once -- one in which the p-value is a continuous measure of evidence against a hypothesis, another in which it is merely a decision rule with a controlled long-run error rate. Those are not the same thing, and they license different verdicts in the same experiment. Does the actual practice presuppose one of them, both, or a hybrid that is quietly inconsistent? What view of induction does reading p > 0.05 as evidence of no effect require, and how would a likelihoodist or a Bayesian handle the same data differently? Which rival accounts of inference does the convention rule out outright, which does it merely sit uneasily with, and where is it genuinely underdetermined, so that several theories would license the same reporting behavior?

Finally, separate what the practice strictly requires from what it has merely grown accustomed to, and tell me what would have to change -- in journals, in preregistration, in how results are reported to people like me -- if the field replaced the presupposed view with an explicitly Bayesian or likelihoodist one. I am after the implicit theory, not a defense of any camp, and I would rather you mark the points where the practice is philosophically silent than invent a doctrine it never held.
