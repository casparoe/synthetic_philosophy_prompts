*Epistocrats and epistemic democrats both hold that political decisions should be judged by whether they get things right. Explain what actually divides the two positions, and then say how much of each side's case survives once you ask what "getting it right" would have to mean in practice.*

And here is her answer, transcribed as she wrote it:

*Both sides agree that there is a right answer to political questions — if there weren't, the whole debate would be pointless. What divides them is who gets to decide. Epistocrats want decision rights allocated according to competence: votes weighted by how much the voter knows, or an expert chamber with a veto over legislation that fails some basic test, or similar arrangements. Epistemic democrats want everyone included, but they justify that on truth-tracking grounds rather than because participation is valuable in itself — the point is that inclusive procedures tend to get things right, not that they express anyone's will. So the disagreement is about the mechanism, not the standard.*

*The epistocrats' case is basically empirical. There's a large literature on voter ignorance, and even if you think it's overstated, it's hard to look at it and conclude that the average voter's competence sits comfortably above the threshold. The democrats' best reply is Condorcet's jury theorem: the more voters you add, the more likely the majority is to be right, and the only condition the theorem really needs is that each voter be better than a coin flip. On that basis a mass electorate should out-decide any small panel of experts, which is why handing decisions to the knowledgeable would be self-defeating.*

*The democrats' other reply is that experts disagree about basic political questions, so there's no clean way to measure the competence the epistocrat wants to distribute power by. And even if we grant that an epistocracy would make better decisions, that would simply show that its decisions are legitimate — the objections that remain are practical ones, like who writes the competence test and how you stop it being rigged by whoever is already in power.*
