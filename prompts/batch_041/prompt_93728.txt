Some context before the question: I am a novelist, a year into a book whose whole effect rests on interiority — a first-person narrator who gives a fluent, plausible account of her own motives and is wrong about them in ways she cannot see. The craft problem and the philosophical problem have collapsed into each other for me, and I want help deciding where to spend my thinking.

Suppose I have a year of drafting left and no appetite for a reading list; what I want is a working agenda. Which open questions about the reliability of introspection actually deserve a novelist's attention, ranked, with your reasons? I am after the upstream ones — questions whose answers would constrain how I write the gap between what my narrator says and what is happening in her, and questions on which a novelist's ordinary assumptions (that people misdescribe their motives in patterned and legible ways; that a confession is evidence about the confessor; that inner speech is a window rather than a construction) turn out to be exactly what is in dispute. I would also like the triage to separate three errors that usually get run together: misreporting a state, misidentifying the cause of a state one reports correctly, and there being no determinate state to report in the first place.

One framing I keep returning to, which I offer as a candidate and not a conclusion: that introspection is less an instrument reading than testimony from a witness who is also a party to the case — which would make the central question one about interested testimony rather than perceptual acuity, and would put the report and the state reported under a single act. If that reframing is as fertile as it feels, say why; if it collapses into a distinction older and duller than I think, say that too, and tell me what to replace it with.

Defend the ordering by your criteria — how much downstream work depends on each question, how tractable it is for someone who reasons rather than runs experiments, whether an answer would change a sentence I write — and name the questions I should leave alone precisely because nothing downstream turns on them.
