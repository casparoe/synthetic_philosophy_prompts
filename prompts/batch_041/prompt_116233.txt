Some friends and I got into an arguement at a barbecue over the weekend about whether pleasure and pain can even be put on the same scale, and I've been chewing on it all week, mostly when I should have been reviewing pull requests. I'm a backend engineer, zero formal philosophy training, but here's where my gut actually lands, case by case. Sitting through a two-hour all-hands that could have been an email in exchange for a really good sandwich: fine, I'd take that trade every time. A moderate sunburn in exchange for a genuinely great week of vacation: also fine. A million people having a mild headache for an afternoon versus one person with a broken arm for a week: I think the million headaches are worse, and I'd break the arm to prevent them. But a million people with a mild headache for an afternoon versus one person being tortured for an afternoon: not close, no trade, and I don't think any number of mild headaches piled on the other side changes that. Here's the one that keeps me up though: one person spending a decade in moderate, unrelenting, treatment-resistant depression, in exchange for a billion people never getting a cold again for the rest of their lives. I've flipped on that one four times today and I can't get a stable read. And when I try to actually name the number of mild headaches that equals one broken arm, I stall out — it feels like there's no fact of the matter, and that whatever number I'd give secretly depends on who the people are, which I don't think it should.

A friend who minored in philosophy says what I've got is a 'lexical threshold' view: below some level of badness, pain and pleasure really are on one scale and you can trade them at some exchange rate, but above that level the badness is lexical — nothing pleasant, however much of it, buys it off. I want to know whether that's genuinely the best principle for what I said, or whether something simpler fits better and just makes me give up one of my verdicts. I'm suspicious of it anyway, because of the notch problem: if there's a line, then a pain a hair below it is fully payable in pleasures and a pain a hair above it is payable by nothing at all, which sounds like nonsense, and I can't see how to patch that without losing the whole idea. There's also the thing where I happily add up mild pains against a broken arm but refuse to add them up against torture, and I can't tell if that's one principle or two stitched together.

So: give me the principle, or two or three candidates, stated as precisely as you can, and tell me which of my cases forces each clause. Then tell me what your version says about cases I haven't thought of — throw a few at me — and be honest about which of my verdicts you have to throw overboard to make it work. Please don't send me a reading list or a bunch of names to go look up, I won't follow up on it; just reason it out on the page.
