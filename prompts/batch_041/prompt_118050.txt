I have recently become responsible for welfare at a small aquaculture operation — finfish and a prawn line — and it has pushed me into a position I did not expect to hold: that the animals we farm are sentient enough to be owed a genuine welfare regime rather than a mortality-and-growth regime, and that the burden of proof sits with those who deny it. I want the defeaters, ranked, and I especially want the ones a committed defender would concede rather than deflect. Treat the position as a package: an evidential claim (avoidance behaviour that analgesics blunt, plus willingness to trade a preferred resource against a noxious stimulus, gives us as much reason to posit pain here as we have for animals nobody doubts), a threshold claim (sentience rather than cortical architecture is what matters morally), and a practical claim (it should already change how we stock, slaughter, and grade these animals, under uncertainty). Be blunt about which part has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and say what would have to happen for me to be wrong in a way I would actually notice. I would find most persuasive a defeater that does not simply reassert a substrate requirement, since I take that to be the weakest form of denial. No citations or reading lists — argue from what the position commits me to. Give exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, ordered from fatal to merely awkward, with no introduction or conclusion.
