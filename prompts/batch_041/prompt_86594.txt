My friend and I argued for most of yesterday evening about whether she should find out whether her former partner had been unfaithful, years ago, in a way that would change nothing she now does. Her view was that she would rather not know: the knowing would cost her something and buy her nothing. Mine was that if it truly could not change anything she would do, then it could not be worth anything to her either, and so she had nothing to lose by finding out. Neither of us could say what was wrong with the other's argument, and I suspect we were running two senses of worth together.

What I keep circling is that the worth of a piece of information does not seem to be a property of the information. A fact can overturn something I have believed for years and be worth nothing, because no choice of mine turns on it; a barely perceptible shift in odds at the exact point where I would change course can be worth a great deal. The worth also seems to depend on what else I know: learning that a coin came up heads is worth nothing to me until I also learn that the prize is paid on heads, at which point the same fact becomes decisive. So I cannot price items of knowledge one at a time, and the prices do not add up. There is also a temporal asymmetry I cannot make sit still. Looking forward, it seems I can never be made worse off in expectation by learning something, provided the learning is free and I go on choosing as well as I can. Looking back, I plainly can be made worse off, since I cannot unlearn it and may be bound to act on it.

Is there a name for this cluster, or for any of its pieces — the worth of information being a property of the decision rather than of the information, the failure of additivity across items, the forward-looking guarantee and the conditions it needs? I take it decision theorists have a term for what a test is worth before you run it. I want to know how far that term reaches: which of the three pieces above it covers, which it leaves out, and whether my friend's case is a genuine exception to the guarantee or just an ordinary cost of an unusual kind. If some piece has no name of its own, I would rather have a definition and a boundary than a label.
