Ok weird ask incoming but I need to think something through and I can't do it by myself.

I manage the purchasing team at a mid-size manufacturer — not a philosopher, closest I got was one intro course in first year that I dropped. Six weeks ago we switched suppliers on one component. Cheaper by about a fifth. And in the meeting, and again in the email thread, everybody said it out loud: the new supplier's plant discharges into a river. Nobody hid anything. It just wasn't the reason. The reason was money. So we switched, the discharge kept happening, the local paper ran with it, and now the office has split in half. One half says we did it on purpose, that we knowingly poisoned a river to save a buck. The other half says no no, we didn't *mean* it, it was a side effect — the plant was dumping before we showed up and it'll be dumping after we're gone.

Here's the part that's eating me. The exact same fact is a harm to the people downstream and a win to our board (we got a bonus out of the savings), and I've watched people I like flip between "on purpose" and "just a side effect" depending on who's in the room. Nobody's knowledge changed. Nothing about the act changed. Only who was pleased about it changed. And I've caught the same flip in my own head — when it's someone I think is a decent guy I hear "side effect," when it's someone I'm annoyed at I hear "on purpose." I'm the one who signed it off, so I'm not exactly a neutral observer here.

I know some of this has a name. Someone called Knobe, I think — there's an effect named after him — where people will say you brought a bad side effect about on purpose but not a good one. But my case doesn't sit still inside that, because here the side effect is bad for one audience and good for another *at the same time*. Which leaves me with:

(a) "on purpose" is a moral notion all the way down, and my colleagues aren't confused — sorting out who to resent is what the concept is *for*, and the fact that it comes out different for different people is a feature, not a bug; or
(b) they're smuggling disapproval into a claim about somebody's mind, and there's a plain fact about what I intended that has nothing to do with whether you like me, and everyone who says otherwise is making a mistake.

Also relevant: I genuinely didn't care about the river. Wasn't glad, wasn't sorry. It wasn't the point, it was the price. No idea whether that helps (a) or (b).

So: write me a dialogue between two people who've thought about this a lot harder than I have, one arguing (a) and one arguing (b). Some things I want out of it:

- Both of them sharp. I don't want one to be the smart one while the other plays dumb. If one of them is going to come out ahead, it should be on the merits, and each of them should have to give real ground somewhere.
- They should fight about *my* case, not trade general positions. I want to see what each view does with the same outcome being a harm for one crowd and a win for another, and with the fact that I was flat indifferent. And make them say what happens if I flip it — say the new plant happened to be filtering a river nobody asked it to filter. Does anybody in the office say we did *that* on purpose?

I can't look anything up anyway, so don't send me off to read something.
- Real back and forth. One of them says a thing, the other goes after that specific thing, and on from there. Not two monologues stapled together.
