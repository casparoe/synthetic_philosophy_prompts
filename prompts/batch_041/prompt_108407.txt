Here's the seed of the idea. Everyone argues about aesthetic testimony as if it's one thing, and as if the only question is whether you can come to *know* or have *reason to believe* that a thing is beautiful on someone else's say-so. I think that's two questions wearing one coat. There's testimony that hands you a verdict, this film is great, that sonata's a bore. And there's testimony that steers your attention, the kind a good teacher gives you, listen for how the bass line keeps refusing to resolve, watch what the light does when she turns. Call the second kind guidance. My hunch is the pessimist is basically right about verdicts and basically wrong about guidance, and that a lot of the standoff in the literature is people defending one verdict about the whole category when they should be splitting it. But I'm not at all sure the split is clean. Maybe guidance is just verdicts in disguise, maybe verdicts smuggle guidance in through the back door, maybe the whole distinction collapses once you press on what "steering attention" is supposed to be. That's part of what I want the proposal to work out how to test.

So: can you turn this into a proposal? I need the question stated so precisely it has a definate answer, not "are aesthetic judgements autonomous" but something a referee could say yes or no to. I need you to say why the existing positions leave it open, I know there's a pessimist camp and an optimist camp and a few middle roads, but the proposal has to show my split isn't already covered by one of them, or if it is, exactly where. I need a method, what I'd actually do in three months, month by month, and why that's the right method rather than jsut running more of my own intuitions past myself. I need to know what a result would even look like here and what would count as the project failing, I'd rather the panel see I can imagine it going wrong than pretend it can't. And give me an honest scope check, is three months enough or is this secretly a dissertation chapter.

Think carefully and brainstorm the possible framings before you settle on one, I'd much rather watch you discard two or three ways of stating the question than commit to the first one that sounds good. Write it as continuous prose, please, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just the way you'd write an email laying it all out. And don't hedge everything into mush, if you think the split doesn't hold up then say so and propose the nearest thing that does.
