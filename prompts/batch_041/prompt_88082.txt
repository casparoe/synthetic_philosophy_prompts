Ok so quick background: I'm a backend engineer, I've never taken a philosophy class in my life, and I've fallen down a rabbit hole about picking politicians by lottery instead of electing them. Jury duty but for parliament. Could you write me a short story set in a country that actually did this?

Here's the setup I want, and this is the part I care about, so please don't smooth it into a generic "random people govern" story:

The country draws its parliament by lot every two years. No campaigns, no real parties, and once you've served you can't be drawn again for ten years, so there's no career in it. Refusing is free: if you're drawn you get a week to say no thanks, nobody asks why, no penalty, and that's it. Fine so far.

But here's my invention, the second body: anyone who refuses goes into a seperate pool. At the end of the drawing, nine names come out of that refusal pool, and those nine become the Audit. The Audit can't propose anything, but nothing the parliament passes becomes law without a majority of the Audit signing off. Same two-year term, can't be removed, and they get paid the same as parliamentarians. So the people holding a veto over the government are, by construction, the ones who turned the job down.

I want the story to follow one person who refuses and then gets drawn for the Audit anyway, and ends up having to actually use that power over something concrete that affects people they know.

And I want real arguments in it, not just vibes. I specifically want characters to fight about:

- whether the Audit's veto is a bug or secretly the smartest part of the system
- what sortition's legitimacy actually rests on. Is it that a random body is a statistical mirror of the population? Or that everyone has an equal claim to rule? Because if it's the mirror thing, the Audit is a catastrophe, refusers aren't a random sample of anybody. And if it's the equal-claim thing, does refusing disqualify you, or maybe kind of qualify you?
- accountability, when nobody in either body ever faces a voter and the only feedback loop is the lot itself.

Make it feel like a real country with real adminstrative mess and bored civil servants, not a thought experiment. And don't lecture me in the last paragraph, let the arguments come out of what the characters actually want. Maybe 1500 words or so.
