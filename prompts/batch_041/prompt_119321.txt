Two of my friends got into an argument at a birthday dinner and I was supposed to referee and made a mess of it, so I'm handing it to you.

The setup: a friend of ours, her uncle runs a gallery, and he's offered her money to come to genuinely love a particular painter's work. Not to pretend — he wants a relative who really shares his taste, so he pays for the gallery trips and the art history course, and says he'll keep paying as long as the love is real. She took him up on it.

Jonah's line was that the money is no reason to love the paintings. It's a reason to want to love them, or to go and look at them until maybe you do. A reason for an attitude has to be about the paintings; the cheque is about her. Marta said that can't be all of it — if the looking did work, then the money is part of why she loves them, and we'd say she loves them for a bad reason, not that it isn't a reason at all. She added that if a doctor paid you to take a pill that made you cheerful, nobody would say the money isn't a reason to be cheerful.

Jonah: "for a bad reason" is exactly the phrase — the money explains how the attitude got there, it doesn't justify it. Marta: for love, what is justifying, other than pointing at the paintings? If she can do that sincerely, the love is in order. Jonah: then she's fooling herself, because we know the actual story. He kept calling the money the wrong kind of reason; she said that label is empty unless it's doing some specific work.

Marta then tried a test: hold the paintings fixed, take the money away, see whether she'd still love them. If yes, the money wasn't doing the work; if no, the love was bought. Jonah said the test proves too much, since take away her upbringing and her temperament and she wouldn't love them either, and nobody calls those the wrong kind of reason.

Marta came back with: the point isn't money versus no money, it's whether what she loves is tracking the paintings or tracking the cheques. Which is why it matters what we'd say if the uncle had paid her to love a much worse painter and she now loved that one instead.

Neither of them budged and I couldn't tell who had the better of it.
