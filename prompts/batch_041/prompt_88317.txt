I am a high school student preparing a comparative case-study essay for a course on environmental policy and political theory. I have chosen the contrast between Germany's decision to exit nuclear power and France's decision to retain and now expand its nuclear fleet. I know the rough outlines: Germany's anti-nuclear movement has deep roots, the phase-out was accelerated after the Fukushima accident in 2011, and its last reactors closed in 2023; France built its fleet after the oil shocks of the 1970s, still derives most of its electricity from nuclear, and has recently announced plans for new reactors. I also know that both countries say they are committed to decarbonization and to the energy transition, and that both face the so-called energy trilemma of security, affordability, and sustainability. What I want is not a history of either program but an analysis of the episode through theory.

Please act as an expert at the intersection of science and technology studies, comparative energy policy, and political theory. Analyze the German and French trajectories through two lenses. The first is the idea, associated with Ulrich Beck, that modern societies are increasingly organized around the production and distribution of risks rather than the distribution of goods, and that expert institutions that once promised to control hazards instead become sources of contestation. The second is the precautionary principle, understood as the claim that lack of full scientific certainty should not be a reason for postponing measures to prevent serious or irreversible harm. As you work, pay special attention to the distinction between risks that can be assigned probabilities and uncertainties that cannot, and to whether the two countries were responding to the same kind of thing or to different things wearing the same name. Map the relevant actors (governments, utilities, regulators, expert bodies, publics, social movements), the options they faced, and the risks as each side framed them. Then say which features of the two national trajectories each theory explains well, where it misdescribes them or falls silent, and what the comparison shows about the theories themselves: does the case confirm them, complicate them, or expose a case they never anticipated? I want the analysis to run in both directions, with the theories illuminating the cases and the cases testing the theories. Where a theory cannot account for something, say so rather than forcing it to speak.

Before you write the analysis, brainstorm in a short section: list the actors, the risks, and the possible readings of each country's choice. Then give the full analysis. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations, since I cannot look them up. Write for a reader who knows the basic history but not the theory. At the very end, state your verdict in a fixed, machine-readable form: make the final line begin with \answer{...} and contain a short phrase saying whether the case better confirms, complicates, or refutes the risk-society framing.
