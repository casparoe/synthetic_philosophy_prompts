In my ethics reading group last week, a friend disposed of the torture-versus-dust-specks case with a single line: "The needs of the many outweigh the needs of the few." As he set the case up, we are choosing between two outcomes and nothing else is at stake: in the first, one person is tortured for fifty years; in the second, an unimaginably large number of people — more than could be written out in digits — each receive a single dust speck in the eye for an instant. He held that we must choose the dust specks, and he offered the line as the whole of his argument. I would like the line taken apart rather than defended. Set out the distinct claims it could be making, ordered from the trivial to the radical, and say of each whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or plainly false. I especially want to know which of these readings would actually license his verdict on the case, which would underwrite the opposite verdict, and which reading people ordinarily have in mind when they repeat the line. Please lay the readings out as a menu rather than choosing one and expounding it.
