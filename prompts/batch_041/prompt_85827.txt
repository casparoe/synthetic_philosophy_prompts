In Spinoza's political philosophy a commonwealth's right is coextensive with its power, and its power over its subjects grows to the extent that their wills actually agree with its commands; on this basis he presents democracy as the form of state closest to natural liberty, since there the sovereign will is the common will. Argue that this power-based account is by itself unable to establish democracy's superiority to a well-ordered aristocracy, and that Spinoza's preference for democracy therefore depends on a normative premise his metaphysics of power does not supply. Give the argument as exactly five numbered claims, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
