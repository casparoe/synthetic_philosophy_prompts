I am a doctoral candidate in the philosophy of physics, and I am trying to work out what the so-called quantum-immortality thesis actually commits one to. I take it to rest on three components, each of which I would like you to hold fixed for the purposes of this question: first, an Everettian, no-collapse reading of quantum mechanics, on which every physically possible outcome of a measurement is realised in some branch; second, a continuity-of-consciousness view of personal identity across branches, on which my future selves in different branches are all genuinely me; and third, a self-locating rule on which, in considering my own future, I assign no weight to those branches in which I do not survive, so that my own death is never among the outcomes I anticipate. I recognise that the third component is contested, but I want to take it as given and see what it implies.

Please set out the most important implications of this package for practical deliberation. I am particularly interested in three things. First, whether it should change how a rational agent weighs catastrophic risks — both risks borne by the agent herself, such as a medical gamble that is almost certainly fatal but offers a large benefit if survived, and risks borne by others, such as a policy carrying a small probability of global catastrophe. Second, what it implies for the intelligibility of ordinary self-interested risk aversion, and for whether the agent's preferences become in some way inconsistent or non-consequentialist. Third, what it implies for the standard decision-theoretic apparatus of probability and expected utility: in particular, whether the notion of a branch's measure can do the work that probability is meant to do, and what becomes of expected value when the outcomes that would give it its weight are ones the agent never expects to experience. Where your conclusions depend on one or another of the three components above, or where the implications pull in opposite directions, please make that dependence explicit rather than smoothing it over.
