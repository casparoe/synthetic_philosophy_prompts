A group of us have been circling, for weeks now, the question of whether a mind can be assembled out of parts that are each entirely mindless, and the case everyone keeps reaching for is the ant colony. The familiar picture runs like this: a colony regulates its own temperature, redistributes foragers toward the richer food source, chooses among candidate nest sites, repairs itself after damage, and generally behaves like a single thing pursuing ends — and yet no individual ant has the faintest idea what the colony is up to. Each ant follows local rules keyed to what it bumps into and what the last ant left behind, and the intelligence shows up only in the aggregate. So, on this picture, the colony stands to its ants roughly as a brain stands to its neurons: the understanding belongs to the whole, and the parts are more or less interchangeable ciphers whose significance is exhausted by their position in the larger loop. One friend draws a strong conclusion from this. If a colony can be a mind-ish thing built out of insects that individually grasp nothing, he says, then there is nothing metaphysically exotic about a mind built out of transistors that individually grasp nothing; our reluctance to grant the machine a mind is mere parochialism about carbon.

Another friend thinks the analogy is carrying the whole argument and has no right to. Ants, he points out, are not neurons. Each ant is already an agent: it has its own sensory world, its own repertoire of goals, its own stake in how things turn out, and it will defect from the colony's apparent purposes whenever its own diverge. A neuron has no interests to consult and no alternative career. On his view the colony is far better modeled as a market, an economy, or a polity — a society of small agents whose interactions produce order without anyone intending it — and calling it one big mind is a category mistake that flatters the comparison with brains.

What I want is an audit of the comparison as an argument rather than as a bit of evocative prose. Give me the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of the neuron, the synapse, the signal, the wiring, the unified output — then tell me where the mapping is tight, where it is strained, and which of the disanalogies actually bear on the conclusion my first friend wants to draw rather than merely on his vocabulary. Two questions in particular. First, does the colony analogy earn the claim that mindedness can be assembled out of mindless parts, or does it quietly presuppose it — and what exactly does the brain side of the analogy smuggle in (unified output, a single body, a sensory bottleneck, components with no independent viability, an integrated memory) that the colony side lacks, or vice versa? Second, if the colony is indeed better understood as a society or a market than as a brain, does that defeat the emergence claim or merely relocate it — and if it relocates it, what would the better source domain be, and what conclusion would that analogy actually license? Conclude with your own verdict on each of my friends, and say plainly what the ant colony is and is not good evidence for.
