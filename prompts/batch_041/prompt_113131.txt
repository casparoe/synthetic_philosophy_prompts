I recently came across a blog post explaining Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes, and I want to know how much of it I can trust before I cite it in a seminar paper. The post says:

"Lakatos's key innovation was to solve the problem of how science progresses by combining Popper and Kuhn. Like Popper, he thinks falsifiability is the mark of science; like Kuhn, he thinks scientists work within paradigms. His solution is the research programme: a set of theories with an unfalsifiable hard core that scientists refuse to abandon, surrounded by a protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses that absorb the shocks of experiment. A research programme is progressive if it predicts novel facts and degenerating if it only accommodates facts after they are discovered. Once a programme is degenerating, Lakatos says it is irrational to continue working on it — that's what distinguishes his view from Kuhn's relativism. The hard core is unfalsifiable because scientists have decided, as a matter of faith, that it is true; the protective belt is where the real empirical action is. Lakatos's sophisticated falsificationism is just Popper's falsificationism with the added insight that theories are never tested in isolation. So when a programme's hard core is contradicted by experiment, the rational response is to abandon the programme and adopt a rival that is not contradicted."

I know Lakatos is usually read as more subtle than this, but I don't know enough to say exactly where the post goes wrong. Could you go through it sentence by sentence and tell me what Lakatos would accept, what he would qualify, and what he would reject outright? I'm especially unsure about the claim that a degenerating programme should be abandoned, and about whether the hard core is really a matter of faith. I'd also like to know which of the post's claims, if corrected, would still support its overall picture of Lakatos as a kind of Popper-Kuhn synthesis, and which would not.
