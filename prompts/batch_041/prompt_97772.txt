I dropped out of school at 16 and I've never read any proper philosophy, so go easy on me if I say something daft, but I've been chewing on this for a while and I can't get it straight in my head.

About two years ago I started a Sunday evening group at our community centre. Eight of us, mostly retired, a couple of younger ones. What we do is take a biography of someone we admire — at the moment it's a book about a local woman who ran a homeless shelter here for about thirty years, before that it was a wartime nurse, before that some Victorian reformer — and we read a chapter a week and then sit around trying to work out what we should take from it for our own lives. She did X when Y happened, so what does that look like in my job, etc.

I thought it was going well. But lately I've got this nagging worry I can't put into words. It's like the more we study these people the more they turn into statues. We sand off everything messy, we explain away the bits where they were a nightmare to their own families, and then we hold up this polished thing and say, right, be like that. And it sort of works — everyone leaves feeling inspired — but I don't think anyone actually changes, and I'm not even sure the lessons we draw are the right ones. One week we decided the lesson from the shelter woman was "be stubborn", the next week from the war nurse it was "don't be stubborn". I made a joke that every life we read turns into the same saint with different hair, everyone laughed, and then I went home and thought, is that not the whole problem?

So what I'm asking is whether this is a real way of learning how to live or whether I'm fooling myself and my friends. And if it is real, how do you do it properly — how do you read a life so it actually does something to you instead of just making you feel warm for an hour? And is the statue problem my fault for picking the wrong people, or is it built into the whole idea?

Be straight with me about what's actually settled on this and what people who've thought about it properly still argue about — don't just hand me one camp's answer as if everyone agrees.

Give it back to me as a JSON object with these fields: "the_real_question" (the general problem my little group is an instance of, in plain words), "the_name_for_it" (what people who study this call it, if there's a name), "settled" (array of things that are broadly agreed by people who actually know the topic), "contested" (array of objects with "view" and "who_roughly_holds_it"), "whats_happening_in_my_group" (your diagnosis of the statue problem), "what_to_do" (array of concrete suggestions for how to run the reading differently), and "where_this_answer_is_weakest" (the bit I should be most suspicious of). British English, please.
