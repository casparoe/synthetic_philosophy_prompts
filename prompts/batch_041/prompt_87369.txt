ok so for my history class we have to do a project on local history using primary sources and i picked the county home (the old poorhouse, they tore it down in the 60s, my grandma used to tell stories about it). i went to the historial society and read the admission books and the inspection reports. the weird thing is how empty they are in some ways. the admission book has a column for "cause of dependency" and for a lot of the women it just says "no husband" or "deserted" and for a bunch of people it says "unknown". nobody is quoted anywhere. there are no letters from the residents, no photos, nothing in their own words. the inspection reports are all about the building, the roof, the coal, the food, whether the matron was doing her job, and almost nothing about the people. one page just lists "inmates" by number.

so at dinner i said i was definately going to write about the people who lived there, and my aunt (she volunteers at the historical society) said thats right, the records are a record of power, the county wrote down what it needed to run the place, so people show up only as problems to be administered, and the silences are the whole point. she said you have to read against the grain: the "no husband" line tells you the county treated a woman alone as a burden, and every gap is a trace of what the record was built to leave out. she also said that being in the book as "no husband" isnt even really silence, its the county talking about her, shes there but only as the county's problem. she said if you only write what the documents say on their face then youve just written the county's side of the story.

then my cousin (hes a grad student in history, hes staying with us) said hes not so sure. he said a gap is only evidence of something if you can say what would have been written down if things had been different. the clerk wrote "unknown" because he had a box to fill in, not because he was hiding anything - if nobody told him why a woman turned up, he wrote unknown. so you cant go from "no cause listed" to "she was abandoned", and you definitely cant get to how she felt about it. he said when you read against the grain you decide in advance what you want to find, and then the gaps turn into whatever you needed them to be. he said the honest move is to read with the grain first - work out what the clerk was actually doing, what his job was, what the form made him ask - and that tells you what the silence is a silence of. and then the silences that are left over are about the office, not about the people.

aunt said but that just makes the office the only subject. she said reading with the grain still means the past is whatever the county happened to notice, and calling that the honest approach is a political choice dressed up as a method, plus you cant have a neutral reading anyway - deciding the forms were boring and bureaucratic is already an interpretation.

cousin said sure, but its the only reading the documents can push back on. if every reading is just interpretation, then the one that finds cruelty in every gap isnt truer than the one that finds paperwork, and in practice reading against the grain lets you tell whatever story you already believed about power. he said also not every silence is power - some things dont get written down because nobody cared enough to write them, or because the record keeper had forty people in line.

aunt said nobody cared and nobody with power cared are the same sentence when the person is in your ledger because they had nowhere else to go. she said he was treating the county's indifference like it was a fact of nature instead of a decision about who was worth the ink. and she said his rule, say what would have been written if things were different, is doing all the work, because he gets to pick which counterfactuals are reasonable and he'll always pick the one where nothing got suppressed.

they went back and forth and i honestly couldnt tell who was winning. can you go through it move by move - the read against the grain bit, the a gap needs a counterfactual bit, the silence of the office vs silence about people bit, the not every silence is power bit - and say whos right about each one, and whos more right overall? and say whats missing, if theres something theyre both not seeing.
