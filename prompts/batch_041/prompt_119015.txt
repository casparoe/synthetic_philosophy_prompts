My talk is on Buridan's ass — the donkey that starves equidistant between two identical bales of hay — and my thesis is basically that the story is a reductio only against a picture of reasons nobody should have accepted in the first place: the idea that a rational act must always be favoured over its alternatives by a reason that tips the balance. I want to argue that when two options are genuinely on a par, arbitrarily picking one is not a failure of rationality; that the puzzle only really bites if you also insist a choice must be *explained* by a balance-tipping reason; and that the ass, if it truly had no tie-breaking dispositions whatsoever, is a limit case of agency rather than a picture of irrationality.
