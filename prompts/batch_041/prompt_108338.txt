I've been chewing on acausal trade alot lately and I've hit the point where I can't tell which bits of the story are load-baring and which are just decoration. The core idea as I understand it: two agents who can't causally affect each other at all still end up cooperating, because each one's choice is evidence, or a logical consequence, of what the other one does. I want a tier list out of this.

Take the candidate mechanisms below and sort them into tiers by how much you'd trust each one to actually get two roughly rational agents to cooperate, assuming both of them know the setup and both know that the other knows it, etc. Use as many tiers as you need and name them whatever you like, give me one or two sentences per entry on why it lands where it does, and at the end tell me which entries you think are really the same mechanism wearing a hat.

- Same universe, both agents can read each other's policy or source code before either one acts, so defecting just isn't in the program.
- No interaction at all, but each knows the other is running the same decision procedure on the same problem, so my choice is evidence about theirs.
- Each agent simulates the other and trades with the simulation, betting that the other is doing the same.
- Their values overlap enough that each would do the cooperative thing anyway, and the trade is just the two of them noticing afterwards.
- Agents in different regions or branches of the multiverse with no causal link whatsoever, each of whom only knows that the other might exist and might reason like them.
- An agent trading with a counterfactual version of itself, the one that made the other choice.
- An agent trading with an agent it could create later (a sim, a successor AI) rather than one that already exists.
- Two agents who can't reach each other, but who'd each have signed the same bargain behind a veil of ignorance about which of them they turned out to be.
- A third party who can't causally punish anyone, but whom both agents nonetheless expect to punish defection against either of them.
- An agent that leaves its policy lying around in the world where a stranger might find it, and cooperates on the assumption that whoever finds it will see it and reciprocate.
- An agent that expects to be duplicated at some point and trades with its future duplicate now, betting the duplicate honors the deal.

Then the part I actually care about most: give me a single number between 0 and 1 for how much of the credibility of the whole acausal trade picture hangs on the one decision-theoretic claim that my choice counts as evidence about what a similar agent chooses. If you think it hangs mostly on something else instead — the existence of counterparties, the structure of the game, the prior over other minds, whatever — then give me the number for that thing and say why it's the load-bearing one.
