My old high school is retiring its honor code, and the head of school has asked me to give this year's commencement address and to actually talk about the change instead of dancing around it. The setup: under the old code, if you saw someone cheat you were expected to turn them in, and there were assemblies where students who'd been caught stood up and admitted it in front of the whole school. The new system is a private conduct board — one-on-one, confidential, no assembly, no audience. Half the room will be alumni and old faculty who grew up under the code, some of whom are still furious about it; the other half are this year's graduates, who never had it and think the whole thing sounds barbaric. One retired teacher keeps saying the old code only worked because everyone boarded on campus and nobody could leave — that shame needs a closed community, and ours stopped being one. I dont know if he's right.

What I want the speech to actually do is take a real position on whether shame or guilt is the better moral emotion, and on whether a school — or a culture — can just decide to switch from one to the other, rather than falling back on "both have their place." It has to land for both halves of the room, and it's a commencement, so it has to send the kids off feeling something rather than feeling lectured at. Write it.
