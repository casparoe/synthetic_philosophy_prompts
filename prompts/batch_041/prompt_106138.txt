I am a PhD student in epistemology, currently working on how testimonial warrant behaves when the testifier is a machine-mediated interface rather than a person, and I keep colliding with a version of the hinge problem that I cannot get to sit still. I would like your credence on one proposition, together with the decomposition that yields it.

Proposition P: For an agent whose entire informational environment is a single machine-mediated interface — picture a researcher for whom every record, instrument reading, and colleague's report arrives through one system she cannot inspect — there exists a possible evidential history in which it would be epistemically rational for her to abandon a proposition that functions as a hinge for her, a proposition she must presuppose in order to conduct any inquiry at all, while continuing to rely on that same interface for everything else.

Three stipulations, so that P has a truth value. First, "hinge" is meant in the Wittgensteinian sense of On Certainty: a proposition that stands fast, that is not itself the object of evidence or doubt, and that is presupposed by the questions we do ask. Whether hinges are propositions or non-propositional attitudes I am treating as a parameter rather than as part of P. Second, "epistemically rational" is meant to be neutral between a justification-based and a mere-entitlement-based reading; state which you are using. Third, the evidential history in question must not already contain, in a form available only once the hinge has been given up, the information that settles the hinge's falsity — the question is whether the transition itself can be rational, not whether the destination is.

What I am asking for:

1. A single credence for P.
2. The sub-probabilities you actually used. At a minimum I expect terms for: the probability that hinges are non-propositional and therefore not the kind of thing that can be rationally revised at all, in which case say whether you score P false or ill-formed; the probability that, if hinges are propositional, the riverbed-and-river picture permits erosion by accumulated experience rather than only wholesale changes of practice; the probability that any such transition is better redescribed as a change in practice that is rational only retrospectively; and the probability that the delegated, single-interface case differs in kind from the ordinary social case, where other people supply critical friction, rather than only in degree.
3. How those terms combine, and whether you regard the combination as a genuine probability or as a weighted verdict over incompatible readings of P. If the latter, give the weights you would defend.
4. The sub-question your number is most sensitive to, and the shape of the argument or evidence that would move it most. I do not want citations; I want the structure of what would shift you.

For what it is worth, my own number is about 0.35, and I suspect I am anchoring on the thought that hinges can be abandoned without being rationally abandoned, which may be no more than a verbal point about "rational". If you think my number is off chiefly for that reason, say so.
