I'm working on something about AI alignment and I keep going back and forth on it. Suppose we build a superintelligent system that has to decide how a big pot of public resources gets used, and it genuinely doesn't know which values to serve. One camp says: aggregate. Give everyone a budget of moral credits, let them spend it on the causes and ways of life they care about, and have the system implement whatever bundle of values attracts the most credits. If someone cares intensely, they can buy credits from someone who cares less; thats a gain from trade and it respects intensity as well as headcount. Call it a moral credit auction. The other camp says: don't aggregate, be neutral. The system should stick to a thin framework of fair rules and not throw its weight behind any particular vision of the good life, because putting fundamental values up for sale means the rich, the organised, or the fanatical can buy the world, and some commitments shouldn't be tradeable at all. I want an essay that takes a side. Weigh the best version of each position, then defend a verdict on whether an aligned AI should run something like the moral credit auction, or whether there's a principled line against commodifying certain values. I'm not after a literature review, obviously. Don't just say it depends; if it does depend, say exactly what it depends on and which way you'd bet under realistic conditions. Start the essay with your verdict in the first sentence, then give the argument. No preamble, no restating the question, no announcing what the essay will do.
