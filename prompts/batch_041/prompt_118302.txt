setting: a small town, basically mine but ill change the names. theres one old cider press thats been run by the same family for like a hundred years, and every october a three-person board tastes the years barrels and decides which ones get stamped "town standard." getting the stamp matters alot — the stamped barrels sell for double, the rest get dumped into vinegar. main character is a 17 year old girl named june whose uncle runs the press. shes on the board this year only because her grandma was on it for decades and died in the spring, so june is filling the seat as a one-year provisional thing.

the boards rule is they certify by taste alone, and a barrel only gets stamped if all three agree. if even one says no, it fails. that rule is what drives the whole plot, so dont let anyone bend it.

heres the stuff i need the story to actually do, but no character can ever give a philosophy lecture, and nobody can name a philosopher, and nobody should say the words "objective" or "subjective" as a thesis (someone can say "subjective" as a dig, thats fine):

june has a weirdly good palate. in barrel 7 she notices two separate off-notes that nobody else can even find. one of the other board members, an old guy whos judged for 30 years, catches one of them. the third member catches neither and thinks theyre both making it up. so youve got a person who perceives more than the others, a person who perceives less, and a disagreement between the two who perceive — and the story should let that be genuinely impressive and also genuinely not decisive.

later the press gets cleaned and they find the actual cause: a leather washer that had gone bad and a rusted iron fitting. so both of them were right and the third person was wrong. but dont let that be a slam dunk. the story isnt about who was right, its about what to do next. does finding the cause make the barrel bad? does it matter that the customers love it? does it matter that june loves it?

the ledger. june finds the towns record of verdicts going back to the 1890s. barrels that failed in the 60s are now spoken of like legends. barrels that got the stamp are now called thin and too sweet. theres one page where the board reverses a verdict from ten years before, and the margin note basically says we were wrong then. june has to sit with whether thats a correction or just a change in fashion. this is the part i care about most. i want the story to take seriously that taste can genuinely get better AND that "better" might just mean "later," and i dont want it to pick one.

june catches herself being a snob. shes sure the third board member has bad taste, and shes kind of horrified to notice how sure she is, given everything else shes been thinking. let that sit too, dont have her talk herself out of it.

ending: june likes barrel 7. its actually her favorite. so her read on whether its good and her read on whether its correct are pulling in different directions, and she has to write a line in the ledger anyway, in ink, with her grandmas pen, and the pen is scratchy and annoying. end on her writing it and on the pen, not on a revelation. dont end with "and maybe beauty is in the eye of the beholder" and dont end with "and maybe there really are timeless standards." end in the mess — a decision gets made, someones hurt by it, the ledger has one more line in it.
