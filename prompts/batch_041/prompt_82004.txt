Write a dialogue between two smart people going at it over whether "national character" is a coherent thing to reason with, and separately whether it's ever ok to say such things out loud. One of them says sure, as long as you read the claims as being about the *shape* of a distribution rather than an average -- what matters is how tight or loose the spread is, how much overlap there is with other peoples, and the average tells you nothing about the person in front of you. The other says that's a dodge: character predicates are for individuals and their more-or-less voluntary dispositions, so the moment you attach one to a whole people you've either said something about institutions or something about blood, and neither is what you meant. Make them actually respond to each other's points. The defender has to say which people he even means -- the diaspora, this generation, the ones in the capital? -- and the skeptic has to say what exactly is broken about the distribution reading, since spread is a real fact about a population. Let the structural idea come up (the traits belong to the arrangement, not the persons; institutions select who gets promoted, and what looks like character is just the filter), and the looping idea (a description of a people can get absorbed and make itself more true -- a reason for care, not automatically a reason to think the description was wrong). And let them fight about the one case that seems to survive everything: where the claim is really about how others treat the group rather than about the group itself. Neither one a strawman, no name-dropping, no citations, i dont want homework. End with a single line starting "Verdict:" saying in one sentence who came out ahead and on which issue.
