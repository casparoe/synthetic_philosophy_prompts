In Germany the state tax offices collect a church tax, a surcharge on income tax remitted to the major churches; one is enrolled as a church member by baptism and remains enrolled and liable until one files a formal declaration of exit at a government office, and the state itself decides which religious bodies are eligible for the arrangement. France, by contrast, constitutionally separates church and state, yet the départements of Alsace-Moselle still operate under a concordat in which the state pays the salaries of the clergy of four recognized religions. What conception of religious membership and consent does the German arrangement presuppose — that affiliation is an inherited status one must actively renounce rather than a voluntary association one must actively join? Is that conception consistent with the religious neutrality the same state professes? Is what is objectionable the collection of a church's dues by the state's coercive machinery, or only the inertia of the default — so that a church levy falling solely on those who have positively affirmed membership would be unobjectionable? Distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely accommodates, say what the French arrangement presupposes instead, and tell me what would have to change in Germany if the voluntary-association model replaced the status model.
