theres a pattern I keep noticing in arguments about automation and I cant find a name for it, and I dont think the obvious names fit.

Heres the shape of it. A workplace automates something. The machine doesnt swallow the whole job -- it takes the middle of it, the part that was the actual recognized skill, the judgment call people got trained for and paid for. What the human keeps is whats on either side: getting the inputs clean enough for the machine to use, and handling whatever comes back out that the machine couldnt deal with, which in practice is the weird cases, the annoyed customer, and the explaining afterwards about why it happened. So the human has been handed the leftovers.

Now the part I cant get past. Everyone calls this upskilling. The job is described as higher-level now, because the routine skilled middle is gone and whats left is supposed to be more advanced. But the humans remaining work is mostly stuff nobody ever called a skill -- cleaning up, soothing people, absorbing blame. Its the residue, and the residue is the worst part, but the story is that the human moved up. And the story does work: its used to argue the worker should retrain on their own dime, that the pay shouldnt rise, that the job is now more interesting so complaining is ungrateful.

Two examples so I dont sound like Im inventing it. Pilots: the autopilot does the flying, the human monitors and then has to account for the one-in-a-million, and were told flying is a higher-level job now. Or an algorithm that flags which patients get scanned: the human now only sees the flagged ones, which are the hard ones, so they get worse at the ordinary ones and only ever see the mess.

I know deskilling is nearby, but that seems to be about the machine replacing the skill outright, not about what the human is left holding. And Ive heard the point that automating the easy bits leaves people the hard bits -- but that one says the human is left with something harder, whereas I think here the human is left with something lower-status and worse and is told its harder. Is there a name for this exact shape -- machine takes the skilled middle, human keeps the unskilled leftovers, and the whole thing gets rebranded as the human rising? If there isnt a name, make one up, but I mostly want the carving: what this is, what it isnt, and how its different from deskilling, from the left-with-the-hard-bits idea, and from just saying the human and machine are complementing each other.
