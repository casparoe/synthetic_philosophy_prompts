And then it clicked that this is roughly the thing philosophers argue about under the name cognitive penetration -- whether your beliefs and expectations can change what you literally see, as opposed to just what you judge or report afterwards.

So here's what I want: restate that debate in my vocabulary, and do it as an actual translation, not a summary with my words sprinkled on top. My vocabulary is inputs and activations, priors and likelihoods, top-down connections, learned invariances, adversarial perturbations, eval sets. When my expectation of a snake in the grass makes me see a snake, is the rendered image different, or is it the same image getting a different caption?

I already suspect one thing is going to be a problem: anything about the same stimulus looking different, because I don't think I have a clean notion of the same input twice. And I suspect the reason philosophers care about this in the first place is that experience is supposed to justify belief, and I don't think I have any notion of justification, only accuracy on a held-out set. For each piece that doesn't come through, tell me whether that's my framework being too thin or the original claim being vaguer than it looked.

Last thing, and it matters more than the rest: is the image/caption framing a good translation, or does it beg the question by assuming there's a clean line between what gets rendered and how it gets described?
