One of my trustees has gotten really into the insect-sentience stuff and is now pushing for us to move a big chunk of the budget to invertebrates — farmed insects, wild insects, the works — on the grounds that there are just so many of them (a billion billion, or whatever the number is) that even a minuscule probability that each one matters adds up to more expected moral value than everything else we fund put together. I dont think the argument is crazy, but I also dont think "there are a lot of them" settles it, and I need to be able to say what would settle it, or at least what would move it.

Act as a philosopher of mind who has also spent real time in the effective-altruism and animal-welfare funding world. Think carefully before you answer — brainstorm a bit first, map the space of positions, and then commit to a ranking rather than hedging at the end.

What I want is a prioritised agenda: which open questions around the scale argument matter most for a decision like ours, in what order, and why. Not a survey, and please dont send me a reading list or a bibliography — I want to know what to fund, what to wait on, and what to ignore. Take seriously the following, which may or may not be worth anything:

- that the scale argument quietly assumes moral weight is linear in (number of individuals) times (probability of sentience per individual), and that the real fight is over whether those two dimensions should be multiplied at all, or whether there's some threshold below which a per-insect probability rounds to zero instead of to "very small";

- whether there's any empirical result — a nociception assay, a behavioural trade-off, some neuroanatomical marker — that would shift our probability of insect sentience by more than an order of magnitude, because if nothing can, then funding empirical work is pointless and we should fund the conceptual work instead.

For each item on your agenda, tell me why it's upstream of the others, how tractable it is for people who aren't going to solve consciousness, what evidence over the next couple of years would move it up or down the list, and roughly what share of a small budget I should put behind it.
