Next month I'm giving a colloquium paper and I want a Q&A prep sheet for the room I'll actually be facing, not the one I'd like. The audience: our department plus a visiting crowd from the law school and the journalism school. Mostly Kantian ethicists (duty of truthfulness, the humanity formula, the whole apparatus), but also two people who do semantics and pragmatics -- Grice, Stalnaker, implicature, that kind of thing -- and they always come to these and always ask the sharpest questions.

My paper argues that the moral distinction everyone draws between lying and merely misleading is basically arbitrary. If two speakers produce the same false belief in the same hearer with the same effort, one by asserting something false and one by asserting something true that implicates something false, the wrongness is the same; our instinct that the liar is worse is a fetish about words rather than a real moral difference. I call it the same-deception-same-wrongness principle and I put the burden on anyone who wants the line to show me what it tracks that isnt just sentence semantics.

So: play the ethicist who also knows the linguistics cold. Think it through before you start -- brainstorm the candidate objections first, then rank them. Give me the objections I'll actually get from this room, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, phrased the way the Kantians will phrase them and separately the way the pragmatics pair will, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft, what I should just concede up front, and the one question I'm praying nobody asks. Skip the citations and reading lists, I know the literature. And if the thesis as I've stated it is muddled or this prep sheet can't honestly be done, tell me that instead of humoring me.
