I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for a long time, and one of the things they train you to do is notice when a distinction you keep reaching for turns out not to have a name. I have one of those now, about church and state, and I'd like help either finding the name or forging one.

Here is what I keep noticing. Take four countries whose church-state arrangements are usually treated as four different kinds of thing. England has an established church — the monarch is its head and some of its bishops sit in the legislature — and yet almost nobody attends. France is the paradigm of separation, and yet the state owns and maintains a great many churches and pays the salaries of teachers in religious schools. Germany has no established church at all, and yet the state collects a tax on the churches' behalf through the ordinary tax system and teaches religion as a normal school subject. The United States constitutionally forbids establishment, and yet has an official motto invoking God, opens legislative sessions with prayers, and exempts clergy from taxes in ways no other occupation enjoys.

What bothers me is not that these arrangements are inconsistent — everyone grants that — but that the same facts can be described, with equal fidelity, as establishment or as neutrality, and which description you reach for is doing rhetorical work. France funding religious schools can be told as the state refusing to privilege any religion, since it funds them all alike, or as the state underwriting religion with public money. England's establishment can be told as the state capturing and taming the church — the church is weak precisely because it is established — or as the church capturing the state. And the ordering of these countries by how religious they actually are cuts across the ordering by how established they legally are, which suggests the thing I am pointing at is not the legal form at all but some effective settlement that the legal form only loosely tracks.

I don't have a word for that effective settlement, and I suspect the reason is that our vocabulary was built for the legal form. Establishment, separation, secularism, laïcité, secularization, religious freedom — I know all of these, and each names either a form, or a process, or a value, and none names the gap I keep circling. Is there an established concept for it, in law or political theory or the sociology of religion, or is this a case where the field simply never needed the distinction and I should coin one? I am not asking for a bare label. I want the carving: what exactly the thing is, how it differs from the nearest concepts, and where its edges are. In particular, I cannot decide whether a system that is voluntary on paper but sticky in practice — say, a church tax you can stop paying only by formally declaring your exit at a government office, so that the churches' revenue tracks the friction of leaving more than it tracks belief — belongs under the same concept or a different one, and I'd like you to say which and why.

Please write this as continuous prose — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, the way you would write a letter — and tell me what would change your mind: which consideration or example would push you toward carving it differently, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
