I'm a novelist, about two-thirds into a book set in a cut-off Martian city that runs its economy by committee, and two of my characters have been arguing for forty pages straight and I've realised I dont actually know where their disagreement lives. He's an old Hayek type: the knowledge you'd need is scattered and tacit and never in one place, prices are a discovery procedure, so a planned economy fails no matter how clever or honest the planners are. She's a young Lange-style market socialist: you don't need the knowledge up front, you need shadow prices and a board that iterates -- steel shortage, raise the price, try again -- and anyway, she says, he's already conceded that a big enough computer could grind out the equations, so the real fight has to be somewhere else.

Write me the joint statement those two could both put their names to. Numbered claims they both accept first -- what the debate was ever actually about, what would even count as a working planned economy, which of the classic arguments are now dead -- then the live disagreements, each phrased the way its own side would want it, then the cruxes. I have a hunch the crux is whether prices transmit information or manufacture it, but I might be wrong, so tell me if I've misplaced it. And for each crux give me one concrete case from the novel -- the blackout, the platinum, the drug shortage, the water ration -- that both of them would agree bears on it, with what each of them predicts written down before the scene plays out.
