I am a high-school senior preparing a short poem for our philosophy club’s unit on statistical reasoning, and I would like the poem to be about Simpson’s paradox without merely naming it. Please write a villanelle, or another fixed form with a refrain, in which the refrain first reads as a straightforward trend and then, after the turn, is revealed to depend on how the groups were weighted, so that the same words now carry the opposite meaning. Set it in an ordinary school case: each grade’s average reading score improves, yet the school’s overall average falls because the lower-scoring grade became a larger share of the school. Keep the arithmetic coherent, keep the voice that of a student who trusted the aggregate, and let the form do the work of showing that the reversal is not a contradiction but a lesson about what a summary hides. The poem should not end by simply saying that correlation is not causation; it should make the reader feel the difference between a weighted average and a causal effect.
