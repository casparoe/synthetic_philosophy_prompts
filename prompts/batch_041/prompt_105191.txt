In the long argument over the British Raj — the ledger-keeping defence of empire usually associated with Niall Ferguson, and the rebuttal usually associated with Shashi Tharoor — both sides keep reaching for the same figure: the empire as an enterprise whose net effect can be booked. Assets on one side (railways, a unified legal order, a common language of administration, free trade, the suppression of practices like sati and thuggee, famine relief); liabilities on the other (fiscal extraction, deindustrialisation, famine mortality, partition); and a bottom line that comes out either in credit or in the red. I want an audit of the figure itself rather than another round of the substantive quarrel.

Treat "the Raj as a firm with a balance sheet" as an analogy being asked to carry an argument, and tell me how far it holds. I want the correspondence made explicit: what plays the role of the reporting entity, the reporting period, the unit of account, the assets and liabilities, the equity holders, and — the part that usually goes unstated — the counterfactual baseline against which any entry is scored as a gain or a loss ("compared to what: the Mughal successor states, a different coloniser, no coloniser at all?"). Then tell me where the mapping breaks: the absence of a single entity to keep the books, the incommensurability of a railway and a famine death in one unit of account, the distribution problem (an aggregate net gain compatible with a catastrophic loss for the many and a windfall for the few), the way a balance sheet presupposes an owner with standing to be enriched, and the treatment of effects that fall outside the period or outside the parties. Separate the breaks that actually threaten the net-credit conclusion from those that are merely untidy. Say plainly whether the conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it all along — and, if it was, what figure would do the work better (a leveraged buyout, a public-goods provider, a criminal restitution, something else).

A few things I want from the answer. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this history and this accounting; do not present a contested framing as the consensus. And give me the result as structured data rather than an essay: a single JSON object with these fields —

- "analogy": the figure under audit, stated in one sentence.
- "correspondence": an array of objects, each with "ledger_role" (entity, period, unit of account, asset, liability, equity holder, baseline), "empire_element" (what fills it), and "comment" (how clean the fit is).
- "breaks_that_matter": an array of objects with "feature", "why_the_mapping_fails", and "bearing_on_the_net_credit_conclusion".
- "breaks_that_dont_matter": an array of objects with "feature" and "why_it_is_harmless_here".
- "verdict_on_the_conclusion": an object with "survives" (true/false/qualified), "reason", and "confidence" (a short phrase, not a number).
- "better_analogy": an object with "candidate", "why_it_fits_better", and "what_it_still_gets_wrong".
- "settled_vs_contested": an object with "settled" and "contested", each an array of one-line items.

Keep the JSON self-contained and readable without the surrounding prose.
