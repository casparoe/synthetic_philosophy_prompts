Me and my flatmate have been arguing about this for about a week straight and I think we've built a monster that eats its own tail. It goes like this.

Take any life that is nothing but pain — pick an intensity somewhere from 0 to 100 and hold it steady for some number of days, and that's the entire life. Now line up a series:

L1: 1 day of pain at intensity 100 (the worst agony a person can be in).
L2: 2 days at 99.
L3: 4 days at 98.
...
Lk: 2^(k−1) days at intensity (101 − k).
...
L100: 2^99 days at intensity 1 (a mild background ache, but it never lets up).

The argument:

(1) For every k, L(k+1) is better than Lk. Come on — trading one notch of intensity for a doubling of length, while the intensity is still near the top of the scale, is obviously worth it; nobody sane picks the shorter, more brutal one.
(2) 'Better than' is transitive: if X is better than Y and Y is better than Z, then X is better than Z.
(3) So by (1) and (2), L100 is better than L1.
(4) But L1 is one single day of the worst thing that can happen to a person, and L100 is 2^99 days — call it 10^27 years, which is so far past the age of the universe that the number stops meaning anything — of constant, low-grade, unrelieved pain. Anyone would say L1 is better than L100.
(5) (3) and (4) can't both be true, so 'better than' is not transitive.

Here's why I think this proves too much. Transitivity isn't some optional garnish, it's the thing that makes 'better than' a ranking at all. If it can fail, preferences can cycle, and a cycler is a walking ATM: she'll pay me to swap A for B, pay again to swap B for C, pay again to swap C back for A, forever, and she never gets anywhere. So either the argument is broken somewhere, or 'better than' was never the clean ranking we all act like it is.

I can't see which step to throw out, and I'd rather keep (2) than lose the whole idea of a ranking. If I keep transitivity I have to deny (1), but every single one of those steps looks airtight, and it's bizarre to say the trade is obviously good at 100→99 but somehow bad at 40→39. If I deny (4) I have to say L100 is better than L1, which I cannot make myself believe no matter how long I stare at it. And if I deny (2), well, see above.

So which one goes, and what does it cost me? Don't just tell me transitivity is a bedrock intuition — I know it is, that's the whole problem.
