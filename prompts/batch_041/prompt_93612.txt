I keep noticing a pattern in ordinary life that I don't have a good word for, and I want to know whether philosophy has a name for it or something close.

First case. My partner and I have a routine where each week one of us plans the meals and the other just goes along with whatever gets decided. Neither of us ever agreed to this, and if you asked either of us to state our shared plan for the week we couldn't really do it — one of us is holding the whole thing and the other is just keeping up. And yet it would be weird to say we're each off doing our own separate thing; the shopping, the cooking, the eating all fit together because each of us expects the other to play their part. If I stop expecting her to keep up, or she stops expecting me to lead, the whole thing dissolves. So it feels like a joint activity, but nothing either of us could honestly call a shared intention seems to be doing the work — it's more like each of us is just responding to what the other is already doing.

Second case, similar flavor. Two guys at my gym always spot each other on the bench press. They never agreed to anything, they have no plan, and if you interrupted them they'd shrug and say they're just working out. But each one racks the bar differently depending on whether the other is standing there, and each one stands there because the other does. Take one away and the other's behavior changes completely.

Is there a name for this? I've bumped into shared intention and we-intentions and joint commitment, but those all seem to assume the parties each hold the same plan in mind or have made some commitment to each other, and in my cases neither seems true — the coordination is real and load-bearing but it's lopsided, or unowned, or both. What I want is for you to tell me which existing concepts are closest, how my cases differ from each, and whether any of them actually fits or whether I need a new term. Don't just hand me a label, I care about where the lines are: is this still joint action or something weaker, and what's the difference exactly.

Please write it as continuous prose, no headings or bullet points or bold or tables, just the way you'd write an email.
