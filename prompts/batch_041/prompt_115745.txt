Scheffler's afterlife conjecture holds that much of what we now value depends on our confidence that human life will continue long after our deaths, so that extinction would be bad in part by draining the value from lives that it does not itself end. Argue that the conjecture cannot sustain that conclusion. Taken as a claim about value, it must say that our activities are worth less the sooner the human future ends — but every human future is finite, so the conjecture either demands a future that never ends, which is not credible, or makes the worth of a present life depend on how many millennia separate it from extinction, which is not credible either. Taken as a claim about what we can bring ourselves to care about, it makes the badness of extinction depend on our beliefs about it, so that an extinction no one foresaw would be less bad. Set out both horns, argue that no formulation of the conjecture escapes them, and say what the conjecture would have to give up in order to be saved.
