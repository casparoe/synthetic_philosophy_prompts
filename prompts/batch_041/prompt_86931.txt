I'm in a third-year metaethics seminar and we've spent the last two weeks on the Euthyphro — first dialogue I've actually enjoyed, mostly because it ends with everyone just getting up and leaving. The assignment for reading week is a "creative response": anything counts so long as it shows we've done the work, and she said she'd honestly rather recieve a page of something funny than another essay that restates the two horns and picks one. So here's my plan, and I'd like you to write the thing.

I want a parody of an institutional document: a memo / committee report, deadpan bureaucratic register start to finish, from the "Standing Committee on the Honour Code" at a university that has just stripped the last religious language out of its student handbook and now has to explain why the Code binds anybody at all. Numbered findings. The Committee concludes, at finding 4, that the Code binds because the Board of Governors wills it; one member then asks, in a bracketed note, whether the Board might will the Code because it binds, and the Committee's reply to this should be a paragraph of pure procedural throat-clearing that never actually answers it. Finding 7 deals with the awkward fact that the Board voted 7-4 on the relevant clause and the four have publicly said the clause is unjust: the Committee rules that only pronouncements issued unanimously by the Board count as binding, and does not notice what that does to finding 4. Then an appendix where the Committee reaches for the escape I keep seeing people make, that the Board's own character is the standard, so the Code is neither above the Board nor invented by it — and "arbitrary" is quietly doing two different jobs in that one sentence. The dissenting member gets a footnote: swap "the Board" for "the community", or "our tradition", or "what an informed and impartial observer would approve of", and the memo reads identically, so whatever the Committee thinks it has grounded, it hasn't grounded it in anything in particular. Last section is the practical fallout — the Committee cannot tell whether any given pronouncement of the Board is just, because its procedure for answering that question just is the Board's pronouncements — so it recommends renaming the ethics office and moves on.

Don't mention Euthyphro, Socrates or God anywhere, and don't let it slide into a smug atheist piece either — the target is the move, not the believers, and the joke has to be that a real committee would absolutely write this memo. The philosophy needs to be right enough that a philosopher laughs instead of wincing: the arbitrariness horn, the independence horn, the epistemic version of it, and the business about the gods disagreeing and the shift to "all of them" should all be doing work, just not under those names. End with the Code still in force and the question officially unasked. 700-800 words, and it should still be followable by my flatmate, who has never read a word of Plato.
