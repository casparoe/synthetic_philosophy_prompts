I'm in a small reading group on population ethics and value theory -- four of us, mostly grad students, plus one economist who keeps insisting we formalize everything -- and we've been stuck for months on the spectrum argument. You know the shape of it: take a life that's wonderful, and a life that's just barely worth living; you can get from one to the other in a series of tiny steps, where each step is a life only a hair worse in some respect but with a few more years of mild contentment, or slightly less intense suffering, and at no step does it look like the next life is worse than the current one. Then transitivity does its thing and you land on the conclusion that some enormous number of barely-worth-living lives is better than one great life, which everyone hates. Half the group thinks the honest move is to give up transitivity of "better than"; the other half thinks one of the steps must secretly be a worsening and we just can't say which; and one person keeps saying the whole thing shows that "better than" isn't one relation at all but a grab-bag of dimensions we're pretending is a single ordering.

Whats been nagging me is a distinction we stumbled into last week that doesn't seem to be in the usual framing: incomparability is not the same thing as intransitivity. If A is better than B and B is better than C, but A and C simply can't be ranked, you've got a partial order rather than a cycle -- transitivity survives, you've only lost totality. A couple of us think that once you let "better than" be merely partial, the spectrum argument goes through without any of the spooky intransitivity, and that the real fight has been about totality all along while everyone kept calling it a fight about transitivity. I genuinely can't tell whether that's a dissolution of the problem or just relabeling.

So here's what I want from you: diagnose the impasse. Is this disagreement substantive or verbal -- are the sides attaching different meanings to "better than"? Is it really a disagreement about the facts (which step is a worsening) disguised as one about logic? Is there a hidden formal question, like whether the value structure has to be a total order in the first place? Or are two camps answering different questions under one heading -- one asking what the correct logic of the comparative is, the other asking what we should say about the cases? Then, given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress: a formal result, a distinction, a change of question, or an argument that nothing should be expected. Lead with the diagnosis in your first two sentences, then give the reasons, and keep the whole thing under 600 words.
