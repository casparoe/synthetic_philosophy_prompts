ok so I keep going back and forth on whether lying and just-misleading are actually morally different, and I want to know what principle my gut is really running on, if any. My verdicts, don't be gentle:

- Someone asks if I liked their cooking and I say "you really know your way around a kitchen." True, they take it as praise, I didn't like it. Fine, I'd do it again.
- My dad asks if I've been keeping up with the gym and I say "I went Tuesday." Also true, also leaves him thinking I'm on top of it, I've been once in six weeks. Not fine. And I honestly can't tell you why this one bugs me and the cooking one doesn't.
- My neighbor's dodging a process server and the guy asks me if he's home; I say "his cars not in the driveway." He's home, the car's in the shop, I know both. Fine.
- My kid asks if I ate the last of the cake; I say "cake's gone though, isn't it." True. Not fine, and it feels worse than the neighbor thing even though nobodys hurt either way.
- On a loan form I write "I have never been sued." True — I settled before it ever got filed. Not fine, and honestly worse than plenty of outright lies I've told.
- I've told my partner "I've told you this a million times." Six, maybe. Fine, obviously.
- A recruiter asks if I know Kubernetes; I say "I've worked alot with containers." True-ish, and I know exactly what they'll hear. Not fine.
- A friend who just got dumped asks if her ex ever talked about her, and I say "mostly he talked about work." He didn't, he said he felt relieved. I think this is fine — she's not better off knowing.

I used to think the whole thing came down to did-you-say-something-you-believed-false, but the loan form and the cake and the million-times kill that dead. My half-theory now: you've done the bad thing when you use your words to plant a false belief in someone who's relying on you for the truth about that very thing, and it doesn't matter whether what you said was literally true or false. But then the cooking case should be not-fine and it isn't, and the cake case should maybe be fine and it isn't, so I don't trust it. (Someone told me there's a name for the true-statement-that-misleads move, paltering or something, whatever.)

And be straight with me about which of my verdicts the principle can't save and which one I should give up.
