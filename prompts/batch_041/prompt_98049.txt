My father died three months ago, and since then I have been trying to determine what I actually believe about time. A physicist friend told me, intending it as a comfort, that on the best current understanding of spacetime my father has not ceased to exist — that the past is as real as the present, and that the moments of his life are still there. I have spent the weeks since assembling a view of my own, and before I commit it to paper I would like an audit of whether its parts hang together. Here is where I have landed.

1. Special relativity is true: simultaneity is frame-relative, and there is no observer-independent fact about which distant events are simultaneous with a given event. There is no absolute present.

2. The Rietdijk–Putnam argument is sound. Since any event falls within the present of some observer, and reality cannot itself be relative to an observer, past and future events are as real as present ones. My father's life is not over; it is a region of the block.

3. Time genuinely passes. I reject any moving spotlight that successively illuminates one moment after another, but I do believe there is an objective, non-indexical fact about which events have already occurred and which have not — a fact that is not merely relative to where I happen to stand in time.

4. The future is genuinely open. Determinism is false; the future is not settled in the way the past is, and my deliberations and choices make a real difference to what happens.

5. The arrow of time is real, but it is not written into the fundamental dynamical laws. It emerges from thermodynamics — from a low-entropy boundary condition together with the statistics of large systems.

6. The block universe does not strip my grief or my agency of meaning. Deliberating, and mourning, are themselves events in the block; that is enough for them to be real and to matter.

I suspect that some of these pull against one another, but I cannot see which, and I do not want a general lecture on the philosophy of time. What I want is an audit: which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which merely appear to be, and for the genuine ones how severe the conflict is and what would have to give. Please rank the tensions rather than treating them as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would resolve the most.
