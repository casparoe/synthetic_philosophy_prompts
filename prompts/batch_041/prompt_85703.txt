I am preparing a position paper on future contingents and would like a consistency audit of my commitments before I commit to them in print. Here is the package I hold.

(1) Bivalence and excluded middle for future contingents. Every proposition, including one about a contingent future event, is either true or false, and for every proposition and its negation exactly one is true. I reject the view that future contingents are neither true nor false until the future settles, and I reject the view that their truth value is relative to a context of assessment.

(2) Indeterminism. The world is not deterministic. Some future events are not causally determined by the past and present; there are genuine alternative possibilities. I do not mean by this that more than one future is actual.

(3) Tenseless truth. Truth is not a property that comes and goes. If a proposition is true, it is true simpliciter and at all times; future-tensed statements do not become true as their event time approaches. I do not think that the proposition that a sea battle will occur tomorrow is true today but was neither true nor false a year ago.

(4) Truthmaker principle. Every contingent truth has a truthmaker. For every true contingent proposition, there is something in the world in virtue of which it is true; truth does not float free of being.

(5) No future facts. The past and present are real, but the future is not yet real. There are no future events, no future facts, and no future entities that could serve as truthmakers for propositions about what will happen.

(6) No present grounding. The truth of a future contingent is not grounded in any present disposition, causal tendency, propensity, or seed in current things. To say that it is true that a sea battle will occur is not to say that present conditions already contain the battle in embryonic form.

(7) Fixed past. The past is fixed and cannot be changed; there is no backward causation.

(8) Power entailment. If a proposition is true and its truth entails that I will not do X, then it is not within my power to do X. I take this to follow from the fact that I cannot make a true proposition false.

(9) Free will. For at least some of my future actions, I can do otherwise; deliberation is not pointless.

Please separate the commitments that are genuinely in tension from those that only appear to be. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is, what exactly generates it, and what the cheapest revision would be and what that revision costs. Rank the genuine tensions by severity. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and whether there is a way to keep (1) and (9) while abandoning something else. Do not simply list standard positions; trace the commitments.
