I am a third-year philosophy student currently taking a seminar on the epistemology of logic, and we have spent the last fortnight on Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the tortoise. I understand the mechanics of the regress well enough: the tortoise grants each of the two premises in turn, then, when asked, grants the conditional that links them to the conclusion, then, when asked, grants the conditional that links those together, and still declines to grant the conclusion, the list of premises growing without end. What I cannot settle is what the regress shows. Our seminar leader offered a reading I have not met elsewhere and can neither dismiss nor accept. On her account, it is not really a regress of premises at all. Each further conditional the tortoise extracts should be read as an attempt to state explicitly what it is to apply a rule of inference, and it fails for the reason that any explicit statement of a rule fails to determine its own application. If that is right, the tortoise is not a sceptic about deduction in particular but the rule-following problem in miniature: he is the general fact that a practice cannot be exhaustively codified into propositions, dressed up as a missing warrant.

What I want from you is a credence, not a verdict. Let T be the proposition that Carroll's regress does not show that deductive inference stands in need of a justification it cannot have — that is, that the tortoise's demand, however we characterise it, is either not a legitimate demand or is already discharged by the act of inferring itself. My own credence is about 0.6, and the decomposition I have been working with, with my numbers attached, runs as follows: (a) that the rule-following reading of the regress is correct, so that what is being pressed is the indeterminacy of rule-application rather than a missing premise — 0.5; (b) that, conditional on that reading, the regress is harmless, since it shows only that no practice can be exhaustively codified and that this is not a defect in the practice — 0.6; (c) that, conditional on the reading being wrong and the tortoise genuinely demanding a warrant, such a warrant is available, of a kind that is neither inferential (which would simply restart the regress) nor a re-assertion of the very step in question — 0.2; (d) that the demand is independently illegitimate, in the sense that justification does not require immunity to failure, so that what the tortoise wants is a guarantee that no epistemic notion should be expected to supply — 0.4; and (e) that the question has a determinate answer at all, rather than trading on an equivocation between entitlement and truth-conduciveness — 0.9. I am aware that these figures do not reproduce my headline number, and I take that to be a sign that I have mis-specified how the terms depend on one another, which is part of why I am asking.

Give me your own number for T, your own decomposition (revise mine — if two of my terms are in fact one term, or a term is missing, say so and repair it), a probability for each term with a line of justification, an explicit account of how the terms combine into the total, and a statement of which term the total is most sensitive to and what argument or consideration would move that term furthest. I would rather have an honest map of your uncertainty than a survey of positions, so please do not pad the answer with citations or with gestures at what the literature supposedly says; argue it through on the merits, and where you think the question is confused, say where and why.
