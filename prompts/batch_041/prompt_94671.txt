Compare the mechanism behind Polybius's cycle of constitutions with the mechanism behind Ibn Khaldun's cycle of dynastic rise and decay. The interesting question is not the bare fact that both are cyclical but what each identifies as the cycle's moving part: for Polybius, a structural logic internal to each regime type that generates its own successor; for Ibn Khaldun, the erosion of group solidarity by the very prosperity that solidarity makes possible. Address two things. First, the standard contrast is that Polybius's mixed constitution can retard the cycle while Ibn Khaldun's cannot be halted at all; is that contrast genuine, or do both theorists in fact treat the cycle as indefinitely deferrable but never terminable? Second, consider a variant that neither endorses: a cycle in which every turn begins from a lower baseline than the last, so that the polity spirals downward rather than returning to its starting condition. Which of the two mechanisms can be extended to yield this result without abandoning its central explanatory commitment, and what would the extension cost the theory?
