ok so ive been chewing on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism for a couple months now and i genuinely cant tell whether the debate has actually stalled or whether im just not seeing the move that would break it open. quick version of the setup: hedonism says well-being is pleasure minus pain, but the states we call pleasures are all over the place -- the taste of a ripe peach, the relief when a headache finally lets go, finishing a hard proof, a warm bath, schadenfreude, the ache in your legs when you stop running. whats the common core? if theres none, then either 'pleasure' names a disjunction so gerrymandered it cant carry the whole value theory, or it names nothing and the theory is empty. both sides know this argument cold by now. one side keeps rolling out fresh counterexamples, the other keeps patching -- its attitudinal not sensory, its a mode not a content, its a felt tone, its whatever we desire under the right conditions -- and nobody moves an inch.

what i want is a diagnosis, not a verdict. is this a verbal dispute about the word 'pleasure'? a substantive disagreement about whether theres a natural kind sitting there at all? or are the two sides actually answering different questions under one heading -- one asking what makes a state a pleasure (about the essence of a mental state) and the other asking what unifies the class our word actually picks out (about our concepts and our psychology)? my own hunch is its mostly the third, and that the 'heterogeneity problem' only looks like an objection to hedonisms value claim because the taxonomy question and the value question got fused together and neither side notices. but i might be wrong, and maybe theres a hidden empirical thing doing the work -- does pleasure even come in one neural currency, or is 'liking' and 'wanting' splitting the whole thing down the middle in a way the philosophers havent caught up with.

so tell me what kind of disagreement this really is, and given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a distinction, an empirical result, giving up the demand for a definition, or nothing at all, because the impasse is exactly what youd expect when one side wants a single essence and the other is happy with a cluster. argue for it, lean on what each side actually says, and dont just survey the positions back at me.
