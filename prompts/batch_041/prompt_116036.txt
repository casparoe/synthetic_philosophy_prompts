I'm applying for a small summer research grant (three months, no teaching) and need a proposal, maybe 1200 words. Here's my half-formed idea, and I'd like you to turn it into the actual proposal.

Everyone knows the Fitch-style proof: if every truth is knowable then every truth is known, so the antirealist's knowability principle either collapses into the claim that we already know everything or has to be restricted somehow. The going reactions are to restrict the principle to propositions that aren't self-defeating to know, to dispute the scope of the possibility operator, or to deny that knowledge is closed under conjunction elimination (giving up classical logic doesn't help, since the proof goes through intuitionistically). What bugs me is that all of these take for granted that 'knowable' is a monadic property of a single proposition, and that the closure step — whoever knows a conjunction knows each conjunct — is just logic. But look at what the proof actually makes you know: some p, and also that p is not known. That's a state no single inquirer can be in at a single time. So maybe Fitch's proof isn't really a constraint on the antirealist at all; maybe it's a constraint on a picture of knowledge as the possession of a single, ideally situated, closed-under-conjunction-elimination knower — which is exactly the picture a verificationist about meaning shouldn't be saddled with, since verificationism was always about what a community could come to know over time.

My hunch is that if you spell out a 'distributed' reading — a truth is knowable when some possible community could, over time and dividing the labour, know it — the proof either fails or has to be reproved in a form that says something different and more interesting. But I dont know if thats a real escape or just relocating the problem: a community is still a knower of some kind, and if the community knows p and knows that it doesn't know p, that's still incoherent. So the real question is whether there's a precise collectivist or diachronic notion of knowability that (a) is what the antirealist actually needs, (b) is not refuted by a Fitch-style argument, and (c) doesn't just smuggle an idealised single knower back in.

Write the proposal with: the question stated so that it has a determinate answer; why the going responses leave it open (no citations needed, I'll add those myself); the method — I'm imagining a formal part, where I set out candidate closure principles and candidate readings of the knowability operator and see which combinations the proof needs, and a conceptual part, arguing which notion of knowability the antirealist is actually entitled to; a rough month-by-month plan for the three months; what a result would look like and what would count as the project failing (the honest failure mode, I think, is that every precise collectivist notion I can build either collapses into the single-knower one or gets refuted by a variant of the proof — if so, I want the proposal to say so in advance); and an assessment of whether three months is realistic or whether I should scope down to just the formal part and leave the philosophical payoff for later.

Start the proposal with the bottom line in the first sentence — one sentence saying what the project asks or claims — and then give everything else. No preamble, no restating what I've asked for.
