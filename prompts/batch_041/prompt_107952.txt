I keep meeting a pattern in discussions of procedures that consist of infinitely many steps carried out within a finite time, and I want to know whether it has a name. The individual steps are always described in perfectly ordinary terms, yet the description often fails to fix what is the case once the procedure is over. A switch is flipped at each stage, each stage taking half as long as the one before: nothing in that description says whether it starts on or off, and either answer sits equally well with everything that happens at any finite stage. A container has ten objects put into it and one taken out at every stage: the description is the same whichever object is removed, yet depending on that choice the container ends up empty or not. In both cases the final state is stipulated rather than derived. Yet elsewhere the outcome does seem to be forced, by assuming that the quantity of interest varies continuously right up to the final moment; when the quantity is a discrete state, or one that does not depend continuously on whatever the description leaves open, nothing is forced. Is there an established name for this — for a fully determinate infinite sequence of steps leaving its own outcome open? I have seen the point made with a lamp, but I am after the general phenomenon, and in particular what has to be added to a description to close the gap, and whether that addition is a physical assumption or a stipulation. If there is no settled term, please coin one and say exactly where the line falls between outcomes that are determined and outcomes that are merely chosen.
