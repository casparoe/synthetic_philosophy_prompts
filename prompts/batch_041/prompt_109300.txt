Every time I try to pin down what's actually still live in the socialist calculation debate — Mises's impossibility argument, Hayek on dispersed and tacit knowledge, Lange's market-socialist reply — I get the same canned summary: Lange won on incentives and static allocation, Hayek won on knowledge and dynamics, done. I'm a third-year PhD student in political economy and I don't buy that summary, for two reasons. First, a firm like a supermarket chain or Amazon is basically a planned economy on the inside — HQ decides what gets produced and shipped where — and nobody calls that impossible, so the impossibility claim has to be about something narrower than planning as such, and I can't say what. Second, everyone now claims cheap computation and big data revive the case for central planning, and I genuinely can't tell if that's an arguement or a vibe.

So I want a defended agenda, not a survey: what are the real open questions left in this debate, and which two or three would you prioritize for someone with maybe a year and a half and a philosophy-of-social-science background? Tell me which questions are upstream of which, which are effectively settled and should just be dropped, and what argument or evidence in the next year or two would move something up or down the list. Rank them and defend the ranking.
