I don't have a philosophy background, so please pitch this at the level of the ideas rather than the literature. There seem to be two ways of doing Darwinian cultural evolution: the meme's-eye view, on which cultural items are replicators in their own right and their spread is explained by their own fitness, and the gene-culture view, on which culture is a second inheritance system whose transmission biases were themselves shaped by genetic fitness. Compare them as competing theories rather than two names for the same thing. Where do they actually diverge in what they predict? Work through two specific points: first, whether cultural items meet the usual requirements for a replicator — faithful copying, fecundity, longevity — given the standard objection that human imitation is too lossy for the analogy to hold; and second, whether cultural fitness can come apart from host fitness systematically, and what follows if it can. Then use a case neither was built for as a tie-breaker: machine-generated text, images and video that are selected by engagement-optimizing software rather than remembered and retransmitted by people. Copying there is essentially lossless, but the selective environment is a product team's objective function instead of a human mind. Which framework handles that better, and is there anything in it that either theory can't accommodate? Say which one you'd defend, and be explicit about what would change your mind.
