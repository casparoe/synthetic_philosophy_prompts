I am a novelist at work on a book whose central episode is a town that receives an anonymous offer: each resident may submit as many or as few entries as she likes to a lottery whose prize is a fixed sum divided by the total number of entries, with one entry drawn at random. The offer is a version of the Luring Lottery, and the town’s response turns on a puzzle that has the shape of the Platonia Dilemma: if everyone could coordinate on almost no entries, each would be better off, but each has a private incentive to submit more, and the cooperative outcome depends on each treating her own choice as evidence of what others like her will do. I want to turn this into a two-page proposal for a six-month research fellowship at a center for ethics and public life. The question I want to pose is this: in games of this kind, when the superrational recommendation is derived under assumptions of symmetry, common knowledge, and identical reasoning ability, what happens to that recommendation when agents are uncertain whether the others are superrational, and when their confidence in others’ reasoning is itself asymmetric? I am calling this the Asymmetry of Faith. The usual defenses of superrationality assume that all players are identical reasoners facing the same problem; they do not tell an agent what to do when she thinks there is only a sixty percent chance that the others will follow the superrational logic, or when different players have different evidence about one another’s rationality. I want the proposal to state the question so that it has a determinate answer, explain why the standard treatments leave it open, set out the method with a month-by-month plan, say what a result would look like and what would count as failure, and give an honest assessment of what can be done in six months at ten hours a week. The method I have in mind is formal modeling of heterogeneous priors and partial common knowledge, paired with a small set of narrative cases from the novel used as intuition pumps, and I want you to explain why that combination is better here than either a purely formal treatment or a purely literary one. Do not give me a bibliography, reading list, or citations; you may refer to positions and arguments by name, but I will add the references myself. The proposal should be written for a fellowship committee that includes both philosophers and working artists, so it needs to be precise without being technical for its own sake, and it needs to make clear why a novelist is the right person to do this work.
