I am an undergraduate in a seminar on the philosophy of biology, and our current unit is on heritability. For the final assignment I have to produce something more ambitious than an essay: a joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between the two camps in this dispute could actually publish over both their names. I would like you to draft that document for me. Take the role of a philosopher of biology with a solid working knowledge of quantitative genetics — someone who understands both the mathematics of variance decomposition and the conceptual objections to it — and begin with a short brainstorming pass before you write, so that the formulations you settle on are considered rather than reflexive.

Set up the two parties as follows, and refer to them only by these labels rather than by the names of any particular scientists, since I have no way to check attributions and do not want the document to rest on one.

The variance-decomposition camp holds that a heritability coefficient is a well-defined parameter of a specified population in a specified range of environments: it reports the share of phenotypic variance in that population attributable to additive genetic variance, it is estimable from twin, family, and genomic designs, and within its proper scope it licenses real predictions — about response to selection, about the likely spread of trait values among offspring, and about where to look for genetic influences.

The construct-skeptic camp holds that the same coefficient is so sensitive to the distribution of environments in the population, so easily confused with a causal claim about any individual, and so entangled with the particular ways environments happen to be measured, that for the traits people actually argue about it is at best uninformative and at worst systematically misleading — a number that answers a question no one was asking.

Now produce the collaboration. I want the claims both camps can sign, including about what a heritability estimate is and is not; the residual disagreements stated precisely, each side's position phrased in wording that side would accept as its own; the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one camp; and, for each crux, the argument or case that both camps agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance. Be scrupulous about not strawmanning either side: if you cannot find a formulation that a camp would endorse, say so and explain the obstacle rather than inventing a position for it. Also say explicitly which parts of the dispute, if any, are verbal — for instance, whether one camp is using heritability in the population-variance sense while the other hears an individual-level causal claim — and whether anything substantive survives that clarification.

Because the whole point of the exercise is to push past the standard talking points, I want two things injected into the document that neither camp will have a canned response to.

First, introduce a quantity I will call counterfactual heritability: the heritability that would obtain if the population's distribution of relevant environments were shifted to some specified alternative distribution. Ask both camps to say whether this quantity is well defined, whether the ordinary estimate approximates it or instead approximates something else, and whether it is the quantity each camp respectively needs. Their answers here should be treated as claims in the document, not as asides.

Second, work through an invented case I will call the three-schooling-worlds case. Three societies are alike in every measurable respect except how schooling is allocated. In the lottery world, schooling is assigned by lottery and is uncorrelated with family background. In the inheritance world, schooling tracks parental wealth closely. In the uniformity world, every child receives schooling of identical quality and duration. All three worlds have the same distribution of genotypes. Ask each camp to state, for a standard cognitive measure, what it predicts about the within-society heritability in each world and why, and then to say what the pattern of results across the three worlds would show about the trait's genetic architecture — and, separately, what it would show about the practical question of what raises the measured trait. Where the two camps' predictions coincide, record the agreement; where they diverge, that divergence belongs in the crux table.

Write the document in the following form.

1. A numbered agreed-claims section, in prose, with each claim stated in a sentence both camps would sign and, where useful, a brief note on why both accept it.
2. A disagreements table with exactly these columns: ID; Crux (the neutral formulation of the question); Variance-Decomposition Position (in that camp's own wording); Construct-Skeptic Position (in that camp's own wording); Shared Case or Argument (what both agree bears on the crux); Variance-Decomposition Prediction; Construct-Skeptic Prediction; Moves Variance-Decomposition? (yes/no); Moves Construct-Skeptic? (yes/no); Substantive, Verbal, or Mixed.
3. A short section on the verbal residue, naming any disputes you judge to be about words and giving the reformulation that dissolves them.
4. A machine-readable summary, because my seminar is compiling these documents into a shared dataset. Return a single JSON object with these fields and no others:
   - agreed_claims: array of strings, each a claim from section 1.
   - cruxes: array of objects with fields id, crux_question, variance_decomposition_position, construct_skeptic_position, shared_case_or_argument, variance_decomposition_prediction, construct_skeptic_prediction, moves_variance_decomposition (boolean), moves_construct_skeptic (boolean), dispute_type (one of substantive, verbal, mixed).
   - verbal_disputes: array of objects with fields issue, why_verbal, dissolving_reformulation.
   - counterfactual_heritability: object with fields variance_decomposition_verdict, construct_skeptic_verdict, joint_verdict (null if none).
   - three_schooling_worlds: object with fields lottery_world_predictions, inheritance_world_predictions, uniformity_world_predictions, what_the_pattern_would_show, what_it_would_show_about_raising_the_trait.
   - open_questions: array of strings, the questions the collaboration could not close.

Constraints. Do not cite studies, name researchers, or gesture at a literature; the document must stand entirely on arguments and on the stipulated cases, since it will be read by people who cannot look anything up and I do not want unverifiable appeals doing the work. Do not use a case or result whose details either camp would contest. Where you are genuinely unsure whether a formulation is one a camp would accept, flag it in open_questions rather than papering over it. The tone should be that of a joint statement both parties would be willing to have quoted, not a summary written by a neutral observer.
