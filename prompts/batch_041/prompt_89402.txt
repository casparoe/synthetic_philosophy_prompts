I am a second-year PhD student in philosophy, and last week I was asked to run the opening meeting of our department's epistemology reading group. The topic was the regress of justification, and it went badly. Within twenty minutes everyone had sorted themselves into foundationalists, coherentists, and infinitists, and then spent the rest of the hour arguing about which label was least embarrassing, without ever noticing that most of them disagreed about what the regress even was. One student kept talking about the regress of reasons for a belief, another about the regress of causes of a belief, a third about what happens when you keep asking a small child "why?" and the fourth about what it would take to analyze a concept down to its elements, and none of the four noticed that they were answering different questions. I want to repair this next month with a handout rather than another discussion: a decision tree, or a sorting quiz with a scoring key, that takes someone from commitments they can actually state to a position in the logical space, and I want the tree to be built out of that space rather than out of the usual list of names.

So please build me that instrument. It should have roughly eight to twelve branching nodes and somewhere between six and ten leaves, and it is meant for first- and second-year graduate students who know the standard three-way taxonomy but have never been forced to say which of its distinctions they actually accept. Every node must be phrased as a question someone could answer without already knowing where it leads -- no node may be "are you a foundationalist or a coherentist?" -- and wherever a node can be made concrete, make it a case rather than an abstraction: a child's interminable "why?", a perceptual belief that would be false if you were a brain in a vat, two experts of equal competence who disagree, a chain of reasons nobody could ever finish writing down. The tree has to reach at least these leaves: a classical strong foundationalism; a modest or defeasible foundationalism; a holistic coherentism; an infinitism that requires only that the chain be extendable on demand rather than that anyone actually possess an infinite stock of occurrent beliefs; an externalist view on which justification is a matter of how the belief was formed rather than of what reasons the believer can give; the framework or hinge family, which treats the demand for a further reason as illegitimate in some domain rather than as one that gets answered; a conservatism or entitlement view on which one may hold a belief without reasons until something challenges it; and a deflationary option on which the whole regress is an artifact of a mistaken picture of justification as a linear chain. Alongside those, I want the tree to carry the distinction between an actual infinite and a merely potential one, and at least one branch node on which of the regresses we are even talking about, since I now suspect that half of my group's disagreement was really about that and I want the instrument to expose the ambiguity rather than assume it away.

Finally, because I intend to use this as a teaching document and not merely a diagnostic one, I want two things attached to the tree. First, for every combination of answers that no actual position occupies, a one-line diagnosis of why it is empty -- incoherent, merely unattractive, or simply unclaimed? Second, an explicit list of the leaf pairs the tree cannot separate, together with the further question that would be needed to separate them. I already suspect that modest foundationalism and holistic coherentism may come apart only over whether the supposedly basic beliefs are themselves revisable in light of how the rest of the web hangs together, and that the externalist and hinge options both refuse the demand for reasons but for reasons so different in kind that a tree asking only about the shape of the chain will run them together. Please be honest where the tree runs out of resolution rather than inserting a node that only pretends to distinguish them.
