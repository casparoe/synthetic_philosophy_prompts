I have been reading about the Humean best-system account of laws of nature, on which the laws are the axioms of whichever deductive system best balances simplicity, strength, and fit with the total mosaic of local matters of fact, and on which laws are not governing entities that compel or push their instances around. What I cannot see is how this amounts to anything more than a denial that there are laws at all. If a law does not govern, and if it is merely the most economical way of summarizing regularities that would hold anyway, then the Humean seems to believe only in the regularities, with the word law doing no work beyond flagging the summaries we happen to find compact and informative. And if simplicity and informativeness are our standards, then which regularities count as laws — and therefore which facts are physically necessary rather than merely accidental — would depend on us rather than on the world. On that reading, shouldn't the Humean position be classified as a form of anti-realism or conventionalism about laws, rather than as a realist account of them?
