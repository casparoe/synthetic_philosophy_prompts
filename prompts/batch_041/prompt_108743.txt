Moore and the hands thing — he holds up one hand, then the other, and says that settles it, there's an external world — and every source I talk to gives me the same knowing line: it's just Samuel Johnson kicking the stone. You know the scene. Someone tells Johnson that Berkeley's idealism can't be refuted, Johnson boots a big rock and says thats his refutation. The moral everyone draws is that Moore's lecture is Johnson's stunt with footnotes: commonsense bluster standing in for an argument, no answer to the skeptic, just a refusal to take him seriously.

A philosopher I trust thinks the analogy is doing alot of work in that dismissal, and that it's a bad comparison. Her point: Johnson was trying to knock down a thesis — that matter only exists when it's perceived — while Moore wasn't attacking the skeptic's reasoning so much as reversing it. He doesn't dispute the skeptic's logic at all; he just treats the fact that there's a hand here as something he knows better than he knows any premise that would undercut it. Thats a move about burden of proof, not a kick.

So can you audit this for me? Lay out the mapping — a table, I'm a words guy — what in the Johnson story lines up with what in Moore's proof, then where the mapping breaks, and crucially which breaks matter for the use the analogy is being put to, i.e. licensing the verdict that its just bluster and the proof is a shrug.

Three things I'd like worked in:

- The analogy seems to assume Moore's hands are as plain a fact as Johnson's stone, and that anyone who doubts them is being silly. But I've read that Johnson's kick is often said to miss what Berkeley actually claimed (he wasn't denying the stone is there, he was denying something else about it), and that commonsense certainties have a habit of getting overturned — everybody was once certain the sun went round the earth. If thats right, what happens to the analogy, and to the bluster verdict?
- If the gesture really does prove the external world, the same template seems to prove the past, or anything common sense asserts, including things we've since dropped. Feature, or reductio?
- Does any of this bear on whether the proof actually works, as opposed to whether Moore was being smug?
