Heres the gist as I understood it. There's a classic lab demo where a blue dot flashes near the top of a screen and a red dot flashes lower down a split second later; people report seeing a single dot travel downwards and turn red about halfway along. The book says this is impossible unless the brain is doing something sneaky: you can't see a colour change at the midpoint until the second dot has already been flashed, so the brain must be holding the first dot in a buffer, waiting to see what comes next, and only then serving you a cleaned-up version of events — which means your experience is always lagging slightly behind the world, and you're never really living in the present. Then it says Dennett showed this is actually a memory trick: you do see the second dot the moment it appears, but your brain goes back and rewrites your memory of the instant before, so you only remember seeing the colour change mid-flight. And because those two stories make identical predictions in every experiment, Dennett concluded that the whole question of when you experience something is a pseudo-problem — there's no fact of the matter about it, and anyone who thinks there is is still picturing a little theatre inside the head where the show gets watched.
