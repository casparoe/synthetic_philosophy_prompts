I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of two decades, and I'd like to build a decision tree that sorts the positions one can hold about information cascades and rational herding, for a study group that has just worked through the sequential-decision setup in which each agent observes the actions but not the private signals of those who went before. Please start the tree from a commitment almost everyone grants — that in that setup a later agent can be rational in setting her own signal aside — and branch only on questions a reader can answer without already knowing which position they lead to. I want the nodes to fall on the real joints of the dispute: whether the observed herd reflects independent signals or a common source (what I think of as the shared-sequence problem, where everyone is updating on the same public evidence and the independence the model assumes is an artifact); whether the agent's payoff turns on being right or on being seen to agree with the others; whether she internalizes the information externality her action imposes on later observers; whether she treats a given cascade as fragile or as a stable summary of the group's knowledge; and whether knowing cascade theory changes the answer, on the grounds that a population that has all read the same account of herding may herd on resisting herding instead. Leaves should include the Bayesian cascade account on which herding is individually rational but informationally inefficient and fragile; reputational herding, where the motive is not information at all; the efficient-market view on which the price or the herd is a sufficient statistic and there is nothing to regret; the contrarian view that any cascade is an epistemic failure to be resisted; preference falsification, where public agreement and private belief come apart; and the reflexive view on which the herd's belief helps produce the fact it is about. Please mark which combinations of answers are unoccupied and say briefly whether each is empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any two leaves the tree cannot separate. Aim for eight or so nodes and ten or so leaves.
