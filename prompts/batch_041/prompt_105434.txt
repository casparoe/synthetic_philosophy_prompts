Me and my mate got into a ridiculous two-hour argument last night about our chess club's constitution and now I genuinely don't know which of us is the idiot.

Here's the setup. The constitution has one clause that says any rule in the constitution can be changed by a two-thirds vote at the AGM. That's it, no exceptions listed anywhere. Last meeting someone moved to amend *that* clause so that a couple of things, the club name and the no-phones-during-games rule, could never be changed again by anyone ever, no matter how big the majority. The chair ruled it out of order, obviously you can't use the rules to lock the door behind you and tie everyone's hands forever. But my mate says that's nonsense, because the only thing giving the AGM the power to change rules at all is that two-thirds clause, and if it really lets you change *any* rule then it lets you change itself, so nothing is off the table and the chair is just making stuff up. Then someone else said fine, if nothing's off the table then a later AGM could just un-entrench the name again, so the whole motion is pointless. Then it got loud.

What's bugging me is what our setup actually assumes about where the authority of the rules comes from. It feels like we're treating the two-thirds clause as a different *kind* of rule from the phone rule, like one of them is underneath all the others holding them up, except nobody ever voted on it being special, it's just sitting there in the same list. Is that a real distinction or something we tell ourselves, and does it survive the moment a rule points at itself? And does *any rule can be changed* actually include the rule that says so, or does it quietly mean any rule except this one, and if so where's that exception written down? What would have to be true for the chair to be right, like would we need some rule that isn't itself up for a vote, and how would that one get there without the exact same problem starting over? I also want to know whether the setup actually forces one answer or whether a few totally different stories about why rules have authority would all fit what we do. Tell me what the rulebook is really committed to, not what we should do about the phones.
