I've been TAing an intro musicianship class this term, and there's a thing that keeps happening that I don't have a word for. We teach students to identify chords by giving a bunch of examples. A student will come up with a rule that fits every example we've given—say, 'a chord is a stack of thirds'—and then use it in a way that's obviously wrong to me, like calling a cluster a thirteenth chord because you can spell it as stacked thirds. I say no, that's not what we mean. They say, but it fits all the examples. So I give another example. They adjust. Eventually I realize I can't state the rule in a way that rules out all the clever wrong rules; the examples don't pin it down, and any formulation I give is itself just another example that can be read in different ways. The wierd part is that I still feel like there's a fact of the matter, and that the fact is settled by what our little community of musicians does—by our training, our corrections, our shared ear—not by anything in the examples or the formula. But then when I try to say what that shared practice is, I can only describe it as: the practice of following the rule. It's circular in a way that doesn't feel vicious, but I can't tell why not.

Is there an established name for this specific phenomenon? I know it's somewhere near the general idea that evidence underdetermines theory, and near the idea of tacit knowledge, and maybe near family resemblance, but none of those seem to be quite it, because the thing I'm stuck on isn't just that the rule is underdetermined by the examples; it's that the only thing that seems to settle it is a communal practice, and the practice seems to be constituted by people going on in the same way, so the settlement looks circular. If there's a name, can you give me the actual concept and not just the label—what it includes, what it leaves out, and how it differs from underdetermination and from tacit knowledge? And if there's no name, can you coin one and tell me what would count as a boundary case? I'd also like to know what would change your mind here: if you tell me the circularity is benign, what argument or consideration would make you say it's actually vicious, or that I'm just confusing a psychological inability to state a rule with a metaphysical fact about rule-following? And where is your reasoning most likely to go wrong?
