ok so this is to close out a reading group — we spent the term on Malthus and the whole right-but-early business, and I want to end with a poem instead of another summary, because at this point another summary would just be us going in circles.

here's the shape I want. Terza rima, or anyway the interlocking kind, aba bcb cdc — the point of it being that the middle line of every tercet is left dangling: its rhyme doesn't come back until the first and third lines of the *next* stanza, which then leaves that stanza's middle line dangling in turn, and so on down the poem until the one extra line at the end finally shuts the chain. I want the form to do the arguing for me. In a scheme like that the poem is never allowed to finish a thought where it stands, it's always exactly one stanza away from paying off the sound it just made, and thats the shape of the move we kept fighting about — the date passes, nothing happens, and the fix is never to drop the claim, it's to say the horizon was drawn too near, so vindication just slides out one more step, and every one of those steps is individually reasonable, which is why nobody ever counts one as a strike against the theory.

But I don't want a smug poem that lands on haha unfalsifiable. The honest version of the problem is that at any moment before the terminus, early and wrong are the same observation — you only get to tell them apart from a vantage point past both, which is a vantage point nobody in the argument has. And sometimes a thing genuinely is early. Wait longer isn't always a dodge; sometimes it's just true, and there's no way to know that yet either. The poem has to leave that open rather than resolve it, since that's honestly where we ended up.

Voice: let it be the doctrine itself, first person, talking to the people who keep setting dates on it. Not triumphant — a bit tired, maybe. It should come through that it didn't choose this, that any claim whose timescale runs longer than its audience's is going to look like this from the inside, and that this is a fact about audiences as much as about claims.

Ending: that last line that closes the rhyme chain — I want it undecidable whether it's the vindication actually arriving or just the poem running out of room. That's the whole problem in one line, if it works.

Five or six tercets plus the closing line is plenty. Don't have it name-drop Popper or use unfalsifiable as a punchline word; earn it. And keep the meter and rhyme clean enough that it reads as a real poem and not a diagram with line breaks.
