I have no formal training in philosophy — I write software — so I want to state the problem in the terms I actually have. I keep meeting the claim that certain categories are socially constructed, with race, gender, and money as the standard examples, and I notice that arguments for and against that claim keep relying on different tacit standards that nobody ever states outright. Before I take a position, I would like the standard itself written down: what must any adequate theory of the social construction of a kind be able to do? Treat this as a question about specification, not about which side is right.

My interest is practical. In my work I deal with categories assigned by software rather than by custom: a fraud model that sorts accounts into trust tiers, a marketplace that awards a top-rated badge, a platform that marks accounts as verified. Some colleagues call these categories artificial and therefore not real; others insist they are every bit as real as money. I want a checklist that tells me what a theory would have to deliver before it can adjudicate that dispute, and I want the same checklist to survive contact with race, gender, and money rather than being quietly re-tuned for each case.

Please write the desiderata. For each one, give the case or consideration that motivates it: the phenomenon the theory must explain, the case it must get right, the distinction it must not collapse, the fact it must not contradict. Then tell me which requirements you regard as non-negotiable and which are merely preferences of a particular camp. I especially want to know whether some subset of the requirements cannot be satisfied together; if so, that is the result I care about most, and I want to see exactly which pair or triple conflicts and why. Finally, say which single requirement does the most to rule candidates out, and why.

To make the list concrete, please test it against at least these kinds, which pull in different directions:

- A platform-assigned trust tier produced by a model that nobody outside the company can inspect, where membership is determined entirely by a score and yet has real consequences for who gets credit or a loan.
- A medical cutoff, such as the threshold at which a continuous measurement is declared to be a disease, which is fixed by committees and revised over time and yet tracks a biological continuum.
- Money, in the ordinary sense of a token that moves goods because everyone treats it as doing so.
- Race and gender, where the construction is contested, the boundaries are politically fought over, and being classified can change how people behave and even their measured biology.

For each of these, say what the theory owes us and where the requirements pull against one another. I do not need a survey of who has said what; I need the specification, the cases behind it, and an honest account of what cannot be had all at once.
