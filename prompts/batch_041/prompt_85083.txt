I run a small experimental-philosophy lab — two PhD students and a postdoc, roughly three years of runway — and I want to commit the whole group to the side-effect effect: the finding that people are far more willing to call a foreseen but unintended side effect intentional when it is harmful than when it is helpful. My working hunch is that the effect is not really about the concept of intentional action at all. It is about a broader asymmetry in how people fold foreseen outcomes into an agent's plan — foreseen harms get absorbed into what the agent was doing, foreseen benefits get written off as accidents. If that is right, the same asymmetry should surface in judgments of knowledge and of causation, and it should be switchable by information about what the agent actually represented to themselves: whether they would have proceeded had the side effect been the only outcome, whether they actively anticipated it, whether they could have avoided representing it at all.

Assuming that hunch, what are the most important open questions here, and which should my group take on first? I want a defended agenda rather than a survey: for each question, why it matters, whether it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is with the tools a lab like mine actually has, and what result over the next three years would move it up or down the list. Tell me what to deprioritize even though it looks central, which disagreements in this area are really about method rather than substance, and what would change the ranking. Argue from the structure of the problem rather than from who has said what — do not point me at particular papers, authors, or readings to settle any of it.
