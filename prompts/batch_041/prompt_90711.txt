I write fiction for a living, and I've talked myself into a rule I actually rely on. Call it the rehearsal test: a scenario is really possible if I can imagine it all the way through from the inside, unhurried, walking a character through it beat by beat, until nothing snags. When my imagination keeps catching — the way it does when I try to hold a shape that's perfectly round and perfectly square at the same time — I take that as a sign the thing can't happen, full stop. Yes, I know this is just the old conceivability-to-possibility move, but I've bolted on the "from the inside" and "unhurried" conditions, and I want to know what would count against the rule as I've actually stated it. What should make me drop it? I want the specific defeaters: a case that's plainly possible but that I can't rehearse from the inside no matter how long I sit with it, or one I rehearse smoothly that turns out to be flat impossible. The thing I genuinely can't decide is whether a snag tells me about the world or just about my own head — whether it's a fact about possibility or only about what I can hold in mind at once. And be honest about whether those two extra conditions have quietly turned the whole thing into something no evidence could touch, and if so whether that's fatal or just the price of a rule like this.
