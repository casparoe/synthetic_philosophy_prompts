In my reading group we've now spent like four sessions on money pumps and nobody's budged, so what I actually want from you is a diagnosis of the dispute rather than a verdict on it. Here's the setup, as fairly as I can put it. Take someone with cyclic preferences: she prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A. Hand her C. Since she prefers A to C she pays a penny to swap, so now she has A; since she prefers B to A she pays a penny to swap again; since she prefers C to B she pays again. She's back where she started, three pennies poorer, and she'll run the loop again if you let her. Half the room treats the textbook moral as settled: this shows transitivity is a requirement of rationality, the pump is the proof. The other half says the pump proves nothing of the sort. It shows she's exploitable, or that she'd be poorer, or that she'd be better off without those preferences — prudential facts, not facts about what it's rational to prefer. At every step she acts on the preference she has at that moment, and acts correctly, so there's no inconsistency anywhere; you only get a contradiction by adding a requirement that her choices over a sequence hang together or that she stick to a plan, and nobody has ever argued for that requirement except by running the pump again in a different hat.

My hypothesis, and tell me if it's right, is that this is a dilemma rather than a debate. Allow that rationality can demand something across time — that the person at t2 is answerable to the plan made at t0 — and the pump is devastating, but you need an argument for the diachronic requirement and the pump can't give you one. Say rationality is entirely at-a-time and the pump has no normative force at all. Each side's position just is the negation of the other's premise, so of course they dont move. I'm suspicious of my own diagnosis for three reasons, and I want you to deal with them. First, I can't tell whether the pump really has to be diachronic: someone in the group claims you can do it in one shot, offering her a single choice between bundles where each is one she'd trade away, or a bundle plus a ticket entitling her to one further swap, and if that works my whole dilemma is fake. Second, there's a parallel fight about diachronic Dutch books that seems to run the same way, and I can't tell if it's the same disagreement in different clothes — if it is, the resolution should transfer, and I'd want to know what the transfer actually shows. Third, someone said the pump only works because we measure the loss in money, which she may not care about much; if you measure it in what she actually cares about, exploitability and intransitivity might come apart, which would make the "vulnerable iff cyclic" equivalence do all the work and the exploitability intuition a fifth wheel.

So: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one about what 'rational' is being used to mean, or two groups answering different questions — one about which preferences a person can coherently hold, one about what she should do given the ones she has? If it's substantive, say exactly where it lives and what would settle it: a distinction, a formal result, a change of question. If you think it can't be settled, say so flatly. And if my dilemma is the right shape, pick a horn and tell me what you're giving up, because I'd rather argue with a committed answer than read a list of positions. Don't tell me to go read anyone.
