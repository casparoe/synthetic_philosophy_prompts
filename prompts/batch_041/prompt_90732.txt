I've been puzzling over the sense in which DNA is a code. The genetic code is arbitrary — no chemical necessity ties a given codon to the amino acid it specifies — and translation happens because a real decoder, the ribosome, reads codons off an mRNA tape. But codes are conventions: a mapping that some party fixes, and that a reader must hold the key to in order to decode. So two things puzzle me. If the codon-to-amino-acid mapping is arbitrary, doesn't that make it a convention rather than a natural fact — something evolution settled on rather than something forced, and hence revisable in principle? And if reading a code requires having the key, where does the cell keep its copy of the codon table, and how does the ribosome consult that table without yet another reader to interpret the table in turn? I suspect I am conflating two senses of "code" but cannot locate the conflation. Please say where the reasoning fails, and what would move you to the other side — what evidence or argument would convince you that the genome carries information in a genuinely semantic sense rather than only a statistical one?
