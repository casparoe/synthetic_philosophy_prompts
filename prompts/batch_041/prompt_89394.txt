Hey — I'm building the first-week handout for an undergrad seminar on pleasure and wellbeing, and I want a "which theory of pleasure do you actually hold?" quiz that students fill in before we've named any of the options. Eight to ten questions, each one a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. The rule for every question: a student has to be able to answer it off the top of their head, without knowing which side of the debate its on. No question that just asks "so is pleasure a sensation or an attitude?"

Cases I want somewhere in the mix (swap in better ones if you've got them):

- scratching a mosquito bite — hurts and feels good in the same place at the same time
- the patient who says the pinprick hurts, plainly, but it just doesn't bother her; she isn't suffering at all
- two people taste the same wine, one has been told its a $200 bottle. Does the pleasure differ, or only the report?
- someone absorbed in a film who, asked afterwards, says yeah, they were enjoying it — but there was never a moment of "ahh, pleasure"
- a pill that makes you pursue something much harder and consume much more of it, while you insist it isn't any more pleasant than before
- the sad song, the nostalgia, the bittersweet thing: pleasure that seems to be inside the sadness rather than next to it
- relief — the scare is over, nothing pleasant has been added, something unpleasant just stopped
- the masochist, or anyone who enjoys what they'd rather not enjoy
- anticipation: the wanting itself feeling good
- and one where the inner state is held fixed and only the cause changes — does anything about the pleasure change?

Positions that need to come out as labels in the key (standard names where there are standard names, plain descriptions where not): (1) pleasure is one distinctive sensation/feeling-quality, present in all of them; (2) it's an attitude — liking, being attracted to a state of affairs — that can take any content; (3) it's a mode of experiencing rather than a thing experienced, a way any content can be given; (4) functionalist: whatever plays the role — reinforcement, choice, learning — is the pleasure, felt quality optional; (5) no common property at all, family resemblance/prototype category; (6) eliminativist: "pleasure" is folk vocabulary, not a natural kind, keep it for prediction only; (7) hybrid — felt tone plus functional role, neither one alone. If students will end up somewhere that isn't on that list, add it. I'd rather the questions fall out of the real disagreements (sensation vs attitude, occurrent vs dispositional, what makes it pleasant vs what causes it, one thing vs many) than out of a list of authors — and no citations, I just want the positions.

Two extra things: tell me which of those seven my cases can't actually pull apart, and which questions are decorative; and if some answer pattern doesn't land on any view anyones actually held, say so instead of inventing a label for it. Under 1000 words, and no single question longer than three lines.
