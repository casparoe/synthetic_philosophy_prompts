I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy training to speak of, and I keep hitting this "simplicity" thing whenever I have to choose between two explanations for the same thing. I've got a pile of gut calls and I want to know if there's one principle behind them or if I'm just being inconsistent. I think some CS people have names for parts of this (shortest-program stuff, a universal prior) but I never really got it, and I don't want a history lesson or a reading list, just the idea.

Whenever two stories both account for what I saw, I feel like the "simpler" one deserves more of my belief. But I can't pin down what simpler is. Here's my stuff:

1. Two programs both print the same 1000-character file. One is ~30 lines. The other is 900 lines of spaghetti that hardcodes the output. I say the 30-line one is the better explanation. Easy.

2. I fit some noisy measurements. A straight line fits okay. A degree-20 polynomial passes exactly through every point. I trust the line way more, even though the polynomial is literally truer to the data.

3. Short string, "0101". Option A: it's an alternating pattern. Option B: it's a lucky coincidence. I lean coincidence, I don't feel there's enough there to call it a pattern.

4. Same deal but 600 characters of "010101...". Now I'm certain it's the pattern. So my verdict flips with length, which is weird because it's the same kind of claim.

5. The string is the first 600 digits of pi. Option A: "it's the digits of pi." Option B: "it's random." I pick A.

6. Now the one that bugs me. Same pi string, but option B isn't "random," it's "someone generated 600 random digits and then a cosmic ray flipped them into exactly the pi digits." I also reject that. But the cosmic-ray story and the pi story predict the exact same string, so this isn't about prediction at all. It's about how short the process description is. Yet in case 2 I said I care about how well it fits. Those can't both be the whole story.

7. Fair coin vs. a coin biased at p = 0.5000001. With 20 flips I say fair. With 10^9 flips and the bias clearly showing, I say biased. Flips with data again.

8. Here's my tentative principle, tell me if it holds: the explanation to believe is the one with the shortest total description, where total = (length of the hypothesis) + (length of the data given the hypothesis).

9. Test it on this: "the data is just noise" vs "there's a hidden law with 3 free parameters." With 5 points I say noise. With 5000 points I say hidden law. Does the principle get that? And what does "noise" even describe?

10. Language worry. I write hypotheses as programs. Switch to a language where "alternating 01" is a single built-in opcode, case 3 gets cheaper. Switch to one where the pi digits are a single opcode, case 5 gets cheaper. Does that mean simplicity is relative to my programming language, i.e. to me? My gut says no, it's about the world, but I can't see how to cash that out without picking a language, and picking one feels arbitrary.

11. All emeralds I've seen are green, vs. all of them are grue (green until next Tuesday, then blue). I say green. A friend points out grue is just as short if you take grue as a basic word. So is my call about the world or about my vocabulary?

12. Conspiracy case: "the measurements follow this law" vs "a huge conspiracy is fabricating the measurements to look like that law." The conspiracy story is short to state, explains anything, and I disbelieve it. Does my principle in 8 say why?

What I want: tell me what principle, or small set of competing principles, actually captures these calls. State it precisely. For each clause, point at the case that forces it. Then tell me which of my verdicts it can't keep and which I should give up. Also say what it predicts about a case I didn't list, pick a good one yourself. And one more thing, which I suspect is the real crux: is "simpler" a fact about the hypothesis, about the hypothesis plus the data, or about the hypothesis plus the data plus me (my language, my priors)? My cases seem to pull all three ways. If there's a way to have all three, I want the way. If not, tell me which one to drop.
