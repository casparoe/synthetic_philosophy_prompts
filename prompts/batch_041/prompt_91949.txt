my brother-in-law keeps saying singapore is basically a well run company: the government is management, the citizens are the shareholders, and the product is prosperity and order. management gets judged on whether it delivers, and if it delivers, who cares whether the shareholders got to elect the board. he uses this to say performance legitimacy is enough — growth and safety are the dividends, and dividends are what owners actually want, so the democracy question is beside the point.

how far does it actually hold? start with the correspondence table — what maps onto the shareholders, the board, the shares, the dividends, the profit and loss statement, the takeover, the bankruptcy — then the breaks, then which breaks actually matter for the conclusion he wants and which are trivia. a few things im unsure about and want you to rule on: are citizens really shareholders, or are they closer to residual claimants who cant sell out, cant diversify (you only get one state), and cant be bought out? does it matter that the state's product is largely a public good nobody can be excluded from, and that its toolkit includes coercion — police, prisons, tax — which no firm is allowed to sell? and does the firm picture quietly depend on the market test (bankruptcy, takeover) that a state doesn't face, unless you count emigration and capital flight — and if you do count those, does that help him or hurt him?

finish by saying whether his conclusion survives the disanalogies, and if it doesn't, whether there's a better analogy for whatever is right in his point.
