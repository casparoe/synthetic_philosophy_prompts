I've been reading a lot of Szasz lately and I think I basically buy the line, but my version of it is my own and I want to know if it actually hangs together before I go defend it anywhere. Here's where I've landed:

1. "Mental illness" is a metaphor, not a real illness. A disease is defined by a bodily pathology; where there's no pathology you've got a problem in living, not a disease. Look at general paresis — the moment they found the spirochete in the brain, it stopped being a mental illness and became a brain disease. I take that as the model case, not an embarrassment.
2. I'm not a dualist though. Mind is brain, and I'm happy to say some things we call mental disorders just are brain diseases — Huntington's, syphilis in the brain, whatever was going on with Phineas Gage after the rod.
3. Involuntary psychiatric treatment is wrong. It's coercion in a white coat, and the coat doesn't launder it.
4. People are responsible for what they do, full stop. I'd scrap the insanity defense.
5. None of this is me saying suffering isn't real. I'm not telling miserable people they're making it up.
