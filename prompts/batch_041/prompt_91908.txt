I am preparing a position statement for a workshop on the norms that govern cooperative arrangements, and I would like an adversarial audit of it before I circulate it. The commitments below are the ones I currently hold. Some I have argued for at length; others I have absorbed without much scrutiny. I am not asking for a survey of the literature on any of them — I want to know what my own set costs me.

1. When I ask whether a proposed change to the rules of an interaction is an improvement — a renegotiated protocol, a changed default, a transfer, a change in who controls what — I do not want the verdict to depend on my beliefs about how the parties will behave. The test I want to apply is this: the change must leave every party at least as well off as the original arrangement under every way of playing the new arrangement that is compatible with the structural assumptions we are entitled to make about how games are played. By a structural assumption I mean one that connects the two arrangements in virtue of their form — for instance, that two arrangements with the same structure will be played in the same way — rather than a claim about which of the several possible ways of playing a given arrangement will in fact occur. A change that passes this test I call safe; changes of this kind are sometimes described in the literature as safe improvements on the original arrangement. I do not regard safety as an optional refinement. A change that fails the test is not justified by the fact that it improves matters under the play I happen to expect, since the change itself perturbs the behavior on which that expectation rests.

2. Where credences are available, expected utility maximization is the fundamental standard for what an agent ought to do, and no agent is ever required to forgo an option that maximizes expected utility in order to satisfy a requirement of robustness. Robustness is a way of managing uncertainty about other agents, not a further demand layered on top of expected utility.

3. The criterion should be neutral about how the parties choose. It should not presuppose that they maximize expected utility, or that they play equilibrium strategies, or that they hold credences at all; the point of the criterion is that it be usable by parties who disagree about the theory of rational choice. Of the items on this list, I take this one to be the least contentious.

4. The parties' preferences enter the comparison as inputs. I take no view about where those preferences come from, and I do not think a theory of justified rule change is in the business of telling people what they ought to want.

5. Take an arrangement that shifts the terms of an interaction toward the weaker party — a renegotiated protocol, a changed default, a transfer. I hold that such a change is justified, and that the fact that a party's delegates might respond in ways that leave that party worse off than it would have been under the previous arrangement does not count against the change's justification. A party that ends up worse off is owed an argument, not a veto.

6. A safe change leaves every party better off with certainty. But in any actual interaction the outcome is genuinely open: I do not know how the parties will play, no one does, and in general there is no fact of the matter about how the interaction would have gone had the rules been different.

7. The safety test asks only whether the parties to the interaction are better off. I also hold that the interests of those who are not party to it — bystanders, those excluded from the arrangement, people who will exist later — count for just as much as the interests of the parties, and that an arrangement can be wrong even though every party to it benefits.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Tell me which of these seven pull against one another, which only appear to, and how severe each genuine conflict is. For each genuine conflict, say which item has to give and what I lose by giving it up. Then tell me which single revision repairs the most. I would rather be told that one item is doing all the damage than receive a list in which everything is flagged. Where a conflict is only apparent, say precisely which distinction dissolves it, and whether that distinction is one I am entitled to draw or one I am simply stipulating.
