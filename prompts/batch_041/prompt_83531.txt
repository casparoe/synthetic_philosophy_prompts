My business partner and I are winding down our company and I've realized I have no idea what I actually think is fair, so I want you to reverse-engineer me. I'll give you my gut verdicts on a few versions of the split, and you tell me what principle, if any, they add up to. Assume throughout that the business is worth nothing unless the two of us do a deal together, and that we're free to divide the proceeds however we like.

1. One buyer, $100k, no strings. If we can't agree, we both walk away with nothing. Gut: $50k each, and nothing else feels even close.

2. Same buyer, same $100k, but this time I have a standing offer from an old colleague to buy our client list for $60k, and she's got nothing like that. Gut: I take $80k, she takes $20k. The $40k that only exists because the two of us cooperate gets cut in half.

3. Same numbers as 2, except the $60k isn't a standing offer, it's what I could get by quietly taking our three biggest clients with me on the way out. I wouldn't do it, it'd be a rotten thing to do, but I could. Gut: $80k/$20k feels wrong here in a way it doesn't in case 2, even though the arithmetic is identical. I want to say she should get alot more than $20k, but I can't say how much, and the only difference I can point to is the story about where my $60k comes from.

4. Back to something like 2, but now my walk-away number is $30k and hers is $0, the total is still $100k, and this time she's the one who did about 80% of the work over the years while I mostly coasted. Gut: I know I can't go below $30k, but something closer to even, $50k/$50k, feels right, and $65k/$35k feels like it's cheating her.

5. Now there are three of us. All three together can still get the $100k, and none of us has a fallback alone, but she and the third one have a little side thing going that's worth $60k to the two of them without me. Gut: I should get about $20k and they split the $80k. I don't know where the $20k comes from except that me joining turns their $60k into $100k, and half of that extra $40k is $20k.

A friend of mine took a game theory class and says the rule is this: each person should end up the same fraction of the way from what they'd walk away with to the best they could possibly do. She says that gives 50/50 in case 1 and 80/20 in case 2, that it's definately the only rule that doesn't care whether you measure in dollars or percentages or apples, and that it has a nice property most rules don't, where making the pie bigger in a way that favors you can never leave you worse off. I don't follow the last two bits at all.

So: what principle, if any, gets my verdicts? Do I have to give one of them up, and if so which? Does the friend's rule get cases 3, 4 and 5, and if not, can it be patched without turning into a different rule? And tell me what your favored principle predicts for a case I haven't described: the assets can't be cut in half, so one of us ends up with the equipment lease and the other with the client list, and there's no cash lying around to even things out.
