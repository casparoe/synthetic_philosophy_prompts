I'm a novelist, working on a book with a philosopher in it, and I'd rather not make an arse of myself. She gives a speech where she makes what she calls a transcendental argument: that we can only grieve for people who never existed, argue about what they'd of done, and feel they've let us down because those people are real in some sense — there has to be something our talk about them is about, or the whole thing falls apart. As I understand it, 'transcendental' means the argument goes beyond what we can observe to establish how things really are, so if she's right then my characters genuinely do exist in some sense, and I can end the novel on that. Is that how it works? And how far does the argument actually reach before I stake the ending on it?
