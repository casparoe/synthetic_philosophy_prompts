So I've been going back through the MIU-system chapter again, and the MU-puzzle part keeps bugging me, but not for the reason it bugs most people. I get that MU is unreachable. You start with MI, you've got the four rules (append a U to anything ending in I, double whatever comes after the M, swap any III for a U, delete any UU), and the trick is that the count of I's is never divisible by three -- MI has one, none of the rules can change that, and MU has zero. Fine, I'm not asking anyone to re-explain that.

What I actually want explained is the conceptual move underneath it. When I spot the invariant, the story goes that I've "jumped out of the system." But what is that, really? I can write a dumb little program that just crunches the derivation rules, and I can write another one that checks the mod-3 property, and both are still just programs pushing symbols around. So is JOOTSing a genuinely special act, or is it just "running a system that happens to talk about a different system"? If it's the second one, doesn't all the magic evaporate -- is there anything left that distinguishes jumping out from just... using more machinery?

And the thing I really can't get straight: people keep pairing this with the "inviolate level" idea from the Escher stuff, like Drawing Hands, where the two hands are drawing each other but something has to be doing the drawing that isn't itself one of the hands. How does that actually map onto MU? Is the level of the rules the inviolate level and the strings the tangled part? Or is the invariant argument itself the inviolate thing, sitting above both? And then the obvious follow-up -- can I jump out of the inviolate level too, or does the buck stop somewhere?

I'm not looking for the Gödel spiel again, I've had that drilled into me. I want to know specifically what the "out" in "jumping out" refers to, and whether there's a principled line between stepping out of a system and just stacking one more level on top. If your honest answer is "there's no principled line, it's systems describing systems all the way down," then say that and defend it, because that's the conclusion I keep half-suspecting and I want to know if it holds up.
