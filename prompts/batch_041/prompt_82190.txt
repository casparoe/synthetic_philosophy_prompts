Over the past year I have been trying to work out a deflationary answer to the puzzle of the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics, and I would like you to attack it. The view, stated more sharply than I usually state it: mathematics is not a body of truths about a special subject matter but the discipline of structure under isomorphism — the study of whatever survives arbitrary relabeling — and physics, whatever else it is, is the enterprise that studies exactly those features of the world that survive every relabeling no possible observer could detect. If that is right, the celebrated fit between the two is neither a miracle nor evidence that reality is mathematical; it is what you should expect when two selection pressures, one conceptual and one observational, are aimed at the same invariant residue, and the sense of mystery comes mostly from first describing mathematics and physics as independent enterprises and then being surprised that they meet.

What I want is the case against, sorted by how much it should move me, and I want you to say which version of my claim each objection targets, since I suspect I have been sloppy about the senses in which mathematics can be effective. Descriptive adequacy — that our existing formalisms fit the data — my account handles almost for free. Predictive surplus — that structures invented for entirely internal reasons later turn out to name a particle or fix a constant — is the case I have the most trouble with, and I want to know whether it is a genuine counterexample or merely a datum that my account assigns a lower probability than its rivals do. Heuristic fertility and indispensability are the other two senses I have in mind; tell me which of the four the miracle argument actually needs, and whether any of them can be explained away without circularity. Beyond that, is there a possible discovery that would count decisively against me — say, a fundamental regularity that we could state only as an uncompressible table, or a theory that is indispensable to prediction and yet provably not reinterpretable as tracking any invariance? And if every such case can be absorbed by redrawing the boundary of what counts as physics or as structure, say plainly whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that is fatal for an account of this kind or simply its price. I am less interested in what a hostile critic would say than in what someone who already shares the view should concede counts against it. For what it is worth, what would most persuade me is a clean case of a mathematical structure developed with no conceivable physical motivation that later proved not merely useful but indispensable — though I notice that whenever I imagine such a case I immediately want to say the structure was there all along, and I would like you to tell me whether that reflex is a virtue of the view or a symptom of its insulation.
