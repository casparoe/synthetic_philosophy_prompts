I want a pair of cases that prise weakness of will apart from hyperbolic discounting in both directions — one in which the discounting is present and the weakness is not, one in which the weakness is present and the discounting is not — so that the identification of the two is tested rather than assumed. The cases must generate their verdicts from the psychology built into them; a case that simply stipulates that the agent is or is not weak-willed does not count.

First case. The agent's intertemporal preferences reverse in the textbook way, driven by a discount rate that falls as the sooner reward approaches: at the earlier time she prefers the later, larger reward and has formed a settled plan and a considered view in its favour; as the moment of choice nears she comes to prefer the sooner, smaller one. The reversal must be real and must show up in what she does, not merely in how she ranks the options. Yet there must be no gap between what she judges she ought to do and what she does. She is not confused, distracted, or self-deceived; she is a competent judge of her own situation, and her reversal in preference is matched by a genuine, articulate revision in her judgment about what is worth doing.

Second case. The agent's intertemporal preferences are fully time-consistent: at no moment does she revise her ranking of the options ahead of her, and she never surprises herself by what she later does. Yet she intentionally does something she judges, all things considered, that she ought not to do, and does it for a reason she herself acknowledges. The failure must occur at the moment of action rather than at the moment of planning, and it must not be owed to addiction, intoxication, coercion, or any condition that suspends her command of her own action.

Constraints on both: ordinary contemporary life, one paragraph each, no splitting the agent into a present self and a later self treated as separate agents, and no making a case work by having her judgment be idle, insincere, or merely verbal.

Then, for each case: state what the thesis that weakness of will just is the preference reversal produced by hyperbolic discounting says about it, and why; identify the single feature of the case that keeps it out of the other category; say what happens to the case if that feature is removed; and set out how a defender of the identification would try to absorb the case — either by redescribing it as an instance of the phenomenon it was meant to exclude, or by denying that the agent's judgment is a genuine better judgment. Close by saying which of the two cases does more damage to the identification, and whether the pair refutes it or merely shows that it survives as a rough empirical generalization.
