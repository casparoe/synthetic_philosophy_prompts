Write a mock internal document — a "Research Programme Performance Improvement Plan" (PIP) issued by the Research Integrity Office of an imaginary institute to one of its research programmes, which has been flagged as degenerating. Aim for roughly 650–800 words. Sustain a deadpan administrative register throughout: numbered clauses, defined terms, compliance deadlines, an appeals procedure, a signature block. There should be no narrator who admits the joke and no winking; the document must read as if written in perfect earnest by an office that has fully absorbed a Lakatosian appraisal policy without understanding it.

The document must presuppose the following policy, which the institute treats as its own house style: a research programme consists of a hard core of assumptions that its adherents refuse to abandon by methodological decision, surrounded by a protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses that absorb anomalies; it is appraised not by its successes or failures but by its heuristic — a programme is progressive when its belt-generating heuristic predicts novel facts, and degenerating when it merely offers post-hoc accommodations of facts already known or already predicted by rivals. Let the office's demands be exactly what this criterion requires and what a real administrator would garble: the programme must "generate novel predictions" by a stated date, must "cease unauthorized modifications to the protective belt," and must "demonstrate that recent belt revisions were not ad hoc." The comedy should arise from the impossibility of doing any of this on a deadline — the hard core cannot be revised by contract, a positive heuristic cannot be scheduled, and the distinction between a novel prediction and a post-hoc save is precisely the distinction the document needs to draw and cannot. Include at least one clause in which the office, without noticing, instructs the programme to protect its hard core more rigidly, which is the opposite of what would make it progressive; include an appeals clause whose criteria are vacuous; and let a rival programme be invoked as a benchmark in a way that quietly concedes the rival is doing better.

Review the following invented programme, and invent further belt revisions as needed. The Aurelio programme, in tidal geophysics, holds a hard core of three commitments: (1) every large-scale tidal pattern is driven by a single resonant mode; (2) that mode's frequency is fixed by the body's rotation; (3) anomalies are absorbed by adjusting coupling coefficients, never by abandoning (1) or (2). Its protective belt has lately been patched to explain the Marnau anomaly by adding a damping term that predicted nothing new; to explain the Sefridge anomaly by widening the measurement window after the fact; and to account for the "quiet fortnight" phenomenon only after the rival Halvorsen programme had predicted it.

Do not mention or quote any real scientists, do not cite any works, and do not attribute the framework to anyone by name. A reader who knows the framework should recognize the target as fair rather than wince, and a reader who does not should still find the document funny and legible.

End with a single line beginning "Disposition:", stating in one sentence whether the office rates the programme progressive or degenerating and why.
