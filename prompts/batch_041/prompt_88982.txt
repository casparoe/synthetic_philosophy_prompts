I'm a third-year student taking a module on consciousness science, and the assessment is a short research proposal of roughly 1,500 words. My provisional idea sits in the area of global workspace theory and the access/phenomenal consciousness distinction. As I understand the debate, the theory is widely thought to give a satisfying account of access — information becoming globally available to a wide range of subsystems, and thereby to report, reasoning and the guidance of action — while leaving it unclear whether anything in the account explains why a broadcast state should feel like anything at all. I want to approach this from a different angle. I would like to introduce and defend a category I will call silent broadcast: a state whose content really is broadcast, in the sense that many subsystems are sensitive to it and it shapes behaviour, but which is never available to the processes that would let the subject report on it, reflect on it, or use it in deliberate inference. My hunch is that this case is precisely where the access/phenomenal distinction either earns its keep or collapses, and that asking whether silent broadcast is access-conscious, phenomenally conscious, both or neither is a more tractable question than the usual global one.
