so I'm writing a position paper on legal moralism — the Hart/Devlin thing, whether the criminal law can punish conduct that harms nobody but the person doing it — and before I commit to a draft I want to know whether my view is actually one view or three views wearing a trenchcoat. Here's where I've landed:

(a) It's legitimate for the criminal law to prohibit conduct that harms no one but the agent, so long as the conduct violates a moral standard that's genuinely shared in the society — not just a majority preference, but something basically everyone treats as binding on everyone.

(b) What makes that legitimate isn't that immorality is intrinsically bad, or that the law's job is to make people good. It's that a shared morality is part of what holds a society together, and a law that stops enforcing it lets the society come apart.

(c) But offence and disgust are never a reason for the state to act. That something grosses people out, or that they'd be upset to see it done, is not a justification for criminalizing it. I'd rather the law stay out.

(d) I'm a hard anti-paternalist: the state may not restrict what I do for my own good, full stop.

(e) When I say "shared morality" I mean what people in the society in fact overwhelmingly believe and do, not what they'd believe if they all thought harder about it. The law isn't a philosophy seminar and shouldn't be enforcing anybody's idealised moral code.

(f) One verdict I'm sure of: prosecuting adults for what they do to each other consensually and in private, where nobody is deceived and nobody is hurt — the old sodomy laws being the obvious case — is an injustice, and not just because those laws later became unpopular.

People keep telling me (a) and (d) can't both be mine but I don't see it. What I actually want is an audit: which pairs here are genuinly inconsistant, which only look it, and how bad the real ones are. Rank them, say for each what the cheapest revision would be and what that revision costs me, then tell me which single change cleans up the most. And if (f) has to go, say so.
