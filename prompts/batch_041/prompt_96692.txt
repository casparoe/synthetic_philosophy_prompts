I'm a second-year undergraduate in a seminar on Stoicism and its modern reception, and I've been asked to draft the practice exam our study group will use before the final. The unit everyone trips over is the dichotomy of control, and I want one essay question that can't be answered by reciting the familiar list of things that are and aren't up to us, or by paraphrasing the Serenity Prayer.

The question should force students to choose between three readings our seminar keeps running together: control as causal power, where an outcome is in my control whenever my action makes a difference to it; control as guarantee, where it is in my control only if nothing external can prevent it; and up to us as a matter of attribution, where what is up to me is whatever counts as my doing rather than as something that merely happens through me. A hostage who can talk a captor down but cannot compel him is a case where the three come apart, and I'd like the question to use it or something like it.

Please write the question, a model answer of roughly 700 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain. It should also make students say which reading the modern self-help revival actually needs, and whether the ancient doctrine survives being detached from the claim that only virtue is good, or whether it collapses into a technique for getting the outcomes you want. Mark the scheme out of 20, giving the marks for each move and, for each, one line on how students typically get it wrong.
