I am a student in a political theory seminar on historical injustice and collective responsibility, and I keep encountering a structure that I cannot match to any single term we have been given. The structure appears when a present-day political community is asked to acknowledge guilt for a wrong committed by an earlier state or regime. The wrong is attributed to the community as a whole rather than to named individuals. But the community that is asked to acknowledge it is not, as a decision-making body, the same community that committed it: the constitution has changed, the franchise has changed, some current members are descendants of perpetrators, some of victims, some of neither, and some are immigrants with no ancestral connection to the event at all. The acknowledgment is supposed to be collective and inherited, yet the only acts that can express it, such as official apologies, reparations, curriculum reform, or monuments, are acts of the present state, which is a different legal and political agent from the one that acted. The result is a demand for guilt that is collective and intergenerational but not attached to any continuous collective agent. It is not merely collective guilt, because collective guilt can be synchronous and can attach to a group that did act together. It is not merely inherited guilt, because that phrase suggests a quasi-metaphysical transmission of culpability, whereas the structure I am describing can be generated by institutional continuity alone. It is not merely beneficiary responsibility, because some of those asked to acknowledge may not have benefited, and some beneficiaries may be outside the group. And it is not merely structural injustice, because the wrong is a past event, not an ongoing system, even if its effects persist. Is there an established name or literature for this exact structure, namely responsibility that is collective and intergenerational but discontinuous in agency? If there is, I would like to know what it is and how it differs from the nearest concepts. If there is not, I would like you to coin a term and define it, specifying what falls inside and outside it, and explaining how it relates to collective guilt, inherited guilt, beneficiary responsibility, and structural injustice. I am not satisfied with a bare label; I need the carving.
