Right, I'm putting together a set of essay prompts for a philosophy reading group and I need one on prioritarianism and the levelling-down objection, but pitched so that people can't just recite the standard moves back at me. Here's the angle.

The usual story goes: telic egalitarianism gets clobbered by the levelling-down objection -- dragging the better off down without helping anyone is supposed to count as an improvement for equality's sake, which is absurd -- and prioritarianism dodges all that because it only cares about how well off people are in absolute terms, not about the size of the gap. Fine. But I want the essay to interrogate whether that escape is real or just a relabelling.

To make it concrete, use this case, which I've made up so there's no canned answer floating about for it. Two villages, Low and High. Low is badly off, High is much better off. There's a dam project that diverts water from High to Low. Build it and Low's situation improves a little -- measurably, but honestly not by much -- while High's situation gets substantially worse, though High is still better off than Low afterwards. So building the dam lowers the total and narrows the gap. Now, the standard objection says it's absurd to prefer a world where you drag the better off down for no gain to anyone. But here there is a gain, to the worse off. So the real question is whether the prioritarians willingness to accept a large loss to High for a small gain to Low is a genuine escape from the objection or the same vice wearing a different hat.

Some threads I want pulled on, roughly in this order:

- whether the answer turns on how much priority you give the worse off -- i.e. whether that priority is absolute/lexical or merely a steep concave weighting -- and what happens to the view at each extreme
- what separability and continuity are doing here, and whether you can keep the levelling-down-avoiding feature while also keeping continuity
- the sub-threshold twist: suppose Low's gain is so small it wouldn't even be noticeable to the people in Low, while High's loss is enormous. Does the prioritarian still have to say build the dam? Does that change anything?
- whether the objection is really about equality at all, or whether it's secretly about something like the person-affecting restriction

Then reach a verdict and defend it. I don't want a wishy-washy "it depends on your weighting" ending -- if you think it depends, say on what, and then commit to which side of that you land on. Aim for maybe 1200-1500 words, and write it in American English.
