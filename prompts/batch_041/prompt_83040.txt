I hold the following view about vagueness, and I want to know what should move me off it. Vague predicates are semantically underdetermined: the meaning of a word like 'heap' leaves open a range of admissible sharpenings, each of which draws a precise line, and a sentence is true when it comes out true on every admissible sharpening, false when it comes out false on every one, and otherwise neither true nor false. I want to hold this while refusing two things. I won't say the world itself is vague — the grains are arranged however they are arranged, and it is my language that fails to decide — and I won't say there is a sharp line that we merely cannot know, since that reinstates exactly what vagueness was supposed to be about. I also want classical logic to survive: excluded middle should hold, and I should be able to say that it is true that he is bald whenever I can say that he is bald.

What would count against this? Give me the arguments, cases, and formal results that should move me, ranked by how decisive they would be rather than by how often they are cited, including ones a defender would have to treat as costing something. Tell me where the view's own commitments collide — above all anything showing that it collapses into the epistemic view I rejected, or that 'admissible sharpening' is only a name for what we happen to accept, or that the notion of admissibility is itself vague in a way the theory cannot absorb. Say what the view predicts that its rivals do not, and whether it risks anything at all or has been insulated by qualification. Then say which defeaters a committed defender would concede and which they would dismiss as one more borderline case.

Answer with: 'The view, restated' (a short paragraph, under 150 words); 'What would count against it' (the bulk of the answer, each item stating what it would show and how much it would cost); 'What the view predicts' (short); 'What a defender concedes' (short); 'Where it risks nothing' (short); and 'My verdict' (a few sentences on whether the view is well insulated and whether that is a problem). British English throughout.
