The idea: people talk about the longue durée as if "slow structures explain" were one kind of claim, but I think there are two, and the Braudel debates (and the recent revival of the longue durée in global and climate history) slide between them. One is a filter/constraint reading: the slow variable doesn't cause the event, it makes certain events impossible or cheap and others expensive — it's a selection effect over the space of what could happen. The other is a constitution reading: the slow structure is what makes the relevant entities count as entities at all (what counts as a "market", a "peasant household", a "state" in a given setting), so it's prior to the event not in time but in the order of explanation. I suspect alot of the heat in the "does geography/climate really explain history" fight comes from sliding between these, and that some standard moves — the event is just foam on the wave, the individual is a fiction of the short term — are only defensible on one of the two readings and look silly on the other.
