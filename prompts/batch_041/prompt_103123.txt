Bit of a weird one — I'm a software engineer (backend, no philopsophy background) and I've been reading AI safety stuff on the side, and I keep running into this idea that we can build agents that are "low impact" or that "avoid side effects": do the thing you were asked to do, but don't wreck everything else in the process. It sounds so clean when people say it, and then it falls apart the moment I try to imagine actually implementing it. In a little gridworld it's fine — walk to the goal, don't knock the vase over — but the second the task is open-ended I can't tell what the "side" in side effect is even supposed to be relative to. If I'm asked to make coffee, is rearranging the kitchen a side effect? Is failing to tell my neighbor his house is on fire a side effect of me making coffee? And a really timid agent that does nothing at all has basically zero impact, so that obviously can't be the standard. I've also noticed the measures I've seen seem to need either a baseline of "what would have happened otherwise" or some set of other goals to protect, and both of those look like they smuggle a value judgment in somewhere.

So before I read any more of this, I want the requirements list: what does any account of impact / side effects have to get right to be worth anything at all? Concretely — which cases it has to handle (the vase thing, the agent that does nothing, an agent that destroys information, an agent that keeps a human from hitting the off switch, an agent that breaks something on purpose to prevent something worse), which distinctions it absolutely cannot collapse (side effect vs. the main effect you actually wanted, impact vs. real harm, reversible vs. irreversible, the agent's impact vs. everyone else's, physical footprint vs. informational), and what it has to be consistent with: it needs to be computable or at least estimable, it can't require the agent to already know the full value function (or can it? honestly that's part of what I want to find out), and it can't make the agent useless.

For each requirement, tell me the case or intuition behind it, and say which ones are genuinely non-negotiable versus which are just things one camp of researchers happens to care about. Then the part I most want: tell me whether some subset of these can't be satisfied together. My gut says any measure defined without reference to what we actually value will either forbid stuff we want (helping, intervening, breaking the window to get the kid out) or permit stuff we don't, and if that's right I want to see it laid out as a conflict between the requirements rather than just asserted. If it's easy, score the two families of proposals I've seen — the ones that measure how much you shrink the set of reachable futures, and the ones that measure how much you degrade your ability to hit some set of auxiliary goals — against the list, but keep that part short; the criteria are what I'm after. No citations needed, I'm not going to chase papers down, I just want the thinking.
