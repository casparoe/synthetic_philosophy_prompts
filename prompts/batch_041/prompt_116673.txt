ok so I want to pick a fight with a real federalist, not the cartoon version, so here's the setup. Play someone who honestly believes in ever closer union and thinks the democratic deficit is real but is a problem of *incomplete* integration, not a reason to slow down. Not a eurocrat, no press release voice -- you argue, you get specific, you can ask me questions back. You can give ground on facts (the Commission isn't answerable to anyone in a way a voter would recognize, the spitzenkandidat thing got quietly shelved, turnout is grim) but you never concede the main claim, which is that there's nothing structurally undemocratic about the EU. Your line is that demoi get made, not found, and that the no-demos arguement is a rationalization that would have sounded just as good about the United States in 1790. One paragraph per turn, stay in character until I say stop, and please don't break out of it to hand me a balanced survey of both sides -- I know both sides, I want you.

Here's my opening move. I think you've got the deficit in the wrong dimension. Everyone fights about the spatial version -- Brussels is far away, voters can't reach it -- which I think is a seperate and much less interesting problem. Look at where the power actually sits instead: the bank, the fiscal rules, competition policy, state aid, the whole ordoliberal kit. None of that is insulated from national voters; it's insulated from any voters at all, national or European, and that was the point. The member states built it as a commitment device so that nobody, including their own future electorates, could undo the bargain. Which means your cure misses the disease: a stronger European Parliament doesn't touch any of it, because the insulation isn't a side effect of the Parliament being weak, it's the architecture. And the obvious fix -- let a European majority un-commit, rewrite the bank's mandate, blow through the fiscal rules -- tells the Germans the euro is a paper promise again, and we're back to the credibility problem that built the thing in the first place. So which do you give up, the democracy or the commitment? Or is there a third option I'm not seeing?
