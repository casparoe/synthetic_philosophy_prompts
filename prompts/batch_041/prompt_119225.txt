I serve on the ethics advisory board of a mid-sized mental-health app company, and I've been using the app myself since a difficult year of caregiving, which is how I first noticed the feature at issue. The company markets its approach as a modern revival of Stoicism, and its flagship exercise is a dichotomy tool: users log a worry, the app asks them to sort it into 'within my control' or 'not within my control,' and the second pile is archived with a prompt to let it go. The product team now wants to ship a third category, 'within our shared control' — meaning outcomes that depend on other people's choices and can be changed only by negotiation, coordination, or collective action — and to route those worries into a new 'influence together' module with suggested conversation scripts. They also want to display a 'shared-control ratio' next to the existing 'control ratio' on the home screen and to nudge users whose shared-control ratio is high toward those scripts. The clinical advisors on the board worry that this dilutes the Stoic point and gives users a false sense of agency over other people; the accessibility and disability advocates worry that the original two-category tool already slides into victim-blaming, because it treats systemic barriers as things to release rather than contest. I'm the one who has to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's vote, and I need it to recommend a concrete decision — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with a time frame and a clear rationale, not just a summary of the debate.

Please act as a philosopher with expertise in Hellenistic ethics and the ethics of mental-health technology. Think carefully and begin by brainstorming the strongest case for each of the three options, then draft the memo I'll circulate. It should engage the losing side's best argument honestly, say what the board should do and by when, and explain how the decision treats the difference between a worry that is genuinely outside one's control and one that is outside one's individual control but inside a group's. I'd also like you to state how confident you are in the recommendation and to single out the one point in your reasoning you are least sure about.
