ok so for a seminar wrap-up i want a poem that *is* the discursive dilemma (the doctrinal paradox thing — group thats perfectly consistent item by item and inconsistent all at once), not a poem that just gestures at it. so it needs a structure a reader can actually check. heres what I want:

three tercets, one per voter — A, B, C — all three voting on the same three questions about one small concrete dispute (a broken boiler, a hiring call, a penalty in a match, whatever; pick something with a bit of texture, not p's and q's). the three questions: whether p, whether q, and whether r, where r is just p-and-q together, the conclusion. every voter answers all three, and every voters three answers have to be consistent with each other. so:

A: p, q, r
B: p, not-q, not-r
C: not-p, q, not-r

nobody in there is being stupid or inconsistent, thats the point. the link between p, q and r isnt up for a vote, its just the logic of the case.

now in each tercet, the two lines that are going to end up in the majority should rhyme with each other, and the one thats going to lose should be left without a rhyme partner, hanging. so the rhyming pair in each stanza is the coalition that carries that proposition.

fourth stanza, same three-line shape: made only of lines lifted from the first three, one per voter, and every line in it has to be a line that appeared in two of the three voter stanzas, i.e. a line that won. so it should come out as p, q and not-r — all three majority-supported, and all three impossible to hold at once, since p and q together give you r. and it should be unrhymed, because each of those lines had a rhyme partner back in its own stanza and none of the partners are here.

then one more line on its own, and this is the kicker: the answer you get if you dont vote on r at all, you just take the groups verdict on p and the groups verdict on q and let the logic run. that gives you r. so the poem ends with the voted not-r and the derived r sitting next to each other, neither of them wrong.

dont editorialize at the end — no closing line about the fragility of democracy or the wisdom of crowds, the clash should do it. plain title is fine, just dont let the title explain the trick, and no epigraph.

then under the poem, two sentences telling me which line came from which voter, so I can check the lift. near enough counts as lifted, it just has to be recognisably the same line.
