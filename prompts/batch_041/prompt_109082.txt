I've got a seminar slot with our invertebrate neuro group in a few weeks and I want to use it well. The room is mostly Drosophila and locust people who've spent decades on olfactory circuits and motor control, one postdoc who works on bee cognition, and a philosopher of mind who comes to everything. Empiricists to a person; they think the behavior-to-experience inference is where sloppy science goes to die.

The arguement I'm putting up: industrially farmed insects -- mealworms, crickets, black soldier fly larvae -- should count as the biggest animal welfare problem going, and the fact that we can't say whether they feel pain isnt a reason to wait and see.

The move I expect to get shredded, so help me see it coming: don't discount a creature by your credence that it's sentient, compare your credence to how many individuals are in play. If I'm 1% confident a species is sentient, then any practice that touches more than about a hundred of them has to be treated as morally live, and insect farms touch billions a year, so that threshold got crossed a long time ago. The low probability doesn't get anybody off the hook, it just sets the headcount at which you have to start caring. I have a pat answer that this isnt Pascal's wager because the stake scales with the number of individuals instead of going to infinity, and I don't entirely believe it myself. I also want to claim there's a principled place to stop somewhere -- not neuron count but whether the animal can be motivated against its own escape reflex -- and I suspect the evidence there is thinner than I'm letting on.

Prep me for the question period. Give me the objections this room will actually raise, in their vocabulary, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Tell me what to concede in the first thirty seconds and what I should hold. Tell me the one reply that has to land or the whole talk sinks. And the question I'm hoping nobody asks. No citations and dont invent studies -- if an objection turns on some particular finding, describe the finding and tell me to go check it.
