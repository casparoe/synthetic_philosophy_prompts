Knowledge graphs and the Semantic Web encode a fact not as a sentence but as a bundle of three slots — a subject, a predicate, and an object packed into one record (an 'RDF triple'), so that the claim that Paris is the capital of France is stored as Paris / capital-of / France. Formal logic does something similar when it writes a proposition as an ordered tuple of a predicate and its arguments, and a database does it when it stores a fact as a row. Treat this as a design choice and ask what it presupposes about the unity of the proposition. If a proposition is just its constituents bundled together, what is supposed to make the bundle one thing rather than several — and does the triple format quietly assume an answer, or does it merely hide the question by putting the constituents into a container? Bradley's regress says that any relation invoked to tie constituents together must itself be tied to them, and so on without end; does the triple escape that regress, or is it precisely the picture that generates it? Reconstruct the implicit theory of unity, distinguish what the format strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with, say which rival accounts (a primitive tie or act of predication, Frege-style unsaturated concepts, a multiple-relation view of judgment, propositions as facts rather than containers) it rules out and which it leaves open, and say what would have to change in the representation if one took the regress seriously.

Deliver the analysis as a table, one row per design feature, with these columns: Design feature (the specific choice in the representation — e.g., the predicate occupying its own slot, the fixed arity of three, the ordered positions, the record treated as a single unit); Presupposed commitment about unity; Rival account it excludes, or none; Verdict (answers the regress / sidesteps it / presupposes it); Underdetermined? (yes or no, with the rival views still compatible). Follow the table with no more than three sentences naming the single feature you judge to be doing the most unacknowledged work, and why.
