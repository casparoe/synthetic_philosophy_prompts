I keep running into game theorists and economists going on about common knowledge of rationality, and I want to know how much of that debate survives being put into my vocabulary.

Write as someone who knows epistemic game theory and the distributed computing literature equally well — the kind of person who can do this translation honestly rather than just dressing up one side in the other's words. Before you write anything, spend a bit of space brainstorming: list the moving parts on each side, and flag which ones you expect to line up and which look suspicious. Then do the translation. I'd rather have a careful few thousand words than a shallow sketch, but don't pad.

The translation first. Restate, as faithfully as you can, the main claims and arguements about common knowledge of rationality in epistemic game theory — why it's supposed to be needed for backward induction in long finite games and for iterated elimination of dominated strategies, the results about how many levels of mutual knowledge buy you how many steps of reasoning, the agreement and no-trade style results, and the "almost common knowledge" results where an arbitrarily small amount of uncertainty blows up coordination. Do it in terms I actually use: protocols and strategies, message delivery guarantees, knowledge levels as a hierarchy of acknowledgements, common knowledge as (I think?) a fixed point of the everybody-knows operator rather than as the tower, the gap between what's true of the system and what any process can know, timeouts, failure detectors. Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. What went through cleanly? What only went through with distortion, and what got distorted? What in their debate has no counterpart at all in my vocabulary? And I want a real verdict on the rationality half, not a both-sides shrug. Common knowledge in my world is knowledge of a fact about channel state — either the system has it or it doesn't. Rationality looks like a different kind of animal: a property of an agent's choice rule, counterfactual (it has to hold at nodes that never get reached), and required of the other player at exactly the nodes that rationality itself rules out. Is that a genuine mismatch, something my framework can't express? One thing I've been chewing on and want your take on: a failure detector is the closest thing I have to knowing what another process will do, and it's explicitly allowed to be wrong — it outputs a suspicion, not knowledge.
