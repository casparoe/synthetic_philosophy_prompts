the regress, quick version so we're on the same page: take a proposition like a is F. youve got the object a, the property F, and what looks like a relation holding between them, instantiation. if instantiation is a third thing sitting alongside a and F, then something has to tie *it* to a and F, so now youve got a fourth thing, then a fifth, forever, and the proposition never actually gets built. if instead instantiation is nothing over and above a and F, then theres nothing there doing any unifying and you just have two items lying next to each other, which isnt a proposition. either way unity looks impossible.

heres the line i want you to defend: what if the regress is really a loop. the relation that unifies a and F is itself one of the things unified by that very relation -- not a fresh relation at each step, just the same one coming back around. the proposition isnt assembled from below in stages; its a closed structure, held together the way a knot is held together by being tied rather than by an extra piece of rope. i want you to argue that this genuinely answers the unity problem rather than relabeling it, and that it survives the obvious charge that a circle is worse than an infinite descent.
