I'm on my state's sentencing commission and we've got a weird case: a man convicted of fraud, and his elderly mother—who's in poor health—has volunteered to serve his prison sentence for him. The defense is calling this a form of penal substitution and arguing that if we really believed in vicarious punishment we'd allow it. Half the commission thinks it's absurd, half thinks there's a real point buried in there. I need to draft our written decision: recommend whether to accept the substitution, with a clear bottom line and a timeline for whatever we decide. In the reasoning, I want you to seriously engage the argument that punishing an innocent volunteer might satisfy some purposes of punishment—like restitution, if she pays back the money—but not others, like deterrence or retribution. And I want you to address the tension that lots of people accept penal substitution in theology but reject it in criminal justice; are they being inconsistent, or is there a principled line? Keep it tight, maybe 800 words.
