I'm in a little Zoom reading group on decision theory — five of us, we meet Thursdays — and last week we got stuck on "safe Pareto improvements." The gist, the way I understood it: sometimes you and another player both prefer to move from a lousey equilibrium to a better outcome, but a plain Pareto improvement is a trap, because it's only an improvement if the other player actually goes along. A *safe* one is a change to the game that leaves you no worse off than the status quo no matter what the other player does — including if they play something weird, spiteful, or flat-out irrational. Only then is the improvement really safe. One of us sketched a concrete game to argue about and now I can't stop thinking about it.

Here's the game: two players, each picks C or D at the same time. Both pick C, you each get 3. Both pick D, you each get 2. One picks C and the other D, the D-player gets 5 and the C-player gets 0. So (C,C) beats (D,D) for both of us — but if I play C and you don't, I eat a 0, which is worse than the 2 I'd have gotten by just playing D. That's the trap. Now here's the list of "fixes" people threw out, and I want you to rank them from best to worst as *safe Pareto improvements* — mostly by how airtight the guarantee is that I never drop below 2 whatever the other player does, but also counting whether the thing actually delivers the 3,3 when the other player cooperates. (a) Both of us sign a contract enforced by an outside referee who, whenever one plays C and the other plays D, takes enough from the D-player to top the C-player back up to 2. (b) We agree to flip a fair coin to decide who plays C and who plays D, so each of us expects 2.5. (c) We both agree to play C, but each of us quietly keeps a "tremble" rule: if tempted, we play D. (d) We both delegate our move to a single mediator we both trust, who plays C for both of us. (e) I unilaterally burn my bridges so I'm physically unable to play D. (f) We each play C only if we get a cheap signal that the other is a "cooperator type." (g) We each run a no-regret rule: keep playing C as long as my running average is above 2, otherwise switch to D.

I know a couple of these are obviously duds, but I'm less sure about the middle of the pack, and I suspect at least one of them is safe in a boring technical sense while being useless, or vice versa. Give me the ordering, a short reason for each placement, and tell me how confident you are in the overall ranking — and single out the one placement you're least sure about.
