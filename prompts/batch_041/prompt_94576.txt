I work as a software engineer and have no formal philosophy training, but I have been drawn to a view I associate with Schrödinger and the Vedanta tradition: that consciousness is fundamentally singular, that there is exactly one subject of experience, and that the apparent multiplicity of minds is more like a set of partitions or virtual machines running on a single substrate than a plurality of basic subjects. I would like you to play this view straight and tell me what would count against it. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question. I want the evidence, arguments, intuitions, and possible cases that should move someone who holds this view, sorted by how decisive they would be, and I want an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability and whether that is a problem for a view of this kind.

Be concrete. I am especially interested in cases where the boundaries of a single consciousness seem to come apart or fail to line up with the boundaries of a single brain or body: split-brain patients, dissociative identity, craniopagus or conjoined twins who share neural tissue, and experiments that couple two brains through direct interfaces or shared sensory feedback. For each, say what the one-subject view would predict, what it can accommodate after the fact, and what would be a genuine anomaly rather than a puzzle it can redescribe. I also want the conceptual defeaters: if there is one subject, why is experience partitioned so cleanly that I cannot feel your pain or remember your breakfast, and why does a single subject not have contradictory experiences at the same time? Is the decombination problem a serious structural problem for this view, or can it be dissolved by saying that the one consciousness is not a subject in the ordinary sense? What would the view's defenders themselves concede as counting against them, not just what critics say? Finally, tell me which of these defeaters would be fatal, which would be merely awkward, and whether the view risks nothing at all. If it risks nothing, say so plainly and say whether that is a defect or simply the price of a metaphysical view.
