- A member of the group uses S to another member, joking around. Fine, or at least not my business.

- An outsider uses S as an insult. Obviously bad.
- An outsider uses S with zero insult intended, like quoting a line from a movie or telling a story about his grandpa. Still not fine to me.
- An outsider uses S warmly, as a nickname for a friend in the group, meaning it as affection. Also not fine, and honestly that one bothers me because he means well.
- A member uses S to insult another member. Bad.
- A member uses S toward someone who's already asked him to stop. Bad.
- Somebody uses S while explaining to a class that S is a slur. Fine.
- Somebody uses S to mock the kind of person who uses S. Not fine, somehow.
- The same sound S, but in a different language where it just means kitchen table. Fine, obviously.
- Somebody uses S about a person who isn't even in the group, they got them confused. Bad, but it feels different from the plain insult, less bad maybe?
- Somebody writes S in a private diary and never shows anybody. Honestly no idea. Maybe it's nothing?

The rule I keep reaching for is "it's about who's allowed to say it, not what they mean," but that can't be right, because the member insulting a member is a member and it's still bad, and the outsider meaning well is still bad. So it's gotta be some mix of who says it and what the word does, and I can't work out the mix.
