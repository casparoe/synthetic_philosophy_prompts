I keep noticing something about how I end up believing things, and I want to know if it has a name or if I'm just describing something obvious.

Here's the thing. Someone tells me something -- the pharmacy closes at six, the road's flooded, whatever -- and I notice that I never actually decide to believe it. The belief just shows up, already in place, the way a smell shows up. There's no moment where I weigh how trustworthy the person is. It feels much more like seeing than like reasoning.

But then if someone later asks me how I know, I produce a reason: "the pharmacist told me, and pharmacists don't lie about opening hours." And that reason feels fake, like I'm inventing it after the fact. The weird part is that I could produce a reason just as good for things I *don't* believe -- "my uncle told me the pharmacy is a front for the CIA, and he seemed sure." Same structure, same move. So the reason can't be what's doing the actual work of sorting the true from the false. It looks like believing is the default, and *not* believing is what needs a trigger -- and the trigger seems to be more about whether the claim fits with stuff I already think than about the speaker at all.

Two more bits that might be part of the same thing or might be separate. First, I often can't tell you *where* I got something -- was it a person, a podcast, did I read it? -- and yet I'd still defend it if pushed. Second, when the same person tells me the same surprising thing a fifth time, I start trusting it *less*, which seems backwards if each telling is supposed to be evidence.

So: is there an established name for this whole cluster -- the automatic acceptance, the after-the-fact reasons, the can't-remember-the-source, the repetition-backfiring? I have a feeling I've brushed against it somewhere, maybe something about whether you need to have reasons before you can trust anyone, or maybe about whether taking someone's word is more like seeing than like inferring. But I don't know what the actual positions are called or how they divide up, and I'd rather not be fobbed off with a single label. What I want is the carving: which of my four observations are the same phenomenon and which are different, what the nearest named ideas are and where each one fails to cover what I'm describing, and whether any of it is a genuine puzzle or just me narrating normal cognition. If there's no single name, I'd take a proposed one with a clear definition and some edge cases that would tell me where it stops applying.
