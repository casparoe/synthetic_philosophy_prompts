I'm a novelist and I've wandered into something more conceptual than I bargained for. My book in progress has two timelines: a village where a minority community is destroyed in the 1920s, and a granddaughter in the present trying to get what happened recognised. And I keep tripping over this. In the 1920s chapters nobody has a word for it. The family grieves three cousins, a burned church, a neighbour who vanished -- a heap of seperate losses, each one grievable on its own. The granddaughter has a word, and grieves the whole thing as one event. Same events, but it only becomes a single wrong once there's a category for it. And the category didn't come from the people it happend to. It came from lawyers and international committees, and then their own account of it gets measured against that -- was the intent there, was the group the right kind of group, was it big enough. So the new word both lets them be heard and hands them a new way to fail.

Is there a name for that pattern? Not the word, the pattern: a harm that can't be lived as one harm until a category exists, where the category arrives from outside and then doubles as the test the victims have to pass. I suspect this is near something in philosophy of language or political theory, but I want to know which established concept comes closest, where it breaks down against what I'm describing, and whether my second bit (the category as a filter that some victims fail) is part of the same phenomenon or a different one. If nothing quite fits, name it yourself and tell me where its edges are -- and please don't hand me a reading list, I need the carving, not the bibliography.
