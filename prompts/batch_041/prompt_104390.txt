I'm a graduate student in ethics, and I've been asked to circulate a short statement of my views on well-being ahead of a seminar on hedonism, so that the other participants have something concrete to attack rather than a vague target. Before I send it out, I'd like you to check whether the package actually hangs together. Here is roughly where I've landed. (a) I'm a hedonist about well-being: the only things that are intrinsically good or bad for a person are pleasure and pain, and a life goes well for someone just to the extent that it contains more pleasure than pain. (b) I accept the heterogeneity of pleasure — when I introspect, the taste of a ripe peach, the satisfaction of finally getting a proof to work, and the relief when a headache lifts don't seem to share any single feeling tone or common experiential core, so whatever makes them all count as pleasures, it isn't some one quality they each exhibit. (c) I nonetheless think the word 'pleasure' names a genuine natural kind rather than a grab-bag: the folk category isn't just a disjunction of unrelated states, and in using the term we're tracking something real. (d) I think the goodness of a given pleasure is explained by its being a pleasure — its pleasantness is what makes it good, rather than its being good for some further reason. And (e) I think it makes sense to weigh and sum pleasures of very different kinds against one another on a single scale, which is what hedonism seems to need if it is going to say anything about trade-offs. I don't want to be talked out of hedonism; what I want to know is which of these commitments actually pull against each other, which merely look like they do, and, for the genuine conflicts, the cheapest way to restore consistency and what that cheapest fix would cost me.

Please give me your answer as exactly five numbered items, ordered from the most to the least serious conflict, with no introduction and no conclusion. For each item, name the commitments involved, say plainly whether the conflict is genuine or merely apparent, and — where it is genuine — state the cheapest revision that would resolve it and what adopting that revision would cost me.
