I've been going around in circles on the absent-minded driver problem and I need someone to just sort me out. The version I know goes like this: you're driving down a road with two exits, and the payoffs are rigged so that if you're at the first exit you should keep driving and if you're at the second you should turn off, but when you get to the fork you've forgotten whether you already passed the first one, so you don't know which exit your at. My roommate says this is just the Sleeping Beauty problem wearing a hat (she gets woken up, doesn't know if it's Monday or Tuesday, gets put back under, etc.), and that whatever you decide Beauty should believe about which day it is, you have to give the matching answer about what the driver should do. I don't buy it, but every time I try to say why, the objection dissolves in my hands, and then I find some other spot where they obviously come apart, and then that one dissolves too. For the record I'm not a philosopher, I just read too much of this stuff on the internet instead of sleeping, and I don't want a reading list or a bibliography — I want the actual argument, laid out, so I can see where I'm going wrong.

Here's what I'm after. First, tell me straight what the two problems really share: both seem to be about someone who can't tell which of two situations they're in, where the situations are chained together in a special way — one is a rerun of the other, or one is the continuation of the other, depending on which puzzle you're looking at. Second, tell me where the genuine disanalogy is, if there is one. My own half-baked theory is that the driver problem is about one agent spread across two moments, while Beauty's problem is about one agent spread across two days — and I can hear that that's the same sentence twice, so either my theory is empty or I'm missing the actual joint. Third, and this is the part I really care about: strip the payoffs out of the driver problem. Forget the money entirely. Just ask, given that you've ended up at the fork and you can't tell which exit it is, what should you think about where you are? Does that collapse into Sleeping Beauty exactly, or does something survive the surgery? Because if the driver problem is only hard because of the payoff structure, then all the interesting machinery is decision theory and the self-locating belief part is scenery — but if it's still hard with the payoffs gone, then the two puzzles are closer than I want them to be and my roommate wins. (One more thing I keep tripping on: the driver's uncertainty about which exit he's at seems to depend on what he was going to do, which is the very thing he's trying to decide. Beauty's uncertainty depends on a coin flip she's told is fair. That loop feels like it should matter, but I can't tell if it's the real difference or just a distraction I've talked myself into.)
