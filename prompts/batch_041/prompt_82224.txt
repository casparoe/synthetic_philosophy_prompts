I'm a graduate student in philosophy, and for a seminar on the metaphysics of content I have drafted a short paper on the unity of the proposition and the regress usually named after Bradley. The draft is still rough, and I would like your comments on it before I decide what to keep. Here is the argument, condensed into steps. (1) A proposition such as *that Socrates is wise* has constituents — an object and a property — yet it is one thing, and it says something, whereas the bare pair {Socrates, wisdom} is two things and says nothing. (2) So something must unify the constituents. (3) If the unifier is itself a constituent — a relation of instantiation, the copula, whatever — the same question arises for the enlarged collection {instantiation, Socrates, wisdom}, and so on without end. That is the regress. (4) The two familiar exits: primitivism, on which propositions are not assembled from constituents and 'constituent' is a façon de parler; and the binder view, on which unification is not an additional item but a form, so the demand for a further unifier is illegitimate at the very first step. (5) My own line, which I have not seen put in exactly this way: the regress gets going only because we assume what I call *item-dependence* between a proposition and its constituents — the assumption that the proposition is something over and above them and therefore needs one more item to hold them together. Replace it with *position-dependence*: a constituent just is whatever fills a certain role in the structure, and is identified by that role, so 'Socrates' in the proposition is not a free-standing item waiting to be bound but already the thing-in-that-position. Nothing further is required, hence nothing further is demanded, and the regress stops at step 3. (6) The cost: position-dependence makes the whole explanatorily prior to its parts. But we seem to grasp new propositions by understanding their parts and combining them — someone who understands 'Socrates' and understands 'is wise' can grasp the proposition — and on this view that competence is mysterious. (7) A lingering worry I cannot dispel: have I stopped the regress, or only relocated it into the structure, which must itself be one thing, so that something must unify *it*?

What I want from you is a critique rather than a summary. Specifically: is the distinction in (5) a genuine distinction or a relabeling of the binder view in (4)? Is the cost in (6) unavoidable, or is there a way to keep position-dependence and recover compositionality? Is (7) fatal, or does the structure escape the regress by some principled asymmetry? More broadly, tell me whether any of this is interesting: I suspect that (1)–(4) are simply the standard dialectic and that whatever is mine lives in (5)–(7), but I may be fooling myself, and I would rather be told plainly that the whole thing is well-worn than be flattered. Flag any step where I have the received discussion wrong, and any place where I lean on an assumption I have not earned. I am not asking for reading lists or citations; I would rather you argue with the argument as it stands, granting each position whatever you take to be its strongest formulation.
