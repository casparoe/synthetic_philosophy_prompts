Okay so I'm a junior and I'm in this philosophy club where we do like a debate-style thing, and I've been reading about mental causation — the idea that your thoughts/beliefs/desires can't really cause your body to do stuff because physics has already sorted out what your body does. My friend Maya wrote up this argument for our session and I think it proves way too much, like it doesn't just kill mental causation, it kills basically every explanation that isn't stated in the language of fundamental physics. Here's her argument, I'm copying it from her doc:

(1) Any event that has a cause has a cause that is by itself enough to bring that event about.
(2) Any physical event that has a cause has a physical cause that is by itself enough to bring it about. (physics is "closed," i.e. nothing outside physics ever needs to chip in)
(3) If an event already has a cause that is by itself enough, then nothing else is needed in order for the event to happen.
(4) If nothing else is needed in order for the event to happen, then nothing else causes the event — a "cause" whose contribution is literally zero isn't a cause.
(5) So if a physical event has a physical cause that's by itself enough, no non-physical thing causes that event.
(6) And you never get two separate causes that are each by themselves enough for the exact same event — that would be weird "overdetermination," like two bullets each independently fatal hitting the heart at the same instant, and if that happened all the time it'd be a crazy coincidence.
(7) Therefore thoughts, beliefs, desires — anything non-physical — don't cause physical events.

So far, whatever, that's the standard scary conclusion. But here's where I think it eats itself. Take my grandmother, who died of a heart attack. Her death is a physical event, and there was a physical cause by itself enough for it (the heart attack, the tissue damage, etc.), so by (1)–(6) nothing non-physical caused her death. Fine. But now run the same thing on the heart attack itself: Maya says of course "having a heart attack" isn't literally the same property as "these particular cells doing this particular thing" — that's the whole multiple-realization thing — so the heart attack is a higher-level thing sitting on top of a lower-level physical cause that's by itself enough, which means (by (5)) the heart attack doesn't cause anything either. And then the cells don't cause anything, because the molecules are by themselves enough. And it just keeps going down until only the fundamental particles cause anything, and "she died of a heart attack" is false, and "she died of a heart attack" and "she died of cardiac arrest" can't both be true since only one thing did the causing, and honestly you can run this on anything — "the bridge fell because of metal fatigue" false, "the economy tanked because of the housing bubble" false, every why-question anyone has ever asked is false unless it's answered in particle physics. Maya's response is basically "yeah, maybe that's just true and everyone's been wrong," which I feel can't be right. But I can't find the bad step, because every one of them looks obviously true when I read it alone, and I really want to keep (2) because I'm a physicalist and I want to keep (6) because the alternative sounds like everything is overdetermined all the time.
