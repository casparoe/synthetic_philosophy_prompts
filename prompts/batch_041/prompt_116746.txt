Act as a philosopher of language and social ontology who knows David Lewis's Convention: A Philosophical Study (1969) inside out and also works on how norms emerge and change. I'm an undergraduate writing an essay for a social ontology course, and I want you to work out what Lewis himself would have made of something that postdates him entirely: the norms governing whether the weights of frontier AI models get released openly.

Please think this through carefully rather than reaching for a generic "social norms" vocabulary. Begin by brainstorming what the open-versus-closed release decision looks like when modelled as a coordination problem — what the equilibria are, which of them is salient to which population, and where precedent points — and then test the emerging practice of releasing weights against each of Lewis's conditions for a convention (coordination payoff, salience, common knowledge, arbitrariness), saying which conditions it satisfies and which it fails.

I'd especially like you to press on two points. Second, ask whether the relevant population — states, labs and hobbyists with partly opposed interests and imperfect mutual monitoring — can sustain the common knowledge his definition demands, and whether a convention could instead arise among the AI systems themselves. Keep separate what Lewis's texts commit him to from what you are extrapolating on his behalf, and flag anything about which you are genuinely unsure.
