Here is the question as it was set, followed by the script in question.

Question 3 (20 points). Your state space is generated by two questions — whether a certain hypothesis H holds, and whether a certain condition E obtains — so there are four atoms. Your prior is P(H) = 1/2, P(E | H) = 0.8, P(E | ¬H) = 0.4. You then have an experience (think of looking at a candle through frosted glass) that leaves you 75 percent confident of E and 25 percent confident of ¬E, and no more certain than that of either. (a) [6 points] State the update rule you use to get your new probability for H, and state precisely the condition that licenses its use. Give the condition in symbols, not just a slogan. (b) [6 points] Compute your new probability for H, showing the quantities you need along the way. (c) [8 points] A classmate says: 'What you are really doing is revising as little as possible: among all the distributions that give E the new value 0.75, yours is the flattest — the one that maximizes entropy subject to that constraint.' Is that a correct characterization of the rule? Answer in a way that lets me check your answer against the numbers in (b).

Script: "(a) The rule is Jeffrey conditionalization, or probability kinematics. For a partition {E_i} with new probabilities q_i, P_new(H) = Σ_i q_i P_old(H | E_i). Here that is P_new(H) = q_E P_old(H | E) + q_¬E P_old(H | ¬E). The rule is licensed by rigidity: the experience must leave the evidential significance of each cell alone, which we write as P_new(E_i | H) = P_old(E_i | H) for every i. In other words, the experience may change how much weight you put on each cell, but it must not change what each cell tells you about H; if the likelihoods themselves were shifted by the experience, the change would not be confined to the cell weights and the formula above would be the wrong rule to apply. (b) P(E) = P(E | H)P(H) + P(E | ¬H)P(¬H) = (0.8)(0.5) + (0.4)(0.5) = 0.6. P(H | E) = P(E | H)P(H)/P(E) = 0.4/0.6 = 2/3 ≈ 0.667. P(¬E) = 0.4, and P(H ∧ ¬E) = (0.2)(0.5) = 0.1, so P(H | ¬E) = 0.1/0.4 = 0.25. Therefore P_new(H) = (0.75)(2/3) + (0.25)(0.25) = 0.5 + 0.0625 = 0.5625. The answer lies between the two conditionalizations, 0.25 and 2/3, and above the prior of 0.5, which is what one should expect when the experience is favourable to E but does not settle it. Note that P(H | E) and P(H | ¬E) are the same as they were before the experience; only the weights on the two cells have moved. (c) Yes. Jeffrey conditionalization is the minimal-revision rule, and minimal revision is normally cashed out as maximum entropy: you keep the constraint the experience imposes — here P(E) = 0.75 — and otherwise leave the distribution as flat as you can. That is exactly why the number in (b) does not jump to either 2/3 or 0.25: the constraint fixes one degree of freedom and the maximization settles the rest. Conditionalizing on E outright would have given 2/3, which overstates your position, since you are not certain of E."
