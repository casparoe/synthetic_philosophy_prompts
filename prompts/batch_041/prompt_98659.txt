I'm in a political philosophy seminar that has just finished working through Nozick's entitlement theory — the three historical principles of justice in acquisition, justice in transfer, and the rectification of past injustice, together with the Wilt Chamberlain argument that liberty upsets patterns. I want to test the theory against a real episode our syllabus never touched: the British emancipation of the 1830s. As I understand it, Parliament abolished slavery throughout most of the empire in 1833, and to secure the planters' acquiescence the state paid them roughly twenty million pounds — something on the order of forty percent of the government's annual spending at the time, disbursed through tens of thousands of individual awards — as compensation for the loss of the human beings they had claimed to own, while the formerly enslaved received their freedom and no money at all; some of the government annuities used to finance the payout were not finally redeemed until 2015. I would like you to run that settlement through Nozick's machinery rather than through utilitarian or Rawlsian categories.

Work through the principles themselves and resist the temptation merely to gesture at them. On Nozick's commitment to self-ownership, could the slaveholders ever have held a legitimate entitlement to other persons such that the state owed them compensation for its loss — or does the theory's own starting point rule out the very holding that was being compensated? What bearing, if any, does the Wilt Chamberlain argument have here, given that it appears to presuppose a baseline of justly held property and genuinely voluntary transfers, which is precisely what I suspect fails in this case? And then the question I find hardest: does the entitlement theory actually deliver a determinate answer about what rectification would now require — restitution to the descendants of the enslaved, disgorgement of the funds, or nothing at all — or does the episode locate the point at which Nozick's account of rectification, which he himself left conspicuously unspecified, goes silent? Please keep the mapping disciplined: state what each principle does and does not say, mark where the theory has nothing to offer instead of forcing it to speak, and close by telling me whether this episode counts as a success for the entitlement theory or as a case it cannot handle. I am not after sources or further reading — I want the analysis itself, worked out in full.
