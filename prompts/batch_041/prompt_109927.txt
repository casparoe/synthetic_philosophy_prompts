Construct a thought experiment to order, for a unit on the problem of evil in which I want to show students whether the free will defense and the soul-making theodicy can simply be stacked. The case should be a single episode of wrongdoing, realistic and contemporary, told in one paragraph, with four stipulated features: it is a wrong done by one person to another, so that the free will defense applies in its standard form; it does in fact produce real moral and spiritual growth; that growth accrues to the wrongdoer rather than to the victim; and the victim is left with nothing — no growth, no compensation, no benefit to anyone else. It should not be horrendous, and it should not be a case of natural evil, so that nothing turns on sheer magnitude or on the scope of either defense.

Then work it through. For each theodicy, say whether it covers the episode, what it must claim about God's relation to the wrong — permitted only, or intended as a means to a good — and whether those two claims can be held together in their standard forms. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens if you remove it by letting the growth accrue to the victim instead: does the difficulty dissolve, or merely change shape? Close with how a defender of whichever theodicy comes out worse would attack the case, and what a skeptical theist would say that bypasses the dispute altogether.
