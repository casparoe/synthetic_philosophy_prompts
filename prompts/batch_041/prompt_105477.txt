I'm taking a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, and my term paper concerns whether the accuracy-first program can vindicate the standard norms of credence without first committing to a particular way of measuring accuracy. I would like a worked case rather than another paraphrase of the familiar examples, so please build one to my specifications.

Set it in a realistic setting: a physician, say, who must place credences over exactly three mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive hypotheses about what is causing a patient's symptoms. She has two candidate credence functions she could adopt. Both must be coherent, both must assign every hypothesis a probability no lower than 0.05 and no higher than 0.95, and both must reflect the same total evidence, so that the only difference between them is how confidence is distributed across the three hypotheses. No decisions, payoffs, or utilities should enter; the case is purely about the accuracy of the credences.

Here is the specification the case has to meet. Fix one of the three hypotheses as the actual one. At that hypothesis, the Brier score and the logarithmic score, both understood as scores over the three-cell partition, must rank the two functions in opposite orders, and the function the logarithmic score prefers must be the one that assigns the higher probability to the actual hypothesis. In other words, the measure that rewards greater confidence in the truth is the very measure that rates that function lower.

Then demonstrate that the case does what I asked. Exhibit the two functions as numbers, compute both scores at the actual hypothesis, and show the reversal rather than merely asserting it. Tell me which feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens if I remove it, in particular whether the reversal survives if there are only two hypotheses instead of three. Say whether the case bears on the norms of credence themselves, such as probabilism and conditionalization, or only on how two coherent functions are ranked against each other. Finally, give the strongest response an accuracy-first epistemologist could offer, and tell me whether it saves the program or only relocates the difficulty. If some of my specifications turn out to be jointly unsatisfiable, say so and show why.

Please write the whole thing as continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, and in American English.
