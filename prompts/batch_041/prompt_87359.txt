I chair the standards committee of our regional emergency-medical-services alliance -- about 40 ambulance services, a dozen hospitals, and a bunch of volunteer squads that only turn up for the big meetings. Our triage tags are color-coded (red for immediate, yellow for delayed, green for walking wounded, black for deceased) and the meanings are the same across the whole region, but nobody can tell me why we picked these colors. The colors are the only thing the system runs on -- a medic from another service has to read a tag at a glance -- so changing them means retraining everybody, reprinting everything, and probably a year of confusion.

There's a better system on the table: it uses shapes as well as colors, so it still works for the color-blind (maybe 8% of our medics), it has a field for the time the tag went on, and two neighboring regions already use it. My colleague Dana is against switching. She says our colors are a convention in David Lewis's sense: a regularity everyone follows because everyone expects everyone else to follow it, and because going along is the smart move as long as the others do. And conventions are arbitrary by definition, she says -- any one of several arrangements would have solved the same problem -- so the fact that the new one is better on the merits is beside the point. The only real question is whether 40 services can move at once, and they can't, so we shouldn't try.

I think that last step is wrong but I can't say why, and I don't want to just outvote her. Complicating things: three of the urban services say they'll switch with or without us, and the biggest one, which does most of our training, says it's waiting a year -- which would leave us running two codes at once, and I think that's worse then either one alone.

Draft the memo I'll circulate to the committee before the vote. Give me your recommendation and the reasoning, and deal honestly with Dana's argument: if she has part of Lewis right, say which part and why it still doesn't get her to her conclusion. Hard limit 600 words, and lead with the recommendation in the first two sentences. American English.
