ok so I'm in a seminar on early modern women philosophers and we just finished Du Châtelet's Discourse on Happiness, the one she wrote in the mid-1740s for herself and that only got printed long after she died. Our assignment for next week is to bring in a poem instead of a response paper, and I want it to actually argue something rather than just be a nice piece about being happy.

Her reciepe is basically a list: be healthy, get rid of your prejudices, have passions, make the passions serve your happiness, swap passions for tastes as you get older, guard your illusions carefully, be virtuous, never repent, keep the thought of death at a distance. Two things bother me about it in a good way. First, she tells you to strip off your prejudices and keep your illusions in almost the same breath, which seems like it should be a contradiction and I can't decide if it is. Second, she says we're the masters of our own happiness, but the whole thing is addressed to someone who already has what she calls a ready-made fortune, and her three real passions are gambling, love and study, which are three totally different kinds of dependence — love needs another person, gambling needs luck and money, and study (on my reading at least) needs nothing but you and time. So the list quietly sorts itself into things you can do and things that just happen to you.

So: write me a poem in the form of a regimen, a numbered set of instructions for happiness, either in her voice or in the voice of someone trying to follow her advice. The numbered form should do the argumentative work — let each item be a little less within the speaker's control than the last, so the list itself drifts from this is up to us to this is not, and the last item should land on whatever the speaker claims depends only on her, and show what it costs her to claim that. Get the gambling, the love and the study in there as three seperate modes of dependence. And no sneering at her — she knows the conditions are uneven and half says so, so it should read like someone using the reciepe honestly and finding out where it holds and where it doesnt.

Give it to me as exactly five numbered stanzas, each one a sentence or two, no intro and no outro, just the five.
