Payoffs: get off at the first exit and you score 0, get off at the second and you score 4, sail past both and you score 1. The best strategy from before he ever sets out is to randomize — keep driving with probability 2/3 — but that's a plan he couldn't defend to himself in the moment, sitting at an exit he can't identify. Give me exactly five stanzas of four lines each, no intro or outro, and make the last line of every stanza the same line, or almost — by the final stanza one word is allowed to change. The repetition has to do the work: he keeps arriving at "the same" decision, and the reader shouldn't be able to tell any better than he can whether a given stanza is the first exit or the second.
