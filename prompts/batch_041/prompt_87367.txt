ok so theres this puzzle i keep coming back to. you start with the string MI, and youve got four moves: if a string ends in I you can stick a U on the end of it; if it starts with M you can double everything after the M (so MIU becomes MIUIU); you can replace any III with a single U; and you can delete any UU. the question is whether you can ever get to MU. and the answer is no — i'm pretty sure the reason is that the number of I's is never a multiple of three, and MU has no I's at all, so you can sit there rewriting strings until you die and never touch the thing you want. the thing that gets me is that you cant see that from inside. you have to stop playing with the strings and start counting something the rules never mention. someone described that move to me as jumping out of the system and i havent stopped thinking about it.

i want a poem about this. specifically a pantoum — quatrains where lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next one, so the poem keeps recycling its own lines the way the rules keep recycling the string (normally the last stanza wraps around so the poem ends on its own first line; i dont want that). give me 4 quatrains done strictly, then a 5th quatrain that breaks the rule completely — nothing carried over, the loop doesnt close — because the only way out is to stop doing the thing. that last stanza is the counting.

stuff i care about:
