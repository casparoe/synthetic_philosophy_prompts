I am a high school student in a philosophy club, and I have spent the last few months reading about the White Horse Dialogue associated with Gongsun Long and the School of Names, and I keep running into the same frustration. The text is short, the central claim is easy to state, namely that a white horse is not a horse, and yet the scholarly discussion seems to have been stuck for a very long time. One camp treats the dialogue as a serious piece of early logical analysis, perhaps about the difference between a compound term and a simple one, or about how a modifier restricts a kind; another treats it as a sophistical paradox, a reductio of a bad theory of names, or a linguistic joke; a third reads it as an allegory about political naming or about the relation between a whole and its parts. Everyone seems to agree on the words and on the basic structure of the argument, and yet no one moves. I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a substantive disagreement about logic or metaphysics, a verbal dispute about how to translate the key terms, for instance whether the classical Chinese sentence is better rendered as a claim about a particular white horse, about white-horseness, or about the compound kind white horse, or is it two groups answering different questions under one heading, one historical and one formal? Could the impasse be due to the text being underdetermined, so that any reading requires importing assumptions the text cannot adjudicate? Or is the real disagreement about what counts as a charitable interpretation, with one side prizing logical reconstruction and the other prizing historical context? My hypothesis, which I offer only as a hypothesis, is that the debate is not about the text at all but about the standards we use to decide when an ancient argument is valid, and that the apparent stalemate is a sign that the question has been posed in the wrong way. Please diagnose the structure of the disagreement, using what each side actually says about the passage, and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress: a formal reconstruction, a translation that makes the argument valid without begging the question, a distinction between kinds of negation or predication, a change in the question, or an argument that no progress should be expected. I am not asking for a survey or a reading list. Please write your answer in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write a letter.
