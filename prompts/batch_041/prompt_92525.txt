I commission long-form features for a history magazine and we're launching a series on societies that 'fell' -- the Late Bronze Age world, the Classic Maya, Rapa Nui, that sort of thing. The trouble is every writer means something different by collapse: some mean a state loosing its grip, some mean a whole population going under, some mean a civilisation that just changed and carried on, and our house style is loose enough that it all slides through. I want a decision procedure I can hand to writers and fact-checkers -- a checklist or a flowchart in words, not an essay. Start from the point that a collapse claim is always relative to a stated system and a baseline, so the first questions have to fix what's supposed to be collapsing and against when. Then the ordered questions that pin down the claim, what evidence would settle each, where the answer comes out as not-collapse (keep decline, reorganisation, absorption and plain abandonment apart from each other), and where it's genuinely a values call about what counts as a society failing, which has to come to me rather than the writer. It has to be usable by someone who's read none of the archaeological debate, so keep the theory in the background -- but don't let it disappear: if there's a real distinction from the literature that has to survive the simplification, keep it and say why. Then run it on two cases, the Classic Maya and Rapa Nui, including one where it gives an answer our writers won't like. Finish with what the procedure can't do. British English please.
