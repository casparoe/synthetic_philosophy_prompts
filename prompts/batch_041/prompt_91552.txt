I've been in a reading group that's spent a year going round in circles on who started the Cold War, and I want to make the two loudest members write the document they should of written instead of shouting at each other. One is a fairly unreconstructed traditionalist: Stalin was expansionist, the West was reacting, that's the story. The other thinks who-started-it is itself the problem — you had two victorious powers with incompatible visions filling a power vacuum in Europe, and 'starting' is just blame-language dressed up as history. Write the joint statement the two of them could both put their names to. I want: the claims they agree on about what happened and about what the surviving evidence can and can't settle; their real disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; and the cruxes — I think one is whether the security dilemma in Europe was genuine or manufactured, another is whether blame attaches to intentions, to foreseeable consequences, or to the structure of the situation, and a third is what a counterfactual would have to look like before either of them would count it. For each crux, give something both would agree bears on it, and what each side predicts about it. And if part of the row is verbal — if they mean different things by 'starting' a war — say which part, and whether pinning down a definition would settle it or just move the fight somewhere else. British English please, agreed claims as a numbered list and the disagreements in a table.
