I've been lurking on LessWrong and various philosophy-of-science corners of the internet for like a decade, and I'm in a small reading group thats been grinding through the laws-of-nature debate — Lewis-style best-system Humeanism on one side, the dispositional-essentialist crowd (Bird, Ellis, that kind of view) on the other. My gripe is that the whole thing gets sold as whether laws govern or merely describe, which makes it sound like the two sides agree on which generalizations are laws and only differ over modal window-dressing. I don't think thats true and I want a concrete case to prove it. Also I've noticed the stereotype is that the Humean is the permissive one about what counts as a law and the necessitarian is the restrictive one, and I'd love to flip that.

So here's what I want you to build. A single possible world, describable in one paragraph, in which a best-system Humean and a dispositional essentialist disagree about whether one particular generalization is a law, and where the disagreement runs in the unexpected direction: the Humean says it IS a law, the essentialist says it's a cosmic coincidence. My hunch is you do it with two fundamental properties that are perfectly correlated across the whole world — every instance of one is an instance of the other and vice versa — with nothing in either property's essence that could underwrite the correlation. The Humean's best system definately buys the biconditional as an axiom, because it's one simple sentence that pays for itself in strength, while the essentialist looks at it and says neither essence mentions the other, so tough luck, it's a brute regularity. Constraints: no God, no sci-fi devices, no smuggling in necessary connections on either side, and the two views should agree about every other generalization in the world, so the case isolates the single variable. I also want the correlation to be strict and everywhere-and-everywhen, not a statistical tendency, so nobody can wave it off as noise.

Then walk me through it like I'm going to post it: state the world, state the generalization, give the Humean's reason for calling it a law and the essentialist's reason for calling it accidental, and be explicit about which feature of the setup is doing the work. Do the robustness check — if I delete that feature (say, the correlation only holds in part of the world, or one essence does happen to mention the other), does the case collapse into something both sides call a law, or both call accidental? And steelman whichever side comes out looking bad: whats the most plausible non-cheating move for them, and what does it cost them? What I care about is that the verdicts fall out of the theories and not out of how I defined "law" — if the case only works because I stipulated the answer, it's useless to me. If you think the direction I'm asking for is actually impossible, tell me that instead of fudging a world that doesn't do the job.
