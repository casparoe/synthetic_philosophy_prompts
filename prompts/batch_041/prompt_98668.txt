Ok so I've been using the Kalai-Smorodinsky bargaining solution as a lens for real negotiations and I keep flip-flopping on whether it's actually the right tool. Take Camp David in 1978 — Carter, Sadat, Begin, the whole thing. My rough picture of it: the disagreement point is no treaty, continued state of war, Israel sitting on Sinai; Egypt's ideal point is getting all of Sinai back under full Egyptian sovereignty; Israel's ideal is closer to keeping what it took in '67 plus recognition and an end to the war; and the feasible set is all the packages in between — how much Sinai, what demilitarization looks like, what "recognition" concretely means.

I want a disciplined run at this. First map it: who the players are, what the disagreement point really is, what each ideal point is, what's actually in the feasible set, and whether the US is a third player or just a mediator constructing the set for the other two. Then say what KS actually picks — the point on the Pareto frontier where each side's gain over the status quo is proportional to what it could've gotten at its ideal — and how that differs from what Nash would pick (max product of gains, the whole IIA thing). Does the treaty that came out look more like one than the other, and is that even the right question?

Then the part I actually care about, where the model breaks. KS leans on ideal points. But in a real negotiation you don't just have an ideal point, you have a reason to inflate it, and the other side knows that. So the axiom that makes KS attractive — individual monotonicity, your payoff doesn't drop when the feasible set grows toward you — looks like exactly the property that gets exploited when ideal points are cheap talk. Is that a genuine problem for applying it here, or does the mediator's role (Carter more or less building the feasible set) patch it up? And what does the case show back about the theory: is Camp David evidence that proportionality is what fairness means in bargaining, or evidence that the axioms are solving a problem no real negotiation is?

Also flag where KS is just silent — concession order, the risk of walking out, why Sadat going to Jerusalem changed the game at all. Don't force the theory to speak there, just say so.
