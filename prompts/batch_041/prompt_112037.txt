ok so i need a piece of sustained deadpan satire for a writing exercise, and i want it in the form of an internal document: a sentencing manual. Like the guidelines manuals courts use — base offense levels, enhancements, mitigating role, downward departures, that whole apparatus — except the body issuing it doesn't exist. Its the crowd. It's for administering informal social sanction: pile-ons, callouts, deplatforming, no-platforming, someone getting quietly removed from every group chat they're in.

Format it as numbered sections with headers, and pick your own title (something like Guidelines Manual for Informal Sanction, or better). About 700 words. Write it in complete bureaucratic earnestness, from the point of view of somebody who believes in what they're doing and has absorbed a bit too much punishment theory. The funny has to come from getting the structure right, not from winking at the reader.

Some things i want in there:

- Offense levels and enhancements that track real features of online sanction. A screenshot as the base offense. An enhancement for abusing a position of trust (people actually listened to them). An aggravating role for whoever started it versus a mitigating role for the ten thousand people who just quote-tweeted — and every one of those ten thousand would say their own contribution was negligible, so the manual has to say it too, with a straight face.
- Acceptance of responsibility, defined absurdly narrowly: you get the reduction only if the apology lands before the screenshots, explains nothing, and never mentions what you meant or what was going on in your head.
- A proportionality section that keeps using the word 'levels' and never once says what a level is worth, or how you'd know you'd gone too far.
- A short section on why the guidelines are only advisory: there's no court, so there's no appeal, and the bit explaining the appeals process should dissolve into nothing, ideally in a footnote.
- Ex post facto, double jeopardy, statutes of limitation: each gets a section that says the concept doesn't apply here and doesn't explain why.
- One clause asserting the sanction is not punishment but merely the exercise of free association, sitting directly next to a clause about making an example of the target. Same section, unreconciled.
- One place where the manual shows its humane side and undercuts itself: a provision for restoring someone to good standing with no mechanism attached, or a note conceding that the people doing this have no other recourse because the institutions that should have handled it wouldn't. That bit should be sincere and should land hard. I dont want a piece that just sneers at the people doing the sanctioning — the whole thing should be legible to someone who thinks it's sometimes necessary and to someone who thinks it's a witch hunt, and both should wince at least once.

Stuff you can assume the reader knows, and please use: bills of attainder and why they're banned; the old practice of declaring someone an outlaw, outside the law's protection; the idea that punishment expresses condemnation and not just deterrence; the rule-of-law virtues (prospective, published, consistent, applied as written); the state action doctrine, which is exactly why this manual exists and exactly why there's nothing protective in it; and the sociology of the public denunciation that recasts a member as an outsider — it needs a denouncer who can speak for the group, a public venue, and a target who ends up a stranger to the group's own values. No citations, no footnotes to books, no reading list — the one mock footnote should be in the document's own voice.

Start with the document itself. First line is the title, no introduction, no commentary before or after.
