I'm a novelist, about 60 pages into a book that's partly about a small farming town fighting over whether the county keeps spraying the fields upwind of the elementary school. I don't want to write a pamphlet, I want the arguments in it to be ones that real, smart people would actually make, so I've been reading up on pesticides and I keep running into one line. In the scene I'm working on, the guy who runs the co-op — he's not a villain, he's 58 and tired and he's farmed that land his whole life — says 'the dose makes the poison' and the room basically shuts up, because it sounds like it settles everything. I've seen the same line in a couple of op-eds by ag people, and I once watched a woman at a town meeting get told it to her face about her kid.

Here's what I need. Give me a menu of the distinct, precise claims that sentence could be making, laid out as a list I can keep next to my laptop. For each one tell me whether it's a platitude, whether it's substantive and defensible, or whether it's just false — and then tell me which reading my co-op guy most likely means, and which reading would actually get him to 'so the spraying is fine.'

I want the readings to get specific enough that they're not interchangeable. For instance, I have a suspicion the phrase is smuggling a chemical fact into a value claim, and a second suspicion that it's so obviously true that the real fight is about something else entirely and the slogan is a way of not naming it. I also want to know which reading it takes to get a threshold — a dose below which nothing happens — and whether that's the same for every kind of harm, or whether some harms (the ones you only see in kids years later, the ones where we don't really have a line) behave differently.

Some things I keep bumping into that I'd like you to sort out: the dose that's applied versus the dose that actually gets into a body versus the dose that reaches whatever organ cares; a one-time dose versus a whole summer of them; the dose for a grown man versus for a fetus, a beekeeper, or a stream; the dose of the active ingredient versus whatever else is in the tank; and the fact that spraying lightly and often is supposedly how you breed resistant bugs, which seems to make the whole sentence weird at the level of a practice rather than a chemical. There's also some business where tiny doses seem to pep organisms up instead of hurting them (hormesis? I might be mangling that).

Finally, since it's a novel: tell me what my character would have to believe for that line to be an honest thing for him to say rather than a conversation-ender, and what the woman with the sick kid could say back that wouldn't just be her refusing to do chemistry. I half beleive the guy and I half think he's dodging, and I can't write him until I know which. Please seperate the readings cleanly, and don't just tell me the slogan is ambiguous — I know it's ambiguous, I need to know which way.
