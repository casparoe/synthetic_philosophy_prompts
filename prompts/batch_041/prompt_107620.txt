ok so theres this thought experiment from Avicenna, the flying man: imagine a full grown man created all at once, hovering in midair, eyes covered, limbs splayed out so hes not even touching himself, no sound, no smell, nothing coming in from the senses at all. Avicenna says this guy would still be able to affirm that he exists -- but he wouldnt be able to affirm anything about his body, or even that he has one. so the self-awareness doesnt depend on the body. fine. but the part i want the poem to sit in is the follow up: Avicenna also thinks a soul is individuated by the body it animates, the body is what makes this soul mine and not some other one. which means the flying man can say I am but he cant say which I. he knows that he is, not who.

Write me a ghazal from the flying man's point of view. radif should be 'I am' (or 'am I' if you want the doubt baked in, your call, but pick one and hold it). the couplets should each feel like a fresh start -- no arguement building couplet to couplet, each one just a new attempt to get purchase on himself, because thats honestly what the situation is. keep physical nouns out of it as much as you can, the whole point is he has nothing to touch. then the last couplet should do the takhalus move, the thing where the poet slips their own name in at the end -- except here the name arriving should be the moment he gets individuated, the moment a body sneaks back in, and it should land like a loss or a trap, not a homecoming. someone who knows the argument should feel the twist hit.
