Write a deadpan self-help column, in the voice of an invented productivity writer, that turns Daniel Dennett's four grades of creature into a personal-development ladder. The grades, in order: Darwinian creatures, whose behaviour is fixed by natural selection; Skinnerian creatures, which learn by actually trying things and being rewarded or punished for them; Popperian creatures, which can try an action out in imagination, so that what fails is a plan rather than the creature; and Gregorian creatures, which think with external mind-tools — notation, instruments, archives, and other people. The columnist sells a regimen for climbing the ladder by outsourcing each grade in turn to an app, a wearable, or a subscription service, and presents the resulting condition as the summit of development. The comedy has to come from the taxonomy being right rather than from invented absurdities: a specialist should see that the capacity being offloaded at the decisive step is precisely the one whose point is that the trial can be imaginary, and that the program's end state is a creature equipped like the fourth grade while quietly relapsing toward the second. The target is the habit of treating the delegation of thought to tools as an unqualified upgrade, not any company or named person. Stay in character throughout: never wink, never hint that the author is wrong, and let the reader reach the conclusion unaided. About 600 words, organised under the columnist's own three subheadings, in this order: "The Ladder You Are Already On", "The Regimen", "Six Months In". Then add a final section titled "Notes on the Satire", roughly 150 words, explaining the choices you made and where the joke lives.
