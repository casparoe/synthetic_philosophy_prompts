I've been reading up on Berkeley's money writings for a thing I'm doing and I want a joke out of it. Not the idealism — the economics. In The Querist he goes on and on (all in questions, which is half the joke already) about how money isn't wealth: it's a ticket, a counter, a device for moving wealth about, and the real wealth is the power to command other people's labour and industry. Gold and silver are just counters like any other, so paper would do the job perfectly well, and he wanted an Irish national bank issuing notes against land. A coin nobody will accept in exchange is, on his view, worth nothing at all — the value lives in the circulation and the credit, not in the metal.

Write me a joke that actually turns on that. The punchline should need the ticket-not-wealth point to land (bonus if it also needs the land-bank bit). It can be a short scene, a bit of pub dialogue, whatever — doesn't have to be a one-liner. It should definately be funny to someone who's read the Querist, and at least not gibberish to someone who hasn't. And please don't do the obvious one about him thinking everything's an idea so money must be imaginary — that's the lazy version and it gets his actual monetary view wrong.
