I've been reading around in philosophy of cognitive science and I've ended up fairly convinced by Dennett's picture of four grades of creature, and I want to stress-test it before I write anything that leans on it. Roughly the idea is: a Darwinian creature is wired by natural selection and has fixed reflexes, it doesn't learn anything within its own lifetime; a Skinnerian creature can be shaped by reinforcement, so it learns from its own reward history; a Popperian creature builds an inner model and runs its hypotheses off-line, so it can be wrong in the head instead of dead in the world; and a Gregorian creature has picked up tools for thinking -- above all language and other external devices -- that let it reprogram itself from the outside in. The rung I actually care about is the last one: I think the Gregorian move is the real discontinuity, the thing that separates human cognition from even very clever animals, and that the lower rungs form a genuine ladder rather than just a list of capacities that happen to co-occur. My friend who works in an animal-cognition lab thinks the whole scheme is armchair hand-waving, so I want to know where it's actually exposed.

So: what would count against this? I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be -- things like whether the levels are cleanly separable at all (does reinforcement learning need some inner model to even get off the ground? is the Popperian/Gregorian line anything more than more-of-the-same, just with props?), what comparative work on crows or cephalopods or social insects would have to show to break the ladder, and whether the tool-and-language story is doing independent explanatory work or just re-labelling culture. Also tell me honestly whether the scheme has been quietly qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that's fatal for a framework of this kind or just the price of a good taxonomy. And I'd like to know which of the objections a committed Dennettian would themselves grant as counting against them, versus which they'd wave off -- I don't want the critic's list, I want the map of what the view actually risks.
