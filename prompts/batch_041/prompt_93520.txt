I'm the protocol lead at a lab building a negotiation layer for autonomous agents. Counterparties won't accept a trusted referee, so we've adopted the program-equilibrium approach: before a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma interaction, each side submits its decision policy as source code, and each policy may inspect the other's source before choosing. Our agents are expected-utility maximizers, and our review board will not approve a policy that a counterparty can systematically exploit. Please write a decision procedure my engineers can run on a submitted pair of policies. It should say, in order, what to check about a policy's self-referential structure; when a policy that cooperates only if it can prove its counterpart cooperates against it — the Löb-style self-verification construction — can be relied on to produce mutual cooperation against a copy of itself; how such a policy behaves against an unconditional cooperator and against one that defects unless it can prove its partner will cooperate; what to do when source inspection is unavailable or the policies are too large to analyze within our budget; and which of these steps is a proof obligation versus a judgment call that has to be escalated. Run it on three pairs: two copies of the self-verifying cooperator, that cooperator against an unconditional cooperator, and that cooperator against the policy that defects unless it can prove cooperation. Be explicit about which steps depend on contested assumptions about idealized versus resource-bounded reasoning, and separate what is settled in the decision-theory literature from what is genuinely disputed among people who work on it.
