I've come around to the picture of large language models, popular on rationalist blogs, that a model is best described as a simulator rather than an agent: a device for generating continuations that, when prompted, produces a character, so that the apparent goals, voice, and self-interest of the assistant belong to the simulated persona and not to the model itself. I want a map of what would count against this. Give me the observations, interpretability findings, and arguments that should move me, ranked by how decisive they are, and say explicitly which ones a defender of the simulator picture would concede as a genuine cost versus dismiss as a mere redescription. What does the picture predict that its rivals do not, especially where the persona is ambiguous, where behavior shifts with a system prompt, and where a base model with no assistant persona at all is prompted? Finally, is the view insulated from refutation, given that any behavior can be redescribed as the model simulating a character who does that, and if so, what would it take to falsify it, and is that a flaw in the theory or just the price of a broad one? And underneath all of it: is there a fact of the matter about whether a system has goals, or is goal-attribution always relative to a description and a purpose?
