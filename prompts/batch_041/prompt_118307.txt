I'm trying to write down where I stand on whether neural networks really have representations, and I want a consistency check before I commit to it. Here is what I hold. I'm a realist: a trained network has internal states with determinate representational content, true or false in roughly the way it is true or false that a thermometer represents temperature, and not merely useful fictions. The only evidence that earns a representational claim, though, is causal: if intervening on a direction or a unit changes downstream behavior in the right way, that is what makes it a representation, and if nothing distinguishes it causally then there is nothing there. I also think the decomposition of an activation space into its features is not determined by the network, since any invertible linear change of basis carries exactly the same information, so there is no fact of the matter about which directions are the real ones. At the same time I am persuaded by the practice of factoring activations into far more sparse directions than the space has dimensions and treating what comes out as the network's actual features; that looks to me like discovering structure rather than imposing it. And I think what one particular trained network represents tells you nothing about what another network trained the same way represents, so there are no regularities at the level of representations. Which of these genuinely conflict, which only look as though they do, and what is the cheapest repair in each case? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and be explicit about which parts of this are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who know the area, rather than presenting a contested view as the consensus. Please write it as continuous prose, in American English, with no headings, bullet points, or tables.
