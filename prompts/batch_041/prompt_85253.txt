I've spent years refereeing an argument between two friends and I want them to finally write down the thing they should have written instead of emailing each other. One is a dialetheist: the Liar sentence is both true and false, that's not a bug in language, and it's the reason logic has to stop validating explosion. The other is a paraconsistentist who thinks she's wrong about that: he's happy to give up explosion and reason non-explosively — he does it all the time with inconsistent databases, inconsistent legal codes, inconsistent theories — but he insists nothing is ever both true and false, and that 'inconsistent' is a property of bodies of information rather than of the world. They agree classical logic is a dead end here and then talk past eachother for hours.

Write the joint statement both of them could actually sign. Numbered agreed claims first, including what the paradoxes force on anyone who won't go classical and what each of them refuses to say; then the real disagreements, each phrased the way the person it belongs to would want it phrased; then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling things would move at least one of them, e.g. is there any motivation for dropping explosion that doesn't go through a true contradiction, and can each of them even state their rejection of the other's view in their own language (can he say the Liar isn't true? can she say 'not both'?). For each crux give the argument or case both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance, and what each would count as a reason to abandon their own position — I want at least one crux that's formal enough to actually be settled, e.g. what each theory does when you feed it the strengthened Liar or Curry's paradox. Finish by saying whether any of this is just a verbal dispute, and which part.
