I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of science, and I'm putting together a position statement for a formal epistemology reading group. Before I present it, I'd like an audit: the commitments below were arrived at separately, and I want to know whether they can actually be held together.

(1) Credences are probabilities, and conditionalization is the whole of the diachronic story — to learn something with certainty is just to move to your prior conditional on it. This is the part of the package I'm most attached to.

(2) Logical omniscience is a constitutive ideal rather than a tractable approximation. A rational agent assigns credence 1 to every logical truth and 0 to every contradiction, and is closed under logical consequence; this follows from what credence is, so an agent who is uncertain whether a valid inference is valid has a defective credence function, not merely a resource-limited one.

(3) The problem of old evidence is a real problem and not an artifact of a bad formalism. When a new theory entails a result that was already established — the standard case being a theory of gravity that entails a long-known anomaly in Mercury's orbit — the entailment is part of what confirms the theory, and any Bayesianism that cannot say so is incomplete.

(4) The right repair is the counterfactual one: confirmation is measured by how likely the evidence is on the hypothesis at the credence function the agent would have had before she knew the evidence, rather than at the credence function she actually has now that she knows it with certainty.

(5) Reasoning can be a source of justification. Someone who has just worked through a proof of a theorem she had previously half-believed is rationally more confident of it than she was, and her epistemology should license that transition rather than treat it as a psychological curiosity.

(6) Where evidence arrives uncertain rather than certain, the update rule is Jeffrey conditionalization, rigidifying on the evidential partition.

What I want is an audit, not a summary of the area. Tell me which of these actually pull against each other and which pairs only look incompatible; for each real tension, say how severe it is and precisely what would have to be given up to repair it, and rank the genuine tensions by how much the repair costs. Then tell me which single revision would clear up the most at the lowest cost, and what it would cost. I'd rather be told that a pair is a live disagreement than be handed a confident verdict on a contested point, so throughout please mark off what is settled among people who work on this from what is genuinely disputed, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
