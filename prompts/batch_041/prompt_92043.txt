I am a philosopher of mind at work on a paper about the prospects for a thoroughly historical theory of mental content, and I should like to rehearse the standard objection against a capable opponent rather than read another exposition of it. Please play that opponent: a committed teleosemanticist who holds that a state represents whatever it was selected to represent, so that content is fixed by the selective history of the mechanism that produces the state rather than by that state's current causal role or its current correlations. Defend the position at full strength. Concede only what a resourceful defender would concede, and where my example seems to bite, look for the distinction that rescues the theory rather than retreating from it. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to a single paragraph so that the exchange reads as a conversation rather than a treatise.

Here is the case, and my opening move.

Consider a freshwater snail, which I shall call the reed snail, living in still, poorly oxygenated water. It has a single class of interneuron — the A-cell — whose firing triggers a climbing reflex: the snail crawls up a reed towards the surface, where the water holds more oxygen. Across a long history, A-cell firing has been selected for precisely because it reliably occurred as oxygen was falling, and the climbing it produced restored the snail's supply. Call that the ancestral arrangement.

Now consider a population that has colonised a sulphur spring. There the water is saturated with hydrogen sulphide and permanently low in oxygen, so oxygen levels no longer vary in any way the snail can exploit. The A-cell in these snails, however, now fires in response to hydrogen sulphide, and this too has been selected for: sulphide is densest in the mat of sulphur bacteria on which these snails feed, and climbing carries them towards it. The arrangement is some thirty generations old.

My objection runs as follows. On your view, what a state represents is fixed by what the mechanism producing it was selected for. But here either the history has already been rewritten and the A-cell in the sulphur-spring snails represents sulphide — in which case thirty generations of selection suffice to fix a content, and it becomes hard to see what the appeal to a long history is doing that a specification of current selective pressure would not do just as well — or the history has not been rewritten, and the A-cell still represents falling oxygen, in which case these snails are, on your own theory, in a state of permanent and systematic misrepresentation about a quantity that is not merely unvarying but entirely irrelevant to everything they do. Neither horn looks comfortable. Which do you take, and what work is the appeal to history actually doing in your theory?
