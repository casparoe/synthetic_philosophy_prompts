So I'm a backend engineer, zero formal philosophy training, and I've been going down this rabbit hole ever since a coworker and I got into it over lunch about whether a spinning thing is "really" spinning or if that's just relative to whatever you happen to measure it against. He kept bringing up this old bucket setup, which I guess goes back to Newton: you fill a bucket with water, hang it from a twisted rope, let it go. At first the bucket spins but the water just sits there flat. Then the water catches up and turns along with the bucket, and now the surface goes concave, climbing up the sides. His whole point was that at that last stage the water isn't moving relative to the bucket at all, yet the surface is still curved -- so the curve has to come from somewhere else. Either there's some absolute "space" the water is genuinely turning against, or (if you're the other camp) it's turning against all the distant matter in the universe, the stars and galaxies and whatever else is out there. I get that there are two camps but I honestly cant feel the difference in my gut, and I want a poem that makes me feel it.

Here's the form I was thinking, tell me if its dumb: a sestina. My reason is that the end-words keep coming back in a different order in every stanza, and that feels exactly like the bucket -- same water, same motion, but it reads differently depending on which frame you're standing in, and the words keep circling each other the way the water and the bucket do. Pick six end-words out of the bucket setup (water, bucket, still, turn, edge, rest, whatever works -- your call), and use the way they rotate to carry both readings: the relationist one, where the curve only means something relative to the fixed stars, and the absolutist one, where the water is turning against space itself and the curve is the objective tell no matter who's watching. The envoi should land on the surface itself, the shape of the water, as the one thing both sides are stuck having to explain. I want someone who's actually read the Newton and Leibniz and Mach stuff to nod along and not wince, and someone who's never heard any of this to still get chills at the end. Dont name-drop a pile of philosophers at me, just let the physics and the form do the work. It doesnt have to rhyme if that's fighting the sestina -- I care way more about it being right than pretty.
