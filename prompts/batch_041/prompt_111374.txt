My lab has converged on a picture of low-impact AI that I want to pressure-test before I build anything on it. The picture is the outdoors rule for hikers: you visit a site, you leave it as you found it, you take nothing out and you carry out whatever you carried in. A colleague puts it to work as follows. An agent's side effects are just the traces it leaves on the world. An agent that leaves no traces has avoided side effects. And since leaving no traces is a matter of preserving or restoring the state you started from, side-effect avoidance can be defined without any specification of what is good: the initial state supplies the target, and the agent never needs to know which features of the world are valuable, only which ones have changed. This is the same story told about the impact measures now in circulation, which are advertised as penalizing deviation from the status quo without requiring a value function.

I would like an audit of the analogy rather than a literature review, covering the following points.

1. The mapping. Give a table of correspondences: what plays the role of the hiker, the site, the trace, the state as found, the hiker's own body and gear, the other visitors, the ranger who decides what counts as disturbance, and the rulebook itself.

2. Where it breaks. Separate the disanalogies that bear on the conclusion above from those that are merely picturesque. I am especially interested in: whether any action at all can leave a site unchanged, and whether inaction is itself a trace; whether the site would have changed anyway, so that the baseline must be a counterfactual rather than a snapshot; whether the hiker can identify a trace without already knowing what matters at the site; whether an agent can be indifferent between two states that differ only in respects it does not represent; and what happens when the site contains other agents with plans of their own.

3. What the picture imports. The real rulebook is codified, partial, and contested, and it is a specification of what matters in a wilderness — soil, wildlife, water, quiet — that is silent about things no one cares about. Does the existence of such a rulebook support the colleague's claim, by showing that a partial, non-comprehensive specification is enough, or does it undermine the claim, by showing that a specification was doing the work all along and that no one has produced the corresponding rulebook for the agent's world? Say which reading is right, and why.

4. Whether the conclusion survives. If the analogy can carry the claim that side-effect avoidance needs no value specification, say so and say what in the mapping carries it. If it carries only a restricted version of the claim, state the restriction precisely. If it cannot, say what the claim actually rests on instead.

5. A better picture. If this analogy cannot carry the weight, propose one that can, or argue that the point should be made without an analogy at all.

Assume I am unimpressed by the observation that impact measures are imperfect proxies; the question is what the hiker picture is doing in the argument, and whether the conclusion is carried by the picture or by something else.
