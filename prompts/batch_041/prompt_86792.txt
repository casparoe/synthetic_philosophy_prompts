I've been thinking about the argument that insects might be sentient and there are so many of them that even a tiny chance of sentience makes insect welfare a huge moral priority. Please argue against that scale argument. Make the case that multiplying a very small and very uncertain probability by astronomical numbers doesn't give you a strong practical obligation, and that we should instead use a threshold view: only once the evidence for sentience crosses some bar do the numbers really start to matter. Use the idea of many maybes — a huge pile of uncertain cases doesn't automatically outweigh a smaller pile of near-certain cases. Also say roughly where you'd put the threshold, like whether just having the basic pain-reflex wiring is enough or whether you'd need something richer like them making trade-offs to avoid pain. Defend your position against the obvious objection that this is just a excuse for ignoring insects. Keep it casual.
