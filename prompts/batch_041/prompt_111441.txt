The argument, the way I remember it (I read a cheap translation ages ago so don't hold me to the wording): he says a white horse is not a horse, and when the other guy pushes back he says horse is the word that picks out the shape and white is the word that picks out the colour, and a word that picks out a colour isn't a word that picks out a shape. Then the bit I actually find interesting — if you send someone to the market for a horse, a black one or a yellow one will do, but if you send them for a white horse, a black one won't do at all, so the two words clearly aren't doing the same job. He's meant to be one of the School of Names lot, and there's a whole pile of one-liners floating around in that tradition that sound like straight nonsense, like a bird's shadow never moving, or a white dog being black, and Davey reckons they're all secretly profound while I reckon half of them are just word games. Somewhere in there he also told me about some logician (Frege?) who had the mirror-image headache, that as soon as you put the in front of a concept it stops being a concept, which I don't really get but it feels like the same knot.

(4) And a white horse is a horse, come on, and when I say that I'm talking about an actual animal, that's the thing the sentence is about — anyone who denies it is fiddling with the word is. (5) Meaning is the job a word does: what it lets you ask for, what it lets you rule out, what it commits you to. It's a skill, not a list of objects.
