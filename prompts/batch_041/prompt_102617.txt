My opening: You say the omniscient interpreter argument shows that any interpreter must find most of a speaker's beliefs true. But an omniscient interpreter already knows all truths. So the beliefs it finds true are true. Does the argument therefore prove that most of a speaker's beliefs are true, or only that an interpreter who already knows the truth must interpret the speaker as agreeing with it? If the latter, the argument seems to presuppose the correspondence between belief and reality that radical interpretation was supposed to establish without appeal to a shared world. How do you avoid the circle?
