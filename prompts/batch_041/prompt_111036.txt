My grandma died in March and my mom and I have been slowly going through her stuff, and we found a shoebox of letters she wrote to her sister back in Poland in the 60s and 70s, plus a few pages that look like a diary. My mom's Polish is okay but mine is basically zero, so I've been feeding the letters into Google Translate a few pages at a time. And honestly the output reads really smoothly — whole paragraphs that sound like a person wrote them in English — so at first I thought great, problem solved, I can finally hear her voice. Then my aunt read one of the translated pages and said it was flat, that grandma was funny in Polish and there's no jokes left in the English, and that a couple of sentences about who married who are probaly just wrong in ways she couldn't quite point to. Now I don't know how far to trust any of it, which matters because I'm kind of the one who's going to be the family's memory of these letters.

Anyway I mentioned this to my friend Dani, who does NLP at a startup, and she said I was overthinking it and that there's a classic Hofstadter piece about exactly this, the one in The Atlantic called The Shallowness of Google Translate. Her summary of it was: Hofstadter's whole complaint is that the system doesn't understand anything, it's just matching patterns of words against each other from old UN and EU documents, and he ran a bunch of little tests where it botched idioms and picked the most literal reading every time. She said the argument is basically Searle's Chinese Room applied to translation — symbols getting shuffled around with no meaning behind them — and that his actual prediction was that it would never get much better, which the neural nets then definately falsified, so now the essay is sort of a period piece and people only still cite it out of sentiment. She also said his one good point was that "deep" in "deep learning" is pure marketing, since the tech isn't deep in any meaningful sense. And she said the translations I'm getting are probably fine, because whether the machine "understands" is a philosopher's question that doesn't change anything about the output on my screen.
