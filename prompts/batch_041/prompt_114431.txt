ok so I keep getting into arguments about the Pasadena game and I want to actually get clear on it before I write anything. Here's the setup as I understand it, correct me if I've got it wrong: you flip a fair coin until the first heads; if heads first shows up on toss n, the payoff is 2^n / n but with alternating sign. So each term in the expected value series has magnitude 1/n, and the series is only conditionally convergent — rearrange the terms and you can make it come out to any real number you like, or make it diverge. The St. Petersburg game is the cousin where the expectation blows up to infinity, and there's another cousin where you divide by n^2 instead so the terms fall off like 1/n^2 and the expectation is finite and well behaved. Then there's the Altadena game: Pasadena plus a dollar on every outcome, so it dominates Pasadena state by state, but its expectation diverges to +infinity.

Also tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the obvious candidate theories.
