(1) Me: that's circular. If 'a' just means "whatever gets read as 'a'", you've told me nothing about what the shapes have in common. (2) Her: they don't share a structure, they share a role. What makes them all 'a' is that trained readers treat them as the same letter, and the role is relative — relative to the other 25 letters, to the font they sit in, to the reader's habits. Change the population of readers and the "essence" changes, which is exactly what you'd expect if there's no essence.

(3) Me: roles can't be totally free either. You can't draw a shape that's obviously a 'q' and call it your font's 'a' just because it plays the 'a' slot. There are constraints — only certain deformations survive — and those constraints are the thing I'm trying to point at.

(4) Her: now you're back to prototypes. The constraints are just similarity to the shapes people have already seen a million times, i.e. a statistical summary, not an essence. And similarity is graded, which is my whole point. Take a circle with a little tail, dead between 'a' and 'o' — whether it's 'a' or 'o' is settled by the word it's sitting in, not by the shape.
