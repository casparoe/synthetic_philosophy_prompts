Assume the role of a philosopher of mind who can write institutional prose with a perfectly straight face. I am a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, and I would like to commission a short parody from you. Begin by setting out for yourself, briefly, which features of the puzzle below are load-bearing and how each will surface in the fine print; then draft the piece.

Take the following setup as given. A careenium is a large arena filled with vast numbers of tiny rolling magnets — call them simmballs. At the simmball level nothing happens but ordinary collisions and magnetic forces; yet stable, higher-order patterns among the simmballs can be read as symbols, and the arena as a whole can just as well be described as a device that stores, compares, and manipulates symbols. The question I want the piece to circle is whether the symbols push the simmballs around: whether the symbol-level description names a genuine cause of what the arena does, or merely redescribes what the simmballs were going to do in any case.

Please write a mock product-safety recall notice, roughly 600 words, issued by a fictional manufacturer of home careeniums after a batch of units was returned following a series of incidents. The notice must commit itself on where the fault lies — at the simmball level or at the symbol level — because the warranty's coverage, and the company's liability, turn on that question, and it must be written in the deadpan register of corporate compliance: headings, a defect description, the scope of the recall, remedies, and a short owner FAQ. Let the humour come from getting the metaphysics right rather than from winking: the causal closure of the physical, the exclusion problem, downward causation, multiple realizability, and the reply that the two levels are simply two descriptions of a single happening should all be doing work in the fine print. The company must never announce that it is taking a philosophical position; a reader who knows the literature should nonetheless see precisely which positions its evasions commit it to, and a reader who does not should still be able to follow the story. Close with the notice conceding, in one flat sentence, the thing it has spent the whole document trying not to say.

Do not cite or allude to any real philosopher or text; keep the document self-contained. Give me the notice itself, preceded at most by a few lines of your own framing notes.
