My neighbors Dave and Renata got into an argument at the lake last weekend and I've been chewing on it all week, because I honestly couldnt tell who had the better of it. I was just standing there holding a beer, so no dog in this fight. I think this is the tragedy of the commons thing but I might have the name wrong.

Backstory: the lake between our places is getting fished out. Dave's position is that thats just what happens with anything shared — everybody takes what they can, because holding back only means the next guy takes your fish. He says there's two fixes and only two: carve the lake into private plots with owners, or bring in the state with a rule and a fine.

Renata said thats not what happened here. The old families always had rules — a size limit, a season, a spot on the shore, everyone knew everyone — and the lake was fine for decades. What killed it was the new houses, the sonar, and the bigger nets, so one guy can clean out a whole bay in an afternoon. She says the problem was never the sharing; it was that the rules stopped being backed by anything.

Dave said fine, but those rules only worked because there were six families and no market to sell to. Scale it up and the rules rot. And who writes the rules anyway? Whoever yells loudest. So you're back to an owner or a warden with a ticket book.

Renata said a warden is just a guy watching the shore, which is the thing she's already describing, only with a uniform. She says most of the shared stuff in the world that actually lasts is run by neither an owner nor a government, it's run by the users: clear boundaries, someone watching, and penalties that start small — a warning, then you lose your spot for a week, then you're out. She said its more like a club than a free-for-all.

Dave said that only works for a small, stable group with a shared history, and that there's still the temptation: one guy sneaks an extra bucket and hopes nobody notices. Renata said she never claimed everyone behaves, she said rules and watching. Dave said that's the same thing.

There was also a long detour where Dave kept using his lighthouse example and Renata kept saying the lighthouse isnt even the same kind of thing, because nobody's light gets used up when you look at it, while one fish in my bucket is a fish not in yours. Dave said the temptation is identical either way. Renata said no it isnt, because at the lighthouse you cant take more than your share by looking harder, and at the lake you can, which is the whole problem.

And at one point Renata said she'd happily keep the rule if she trusted everyone else to — she just isnt going to come home empty-handed while everyone else fills their boat. Dave said thats just a prisoner's dilemma with extra steps. She said it isnt, because if she knew the others would keep the rule she'd keep it too, and thats not the same as a case where taking extra pays off no matter what the other guy does.

Can you go through this move by move and tell me whos right about what, and where they're talking past each other? Dont just tell me they're both sort of right. At the end say who had the better of it overall, and the one thing neither of them is seeing. Last line: Verdict: <who was more right overall> — <the one thing they both missed, in one sentence>
