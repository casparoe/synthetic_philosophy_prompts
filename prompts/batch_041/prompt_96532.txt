In my historiography seminar we have spent the term going in circles on the presentism problem, and two camps keep arguing past each other: the presentists, who hold that a historian can never fully step outside the standards of her own time and that the pretense of doing so is a kind of bad faith, and the contextualists, who hold that recovering a past society's own standards is the historian's central discipline and that importing present-day moral or conceptual vocabulary is an anachronism to be avoided. Please draft the joint statement the two camps could actually sign. I want the claims both would accept — about what the evidence can and cannot establish, about the difference between explaining a past practice and endorsing it, and about the fact that past societies were themselves internally divided over their own standards, so that the phrase the past's own standards may not name any single thing. Then state, in wording each side would accept for its own position, exactly where they still differ; I suspect one crux is whether a neutral description of a past practice is possible at all, another is whether moral evaluation is part of the historian's task or merely something to be explained, and a third is whether the anachronism of vocabulary can be separated from the anachronism of judgment. For each crux, give the case or argument both sides would agree bears on it — for instance, how each would handle a historian who writes about a past institution entirely in the vocabulary of the actors themselves — and record what each side predicts about that case. Close with an honest note on which parts of the dispute are merely verbal. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
