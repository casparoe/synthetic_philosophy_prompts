You're a philosopher who works on skill and practical knowledge — the Ryle / Stanley & Williamson kind of stuff, intellectualism about knowing how, all that. I want a dialogue out of you, not an essay.

Setup: two friends, both sharp, both mid-PhD, splitting a pitcher after a talk. One is an intellectualist — knowing how to do something is just knowing a fact, a fact about a way of doing it, and the doing comes after. The other thinks that's backwards: know-how is its own kind of practical capacity and you can have plenty of it with no proposition anywhere in sight. Neither is a dummy, neither gets to win by being smug, and neither is allowed to just restate their position louder.

Before you write a line of dialogue, brainstorm to yourself: jot down two or three things each side has to explain about the case below. Then write.

The case they should fight over (use this, dont substitute a textbook example): a woman works on a line grading batches of dye by eye. She's 97% accurate and the company pays her accordingly. Asked what she's looking for, she says something like "a bit lighter than that one, I guess," which helps nobody. Give her a written rule — a hue-angle threshold — and her accuracy drops to about 70%; the rule seems to get in her way. But she learned the job in the first place by being told that rule. She can also spot a bad batch the rule would call fine, and can't say why.

Make them actually respond to each other — if one says something, the other answers that thing, not the thing they wish the other had said. Let the case pull them both around a bit. It should not be obvious who's winning when you stop.

Format, with these sections and rough lengths:

**The dialogue** (~800 words, two named speakers, real back and forth)
**Where the disagreement is real and where it's verbal** (~150 words — tell me which parts of their spat are them actually disagreeing about the world and which are two vocabularies for the same fact)
**Which one the case hurts more** (~100 words, pick a side and say what it has to give up)

No citations, no reading list. Think it through before you start writing.
