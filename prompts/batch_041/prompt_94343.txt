I'm a backend engineer so take the philosophy with a grain of salt, but our reading group keeps going in circles on the thought experiment where you have to pick between one person being tortured for the rest of their life and an unimaginably huge number of people each getting a single dust speck in the eye for a second -- no damage, they blink, it's gone. Everyone says the torture, obviously. Then someone makes the number bigger, and bigger, and then raises that to itself a few times, and the room goes quiet and we start over from the top.

Write me a poem about it, 20-30 lines, no fixed form. One formal rule I care about: the specks have to be handled by a device that can't actually count them -- a line that keeps coming back with the number climbing by orders of magnitude, or a line that loses a syllable each time it repeats, something like that -- while the torture gets one plain line that's word-for-word identical every time it returns. I want a turn where the reader notices they've been doing the adding up themselves, because thats the actual move the debate is about (my instinct is sum vs max, but the poem shouldn't take my side). And give me two short bits in the middle, one in the voice of somebody who got their speck and already forgot it, one in the voice of the person being tortured -- neither of them knows the arithmetic is happening, only the reader does. Don't let it end on either "the number's too big to mean anything" or "obviously the torture." Leave me where the room is.
