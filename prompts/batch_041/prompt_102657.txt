Q2. Parfit argues that personal identity is not what matters in survival. Consider a variant of his teletransporter that I'll call the Relay. You step into the scanner in London; it records your blueprint and destroys you. The station on Mars builds replica A. The station also keeps your blueprint on file and, by standing policy, will build a second replica, B, from that same blueprint ten years later, whether or not A is still around. A and B never meet and never learn of each other. (a) On Parfit's account, what is the relation between you and A? Between you and B? Between A and B? (b) A student in the seminar proposes the Similarity View: what matters is simply that some later being has your memories, traits, and so on, however it came to have them — even if the resemblance arose by sheer coincidence. Is the Similarity View a restatement of Parfit's position, a rival to it, or something Parfit's position already rules out? Justify your answer.

Here's what the student handed in:

"Parfit's central claim is that personal identity is not what matters; what matters is Relation R, psychological connectedness and/or continuity. Applying that to the Relay: replica A is psychologically continuous with me, so A is R-related to me, and for Parfit whether we go on to call A 'me' is a shallow, merely verbal question — everything that matters survives either way.

Replica B is built from the same blueprint, so B is R-related to me too, and since A and B share that blueprint, B is also R-related to A. So the Relay is survival twice over, and it isn't a case of death at all.

As for the Similarity View, I think it's basically a restatement of Parfit's position. His whole point with the teletransporter is that the cause of the continuity doesn't matter — an abnormal cause is good enough. So he couldn't object if the resemblance arose by coincidence instead of through a causal chain; on his view all that matters is that the psychology is there."
