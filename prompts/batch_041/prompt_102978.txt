A friend and I argued until two in the morning about Dr. Strangelove and never resolved it, so I would like you to adjudicate by reading the film closely. His thesis is that the doomsday machine is an acausal threat: a threat whose entire force lies in being believed rather than executed, since by the time anyone could carry it out the harm it was meant to prevent would already be done. On his telling, the machine is only the crude physical crutch that stabilizes the prediction, and the film's real subject is the peculiar logic of threats that work by being foreseen. My thesis is that this is a category mistake: the machine is an ordinary commitment device in the Schelling sense, a way of making a conditional intention credible by destroying one's own power to reconsider, and the film is a satire about a commitment that cannot be recalled rather than a meditation on the metaphysics of threats. I want a reading that decides between these, not one that splits the difference.

Please do several things. First, state precisely what an acausal threat is and what would have to be true for one to be rational, then say whether the doomsday machine is one, is merely a commitment device, or is both at once; I am genuinely unsure which of us is right, so the analysis matters more than the verdict. Second, anchor the argument in specific moments: the briefing in which Strangelove explains that the machine only deters if it is known about and that the element of surprise is what makes it useless, Muffley's telephone call to the drunken Soviet premier, the discovery that the machine can be triggered by an attack and cannot be disarmed once triggered, Turgidson's first-strike arithmetic about how many Americans would be acceptable losses, and Major Kong's ride down. Where does the film dramatize the gap between a threat that is executed and a threat that is merely believed, and where does it trade on a confusion between the two? Does it understand the difference between a commitment that is credible because it is automatic and one that is credible because it is predicted, or does the satire blur them?

Third, bring in a proposal from the acausal-threat literature that you may not have seen, which I will summarize so that the prompt stands on its own. The proposal is that an agent can protect itself against blackmail by adding a deliberately pointless goal to its preferences — a honeypot, say a commitment to prevent the existence of some absurdly specific object — so that threats get deflected onto the honeypot rather than onto the things the agent actually cares about; the broader idea is that two agents can make themselves jointly better off by adopting such a surrogate goal, and by other safe Pareto improvements of the same kind, before anyone has a chance to threaten them. Now ask whether the doomsday machine is the inverse of this device: rather than deflecting a threat onto a worthless target, it enlarges the target to include the whole world, so that everyone is maximally exposed and no one has anything left to trade. If the Soviets had absorbed this literature, what should they have built instead, and is it fair to hold a 1964 satire to that standard? And what, if anything, does General Ripper's private cosmology — his obsession with the purity of his own bodily fluids, his conviction that fluoridation is a communist plot — contribute to the reading? Is he a man whose surrogate goal has quietly eaten his real one?

Finally, be honest about the seams in both directions. Where the film simplifies or falsifies the decision theory, say so plainly; where the decision theory is thinner than the film — where the drama notices something about threats, credibility, and self-binding that the formalism does not capture — say that too. I am not asking for a plot summary, a bibliography, or a survey of the literature, and I do not need you to quote the screenplay; I want an argument that uses the film as evidence and is willing to conclude that one of us was wrong. End by saying what the film, as a work of drama, adds to this debate that argument alone cannot.
