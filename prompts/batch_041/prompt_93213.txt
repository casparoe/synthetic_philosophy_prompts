A friend who is a graduate student in cognitive science recently sent me the following summary of the dispute between Dennett and Searle over original and derived intentionality, and asked me whether I thought it was fair. I have read enough Dennett and Searle to suspect that the summary is mostly on target but that it tilts the playing field in a couple of places, though I cannot quite say where. I would like an audit of the summary, sentence by sentence, that separates what Dennett would accept, what Searle would accept, what is a common simplification, and what is simply wrong. I am not looking for citations or a reading list; I want to understand the substantive disagreements well enough to judge the summary's overall fairness.

The summary reads:

"Dennett's two-bitser is a vending machine designed to accept US quarters. He imagines shipping it to Twin Earth, where the coins are chemically different but indistinguishable from ours, and then to a country where the currency is different but the slugs are the same size and weight. The machine keeps working. Dennett asks: when it accepts a Twin-Earth quarter, does it believe it is a US quarter? He says there is no fact of the matter. The machine's internal states do not determine whether it is tracking US quarters, Twin-Earth quarters, or just things that fit the slot. That is supposed to show that the machine has no real intentionality, only the as-if intentionality we project onto it when we take the intentional stance. Dennett then generalizes: since our own beliefs are also just ascriptions that work for prediction, and since natural selection built us the way the vending machine was built, our intentionality is no more original than the machine's. Searle's reply is that this is behaviorism in new clothes. He says there is a real difference between intrinsic intentionality, which belongs to our conscious mental states and is a biological phenomenon, and derived intentionality, which belongs to words, pictures, and artifacts because we confer it on them. The two-bitser has at most derived intentionality, so it cannot settle anything about whether we have intrinsic intentionality. Searle adds that Dennett's view cannot even state its own position without using intrinsic intentionality, since the claim that there is no fact of the matter is itself a claim about what Dennett believes."
