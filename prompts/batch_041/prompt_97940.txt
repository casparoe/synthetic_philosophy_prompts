OK so I was up till 2am having this stupid arguement with my friend Dan about the grandfather paradox and now I want to turn it into something. I write a little philosophy-adjacent newsletter and I want to make a which-position-do-you-actually-hold flowchart out of it, like a decision tree that starts from cases and ends at a label. The problem with every quiz I've seen is it just lists the labels up front (you know, the universe-stops-you one, the branching-timelines one, the it's-logically-impossible one) and of course everyone picks whichever name they've already heard. I want it built the other way round: the person answers concrete stuff and the label falls out at the end.

So please write that tree. Roughly 8 to 10 nodes, and every node has to be a question someone can answer without knowing where it leads. Start somewhere like: is going back and killing your grandad even a coherent thing to attempt, or is the phrase doing something sneaky. Then split on whether the past is one fixed sequence or genuinely branches, whether a traveler can be blocked by the universe from ever making a contradiction, whether self-caused loops are a dealbreaker or just weird (the diary you find in the attic that turns out to be your own, the guy who goes back and ends up being his own grandad's mysterious benefactor, info that comes from nowhere), whether changing the past is even the right word when there's no unchanged version left over to compare it to, and one I keep getting stuck on, whether you'd notice, i.e. when you come back do you remember the old timeline or not, and does that even make sense.

Couple of specific things I want in there because they're what we actually fought about: a time machine that can only send you back to a moment after the machine itself was switched on (so you can never reach the really juicy old stuff), and the case where the whole point is that you were always the one who did it. Leaves should be named positions where a name exists and 'nobody really defends this' where it doesn't, each with one line on that view's most awkward commitment. Also flag any two leaves the tree can't actually tell apart, and if some combination of answers is just incoherent rather than merely unexplored, say so instead of pretending it's a position.

Dan's take is that the whole thing is a category error and past-directed travel is simply impossible, so make sure that shows up as a proper leaf and not a footnote. Cheers.
