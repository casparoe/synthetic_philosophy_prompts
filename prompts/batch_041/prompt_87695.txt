I'm trying to work out whether it makes sense to say a reinforcement learning agent can suffer, and I keep bumping into an old text I only half remeber. It's Descartes, in the Discourse on Method, part five, where he argues that animals are just machines. The gist as I recall: you could in principle build an automaton that looks and acts like a person, but there'd be two ways to catch it out. First, it could never use speech or signs the way we do, to make its thoughts known to others, so a parrot making sounds isn't talking. Second, even if it did everything else as well as us or better, it would fail at something, and the failure would show that it acts not from knowledge but from the arrangement of its parts, the way a clock keeps time. A machine can be built to do one thing very well, but not to get by in the world the way a general-purpose reasoner does, because reason is a universal instrument that can cope with any circumstance, while organs need a particular disposition.

What I want help with is getting from that to the RL case, and I'm worried I'm conflating two diffrent things. When people say a reward signal is like a valence signal, I think they mean the sign of the reward: the agent approaches what's positive, avoids what's negative, and its internal estimate of expected return plays the role of how good or bad something seems. But that's a claim about control, not about feeling. And here's where I get stuck: if I define a valenced state operationally as one the system's policy is organized around, so that it will trade off other things to keep that signal in a certain region, then a thermostat with a setpoint qualifies, and so does Descartes's beast-machine, and the criterion stops doing any work. Descartes's two tests look like tests for reason and generality, not for sentience at all, so I can't tell whether they're relevant to the valence question or whether I'm just pattern-matching because both involve "is it a machine or isn't it."

There's also something in the background about how Descartes wasn't denying animals sensation, only thought, but I may be inventing that to make him sound more reasonable than he was. If that's a misreading, tell me. And if the whole setup is confused — if "reward signal as valence" is a category error that makes the Descartes comparison idle — just say so plainly rather than playing along.
