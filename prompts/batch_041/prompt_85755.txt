Write a dialogue between two philosophers who disagree about what it takes for two people to be acting together rather than just acting in parallel. The case they're stuck on: A and B, who cant stand each other, are being paid to haul a piano up three flights of stairs. They've never worked together, they have no loyalty to each other, each is only in it for the money and would walk off for a better offer, and neither would lift a finger for the other under any other circumstances. But on the stairs they're perfectly in sync -- reading each others movements, adjusting their grip, wordlessly coordinating. Philosopher 1 says that's joint action, full stop: each intends to do his part, each intends it partly *because* the other does, and the plan is mutually out in the open; nothing else is required. Philosopher 2 says no, that's just two people coordinating, not a genuine we -- for that each has to be answerable to the other, bound in a way he can't unilaterally cancel, and these two are bound by nothing but their paychecks. Let them go back and forth on the case, and on whether the we is doing any real explanatory work or is just a normative add-on that doesn't belong in an account of acting together. Neither should be a pushover and neither should concede for the sake of harmony. I'd also like them to fight over a spontaneous case -- strangers who all grab the same overturned car and lift it -- since that seems to cut against one of them. Don't force a resolution; end with the disagreement sharper than it started.
