I've spent the past two years reporting on the fight over Integrated Information Theory, and what strikes me is how little of it turns on data. IIT holds that a system is conscious to the degree that it has a certain kind of integrated causal structure; its critics answer that any such system can be "unfolded" into a feedforward network with the same input–output behaviour that the theory itself scores as non-conscious, so that no behavioural test could ever separate the two — which, they say, makes the framework closer to a definition of its target than to a falsifiable hypothesis. My working hypothesis, and I'd like you to test it rather than assume it, is that this impasse is not empirical at all but a disagreement about what a theory of consciousness is supposed to do: one side wants an account that says what consciousness is and then accepts the consequences, the other wants predictions that could fail on grounds independent of the theory's own definition of its subject matter. Is that the real fault line, or is a hidden formal or empirical question doing the work? I don't want a survey of positions. I want a diagnosis, grounded in what each side actually claims, and a straight answer about what could move the debate — a theorem, an experiment, a conceptual distinction — or why nothing will.
