You are a philosopher of mind and perception with expertise in cross-modal cognition, and I want you to work here as a designer of thought experiments rather than as a commentator on them. Think carefully before writing. Begin by listing the variables that a Molyneux-style scenario would have to control, decide which single variable you will vary, and only then commit to a case. Present the case, then demonstrate that it satisfies every specification below.

Background. Molyneux's question asks whether a person blind from birth, who has learned to distinguish a cube from a sphere by touch, could tell which is which by sight alone upon first being able to see. Three families of answer are in play. Treat them as defined here, rather than importing whatever labels you happen to associate with them.

1. Format-neutral: shape is represented in a code indifferent to modality, so a shape concept acquired by touch is immediately available to vision and recognition is instantaneous.

2. Modality-specific: the visual concept of a shape must be built out of visual experience, so a tactile shape concept gives no purchase on a visual array and recognition fails.

3. Contingency-learning: what a perceiver knows when she knows a shape is how its appearance would change as it, or she, moves. This is mastery of the object's contingencies rather than of one sense organ's, so it can be acquired through touch and can transfer to vision, but only when it was acquired by acting, that is, by producing changes and observing their consequences rather than by receiving stimulation.

Task. Construct one realistic case, a habilitation or clinical scenario, satisfying all of the following.

- The subject is blind from birth, with no pattern vision, and gains sight in adulthood through a surgically correctable congenital condition. There is no sensory-substitution device, no camera, no residual light perception, and nothing that could be redescribed as a form of seeing before the surgery. The case must not be the textbook cube-and-sphere pair.

- The case must contain a controlled contrast between two subjects, or two conditions for one subject, that differ in exactly one respect: whether the tactile exposure to the target shape was actively explored or passively received. Amount, duration, and patterning of tactile stimulation, prior familiarity with the shape, attention, motivation, age at the onset of sight, and the visual test procedure must all be held fixed.

- The verdicts I ask for must fall out of the three views rather than be built into the case: the format-neutral view predicts no difference between the two conditions, with both recognizing the shape; the modality-specific view predicts no difference, with neither recognizing it; the contingency-learning view predicts a difference, with the active condition recognizing it and the passive condition failing.

Deliverables. (a) The case, in no more than one paragraph. (b) For each of the three views, the verdict and the reasoning that generates it, including what it says about the condition it predicts to fail. (c) A frank statement of which feature of the case does the discriminating work, and what becomes of the case if that feature is removed, in particular whether it then collapses into agreement with one of the other two views. (d) A statement of what the case cannot settle, specifically whether it can adjudicate between the two views that agree about the contrast. (e) The strongest reply available to the contingency-learning theorist, that is, how she would redescribe the passive condition so that it no longer counts as passive, and whether that reply survives the constraint that attention is held fixed.

State what the case is for: it exists to isolate whether agency is a genuine variable in cross-modal recognition, so a case that secures the verdict pattern by stipulating it, or by smuggling a difference in attention or stimulation into the passive condition, fails.
