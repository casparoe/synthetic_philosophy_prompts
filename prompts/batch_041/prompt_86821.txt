I spent the better part of four decades in an experimental physics laboratory, on problems where the whole point of the apparatus was that it could have told me no, and I have spent the years since my retirement reading philosophy of science with more appetite than discipline. I have arrived at a comparison that I cannot get to come out cleanly, and I would like you to work through it with me.

The comparison is between two ways of arguing for something that no one has observed.

The first I will call *earning its keep in advance*. An unobserved entity is posited to explain something already known, and it is then held to a further standard: it must go on to imply something nobody had thought to look for, and that implication must survive the looking. The neutrino is my favourite example, though the positron will do as well. When the further implication fails, we give up the entity, and we are right to.

The second I will call *buying off a coincidence*. Here nothing new is implied. An ensemble is posited instead — many universes, many domains, many regions, supplied in the case that interests me by eternal inflation and the string landscape — together with some selection effect, and the argument runs that a fact about our own situation that looked like a staggering coincidence is really just what a typical observer in the ensemble ought to expect. The smallness of the cosmological constant, the hospitality of the constants to chemistry and to stars: on this style of argument these are not explained so much as rendered unremarkable.

Here is my difficulty. Darwinian natural selection looks, on its surface, like an argument of the second kind as well — an ensemble of variations plus a selection effect, converting the appearance of design from a datum into an expected outcome. Yet I regard natural selection as among the best-supported theories I know, and the multiverse as, at best, a promissory note. I want to know whether that asymmetry is principled or merely parochial; whether I am drawing a real distinction or simply defending the theories I grew up alongside.

Please compare the two cases as carefully as you can. In particular: what work is the ensemble doing in each, and does it matter that in the one case we can go and inspect both the ensemble and the selecting process, while in the other the ensemble is posited precisely in order to perform the explanatory task for which it is then credited? Is that circularity, or is it ordinary theory construction, of the kind that every unobservable has always required? Does the fact that we could not have observed otherwise function as evidence, or as an excuse? And if the two cases are genuinely disanalogous, say exactly where the disanalogy bites — I suspect it has something to do with what could in principle have gone the other way, but I cannot put my finger on it.

I want an argument, not a survey. I have no library at hand and will take what you say on its merits, so do not send me to the literature; state the case yourself and defend it. If my two categories are bad ones — if the distinction I am groping for is better drawn some other way, or if there is a third style of argument I have overlooked — say so, and draw it better.
