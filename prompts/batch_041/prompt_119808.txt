I'm a backend engineer with no philosophy background and I've been stuck on this for a week, so I'm hoping you'll just settle it for me. Someone on a forum laid out an argument that self-deception is impossible, and honestly every step looks fine to me:

(1) To deceive someone is to get them to believe something you know to be false.
(2) So the deceiver has to know the truth, and the deceived has to end up believing the false thing.
(3) In self-deception the deceiver and the deceived are the same person.
(4) So one and the same person must both know p and believe not-p.
(5) Nobody can know p and believe not-p at the same time; knowing p already includes believing p.
(6) So self-deception is impossible, and what we call it is either a plain error or ordinary wishful thinking.

The part that won't sit right with me: the smoker who only ever reads the studies saying it's fine, the guy who's 'just not ready to look at the numbers' and keeps not looking — those look real, and they look like something he's doing, not just a bug in his reasoning. The deliberate version is worse: deciding on Sunday to stop thinking about a thing, and by Friday genuinely not believing it anymore. That looks intentional and it seems to work.

The one escape route I've heard is that the mind is seperate into two parts and one part deceives the other. My instinct from debugging distributed systems is that this just relocates the problem: the lying part still has to hold the truth somewhere, and that truth still has to steer behaviour, so why doesn't it leak into the part it's lying to? And if the two parts really are sealed off from each other, in what sense is that one person deceiving himself rather than two people sharing a head?

Which numbered step do I cut, and what does cutting it cost me? I've probaly missed something obvious. Give me a definite verdict — name the step you'd actually give up, say why it looked fine, and defend the choice rather than handing me a menu of positions. I'd rather keep the phenomenon than keep (1), but I want to know what I'm paying either way.
