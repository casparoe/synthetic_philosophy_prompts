Ok so I'm putting together a fellowship application — one year, two pages, philosophy of mind-ish — and I want to pitch something I've been chewing on for a while and its driving me nuts. There's this toy model I half-remember from somewhere (can't place it, doesn't matter for the proposal): a big sphere, call it a careenium, packed with millions of tiny balls, simmballs, that do exactly one thing — bump into each other. That's it. No fields, no forces beyond contact, no little arrows painted on them. And yet the whole contraption can be described as storing symbols, comparing them, deciding things; you can tell a story about the careenium in which symbols get shuffled around and operated on. So: do the symbols push the substrate around? When the careenium "decides" and then does something, what did the work — the symbolic story, or the simmballs?

Here's the half-formed bit I need you to turn into a proposal. I think the question is under-specified, because there are at least two things someone could mean by "the symbols did the work." One is that the symbol-level description is an overlay we impose from outside; the other is that the system itself has parts that are sensitive to the macro pattern — some chunk of simmballs that responds to the pattern rather than to the individual bumps. I think only the second version makes the question answerable, and I want the proposal to argue that, and to say what "answerable" amounts to: something like, there are macro-level interventions that are well defined and that make a difference to macro-outcomes across different micro-realizations, so the macro level has purchase that isn't just a summary. I don't want a proposal that just says "the simmballs do all the pushing, the symbols are a description" — I think that's the boring answer and it dodges the question.

So: precise statement of the question, why the two obvious positions both leave it open, the method (conceptual, not a lit review — I'll add citations myself, don't worry about those), and why that method rather than the obvious alternatives. Month-by-month plan for one year, a paragraph on what a real result would look like, and be honest about what would count as the thing failing — I'd say if my macro-intervention criterion collapses into a relabelled micro-intervention, that's a fail, and I'd rather hear that now than in month nine.
