I've been thinking about the absent-minded driver problem and I want a new case in that family, but built to order. The classic version has the driver at two intersections, she can't tell which one she's at, payoffs are 0 for exiting at the first, 4 for exiting at the second, and 1 for continuing past both, and the optimal policy is to continue with probability 2/3. I want a fresh scenario, not driving, that does the same kind of work but with a twist: I want the optimal policy to be a non-trivial mix, and I want causal decision theory and evidential decision theory to disagree with each other about what to do, with one of them matching the optimal planning policy and the other not. Also, the agent should be able to know the full structure of the problem and still be unable to tell which node she's at. Constraints: realistic setting (a kitchen, an office, a phone tree, something like that), no more than a paragraph for the scenario itself, and the payoffs should be such that the mixed strategy is strictly between 0 and 1 and not just a coin flip. After the scenario, walk through what each theory says and why, tell me which feature of the case is doing the work (and what happens if you remove it), and say how a defender of the theory that comes out badly would try to attack the case. I'm using this for a decision theory reading group, so I want the case to be genuinely diagnostic, not just a re-skin of the driver. Also, I'd like the disagreement to hinge on whether the agent treats her own action as evidence about what she does at the other node, not on some weird payoff structure that makes one theory look silly.
