Consider a variant of the physical/design/intentional framework that I will call budgeted realism. On this account, a pattern P is real for an agent A just in case A's best predictive model of the target system — where "best" is measured against A's actual computational and representational budget — traffics in P. Nothing in the account requires that P be intrinsic to the system, or that every possible observer be forced to employ P; realness is indexed to a budget.

Now take a case. A team constructs a system, S, by deliberate search: they enumerate physical configurations until they find one whose future outputs are predicted, to a tight tolerance, by a short intentional-stance model — a handful of attributed beliefs and desires plus a decision rule — and are predicted much worse by any physical or design description of comparable length. Two things are stipulated about the configuration they settle on. First, no part of S's causal machinery can be read as representing the attributed contents; the intentional model is a predictive overlay rather than a mechanism, and no design-level description of S mentions anything that maps onto that content. Second, S was selected precisely because the overlay predicts it, so its intentional-stance tractability is a designed effect rather than a coincidence.

Take the proposition R: there is an observer-independent fact of the matter about whether S's belief/desire pattern is real — that is, whether it belongs in the correct inventory of what exists, rather than only in the class of useful predictive fictions relative to some budget.

Give me your credence in R, decomposed. At minimum I want terms for the following, each with a number and a one-line justification:

(a) the probability that pattern-realism is a substantive thesis at all, rather than something that always collapses into stance- or budget-relativity — i.e., that R is well-formed rather than a category mistake;
(b) conditional on R's being well-formed, the probability that engineered predictive success is disqualifying, given that being reliably readable by an instrument is not normally thought to disqualify a property from being real;
(c) the probability that the absence of internal representation matters for the reality of the pattern, as opposed to only for the reality of its content;
(d) whether the human case is relevantly parallel: if our own intentional-stance predictability is likewise an effect of selection, does consistency force the same verdict about us, and how much of your answer about S is actually driven by your prior about us?

Then combine the terms into a single number, identify which term your credence is most sensitive to and which you are least confident in, and say what argument or piece of evidence would move the final number most. If you think R is ill-formed, say so explicitly and instead give your credence in the best well-formed surrogate you can state, along with what would count as resolving it.
