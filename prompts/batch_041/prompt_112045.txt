I dont have any history training, just a guy who reads too much on the internet. I keep running into this line, usually from someone trying to win an argument about whether the US or "the West" is declining: "Rome didnt fall, it just transformed." The move is always the same -- you say something about collapse, and they say Rome proves that what looks like collapse is really just a change of clothes, so you cant infer anything. I want to know what that sentence could actually be claiming. Not the vibe, the precise claims. I've heard all the usual suspects -- Gibbon and moral decline, barbarians, climate, lead pipes, Christianity, elites not paying taxes -- but I dont want a list of causes. I want the different things the slogan could mean, from the almost trivial to the real historical theses to the ones that are secretly about our concepts rather than the past. Some readings seem almost trivial (the city of Rome is still there), some seem like real claims (the western empire ended but Roman institutions, taxes, churches, and elites kept going under new rulers), some sound like they might be false or at least not supported (nobody noticed a decline in living standards, it was only a political change), and some seem to be about the word "fall" smuggling in a moral story. Also there's the question of whether "Rome" means the city, the citizenship, the empire, the imperial office, the tax state, the culture, or the idea. I suspect alot of the argument is people using different meanings and not noticing. I'm definately not a historian.

Include at least 8 readings, ordered from the most trivial to the most substantive.
