I'm giving a talk in a few weeks on where persuasion ends and manipulation begins, and I want to defend a line I think is right but that I expect to get hammered on, so I'd like the argument sharpened before I'm standing in front of a room.

Here's the position. Manipulation isn't about deception, and it isn't about bypassing someone's rational faculties either — that framing collapses the moment you notice that a good argument can bypass them too, and that a truthful choice of framing can steer someone just as hard as a lie. What makes influence manipulative is a specific asymmetry in what the influencer is tracking. The influencer is tailoring the influence to facts about you that you can't see the influence being tailored to, and that tailoring is what's doing the work. Call it load-bearing opacity. A car ad built around the fact that you just had a kid, where you'd recognize the targetting instantly if it were shown to you, is manipulative even if every word in it is true. A friend who knows you hate being condescended to and pitches her advice a certain way isn't manipulating you, because if you asked why she said it that way she'd just tell you, and the influence doesn't depend on your not knowing.

So the criterion I want to defend: an act of influence is manipulative to the degree that its efficacy depends on the target's inability to reconstruct the mechanism, and legitimate to the degree that it keeps working even under full transparency about how it works.

I want you to argue for this. Three things are definately coming at me and you'll need to deal with all of them. (1) If personalization is everywhere, doesn't the criterion either make everything manipulative or nothing? (2) There are cases where transparency seems to make things worse — tell someone exactly how the ad is working and it may just work harder — which looks like it breaks the keeps-working-under-transparency clause in the wrong direction. (3) The honest demagogue: no lies, no secrets, nothing hidden about what he's doing, and yet the whole technique depends on his audience not noticing it.

Give me the strongest version you can. Take the objections seriously instead of swatting them, and be explicit about what the view has to give up to survive them — if it has to give up the clean criterion above, say so and tell me what replaces it. Don't close by retreating into "well it's all a matter of degree" unless degrees are actually doing work in the argument.
