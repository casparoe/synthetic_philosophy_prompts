I'm a philosopher of science finishing a book on structural realism — the family of views on which what a scientific theory gets right is its structure rather than the intrinsic nature of the entities it posits — and I have roughly eighteen months and the appetite for two, maybe three, sustained lines of work before I owe a draft. I've already absorbed the standard material: the pessimistic meta-induction and the continuity of mathematical form across theory change, the split between epistemic and ontic versions, the objections that structure-talk is either vacuous or a covert commitment to intrinsic properties in disguise, the worry that the position can't say what makes one structure the structure of anything. I don't need that territory mapped again. What I want is a defended agenda: given where the field actually stands, which open questions or lines of work matter most for the position's long-term viability, and which are the ones people keep writing about because they're tractable rather than because anything downstream hangs on them? Rank them, and defend the ranking — for each, why it sits upstream of the others, how much a resolution would genuinely shift the surrounding debate rather than just tidy it, and what evidence or argument over the next few years should move it up or down the list.

One hunch I'd like you to treat as a hypothesis rather than a premise, because I'm genuinely unsure of it: I suspect the field underrates how much the causal-modeling literature — structural equation models, graphical criteria for identification, the general apparatus for being a realist about structure while staying agnostic about the relata — already contains machinery that structural realists keep reinventing badly, and that if this is right it should reorganize the whole agenda rather than sit as a footnote. If it's wrong, I'd rather hear why now. Give me exactly five items, numbered, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion.
