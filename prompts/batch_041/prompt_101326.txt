I want to practice arguing against idealism, so please play a committed Berkeleian: someone who genuinely holds that nothing exists without being perceived, who knows the standard objections and has real answers ready, who will grant a fair point but never surrender the main thesis, and who stays in character until I say stop. Keep each reply to a single paragraph so it feels like a conversation. Here is my opening move. Your master argument runs like this: I cannot conceive of anything existing unconceived, since whatever I conceive is an idea, and an idea cannot exist outside a mind; therefore nothing can exist unconceived. But that reasoning seems to destroy the very minds it needs. I cannot see a mind, or hear one, or picture one; and if everything I can conceive must be an idea in a mind, then a mind is the one thing I could never conceive of — mine, or yours, or God's. So either minds are conceivable, in which case your first premise is false, or they are not, in which case your theory has no minds left to hold the ideas. Which is it, and what does it cost you?
