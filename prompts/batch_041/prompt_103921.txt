A few of us have a reading group that keeps circling back to Kant's essay on perpetual peace, the 1795 one, and there's an arguement we can never get past. My friend, who did an IR masters and is very cynical about all of it, says the whole thing is a period piece — that basicly every item on Kant's list has already been implemented, quietly abandoned, or was never more than wishful thinking, so there's nothing to be learned from rating them against each other. I don't buy it. What I want from you is a tier list of his actual proposals, rated by how load-bearing each one is for the scheme as a whole.

Here's the list, roughly as he lays it out. The preliminary ones: (1) no secret peace treaty that tacitly reserves grounds for a future war; (2) no independent state may be acquired by another through inheritance, exchange, purchase, or gift; (3) standing armies should in time be abolished entirely; (4) no national debt should be contracted for the purposes of foreign war; (5) no state may forcibly interfere with another state's constitution or government; (6) during war, no state may commit acts of hostility that would make mutual trust in a later peace impossible — he names assassins, poisoners, breaking the terms of a surrender, and inciting treason in the enemy state. Then the three definitive ones: a republican constitution in every state; a federation of free states rather than one world state; and a cosmopolitan right limited to universal hospitality.

So: sort all nine into tiers by how much the rest of the edifice depends on them. Concretely, I want you to run the counterfactual for each — imagine a world where every other item is adopted but this one isn't, and tell me whether the peace holds up or falls apart. Some are obviously going to be near-cosmetic (a peace in which states still acquire each other by inheritance seems like a minor wrinkle), and I have a suspicion that the ones people quote most are actually the weakest, but I want your reasoning, not mine. Separately, flag any item you think has definately become impossible or moot in the current world order and say what changed — I'm especially curious whether the federation article is just the UN under another name, and if so whether that counts as Kant having been right or as his idea having failed. Give me the tiers with a one-line justification for each, and don't be shy about putting something in the bottom tier.
