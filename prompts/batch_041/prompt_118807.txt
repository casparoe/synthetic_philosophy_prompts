I've been rereading GEB and I keep snagging on the inviolate level. The Drawing Hands thing: the two hands draw each other, but the drawing itself happens somewhere that isn't in the picture, and Hofstadter's point is that something has to sit outside the tangle for the tangle to get drawn at all. Fine. But I work on proof assistants and we have a very concrete version of this, and I can't tell if it's the same idea or a totally different animal. Everything clever in a prover -- tactics, automation, the elaborator -- is untrusted; all of it just spits out proof terms, and a tiny kernel that nobody verifies says yes or no. That kernel is what every claim ultimately rests on, and it's the one piece that definately has to be right. Same story bootstrapping a compiler: you need some stage-0 seed binary before you can compile the compiler that compiles itself.

So compare the inviolate level, the way Hofstadter uses it, with the trusted base in those engineering cases. Where they line up, where they come apart, and whether the difference is real or just me liking the poetry. My hunch is that Hofstadter's version is doing more work -- the kernel only relocates the trust, it doesn't explain anything, whereas the inviolate level is supposed to be where the meaning or the agency actually lives -- but I don't trust that hunch. For that matter, tell me if the whole thing is just Tarski's object-language/metalanguage split with a nicer picture, in which case I've been romanticizing a hierarchy.

And please don't do the both-sides survey thing. Commit. If only one of these is the right general way to think about self-reference and levels, say which one it is and why, and be specific about what my hunch gets wrong.
