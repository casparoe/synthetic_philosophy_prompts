I build dashboards for a transit agency (ridership and service-frequency stuff, nothing glamorous) and I've gotten pretty fluent in the usual visual-encoding vocabulary -- marks and channels, scales, the data model underneath a spec, redundancy, preattentive pop-out, colorblind-safe palettes, that whole grammar-of-graphics way of talking about a chart as a mapping from fields to visual properties. A while back I learned that the unit chart, the one where you draw a little bus glyph per hundred trips instead of one big bar, is named after a guy, Otto Neurath, who ran a museum in Vienna in the 20s and apparently thought he was building an entire picture language, not just a chart style. I've only read secondhand stuff about him since: that quantities should be shown by repeating identical symbols rather than resizing them, because nobody can tell if a bigger blob means more height or more area; that his figures are flat and never in perspective; that there's a fixed dictionary of symbols and colours; that there's a distinct job, the transformer, whose whole role is turning source statistics into the picture; that he was doing this for working people who didn't read social-science prose, as part of a socialist city government's education program; and that he knew it couldn't be a full language and treated it as a helper for words. I've never read him directly and I'm not a philosopher, so I'm going on summaries. My colleague says it's just Tufte with pictograms and I'm sure that's wrong but I can't articulate why.

What I want is this: take the strongest version of Neurath's position on picture languages and restate it in the vocabulary I actually think in -- specs, encodings, scales, marks, data models, renderers, audiences, accessibility. Be as faithful as you can, and don't help yourself to my framework's assumptions while you do it; in particular, don't just assume there's a clean data layer that the picture is a picture of, if that isn't something he'd grant. Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about: what came through as something I could spec out tomorrow, what only came through as vibes, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. In that last category I'm wondering especially about the transformer as a named human role -- in my world that judgment is either the analyst's or it's supposed to be automated away, and my framework has no slot for a person whose job is deciding what the picture says -- and about the political-education side, since I have words like audience and takeaway but nothing like a chart being an act of mass enlightenment. For each piece of residue, tell me whether it's my framework missing something real or whether it's a sign that Neurath's claim was less about representation than about institutions; and if it's the latter, whether that's a dodge that lets him off the hook on the harder question of whether pictures can carry the logical structure -- negation, conditionals, all, some -- that the verbal language he was supposedly competing with carries fine. I'd rather you tell me the translation fails in an interesting place than smooth it over.
