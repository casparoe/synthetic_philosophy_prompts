ok so for my ethics class final i have to do a creative response to our unit on supererogation (acts that go beyond the call of duty) and i'm definately overthinking it, but my idea is to write it as a chunk of an employee handbook from a big tech-ish company that has basically decided that going above and beyond is just... the job. the handbook keeps sliding between "we're proud that our people do more than they have to" and "doing more than you have to is a core expectation," and nobody at the company ever notices the slide. by the end it should have gone all the way: the company has quietly abolished the difference between what you owe and what's extra, which i think means there's nothing left to praise anyone for, and the handbook is somehow fine with that. they're also weirdly confident about a philosophical claim -- something like "if an act is the best thing you could possibly do, then it was never optional" -- and they treat that as an HR policy rather than a moral theory.

can you write it? around 650 words, deadpan, no winking, formatted as numbered handbook sections with a few headers, and then a short FAQ at the end from the Office of Duty Compliance. give them some internal system they've invented -- i was thinking an internal market where employees trade credits for acts of heroism, and the joke is the credits keep losing value as the company makes more and more of those acts mandatory, but the company keeps trying to prop the price up. it should be funny to someone who's actually read about supererogation (the puzzle about whether the best act can really be optional, whether heroes are doing anything extra at all, the demandingness stuff) but my friends who haven't read any of it should still get the joke. dont have anyone in the piece explain the philosophy or name the paradox -- the reader should be able to work out what's being made fun of. the FAQ could answer things like whether you can opt out of the heroism tier, deadpan.

and please just start with the handbook itself, first line, no intro or explanation before it.
