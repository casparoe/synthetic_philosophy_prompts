I'm putting together a unit for a graduate semantics-and-pragmatics course on slurs, and I've reached the point where I'd rather have the specification than another theory. Every account I read seems built to handle three or four examples and to stumble over the rest, and I've started to suspect that half the disagreement in this literature is really about what a theory of slurs is supposed to deliver rather than about the words themselves. So before I assign anything else, write me the desiderata: the full list of requirements an adequate account of slurs has to satisfy. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it — the linguistic phenomenon, the pattern of judgments, or the fact about usage that the theory has to accommodate or predict — and keep the weight on the criteria themselves rather than on scoring the contenders.

I should say what I'll count as a requirement, because I don't want the list confined to what a semanticist would put on it. I do want the standard puzzles: how the derogatory content behaves when the word sits under negation, inside a conditional, or in reported speech; whether that content belongs to truth-conditional content or to some other dimension; what the relation is to the word's neutral counterpart, and what to say about the words that appear to have none; why the same string derogates in one language and is innocuous in another; why a word that is a slur in one use is an innocent homonym in another; why offensiveness varies in degree from word to word and from context to context. But I also want the requirements generated from outside semantics — from the fact that slurring is something we hold speakers responsible for, that hearers report being unable to hear certain words as neutral even when they want to, that members of the targeted group use the word among themselves and that this is not simply the same act, and that a speaker who doesn't know a word's history can still have done something with it. If any of these pull against the semantic requirements — if a requirement generated by the social facts can't be stated without already settling a semantic question — say so instead of smoothing it over.

For each requirement, tell me whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp, and flag any pair you think cannot be jointly satisfied. I expect the interesting result to be there: an account that locates the derogation in the content seems to collide with one family of cases, while an account that locates it entirely in use seems to collide with another, and I want that trade-off stated precisely rather than gestured at. Then identify the single requirement that eliminates the most candidate theories, and say which family of views it rules out. No reading list — I'm not asking you to cite anyone; I'll run the theories against your list myself.
