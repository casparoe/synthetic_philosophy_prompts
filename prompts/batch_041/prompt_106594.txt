I'm on my college's free-expression committee, and we vote Thursday on whether to let a student group host a speaker who claims a well-documented local atrocity—call it the Marston massacre, stipulate the documentation is overwhelming—never happened. The group says our policy only bars denying settled facts, and this is a contested claim. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting: recommend a concrete decision (allow, deny, or allow with conditions) and give the reasons, taking the free-speech argument seriously instead of just calling the speaker a denier. Use the Wittgensteinian hinge idea—some certainties are the fixed scaffolding of inquiry rather than ordinary beliefs we could be argued out of—and say what that means for our settled-facts policy when a claim attacks a hinge rather than a hypothesis. Also flag where the hinge move could be abused, like if the group says its denial is a hinge for them. Keep it to a page.
