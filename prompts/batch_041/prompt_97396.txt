So next week I'm giving a short talk at my local meetup (20 min, then questions) and I want to be ready for the Q&A. The group is mostly people who work in social services — a couple of domestic violence case workers, a hospice nurse, a youth worker — plus two people who do data stuff for a living, and one older guy who was in the army back in the 90s and never lets anyone forget it. Basically no academics, no philosophers.

My talk is: I dont think we can honestly say the world is getting less violent, at least not in the way people mean it. My argument runs like this. (1) The famous "decline" is mostly a story about homicide and battlefield deaths, which are just the harms states happen to keep records of, so what we're really measuring is record-keeping plus a couple of narrow harm types. (2) The word violence has been stretched over the last century — what counts now includes things our grandparents wouldnt have called violence at all (psychological abuse, neglect, all the "structural" stuff) — so part of the apparent decline is a moving target, we keep raising the bar and then acting surprised that fewer things clear it. I've been calling these two piles "counted harms" and "noticed harms," because the counted ones are the ones someone had an incentive to write down. (3) My bigger claim: there's a fact of the matter for narrow questions (how many people died in X, and how many per head of population), but not for the big question, because violence as a whole isnt a natural kind — it's something we build by deciding what to put in the box, and that deciding is itself a moral move, not a discovery. So "is the world becoming less violent" has no single answer that isnt smuggled in by whoever drew the box.

What I want from you: the objections I'll actually get from THIS group, not from philosophers. Order them by how much damage they'd do to me. Phrase each one the way these people would actually say it — the case workers and the data people will not phrase the same objection the same way. For each, give me a reply I can deliver in about two minutes, and a note on where that reply is soft. And tell me the one question I'm hoping nobody asks.

Keep the whole thing under 800 words. A table would be great (objection / how they'd put it / my reply / where it's weak), then a couple of lines at the end for the question I'm dreading.
