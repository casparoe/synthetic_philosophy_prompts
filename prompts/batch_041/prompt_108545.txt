I've been going down a rabbit hole on Schrodinger — not the cat, the man — and how far he went with the Upanishads, the idea that there's really only one consciousness and that me feeling like a separate person from you is some kind of illusion. Everytime I read anyone explaining it, they reach for the same picture: one sun reflected in a lot of different pots of water, or one ocean with a lot of waves on it. One reality, many apparent copies. Someone used that on me the other day to argue that if the separation is only apparent, then living for your own little wave is a mistake, and that the whole idea of you and me being two separate things is just a failure of perspective.

How far does the picture actually hold? I want the mapping first (what plays the sun, what plays the water, what plays the pot), then the places it breaks down, then what it quietly smuggles in that the real view doesn't have, then whether the conclusion I was sold survives all that, and finally what a better picture would be if there is one. Numbered list, exactly five points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no conclusion, and British English please.
