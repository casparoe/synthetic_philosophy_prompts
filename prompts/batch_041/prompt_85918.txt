I'm 71, spent most of my working life as a geochemist, and since I retired I've been reading philosophy badly and out of order, mostly on trains. There's one puzzle I keep coming back to and I'd like you to turn it into a short story for me.

The puzzle goes like this. A man is stranded on a road out in the desert. Someone pulls up and offers him a lift into town on one condition — that when they get there, he pays him a hundred dollars. The man has no money on him but he says yes. Only, by the time they've actually arrived, with the car parked and the driver unable to do anything about it, he has no reason at all to pay. Paying would just be him being a sucker. And the driver can see all this coming, so a driver who's thinking clearly wouldn't offer the lift in the first place. Which means the man only gets his ride if there's something about him *right now* that makes the payment later something he'll actually go through with. And the twist that gets me is that "deciding now to pay later" isn't really a thing you can do, because the deciding happens later, when later comes. I like it because it isn't about deserts or money at all, it's about the fact that we're allowed to care about what we'll do and not just about what we want this second, and about how odd it is to try to make yourself into a certain kind of person on purpose.

Anyways, the standard illustrations of this bore me — the guy turns out to have a conscience, or the driver takes a chance on him, or he pays and feels warm inside. I want something else. Here's what I've been imagining instead: a small town where a cheap, boring, completely reliable little device exists that lets you lock yourself into some future act. Not a contract, nothing legal, just a physical thing that will do what you set it to, and it's public — everyone can see what everyone else has locked themselves into. It's so ordinary that nobody in the town finds it interesting. 

So what happens to that town? I'd like the story to get at:

- the people who use the thing constantly, and what they slowly turn into
- the one person who won't use it, and what that costs them
- whether the lock-ins turn into a kind of currency, and who ends up rich
- and the part I really care about: whether a town full of people who have all locked themselves into never backing down is a nicer place to live or a worse one. My hunch is the logic pushes everyone toward becoming a bit of a fanatic, since the person who can't be moved is the person who wins, and I'd like a story that either confirms that or shows me why I've got it wrong.

Ground rules: no philosophers, no jargon, nobody stops to explain the puzzle, and no desert and no hitchhiking — I want the shape of the thing without the props. Somewhere around 1500 to 2500 words. And I want one scene where a character could break one of these lock-ins at absolutely no cost to themselves and doesn't, and I want it to stay genuinely unclear — to them and to me — whether that was admirable or just a thing that happened. Don't tidy it up at the end. 

Also, don't make the device magic. It can't read minds or see the future. It just does what it's set to do, and people set it badly.
