Ok, I have to write up a short position statement for my philosophy of mind seminar this week and I want a sanity check first, because I have a nagging feeling my commitments dont actually fit together and I cant figure out where the rub is.

Here's where I've landed:

(i) Epiphenomenalism about experience. My being in pain is caused by what's going on in my brain, but the pain itself doesn't cause anything downstream. It doesn't make me wince, doesn't make me reach for the ibuprofen, doesn't do any work in the world. Experience is a shadow the physical processes cast, not a player in the game.

(ii) A causal condition on knowledge. For a belief to be knowledge of some fact, that fact has to be doing real work in producing the belief — there has to be a genuine causal/informational line running from the fact to the belief. A true belief that's simply disconnected from the fact it's about is a lucky guess, not knowledge.

(iii) Introspection really tells me something, and what it tells me about is the experience itself. When I look inward I get the pain, the orange, the ache. I don't get a readout of my neurons. So my introspective knowledge is knowledge of the experience, full stop.

(iv) Experience is a product of natural selection. The capacity for experience — the experiences themselves, not just the wiring underneath them — is here because it did something for my ancestors. If it did literally nothing, the filter would have dropped it.

(v) Physicalism. There's nothing in the world over and above the physical.

The thing that keeps bugging me: if pain doesn't cause anything, how do I ever end up yelping? That seems flatly impossible to me, so something above has to be broken, but I honestly cant tell which piece.
