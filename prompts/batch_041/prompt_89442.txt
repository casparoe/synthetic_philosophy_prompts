Write a satirical internal engineering design doc — an RFC — proposing that my platform team adopt the MIU string-rewriting system as the company's official problem-solving methodology, with shipping the string MU as the flagship Q3 deliverable. Use the system's actual rules: the sole axiom is MI; any string ending in I may have a U appended; any string Mx may be doubled to Mxx; any III may be replaced by a single U; any UU may be deleted. MU cannot be derived from MI by these rules, and the standard proof is an invariant — no derivable string contains a number of I's divisible by three, and MU contains none. The doc must treat that fact not as a refutation but as a delivery risk to be mitigated.

Form and length: numbered RFC sections (Summary, Motivation, Design, Alternatives Considered, Drawbacks, Rollout, Success Metrics), 600–800 words, in the voice of an earnest staff engineer who believes every word. The comedy should come from the system being real: every absurd commitment in the doc should be something the rules actually license or actually forbid, so that a reader who has worked through the MU-puzzle laughs because the document is accurate rather than because it is zany. The target is the habit — managerial and philosophical alike — of answering a formal limit with "we'll just step outside the system."

The centerpiece is a section on "jumping out of the system" as the mitigation: the plan is to escape the system's limits by stepping outside it. I want the doc to keep not noticing that the way it steps outside — proving a theorem about the system, then adopting that theorem as a new rule — is the same rule-following one level up, in a slightly larger system. Include one dissenting footnote from a skeptical staff engineer who raises precisely that objection and is overruled in the next revision. Land the point, without stating it, that there is no final outside to jump to, and that "think outside the box" is a request to build a bigger box.
