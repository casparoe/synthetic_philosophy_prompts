I sit on the board of a small foundation that makes grants on reducing risks from advanced AI. A staff member has put forward a proposal for a formal non-compliance policy: the foundation would publicly and irrevocably commit that if any party — including a hypothetical future AI system — tries to influence our grantmaking by threatening us with punishment, we will redirect a fixed portion of our endowment to whatever cause that party most opposes. Her reasoning is that such a threat has no causal channel to us and draws all its force from the threatener's prediction that we will cave, so a commitment we would never otherwise want to honor is precisely what makes caving pointless, and announcing it in advance is supposed to neutralize the threat. Several trustees object that this is either empty posturing — no future superintelligence will care what a small foundation pledged decades earlier — or a way to let any bluffer dictate where our money goes. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's vote: recommend a concrete course of action (adopt, reject, or adopt with stated conditions), give a time frame and the conditions under which we would revisit the policy, and give the trustees' objection its strongest form rather than brushing it aside. Assume some readers have never encountered this kind of argument, so define your terms as you go.
