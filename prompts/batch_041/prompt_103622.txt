Im editing a small public-history zine and the back page needs a satirical bit. Write a citizen's FAQ, deadpan municipal-leaflet style, numbered Q&A, for a fictional city office -- call it the Office of Memory-History Reconciliation -- that exists to rule on cases where the official record and what the community remembers disagree, and to say which one has to give way. Give it an appeal process, two or three invented criteria with bland bureaucratic names, one precedent where the record won and one where the memory won, and a question about who counts as a 'constituent' of a fact (the office's rule can be that a document only has standing if someone still holds it). Include a case where the community remembers something that plainly didn't happen and a case where it remembers the same event differently, and have the FAQ run both through the same procedure, as if the office can't tell the difference. The joke should be that the two precedents are indistinguishable under the office's own tests and nobody inside notices -- a community archivist and a professional historian should each recognize a real problem of their own in it, so keep it from becoming a smug defence of the archive or a sneer at ordinary people. Around 650 words, stay deadpan, and don't let anyone announce the point out loud; a footnote from the office's own archivist, the only one who seems bothered, is fine.
