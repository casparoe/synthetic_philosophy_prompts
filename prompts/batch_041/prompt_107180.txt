I want a teaching case for a graduate seminar in modal metaphysics, and I want it engineered rather than merely illustrated. Please construct a thought experiment that separates transworld identity from counterpart theory, and then demonstrate that it does so.

The design brief. The case should turn on a single feature: the arity of the relation that links an individual to its other-worldly representatives. Transworld identity, because it is identity, lets an individual occupy at most one position in any given world; counterpart theory, because it rests on a resemblance relation that need not be one-to-one, lets a single individual have two or more counterparts within the same world. Build a case in which that difference is forced. Do not let the case trade on the familiar complaint that counterpart theory changes the subject when it reinterprets de re modality; I want the divergence to come out of the one-to-many possibility itself. Keep the modal claim at issue mundane, a claim about an ordinary person's possible life, career, or relationships, rather than a baroque stipulation, and keep the setting realistic: no branching universes, no twin-Earth-style duplication, no exotic physics. The case should run no longer than a few paragraphs, and it must generate the two theories' verdicts from their own machinery rather than stipulating them. If the verdicts fall out only because I told you what they should be, the case has failed.

The demonstration. After the scenario, do the following, in order. First, state precisely the de re modal claim the case is about, and give the verdict of transworld identity and the verdict of counterpart theory, showing the step in each theory that produces it. Second, identify the one feature of the case that does the work, and explain why removing it, say by making the resemblance relation one-to-one, or by confining the case to a single world, would let the two theories converge again. Third, say how a defender of counterpart theory would try to disarm the case, and whether that reply succeeds on counterpart theory's own terms. Fourth, say how a defender of transworld identity would try to disarm it, and whether that reply succeeds. Finally, note honestly which of the two theories the case makes look worse, and whether that is an artifact of how you built the case or a real cost of the theory.

Write the whole thing in American English, and keep the tone precise and unadorned; I intend to hand it to students who already know both theories and are ready to see where they grind against each other.
