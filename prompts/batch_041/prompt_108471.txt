I have kept a written record of my choices across a series of one-shot gambles, and I want to know whether a single principle generates them or whether I am simply inconsistent. In each case assume that I choose once, for real money, that I cannot hedge, resell, or renegotiate the gamble, that my other assets and obligations are fixed, and that I care about nothing but the money. For each pair I mark the option I actually take.

1. $2,400 for certain, or an 80% chance of $3,500 and a 20% chance of nothing. — I take the $2,400.
2. A 40% chance of $3,500 and a 60% chance of nothing, or a 50% chance of $2,400 and a 50% chance of nothing. — I take the 40% chance at $3,500.
3. A 1% chance of $5,000 and a 99% chance of nothing, or $55 for certain. — I take the 1% chance.
4. A 1% chance of losing $5,000 and a 99% chance of losing nothing, or a certain loss of $60. — I take the certain loss.
5. $400 for certain, or a 50% chance of $1,000 and a 50% chance of nothing. — I take the $400.
6. A 50% chance of $1,000,000 and a 50% chance of nothing, or $400,000 for certain. — I take the 50% chance.
7. $1,500 for certain, or a 50% chance of $4,000 and a 50% chance of nothing. — I cannot decide; I reverse myself from one week to the next.

There is one more choice that feels different in kind from the others. 8. A one-in-ten-thousand chance of losing my house and a 9,999-in-ten-thousand chance of losing nothing, or a certain loss of $900. — I take the certain loss, and I would take it against any prize whatsoever attached to the gamble: I will not accept even the smallest nonzero chance of losing the house, however large the upside.

What I want from you is a principle — a rule for ranking gambles — that reproduces these verdicts if one exists, and if none does, the closest principle together with a clear statement of which verdicts it cannot reproduce. State the principle precisely enough that I could apply it to a new pair myself; identify which clause of it is doing the work for each of my verdicts; and say what it predicts for case 7 and for at least one further pair of your own devising that I have not considered. I am also asking you to be candid about the state of the question: mark clearly which parts of your answer are settled and which are genuinely contested among people who study decisions under risk, and do not present a contested position as though it were the consensus.
