I've somehow ended up on the free-expression review panel of an international philosophy society — I'm a philosopher of language by training and I never expected to be adjudicating anything, but here we are — and we have a live case that has to be resolved before the annual meeting. A member's plenary paper has been accepted. The abstract argues that certain religious practices deserve much harsher criticism than academic etiquette currently permits, and it includes a passage that our US members read as protected political speech and several of our European members read as degrading speech against a religious minority. The speaker is a friend of mine, which is making this even more awkward. The panel must decide whether to let the session go ahead as planned, move it to a smaller room with a content note, or pull it entirely. Our current policy is a mess: it commits us to free inquiry and to an inclusive environment, and it has a clause inherited from a merger with a European sister society requiring us to consider whether the speech would breach a hypothetical European hate-speech statute. Nobody on the comittee knows how to operationalise that clause. One member wants a dignity-impact threshold modelled on the European idea of degrading treatment; another wants a marketplace-of-ideas presumption borrowed from the US tradition. I was talking to a friend who teaches in Berlin and another who teaches in Chicago, and they seemed to be living in different moral universes. I definately need to circulate a written opinion before Thursday's meeting.

Please draft that memo. It should recommend a concrete course of action, with a timeline and any conditions, and give the reasoning the panel could actually argue from. I want a clear recommendation up front, not a survey. Take the First Amendment-style position seriously — don't strawman it as an American eccentricity — and take the European dignity-based position seriously too, including the thought that some speech acts are harms rather than mere expressions. But don't just restate the two traditions; say how a body like ours, which is neither a state nor a platform, should handle this case. If there's a principled way to distinguish this from a straightforward slur, say so; if there isn't, say that. I'd also like the memo to be honest about what is actually settled in this area and what is genuinely contested among people who work on hate speech law, free expression and political philosophy — don't present one side's framework as the consensus. Write in British English, please. And I'm not asking for a reading list or citations; we need something the panel can act on.
