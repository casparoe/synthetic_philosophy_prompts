I teach a graduate seminar in biomedical ethics, and I am building a case dossier for the unit on the therapy/enhancement distinction. My students can recite the usual positions without effort: that medicine aims at restoring or maintaining species-typical functioning, that the distinction collapses once one notices that health comes in degrees and that much enhancement is prevention by other means, and that what really matters is welfare or positional fairness rather than the line itself. I want to make them run one of those positions through a case in which the distinction is wielded by a regulator with actual power over a person's body, rather than merely discussed in the abstract. A colleague and I have been arguing about the right case for two terms now, and I have settled on the eligibility regime for athletes with differences of sex development in women's athletics. The salient facts should be treated as given: an athlete born and raised female, with a difference of sex development and naturally occurring testosterone in the male-typical range, is required by the sport's governing body to bring her testosterone below a fixed threshold, in practice by taking medication she does not otherwise need, as a condition of competing in certain women's events; if she declines the medication she is excluded from those events; and she has contested the requirement at the Court of Arbitration for Sport and, subsequently, before Swiss and European human-rights bodies. An earlier and broader version of these rules was suspended by the same tribunal for want of evidence that the hormone confers the advantage claimed for it, and the regime that replaced it has since been tightened. I am not interested in the prior question of how the eligibility category itself should be drawn, nor in whether sex-segregated sport is defensible at all; take both as fixed. What I want is the therapy/enhancement distinction put to work.

Please write me a case analysis of roughly a thousand words, organized around three tasks. First, the mapping: in the vocabulary of the distinction, what exactly is being done to this athlete — restoration of normal function, prevention of a harm, suppression of a natural advantage, or something for which the distinction has no name — and which of the available accounts of the line (species-typical functioning, the welfarist account, the appeal to positional fairness) best describes what the regulator is doing, as distinct from what it says it is doing? Second, the stress test. I have been trying a diagnostic on my students that I call the restoration counterfactual: for any intervention, ask which baseline it moves the subject toward — the statistical norm for the human organism, the subject's own untampered-with constitution, or the normal range for the reference class that the competition itself defines. Apply it here, and say whether the three baselines give the same answer; if they do not, identify which baseline the rules are in fact tracking and whether that choice can be defended. Then add a second test of my own devising: had the same hormone level been produced by an exogenous route rather than by her own physiology, the rules would prohibit it outright, so say what that asymmetry reveals about whether the distinction is tracking function at all rather than the provenance of a trait.

Third, the verdict: does the case confirm the distinction, refute it, or show that it was always doing less work than its defenders claim, with the real normative labour done by a separate idea about parity of competition? Where the distinction is simply silent, say so rather than forcing it to speak, and if the case shows that the therapy side of the line can be invoked to license a bodily intervention that serves no therapeutic aim for the person on whom it is imposed, say what that does to whichever account you found most defensible in the first part. A brief comparison with the older dispute over a bilateral amputee sprinter's running prostheses is welcome if it sharpens the analysis, but it should not take over. No citations or reading lists, please; I want your own reasoning about the case.
