I'm giving a talk next month at a small interdisciplinary workshop on probabilistic inference in cosmology, and I want a Q&A prep sheet rather than a defense. The audience is mostly astronomers and statisticians who work on observational selection effects — detection functions, survey incompleteness, the ways a sample is shaped by what could have been observed — plus two philosophers of physics, one of whom is a longtime defender of the self-sampling assumption and will know the literature better than I do. Several of them are impatient with anthropic reasoning generally, because the main place they have seen it used is in fine-tuning arguments for design, and they associate it with people who want to settle empirical questions from the armchair.

My thesis: the self-indication assumption is the correct principle for self-locating belief, and the "presumptuous philosopher" result is not a reductio of it but a consequence we should accept. The principle, as I'll state it: an observer should reason as if randomly selected from the set of all observers that exist, weighted by the probability of their existing, so that a hypothesis implying more observers is thereby more likely. The rival, self-sampling, says you are randomly selected only from the observers in your own world, so the mere fact that you exist tells you nothing about how many observers there are elsewhere. The case that gets me in trouble: two hypotheses with equal prior, one in which the universe contains a few billion observers and one in which it contains a trillion times as many. Self-indication says I should be overwhelmingly confident in the second, on the strength of an argument that uses no telescope at all. I intend to say that is simply the right answer, and that the intuition against it is a prejudice trained on finite sampling frames.

Give me the objections this audience will actually raise, in their vocabulary, ordered by how likely and how damaging each is, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is softest, plus what I should concede up front and the one question I am hoping nobody asks. Write in American English.
