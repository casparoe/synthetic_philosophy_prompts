A small foundation has three philosopher-years to spend on Dennett's real-patterns program: the claim that a pattern is real when it compresses the data better than its rivals, and that the physical, design, and intentional stances each pick out such patterns at a different level. Please set a prioritized agenda for those three person-years — which open questions most deserve the effort, in what order, and on what criteria. I care more about the dependency structure than the list: for each question, say which other questions are upstream of it, how far it can be pushed by conceptual argument alone rather than by empirical work, and what result or objection in the next few years should move it up or down. Then give me a verdict on one proposal that has been floated: that stance-relative realism collapses into a structural realism, so the live question is not whether beliefs and other intentional patterns exist but which patterns are genuinely projectible — whether there is a stance-independent criterion of pattern-hood at all. Should that proposal sit at the top of the agenda, or is it a dead end that would absorb the three years for nothing, and what would have to be true either way?
