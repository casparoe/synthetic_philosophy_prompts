I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about al-Ghazālī's occasionalism, and I came away persuaded that the standard framing of the debate conceals the genuinely open question. I'm now assembling a short research proposal — a term-paper plan, roughly six thousand words over one semester — and I'd like you to write it for me. The thought I want to develop is this: readers routinely take the anti-necessitarian arguments associated with al-Ghazālī (the ones denying that any necessary connection holds between an alleged cause and its alleged effect, so that the fire's contact with the cotton does not by itself produce the burning) to establish occasionalism, the thesis that God alone is the true agent and that creatures are mere occasions. I suspect the argument, on its own, underdetermines the metaphysics. It looks equally hospitable to a position I have been calling nomic occasionalism, on which God does not intervene in each event but instead sustains a world containing no intrinsic causal glue, so that the regularities are brute divine decrees rather than per-event actions, and to a still weaker position on which created things possess genuine powers that are merely contingent rather than necessary. Whether the received arguments reach the strong per-event thesis, or only the weaker denial of necessary connection, strikes me as unsettled — and as a question a semester's worth of careful conceptual work could actually move.

So please frame the proposal around a precise statement of that question: does the anti-necessitarian argument entail occasionalism, or is it compatible with concurrentism (God and creatures both genuinely causing) and with the weaker views just described? The proposal should explain why the existing positions do not settle it, lay out the approach I would take and why that approach rather than the obvious alternatives — reconstructing the argument in its strongest form, isolating exactly what notion of power and necessity it presupposes, and testing whether it overgenerates by proving too much or undergenerates by failing to reach occasionalism — give a realistic month-by-month plan, and state what would count as the project's succeeding and what would count as its failing. Assume I will add the citations myself, so don't supply a bibliography or a literature review by reference.

If the distinction I'm drawing collapses on inspection, or if the question turns out to be merely verbal, say so plainly rather than building a proposal around it. And please begin with the proposal itself, with no preface or commentary.
