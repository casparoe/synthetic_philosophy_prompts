I am trying to pin down a pattern in psychiatric classification and should like to know whether it has an established name. A category is introduced with a provisional checklist of features, hedged as a research diagnosis. Over the following decade, patients who suspect they have it begin to present in precisely the way the checklist describes; clinicians, now expecting that presentation, elicit it and record it; the next edition of the manual promotes the checklist to formal criteria; and the resulting inter-rater reliability is then cited as evidence that the category is valid. The category has not so much been discovered as taught to its own instances. I know this lies in the neighbourhood of Ian Hacking's looping effects, and also of iatrogenic illness, diagnostic drift and self-fulfilling prophecy, but each of those labels seems either too broad or too narrow: looping effects cover the whole feedback relation, whereas what I am after is the specific step in which a manual codifies the uptake of a category as though it were the category's underlying nature, and then mistakes the resulting reliability for validity. Does this narrower phenomenon have a name? If not, I should like a term and a definition that says exactly what it is, what it is not, and how it differs from ordinary construct validation, from concept creep, and from the case in which patients merely exaggerate symptoms they already have.
