I'm giving the toast at my sister's wedding on Saturday and I've been putting it off for a month because every draft I write says something that half the room is going to hear as a lie.

His family is Korean, mine is from Ohio, and the two sides don't agree about what's happening up there. His grandmother explained her view to me once, through her daughter, so I know I'm not just guessing at a vibe: on her picture, a person isn't an individual who happens to have relatives. You're made out of the relations. Take those away and there's no leftover you underneath that was there all along. So for her this wedding isn't two people who picked each other, it's a new joint in something that was already standing and will outlast them, with duties running in both directions. My dad's line, and he says it about other things, not about them, is that a love you can't say no to isn't love, it's a debt, and that someone who can't say no to family isn't good, they're just not there. Alot of the room is going to hear that in whatever I say.

I don't want to stand up there and split the difference with vague stuff about two families becoming one. I want the toast to actually say what a marriage is, in a way his grandmother can nod along to and my dad can't call a cop-out. I think there's something true that does that, but I keep losing hold of it when I try to write it down.

Write the toast for me. Hard cap: 500 words. It gets spoken out loud, so no jargon, no quotations, no citing philosophers at people. It has to land for the relatives who flew in from Seoul and for the ones who drove down from Ohio, and it can't end on both sides are right in their own way — say what those two are actually doing on Saturday.
