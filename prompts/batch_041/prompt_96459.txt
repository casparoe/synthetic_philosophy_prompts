I retired a few years back after 35 years of building particle detectors, and I've been teaching myself philosophy of science since — mostly probability and confirmation, since that's where my math is still half-decent. I've hit a snag and I'd rather have a proper map of the positions than read another intro chapter.

Here's the snag. I'm a card-carrying Bayesian about updating: new certain evidence E comes in, you condition, P(H | E). Fine. But in my old line of work the evidence was never certain — a detector would give me a readout that shifted my credence over, say, {signal, background} to 0.7 / 0.3, not a clean fact. So I went looking and found the rule where instead of conditioning on an event you shift your credences over a partition and re-weight the cells — Richard Jeffrey's thing. It looked right until I tried a toy case: I recieve two independent noisy reports in a row, and updating on them in one order lands me somewhere different than updating in the other order. That can't be right — the order I happen to read my instruments in shouldn't change what I ought to believe. So now I don't know whether I've misread the rule, or the rule really has that hole, or the whole notion of uncertain evidence is where the trouble starts.

What I want is a decision tree / taxonomy I can pin above my desk. Start from someone who already accepts ordinary conditionalization on certain evidence and walk them, node by node, to a named position on what to do when the evidence itself is uncertain. Every branch point should be a question I could answer without already knowing which label it leads to — something like: do you think there's any such thing as evidence you're less than certain of, or does every real learning event reduce to becoming certain of some fact about your own mental state? If the shift is over a partition, must your credences conditional on each cell stay put while only the cells' weights change? Does the order-dependence bother you, and if so do you patch the rule or throw it out? Keep going until every leaf is either a named position or an honest nobody-really-holds-this.

A few things I'd like you to do:

- Don't just list names. Build the space first out of the yes/no questions, then drop the actual positions into it. If some combination of answers is empty, say so and tell me whether it's empty because it's incoherent, because it's merely unattractive, or because nobody's bothered to occupy it.
- Make sure these turn up somewhere as leaves: the plain view that all evidence is really certain, so you just condition on the fact that you saw the readout; the partition re-weighting rule; the move of making the uncertain report itself the certain thing you condition on; and a leaf for people who think the question is malformed from the start.
- If two leaves come out the same on every question I can actually answer, flag them as ones the tree can't seperate — don't fake a distinction to make the picture tidier.
- The order-dependence case above should be one of the branch nodes, not an afterthought tacked on at the end.
- Keep it to something I can actually draw on one sheet of paper — say eight or so questions, and however many leaves that gives you.
