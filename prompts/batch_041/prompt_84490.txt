My uncle owns three hardware stores and he's about to put my cousin on the business bank account. The kid is 22, has worked the floor since he was 15, and my uncle tells anyone who'll listen that he's honest to the bone — seven years, never a missing dollar. My aunt rolls her eyes at this: he's never had the till key, never had a reason to want one, so what exactly has been tested? My uncle's plan to settle it is to leave $600 in an unlocked drawer with a nanny cam pointed at it and see what happens. I think thats a terrible idea but I cant quite say why — it feels like whatever he does, we won't learn the thing we wanted to learn, and if he works out it was a setup, that's its own kind of damage. (The one real moment was two years ago, when a customer left her wallet with $200 on the counter and he chased her down the block — but the whole store was watching, including his dad.) And honestly, the kid is a bit scared of my uncle. So is my aunt right that we know nothing about him? What would even count as finding out whether someone is honest?
