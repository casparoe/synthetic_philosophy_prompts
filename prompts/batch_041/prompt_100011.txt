A freind and I have been going around in circles about the surprise exam paradox for weeks now and I think I've finally landed on a take I like, so I want you to tell me where it breaks. Setup in case it helps: the teacher says there'll be an exam some day next week and that it'll be a surprise — meaning you won't know the night before that it's coming the next day. The students reason backwards: if we get to Thursday night with no exam we'd know it's Friday, so it can't be Friday; the same move rules out Thursday, and so on back to Monday; so there'll be no exam at all. Then the exam lands on Wednesday and they're all caught out.

My claim is that the induction doesn't break at the end, it breaks at the very first step, and it breaks because the students are treating the announcement as something they can *know*. Here's the argument as I have it: suppose the students are certain of the announcement. The announcement entails that the exam is somewhere in Mon–Fri, so they're certain of that too. Now suppose no exam has happened by Thursday night — they're certain it's Friday, so it isn't a surprise, so the announcement is false. So whenever they're certain of the announcement it's false; it can be true, but never known. That's enough to kill the induction, because the Friday step only goes through if they *know* the exam is somewhere in the window, and the announcement is their only route to that. Two things I think fall out of this. First, the finiteness of the window is doing all the work: if the teacher said the exam will come at some point before you graduate, nothing can be ruled out and the whole thing evaporates, which is what my account predicts. Second, here's a case I want you to test the account against: the teacher says there's a decent chance the exam gets cancelled, and otherwise it'll be a surprise exam on one of Mon–Wed. My story says the announcement can now be certainly true, because on Tuesday night (assuming no exam yet) they aren't forced onto Wednesday — cancellation is still live. I also had a false start where I thought that if the students had to put money on a day, the teacher could always pick another one and surprise them for sure, but I think the induction just comes back, since by Thursday night they'd know it was Friday.
