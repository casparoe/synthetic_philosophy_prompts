For most of the past year I've been reporting on a dispute among consequentialists that never quite resolves, and I've finally persuaded the two most articulate partisans to sit down together for a structured adversarial collaboration. One camp, which I'll call the aggregationists, holds that the utility monster is not a reductio but a genuine and uncomfortable implication of any morality that sums welfare across subjects, and that the honest response is to accept the verdict and revise our intuitions rather than the theory. The other camp, the criterion-revisers, treats the very same scenario as proof that a bare sum of welfare is the wrong thing to maximize — that any principle licensing the monster has refuted itself by its own lights. They have been talking past each other for years, and what I want is the document they could both put their names to, not a transcript of the quarrel.

Please draft that joint statement. I want, first, a numbered list of claims both can sign — about how the scenario is set up, what it does and does not stipulate, what each side is and is not thereby committed to, and what would count as changing the subject rather than answering it. Second, the precise residual disagreement, stated twice, once in wording the aggregationists would accept as their own and once in wording the criterion-revisers would accept as theirs. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side, with a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on each crux and each side's prediction for it written down in advance. Where you think a stretch of the dispute is merely verbal, say which stretch and why.

There is a wrinkle I'd like the document to confront head-on, because it is the thing my reporting keeps circling and I've never seen either side address it cleanly. I've been calling it the one-way monster: a being that is constitutionally incapable of suffering, every moment of whose existence is at least as good as our best, with no ceiling on how good its best moments can be. Set that being alongside the ordinary utility monster of the familiar story and tell me what changes. Does a creature that cannot suffer have a welfare level that can be placed on the same scale as ours, and traded against ours, at all? Does the aggregationists' sum even have a common currency here, or does the one-way monster expose a measurement assumption that the standard version smuggles in? I'd like the agreed-claims list and the disagreements table to do real work on this. Finally, close with a candid confidence note: how sure are you that each agreed claim is genuinely neutral rather than a disguised concession by one side, and which single claim in the whole document are you least confident belongs there?
