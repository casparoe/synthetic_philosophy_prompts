I just got a promotion I'd wanted for years, and instead of feeling proud I feel like I cheated. The wierd part is I definately didn't do anything dishonest. Here's the pattern I can't stop noticing: the people who set the promotion criteria are the people who already got promoted, and the criteria just happen to reward the things they were already good at. Then once you meet the criteria, everyone treats your position as deserved. My partner sees it in her field too -- the entrance exam tests exactly what the current students were trained in, and passing it is treated as proof they earned their place. It's like the game is rigged, but not by anyone, and the winning is used as proof the game was fair. Is there a name for this? I know it's near the just-world fallacy and survivorship bias, but those don't capture the circularity -- the criteria being shaped by the winners and then the winners being validated by the criteria. If there's no established term, can you name and define it, and tell me what it's not? I want the carving, not just a label. And seperate what's actually settled from what's contested among people who study meritocracy -- don't just give me the standard critique as if everyone agrees.
