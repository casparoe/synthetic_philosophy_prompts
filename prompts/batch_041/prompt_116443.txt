ok so a few of us have been arguing about Moloch-type stuff — multipolar traps, races to the bottom, that whole cluster — and I've realized we dont actually agree on what a theory of this stuff is even supposed to do, we just keep flinging examples at each other (overfishing, arms races, antibiotic resistance, everyone advertising, the escalation dynamic you get in an auction where the bid keeps climbing). Before we go any further I want the spec first. Write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of multipolar traps and races to the bottom has to deliver. Which cases it has to get right, and for each one why it's a genuine test case and not just a vivid example. Which distinctions it can't collapse — trap vs. ordinary collective action problem, structural trap vs. one that only exists because the agents are mis-modelling each other, a race genuinely to the bottom vs. one that's actually to the top, outcome that's Pareto-bad vs. outcome that's merely bad for the parties involved. And which results it has to stay consistent with: the game theory, plus the old second-best idea from welfare economics, where if you can't satisfy all the optimality conditions at once then satisfying more of them isn't necessarily an improvement — I have a hunch that one cuts against a lot of how people casually talk about these traps.

Then, since I know you can't resist: which of these are actual requirements and which are just prejudices of one camp — the crowd that insists the trapped outcome must be a Nash equilibrium, the crowd that insists it must be Pareto-inefficient, the crowd that wants it to be a stable attractor. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied; honestly that's the part I care about most, if the list itself turns out to be the result. And say which single requirement does the most to kill off the popular accounts. Skip the survey of who said what — I don't need a reading list and I can't look anything up anyway.
