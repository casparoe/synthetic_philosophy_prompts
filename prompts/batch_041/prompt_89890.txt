Two years ago I moved from Stockholm to Oslo for my partner's job, and I've now had the same argument at roughly fifteen dinner parties: which of these two countries is actually the more liberal one. It goes nowhere every time. My Norwegian friends hear "liberal" as Venstre and think I'm asking about party politics; my Swedish friends hear it as the thing Americans call liberal and start listing welfare policies; and one guy insists the whole question is just asking which country has a bigger state, which is a different question. My gut says Norway is more liberal in the old-fashioned sense (they let people do more, they're outside the EU, they're weirdly relaxed about a lot of things) but then someone points out the alcohol monopoly and I lose the thread.

Then my editor asked me to turn this into a semi-regular column — four times a year, comparing a pair of neighbouring countries on exactly this question (Sweden/Norway, then Canada/US, then Austria/Germany, she has a whole list). So now I actually have to do it properly, and I'd rather have a procedure than keep improvising.

What I want is a decision procedure I can run four times a year in about a week of evenings, with no budget, no polling, no commissioning anything, using only public sources I can look up. Write it as steps in order, and for each step say what counts as a sufficient answer, and where I'm allowed to stop and write a verdict.

Things it has to do:

- Keep the two main readings of "liberal" apart instead of quietly averaging them: freedom *from* the state (fewer restrictions, more room to do as you like, constitutional limits, exit) versus the state *securing* individual autonomy and the material conditions for it (so a big welfare state or a school system can count as liberal, not as its opposite). If the two readings give different rankings, I want the procedure to say so out loud rather than pick one for me.
- Give me a fixed list of dimensions to check — speech, religion, association, privacy and surveillance, drugs and alcohol, economic regulation and taxation, immigration and who gets to enter, family and personal law, criminal justice, education, the labour market — with a concrete test for each dimension, not just a name. Something like: for this dimension, what do I look at, and what makes country A score higher than B.
- Handle the hard cases honestly rather than legislating them away. Two I know will come up: a state alcohol monopoly (restricts what I can buy, but it's democratically endorsed and plausibly harm-reducing — is that less liberal, or differently liberal?), and school choice funded by public money, which Sweden has a lot of and Norway much less of. Is that more liberal (parents choose) or less (public money to private providers, equality of provision at risk)? I don't want a fudge on either; I want the procedure to say which reading each one turns on.
- Tell me what to do when the usual indices disagree. Fraser/Cato type economic freedom, V-Dem, Freedom House, World Values Survey — I already know they pull in different directions, so "just cite the index" is useless to me. I want a rule for combining or refusing to combine them.
- Have a stop rule for "too close to call", with some kind of threshold, so I'm not manufacturing a winner out of noise. And a rule for when the honest verdict is "more liberal for some people and less for others" (citizens vs residents vs people trying to get in) rather than a single ranking.
- Mark clearly which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure — especially the weighting, if the ranking turns out to require weights.

Then run it twice on Sweden vs Norway, all the way through, including one where the answer comes out against my gut. And finish with what the procedure can't do, and whether there's a version of the question it would have to declare malformed.

Don't give me a reading list, I'll add references myself, and please don't tell me to commission a survey or find a co-author. I also need the last step to be about presentation, because my editor wants 1200 words and a headline that isn't a lie — so tell me how to state a result like "more liberal on this reading, less on that one, and the rest is a value call" without it reading as a cop-out.
