My town's school board voted to close Lincoln Elementary, then un-voted it when the petitions started coming, and then appointed a "citizens' study panel" so they wouldn't have to decide — and somehow I'm chairing it. First meeting is Thursday in the cafeteria, sixty-odd people, and I have to give the opening remarks, five minutes max. The room is roughly: parents who've been at every meeting for a year and know the enrollment projections cold, retirees and shopkeepers who showed up because somebody told them their taxes might go up, and four or five people from the university who come to everything. I'm a Lincoln parent myself, so they all know where I live, and I'd rather say it than have it whispered.

What I want out of the speech is Dewey — not "Dewey says democracy is nice," but the actual machinery: democracy as a mode of associated living rather than a form of government, inquiry as the conversion of a messy indeterminate situation into a determinate one you can actually test, and conclusions that are warranted for now rather than true forever, so the whole thing carries a revision date. I want this panel to feel like an experiment we're running together, not a jury picking a winner. Which means the speech has to say out loud what would count as evidence, what we do if we turn out wrong, and when we meet again — while not sounding like I'm telling people that how they feel about the school doesn't count, because the moment I say "experiment" the room is going to hear "he's already decided and we're the data," and honestly they'd have a point after what the board pulled.

Then tell me honestly where the speech is leaning on a contested reading of Dewey, and whether the experimental framing is doing work it can't really do. My worry is that closing a school isn't testing a hypothesis, and Dewey's move — that inquiry reconstructs the values too, so there's no separate value question left sitting outside it — might be too quick to save him. If I'm wrong about that, say so, and either way tell me what would change your mind, and dont be polite about it.
