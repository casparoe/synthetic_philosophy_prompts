You are a poet who also knows the philosophy of physics well enough to teach it. Write a short round for four voices — the canon form in which each voice enters in turn and the lines stack — that enacts the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe rather than describing it. The argument, in brief: because simultaneity is frame-relative, any event whatever is simultaneous with somebody's present in some inertial frame; if what is simultaneous with a real present is real, and if being real is transitive, then the chain of frames makes every event real and no moment privileged. Think about what the form can carry before you write: a round has a single fixed score and an unfolding performance, no voice enters first in any privileged sense, and the loop never has to close. Every line must begin with the word Now, so that when all four voices are singing several nows overlap at once and now has become an indexical like here — true wherever it is said, picking out nothing in particular. Each line should be doing work at its own slice rather than restating the one before it, and the whole should be accurate enough that a philosopher of physics would not wince. Let the line that enters last expose the premise doing the most work: the slide from reality-relative-to-a-frame to reality, full stop. Four to six lines; sense matters more than meter. Then, in no more than three sentences, say which premise each line carries and where you take the argument to be weakest.
