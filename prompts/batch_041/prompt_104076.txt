Last month I helped canvass for a local ballot measure, and one of the organizers kept repeating 'every vote counts' — including once when a volunteer raised the puzzle that in any sizeable election the chance of a single ballot deciding the outcome is astronomically small, so that voting looks like an irrational use of an afternoon. Rather than answer, the organizer simply said it again, as if repetition were an argument. I have enough economics in my background to know the shape of the so-called paradox of voting: the expected payoff of voting is the value you place on your preferred outcome times the probability that your vote is pivotal, and in a large electorate that probability is tiny, so the expected payoff seems to fall short of the cost in time and effort; and I have never found the standard replies to it fully satisfying. So I would like you to treat 'every vote counts' as an object of analysis rather than a platitude.

Lay out the distinct precise claims that one sentence could be making, as a menu I can carry back to the next canvass. I expect the range to run from the trivial to the radical: that your ballot is at least recorded and tallied; that the probability of being pivotal, while small, is strictly positive; that this probability is small but not negligible, or not small in the way naive calculation suggests; that the aggregate of many individually inconsequential votes decides the outcome, even though no single one does; that a vote counts expressively or as the performance of a duty, independently of whether it changes anything; that it counts constitutively, as part of what makes the outcome legitimate or the citizen a participant. For each reading, give a one-line verdict — platitude, substantive and defensible, contentious, or simply false — and say explicitly which readings would actually do the work the organizer needed, that is, which ones defuse the paradox of voting rather than quietly changing the subject. Flag any pair of readings that cannot be held together, and tell me which single reading you think the organizer most plausibly meant, and whether that reading leaves the paradox standing. Order the menu from the most trivial claim to the most radical. Keep the whole response to no more than 750 words.
