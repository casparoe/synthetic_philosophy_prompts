Me and a friend have been arguing about quotas for two years — she thinks they're a necessary correction to a rigged starting line, I think they're a licence to discriminate by group — and our seminar has asked us to stop and write the document we should've written ages ago: a joint statement we could both actually put our names to. Here's what I want in it. The claims we both accept about what a quota does and doesn't do, and what evidence would count for or against it. Then each remaining disagreement stated in words the side that holds it would accept as fair, no strawmen. Then the cruxes, the sub-questions where settling things would actually move one of us, and for each crux some case or bit of evidence we'd both agree is relevant, with our predictions written down before we look. I suspect at least one crux is about the right counterfactual baseline (who would have gotten in anyway) and another about whether the point is representation of a group or opportunity for individuals. Do it across the three settings people always mush together: US university admissions, India's reservation system with the caste-based seats and the newer income-based quota layered on top, and Norway's boardroom quota for women — a chunk of the fight, I think, is people slideing between these three as if they raised the same question. And if part of our disagreement turns out to be about words rather than substance, say which part and why. Write it as one continuous piece of prose, the way you'd write a long email to a colleague — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — and if you want to number the agreed points, do it inside the sentences.
