I am setting the final examination for an upper-level philosophy of biology seminar, and the closing essay is meant to test the Gould–Lewontin critique of adaptationism. I want a question that cannot be answered by reciting the paper's thesis, so please build it around a case the students cannot have seen.

Please produce, in this order: (1) a single essay question of no more than 120 words; (2) a model answer of roughly 550 words; (3) a marking scheme.

The question should require students to separate at least three possible readings of the critique — as a claim about testability or method, as an empirical claim about what fraction of the phenotype is adaptive, and as a claim about the explanatory default or priority assigned to selection — and to say which reading survives the standard objection that the critique is either trivially true or self-undermining, and which does not. It should also require them to distinguish a spandrel from a bare developmental or physical constraint and from an exaptation, and to explain why the label 'spandrel' can be description-relative: the same feature may be a necessary byproduct under one decomposition of the trait and a target of selection under another.

Use this invented case as the anchor: in a fictional rodent, the granite vole, adults in populations living on abrasive substrate develop a thickened patch of skin on the crown; the patch thickens with age and correlates with survival. One research group concludes that it is an adaptation for protection against abrasion; a rival group argues that it is a structural byproduct of the skull's growth plate under mechanical stress, with no function of its own. Students must say what evidence would distinguish the two hypotheses, whether the byproduct hypothesis is refuted by the patch's reliable correlation with survival, and whether the spandrels critique changes what counts as a satisfying explanation of the patch.

For the marking scheme, name five must-hit points, assign marks summing to 20, and add one line per point on the specific error that should lose marks — for instance, treating the critique as the claim that adaptationism is false, or conflating a trait's present utility with its historical origin. Keep the entire response under 1000 words.
