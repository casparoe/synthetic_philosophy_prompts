I want to use the Navajo code talkers in WWII as a test case for linguistic relativity, and I want it done honestly in both directions -- theory explaining the case, case testing the theory. Skip the military history. Here's what I'm working with: the Marine Corps built a code on Navajo, mostly a substitution where each letter of the English alphabet was represented by a Navajo word, plus a vocabulary of invented Navajo renderings for military terms (submarine came out as iron fish, that kind of thing); the messages were English underneath the whole time; and the code was never broken in the field. The standard pop gloss is Whorfian -- Navajo is so alien the Japanese couldn't get inside it -- and i think that gloss is doing something shady. Run the episode through relativity properly: what does an incommensurability claim actually predict about a code like this, what does the episode show instead, and (the part I care most about) where does the theory have nothing to say, because the code talkers were not using Navajo the way Whorf's subjects used their languages?

One distinction I keep using, and I want you to use it too: a *learning* barrier versus a *thinking* barrier. A learning barrier is a fact about who happens to have been trained; a thinking barrier is the claim that the other side's concepts can't be reached from yours at all. My read is that the code talkers story is all the first and none of the second, and that this is exactly why it gets misused as evidence for the second. Push back if I'm wrong.

There's also something the theory never anticipated: speakers deliberately and adversarially re-engineering their own lexicon under deadline, to say things their language had no words for. Say what that does to relativity -- friendly case, anomaly, or just outside its scope?

Format it like this, roughly:

- **Setup** -- what the case actually was, three or four sentences, no more.
- **What relativity predicts** -- one short paragraph splitting strong and weak readings.
- **The mapping** -- learning barrier vs thinking barrier applied to the code, its security, and its speakers; the longest part, a few paragraphs.
- **Where the theory goes silent** -- short.
- **Verdict** -- does the case help relativity, hurt it, or is it not really a test? Open with the one-sentence bottom line, then justify it in a paragraph.
