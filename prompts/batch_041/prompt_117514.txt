I'm preparing material on whether 'better than' is transitive, and I need a case built to order. Take two options, A and B, of the same kind, differing along only two or three ordinary dimensions — two flats, two offers in the same line of work, two treatment plans — and close enough that neither is clearly better than the other and that a slight improvement in either does not make it better than the other. Each option then has an improved version, A+ and B+, the improvement in each being plainly real and plainly small. Set the case out in one paragraph, and hold to these specifications: the natural verdict on A and B is that neither is better; the case must not turn on the options being goods of different kinds, on any doubt that the improvements are improvements, or on numbers; and if incomparability is where the case ends up, the argument must earn it rather than the scenario stipulating it.

Then: (a) run the argument, and identify the precise principle in the logic of 'better than' that does the work; (b) lay out the three ways out of the case — insisting that a small improvement does after all make A+ better than B, so that the comparison is determinate even where we cannot tell; denying that A and B are comparable at all; and denying the principle from (a) — and say what each costs and which is least costly; (c) say which feature of the case is doing the work, and what becomes of the case if the improvement is enlarged or the gap between A and B widened; (d) say which way out the case actually supports, and whether the same material could be turned into a spectrum argument for the intransitivity of 'better than'.
