ok so I'm running the session for our dept's interdisciplinary reading group next month and I need help designing it. 12 people, mostly philosophy grad students but there's two economists and one comp-sci guy, 80 minutes total, and the topic is multipolar traps / the whole Moloch thing. I really dont want to do the standard thing where I put up a prisoners dilemma, point at the dominant strategy, and everyone nods because theyve seen it a hundred times — that makes the idea look trivial and it isnt.

What I want is a game where they feel the specific thing that separates a multipolar trap from a boring collective action problem. So a few design constraints, and these are the important part:

The payoffs have to be positional — what you get depends on your standing relative to the others, not on your absolute level. That way there's no effort level that's jointly optimal in the naive sense, and no one can just say "well why dont we all agree to do less."

Then, somewhere around the two-thirds mark, I want them to be allowed to talk, reach an explicit agreement, and actually keep it. I want the trap to survive that. The agreement should be something everyone can sincerely honour and still end up worse off, and I want them to notice that themselves before I say anything. If there's a clean way to also give them a unilateral move that's legal under the agreement but still restarts the escalation (some kind of efficiency or tech upgrade rather than just raising the quantity), use it.

Optional if it fits in the time: one round where I act as a central authority with full enforcement power and impose the best rule I can, and it still doesnt get them out. If that's too much, put it in the debrief instead.

Need: rules, materials, a payoff scheme that works without real money, a minute-by-minute timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments onto the structure (and that explains why this isnt just PD — what repetition, communication and enforcement each fail to fix). Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it, and be honest with me about what the game cant reproduce — I suspect it cant reproduce the fact that nobody chose to be in the game in the first place, and that real versions run across generations with agents that arent unified actors, but tell me if I'm wrong about that and what else is missing.

Dont let them hear the word Moloch or multipolar trap until the debrief. Thanks.
