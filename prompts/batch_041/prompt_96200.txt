I retired last year after 34 years as an entomologist -- insect neurobiology, so I've spend my whole working life around bugs -- and I've finally got time to read the philosophy I never had time for. The thing that's got its hooks in me is which animals are conscious. It's not idle curiosity: I've watched insect farming go from a novelty to an industry, I don't know what I think about it, and I suspect a lot of people happily eating cricket flour dont either.

The problem is I've got maybe ten decent years of part-time work left in me and no lab, and the field looks like five different questions being answered past each other. So what I want from you is an agenda, not a survey. What are the real open questions here, and which two or three would you actually put my years into? I want the reasoning: which questions are upstream of which, which ones someone with my background could actually move, which ones would change what anyone does about a cricket or an octopus, and what would move a question up or down your list.

Two things I keep circling. First, someone told me there's an argument that at least some of the leading theories can't be told apart by any experiment, because for any system that counts as conscious on one of them you can unfold it into a functionally identical system that the same theory says isn't conscious at all. If that's even half right it seems to reorder everything, and I want to know where it belongs in the ranking. Second, is it actually settled that there's a fact of the matter about a bee, or is the honest answer that consciousness is simply indeterminate in the borderline cases? And if it's indeterminate, is that upstream of all the other questions, or is it a lovely puzzle that wouldn't change anyone's behaviour?
