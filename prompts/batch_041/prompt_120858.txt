ok, I need a satirical piece for a project. setup: a mid-size company has decided to run its big restructuing through iterated elimination of dominated strategies -- actual game theory, applied to its divisions, product lines, and headcount. write the internal memo from the Chief Strategy Officer announcing the results. 600-700 words, numbered sections, deadpan the whole way, sounding like a real corporate memo: reassuring tone, a bit of jargon, an FAQ at the end. don't ever break character.

the comedy has to come from the game theory being correct rather than from the memo-writer botching it -- a game theorist should laugh, not wince. so please get these right:

- some divisions get cut for being strictly dominated: worse than some other division no matter how the market goes. the memo is smug about this part, since the survivors come out the same regardless of what order you eliminate in.
- at least one division isn't beaten by any single other division but is beaten by a mixed strategy -- it loses to some weighted blend of two others -- and the memo has to explain, correctly, why that still counts.
- the memo concedes that eliminating weakly dominated strategies depends on the order you do it in, and then treats that as a feature: "we ran the elimination in the order that best aligns with our strategic priorities." math right, sleaze in the application.
- the whole thing is justified on the grounds that the divisions are rational and that everyone knows everyone is rational, which is why HR is rolling out a mandatory Rationality Training, followed by a training about everyone having completed the training, and so on -- which is why the program's end date is listed as TBD.
- one FAQ entry asks why they don't just look for the company's Nash equilibrium instead, and the answer should be genuinely correct about what dominance reasoning does and doesn't need.
- another FAQ entry should admit that an option everybody was actually going to pick got eliminated along the way, and that this is a known feature of the method, not a bug in our implementation.

no footnotes, no citations, no bibliography. invented names and an invented outside skeptic are fine. American English. someone who's never seen a payoff matrix should still be able to follow it.
