I teach a grad seminar on longtermism and global governance, and every year the same thing happens. We do the singleton material — the idea that the world could end up ordered around a single decision-making agency at the top, one agent that gets to make the top-level calls and that no rival can overrule — and within twenty minutes half the room has collapsed it into world government or one cartoon emperor, and the other half has decided it's an engineering problem about keeping the thing stable, as though legitimacy and value lock-in were somebody else's department. I want to hand them something meaner than another reading. So here's the ask: write me a leaked internal onboarding memo, or an excerpt from an employee handbook, from an outfit that exists in a world where this has already happened. Call it something like the Office of the Transition, or the Coordination Authority, whatever you like. Roughly 700 to 800 words, numbered sections or clauses are fine, deadpan corporate-legal register throughout, no winking, no nudging the reader, and absolutely no line where the document admits it's absurd. It has to read like a document that was actually circulated.

The humor has to come from getting the concept right, because that's the whole point of the exercise — a student who knows the literature should laugh and also wince a little at how fair it is. So the memo should be quietly precise about the things people always get wrong: that a singleton is defined by the absence of rivals at the top rather than by being one mind or one government, that it could be a democracy or a firm or a machine or simply the condition of nobody else being left standing, that what's supposed to make it sticky is that once you're the only player you can stop rivals from forming in the first place, that it isn't inherently good or bad and the live fear is lock-in while the live promise is coordination and not blowing ourselves up, and that it can be a mess of competing factions inside and still count as one from the outside. Work those in as bureaucratic furniture — a clause on rival prevention, a clause on internal disputes being an internal matter, maybe a sidebar or an FAQ item that fusses about who consented to all this without ever using the word legitimacy. I'd love the reductio to land somewhere in the fine print: succession planning, or an exit interview, or a termination clause for the whole arrangement, handled as if it were a routine HR matter. It should be funny to someone who's read the literature and perfectly readable to someone who hasn't heard the term before. Don't tell me what the joke is afterward, and don't append a note explaining the satire — just the document.
