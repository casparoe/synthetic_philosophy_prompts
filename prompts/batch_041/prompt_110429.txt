I'm in the sixth form and I've been asked to contribute a short comic piece to the next issue of our school philosophy society's magazine. I'd like to write a joke about the centipede game — the two-player game in which the pot grows every time a player passes it across the table, and in which the player who takes pockets a share slightly larger than the other player's, so that taking always looks better than passing even though passing all the way would leave both players far better off. What I want is a joke whose punchline turns on the actual content of the paradox rather than on the general observation that people fail to behave rationally. In particular, please let it hinge on the way backward induction unravels the whole thing: at the final round the player to move takes, since taking beats passing there; so the player to move at the penultimate round takes; and so on, all the way back to the very first move, where the first player takes at once. It would be even better if the joke also traded on the fact that this unravelling only gets going if it is common knowledge that both players are rational and that the game has a definite, known final round — an indefinite horizon, where there is always one more round to reason about, is exactly the sort of loophole I'd like the joke to exploit. I'd prefer the setting to be something quite ordinary rather than a laboratory or a seminar, and I don't want the punchline to be a pun about actual centipedes. It need only run to a few lines, and I'll write any explanation of the theory myself, so the joke itself needn't teach the reader the game. Could you write it in British English, please?
