I write a column on science and ideas, and I want a thought experiment for a piece that runs against the grain of the standard telling of Goodhart's law. The usual story is that any measure which becomes a target ceases to be a good measure; I want a case in which a metric is deliberately gamed by everyone subject to it and yet remains a faithful proxy for the thing we actually care about — the inverted outcome, reached honestly rather than by stipulation. Constraints: set it in an ordinary institution (a school, a hospital, a firm, a city agency), not a laboratory toy; no cartoonish fraud or sabotage; and the resilience has to fall out of a structural feature of how the measure relates to the goal, not out of the agents being unusually virtuous or well-intentioned. After the scenario, do the analysis: name the target and the metric, show what each plausible gaming move would do to the gap between them and why it fails to widen it, and say which single feature of the case is doing the work. Then tell me what happens if that feature is removed, and how a skeptic who thinks Goodhart's law is inescapable would try to recast my case as an ordinary instance of it after all.
