Me and this other kid in my school's philosophy club have been arguing for like four months about whether someone can have a reason to do something they have zero interest in doing, and we're going in circles. My position: a reason has to hook onto something you already care about. If it doesn't touch any want or goal you've got, it's just a fact about the world, not a reason *for you* — if it couldn't even in principle move you, what would it even mean to say you have it? His position: that's backwards. If his uncle genuinely doesn't care about his kids and never will, that doesn't mean he has no reason to show up for them, it means he's a bad father. Reasons are about what the facts actually support, not about what happens to be rattling around in your head. Otherwise telling someone they should've done otherwise is just saying they didn't feel like it, which makes moral criticism meaningless.

The problem is we just throw the same examples back and forth — the indifferent uncle, someone raised in a cult so they don't care about their own survival, a depressed person who can't get out of bed — and neither of us ever goes "oh ok, you're right." One kid in the club said the whole thing is a fake fight over the word 'reason,' that one of us is talking about what explains why a person acts while the other is talking about what's true or justified, and we're using one word for two things. Another kid said no, it's really about whether you can only have a reason to do stuff you're actually capable of doing. A third said maybe it depends on whether a reason is supposed to show up in the person's own thinking when they decide, or is what you'd tell them from the outside if you were giving advice, and that we're each quietly picking one of those.

I want a diagnosis, not a winner. Is this a real disagreement, a verbal one, or two different questions wearing one name? And whatever it turns out to be, tell me what would actually count as progress — a distinction, a case, an argument, some kind of formal result — or whether it's just hard and we should stop expecting it to get resolved.
