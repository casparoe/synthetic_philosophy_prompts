Im building a set of fiction prompts for a phil of mind unit and need one more. Write me a short story that dramatizes competence without comprehension — the Darwin/Turing move where real design, or real computation, or real smarts comes out of a process that has no idea what its doing, and the "understanding" turns out to be an effect rather than the source. Skip the obvious traps: no computers, no robots, no evolution or bees or neural nets. Set it somewhere small and human — a bakery, a church committee, a family firm, a municipal office — and let the inversion run through people and institutions instead. I want the reader pulled into treating one character as the one who really gets it, then gradually seeing that the skill was humming along fine before anyone understood a thing, and that whoever finally "explains" it is just narrating a competence they don't control either — maybe even confabulating it, harmlessly, while the thing keeps working. Let the competence live in something dumb and repeatable (a jingle, a rota, a set of hand gestures, a rhyme) rather than in anyone's head. Under 1500 words, third person, and don't have anyone name the idea or quote a philosopher at the end — the reversal should just land quietly, with one loose detail that leaves the reader unsure whether the explanation was ever doing any work or is itself just another blind output.
