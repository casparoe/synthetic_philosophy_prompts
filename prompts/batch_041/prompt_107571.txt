I'm assembling a handout for a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of time, and I want the positions ranked against two axes rather than merely described. The axes are these.

First, translation invariance, scored from 0 to 1. A position scores 1 if its fundamental description of reality contains no fact that picks out which events are present, so that relabeling which events count as now changes nothing at the fundamental level. It scores 0 if the fundamental description must single out some privileged set of events, or a moving marker, as present.

Second, phenomenal accounting, also scored from 0 to 1. A position scores 1 if it positively explains, rather than merely tolerates, the felt asymmetry of time: why the present seems experientially special and why the future seems open. It scores 0 if it denies that datum outright or explains it away as illusion without remainder.

Please rate the following six positions on both axes, then assign each an overall tier of S, A, B, or C, with a one-sentence justification for the placement. Treat the two axes as equally weighted unless you argue for a different weighting, in which case say so explicitly.

1. Presentism: only present events exist, and past and future events are not real.
2. Growing block theory: past and present events are real, the future is not, and the boundary of reality advances.
3. Moving spotlight theory: the whole B-series exists, and a primitive property of presentness moves along it.
4. Block universe, or B-theoretic eternalism: all events exist, and now is indexical, like here, with no further fact about which time is present.
5. Fragmentalism: there is no single tensed whole; reality consists of a plurality of tense-perspectives or fragments, each internally coherent but not jointly composable into one view from nowhere, so that no fragment is privileged over the others.
6. A position of my own, which I'd like you to evaluate on its merits rather than by association with anything you already know. Call it scattered presentism. On this view, presentness is real but neither global nor absolute; it is a local, relational feature, such that an event is present relative to the causal-informational frontier of some subsystem. The present is therefore a distributed cloud of local nows rather than a single cosmic slice, and no single slice is metaphysically privileged. The view keeps a primitive notion of the present while denying that the present is any single global hyperplane.

After the ratings, tell me whether any of the six can score high on both axes at once, or whether the two axes stand in genuine tension; if they do, say which axis you would sacrifice and why. Finally, if you could teach only one of these positions in a one-semester course, which would it be, and what would the course lose by excluding the others?
