A friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether a person is wholly present at each moment of her life or whether she is spread out over time the way an object is spread out over space, and the whole fight turned on an analogy he would not let go of. He says a persisting thing is like a road. The road from here to the coast is extended along its length, and no single point along it is the whole road, so why should a person be any different? On his picture my individual moments are like mileposts, the whole of me is like the whole road, and it follows that I was never wholly present at my own wedding, since only a milepost's worth of me showed up. He thinks this shows that the endurantist's insistence that one is wholly present at every moment is just a picture we inherit from grammar, and that the supposed puzzle of personal identity over time dissolves once you stop asking which stage is the real me.

What I want from you is an audit of the analogy, not a verdict delivered by fiat. Lay out what is supposed to map onto what, the road, its points, its length, the traveler, the now, and say which of those correspondences actually hold. Then tell me where the road picture breaks down in ways that matter for the conclusion he wants to draw, and which breakdowns are merely decorative and can be waved away. I am especially suspicious that the analogy quietly imports a picture of time as one more spatial dimension, and that it treats part as meaning the same thing in part of a road and part of a life when that is precisely the disputed question. Having done that, tell me whether his conclusion, that the endurantist is simply in the grip of a picture and that nothing is really at stake, survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the argument the entire way and would collapse without it. If there is a better comparison for persistence, whether it favors his side or mine, I would like to hear it, but only if it earns its keep. Please write this out as continuous prose, the way you would explain it in a long letter to a friend, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and in American English.
