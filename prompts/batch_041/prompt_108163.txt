I'm in a value theory seminar this term and I've been trying to write down what I actually think about betterness before I commit to a paper topic, and every time I do the list starts eating itself. My friend in the decision theory program keeps saying I'm helping myself to the good stuff, so I want a referee before I show anyone. Here's the pile: (1) Better than is transitive, and I definately don't think that's some optional extra you could just drop -- if A beats B and B beats C and C beats A, there's no coherent way to choose and someone can pump money out of you, so transitivity seems to be part of what it even means for something to be a betterness relation. (2) But I can't shake the spectrum-style cases. If a life goes a hair better -- one more mildly good day, the ache turned down a notch -- then it's better, and there's no smallest notch that counts, so I can run the chain: every link is an improvement on the one before, and yet the far end strikes me as clearly worse than where I started. I realy don't think I'm just miscounting. (3) Lately I've been talked into the idea that two options can be on a par: genuinely comparable, it's not that the question has no answer, but neither is better, nor worse, nor exactly equal. The analogy I like is two very different careers that are about as good for you without either one winning. (4) I still think that for any two options there's a fact of the matter about which of those relations holds, and I don't believe in real incomparability where the question just goes unanswered -- that always felt like a cop-out. (5) And I'm a welfarist about all of this: what makes an outcome better is how well it goes for the people in it, nothing else.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each real clash? Order the real ones by what they cost me. For each one, tell me the cheapest thing to give up and what else goes overboard when I give it up, and then say whether there's a single move that cleans up the most mess at once. Don't just tell me to go read somebody -- I want the argument itself, and I don't need a reading list, I need to know what has to move.
