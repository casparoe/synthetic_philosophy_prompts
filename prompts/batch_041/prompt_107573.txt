Domain is the paradox of deontology. You know the setup: deontological constraints say I can't kill one person even to save five, and then it looks like the theory is telling me to let a worse outcome happen, which seems either irrational or arbitrary. The usual replies say constraints are agent-relative, or that they express a different kind of moral consideration than outcomes do. My hunch is that the whole thing is set up wrong, because it holds everyone else's behavior fixed. In real life a constraint isn't a private rule I apply case by case — its more like a public commitment that other people rely on to predict what I'll do. So the question I want stated precisely: does the paradox of deontology survive once constraints are modeled as public, expectation-stabilizing commitments rather than act-by-act prohibitions — and if it doesn't, is that a vindication of deontology or just a sneaky way of smuggling in rule consequentialism?
