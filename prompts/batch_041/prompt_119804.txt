I need to turn a half-formed idea into a term-paper proposal for a political philosophy seminar — one semester, roughly 6000 words, and the seminar leader wants the plan itself, not a lit review. Heres the idea, and I cant get it to sit still. Almost everything written on the ethics of voting — should you vote at all, is voting for a lesser evil complicity or prudence, is a vote better understood as expression than as a contribution to an outcome — quietly assumes a plurality rule: one mark, most marks wins. I think that assumption is doing a lot of hidden work. Under plurality, wasted-vote reasoning and lesser-evil reasoning both look like forced moves. Switch the rule and they change shape: under approval voting you can approve of your favourite and the tolerable one at once, so the lesser-evil dilemma partly dissolves; under instant-runoff the whole idea of a spoiler shifts; under a Condorcet-style rule the strategic picture moves again; and under approval voting you get the opposite temptation — approve only your favourite, because approving the compromise can knock your favourite out (I think people call this bullet voting, the point being you'd be hurting your first choice by being generous). So the question I want to make precise is something like: is there a rule-independent ethics of voting, or is every argument about duty, expression, and the lesser evil really an argument about the aggregation rule in disguise? And if it's the latter, is there any rule at all under which sincere voting is what you're morally required to do, or does strategic pressure reappear under every one of them?

Write me the proposal. State the question so it has a determinate answer, explain why the existing positions don't settle it, lay out the method — what I'd actually do week by week, and why that's the right approach rather than just cataloguing how each rule changes the strategic incentives — then say what a successful result would look like, what would count as the project failing, and whether this is honestly a semester-sized thing or too big. I don't want a reading list or citations, I'll fill those in myself; argue from positions, not from names.
