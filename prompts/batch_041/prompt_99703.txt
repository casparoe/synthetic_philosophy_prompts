Bit of an odd request, but here's the situation. I've got a mate who's really into the idea of karma as a theory of moral desert — not the pop-culture version, the actual claim that what you accrue tracks what you morally deserve, across lives or at least across your standing in the moral ledger. Over the years he's given me verdicts on a bunch of cases about who picks up bad karma, and I've been writing them down:

- A surgeon does everything by the book, genuinely wants to save the patient, but the patient dies of a complication nobody could have foreseen. He says: no bad karma.
- A driver speeds through a school zone and a kid runs out and is killed. He says: bad karma, obviously, even though the driver wasn't trying to hurt anyone.
- Someone walking through a field crushes a beetle they never saw and couldn't have seen. He says: no bad karma.
- Someone pays a hitman to kill a rival. He says: bad karma, full stop, even though the person never laid a hand on anyone.
- A bloke inherits a fortune from his father's business, a business he had nothing to do with running and which he now learns cut corners that got workers killed. He says: hmm, probably no bad karma, the son didn't do anything.

Now the new case. I buy a cheap phone. It's pretty widely reported that phones like this one are made using forced labour somewhere down the supply chain, but I don't look into it, I don't intend anyone any harm, and honestly it's not like I could trace the exact components. I just want the phone. Does he say I accrue bad karma?

What I actually want from you is less the yes/no and more the machinery. Tell me the rule he seems to be running, and then at least two other rules that fit everything he's said just as well — I suspect things like "karma tracks the quality of your will and your negligence" versus "karma tracks whether you knowingly benefit from harm" versus "karma tracks your causal contribution, aware or not" are all still live. Say what each of those predicts about my phone, give me your best guess at his actual verdict and why you'd bet on it, and then name the one further case I should put to him that would best split the rules apart. Where his past verdicts genuinely underdetermine the answer, say so and tell me what he'd be committing to either way.

Oh and write it in British English please, I'm fussy about that.
