My roomate is a philosophy grad student and he's been trying to get me to sign on to his take on the special composition question — you know, the question of when a bunch of things add up to one bigger thing. He's a deflationist: he thinks there's simply no fact of the matter. His go-to pitch is that asking whether these atoms compose a chair is like asking whether a particular stretch of land is France or Germany. The land doesn't know or care where the line falls; we draw it, we sign a treaty, we redraw it later, and not one pebble changes. So "composing a chair," he says, is just us drawing a border around some simples that were getting along fine on their own — composite objects are our carvings, not nature's furniture. When I push back he sometimes swaps in a second line: a heap of sand is a heap only because we call it one, no single grain makes the difference, so the whole thing is our doing too.

I can feel the pull of the border picture but something about it is off and I cant put my finger on it. Can you actually audit it for me? I'd like the correspondence laid out explicitly — what's playing the role of the land, of France and Germany, of the border, of the treaty, of the mapmaker — and then the places the mapping breaks down. More important, tell me which of those break-downs actually matter for the conclusion he's using it to push (no fact of the matter; composites are conventional) versus which are just incidental noise. My suspicion is that it quietly assumes something: France and Germany have to already be there, as things with governments and citizens, before anyone can squabble over their border — and whether the chair is there at all is kind of the whole thing we're supposed to be deciding. But I don't trust my own hunch and I might be missing a better objection or overrating a bad one.

So: does the deflationist conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it the whole time? And does the heap line do any better, or does it trip over the same thing? If there's a cleaner analogy for what he's after, I'd like to hear it, with a note on what it buys and what it gives up.
