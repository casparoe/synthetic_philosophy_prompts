Alright, I've talked myself into a pretty strong version of the competence-without-comprehension line and I'd like you to try to knock it down. The claim: once you've fully fixed what a system does -- its input-output profile, how reliably, over what range of cases -- there's no further fact of the matter about whether it "understands". Comprehension isn't a second ingredient ladled on top of competence; it's just a flattering name for a certain competence plus our reaction to it. Darwin made design-without-a-designer respectable, Turing got the appearance of thought out of clockwork, and the moral I keep drawing is that comprehension was never in the recipe -- we read it back in afterward.

The part I'm least sure of, and the part I actually want stress-tested: I want to say comprehension just is competence that survives novelty -- the same dispositions holding up on inputs the system was never selected for. Call it the "holds-up" reading. If that's right the thesis is empirical and testable, which is nice. But maybe it's doing too much work for me.

So: what would count against the whole thing? Not the lazy "but it's not conscious" move. I mean what would actually have to be true, or get observed, for me to have to drop it. In particular:

1. Is there a case where two systems have exactly the same competence profile but we're forced to say one comprehends and the other doesn't -- and what would make that forced rather than just a preference?
2. Is "comprehension" doing real explanatory work that competence alone can't, say predicting how a system generalizes off-distribution, or does the "holds-up" reading just relabel the same facts under a nicer word?
3. Does the view risk collapsing into a definitional truce where "comprehension" ends up meaning "competence I happen to approve of"? If so, is that fatal, or just the normal price of a deflationary view?
4. Turing's old reply to the objection that a machine only ever does what we programmed it to do -- does that reply actually support me, or does it quietly smuggle comprehension back in through the back door?

Rank whatever you give me from "this really would sink it" down to "awkward but survivable", and tell me which ones a defender like me should just concede up front. I'd rather know now if I'm defending a view that can't loose.
