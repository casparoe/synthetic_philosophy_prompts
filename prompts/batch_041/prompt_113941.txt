ok so I've been trying to pin down what I actually think pleasure is by just going with my gut on a pile of cases, and I want you to tell me what principle (if any) is behind my verdicts, and which ones I have to give up to get a clean principle. Here's where I land.

The relief when a bad toothache finally stops: pleasure, no question. But the mere absence of the ache an hour later, when I've forgotten about it: not a pleasure, just nothing. Scratching a really bad itch: pleasure, even though the itch itself is unpleasant and the scratch is half pain. The cold water when you're dying of thirst: definitely pleasure. Finally getting the trick in a proof I'd been stuck on for a week: pleasure, and not a sensory one as far as I can tell, so that's a problem for the "pleasure is a certain feeling-tone" picture. The mild comfort of a boring routine I don't even notice: I want to say barely a pleasure, like a 1 out of 10. Watching a rival get humiliated: feels good, but calling it a pleasure feels off, more like satisfaction with an ugly flavor. A masochist who genuinely enjoys the sting of a whip: I think that's a real pleasure for him, weirdly.

Now the two that break me. (a) Suppose you zap my reward circuitry so I feel something exactly like the water-pleasure but I don't want it, wouldn't seek it, and afterward say it wasn't enjoyable, I just felt a warm pulse. I want to say no pleasure there, but it's phenomenally identical to one. (b) Invert it: I keep going back to some activity, want it, am glad I did it, but at the time there's no felt quality at all, just momentum. I want to say that's a pleasure too, which now contradicts (a).

Here's the principle I half believe, tell me if it survives: a pleasure is any experience you want to continue while you're in it, or want more of afterward. I already know this dies on the relief case, since I don't want the relief to go on, I want the pain gone. Maybe it also dies on the routine case.

So: is there one account that gets most of these, or do I have to sacrifice a verdict? If it's a sacrifice, say which and why the others are worth more. And please tell me what your favored principle predicts for a case I haven't listed: the pleasure of anticipation, where the thing itself then turns out dull and I retroactively say the looking-forward was the good part.
