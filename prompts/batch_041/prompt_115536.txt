I am a graduate student in epistemology, and I am trying to get clear on the regress argument for foundationalism. The standard presentation distinguishes three responses: the chain of justifying reasons terminates in some non-inferentially justified beliefs; it circles back on itself; or it continues infinitely, with every belief justified by a further belief. What I find frustrating is that the discussion usually proceeds as though the only question is whether the regress is vicious, and then treats viciousness as if it were univocal. I would like a careful explanation of the conceptual issue: what exactly is supposed to be bad about an infinite regress of justification, as opposed to an infinite regress of causes or an infinite regress of explanations? Is the alleged viciousness a matter of the structure of the support relation, of the psychological impossibility of completing an infinite task, or of something about the epistemic status of an infinite chain as a whole? In particular, I want to understand whether infinitism is best understood as a version of foundationalism (because every link in the chain is a reason, and the chain itself is the basic structure), as a version of coherentism (because the justification of each member depends on the whole chain), or as genuinely a third option.

A classmate has proposed a position I have been calling procedural infinitism: a belief is justified just in case the believer has an unbounded disposition to meet any challenge with a further reason—there is no question that would leave her silent—even if she never actually holds an infinite number of beliefs. Please explain how this proposal relates to the three standard positions. Does it stop the regress, relocate it, or merely redescribe coherentism? And what would have to be true for the regress of reasons to be benign rather than vicious? I am less interested in a survey of who said what than in a clear conceptual map: the relata in the regress, the relation that generates it, the difference between having a reason and being able to produce one, and the precise sense in which the regress is or is not a problem.
