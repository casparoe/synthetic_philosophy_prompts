Here's what's been bugging me. As I understand it, Lewis's core move is that a convention is an *arbitrary* regularity — we all drive on one side of the road, we all use the same word for cat, and the arbitrariness is the whole point: the regularity isn't rationally required, it's just one of several equilibria we happened to settle into. Fine. But then I read people saying that norms evolve to support cooperation, or that this norm is worse for social welfare than that one, or that some conventions are more efficient than others — and the ground feels like it shifts under me. If a convention is arbitrary by definition, then (a) any stable arbitrary regularity should count as a convention, which makes the concept toothless, and (b) there's no basis for ranking conventions at all — better would have to mean something the arbitrariness rules out. So is all the norm-evolution talk just people sliding between convention and norm without noticing? Or am I missing something about what arbitrariness is doing in Lewis's account?
