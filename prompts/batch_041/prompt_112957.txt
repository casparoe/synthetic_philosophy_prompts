Im a 3rd year PhD student in statistical genetics (GWAS, polygenic scores, a bit of Mendelian randomization) and I keep ending up in arguments with the two philosophers of biology who crash our journal club, always about whether DNA is a code. I'd written it off as a verbal dispute until one of them said something last week I cant shake: the DNA is at most a coded message, and the code itself — the mapping from codons to amino acids — isnt in the DNA at all, it lives in the tRNAs and the synthetases and the ribosome, so it's really a fact about the cell's machinery. Which makes "the genetic code" a slightly dodgy name, like calling a page of Morse "the Morse code". Her second point was that information in the Shannon sense (X carries info about Y just in case they covary) is cheap and everywhere, so it can't be what anyone means when they say the genome is informational; they must mean the semantic kind, where the carrier has to be able to be about something and to be wrong about it — and she thinks nothing in a cell is in the business of being wrong, so there's no code in the sense that matters.

What I actually want from you is the most important implications of this. Concretely: if she's right, what changes for someone like me? How should I be interpreting a GWAS hit or a polygenic score, what should I stop saying about "genetic effects" in talks, what breaks in how we talk about genetic disease and about editing a gene — and then the flip side, what would I have to give up if DNA does turn out to be a code in the full semantic sense? Give me the 4 or 5 implications that genuinely matter rather than a survey of the literature.
