I was at a reading group where the discussion turned to the color phi phenomenon, and two members, Marta and Dev, got into a long argument they could not resolve. The phenomenon, as it was described to us: when two dots of different colours are flashed in quick succession at two different locations, observers report seeing a single dot travel from the first location to the second and change colour about halfway, with the change apparently occurring before the second dot had even appeared. I would like you to adjudicate their exchange move by move, saying who is right about each specific claim and what, if anything, both of them are missing.

Marta's opening claim was that the phenomenon shows experience is not assembled in real time but edited after the fact. Since the observer's experience of the colour change seems to be located at a time before the information needed to produce it had arrived, the brain must have fabricated that experience and inserted it retroactively into an already-elapsed interval. She called this an Orwellian revision, on the analogy of a regime that rewrites the historical record while the events themselves proceed normally. Dev replied that nothing forces this reading. The brain might instead simply delay the experience until both stimuli have arrived and then present one already-coherent episode; on that reading nothing is edited or falsified, because the experience never existed in an earlier, unrevised form. He called this a Stalinesque revision, where the show is staged only once the evidence is in. The dispute, as he framed it, is about whether the fabrication is inserted into a record that was already there, or whether the record is only ever written once, late.

Marta then appealed to timing. Observers do not merely report a colour change, she said; they report it as happening at a particular point, before the second dot. If the whole experience had simply been postponed, we would expect the report to be postponed too, and we could measure the lag; the fact that the reported timing lands where it does shows the timing itself was reconstructed, not that the experience was merely late. Dev answered that the reported timing is just more content, and content can be constructed along with everything else; a delay need not be a uniform shift, so there is no reason a postponed experience should look postponed from the inside.

To settle it, Marta proposed a Libet-style clock: show the observer a rotating hand and have them report the clock position at the instant the colour change occurs. If the reported position precedes the second stimulus, she said, we have a timestamp on the experience, and it shows the brain antedated it. Dev objected that the clock reading is delivered by the same machinery that is under dispute, since the observer's report of where the hand was is itself part of the constructed episode and so cannot serve as an independent record of when anything was experienced. Marta replied that this proves too much: if every report can be redescribed as constructed, then no experiment could ever distinguish the two stories, and a distinction that no possible evidence could touch is not a scientific distinction at all, which she took to be a concession that her Orwellian reading is at least as good as his. Dev said it cuts the other way, and that it shows the question of which one is really happening is the wrong question.

Please go through the exchange and adjudicate it move by move, the way you would referee a debate: for each of the moves above, say whether it is right, wrong, or right in part, and explain briefly. Then say who has the better of the overall dispute, and identify what both of them seem to be assuming that might be worth giving up. I am not on either side and genuinely do not know how to call it.
