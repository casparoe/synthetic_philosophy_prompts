A friend of mine has spent years thinking about the ethics of eating animals, and she takes wild animal suffering seriously — she is not the sort to wave away the mice and birds killed by harvesters, or the animals displaced when land is turned to agriculture. Over time she has settled into firm, considered verdicts about which of two food choices is the more defensible for someone with her commitments. Here they are, in the form in which I put the questions to her, with the reasons she gave.

1. A portion of factory-farmed chicken or a portion of beef: beef, and not close. A single cow feeds hundreds of people where a single chicken feeds a handful, so per portion the beef stands for a small fraction of one death; the chicken also lived a short, miserable life in a shed.

2. Beef from a feedlot or beef finished on pasture: the pasture beef, though by a smaller margin. The feedlot animal was fattened on harvested grain, and the harvest crushes a certain number of field animals, so that option carries extra deaths; the pasture animal also had the better life.

3. A dish of legumes and grains or a portion of chicken: the legumes, comfortably. Harvesting the crops kills some field animals, but far fewer than the chicken stands for.

4. A portion of farmed salmon or a portion of chicken: the chicken. The salmon were reared on wild-caught fish, so each salmon portion stands for the deaths of wild fish taken to feed it.

5. Beef from a dairy cow that has just been culled or beef from an animal raised specifically for meat: the culled cow's beef, because that cow was going to be killed in any case, so eating that meat adds no death to the world.

The next case I want to put to her is a portion of farmed shrimp — a couple of dozen small shrimp — against a portion of pork from a pig. Before I do, tell me what she is likely to say, and why. I care less about the verdict than about the rule she appears to be running: state it, then set out the other rules that fit all five of her answers equally well, say what each of those rules predicts about the shrimp and the pork, and name the single further case I could put to her that would best tell them apart. If her answers already rule out some otherwise plausible rule, say which and on what verdict it founders. And tell me whether any of the five verdicts sit awkwardly with each other, or with the weight she claims to give wild animal suffering; I would rather hear about the friction than have it smoothed over.
