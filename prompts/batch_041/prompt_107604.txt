I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of mind, but I spent four years before this as a backend/infrastructure engineer, and I still think in that vocabulary more comfortably than in the phenomenological one. I know distributed systems well enough to be dangerous — I've built and debugged systems with leader election, log replication, causal consistency, and snapshotting — and I'd like you to restate the case for the multiple drafts model against the Cartesian theater entirely in that vocabulary, then give me a residue report.

Take the Cartesian theater to be the assumption that there is a single place and a single moment at which a content is presented, yielding one authoritative, globally ordered stream with a privileged observer at the end of it. Take the multiple drafts position to be the claim that content fixation is a matter of influence, repetition, and competition across many processes, none of which is the place where it all comes together, together with the further claim that some questions about when a content became conscious may have no determinate answer at all. The target vocabulary is yours to pick from — linearizability and sequential consistency, logical clocks and partial causal order, consistent cuts and snapshot algorithms, leader election and replicated logs, buffering versus retroactive rewriting of a record, and the gap between a system's externally observable history and its internal one.

Be as faithful as you can. Map each component of the argument onto a real piece of that vocabulary, and don't let the engineering side win by fiat: if a mapping is available, use it, even where it makes the philosophical claim look better than my former colleagues would like.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want. (1) What translates cleanly, without distortion. (2) What translates only with distortion, with a precise account of what gets bent and in which direction. (3) What has no expression in that vocabulary at all. Finally, adjudicate: is the residue a defect of the distributed-systems vocabulary — something the philosophy can say and the engineering cannot — or is it a sign that the original claim was less clear than it looked, e.g. that a metaphysical thesis about consciousness is really a choice of consistency model plus a stipulation about which descriptions count as canonical? No references needed; I want the translation and the ledger, not a bibliography. Two moves I'd like you to resist in particular: treating the absence of a global clock as a solved engineering matter that automatically settles the philosophical dispute, and treating the observer in the theater as merely a client of an API. I know both moves; I want to know what they cost.
