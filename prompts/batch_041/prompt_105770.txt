I'm a backend engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and for the past week a colleague and I have been arguing over a question that keeps turning out to be harder than it looks: is Norway or Sweden the more liberal country? He treats it as an empirical matter — compile the right table of statutes and policies, he says, and the answer falls out — while I've come to suspect the dispute is at least half conceptual, and that any table would decide it only after we'd settled what we meant by liberal and whose freedom was being counted. I'd like you to write an essay that adjudicates this properly. Please lay out the main candidate readings of the term — a formal-legal reading on which the more liberal country is simply the one that prohibits least; an economic reading on which it's the one that leaves the most economic decisions in private hands; and a social-democratic or republican reading on which liberty consists in the absence of domination of some people by others, so that more state action can mean more liberty rather than less — and then say which reading you think is the right one to apply to a question asked this casually. From there, argue to a verdict: which of the two countries is more liberal, on the reading you defend, and don't retreat into "it depends." If the honest answer is that the question is underdetermined as posed, that is an acceptable verdict, but you have to show why the ambiguity cannot be resolved rather than gesturing at it. (For what it's worth, I've been sketching taxonomies of freedom on a whiteboard at work, which my teammates have found unhelpful.)

One further thing I want you to take seriously, because my colleague proposed it and I can't decide whether it's clever or confused. He suggests a revealed-preference test: the more liberal country is the one a resident of either country, given the means and the legal right to move, would prefer to live in — so we could in principle settle the matter by looking at who moves where, holding income and language aside. My worry is that this measures how attractive a country is rather than how free its residents are, and that the two can come apart, since a wealthy and conformist country might be a pleasant place to live while permitting very little. Assess that test on its merits, and then, in a closing section, tell me what would actually settle the dispute: what evidence or decision procedure would move you, how you would operationalise the key terms so that the comparison isn't merely trading adjectives, and which residual disagreements are value disagreements that no amount of data could resolve. I don't need a bibliography, a reading list, or citations — I can't look anything up at the moment and neither can you — so keep the argument self-contained, and wherever a factual claim about either country's laws or institutions matters to your case, state it as an assumption and flag how confident you are in it.
