I am a retired experimental physicist. For most of my career my work consisted in inferring the properties of things I could not see from the behaviour of instruments I could; the inference was licensed by theory, and when the theory was thin we said so and lowered our confidence. Since retiring I have been reading philosophy, and lately I have been reading the safety frameworks that the frontier AI laboratories publish. I would like a philosophical reconstruction of what those frameworks presuppose, because the more I read them the less I can tell whether their parts rest on the same view of the world.

Consider three things such a framework does. First, before a new model is deployed, the laboratory runs a battery of evaluations and adversarial probes, and if the model fails to produce dangerous outputs under those conditions, deployment goes ahead; behaviour observed under test is treated as warranting a conclusion about what the model is disposed to do in the wild. Second, the same frameworks, under the heading of control, assume the model may be an adversary: they design protocols so that safety does not depend on the model's being well disposed — a weaker model monitors the stronger one, the stronger one's actions are restricted, it is kept away from the levers that matter — on the explicit supposition that a misaligned model could behave impeccably while it waits for its moment. Third, engineers in practice often ask the model to explain its reasoning or to report whether it is complying, and treat the answer as evidence.

My questions are these. What account of the relation between observable behaviour and inner disposition does each of the three practices presuppose, and are those accounts consistent with one another? Does the first practice commit its user to something like a behavioural criterion of alignment — that a system which behaves well across every test we can devise is thereby aligned — which the second practice seems designed to deny? If the reply is that the tests merely sample the space of situations, tell me what licenses the extrapolation from sample to disposition here, given that in my old field we had laws to do that work and I cannot see what plays that role. What notion of trust is at stake when one trusts an artefact: a prediction about its future behaviour, or a stance one adopts toward it regardless of the prediction? Is alignment best understood as a property of the model, or of the model together with the protocol it runs inside? And where, in any of this, does responsibility for a harmful act come to rest?

Please reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what these practices strictly presuppose from what they merely fit comfortably with, say where they are philosophically underdetermined so that several rival theories would license the same engineering, and tell me what would have to change if the presupposed view were replaced by a rival. No bibliography is needed; I will find the literature myself.
