I am grading the final examination for a graduate seminar in modal metaphysics and would like help with one of the answers. The question and the student's response are below.

Question 3 (20 points). In lecture we reconstructed Gödel's modal ontological argument as follows. Positivity is treated as a primitive predicate of properties, written P(φ). The background modal logic is a normal modal logic containing the T principle. The axioms are:

A1. If P(φ) and □∀x(φ(x) → ψ(x)), then P(ψ).
A2. For every property φ, exactly one of φ and ¬φ is positive.
A3. Godlikeness is positive.
A4. If P(φ), then □P(φ).
A5. Necessary existence is positive.

The definitions are:

D1. x is Godlike iff x has as essential properties all and only the positive properties.
D2. φ is an essence of x iff φ(x) and, for every property ψ, ψ(x) iff □∀y(φ(y) → ψ(y)).
D3. x necessarily exists iff, for every essence φ of x, □∃y φ(y).

(a) Reconstruct the argument, making clear what work each axiom does. In particular, say whether the possibility of a Godlike being is a premise of the argument or a result derived within it.

(b) The argument is widely said to generate a modal collapse. Say precisely what the collapse is, explain how the axioms generate it, and say whether it shows that the axioms are inconsistent.

(c) Suppose A5 is replaced by A5*, which says that necessary existence is not negative, that is, ¬P(¬NE), where NE is the property of necessary existence. Does the argument still establish its conclusion? Explain.

The student's response:

(a) The argument depends on treating positivity as a primitive, technical notion rather than a synonym for moral goodness, and on the axioms that constrain it. A1 says that positivity is closed under necessary entailment: if a positive property necessarily implies a property ψ, then ψ is positive as well. A2 says that every property has a determinate polarity: for each φ, exactly one of φ and ¬φ is positive. A3 supplies the argument with a subject matter by declaring Godlikeness positive, A4 says that positivity is not a contingent matter, since positive properties are positive in every world, and A5 is the bridge to the conclusion, since it puts necessary existence itself into the positive class.

The proof then runs as follows. Suppose some positive property φ were necessarily unexemplified. Take a positive property ψ, for instance Godlikeness, which A3 guarantees is positive. Since nothing anywhere is φ, ψ necessarily implies ¬φ; by A1, ¬φ would be positive, contradicting A2. So every positive property is possibly exemplified. Because Godlikeness is positive, it follows that a Godlike being is possible. Notice that the possibility of a Godlike being is not an extra premise imported from outside the system; it is a theorem. That is what distinguishes Gödel's version from Leibniz's, where the coherence of the perfect being has to be assumed.

Godlikeness is also an essence of any Godlike being. If g is Godlike, then every property g has is positive: if g had a property ψ while ¬ψ were positive, then ¬ψ would be one of g's essential properties, contradicting ψ(g). And since A4 makes positive properties necessarily positive, every Godlike being in every world has ψ. So for each property ψ of g we have □∀y(G(y) → ψ(y)), which is exactly what D2 requires. Finally, g has all positive properties, hence necessary existence by A5, and by D3 every essence of g is necessarily exemplified. Godlikeness is such an essence, so □∃y G(y): a Godlike being exists in every possible world.

(b) The modal collapse is the consequence that the axioms deliver φ → □φ, so that whatever is the case is necessarily the case. It falls directly out of the machinery above. Because A4 makes positivity rigid, Godlikeness counts as an essence of any Godlike being, and an essence necessarily entails every property its bearer has. Take any true proposition p and the property of being such that p, which every object has exactly when p is the case. A Godlike being has that property, so it is necessarily entailed by Godlikeness; and since a Godlike being exists in every world, p holds in every world. Every truth is therefore necessary, and there is no contingency at all.

This is not a curious side effect but a fatal one. It shows that the five axioms are inconsistent: no assignment of extensions to the positive properties can satisfy all of them at once, which is why the derivation yields such an extravagant result. The argument is best read as a reductio of the positivity axioms, not as a proof of theism.

(c) Replacing A5 with A5* would break the argument at its last step. A5 places necessary existence in the positive class; A5* says only that necessary existence is not excluded from it. Since Godlikeness is defined as the having of all positive properties, the inference from g is Godlike to g exists necessarily requires necessary existence to be positive. If all we know is that it is not negative, Godlikeness no longer entails it, and the derivation of □∃y G(y) stalls: we can still establish that a Godlike being is possible and that Godlikeness is an essence of any Godlike being, but nothing forces any essence of that being to be necessarily exemplified. The modification removes precisely the axiom that carries the conclusion.
