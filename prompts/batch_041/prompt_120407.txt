Okay so I've been going down a rabbit hole on the leveling-down objection and I think there's a version of it that actually bites prioritarianism, not just egalitarianism, and I want a real number on whether I'm onto something or just confusing myself. Quick setup so we're on the same page: leveling down is the charge that a theory is in trouble if it can say some outcome is better even though it leaves nobody better off and at least one person worse off. Prioritarianism is the view that a benefit matters more the worse off the person getting it is, and that equality is not itself valuable. The usual line is that prioritarianism is immune to the objection because it only cares about people's absolute levels, so dragging the better-off down can never count as an improvement all by itself — that's the standard reason people give for preferring prioritarianism over egalitarianism, and I basically bought it for years.

Here's my case, and I'm definately open to being told it's broken. Take a version of prioritarianism where the priority weight depends on how far below other people you are rather than on your absolute level, so a benefit to the worst-off person counts for more the bigger the gap between them and everyone else. Now: World A has Ada at 100 and Bo at 10. World B has Ada at 90 and Bo at 10. In both worlds Bo later gets the exact same benefit, say +10, and it happens regardless of which world we're in. The relational version seems forced to say B can be better than A, because Bo's +10 gets weighted more heavily when Ada is sitting at 90 than when she's at 100, and that extra value can outweigh the 10 units Ada loses. But in B nobody is better off than in A and Ada is strictly worse off. That looks like leveling down inside a view that's supposed to be a species of prioritarianism. The part I think is actually novel-ish is that Ada's loss isn't paying for a benefit to Bo — it's paying for a bigger weight on a benefit Bo gets either way, which feels harder to wave away than the usual version.

So: what's your credence that some version of the leveling-down objection refutes prioritarianism in general, not just egalitarianism? I'm at about 0.35.
