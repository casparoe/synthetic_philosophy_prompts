I am a novelist, and the book I am writing turns on a character who never utters a falsehood and nevertheless ruins a friend's life by what she lets her listeners infer. I have spent a year reading in the philosophy of deception and keep meeting the same question — whether lying is worse than misleading, and why — answered in ways that do not help me write her. What I want instead is an agenda: which open questions about the distinction between lying and misleading would genuinely change how a character like hers is written and judged, ranked, with the ranking defended. Assume a year of my time, no appetite for disputes that turn out to be terminological, and nothing I could use only if I had a philosophy library to hand.

Here is the working view you may push back on before ranking anything. On my account an utterance misleads only if the speaker relies on an inference the hearer is entitled to draw given the shared norms of the conversation; call this the entitlement condition. The questions I want ordered are the ones upstream of it: whether entitlement is fixed by convention, by the speaker's design, or by what a reasonable hearer would infer; whether misleading requires intent, and what it is to intend an inference rather than merely foresee it; where the paltering case — true statements arranged to produce a false impression — belongs, with lying, with misleading, or in a third class; whether the supposed moral gap between lying and misleading survives once consequences, breach of trust, and entitlements are held fixed; and whether the distinction marks anything in the world or only in our vocabulary.

Rank them by how much each would change the writing — which would alter what the reader takes to be her crime, which would alter her own self-justification, which would alter nothing but my prose — and say plainly which criteria you are using, where those criteria pull against one another, and what argument or example would move a question up or down the list. If I have left out a question that should outrank all of these, put it first and tell me why.
