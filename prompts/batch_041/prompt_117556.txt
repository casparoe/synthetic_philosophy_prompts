I'm a software engineer with zero philosophy background and I've somehow ended up as the person who writes up recommendations for a small giving circle I'm in — 30-ish of us, we pool about $40k a year, anything over $2k has to go to a full group vote, so I'm basically the triage. People keep bringing us "trades" and I keep saying "I'll think about it" because I can't tell which ones are fine and which ones arn't. The idea, as I understand it (someone said the term is moral trade) is that two people who care about different things can both come out ahead if each does something the other cares about more than they do themselves — the vegetarian pays the meat-eater to go veg for a year, because the vegetarian cares more about the meat-eater's diet than the meat-eater does. That seems obviously fine, but the exact same shape seems to cover stuff that feels like extortion.

Act like a philosopher who actually works on this, and think it through carefully before you answer. Start with a quick brainstorm of the hard cases, then give me a decision procedure I can actually run on a proposal: the questions in order, what a good-enough answer to each one looks like, where it stops with a no, and where the answer is a values call that has to go to the group instead of to me. Half an hour per proposal is my budget and I can usually get one clarifying email out of whoever's proposing. The two things I keep getting stuck on: whose values do we score the trade against, ours or theirs, and whether saying yes to one of these teaches everyone in the group to start making offers we'd rather not get.

Then run it on two proposals we actually have on the table:
1. A member will go vegetarian for a year if we donate $2,500 to a charity they pick that alot of us think is way less important than what we normally fund.
2. Another member says that if we don't fund their project they're quitting the circle and stopping their volunteer work, and the project is honestly decent, so I'm tempted to just fund it.

Be committed: if the procedure says no to something that would put money in a good place, say that straight and tell me why, don't hand me both sides and let me pick. And tell me what the procedure can't do. No reading list please, I'm not going to read a paper.
