I'm putting together the questions for a recorded conversation I'm hosting next month, and the guest is a philosopher who's just brought out a book with quite an unusual line on the whole do-LLMs-understand thing. Her claim, roughly, is that both sides of that fight — the stochastic-parrots side and the "obviously they understand, look at the benchmarks" side — are asking the wrong sort of question, because they treat understanding as a state that a system either has or lacks, when really it's a standing: a normative status you hold inside a practice where people can hold you to account, ask you for your reasons, disagree with you and expect you to take the disagreement on board. She thinks current models do hold that standing — not because of anything going on inside them, but because we've built whole institutions around them (evals, the fine-tuning, "the model got that wrong") in which they get treated as answerable. So the question stops being whether they understand and becomes whether we should keep extending the standing.

I want eight to ten questions that go at the actual joints of that, rather than questions that just let her restate the thesis. It's a mixed room and alot of them won't have read her, so please don't write anything that needs her to cite literature or that turns the whole thing into a bibliography chat.

Things I definitely want in there:

- one on who exactly holds the standing — the model, the model plus its trainers and users, or the lab? And what she loses if she says it's the whole socio-technical set-up rather than the model.
- one on whether understanding on her view can come in degrees, since it looks like her story makes it all-or-nothing at the level of the practice, which feels wrong for, say, understanding chess openings versus understanding irony.
