I'm a backend engineer, zero philosophy training, and I somehow ended up hosting a small interview podcast on the side. Next week my guest is someone who defends the view that a hypocrite loses the standing to blame: if you're doing the very thing you're condemning, you're in no position to condemn it, full stop. I've argued about this on a forum and got absolutely wrecked, so I want questions that make him work, not ones where he just restates the slogan for ten minutes.

Can you draft eight to ten interview questions, in an order that builds on itself, where each one presses on some specific piece of the view, and each has a follow-up question keyed to the answer he's most likely to give? I need these threads in there somewhere:

- What exactly gets lost. I want him to pull apart (a) the right to feel resentment, (b) the right to say it out loud to the person, and (c) the right to demand that they change. Ask whether those can come apart, and what's left of blame if only (c) goes.
- The self-application thing. If hypocrites can't blame, does that undercut his own argument against people who disagree with him about standing, or is he special-casing himself?
- The reformed hypocrite. Someone who did it for years, genuinely quit, and now condemns it. Is standing a switch or a dial? If it's a dial, what does half-standing even mean, and can it come back over time or only after an apology/restitution?
- Partial hypocrisy. Someone who does it once a year lecturing someone who does it daily, and then the reverse. Does the amount matter, or is it a yes/no thing?
- Borrowed standing. A victim asks a hypocritical ally to speak on their behalf ("yeah I know you do it too, but say it for me"). Does that fix the ally's standing, or does the hypocrisy infect the message no matter who's holding it?
- Institutions. A company suing a rival for license violations while violating licenses itself; an open source maintainer who is vicious to newcomers while demanding civility. Can a company be a hypocrite, and if it loses standing, who lost it -- the spokesperson, the lawyers, the shareholders?
- Standing vs. desert. Even if nobody's in a position to say it, is the target still blameworthy? Does losing standing mean the accused gets off, or just that the accuser is muted? Also: if the blamer's standing depends on facts about what he does in private, can the target just say prove it and keep the blame?
- The victim exception. Does the view imply a victim who's also a hypocrite about that norm loses the standing to object to being wronged? That seems like a result he has to either bite or explain away.

Close with one question asking for the strongest case where even he'd say a hypocrite keeps standing, and one about what would actually change his mind about the whole view. And please no what drew you to philosophy type stuff, and no reading list -- I just want questions I can read off my laptop.
