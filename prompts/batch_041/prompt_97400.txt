I keep going back and forth with a friend about why promises bind, and I want to know what principle actually fits my gut verdicts. Here they are. I promise to meet someone for lunch and then a better offer comes up: I'm bound, breaking it is a genuine wrong. I promise my boss to work the weekend because he's clearly about to make my life hell if I don't: that doesnt bind me at all, it barely feels like a promise. I promise to help a friend dispose of a body (hypothetically): no pull whatsoever, the thing is just void. I promise my dying mother I'll finish her book, and then she dies: still bound, and this is one of the strongest ones, even though she can't be disappointed, can't rely on anything, and no one would ever know if I blew it off. I promise a guy I'll sell him my car, then find out he lied to me about why he wanted it: I feel off the hook, which surprises me, because I did give my word and he's still counting on the car. I promise a kid a trip to the zoo and the zoo turns out to be closed that day: still bound, but bound to do something, not that exact thing. And a promise I make to myself to run every morning -- no matter how much I let myself down, it doesn't feel like the same kind of thing at all. Is there one principle that gets all of these, or do I have to give one up? And what would it say about a promise made to someone who's already forgotten it -- do I owe them anything at all?
