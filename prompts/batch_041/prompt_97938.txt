I teach a upper-level seminar on philosophy of mind, and last week we did that old thought experiment where someone builds a book that answers questions exactly the way Einstein's brain would — the idea being that the whole conversation is somehow "in" the book even though it's just print. My students got completely hung up on whether the book itself is conscious, and the annoying thing was that the discussion kept sliding between three or four different versions of the case without anyone noticing they'd switched. So for next week I want to hand them a calibration exercise instead of another reading, and I'd like you to draft the substance of it.

Take the following six systems and give me a rating from 0 to 100 for each — roughly your credence that the thing is conscious, or has experiences, at the moment it's doing its thing. Then order them from most to least confident and sort them into tiers (something like "pretty sure it's conscious", "genuinely don't know", "pretty sure it's not"). The systems are: (1) the original book, closed, sitting on a shelf. (2) The book being read front to back by a normal human reader who follows the dialogue in her head. (3) A version where the whole text is stored as a lookup table — no pages, just a machine that takes a question and spits out the pre-computed answer, and nothing "runs" except the lookup. (4) A version where the book is printed normally but each page can only be read after a long delay, so the entire conversation takes forty years, with the reader forgetting most of it between sessions. (5) A version where the book is bound in a random page order, so the reader meets the sentences out of sequence but still ends up, over time, in the same internal states as the normal reader would (don't ask me how, it's a stipulation). (6) A version where the book is also wired to a thermostat in the room, and a few words change depending on the temperature, so the "conversation" is causally hooked up to the world in a tiny way. For each one give me a couple of sentences on what's actually doing the work in your rating — which feature you're treating as the thing that matters — and flag any case where you think the question is ill-posed rather than just hard. Please don't hedge everything to 50; I want real numbers even where you're unsure, and I want the ordered list at the end so I can put it straight on the handout.
