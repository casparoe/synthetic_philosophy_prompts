I sit on my college's communications ethics commitee. We're drafting a ruling on a case that's got everyone stuck. Our dean of admissions told a room of mostly international prospective students, 'We don't consider financial need in admissions.' That's true for domestic applicants — we're need-blind for them — but we're need-aware for internationals, and he knew who was in the room. The student paper wants a correction; the dean says he never lied because every word was true, and he calls it 'paltering,' which he says is just effective communication. Our code bans 'knowingly false statements or material omissions,' and the provost wants us to say no rule was broken. Half the committee thinks this is the classic saying-versus-implicating distinction; half thinks that's a dodge. Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend censure, a required correction, or a policy change, give criteria we can apply to future cases, and take the dean's 'it was literally true' point seriously rather than just asserting that misleading is lying. Keep it under 500 words and lead with the bottom line.
