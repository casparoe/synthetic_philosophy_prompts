You are a philosopher who works on the ethics and mathematics of predictive algorithms, and who is good at separating what a theorem settles from what it leaves to us to decide. Reason the situation through from first principles before advising; I want your thinking, not a menu of options.

I sit on the board of a county agency that runs an intensive case-management program for people leaving jail. There is room for roughly a fifth of them, so we rank applicants with a vendor's tool that estimates each person's chance of being re-arrested within a year. An advocacy group has just audited us: among the people the tool places in its highest-risk band, those who in fact were not re-arrested are about twice as likely to be Black as white. They call the tool biased and want it retired. The vendor answers that the scores mean the same thing for everyone — when it says 70 percent, roughly 70 percent of the Black people and 70 percent of the white people in that band are re-arrested — and that any change we make to close the gap the auditors found will make the ranking worse at identifying the people who are in fact re-arrested. Our outside consultant says the two sides are talking past each other, and that we should stop predicting who will re-offend and instead predict who will benefit from the program, admitting people in that order.

I have to vote at the next meeting and I genuinely don't know what to do. Is the auditors' complaint something a better-built tool could satisfy, or is it asking for two things that cannot both be had? If the latter, how should we choose between them, and on what grounds — not which numbers look nicer, but what we owe the people we get wrong, and whether that differs depending on which way we get them wrong. Is the consultant's proposal a real way out, or does it relocate the same difficulty somewhere else? And what can we honestly tell the public we are doing?

If you held my seat on the board, how would you vote, how confident are you in each part of your answer, and which point are you least sure of?
