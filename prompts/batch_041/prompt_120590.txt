Give me your credence in a single proposition, stated precisely below, and then decompose that number rather than defending it. I am not asking for a survey of the literature on social trust, nor for the standard caveats about small-sample comparisons; I want a probability, the sub-questions it rests on, a rough probability for each of those, and the arithmetic that combines them into the final figure.

The proposition, which I will call EXPORTABILITY: a polity of at least twenty million people, ethnically and linguistically heterogeneous by the standards of the present Nordic states, with a baseline level of generalized social trust in the bottom quartile of the current OECD distribution, could adopt the Nordic policy bundle — a high marginal tax take, universal and largely non-stigmatizing transfers, union-dense labor markets with centralized bargaining and active labor-market policy, and publicly delivered health, education, and childcare — and within two generations reach a level of redistribution, welfare-state stability, and political legitimacy in the top quartile of the present OECD, without any prior increase in generalized trust. Treat two generations and the top quartile as the operationalization; if you would prefer a different one, give your number for your version and say what you changed.

Decompose the question at least into the following, adding any term you think belongs:

1. The prior-settlement thesis: that the Nordic bundle is downstream of an earlier historical settlement — the weakness of landed aristocracy, early mass mobilization, agrarian organization, literate state churches — and that the bundle transplanted without the settlement does not reproduce the outcomes. What is your credence that this thesis is true, and how much of EXPORTABILITY does it carry?

2. The policy-feedback thesis: that the bundle itself manufactures the trust and legitimacy it is often said to presuppose, so that low baseline trust is a start-up cost rather than a ceiling. Your credence, and its weight.

3. The scale-and-heterogeneity constraint: that the outcomes depend on small population, cultural homogeneity, or the absence of a large marginalized out-group. Your credence that this is a genuine causal constraint rather than a confound with something else, and its weight.

4. The bundle-specification problem: whether there is one Nordic model at all, given the intra-Nordic variation — differing trust histories, differing resource endowments, differing labor-market designs — or whether the model dissolves into a family of loosely related settlements on inspection. Your credence that the bundle is a stable natural kind, and how much EXPORTABILITY depends on that.

5. The binding-constraint question: whether trust is the binding constraint on the whole enterprise, or whether state capacity, administrative competence, or elite coordination is doing the real work, with trust as a by-product. Your credence that trust is binding, and its weight.

Give a number and one line of justification for each term, state which terms are conditionally dependent on which, and show the combination. Then tell me the term your final number is most sensitive to, and what specific evidence would move that term most — I mean evidence, not argument, since argument on these questions is cheap. Finally, suppose two interlocutors give EXPORTABILITY credences of 0.85 and 0.15; say where their disagreement most plausibly lives — in the direction of the trust/institution causal arrow, in the specification of the bundle, or in the counterfactual standard for success — and defend that diagnosis against the alternative that they simply hold different priors about how much history binds.
