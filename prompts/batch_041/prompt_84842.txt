Two years ago I stopped drinking. The change has been complete enough that people who knew me then speak of that man as someone else, and my brother insists he was not me; neither of us can settle it by looking at anything. What strikes me now is that the personal case looks like an instance of a general one — any thing that changes raises the same question about what its persistence amounts to — so I want your credence that perdurantism is the right account of persistence: that a persisting thing is a four-dimensional whole, never wholly present at any single moment, with what exists at each moment being a proper temporal part of it. Endurantism, by contrast, says that a thing is wholly present at every moment of its existence and has no temporal parts. I am at about 0.6 myself; a friend who holds that the two views are equally serviceable ways of talking about the same facts is at about 0.15.

Give me the number through its decomposition. Include at least these terms: whether the dispute is substantive at all, or whether the two views are two languages for one set of facts; whether the problem of temporary intrinsics is decisive against endurance — whether a thing wholly present at two times can have incompatible intrinsic properties without either demoting those properties to relations to times or positing a special having-at-a-time mode of predication, and whether that is a fatal cost or merely a redescription; whether the argument from vagueness goes through — if it is ever vague whether some things compose a further thing then there must be a sharp cut-off, which is absurd, so composition is unrestricted, and if composition is unrestricted then every filled region of spacetime has a fusion, including the instantaneous slices of ordinary things (that last step is the one I doubt most); whether frame-dependent simultaneity tells against being wholly present at any one time; what stage theory, on which the thing at each moment is the thing itself rather than a part of a longer thing and persistence is a matter of counterpart relations rather than identity, does better or worse than the worm view; and whether perdurantism earns anything for the personal case or only relocates the difficulty into a unity relation among temporal parts that then needs its own account. Add any term I have left out, and tell me if one of mine is not a genuine sub-question.

Format, in this order: "The number" (one sentence). "Decomposition" (a table, one row per sub-question, with a probability and one line of justification each). "Combination" (about 100 words: how the terms combine, and which you are treating as independent of which). "Sensitivity" (about 150 words: the one term the final figure hangs on most, which term most likely accounts for the gap between my friend's 0.15 and my 0.6, and what would move it). Reason from the considerations themselves — I do not need names or citations, and where you are reconstructing an argument rather than reporting one, say so.
