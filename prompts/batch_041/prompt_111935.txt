ok so weird end-of-term request. I'm wrapping up the scientific realism unit and next week is the structural realist move: give up on the objects, keep the structure. The pitch is that when one theory replaces another, the equations survive even after the stuff the equations were supposedly about gets thrown out — the old optics keeps turning up inside the new optics, the old mechanics inside the new mechanics, long after everyone agrees there never was any such thing as the medium the whole business was originally built on. So maybe what carries over isn't the furniture, it's the relations between the furniture.

My students nod along at this every year and I know perfectly well that none of them have it. So instead of one more reading I want to close the session with a poem, and I want it to be a sestina, because the sestina is basically the same machine: six end-words, and the form just shuffles them. The words never change. But every time one comes back it's got different neighbours, and it means something a bit different because of them. That's the whole question about what survives a revolution in science and what doesn't, and I want the form doing that arguement rather than just sitting there being fancy.

Specifics:

- you pick the six end-words, but they should be the kind of word that shows up on both sides of a big theory change and comes back with a different job. at most one of them should be dead furniture like ether — the survivors are the interesting cases.
- standard retrogradata (6-1-5-2-4-3), 39 lines with the envoi, and the envoi should still be carrying all six. a title is fine.
- by the fourth or fifth stanza I want to feel a word has been quietly re-employed — same sound, shifted sense, because its neighbours moved. if it reads as a word game you've missed it.
- the envoi is where I want the sting. there's an objection that this whole position is either trivial or it's just ordinary realism with extra steps: if all you've really kept is the shape of the relations, then any old collection of things of the right size can be dressed up so that the shape comes out true, and "we kept the structure" is buying you much less than it sounds. leave that sitting there. dont resolve it, dont hand me a reassuring last line, and please dont tack a moral on the end.
- accuracy over prettiness. the test is whether someone who's been in the debate reads it and hears the position actually speaking through the form. if all they get is the words "relations" and "structure" sprinkled around, it's failed.

thanks — no rush, I just need it before thursday.
