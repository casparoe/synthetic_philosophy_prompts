I've spent the past year building a canonical data model that reconciles customer records from a dozen upstream systems, and the experience has nudged me toward a view I'd like you to check. Every schema we build is purpose-relative: ours handles billing well and analytics badly, and when we tried to force one taxonomy to serve every use case we made it worse. Chemistry looks like it confirms the pattern, since hydrogen has to be placed somewhere in the periodic table and its position is a choice rather than a discovery, much as the boundary between species has to be drawn wherever it happens to be convenient for biologists who study birds but not for bacteria that trade genes freely. Doesn't this settle the natural-kinds debate in favor of the view that categories are conventions all the way down, so that the only real question is which scheme is most useful, and why do philosophers and scientists still talk as though some categories are found rather than made?
