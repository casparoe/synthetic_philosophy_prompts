I sit on the research side of a graduate seminar on moral psychology, and we keep circling the same discomfort: moral foundations theory was built to explain why people disagree, not to adjudicate who is right, yet in policy writing and even in some of the secondary literature the observation that both sides are drawing on genuine moral foundations gets quietly promoted into a licence to settle the question. I want to watch that promotion performed straight-faced, so that the seminar can see what it looks like when a descriptive finding is treated as a warrant to legislate.

Write me a guidance note — around 700 words, in the deadpan register of a British public body's internal circular — issued by the (fictional) Joint Committee on Civic Cohesion of a mid-sized city council, which has decided to resolve moral disputes among residents by psychometric audit rather than by argument. The disputes it handles should be mundane and recognisable: a contested statue, a planning objection, a school uniform rule, a complaint about a noisy place of worship. The note should set out the procedure as though it were unremarkable housekeeping — how a dispute is referred, how each party's submission is coded against the foundations, how the tally then determines the outcome — and the policy should be allowed to follow from the psychology rather than the reverse. The humour has to come from getting the moral-psychology right: which foundations the theory actually posits and how endorsement of them clusters across political camps, what self-report questionnaires can and cannot measure, and whose populations the original samples were drawn from. Do not invent findings to make a joke land, and do not let the note ever announce its own absurdity — the reader should be able to work out what is being sent up from the procedure alone, and a specialist in the area should find the caricature fair rather than clumsy. I would also like a short closing editor's note of roughly 150 words that separates what the piece treats as settled in the psychology from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it, and that states plainly where the satire takes liberties with the ideas. Please write in British English.
