Next month I'm giving a work-in-progress talk to our department's mind-and-metaphysics reading group and I'm trying to get ahed of the Q&A instead of improvising my way through it like last time. The paper defends a restricted version of the old idea that conceivability is a guide to possibility: I argue the guide only ever runs in one direction, and only for what I call 'thick' conceivability -- where you can actually represent the scenario in some detail, not just fail to spot a contradiction -- and I claim that once you draw that line, the zombie case (and the whole famly of imagination-based arguments for dualism) collapses, because what we've really got there is at best thin conceivability. I also argue that even thick conceivability earns whatever evidential force it has from your background theory of the domain rather than from the imagining itself, so it can never do the discovery work people want it to do.

The audience is the hard part. There are two people in the group who work on modal epistemology and know the two-dimensionalist machinery inside out -- they'll want to know how my thick/thin line maps onto primary vs. secondary conceivability and whether I've just renamed something they already have. Then there's a hardline Kripkean who thinks the a posteriori necessities (water, Hesperus) already show conceivability is worthless as a guide and will mostly just want me to admit it. There's also a couple of cognitive scientists who study mental imagery and think 'conceivability' ought to be treated as an empirical psychological kind, plus one historian of philosophy who insists this is all just Hume with extra steps, and a modal logician who keeps asking which notion of possibility I even mean.

Write me a Q&A prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from *this* room, not the generic 'but zombies!' stuff, ordered by how much damage each would do, each phrased the way that particular person would put it, with a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is soft or where I'm bluffing. Tell me what to concede up front before someone else makes me concede it, and finish with the one question I'm hoping nobody asks and how I should answer it if it comes anyway. And if my thick/thin distinction can't survive the two-dimensionalist's first question, just say so plainly rather than giving me spin.
