Here are the two positions as I understood them.

Friend A says CH isn't really a question with a right answer at all. Her analogy is that asking whether CH is true is like asking whether a group is abelian: it's just incomplete until you say which group — or in this case, which universe of sets. Since forcing lets you build universes where CH holds and universes where it fails, and there's no God-given intended universe to appeal to, the honest verdict is that CH has different truth values in different set-theoretic worlds, and the reason it looks like a plain yes/no question is just that we're in the habit of talking as if there were one big V. Friend B thinks that's a cop-out. He says there's a fact of the matter about the universe of sets, we just haven't found the right axioms yet — that some new axiom, either a maximality principle or an inner-model axiom that makes the theory as complete as it can be, will eventually settle CH one way or the other, the way the axiom of choice went from controversial to standard. He even claims we can already say what the two candidate routes would deliver (the forcing-axiom route pinning the continuum at aleph-two, the inner-model route giving you CH), and that the fact we can say that at all shows CH is a determinate question we're just behind on.
