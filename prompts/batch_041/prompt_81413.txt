I have spent the last three months at an artists' colony finishing a novel, one strand of which is narrated by a crow, and I have consequently spent more time than is good for me arguing about animal minds with the other residents. One of them, a comparative psychologist who studies invertebrate cognition, gave me a summary of the state of play that I have been repeating ever since, and I would like to know how much of it I am entitled to keep. His account, more or less as he delivered it: "Consciousness is not a switch but a continuum, and the empirical question has been settled — the neuroscientists who signed the Cambridge Declaration on Consciousness announced that all mammals and birds, octopuses included, are conscious. Since evolution never draws sharp lines, and no one can name the point on the tree where experience begins, and since we now know that insects solve novel problems flexibly, remember individual faces, and weigh costs against benefits, there is no principled place to stop: the only defensible line would be at the single cell. Anyone who still asks whether a particular animal is conscious is asking a metaphysical question that the laboratories have already answered."

What I want is an audit of that paragraph, sentence by sentence: which parts a careful philosopher of mind or a working comparative researcher would accept as fair, which they would wince at as a simplification, and which are simply wrong. I would like to know where he has collapsed two claims that the field keeps carefully apart, where a contested inference has come in through the back door dressed as a finding, and whether his closing claim — that the matter is now purely empirical and closed — actually follows from whatever in his summary survives scrutiny. I am aware that I want to believe him, because the novel is better if he is right, so tell me plainly where I am being flattered.

Please answer in American English, and do not bother with reading lists, citations, or suggestions for further study; I am not going to chase down papers. I want to know what a well-informed person would say to his face, and what I can have my characters assert without embarrassing myself.
