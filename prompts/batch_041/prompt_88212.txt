I am designing the opening unit of a graduate seminar in moral and political philosophy on moral compromise, and I want a specification before we examine any particular account. The target is compromise between value systems: cases in which parties with conflicting moral commitments reach a settlement that each regards as better than the status quo, even though neither has converted to the other’s values and each may continue to regard the settlement as morally imperfect. Consider a hospital ethics committee divided between a bloc that treats a certain intervention as intrinsically degrading and a bloc that treats prohibiting it as a failure of care. They adopt a protocol that permits the intervention only under independent review, directs part of the benefit to those who object, and leaves both blocs free to keep condemning the protocol. Each prefers this to deadlock; neither thinks it is fully justified. What would a theory of moral compromise have to say about this and similar cases?

Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of moral compromise must deliver. Include the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must respect, and the constraints it must not violate, with the consideration behind each requirement. At a minimum, the theory should address the possibility of gains from trade when the currency is value realization rather than preference satisfaction; the difference between compromise and capitulation, bargaining, toleration, and modus vivendi; the normative status of a settlement that both sides regard as morally second-best; the phenomenon of moral remainder or regret even when compromise is justified; the limits of tradability for rights, duties to third parties, sacred or identity-constituting commitments, and values whose violation is thought to be categorically wrong; the effects of asymmetric power, coercion, and unjust background conditions; the difference between compromising one’s own values and compromising values one holds in trust for others; the relation between compromise and integrity; the role of mutual respect, recognition, and sincerity; the epistemic dimension of compromising under uncertainty about one’s own values; the relation between compromise, justice, and legitimacy; the conditions under which a compromise may bind dissenters or future generations; and the question whether there is a duty to compromise or whether it is merely permissible or supererogatory.

Then distinguish genuine desiderata from the prejudices of particular traditions, and say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are negotiable. Flag any subset of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and explain what that impossibility implies for the theory. Finally, identify the single requirement that does the most to exclude the most popular accounts of compromise, and explain why. I do not want a reading list or a scorecard of named views; I want the criteria and the cases that motivate them. A table is acceptable if it helps, but the reasoning behind each entry matters more than the table.
