I've been reporting on organ donation for a few months now and there's something I can't get my head round. In the UK, someone can be declared dead after a doctor tests their brainstem and gets no response — the heart's still beating, they're on a ventilator, they're warm to the touch, but officially they're dead. In the States the rule is stricter: the law wants the whole brain to have stopped, brainstem and all. Fine, different statutes, whatever. But then you've got donation after circulatory death, where the team switches the ventilator off, waits five minutes of no heartbeat, and starts taking the organs out — and by that point nobody has tested the brain at all. So there are two completely seperate routes to being dead in the same hospital, and one of them seems to be about whether the brain is working while the other seems to be about whether the heart is going.

What I want to know is what theory of death all this actually assumes. Does it assume death is a biological fact about the organism as a whole, or is it really a view about consciousness and personhood dressed up in the language of biology? Does it assume that dying is a moment rather than a process — and if it's a moment, why five minutes and not two, or fifty? And is there something a bit off about the fact that in one case we call someone dead while their body is plainly still running, whereas in the other we only call it death because we've already decided not to restart the heart?

I'd like you to reconstruct the implicit theory for me, and then be honest about which bits of the practice actually need that theory and which bits would sit just as happily under rival views — say, if you thought death was the irreversible loss of consciousness, or if you thought it was a process with no sharp line at all. Where is the practice philosophically underdetermined, and where (if anywhere) are its parts quietly assuming incompatible things? And if you swapped the presupposed view for one of the rivals, what would actually have to change on the wards — the tests, the timing, the dead donor rule, the lot?
