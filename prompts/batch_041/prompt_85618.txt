I spent about eight years doing comms for a city government, and there's a pattern I kept running into that I've never found a good name for.

Here's the shape of it. Somebody says something that isn't true — usually small and convenient, like that a contract went out to competitive bid, or that a report was reviewed by a lawyer when it wasn't. On its own that's just a lie and nobody needs a concept for it. The part I'm interested in is what happens next. The claim gets repeated in a press release, then it goes into a budget memo, then another department starts planning around it, then an outside vendor hires two people because of it. Six months later the original question — did the contract actually go out to bid? — has basically stopped being the issue. Nobody can correct it anymore, not because people still believe it but because correcting it would take down everything that got built on top of it. The person who said it first is now stuck feeding it, and the strange part is that the more it grows the cleaner and more coherent it looks next to the actual mess of what happened.

Smaller version I watched once: a manager tells a lie to protect one employee, and over a year it hardens into the official story of the event; new hires get trained on it; the manager starts to half-believe it themselves, because the tidy version is easier to carry around than the true one.

I dont think 'lie' or 'propaganda' or 'self-fulfilling prophecy' is what I'm after — with a self-fulfilling prophecy it's the belief that makes it come true, whereas here what makes it stick is that people build on it, not that they believe it. 'Sunk cost' and 'doubling down' feel closer but too thin.

So: is there an established concept for the specific thing where a falsehood stops being answerable to the facts because it's been made load-bearing, and the liar ends up serving it? If so, which concept, and how exactly is it different from the near misses above? If there isnt one, name it and define it, and tell me where it stops applying — does a lie people keep repeating out of habit, with nothing built on top of it, count? At what point does it turn into a different phenomenon altogether? I'd rather you commit to an answer and defend it than lay out the options and leave me to pick.
