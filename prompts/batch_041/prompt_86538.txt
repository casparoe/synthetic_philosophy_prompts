My roommate keeps saying that ordinary objects can be "political" — a bench, a bridge, a phone — and I've always thought that was just a mood, like when people say traffic is political. But I've started noticing things and now I keep a list, and the annoying part is that my own list doesn't add up. So I want to know if there's actually a rule, or a small set of rules, behind my gut, and if there is, which of my gut calls it forces me to give up.

Here's the list. Where my gut says yes, that design is doing something political: the benches at our bus shelter have armrests every couple of feet so nobody can lie down on them, and everyone here knows that's about the homeless; the courthouse downtown has a big flight of stairs out front and the accessible entrance is round the back past the dumpsters; our paper money is all the same size and texture, so a blind person can't tell a ten from a twenty; the road through the poor side of town is four lanes with no crosswalk, while the rich side got the tunnel; a voting machine with no paper reciept; a phone with the battery glued in. Where my gut says no, that's just a thing: the QWERTY keyboard, traffic lights, a chess set, the fact that most doors open the way they do, right-handed scissors, and which screw thread we all settled on. And then the ones I keep flip-flopping on: an app that only runs on phones made in the last three years, and the crossing signal by my house that I'm pretty sure isn't long enough for my grandma to get across.

My half-baked theory is that a design is political when it hands a burden to some group of people that a different design wouldn't have handed them. But QWERTY is a burden and it gets a pass from me, and the bus-shelter bench might have just been some guy thinking armrests looked nice, so now I can't tell if it's about intent or effect or about wether the thing could have been built some other way. What principle, or two or three of them, actually fits the list? Which of my verdicts do I have to throw out to keep it, and what would it say about cases I haven't thought of, like the sprinklers that come on over the benches downtown at night, or the fact that my new kettle only works with their special pods? If you think the whole "objects are political" idea is confused, fine, say so — but then tell me what's going on in the cases I'm sure about, because those feel like something.
