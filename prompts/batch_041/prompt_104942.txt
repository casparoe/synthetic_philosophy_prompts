I've been going too deep on the procreation asymmetry again — the intuition that it's wrong to bring a miserable person into existence but not required to bring a happy one into existence — and every time I try to build an actual theory around it, the thing explodes somewhere. I mostly care because of the longtermism angle (if creating happy people isn't required, that's a huge deal for how much weight the far future gets), so I want the foundations straight before I pick a team.

Before I read any more theories, give me the spec. What does a theory of population ethics actually have to deliver to count as adequate? I want the desiderata list — the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results it can't contradict — with the intuition or case behind each one, and your honest call on which of them are genuine requirements versus just one camps house style.

For the cases: it obviously has to handle the asymmetry itself, but also the non-identity stuff (where the act that leaves someone worse off is also the act that makes them exist at all, so you can't just say "it harmed them"), the repugnant conclusion, the mirror-image horror where adding miserable lives somehow beats adding happy-but-lower-welfare ones, and the plain weirdness of comparing outcomes that contain different numbers of people. Invent a couple of cases if it helps pin the theory down — say, a button that creates one very happy person, a button that creates a hundred mildly happy ones, and a button that creates nobody — and ask what's required, permitted, and forbidden, and whether the resulting orderings even have to be transitive.

For the distinctions: don't let it collapse "wrong" into "bad for someone," or "worse than nothing" into "bad," or "better for this particular person" into "better, full stop." And there's a live tension I keep tripping over, between wanting the theory to be person-affecting (an act is only wrong if it's bad for somebody) and wanting it to condemn things like trashing the world for future people who'd only exist because you trashed it.

Here's the part I most want: flag any subset of these that can't all be true at once. I have a nagging sense there's an impossibility result buried in here — like you can't simultaneously get the asymmetry, dodge the repugnant conclusion, avoid the sadistic mirror, keep transitivity, and stay person-affecting, and something has to give. If that's real, tell me exactly which requirements are in conflict, which one you'd sacrifice, and what the price of dropping each is. Skip grading the actual views, I'll do that once I've got the checklist.
