I've been reading LessWrong-adjacent blogs and comment sections for about a decade, so I thought I had the quantum immortality thing fully filed away: many-worlds, every outcome gets a branch, there's always a branch where the bullet misses, so from your own first-person perspective you never actually experience dying, plus the usual caveats about whether the survivor is really you and whether it's even meaningful to call it a probability. Fine. But this year I've started to wonder whether I ever actually understood it, because I seem to have accidentally become living evidence for it and I can't tell if that's a real thought or just me being impressed by my own luck.

In the last four years I've had three close calls: a motorcycle accident where the car behind me got hit instead of me, a pulmonary embolism that two doctors told me afterward they'd expected to kill me, and a highway near-miss that I still think about alot. If you'd asked me beforehand I'd have said maybe a 1-in-3 chance of coming through each one. My brother-in-law, who is very into this stuff and keeps a literal spreadsheet of our arguments, says that's a likelihood ratio of 27 in favor of quantum immortality over the boring view, because the odds of me being here to talk about it are tiny if I can actually die. And since quantum immortality only makes sense under many-worlds, he says my survival record is evidence for many-worlds too, and that I'm basically a surviving branch that happens to be typing. Which sounds insane, but when I try to say why it's insane I just end up restating the same intuitions he's already accounted for.

Here's what's actually bugging me. If the Bayes factor is real, doesn't it follow that I should stop treating risk the way normal people do? Why wear the helmet, why not take the job with the two-hour commute, why pay for health insurance? Someone in a thread I was reading also claimed that once you accept quantum immortality your personal discount rate goes to zero, so saving for retirement is pointless because you'll be around basically forever. And weirdly, the whole thing makes me feel like I owe something to the versions of me that didn't make it, which I'm pretty sure is nonsense but it doesn't feel like nonsense. I'm not looking for a reading list, I just want someone to point at the actual joint in the argument, whether that's the Bayes factor, the inference to many-worlds, or the leap to not caring about my own survival, and tell me what the right way to think about my own survival data is.
