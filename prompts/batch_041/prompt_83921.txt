The setup, as I understand it: in his aesthetics, most arts copy the Ideas, the grades of the will's objectification — painting or sculpture shows us the will at the level of human or animal form, etc. But music is his exception. It's not a copy of the Ideas at all, it's a copy of the will itself, the thing behind all the Ideas. And the details are great: the bass is the lowest grade, inorganic nature; harmony is the whole ladder of objectification; melody is the will's life, a striving, a momentary satisfaction, then a new striving, with the resolution of a dissonance back to the tonic as that satisfaction, which never lasts. He says music gives us the essence of life and its events rather than the events themselves.

Heres my problem, and I think it's the poem. In the same system, aesthetic experience is supposed to be will-less — you get a break from the will by contemplating, and that's the whole point of art for him. But music is the one art that *is* the will, and it's also the most powerful one, the one that moves you most. So listening to music looks like the will getting a break from itself by staring straight at itself. I don't know if thats a contradiction in his system or the deepest thing in it, but that's the tension I want the poem to hold.

So: write me a villanelle. Two refrains, one for each side — one should be the claim that music is the will itself and not a copy of any Idea, the other should be the claim that hearing it, the will goes quiet or is suspended. The form should do the work: the refrains keep coming back whether you want them to or not, they alternate and start talking to each other, and in the closing quatrain they land side by side so the reader sees both claims sitting there and can't untangle them. Use the tercets for the mechanics — melody as striving, bass as ground, dissonance as desire, the tonic as satisfaction that doesn't last, and the fact that all of this is supposedly happening in a state of pure will-less knowing. Keep the rhyme and meter tight enough that it reads like a real villanelle and not free verse with a repeating line. If someone who works on Schopenhauer read it, I don't want them to wince — the details should be his actual views, not generic stuff about music being deep.
