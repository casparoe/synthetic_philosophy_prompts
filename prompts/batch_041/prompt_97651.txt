Me and my friend got into it for like two hours last night about the restitution case our museum board has to vote on Thursday, and now I have to actually write the memo, so help me out. The case: a mid-sized civic museum holds four carved shrine figures taken in 1897 by a British officer during a punitive expedition, donated to us by his family in 1932. Two claimants. The national government of the country wants them in the national museum in the capital. The priestly lineage from the village they were taken from says they aren't property at all -- they were never meant to be owned or sold, so even the government's "return them to the nation" framing strikes them as the same mistake in a new suit -- and they want them back at the shrine. Trouble is, part of that same community actually wants them kept away from the shrine, because restored ritual use would mean they get worn down or destroyed. Our director's proposal is to transfer legal title to the community but keep the objects here on a renewable long-term loan with shared conservation. Half the board thinks thats real restitution, half thinks its a fudge that just lets us keep holding the goods. I need the written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting: a concrete recommendation with a time frame, the reasoning, and the strongest case for whichever side I come down against, taken seriously rather than knocked down. Skip the Parthenon Marbles and Benin Bronzes boilerplate, I've read all that. What I actually care about is whether title, custody and ritual use can come apart like this, and what we owe the community when they do.
