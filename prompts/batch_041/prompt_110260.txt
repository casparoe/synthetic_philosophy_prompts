"Al-Ghazali's occasionalism is basically the view that there are no real causal powers in nature. Fire doesn't burn cotton — God directly creates the burning each time. The label comes from the idea that natural events only line up occasionally in the patterns we expect, so what we call a law of nature is really just chance, and that's why he thinks we can never actually know what's going to happen next. His target was the philosophers' claim that the world runs on its own steam, by necessity; he wanted to leave room for miracles, so his move is to say God is the only real cause and can therefore interrupt the order of nature whenever he likes — and since no cause really constrains any effect, God could in principle do literally anything, contradiction or no. People call him the Hume of Islam because, like Hume, he points out that all we ever observe is one thing following another and never a necessary connection.
