I teach phil of mind and I'm running a small reading group next term on the Malcolm/Dennett fight over whether dreams are experiences at all — whether dreaming is something you undergo, or whether saying you dreamed something is closer to telling a story on waking than to reporting a sighting. Two meetings in I realized the room is trading verdicts without anyone having said what a theory of dreaming would have to do to earn one, so before we go back to Malcolm and Dennett I want the spec. Write me the desiderata for a theory of dream experience: what any account has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each requirement, and say which ones you think are non-negotiable and which are just the prejudices of a camp — I have a suspicion that a lot of what gets demanded here is verificationism wearing a lab coat, and I'd like to be told if that's unfair.

Some of what I already know I want on the list, so you can see the shape: it has to fix the relation between the dream and the report of it, given that reports are always given later from a waking state and often look filled in after the fact (I keep coming back to the alarm clock that turns up in the dream as a bell or a phone, and the fact that you can never tell whether the dream was already running and absorbed the noise or whether the whole thing got assembled in the second before waking); it has to handle the dreams we're sure we had and can't retrieve, and the fact that recall rates swing wildly; it has to do something with the lab facts — that people woken out of REM often report dreams but sometimes don't, that reports come out of non-REM sleep too, and that lucid dreamers can signal with prearranged eye movements mid-dream — without letting the lab settle what looks like a question about experience rather than report; and it has to say what it's doing when it attributes dreams to a dog or a newborn or a sleeping person who can't tell us anything, since that's where the whole thing either becomes a claim about behavior or a claim about experience. And the part I most want: flag any pair of requirements that can't be jointly satisfied, because I have a hunch that the requirement that reports are our only evidence and the requirement that we can meaningfully attribute dreams to creatures that can't report can't both be had, and if that's right then that's the seminar. Be explicit about what's actually settled among people who work on this and what's a live fight, and don't hand me a contested reading as the consensus. No bibliography, just the list and the reasoning, and please keep it in American English. Sorry for the long setup — the seperate requirements matter more than the verdict here.
