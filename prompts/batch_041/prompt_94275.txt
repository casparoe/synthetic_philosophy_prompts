I am helping design an accuracy-first framework for a forecasting platform, and before we commit to a scoring rule I want the full specification of what a theory of epistemic utility in this area has to deliver. Write the desiderata: the cases any adequate accuracy-based account must get right, the distinctions it cannot collapse, and the results it must remain consistent with, each paired with the consideration that motivates it. At a minimum, work through the following. First, the demand that a scoring rule be strictly proper, so that a subject maximizes expected accuracy only by reporting her actual credences: state what that requirement is really buying and what it leaves open. Second, the tension between scoring a single credence for one proposition and scoring an entire credence distribution over a partition, including whether the theory must treat the partition as fixed or allow the subject to choose it. Third, whether the account must extend to imprecise or interval-valued credences, and if so what "accuracy" could even mean for a set of probabilities rather than a point. Fourth, whether accuracy can be defined for credences about the scoring rule itself, as in a subject who assigns probabilities to which rule the platform will adopt and is then scored by whatever rule is chosen. Fifth, whether the theory must say something about the expected accuracy of the credences a subject would hold after further inquiry, not merely the credences she holds now. For each requirement, say whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp, and flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied; that conflict is the part I most want to see. Then, briefly, how a quadratic (Brier-style) score, a logarithmic score, and a truthlikeness-style measure fare against the list, with the understanding that the scoring is secondary to the criteria. Please write in American English.
