Next month I am giving the commencement address at my alma mater, a women's college, and I want to use the occasion to be honest with the graduating class about a result in my own field — the one that is usually called the gender-equality paradox. I work on this, so I am not neutral about it, and I do not want the address to become a brief for my own view.

The finding, stated as I would state it to people who have never heard of it: on several measures that have been reported across countries — the proportion of women among graduates in math-intensive fields, average sex differences in interest in working with things rather than with people, and sex differences on some personality measures — the differences come out larger, not smaller, in countries that score higher on indices of gender equality and economic development. The pattern itself has been reported often enough that the interpretation, not the pattern, is what is in dispute.

Three readings of it seem to me to be live, and I want the address to keep all three open rather than quietly settling on one:

- the discovery reading: where external constraints are lifted, what remains is preference, and preferences differ on average between the sexes;
- the expressive reading: where a career stops being mainly a means of provisioning and becomes a means of self-description, the vocabulary available for self-description is already gendered, so greater freedom produces more sorting — on this reading the pattern is evidence of culture working harder, not of nature showing through;
- the artifact reading: the indices, the field classifications and the personality instruments are carrying more of the result than the headline suggests, and there is less here than meets the eye.

What I need from you is the address itself, written to be delivered, not read. Please observe the following constraints, which are the point of the exercise:

1. No names of researchers, no titles of studies, no statistics, and no dates. I will be at a podium, I cannot source anything, and a large part of this audience would catch me if I got a number wrong. Everything must be said in a form that survives not being sourced.
2. Avoid the three ready-made lines: that the barriers are gone, so whatever pattern a graduate finds in her own life must be her own preference; that the science has settled the question in favor of nature; and that the finding is a myth and may be ignored. Each of these closes the question, and closing it is the one thing I am sure is wrong.
3. The advice must be invariant. Whatever I tell them about how to choose, how to want, and how to treat people who choose differently has to hold under all three readings. Advice that only works under one of them is a bet, not advice, and I am not willing to stake their twenties on my side of a live dispute.
4. The ending must not resolve the dispute or offer a consolation that depends on its being resolved. I would rather leave them with a way of holding the question than with an answer.
5. Include one short concrete scene, told as mine, so the abstraction has somewhere to land.
6. It has to land for two audiences at once: the faculty, several of whom work on exactly this and one of whom was on my dissertation committee and thinks the paradox is mostly a measurement artifact, and the parents and grandparents, who have heard versions of this argument in the culture war and will hear any hint of biological determinism or of sociology-as-excuse. No jargon, except that terms like things and people are self-explanatory and may stay.

Deliver a seven-minute address of roughly 950 to 1100 words. After the address, give me a compact JSON object, with no commentary around it, using exactly these fields:

- "address": the full text of the address, as a single string.
- "readings": an array of three objects, one per reading, each with "name"; "what_the_pattern_would_show_if_true"; "what_would_have_to_hold_for_it"; "implication_for_justice"; and "does_the_address_lean_on_it", one of "yes", "no", or "leans".
- "claims_relied_on": an array of objects, one per substantive claim the address makes, each with "claim_as_stated"; "kind", one of "empirical", "interpretive", or "normative"; "status", one of "well-supported", "contested", "possibly-measurement-artifact", or "unknown"; "licenses", stating what a careful listener is entitled to conclude from it; and "does_not_license", stating what they are not.
- "invariance_check": an array of objects, one per distinct piece of advice the address gives, each with "piece_of_advice" and "holds_under", a list of the reading names under which it still holds.
- "deliberately_omitted": an array of objects, one per thing I chose not to say, each with "omission" and "reason".
- "bottom_line": an object with the fields "nature", "culture", and "justice", each holding a single sentence stating what, on the address's own showing, does and does not follow about it.
