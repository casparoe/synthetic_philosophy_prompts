ok so I'm a cog sci PhD student and this is definately a procrastination-adjacent question, but I actually need it for a chapter, so bear with me.

There's a thought experiment of Dennett's, the careenium, that I keep getting stuck on. Here's the setup in case it's not in your memory: a frictionless table covered in a huge number of tiny magnetic things called simms, all jiggling around. When two simms hit each other slowly enough they stick, and clusters form. A cluster is a simmball; it can be made of millions of simms and it may loose or gain simms at its boundary. The simmballs drift and knock into each other, and the whole thing is rigged so that the simmball-level activity implements a game of checkers — the arrangements of simmballs carry the board state, their collisions are the moves. No homunculus, no designer writing symbols down. The symbolic level is supposed to emerge from the simmball dynamics.

Here's the fight I want staged. One person holds that the simmballs are real and that they push the substrate around — the symbolic level is genuinely causal, not a summary we write after the fact. The other holds that no, the simms push the simmballs and that's the whole story; the simmball is a pattern we find useful, but every actual shove is a sim-level event, so the symbols are at best a convenient abstraction and at worst epiphenomenal. Dennett's own move is to say the pushing goes both ways, and I want to know whether that's a real position or a dodge.

Write the dialogue between these two. Both have to be sharp. I don't want the micro-level person to be a grump who just says "but atoms" over and over, and I don't want the pattern person to win by gesturing at emergence and hoping nobody asks a follow-up. Things I'd like them to actually get into, in whatever order feels natural:

- the intervention problem: if you nudge a simmball with a wand, you've also changed the simms underneath it, so does the simmball-level nudge count as a cause in its own right or is it just a coarse label for a big set of micro-causes? And is there a principled way to tell a good coarse-graining from a gerrymandered one?
- multiple realizability: if the same checkers program could run on a completely different arrangement of simmballs, or on a different substrate entirely, does the program level get any causal standing, or only explanatory standing? Is that distinction even stable?
- the vague boundary: simmballs bleed simms at the edge and can gain or lose huge numbers of them, so what makes it the same simmball over time, and does that matter for whether it can be a cause at all?
- whether "both levels push" is a coherent claim or a category mistake, and what exactly would have to be true for downward causation to amount to more than a redescription of a subset of micro-trajectories
- the real-patterns move, where a pattern counts as real relative to our computational limits — if that's the standard, has causation quietly become observer-relative, and is that a cost or just the truth?

Let them land somewhere, even if it's only a sharpened disagreement. Don't have them converge on "well it depends what you mean by cause" in the first five lines and then just chat amiably; I want the disagreement to survive the whole exchange. Maybe 900-1400 words. If one of them has a better argument than the other, fine, but let the other one give the best reply available.
