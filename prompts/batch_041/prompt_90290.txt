I'm putting together the opening five minutes of a graduate reading-group session on lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, and I've been handed the icebreaker. I'd like you to write me a joke — one joke, properly told, with a setup and a landing — that a room of people who know this material would actually laugh at, and that would fall completely flat if I tried it on someone who didn't. No explanatory afterword: the joke has to carry its own premise, because the audience is a seminar room with no phones out.

For the session we're working with a particular gloss of the view, and the joke should turn on that structure rather than on generic "negative utilitarians are gloomy" material. The gloss: there is a threshold level of suffering such that any episode at or above the threshold has lexical priority over pleasure — no quantity of pleasure, however vast, can compensate for it — whereas below the threshold, pleasure and pain are commensurable and can be traded against one another at some exchange rate. The theory is therefore a hybrid with a lexical zone and an arithmetic zone, and everything hard about it lies in where the line falls and whether it can be drawn non-arbitrarily. What I want the joke to dramatize is the strangeness that structure generates: that the very same twinge can be either unbuyable or trivially cheap depending on which side of the line it lands on; that a marginal nudge across the threshold flips the verdict from "no compensation is possible" to "let me check the current rate"; that two agents who agree about every concrete case except the height of the line will disagree about nearly everything; that a lifetime of small pleasures can be either an enormous moral consideration or literally nothing, and nobody can tell you which without first settling a question no one can settle.

Feel free to set it anywhere mundane — a job interview, an insurance claim, a couple negotiating a holiday, a bar, a customer-service call — and to invent whatever institutional furniture the premise needs. A threshold-adjustment bureau that adjudicates, case by case, which side of the line a given pain falls on strikes me as promising, as does a posted hedonic exchange-rate board, an appraisal form, or a tribunal that has been in continuous session since some absurdly early date. A short piece of prose or a two-hander of dialogue are both fine; what I don't want is a listicle of one-liners, a pun that would work just as well for any ethical theory, or an "imagine if..." sketch that never actually lands a punchline.
