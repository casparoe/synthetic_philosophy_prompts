I've worked out a position on certainty and knowledge more or less on my own, and before I go any further with it I want to know whether it actually hangs together or whether I'm quietly contradicting myself. Here is where I've ended up.

First, I think there are some things I'm more sure of than anything I could ever use to establish them: that I have two hands, that the world didn't come into being five minutes ago complete with all my memories, that the people around me have minds. If someone asks me for a reason for one of these, every reason I could give already takes it for granted. They aren't conclusions I've reached; they're the ground I'm standing on while reaching everything else. And because of that, I don't think I can properly be said to know them, since knowing seems to require having some support behind you, and these have none.

Second, I think the whole business of assessing beliefs — asking for evidence, weighing arguments, refusing to just assert — is the only honest way to hold any belief at all. A belief I can't give reasons for is, to that extent, a belief I have no business holding.

Third, I'm not a relativist. I think some of these ground-level commitments are better than others. Someone who is certain the earth is flat, or certain that some race is inferior, isn't simply living in a different world from me; they are worse off, and it would be a genuine improvement if they abandoned those commitments. I don't think every framework is as good as every other.

Fourth, I think that if someone genuinely doesn't share one of my ground-level commitments, there is no argument I could offer that would move them, because every argument I'd give them is something they already reject. We aren't disagreeing about a fact; we're standing in different places.

Fifth, I think science has overturned things that people were once absolutely certain of, and that it may do so again, including things I now find it impossible to doubt.

Sixth, I do take myself to know plenty of ordinary things — that there's a chair behind me, that water boils at sea level — and I'd push back hard on anyone who told me those are merely probable or merely believed.

I have a suspicion that these don't all fit together, but I honestly can't see where the pressure is. Please sort out for me which of these are genuinely in conflict, which only look like they are, and how serious each real conflict is. Rank the real ones by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. For each genuine conflict, tell me the cheapest way to fix it and what I'd be giving up by fixing it that way, and then tell me which single change would resolve the most at once. Don't just lay out the options and leave the choice to me — take a definite position on what I should abandon, and give your reasons.
