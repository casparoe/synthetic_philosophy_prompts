I'm running a reading group on the gender-equality paradox and i want to close with a sestina that makes the thing feel like a conceptual knot, not a headline. Six end-words: nature, culture, choice, justice, equal, gap. Each stanza should try one escape route -- it's biology, it's socialisation, it's rational adaptation to different constraints, it's just what justice should care about -- and the form should keep dragging the same words back so none of them can be retired. The twist I want: the poem should end by suggesting that if preferences are partly made by the institutions that measure them, then the paradox doesn't tell us whether nature or culture wins; it tells us that the distinction may be doing the work.
