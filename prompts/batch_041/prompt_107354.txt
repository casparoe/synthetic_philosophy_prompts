In the decades after the American Civil War, the freedpeople of the South were, in law, no longer anyone's property: they could marry, move, sign contracts, and sue. Yet the world they entered was governed by Black Codes, vagrancy statutes, crop-lien and sharecropping arrangements, and, in places, convict leasing — a web of law and contract that bound them to land and employers and was enforced by courts, sheriffs, and the threat of the chain gang. Analyze this episode through the three families of liberty you find in the literature: negative liberty as non-interference, positive liberty as self-mastery or self-realization, and republican liberty as non-domination in the neo-Roman sense associated with Pettit and Skinner.

Map the case as carefully as the theory demands. Who is the dominator here, and is there one? What is the difference between being interfered with and being subject to another's arbitrary power, and which of the two better describes a sharecropper whose debts and contracts leave him no lawful exit? What does the distinction say about the black landowner who is left alone by the sheriff but knows the law could be turned on him at any moment — and does the difference between the secure citizen and the lucky dependent do real work in this case, or is it a philosopher's refinement? Then run the analysis the other way. What does the episode expose about republican liberty: does it vindicate the claim that non-domination is a distinct and superior ideal, or does it reveal a gap — for instance, that domination here is impersonal and structural rather than the arbitrary will of an identifiable master, or that the remedy republicans recommend (contestation, the rule of law, the ballot) is precisely what the episode shows to be unavailable? Compare what the positive-liberty reading sees that the other two miss — self-mastery, collective self-rule, the social conditions of agency — and say whether that reading illuminates the freedpeople's predicament or merely redescribes it. I am less interested in a verdict on which conception is correct than in a disciplined case analysis: where each conception cuts, where it falls silent, and what the episode asks of the theory that the theory has not yet answered.
