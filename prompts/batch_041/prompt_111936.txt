So my mate Dan and I somehow spent like two and a half hours last night arguing about why god never actually says anything to anyone, and now I can't let it go. I want to write something about it. Not a rant though — a proper satirical bit, the kind that only works if the theology is definately right. Can you write it.

What I want: a Help Centre page, the deadpan corporate support-site kind, except the "company" is a church and the "product" is a god who has, as far as the record shows, never once spoken to a single user. The voice is a completely unflappable Office of Divine Communications answering support tickets. It stays in character the whole way through — no winking, no nudges, no author stepping out to say "see what I did there". Deadpan.

The humour has to come from the real moves people make in this debate, not from making the believers stupid. So please make the page do these things, all of which are actual positions:

- Keep treating the two problems as if they're one problem — as if "why is there suffering" and "why is there silence" were the same ticket category — while every answer only ever addresses the first one.
- Keep insisting, in every single answer, that anyone who reports not believing is secretly resisting, which is exactly the premise everyone fights about. Ideally one answer has the agent cheerfully admit they can't actually verify whether a non-resisting nonbeliever has ever existed, and then move on.
- Run the "if we just appeared in the sky you'd only believe out of fear, and that wouldn't be worth anything" line.
- Run the "you're at exactly the right distance to grow into the relationship" line.
- Run the "the silence is actually a gift, we're protecting you from something" line.
- Have a ticket asking how much evidence would be enough, and answer it without ever naming a number.
- Have one entry flip it around and suggest the user is the one hiding, and that the evidence is right there for anyone sincere.
- Treat scripture and miracles as the case file, without ever noticing that a reasonable person could doubt exactly that kind of evidence.
- End with a "still not satisfied?" footer that quietly gives something away.

Someone who's never heard of this problem should still follow it and laugh; someone who's read a bit should recognise their own arguments being gently held up to the light. Target the official spin, and the style of answer that treats the whole thing as a non-question — not any real person or church.

Format it like this:

Section 1 — "The Page". Around 700 words. A title, a short intro blurb, then six to eight numbered support entries, each with a question as the user would ask it and an answer in the comms-team voice, then the closing footer.

Section 2 — "What I was actually doing". Around 200 words, plain English, no jargon: which real positions each bit was satirising, and which objections the page leaves standing at the end.

Don't put any real quotes or citations in it, I don't want it leaning on sources. And don't resolve it — I want Dan to read it and not be sure whose side it's on.
