ok so my reading group is doing a session on hofstadters careenium thing -- the giant frictionless table with zillions of tiny marbles (simmballs) knocking around, where clusters of them settle into semi-stable patterns that start acting like symbols, and the sneaky question is whether those symbol-patterns are actually shoving the marbles around or whether its marbles all the way down and the symbol talk is just a free description we lay on top. i want to open the session with a poem, 14-18 lines, and i want the form itself to carry the argument rather than the poem just explaining it.

here's the constraint: rhymed couplets, no stanza breaks, where each couplet is ONE event described twice. first line strictly in the substrate register -- angles, contact, momentum, deformation, no purpose words, no mental words, nothing that says a thing wanted or meant anything. second line strictly in the symbol register -- inference, meaning, mistake, remembering, expecting. the two lines of a couplet have to share a rhyme, so the two descriptions are welded to the same sound even though theyre the same collision seen twice. rule: the symbol line cant reuse any content word from the substrate line (function words are fine, rhyme words will of course differ since theyre different words).

let one concrete physical detail recur the whole way through: a dent in the wall of the table that never fully springs back. it should read as both just a permanently deformed surface and the only thing in there that remembers anything, and i dont want you to tell me which one it is.

at the end i want a turn that someone who knows the debate would recognize: the worry that if the two lines are the same event twice described, then the symbol line isnt an extra cause, its a redescription, and everything that got pushed was pushed by marbles. then i want the last couplet to leave it open instead of settling it, in a way that makes clear that "same event, two descriptions" isnt automatically a demotion -- because whether the top line shoves the bottom one might be a badly posed question rather than a question with a deflationary answer. dont let it slide into a lecture, keep the marbles visible the whole way, and no capital-T Thesis in the final line. if you need to name hofstadter or the careenium, put it in the title, not the body.
