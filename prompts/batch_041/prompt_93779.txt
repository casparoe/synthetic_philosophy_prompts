I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, working on levels of explanation and the causal status of higher-level patterns, and I've hit a snag I'd like your help with. Hofstadter's careenium — the toy world from I Am a Strange Loop (2007) — is familiar ground for me, so let me set out the setup as I understand it and then give you a summary of it that I keep running into. The careenium is a box of tiny magnetic marbles, "simms," bouncing around under simple mechanical rules; because the simms attract one another, they clump into larger, comparatively sluggish structures, "simmballs," which interact in ways that can be described in their own terms, cluster shoving cluster, and whose configurations persist long enough to carry traces of what has happened in the box. Hofstadter's question is which level does the pushing: do the simms push the simmballs around, or the other way about?

A popular book on consciousness that I've been reading gives its answer like this: "The point of the careenium is that the simmballs are not real causes. Every simm's motion is fixed by the mechanics of the box, so once the initial conditions are set, nothing at the cluster level makes any difference to what happens next; what looks like one simmball shoving another is just a convenient summary of an enormous number of simm-level collisions. The careenium therefore settles whether symbols push the substrate around — they do not. Symbols, meanings, and the level of ideas are descriptions we find useful, not forces. Since Hofstadter wants strange loops to be causally real, his own toy model tells against him."

I suspect this is unfair, but I can't say precisely where. Could you go through the summary claim by claim and sort it into what a philosopher of mind or cognitive scientist would accept, what they would wince at, and what is simply wrong? Second, whether the summary is using "symbol" in the sense the careenium thought experiment actually needs, or trading on an everyday sense of the term. Third, whether the closing charge that Hofstadter's strange loops are inconsistent with his own toy model survives once the earlier claims are corrected.
