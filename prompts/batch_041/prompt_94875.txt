I sit on the commission that runs local elections in my city, and we keep having to draw the line on who counts as one of us for voting purposes. Our record so far: non-citizen residents who've been here five years or more — yes, they vote; citizens who moved abroad and kept no property or family here — no vote; adults under full guardianship for a cognitive disability — yes, they vote; people who own a second home here and pay our property tax but live somewhere else — no vote; 16- and 17-year-olds — yes, in municipal elections. I couldnt tell you what rule we've actually been running, and honestly I'm not sure there is one.

Now we've got a referendum coming on a flood-defence scheme, and the town across the river — same country, different municipality — is going to get hit by it: our reservoir changes their water levels and their insurance premiums, and the way the scheme is drafted our water utility would bill their households a small annual levy for the works. They're not on our electoral roll, our bylaws dont bind them, and their own council pays for their own mitigation. Should they get to vote in our referendum? Tell me what we should do to stay consistent with ourselves, what the candidate principles are and what each one predicts here, which of our past calls is the closest analog, and if the record really doesn't settle it, what we'd be buying with each choice — plus what further case would best show which principle we're really running.
