I'm on the trustee board of a small grantmaking foundation and we've got to decide something by the end of the month. Our founder's will says the money should go to whatever our beneficiaries would choose if they knew everything we know and had thought it through properly, not what they happen to want right now, and the deed lays out a procedure for getting at that: a paid panel of beneficiaries, given proper briefings and as long as they need, deliberates and their verdict is meant to settle the matter. We ran the procedure twice last year, changing one setting in the protocol (whether the panel was told the size of the endowment — sounds trivial, but it changed how they framed everything), and the two panels landed on opposite sides of our biggest grant, a harm-reduction programme. On top of that the beneficiaries as a whole just voted against funding it two to one, in a poll we probably shouldn't have run. Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote: a clear recommendation (fund it, don't, or fund with conditions), the reasoning, and a date or trigger for when we revisit it. Be honest about the strongest thing to be said for whichever side you come down against, and dont dodge the awkward bit — I think that's what to do when our best shot at the 'idealized' answer and the actual people it's meant to be for disagree. Write it in British English, keep it readable for trustees who aren't philosophers, and no citations needed.
