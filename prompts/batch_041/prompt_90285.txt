I am drafting a position paper on causation by omission and want a consistency audit of my commitments before I put them in print. Here they are, numbered so that you can refer to them:

1. Singular causation is counterfactual dependence: something is a cause of an outcome just in case, had it not occurred, the outcome would not have occurred (with the usual refinements for pre-emption).
2. Omissions are not causes. Absences are not events, and only events stand in causal relations; when we say that a failure caused a harm, we are making a claim about responsibility in causal idiom.
3. Causation is a mind-independent feature of the world, and the line between a cause and a mere background condition is drawn by the world rather than by our explanatory interests.
4. The criminal law is right to hold people liable for certain omissions and to record those omissions as causes of the harm; the existence of a duty to act is what makes the difference.
5. Responsibility for an outcome requires that the agent could have prevented it, that is, that the outcome counterfactually depended on her conduct.
6. The distracted lifeguard is responsible for the drowning; the indifferent sunbather is not. I want to keep that verdict.

I suspect these cannot all stand together, but I do not know which of them are genuinely pulling against one another and which only appear to. Please audit the set: say which pairs or triples genuinely conflict, which apparent conflicts dissolve once the right distinction is drawn, rank the genuine ones by severity, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost. Do not flag every pair — only those that actually interact.

Return your audit as a single JSON object, in British English and with no text outside the object, using exactly these keys:

- "tensions": an array, ordered from most to least severe, of the combinations of my commitments that interact. Each element is an object with:
  - "commitments": an array of the numbers of the commitments involved, e.g. [1, 2];
  - "status": either "genuine" or "apparent";
  - "severity": an integer from 1 to 5, where 5 is a flat contradiction and 1 is a tension that barely bites (for an apparent tension, rate how tempting the misreading is);
  - "diagnosis": at most three sentences on what forces the conflict, or, for an apparent tension, what the surface conflict is;
  - "resolution": at most three sentences — for a genuine tension, the cheapest revision and what it costs; for an apparent tension, the distinction that dissolves it.
- "most_economical_revision": at most two sentences naming the single change that would clear up the most genuine tensions at once, and what it would cost.
- "overall_verdict": at most two sentences.
