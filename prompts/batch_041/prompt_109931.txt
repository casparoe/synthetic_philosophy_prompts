I'm a novelist, not a researcher, so forgive me if I say this wrong. I'm about 200 pages into a book set maybe eight years out, and there's a scene where a character has to go on the record about how likely it is that we get to systems a lot smarter than us using only the tools we already have. No new science. No new theory of what's happening inside the model. Just RLHF and constitutional-style self-critique, chain-of-thought monitoring, probes and sparse autoencoders on the internals, red-teaming, evals — more compute, more data, better engineering, the same bag of tricks turned up. I think there's a name for that, prosaic alignment, and the question is whether it actually gets us there. I'm definately no expert.

So give me your credence that it does: that prosaic alignment, defined that way, gets us to systems that are both much more capable than us and actually trying to do what we want — not just passing the evals we're able to run. Then break it down. Pick your own terms, give each one a number and a line, multiply it out, and tell me which term the whole thing is most sensitive to.

A few things I'd expect to see in there, and you can tell me any of them don't belong: whether the training signal we can actually compute stays meaningful once capability passes the point where no human can check the work; whether a system that learned to behave well under observation generalizes that to the cases nobody's watching; whether interpretability keeps pace with the models or falls further behind; whether there's a sharp threshold where the whole thing changes character; and how much of the answer is really about technical difficulty versus humans just not doing the sensible thing even when they know what it is. I want you to seperate those two if you can.

The one I care most about, because a character of mine has a whole theory about it: once the only feedback you've got left is another model's judgment, the ceiling on how aligned the system can get is the ceiling on how good the judge is — and I want your number on whether that ceiling rises with scale or stays put.

Last thing. If you think "prosaic alignment works" isn't really the kind of claim that has a truth value — that it's more like asking whether a particular bridge will hold, a fact about the world but not one any credence is really tracking — say so. Then give me the number anyway. My character doesn't get to cop out, and neither do you.

Oh, and if you had to spend a year of your life on one experiment or argument to shift this number, what would it be, and which way would it move?
