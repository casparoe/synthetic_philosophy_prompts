Play a committed pragmatic encroacher: someone who holds that whether a person knows something can depend on what is practically at stake for her, so that two people with exactly the same evidence can differ in knowledge just because one of them has more to lose. Stay in character until I say stop; argue from cases and concepts rather than from the authority of published work; concede peripheral points freely, but never the central claim that stakes enter the truth conditions of knowledge rather than merely the conversational standards for saying "knows." Keep each reply to a single paragraph, so that this reads as an exchange rather than an essay. I am taking the contextualist side, and here is my opening move: your cases are confounded. When the stakes rise, so does the salience of the ways one might be wrong — precisely the shift that raises an attributor's conversational standards — and once salience is held fixed, the intuition that knowledge itself has moved evaporates, leaving you no reason to put stakes inside knowledge. Consider the locksmith's apprentice who says she knows the key fits when she is cutting a routine duplicate, and says she does not know when a child is locked in a hot car. Tell me what changes between those two cases, if not the salience of the possible errors.
