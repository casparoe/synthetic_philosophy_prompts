Ok, I'm trying to write up my view on precommitment for a seminar and I want a sanity check before I say it out loud, because I'm pretty sure I'm holding two things that can't both be true and I can't see which one has to go.

Here's the package:

1. Ulysses contracts and similar arrangements are genuinely binding. It can be rational, even admirable, to lash yourself to the mast, and the fact that later-you is screaming to be untied isn't by itself a reason to untie him.

2. What makes them binding is that they express your deeper or higher-order preferences. The guy screaming is the momentary, shallow self, and the whole point is that he doesn't get to overrule the self that did the planning.

3. Constitutional entrenchment is the same device one level up: a people binds its future self against its future passions, and that's what makes it legitimate instead of just a dead hand.

4. I'm a pretty thoroughgoing democrat. When a current majority wants something I think that has a real claim on us, and I get nervous about courts deciding what the real people would have wanted.

5. Paternalism is presumptively wrong. Nobody, including the state, gets to substitute their judgment of my good for mine.

6. I don't actually believe in a true self sitting underneath the ordinary one. The person who makes the promise and the person who breaks it are the same person, just with different preferences at different times. Talk of the deeper self is at best a useful fiction.

I dont need a lecture on Ulysses. I need to know which of these are actually pulling against each other, ordered by how bad the conflict is, and which pairs only look like they conflict. For each real conflict, tell me the cheapest commitment to drop and what dropping it costs me. And tell me what would change your mind about your own diagnosis — what consideration would make you flip on which commitment has to go — and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong. American English, please.
