My wife and I just finished an IVF cycle and we've got four embryos in the freezer, and this whole process has sent me down a philosophical rabbit hole I can't climb out of. The clinic, our parents, everyone talks about the embryos like they're interchangeable — "whichever one you put back, that'll be your kid" — but that seems backwards to me. If we'd transferred a different one, a different person would be sitting here typing this. So the kid isn't some empty slot that gets filled in; the kid just is whoever grows out of the one we pick.

Then I look at myself and ask: could I have come from a different sperm and a different egg? Not a person with my genes — me. Gut says obviously not, but gut also says the embryo thing above is obviously true, and I can't tell if those are the same intuition or two seperate ones quietly contradicting each other. Also my cousin is a chimera, two embryos that fused into one person, and if you ask which sperm and egg she came from the answer is basically "both," which I'd have thought blows the whole thing up, though maybe it doesn't.

So write me an essay on whether origin essentialism — the claim that you couldn't have originated from different gametes — is really true, or just an intuition trick, using my two cases. Weigh the strongest arguments on each side, and say what the hell the deal is with identical twins, since they come from one sperm and one egg and are definately not the same person. Land on a verdict, don't end on "it depends."
