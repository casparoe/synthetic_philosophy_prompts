My little sister is getting married in June and I'm the best man, so the toast is on me. She's a set theorist — that's her actual job — and her fiance teaches middle school orchestra and I'm pretty sure he still thinks "poset" is a typo. Half the room will be her department, who will be grading the toast whether they admit it or not, and the other half will be his family, who have never heard of any of this. I'm a physical therapist, so I'm definately going to garble the details out loud, but I want the toast itself to be right.

Here's what I want to build it on: Zorn's lemma. If every chain in a partially ordered set has an upper bound, then the set has a maximal element. A chain is just a bunch of elements where any two of them can be compared; "maximal" means nothing in the set extends it — not that it's the best one, since there might be other elements it can't even be compared to. And the way people actually use it is to take all the partial versions of the thing they're trying to build, order them by "extends," notice that any chain of partial versions has an upper bound (usually you just take the union of the whole chain), and that buys you a maximal element — existence without a recipe. It's equivalent to the axiom of choice, and it only works if the upper bound is really in the set, which is the part everyone forgets.

Write me the toast. Four minutes max, American English. It should be warm and funny enough that the orchestra teacher's family gets everything they need without any math in it, and correct enough that nobody in her department winces. The lemma shouldn't be a gag I open with and then drop — I want the whole thing standing on it.
