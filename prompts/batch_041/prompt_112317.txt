Write a satirical piece of roughly 700 words in the form of an excerpt from a rationing handbook: the chapter in which a public health authority in an invented country explains to its members how it decides which treatments to fund. The authority has adopted, as its official welfare standard, a version of lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism. On that view, suffering and enjoyment are incommensurable — there is no common unit in which the two can be added or exchanged — and a threshold of suffering is fixed such that once a patient's suffering rises above it, no amount of enjoyment or other benefit conferred on anyone can outweigh relieving that suffering, while below the threshold the ordinary cost-effectiveness rules continue to apply.

Write from inside the institution, in the deadpan voice of a clinical-priorities officer setting out the rules for members, and never wink at the reader. Every absurd outcome must follow from the framework's own rules rather than from the stupidity, malice, or self-interest of the officials, who should come across as competent and sincere. Invent whatever authority, committee, and document names you need; do not refer to real people, real health systems, or real published works, and do not quote or cite anything.

Give the piece numbered sections with headings and at least one worked comparison of two patients. Let the following features of the view do the comic work, without ever announcing that they are objections:

- the threshold itself, its arbitrary value, and the committee that quietly revises it every few years;
- the fact that the framework denies that pleasure and pain share a scale while nevertheless needing a single scale on which to place the threshold;
- the two-ledger accounting, in which suffering and enjoyment are recorded separately and can never be traded off against each other, together with the escalation rule that applies when a case crosses the line;
- the consequences: effectively unlimited spending to relieve one patient's suffering above the line, the refusal of cheap relief below it, and the reallocation of the entire budget that follows;
- the difficulty of comparing suffering across different people, and of measuring it at all;
- the possibility that, depending on where the line is drawn, the framework is either doing no work or swallowing every other consideration.

A specialist in negative welfarism should be able to see that the author understands the position rather than attacking a caricature, and a reader who has never heard of it should still find the piece funny and coherent. Do not resolve the tension at the end.
