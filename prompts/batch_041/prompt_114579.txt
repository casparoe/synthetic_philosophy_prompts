I'm building out a personal philosophical profile for a seminar and want a consistency audit before I commit to it in writing. Here is where I've landed. (1) Phenomenal properties are real and non-physical: a complete account of my brain in physical terms would still leave out what my experience is like, and no functional or structural description captures the redness of red. (2) Epiphenomenalism: phenomenal properties are causally inert — the physical facts are causally closed, and everything I say and do, including everything I say about my experiences, is fully caused by physical states. (3) My introspective judgments about my current experience are non-accidentally true, and count as knowledge. (4) Introspection is a kind of inner perception: I have a faculty whose outputs are representations caused by the very states they report. (5) Content is causal: what a mental state represents is fixed by the causal relations it bears to the things it represents, so a state is about whatever it is causally sensitive to. (6) Phenomenal consciousness is a biological adaptation — it exists because natural selection favored it.

Please audit this set: which commitments are genuinely in tension with which, which pairs only look like they conflict but don't, and for each genuine tension, how severe it is and what the cheapest revision would be and what that revision costs. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and tell me which single change would resolve the most.
