I'm teaching the liberty unit again next term and I want to open the session on republicanism with a joke, because every year the students nod along alot at "non-domination" and then go straight back to thinking freedom just means nobodys currently bossing you around. So write me a joke. One that actually lands, i.e. one whose punchline only works if you've got the real distinction and not just the slogan.

So you know my version of the distinction rather than having to guess at it: on the republican view what makes you unfree is standing in someone else's arbitrary power, whether or not they ever actually use it. A master who never lifts a finger against you is still a master and you're still unfree, and "but he's so nice to me" is not a reply. The other half is the bit the students find hardest: actual interference isn't automatically a loss of freedom either. If the interference answers to you, or is under your control, or tracks your interests in the right way, the republican doesn't count it as domination at all, so a tax you voted for isn't tyranny and a soldier who was conscripted by a democracy isn't the same as one who was press-ganged. The joke has to turn on one or both of those, not on "politicians are controlling, am I right".

Two things I'd rather you steer clear of. Not the slave and master case, that's the one everybody has heard and it gets a chuckle of recognition and nothing else. And not the abstract state-versus-individual thing either, I want something small and everyday: a boss, a landlord, a committee, a neighbor, whatever. A short dialogue is fine if that's the only shape it works in. Don't explain the joke afterwards either, if it needs explaining it isn't one, though one line of setup at the top is fine if the scenario needs it.

Last thing: I need to be able to read it aloud and paste it into my slides, so no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables. Plain prose, a few sentences of setup and then the punchline.
