My ethics reading group keeps rehashing this argument a friend wrote up, and I can't tell if it refutes suffering-focused ethics or just his crude version of it. The view under attack: suffering is the one thing that's intrinsically bad, and what we ought to do is minimize it.

(1) If suffering is the only intrinsic bad, the right act is whichever one minimizes suffering.
(2) A universe with no sentient beings contains no suffering.
(3) So a universe with no sentient beings beats any universe that has suffering in it, suffering-wise.
(4) There's a button that painlessly and instantly annihilates every sentient being and prevents any new ones — pushing it gets us to the universe in (2).
(5) So the view says we should push the button.
(6) That's monstrous.
(7) So suffering isn't the only intrinsic bad after all.

He blames (1). I dont think the repair is cheap: if you tweak (1) so the button is forbidden, you seem to give up what suffering-focused people care about most, namely that it's good to keep future sufferers from ever existing even when nobody would be worse off if they did. Tell me which step actually breaks, why it looked plausible, and what it costs to give it up. Don't just tell me to restrict the principle somehow — say which premise that lands on and what I lose either way. American English please.
