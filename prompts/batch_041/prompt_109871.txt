I got assigned to defend the position that Kant was right that you shouldn't lie to the murderer at the door, even if your best friend is hiding upstairs. I know that sounds insane, so I'm definately not a Kant expert, but I'm running a slightly weird version of it: the murderer's question isn't a real request for information, it's a threat, and a threat isn't the kind of conversation where the duty of truthfulness even applies. So you don't owe him the truth. But I still think you shouldn't lie, because lying makes you someone who treats speech as a weapon, and that's a betrayal of humanity in yourself. My move is that you can refuse to answer, or give a true but misleading answer, and that's not a lie. I also read somewhere that Kant says if you lie and your friend still ends up dead, you're responsible, which sounds like he's sneaking in consequences, so I'm not sure what to do with that. And I saw a comment that Kant's 'right to truth' isn't a right to every true fact, it's just a right not to be deceived by someone who owes you sincerity—so the murderer doesn't have it.

The audience is mostly kids from my grade who've watched a ton of YouTube ethics videos and think utilitarianism is obviously true—they'll say one lie is worth a life, end of story. My club advisor Mr. Alvarez is a Catholic and really into natural law; he'll say the duty not to lie isn't absolute when it conflicts with protecting innocent life, and he'll bring up double effect and mental reservation. My friend Dana is a virtue ethics person and thinks rules are for robots; she'll say a truthful person with practical wisdom would lie to the murderer and not become a liar. Marcus, who actually read some Kant, will say I'm just repeating Kant and that Kant already said you can refuse to answer, so what's new? Give me the prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ranked by how much damage they'd do, phrased the way each person would put it, with a two-minute reply for each and a note on where the reply is soft. Tell me what I should concede up front so I don't look like a robot, and what I absolutely have to hold. And include the one question I'm praying nobody asks. Don't give me a reading list or citations, just the prep sheet. I'm presenting for like fifteen minutes and then it's all Q&A, so I need to know what's coming.
