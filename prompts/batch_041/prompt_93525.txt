My friends and I have a standing argument about when the current global order ends — Dave says the US is overdue, Priya says it's already over and we just haven't noticed — and we finally agreed to stop yelling and each produce an actual forecast with an actual method, since we're all in a prediction pool together now. I got stuck with the outside view because I'm the one who's read the most Kahneman. Two weekends into trying to build a reference class I've talked myself into a position I can't shake and I want it either defended properly or killed: for singular historical events like the fall of an empire, the outside view isn't a check on the forecaster's bias, it's a laundering device. You pick the class, you pick the answer.

So please argue that for me, in the way a philosopher would rather than a blog post. I want the strongest version of the claim that there's no fact of the matter about the correct reference class for something like "how long does a hegemon last," and that the base rate you end up with is a function of a choice rather than a measurement — and I want the contrast with the cases where base rates obviously do work, medicine and insurance and so on, where the population is fixed by a sampling procedure you didn't get to invent, spelled out so I can see exactly what's missing in the historical case and whether the disanalogy is fatal or just annoying. Deal with the two objections I keep hearing instead of waving at them. One is that the inside view is just as arbitrary, so at least the outside view is honest about its priors — I don't think that survives, but I want to see it taken seriously. The other is the conditioning point, which Dave keeps making: the more you condition on what makes this empire this empire, the closer the reference class gets to a single case, and at the limit you're just doing the inside view with extra algebra, so why not skip the algebra. And then, since I do still have to put a number in the pool on Sunday, tell me what's left of the outside view after your arguemnt goes through — is there a weaker job it can still do, like pricing my own confidence or catching me when I'm being a hedgehog, or should I abandon it and forecast like a historian. I'd rather have a real answer to that than a both-sides conclusion.
