I would like help identifying a phenomenon, and if it has no established name, I would like it named and delimited carefully rather than merely labelled.

The pattern is this. I have a reasonably accurate model of my own future behaviour, and I keep catching myself making decisions whose function seems to be to spare me from having to act on them. A concrete case: at eleven at night, clear-headed, I decide that I will run at six. I do not merely hope to run; I decide to, and the decision is sincere in the way my decisions generally are. I also know, with roughly the confidence I place in any empirical prediction about myself, that at six I will not run — I have run this experiment dozens of times and the result is not in doubt. What I cannot place is the work the decision does. Making it feels like the matter is settled. Having settled it, I stop turning it over. And when six comes and I do not run, the lapse does not land as a failure; it lands as something already folded into the account. The decision did not bind my future self; it discharged my present one. A second case, less bodily: I decide to finally read the primary sources this time, and the deciding is so satisfying that the reading quietly stops being something I still owe.

What makes me think this is its own thing, rather than a synonym for weakness of will or for wishful precommitment, is the direction the discounting runs. In the ordinary story about akrasia, present bias explains why I fail to do the thing. Here it seems to explain why I make the decision at all. The resolution pays out now — relief, the sense of being a person with a plan — while the act it names costs now and pays later. Resolving is thus a cheap way to collect the reward the act was supposed to be for, and the larger the future benefit, the better the resolution feels and the less pressure remains to secure it. That inversion is the part I care about, and it is the part I have never seen stated as its own phenomenon.

I want the carving, not the label. So: does this have an established name? If it is nearest to something existing — precommitment or Ulysses contracts, self-signalling, the intention–behaviour gap, moral licensing, ordinary self-deception — I want to know precisely where the fit breaks. Precommitment is meant to constrain the future self, and this does the opposite. Self-signalling is about what I infer about myself, whereas here the apparent effect is on what I no longer feel I have to do. If nothing established matches, please coin a term, define it, and give me its edges. Is the distinguishing feature the discharge of motivation, the temporal location of the payoff, the sincerity of the resolution, or some combination? Where does it shade into ordinary planning, into self-deception, and into straightforwardly lying to oneself? And what would a clean case look like that has the discharge without the insincerity, or the present-biased payoff without the discharge — something to fix the boundary on either side.
