ok so I've been going back and forth with a friend about Dennett's two-bitser versus Searle's whole original-vs-derived intentionality thing, and I wrote out the argument below, half convinced it settles it for Dennett. The trouble is it also seems to settle something obviously false, so now I dont trust it. Tell me which step to give up and what it costs me.

Quick setup so we're on the same page. The two-bitser is Dennett's vending machine that takes quarters. It goes into internal state Q when a real quarter drops in, and into Q* when someone feeds it a slug, and Q and Q* are functionally identical -- same wiring, same output, it dispenses the same bar either way. Dennett's point (as I read him) is that we cheerfully say the machine 'believes' it got a quarter, and that this belief isnt different in kind from ours, only thinner. Searle's reply is that the machine's aboutness is derived: it only means quarter relative to us and our purposes, whereas a brain's states have original, intrinsic intentionality.

Here's the argument:

(1) For original-vs-derived to be a difference in kind rather than a mood, there must be some intrinsic feature F of the state itself -- a bit of the state you could point at -- such that my belief that there's a quarter in my pocket has F and the two-bitser's Q does not, and its having F (and not our attitudes toward it) is what makes my belief original.

(2) So canvass the candidates for F. Being made of neurons? Searle himself says a silicon system could have intentionality, so substrate is out. Being conscious? That would deny intentionality to unconscious beliefs, and anyway there's no consciousness-bit inside the state to point at. The right causal history? My belief's history is fixed by my brain's design plus my learning; Q's is fixed by the machine's design. Same shape. Conceptual role? Holistic, and Q has a role (quarter in, dispense out). Being learned rather than innate? Lots of my beliefs were never learned in any interesting sense.

(3) So there's no intrinsic F. The difference in kind isnt a naturalistic difference at all.

(4) If it's not naturalistic, the only remaining option is to say original intentionality is a non-physical, intrinsic, mind-stuff property -- i.e. to be a Cartesian.

(5) We shouldnt be Cartesians. So there is no original intentionality: my aboutness and the two-bitser's are the same kind of thing, differing only in richness and degree. Dennett wins.

But (5) proves too much. If it holds, a thermostat 'believes' it's warm in the same sense I believe it's warm, and there's simply no fact of the matter separating me from the vending machine -- which I dont think anyone, Dennett included, actually wants to say. My gut says (2) is doing something sneaky, but maybe I just dont like where this leads. Where does it actually go wrong, and if I have to drop a premise, which one costs me the least? I'd rather not be a Cartesian, but I also dont want to end up saying I'm no more about anything than a thermostat is.
