A roommate of mine and a friend who studies economics got into a long argument about the so-called gender-equality paradox, and I ended up refereeing by default. I have no philosophy background, so I'd like a fair ruling on the exchange as it actually went rather than a general essay on the topic.

His first move was that the pattern is what you'd expect if the underlying differences are partly biological: once material and social pressure to conform is lifted, whatever residual differences in interest exist have more room to express themselves, so the freer and richer countries reveal the real distribution, while in poor ones everyone is pushed toward the same survival-driven choices. Her reply was that the outcome measures are not measures of preference at all. Share of women in engineering, for instance, is a fact about a labor market and a welfare state. Where a safety net exists and the pay premium on technical work is small relative to the alternatives, choosing a low-earning degree is survivable; where it is not, the technical track is the only way out, so the sorting reflects constraint rather than taste. She said he was reading a constraint effect as a preference effect. He conceded that but said her story predicts the wrong direction, since the differences should shrink as countries get richer, not grow, and he added that preference is the wrong unit anyway: a preference is whatever survives contact with an environment, so you cannot use the environment to explain away the preference without first saying which part of the choice was free. She answered that this cuts against him too, because if preference is just whatever the environment lets through, his biological reading is unfalsifiable -- any observed pattern, including the opposite one, could be called the residual after constraints. She then offered a distinction she thought was at least testable: between the sorting of people into fields and the coding of fields as masculine or feminine, since who enters a field seems to shift with how the field is coded, quite apart from what the work actually involves.

Adjudicate this. Tell me, for each of the four moves, who has the better of it and why, and then what both of them are missing about what this pattern can and cannot show. Give it as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, with no introduction and no conclusion.
