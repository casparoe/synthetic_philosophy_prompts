Me and my mate Dave had a two hour row in the pub last night about what people actually believe, and I want to know whether there's a principle behind my gut recations, because he reckons there isn't and that I'm just being sentimental about brains.

Here's my verdicts. Dave has never once considered whether 391 = 17 × 23. Ask him and he works it out in his head in five seconds and says yes, obviously. I say he believed it all along. Now take some theorem of arithmetic whose shortest proof is longer than the age of the universe — he's never heard of it, he'd never get there. I say he doesn't believe it, even though it follows from what he does believe. Dave says that's a fact about his skull, not about his beliefs: believe the axioms and you believe everything they spill out, full stop.

Then: he believes P, he believes if P then Q, Q is staring him in the face, but he's never once put the two together. I want to say he believes Q, but it doesn't feel like the 391 case and I can't say why.

Then there's the crank at work, who believes the axioms of arithmetic, believes a dodgy lemma, and believes a theorem that follows from the lemma. One of the three is false. I don't think he believes a contradiction — I think he believes three things and one of them's wrong. Dave says that's incoherent.

Then my nan, who knows the rules of chess and can see the board perfectly well but can't spot the mate in three. I say she doesn't know there's a mate in three, though it follows from what she knows. Dave says she knows it and just doesn't know that she knows it, which sounds like a dodge to me. And when I point at a computer that spits out every consequence of a set of axioms, he says it believes all of them; I say it doesn't believe anything.

The one I keep going back and forth on is Dave's cousin, who's read half a logic book and says, sincerely, that he believes every logical consequence of what he believes, including the ones he'll never get to. If a man tells you straight out that he believes a thing, doesn't that settle it? But I don't think he does believe it, and I can't tell whether I'm calling him a liar or saying he's wrong about his own mind.

Dave's big move is that believing a thing is different from being committed to it, like signing a contract you've never read. Maybe. Is there one principle that gets all my verdicts, or a small set of candidates? Say which clause is doing the work in each case, what it predicts about a case I haven't thought of, and which of my verdicts I have to give up. Dave kept saying there's a name for all this, something about omniscience, but neither of us could remember it. No reading list please, I'm on my phone. British English.
