I run a monthly Nomic league at a board-game cafe — Nomic being the Peter Suber thing where the rulebook contains rules for changing the rulebook, and on your turn you propose an amendment and everyone votes on it — and it's gotten strange enough that a few of us started a tiny podcast about the legal philosophy the game keeps accidentally generating. Next week's guest is a legal philosopher who has spent years arguing that the so-called paradox of self-amendment is a pseudo-problem: the worry that a rule can't authorize its own amendment without either begging the question or triggering an infinite regress of authorizing rules. Her line is that the paradox only looks real if you assume validity is conferred at the moment of enactment, and that it dissolves once you treat a rule-change as validated retroactively, by the later practice of treating it as valid — so no rule ever has to lift itself by its own bootstraps, because the bootstrapping happens afterward. I want eight to ten questions that actually press her on this, and not one of them should be "how did you get into this."

Here's what I'd like covered, roughly one question each, though merge them if it flows better: what retroactive validation does to the interval between the enactment and the later practice that supposedly validates it — was the rule void during that gap, or valid but not yet confirmed, and is either answer tolerable; whether her move just relocates the mystery into whatever it is that makes a practice count as *accepting* a rule rather than merely conforming to it (she'll probably say this is a misunderstanding and that acceptance is just a fact about what people do, and I'd like the follow-up ready for that); our house rule that any rule-change can be challenged by any player, with a successful challenge voiding the change from the very beginning — which, awkwardly, we adopted by a rule-change that someone then challenged, so nobody is quite sure what the legal status of that rule is now, or of anything passed under it; the case where a player amends the winning condition so that she wins instantly, which we get in some form almost every session; and whether her account has anything to say about constitutions that try to make themselves partly unamendable, like the German constitution's eternity clause — if later practice is what does the validating, what stops a court or a mob from validating anything it likes, and does she think that's a feature or a bug. For each question, add the follow-up she's most likely to need given her stated position, so I'm not stuck improvising when she gives the answer she always gives.
