I've been having a long running argument with a friend about whether culture really evolves the way genes do, and I've ended up defending a pretty strong version of the meme picture: tunes, slogans, rituals, ways of tying a knot, jokes, conspiracies — these are replicators. They vary, they get copied with error, they spread at different rates, and that's a genuine Darwinian process in its own right, not just a metaphor for one. The claim I actually care about is the autonomy bit: what spreads is whatever is good at getting itself spread, which need not be good for the people carrying it and need not be good for anything at all. A chain letter, an earworm, a superstition that costs you real time and money — all of those are fit in the only sense that matters, and any benefit to the host is a side effect at best, not the engine. My friend's counter is basically a design argument: she says you shouldn't call something a cultural adaptation unless you can point to some feature of it — a hook, a rhyme, a bit of structure — that looks engineered for transmission and that would be an implausible coincidence otherwise, and she thinks for most cultural items we just can't do that. I'm taking a seminar on cultural evolution this term and half the reading seems to assume my view and half seems to assume hers, so I want to actually think it through instead of picking a side by vibes.

So here's what I want. Give me the case against my position — not the lazy stuff like "culture isn't genes so the analogy fails", but the objections that should genuinely worry me. Which observations, which patterns in how cultural stuff actually changes over time, which formal or modelling results, which historical or ethnographic cases would count against the autonomy claim specifically, and how hard would they hit? Rank them from near-fatal down to merely awkward, and be explicit about which of them someone who holds my view would themselves concede as a real cost rather than deflect. I also want to see what my position predicts that a rival picture doesn't — say, the picture where culture mostly spreads because it's useful or appealing to the people who adopt it — because if the two make the same predictions everywhere then I've learned something too. And please be honest about whether the view has been quietly qualified into something that can't loose: if defenders have retreated to a version where every failure just gets absorbed as another selection pressure, tell me, and tell me whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind or just the normal price of a broad framework. Throughout, say how confident you are in each verdict, and single out the one point you're least sure about and why.
