In the reading group I attend, a friend has been arguing that the doubt of the Second Meditation ends in something much stronger than people usually allow, and he argues it with a picture rather than a proof. The picture is the one Descartes reaches for at the outset of that Meditation: "Archimedes, that he might transport the entire globe from the place it occupied to another, demanded only a point that was firm and immovable; so, also, I shall be entitled to entertain the highest expectations, if I am fortunate enough to discover only one thing that is certain and indubitable." On my friend's reading, the cogito is that point. The globe is everything else — body, world, the received sciences; the method of doubt is the lever; and since one firm point suffices, the cogito suffices as the point from which the whole of knowledge is levered back into place. He adds two embellishments that he takes to strengthen the picture. First, a fulcrum is indifferent to who stands on it: the point, once found, belongs to no one in particular, so the cogito is a starting point available to any thinker and the reconstruction of the sciences is a public project. Second, the point is not part of the machine it moves — a fulcrum is neither the lever nor the earth — so the cogito is not itself among the opinions that the doubt places in question.

I suspect the picture is carrying more of the conclusion than the argument is, and that the slide from 'point' to 'foundation' is where it happens. Please audit it. Set out the correspondences explicitly — what plays the role of the point, the lever, the earth, the hand that applies the force, and the immovability of the point — and say which of those correspondences are exact and which are stipulations. Then the disanalogies, sorted into those that matter for the conclusion my friend wants (that a single indubitable item is enough to rebuild the sciences, and that it is impersonal) and those that are merely decorative. Tell me what the picture quietly imports that the target lacks — about what kind of thing a fixed point is, what its being immovable consists in, and what must already be in place for leverage to be transmitted at all. Finally, say whether the conclusion survives: if the cogito is better described as something other than a point of support, what is it, and how much follows from it? If there is a better analogy for what the cogito actually secures, offer it and show where it outperforms Archimedes' lever.
