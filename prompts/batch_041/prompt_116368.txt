ok so I've been going back and forth with people in my lab about whether reduction and emergence are actually opposed or whether that's a fake fight, and I realized I should just write down my own view and see if it holds together before I keep making this everyone else's problem. I'm a cog sci PhD student, we have a couple of philosophers who loiter around the building and it's basically ruined my ability to state a position without hedging, so be warned. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Physicalism, the strong kind: every fact is fixed by the physical facts, nothing extra gets added.
(2) The special sciences are autonomous — biology, psych, econ have regularities you can't derive from physics, and that's not a defect, it's just what they're for.
(3) Emergence is real, and not the boring "we can't compute it" kind: higher-level properties do genuine causal work, including on their own parts. Selection is the case that convinced me.
(4) Explanation is answering a contrastive why-question. That's how two models can be incompatible on paper and both be correct — they're answering different questions.
(5) If two theories are predictively equivalent in every circumstance, they're notational variants. There's no further fact about which one is "really" true.
(6) When a higher-level claim and a lower-level claim conflict, lower wins. Physics fixes the space of what's possible; everything else is selection inside that space.

I've definately lost track of which of these are actually pulling against each other versus just sounding spicy when you put them next to each other. What I want is an audit, not a lecture: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which ones only look like it (and why the appearance is misleading), and for the real ones, how bad the tension is and what the cheapest repair would be. Then tell me the single change that would clean up the most at once, and what it would cost me — like, what I'd have to stop being able to say.
