I'm currently taking a seminar on Quine and the philosophy of language, and for a paper I want to press you for a number rather than a survey of the debate. Here is the proposition I would like a credence in:

I: For any two empirically equivalent manuals of radical translation that disagree about the reference of a term such as 'gavagai' — one rendering it as rabbit, another as undetached rabbit part, another as rabbit-stage — there is no fact of the matter as to which is correct. The disagreement is not a case of our being ignorant of a determinate answer, but of there being nothing further to be right or wrong about.

Give me your credence in I, decomposed. I would expect the decomposition to include at least the following terms, though you should add any you think belong:

(a) whether facts about reference supervene on facts about dispositions to verbal and nonverbal behavior under all possible stimulations — that is, how much of the thesis rests on a behaviorist constraint, and whether you accept that constraint;

(b) whether the situation is ordinary theoretical underdetermination, in which rival theories fit the totality of evidence, or the stronger claim that no additional fact exists that could privilege one theory — and how much of I survives if only the weaker reading is granted;

(c) whether I is best read as a metaphysical claim about meaning or as a claim about the semantics of our word 'means', and whether the two readings carry different credences;

(d) whether I is self-undermining: if there is no fact of the matter about what a speaker's terms mean, in what sense is there a fact of the matter about the content of I itself?

For each term, give a probability and one line of justification, show how the terms combine into the final figure, and state which term the final number is most sensitive to. If you think I is not a single proposition, tell me what would count as resolving it and give your credence in each reading separately.

Then run a stress test: suppose we equip the radical translator with complete physical information about the speaker — every neuron, every synapse, the full causal history. Does your credence in I rise, fall, or stay fixed, and what does your answer reveal about whether the indeterminacy is a consequence of evidential poverty or a feature of the metaphysics of meaning? Finally, tell me which argument or result would move your number most, and in which direction.
