My thesis: inevitability claims come in two flavors, and people who say "it was inevitable" almost always mean the second while pretending they mean the first. Closure inevitability = given the actual antecedent conditions, no accessible alternative path existed. Convergence inevitability = even if the antecedents had been different, roughly the same outcome would have arrived anyway. My claim is that convergence claims are the *more* counterfactual-laden of the two, so historians who denounce counterfactuals while quietly writing convergence claims are contradicting themselves — and that the two claims need different kinds of evidence, which is why alot of the fights about this go nowhere.
