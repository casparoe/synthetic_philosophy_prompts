so my rationality seminar did the desert hitchhiker case this week (the Parfit one) and my study group has an argument about it that I definately cant stop thinking about. the case: I'm stranded with no water, a driver called Nadia pulls up and offers to take me to town if I promise to send her 500 once I'm home. I know that once I'm in town I'll have no reason to pay — she cant find me, no one will know, it costs me 500, and she's passing through my town the one week I'll be away so theres no reputation angle either. Nadia can tell perfectly whether a promise is sincere, and she won't take me unless I really mean it.

Marek's argument:
(1) The only thing that could make it rational for me to form the intention to pay is that forming it is what gets me rescued.
(2) The intention only gets me rescued if its sincere, i.e. if it would actually move me to pay.
(3) It would move me to pay only by making me do, in town, something I'll then have no reason to do.
(4) An intention cant be rational if carrying it out would be irrational.
(5) So the intention isnt rational, and so, rationally, I cant get the ride.

I'm pretty sure (5) is wrong but I cant name the slip. Marek says it generalizes into something even bigger: no one can rationally form an intention to do something they'll have no reason to do, so every time you have to bank on your future self doing what it won't want to do you're being irrational — pre-paying for a gym you'll dread going to, telling your advisor the draft will be done, taking the money up front and then being expected to swallow the gross pill tomorrow, Ulysses getting himself lashed to the mast. Those are supposed to be the good cases of rationality. He also says it means Nadia, being rational, should know she'll never be paid and so should never make the offer in the first place, so nobody in my situation ever actually gets rescued.

Which numbered step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? I'd rather keep (1) and (2) than (4), if that helps. And if the whole thing is actually fine and I just have to swallow a cost somewhere I don't want to, say so and tell me where the bill comes due.
