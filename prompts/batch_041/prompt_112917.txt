I recently joined the ethics committee at a small hospital, and the paradox of deontology has become personal rather than academic: we are weighing a protocol under which one non-consenting patient would be exposed to a serious risk so that data could be gathered to save several others, and I keep meeting the objection that a deontologist who refuses to use the one is simply choosing a world with more violations of persons. I want a teaching and briefing instrument for the committee, not a literature review. Please construct a decision tree or taxonomy of the positions on the paradox of deontology, built from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names.

The starting commitment is that persons are ends in themselves, so there are constraints on treating someone merely as a means, and those constraints can require us to let some die rather than harm one. The paradox is the apparent inconsistency between that commitment and the thought that if the point of the constraint is to protect persons, it should not forbid preventing more numerous violations of persons. I want the tree to turn on distinctions that are usually skipped in undergraduate treatments: whether the constraint is a side-constraint on what one may do or a duty not to treat anyone as a mere means; whether the fundamental object of assessment is an act, a rule, a maxim, or a character trait; whether the position treats the constraint as absolute, as having a catastrophe threshold, as merely prima facie, or as fully specifiable so that apparent conflicts dissolve; whether it accepts agent-centered restrictions and agent-centered prerogatives as separate elements; and whether it treats moral residue or regret as evidence of a genuine dilemma rather than as a cost to be minimized. At least one branch must concern whether the paradox is a genuine inconsistency, a practical dilemma, or a malformed question that assumes a maximizing framework deontology rejects.

Build the tree so that each branch node is a question a reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and each leaf is a named position, or unnamed if the logical space contains a combination nobody defends, with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Include leaves for at least absolutism, threshold deontology, specificationism, a pluralist prima facie duty view, an agent-centered restriction view with a prerogative, a rule-consequentialist reconstruction, a Kantian mere-means view that denies the paradox, a moral-dilemma or residue view, and particularism. Use concrete cases at the branch points: at least one case in which the one to be harmed consents, one in which the many are culpable for the threat, one in which the harm is not death but a rights violation, and one in which the many are not strangers but people to whom the agent has special obligations. Do not rely on a trolley case for every branch. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and note any unoccupied combinations, saying briefly whether they are empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Do not give me citations, reading lists, or author names; I want the instrument itself, about eight to ten leaves.
