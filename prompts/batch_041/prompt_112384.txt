I keep getting dragged into this fight and I don't want another round of it, I want a diagnosis of it. It's the cliodynamics thing — Turchin and the secular-cycle crowd, the idea that you can write down dynamical models of whole societies and get cycles of integration and disintegration out of them, driven by things like elite overproduction and asabiya, and then read off where a polity is heading. The other camp is the historians who think the whole project is bunk: that the variables are just theory painted onto the past, that the models are curve-fits with enough free knobs to fit anything after the fact, and that you can't predict history because each period is its own thing. This has been going on for what, twenty years now? And it's total trench warefare — nobody moves, both sides think the other is either naive or dishonest.

Here's what I want. Not a survey, not a both-have-a-point. A diagnosis of the disagreement: what is it actually about? Walk through the candidates and then commit to one. Is there a hidden empirical question doing the real work — whether these models genuinely predict out-of-sample or just retrodict with hindsight? Is it a measurement/construct problem — are elite overproduction and asabiya quantities that exist independently of the theory, or are they coded in such a way that the cycle pops out by construction, so the model can never actually lose? Is it a clash about what counts as an explanation of history at all — subsuming a case under a mechanism versus narrating why this particular sequence happened — two different senses of 'understand' wearing one word? Or is it mostly discipline and status, historians guarding thier turf and the modelers wanting to be physicists? My own hunch, and I want you to test it rather than flatter it: the empirical dispute is real but it's the smaller half, and the actual stall is the explanation-versus-understanding thing, with a thick layer of turf on top. Tell me if I've got that backwards.

Then, given whatever diagnosis you land on, tell me what would count as progress — a real out-of-sample prediction, a formal result about identification, a conceptual distinction, or nothing, because the question is ill-posed and both sides should quit. Take a position and defend it. I don't want a buffet of options with a shrug at the end.
