John von Neumann basically invented the modern computer, and at the end of his life he wrote a short book asking whether the brain is one. I think his answer is basically no, and that he was right, and that this is a problem for people today who talk like AI is going to be a brain.

His first point is about speed. A neuron takes about a millisecond to fire, which is way slower than a transistor switching, like a million times slower. His second point is reliability and this is the part I find most interesting. Neurons are unreliable, they fire randomly, they sometimes dont fire when they should, and somehow the whole thing works for eighty years. Von Neumann thought this was the central puzzle and I think he never solved it.

His third point is about the language of the brain. He asks whether the brain is digital or analog, and he cant decide, and he wonders if it uses a statistical code where the exact timing of a spike doesn't matter, only roughly how many neurons fire in some window. I think this is the deepest part of the book and I wish he had lived long enough to finish the argument.

If the brain doesn't work like that then the comparison breaks at the most important point. So the brain is not a von Neumann machine, which is kind of funny since hes the one its named after.
