I'm a second-year PhD student in political theory, with a background in constitutional law and a side interest in the philosophy of action, and I'm trying to settle on a dissertation agenda over the next three years. My broad area is precommitment: Ulysses contracts on the individual side, constitutional entrenchment on the collective side. The standard questions are familiar—whether a person can bind her future self without assuming a unified self, whether a sovereign people can bind future majorities without a constituent power that stands outside time, how to distinguish legitimate entrenchment from dead-hand rule. What I keep running into is a less standard framing that came out of a workshop discussion, and I want to know whether it should reorganize the field for me. The suggestion was that the most interesting precommitment is not against future weakness of will but against future better judgment: you bind yourself not because you expect to be tempted, but because you expect your future self to have better evidence and yet be subject to pressures—social, institutional, emotional—that will make her interpret the evidence badly. Call it epistemic precommitment for now. On that view, the object of the contract is not a future choice but a future interpretive stance, and the constitutional analogue is not entrenching a policy but entrenching a rule of recognition or a method of interpretation. I find this compelling, but I can't tell whether it's a genuine research program or just a vivid redescription of ordinary epistemic humility.

Given that framing, which open questions in the area of Ulysses contracts and constitutional precommitment matter most for someone with about three years and a philosophy/law background, and in what order should I take them? I'm less interested in a survey than in a defended agenda: for each question, why it is upstream of others, whether it is tractable with conceptual tools rather than empirical work, and what would move it up or down my list. I'd also like you to say which of the familiar questions are overrated once the epistemic-precommitment framing is on the table, and which new questions it makes salient—for instance, whether binding one's future interpretation is even conceptually possible, whether it collapses into a requirement of superhuman self-trust, and how the individual/collective analogy fares when the bound party is a majority rather than a person. Please be explicit about the criteria you're using—importance, tractability, neglectedness, dependency relations, fit with a three-year project—and about the trade-offs among them. I don't need citations or a reading list; I need the reasoning and the ranking.
