Ok so I've been arguing with a friend about all this enhancement stuff and I realized I don't actually know what my own rule is, so I'm just going to list what I think about a bunch of cases and you tell me if there's a principle behind them, because right now it feels like I'm making it up case by case.

Glasses for nearsightedness: fine, don't even think about it. LASIK that gets you to better than 20/20: also fine, and I notice I feel zero weirdness about that. Statins for someone whose cholesterol is through the roof: fine. The same statins for a guy with perfectly normal cholesterol whose dad dropped dead at 50: fine. Adderall for a kid with an actual ADHD diagnosis: fine, no reservations. The same pill for my roommate who just finds his job boring and wants to grind through it: not fine, and I get a little judgy about it. SSRIs for someone with crushing depression: fine. SSRIs for a friend who's just shy and would like to be more outgoing at parties: not fine, though honestly I can't tell you the difference between her and the ADHD kid except that one of them has a diagnosis and one doesn't. Beta blockers before a big presentation: fine, I'd take them. EPO in the Tour: not fine, obviously. Sleeping in an altitude tent: not fine, that's cheating. Caffeine before a meeting: fine, and if you told me it wasn't I'd think you were being weird. A pill that makes you stop wanting to drink, for an alcoholic: fine, sounds great. A pill that makes you stop wanting junk food, for someone thirty pounds overweight: fine I guess? A pill that erases a traumatic memory: not fine, feels like a violation of something. A pill that makes you more patient and less of a jerk: I feel like I should be all for this and I'm not, and I want to know why. A pill that makes you content with your mediocre life instead of wanting more: fine for me to take, not fine to give to my kid. Growth hormone for a kid whose body doesn't make enough: fine. Same thing for a kid who's just short and whose parents are worried about him getting bullied: I want to say fine and I'd probably sign off, but it makes my skin crawl a little. Cochlear implant for a deaf infant: fine, though I know people disagree. Gene edit to stop a kid getting Huntington's: fine. Gene edit to make him taller or smarter: not fine. Nose job on a nose that works perfectly well: not wrong exactly, I just don't respect it, which I realize is a different thing from it being wrong. A drug that makes me 10% smarter but that everyone at my firm will be on within a year: feels pointless, not wrong.

So here's the principle I came up with, tell me if it survives. It's fine to fix something that's bad for you no matter what anyone else does, and it's not fine to do something that only helps you because it puts you ahead of other people. That gets my glasses/EPO split, which is the one I'm most sure about. But it seems to say the Adderall-for-boredom case is fine (a boring job is bad for me whether or not my coworkers are on anything) and I dont think it is, and it seems to say the memory pill is fine, no competition involved, and I'm sure it isn't. It also says nothing at all about the kid cases, where the person taking it isn't the person deciding.

So: is there a principle that gets all or most of these, what would it say about the ones where my verdict is basically a shrug, and which of my verdicts do I have to give up? And is there any version of it that doesn't collapse into "whatever we're used to is fine, whatever we're not used to is cheating," because my grandfather thought Ritalin was a scandal and I think it's a Tuesday, and I'd definately like to think at least one of us is wrong rather than just early or late.
