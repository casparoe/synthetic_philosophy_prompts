I am a philosophy of biology PhD student leading a 75-minute discussion section (roughly eighteen second-year students, most of them biologists with two years of genetics and no philosophy) attached to a concepts-in-biology module. The topic is the species problem, and I want a plan for how to teach it, not an explanation of it.

The target I am teaching toward: by the end the students should see that the disagreement among species concepts is not merely terminological — that the rival criteria are not just competing labels for one and the same underlying division — and they should feel the force of that claim rather than hear me assert it. Please give me the order in which the pieces must be introduced, with the dependency between them made explicit; what has to be in place before the question is even intelligible to them; the single carrying example to organize the section around; the misconceptions they will arrive with or generate, and the precise point in the sequence at which to head each one off; one closing question or brief exercise that would show me whether the point landed; and what I should openly refuse to resolve at this level, with a note on how to say so.

For the carrying example I want to avoid the textbook cases they might answer from memory. Here is a case I have in mind, and I would like you to judge whether it can bear the whole section or needs a partner: a single lineage of small fish distributed along a chain of connected lakes; in the two end lakes the fish are sharply distinct in body form and feeding habit and, when brought together experimentally, interbreed only at low rates; through the middle lakes runs a fully fertile continuum of intermediate forms, and genetic data show low but non-zero gene flow from one end of the chain to the other; and one of the middle-lake populations has recently shifted to obligate self-fertilization, exchanging no genes at all while remaining morphologically continuous with its neighbors. Tell me where each of the main species criteria would draw the boundaries here, and how to keep the students from concluding too quickly that the criteria are simply answering different questions.
