I somehow let myself get talked into running next month's session of the local reading group. Ninety minutes, about fifteen people, smart but not historians — two engineers, a nurse, someone who did a semester of archaeology, and one guy who has read alot of Jared Diamond and will tell you about it whether or not you asked. Topic I picked is "collapse": Bronze Age, the Maya, Rapa Nui. Don't explain collapse to me, I've read enough. Plan how I explain it.

Here's my half-baked frame — tell me if it's wrong, or if it's fine but has to be earned rather than announced. I think almost every famous collapse is soft, not hard. Hard collapse: the people, the know-how, the fields, the buildings all go. Soft collapse: the political hierarchy and the story that justified it go, and everyone keeps farming. And I think the word "collapse" mostly tracks the second one, because the people who write the word down are the ones who loose. The peasant doesn't experience a collapse, they experience a tax collector who stops showing up. So my thesis is that "collapse" is partly indexical — it's relative to whose ledgers you're reading.

What I need from you:

- The order the ideas have to go in, and why that order. What depends on what. My guess is that the room has to stop treating "a society" as one object with one fate before anything else can land, but tell me if that's not actually first.
- The one case that carries the whole session — Bronze Age, Maya or Rapa Nui — and what the winner has that the other two lack. I've heard the Easter Island ecocide story is much shakier than it gets presented, something about the little obsidian blades maybe being farm tools rather than weapons, but I don't trust my memory, so don't lean on that unless it's actually right.
- The misconceptions they'll arrive with and the ones they'll build halfway through, in the order they'll surface, and exactly where in the sequence I head each one off. Note that one of them is the overcorrection: the moment I say the Rapa Nui story is shaky, someone will decide that collapse is never real and it's all just "transformation."
- The distinction about causes I need them to be holding by the end — I think trigger versus underlying fragility, and one bad cause versus several interacting ones, but you tell me.
- The question I ask at the end that shows they got the frame rather than the anecdotes.
- What I should openly refuse to cover in ninety minutes, and say so out loud.

And no reading list — nobody's going to read anything.
