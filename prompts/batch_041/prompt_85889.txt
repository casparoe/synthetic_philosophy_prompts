alright, something a bit different for a change. I want a crowdfunding page -- Kickstarter-style, with the founder's breathless pitch, an FAQ, and a couple of backer comments -- for a startup selling a consumer gadget that is basically Maxwell's demon in a box. You drop it in your apartment, it sorts the fast molecules from the slow ones, spins a tiny turbine off the temperature gap, cools the room, and supposedly pays for its own electricity.

The part I actually care about: the pitch has to be almost right. A demon with a finite memory really does extract a little work before it fills up, and that part isn't the fraud -- the fraud is in what comes next. The memory fills, and you can't reset it for free; dumping those bits has to cost back at least as much entropy as all the sorting ever saved. So the founder's whole game is dodging the reset. Let him trot out the true-but-useless line that measurement dosen't have to cost anything, and the "our computer is reversible so there's no erasure cost" line, and let each one quietly break -- a reversible machine that never erases is a machine that runs once. He can also wave at the real single-molecule experiments where information was turned into work, which is a real thing and which he's misreading, since in those setups somebody on the outside supplies the information and pays for it (don't have him name papers, obviously). And he should get in the line about how this isn't a perpetual-motion claim, it's information-to-energy conversion, as if that were the distinction that saves him.

Then I want one of the backer comments to be deadpan, from someone who can actually do the accounting, and who quietly shows that the books come out to zero -- and I'd love it if that person also corrects the popular version of the story, the one where the demon is beaten because *measuring* costs energy, which was never the real problem. That's the joke underneath the whole thing: the audience nodding along to the wrong refutation.

Keep it deadpan, no winking, no real companies or living people, make up the lab and the backers. Any numbers the founder quotes should be the right order of magnitude. About 650 words, and please don't make him a crank -- he should be a smart guy who's read the right papers and is betting on the one step everyone forgets.
