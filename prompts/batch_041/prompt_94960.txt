I have been reading histories of the Cold War, and I have come to think that the long argument over who started it is not, at bottom, an argument about the evidence. Each verdict seems to be fixed in advance by the point at which the narrative opens. Begin in 1917 and the initiative belongs to the revolutionary state that treated the surrounding order as a truce to be outlasted; begin in 1945 and the initiative belongs to the power that recast the European order in its own image while its ally was still digging out of the rubble. The later chapters differ from book to book, but the verdict is already contained in the opening pages. And the opening is chosen rather than found. Each starting point can be defended, but only by a claim about which state of affairs was the ordinary one, and which departure from it was the first unwarranted one. That claim is about what was owed, not about what happened, and yet it silently decides the causal question.

Is there a name for this structure? A dispute about causes that is settled in advance by a prior, usually unacknowledged settlement of what counts as the normal or undisturbed course of events, such that different starting points yield different and individually defensible verdicts, and the question of who caused it has no answer until the question of what was owed has been answered. If some established concept covers this, I want to know which one, and whether it fits exactly or only approximately — whether it is narrower than what I am describing, or wider, or aimed at a different feature of the case. If nothing established covers it, I would like a name, a definition, and some boundary cases, including cases where the structure is absent: where the starting point is uncontroversial, say, or where one party's first move was unwarranted by any standard one could adopt.

I should say what would not satisfy me. I am not after a reading list, and I am not after a verdict on the Cold War. Nor am I asking to be told that the chain of causes can always be pushed further back, or that "who started it" is a tendentious way of framing the question. I believe both, and neither explains what puzzles me: that the dispute is so stable, that the competing answers feel like discoveries rather than stipulations, and that historians who regard the question as the wrong one keep answering it anyway. The same pattern seems to recur outside historiography, in ordinary quarrels and in attempts to assign legal responsibility for a course of events that nobody initiated, which is part of why I suspect there may already be a term for it that I simply do not know.
