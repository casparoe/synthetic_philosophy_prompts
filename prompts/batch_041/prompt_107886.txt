I am a third-year psychiatry resident, and over the past few months a conceptual problem has become unexpectedly personal. In clinic I record anhedonia as if pleasure were a single quantity that can be dimmed or restored, but the patients I see describe pleasures that seem to have almost nothing in common: the taste of something sweet, the relief of finally lying down, the satisfaction of a task completed, the pleasure of a sad song, the pleasure of scratching an itch, the pleasure of anticipating a visit, and the pleasure of a cruel thought. I am also taking a philosophy of mind seminar, and I have realized that I cannot evaluate hedonism about well-being until I know what a theory of pleasure would have to deliver. At the center of my confusion is the heterogeneity problem: what, if anything, do the pleasure of a warm bath, the pleasure of a proof, and the pleasure of revenge have in common? I do not need a bibliography, a reading list, or citations; assume I cannot look anything up. What I need is the specification.

Please write the desiderata for a theory of pleasure: the full list of what any adequate account of pleasure must get right, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. Include the cases it must handle (sensory pleasures, intellectual and aesthetic pleasures, social pleasures, relief, anticipation, memory, mixed feelings such as bittersweetness, pleasures taken in the bad or in one's own pain, pleasures that seem unconscious or merely implicit, pleasures in non-human animals and infants, and the absence of pleasure in anhedonia); the distinctions it must not collapse (pleasure versus liking versus wanting; sensory pleasure versus attitudinal pleasure; occurrent pleasure versus dispositional mood; pleasure as a feeling versus pleasure as a directed attitude; intensity versus quality; pleasure versus pain and pleasure versus indifference); the puzzles it must dissolve (the heterogeneity puzzle, the unity puzzle, the location puzzle, and the puzzle of how pleasure can be both a felt state and a reason for action); and the empirical or conceptual results it must be consistent with (the way liking and wanting can come apart, the possibility of pleasure without desire and desire without pleasure, the temporal structure of relief and anticipation, and the apparent heterogeneity of pleasures across modalities and species). For each requirement, say why it is a requirement and whether you think it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of one camp. Then flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied; that is the part I most want, because if the list contains an inconsistent pair, the list itself is a result. Finally, say which single requirement does the most to exclude the most popular accounts of pleasure, and why. A table is fine for the list, but keep the weight on the criteria and the cases behind them rather than on scoring existing theories.
