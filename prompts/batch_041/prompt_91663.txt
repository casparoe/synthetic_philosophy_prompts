Is there a name for this? I keep running into a pattern in arguments and I don't have a word for it. Someone credits a whole-level or system-level feature with making a difference -- a committee's deliberation causing a decision, an ecosystem's diversity causing its stability, a person's intention causing her arm to move. The reply comes back that the outcome was already settled by the lower-level facts: given the individual votes, the ecosystem's members, the neurons, the result could not have been otherwise. So either the higher-level feature just *is* the lower-level story under another description, or it is doing nothing at all. What strikes me is that the same reply shows up whether the lower level is deterministic or merely probabilistic, and whether the higher-level feature is offered as a cause or only as an explanation -- people make the move in all four combinations without noticing they've changed cases. I suspect the four are not one argument but several, and that some of the combinations are much weaker than others.

I don't want a bare label. I want to know what the underlying situation actually is: what has to be true of two levels for the complaint even to get off the ground, which of the four combinations it genuinely threatens, and which it only appears to threaten because a causal claim and an explanatory claim have been run together. If there is an established name, tell me which of the nearest things it really is and how it differs from the others I'd be tempted to lump in -- the idea that the higher level is an extra cause over and above the lower one, the idea that it is no cause at all, and the idea that it is a cause but only because it is the lower level. And tell me where the edges are: does the pattern survive if the lower level is indeterministic, if the higher-level feature is multiply realized, or if we never mention causes and only talk about what explains what?
