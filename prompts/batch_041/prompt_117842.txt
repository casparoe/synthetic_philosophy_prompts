I'm a third-year undergrad in this joint seminar ('Minds and Machines') that's half CS students and half philosophy students, and I have to present on Wednesday. My slot is on whether RL agents can suffer, and I'm defending a deliberately narrow thesis: a reward signal can't be valence, no matter how you wire it up, so you can't answer 'does the agent suffer' by looking at what it's maximizing.

My argument, roughly: everyone's going to say 'obviously reward is valence, that's the whole point of the dopamine = reward prediction error story, and it's why we casually say agents "want" things.' My reply is that reward, formally, is a magnitude, not a bipolar axis. Pleasure and pain aren't more and less of one thing, they're opposites that push in contrary directions, and no amount of scaling gets you from one to the other. Reward has no such structure — negative reward isn't the opposite of positive reward, it's just a lower number, and if you flip the sign of the whole reward function you don't get an agent in pain, you get an agent solving a mirror-image task. So an RL agent has preferences at most, and preferences don't have a felt sign. Suffering needs the sign, and the formalism has nowhere to put it, you'd have to bolt it on from outside. I think the RPE point actually helps me: a prediction error is a learning signal, not a valence signal, and I'm pretty sure people just slide between the two because both get called 'reward'.

The room, precisely: the CS half is mostly RL people, two of them do multi-agent stuff and are militantly 'the formalism is the formalism, stop doing metaphysics'; one of them will definately bring up dopamine and TD learning; another will say the agent never optimizes reward anyway, it optimizes whatever the update rule does, which I think is a real problem for me and I don't have an answer to it. The philosophy half has two hardcore functionalists who'll say the sign structure is irrelevant and only causal role matters, plus one Kant-ish person who thinks the whole can-machines-suffer question is unserious, and one person who works on animal pain and will ask me what my criterion actually is. My supervisor is the one who told me the bipolarity point is either the whole argument or a pun.
