Draft the joint statement that two camps in the dispute over what intelligence is could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. On one side are those who hold that intelligence is a single, general, measurable capacity of the individual, evidenced by the fact that performances across very different cognitive tasks correlate positively; on the other, those who hold that intelligence is not a thing in the head but a situated achievement of an agent in an environment, so that individual-difference measures are parochial artifacts of schooling and culture. Write the document they should have produced: the claims both can sign about the phenomena -- the positive manifold, the predictive power of tests, their cultural loading -- then the exact points on which they still differ, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording, and for each crux the argument or case both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. I take the cruxes to include whether the correlations require a single underlying cause or could instead arise from mutual causal interaction among distinct processes, and whether the large secular rises in raw scores show intelligence to be a fixed absolute capacity or only a shifting rank order. Say which part of the dispute is merely verbal. Write it in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
