I'm opening a talk on the Löbian obstacle with a joke, so I want something that lands for people who already know the setup. Dont explain the theorem in the setup — they've got it — but make the punchline turn on the actual reason the obstacle bites: a proof-based agent can't trust its own proofs about what it will do, because the rule that whenever it's proved it will do something, it will do it is a reflection principle that, by Löb, would let it prove anything, so it's off the table. The comedy has to come from an agent that's genuinely wedged — the one inference that would let it act is the exact one it can't make. Put it in some ordinary scene, like the agent trying to commit to a lunch, or to give something back, or to make a promise; dialogue is fine, keep it short. If the joke would still work on someone who's never heard of Löb's theorem, it's not the joke I want.
