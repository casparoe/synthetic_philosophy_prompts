Long shot, but here's one that's been driving me a bit mad. I'm in a small reading group on philosophy of biology and for about four months we've been stuck on whether genes and DNA really carry information — whether 'the genetic code' is a code in anything like the sense a computer program is, or whether it's a metaphor we should just drop once we're being careful. Both camps have their lines down pat and nobody's budged an inch.

One side says the information talk is doing real work: a stretch of DNA specifies a protein, and specifying is a contentful relation — the sequence can fail to do what it's supposed to, and that normativity, this bit being *meant* to make that bit, is the whole point; it's what seperates a code from a mere causal nudge. The other side says no, all you actually have is a reliable causal chain plus a bad habit of speech: 'information' in the useful, measurable sense is just covariation, and covariation doesn't come with a 'supposed to' attached. Strip the metaphor, they say, and you lose nothing explanatory.

My pet hypothesis — which I'd like you to test rather than just nod along to — is that these are really two different questions wearing one word: one lot are asking what the sequence causally does, the other are asking what it's *for*, and the impasse is a word doing the work of a theory. But there's a nagging counter-thought. The mapping from codon to amino acid is arbitrary, it could have been otherwise, and 'arbitrary convention' is precisely what a code is — so maybe the arbitrariness is evidence for the code camp rather than against it, and my two-questions story is too tidy.

Can you actually diagnose this? Is it a verbal dispute, a substantive one about whether there's such a thing as biological content at all, or two questions under one heading? What are the real commitments each side can't give up without ceasing to be that side? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — some distinction, some hypothetical organism or experiment, some formal point about what has to be true for something to be a code — or is the whole thing a pseudo-question that should just be dissolved? I don't want a survey of who said what, I want the structure of the disagreement and an honest verdict on whether anything could break it. And if you reckon my arbitrariness point is a red herring, say so and say why.
