Ok, I'm a retired chemist (35 years in a lab) and I've been reading philosophy since I hung up the pipette. Latley I've gotten stuck on Kant and animals. Here's where I've landed, and I want to know if the package actually holds together:

(1) Kant's ethics is basically right. The core thing is that morality is about respecting rational agency; that's what gives something dignity.
(2) We have direct duties to animals. It's wrong in itself to make a dog suffer, not just wrong because it might make me a worse person.
(3) The standard line I've run into is that Kant thought our duties concerning animals were indirect—really duties to humanity. I think that's probably what he thought, and I think it's a problem for him.
(4) What makes something morally considerable is that it has a welfare and can suffer, not that it can reason or give itself a law.
(5) The formula of humanity is the best version of the categorical imperative.
(6) Mammals, birds, and probably octopuses have welfare and can suffer. Maybe insects too, I don't know.
(7) If an animal is an end in itself, then using it merely as a means is wrong, full stop.
(8) The indirect-duty argument is empirically weak. I've known plenty of people who are soppy about dogs and treat other humans like dirt, so cruelty to animals doesn't reliably make you cruel to people.

Take the case of a dog with dementia: no rational agency left, but clearly still suffering. Under (4) it has a direct claim on us. Under (5) and (1) it looks like it has none, or only whatever indirect claim comes from my character. I can't tell if thats a real contradiction or if I'm just missing a distinction.

What I want: go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad each tension is. Rank them. Then tell me the cheapest revision—what single thing should I give up or restate so the rest can stand? And if there's a version of Kant's view that can keep (1), (2), (5), and (6) together, tell me what it has to say about the dog. No citations or reading lists, please—I just want the logic.
