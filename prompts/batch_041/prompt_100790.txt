I am an undergraduate currently enrolled in a second-year course in epistemology, and we have just concluded the unit on externalist accounts of knowledge. The study guide our lecturer distributed offers the following summary of Nozick's tracking theory, which we have been told to treat as authoritative for the examination:

"Nozick holds that knowledge is true belief which tracks the truth. There are two tracking conditions: first, if the proposition believed were false, the believer would not believe it; second, if the proposition were true, the believer would believe it. These conditions are meant to capture the intuition that a knower's belief must answer to how things actually stand, rather than merely to how they appear. Nozick is therefore a reliabilist: he holds that a belief counts as knowledge precisely when it is produced by a process that reliably generates true beliefs, and the two subjunctive conditions are his way of spelling out what such reliability consists in. The theory earns its keep by its treatment of the sceptic. Nozick grants that knowledge is closed under known entailment — if one knows that p, and knows that p entails q, then one knows that q — and he accepts the consequence that, since one cannot know that one is not a brain in a vat, one cannot know that one has hands either. He regards this as the price of an honest theory, and takes the closure principle to be non-negotiable."

I am inclined to think this is broadly fair, since it matches my recollection of the lectures, but several of its formulations strike me as slightly off, and I do not yet have the background to say where the trouble lies or how serious it is. Could you audit the summary claim by claim? For each sentence I would like to know what a philosopher working on these issues would accept without hesitation, what is a defensible simplification, and what is an outright misrepresentation of the position — and, where the summary distorts something, what a committed defender of the tracking theory would say in reply. I am especially unsure about its account of the two tracking conditions and about its claim regarding closure and the sceptical argument: is the closing verdict, that on this theory one cannot know one has hands, something that follows from the accurate parts of the summary, or does it depend on the parts you think are mistaken? I do not need citations or a bibliography; I need to know which sentences I can safely reproduce in an essay and which I should abandon.
