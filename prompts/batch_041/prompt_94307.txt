I'm a science journalist at work on a long feature about the units-of-selection debate — the running argument in evolutionary biology over whether natural selection is best described as acting on genes, on individual organisms, or on groups, and over whether that choice is a substantive claim about the world or merely a convenient way of keeping the books. A friend who studies social evolution and I have been going around on this for weeks, and I've decided the piece needs one concrete case to hang on rather than the examples that turn up in every undergraduate lecture — the sterile workers of the social insects, the alarm call of the ground squirrel.
