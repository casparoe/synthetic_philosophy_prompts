I am a second-year philosophy student and a student member of my university's Student Conduct Panel. I am currently taking a course on moral philosophy and everyday life, and I have been asked to prepare the panel's written opinion on a case that will be heard next week. The case concerns a visiting exchange student who has been reported for repeatedly ignoring the queue at the main campus bus stop. The stop has no painted markings or barriers. The local convention, which the university's student handbook mentions only in passing, is that each person arriving asks who is last and then remembers their place; the queue is virtual and maintained by mutual recognition. The visitor, who comes from a city where buses are boarded in a free-for-all, walked straight to the doors on three occasions and boarded before students who had been waiting. Two local students have filed complaints under the conduct code's provision on disorderly or disrespectful behaviour. The visitor says he did not know the convention, that no one told him, and that he was not trying to take anyone's place. The panel must decide whether to impose a sanction, dismiss the complaint, or take some other course. I need a short written opinion I can circulate before the hearing. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a time frame, and it should engage seriously with the strongest argument on the other side, in particular with the view that local conventions are morally binding on visitors and that ignorance is no excuse. The opinion should also address whether the queueing norm is a moral obligation or merely a coordination convention, and what difference that makes to how the panel should treat a visitor who did not know it. Please write in British English, and do not include citations or reading lists.
