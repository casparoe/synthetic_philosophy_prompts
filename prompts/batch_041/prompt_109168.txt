I am a program director at a small liberal arts college, and I have recently proposed a series of evening seminars on ethics at which wine is served. A senior colleague has accused me of dressing up a cocktail party as pedagogy, and in casting about for a principled rationale I returned to the discussion of drinking parties in the first two books of Plato's Laws, which I had not read closely since graduate school. I am struck by the Athenian Stranger's suggestion that a properly conducted drinking party can serve both as a test of a person's character and as a kind of gymnasium for the soul, but I cannot see how these two functions fit together. If the wine merely reveals what is already there—as a touchstone reveals the quality of gold—then it cannot improve anyone; if it improves them, then it is not merely a test. Please explain the conceptual issue here: how does the Athenian's account negotiate the apparent tension between the revelatory and the formative roles of wine? In particular, I would like you to explain the function of the sober symposiarch, the comparison with courage as tested in war and with the Carthaginian law that forbids soldiers on campaign from drinking, and the sense in which the drinking party is supposed to be a gymnasium for the soul. Does the exercise work by removing the restraint of the sober leader so that the drinker's true nature shows, or by internalizing that restraint so that the drinker becomes self-governing even in the leader's absence? And does the analogy with physical training hold, or does it break down in ways that matter for the educational claim?

I have been thinking of it as a kind of stress test, where you load a structure to see whether it holds, but I am told that in some engineering contexts the test itself can fatigue the material or even strengthen it, and I wonder whether Plato has something like that in mind or whether the analogy is misleading.

Please be explicit about how confident you are in your reading. Say what would change your mind: which textual consideration or philosophical argument would lead you to a different verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
