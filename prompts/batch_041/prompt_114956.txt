I was listening to a recorded lecture on Arendt and lying in politics while doing dishes, so I only half caught it, but the guy's summary was interesting enough that I wrote down the gist. He said something like:

'Arendt's point in her essays on truth and politics and on the Pentagon Papers is that lying is uniquely corrosive in politics because factual truth is the one thing a tyrant can never refute, only suppress — it's like a mathematical truth in that way, it just stands there and you either acknowledge it or you don't. Ordinary political lying, hiding a mistake or covering up a scandal, is nothing new and isn't really her target. What worries her is the modern kind, where the lie doesn't just misrepresent a particular fact but replaces the whole factual picture with a coherent fiction, and the people doing it end up believing their own story. Thats what she saw in the Pentagon Papers: the problem-solvers in the government had so thoroughly rewritten reality that they lost the ability to judge the actual war. So the lie destroys the liar's contact with reality first, then the publics. And since politics depends on a shared world of facts, once thats gone you can't have politics at all — which is why she thinks any politician who lies has already destroyed the thing they need in order to govern. The obvious objection, that politicians lie all the time and democracies survive, doesn't really touch her, because she's talking about the total replacement of reality, not the everyday fib.'

Then he added that he thinks this basically refutes her, since democracies obviously do survive alot of lying, and she can't have it both ways.
