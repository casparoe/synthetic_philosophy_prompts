I've been lurking on rationalist blogs and forums for like a decade now (LessWrong, the old SSC comment sections, all of it), and one thing I've noticed is that "why do promises bind?" is the kind of question where everybody at a meetup has a confident answer and no two of them are the same. Last week our group split into two camps and I want a joke that only works if you know which camp your in. Camp one, call it the heads-up view: a promise matters because it gives the other person something to steer by. You say you'll be at the airport at 7, and now they can build a plan on top of that, so breaking it is basically the wrong of wrecking somebody's plans after you invited them to make them. Camp two, call it the leash view: a promise matters because you hand the other person a little piece of authority over what you do next. It isn't a forecast, it's a claim, which is why they can release you from it and nobody can release you from a forecast. The two come apart in all the fun spots. A promise made to somebody who then dies before they can rely on it. A promise they never find out about, so it never gave them anything to steer by. A promise to do something they'd never actually want you to do. And my favorite, the promise you keep anyway after they've released you from it, which the heads-up view has to call a weird little gift and the leash view has to call you re-upping a claim that nobody's holding anymore.

So: write me a joke. An actual joke with a punchline, not just a bit of dialogue that lays out the two views, and it has to be funny to someone who genuinely holds one of them -- the punchline should turn on the real content of the disagreement, the way a good in-joke turns on a shared model, not on the mere fact that philosophers argue about promises. If it helps, I've always thought the natural setting is a guy promising a dying friend he'll take care of the ficus, since the friend is right there to receive the promise and then right there not to rely on it, but pick your own setting if something better comes to you, or give me two endings, one per camp, if that's where the funny is. Don't bother name-dropping anyone or citing literature, I don't care about the bibliography, I care about whether the joke lands. American English please.
