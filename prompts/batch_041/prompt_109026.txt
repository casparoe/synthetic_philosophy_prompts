ok, I'm a grad student in comparitive politics and I've been banging my head against this for months. The whole literature on democratic backsliding feels like it's stalled, and I honestly can't tell anymore if it's a real argument or just people talking past each other. Roughly: one camp says look at Hungary under Orban, Turkey under Erdogan, Poland under the last government, the US around 2016-2020 -- this is backsliding, period, and we should define democracy broadly enough (courts, media, minority rights, the whole package) to catch it. The other camp says careful, if you define democracy that thickly you've baked the answer into the definition, and if you define it thinly (free-ish elections, no overt fraud) most of these countries still technically qualify, so calling it 'backsliding' is basically a partisan vibe. And the weird thing is that when they go at each other they mostly agree on the facts -- everyone agrees Orban packed the courts, everyone agrees Erdogan jailed journalists -- they disagree on whether that adds up to 'backsliding' or just 'illiberal but still a democracy.'

So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it a verbal spat about the word 'democracy'? A real fight about which facts count? Or are the two sides secretly answering different questions under one label -- one asking 'is this a democracy, yes or no' and the other asking 'how much democracy is left, on a scale'? And there's the 'who decides' part too: who's even entitled to make the call -- scholars, the democracy indices, the EU, the country's own voters?

Here's my half-baked hypothesis, tell me if it's dumb: maybe the concept is doing two jobs at once -- a diagnostic job (flag a country so watchdogs and courts can act) and a descriptive job (just say what kind of regime it is) -- and it can't do both, because the diagnostic job wants a bright line and the descriptive job wants shades of gray. If that's right, does progress mean splitting it into two concepts, or something else? And if I've got the axis wrong, tell me where the real fault line is.
