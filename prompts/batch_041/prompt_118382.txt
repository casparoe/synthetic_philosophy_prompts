My friend and I just spent most of a long drive arguing about whether Bell's theorem really shows the world is nonlocal, and we ended it exactly where we started, which is bugging me. His position: what the experiments actually rule out is one specific independence condition — whether the two outcomes stay correlated once you've fixed the settings — and that condition isnt what "locality" means. The thing that would really deserve the name, that you can't use one wing to send a signal to the other, comes through untouched, so he says the honest word for the phenomenon is "holism" or "non-separability," and "nonlocality" is just a scary label people slap on it. My position: the theorem killed local hidden variables, full stop, and renaming the corpse doesn't change that. We also got stuck on whether you're even allowed to drop the assumption that the measurement settings and whatever's going on in the box are statistically independent, or whether that move is just cheating to save the picture. So what kind of disagreement is this actually — a fight about the word "local," a real disagreement about which assumptions the theorem needs, or the two of us answering different questions under one name? My guess is it's mostly verbal, but I don't trust the guess. And if it is verbal, which of the questions is the one worth answering, and what would count as progress here — a distinction, a theorem, or an experiment nobody's bothered to do yet?
