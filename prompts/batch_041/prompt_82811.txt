I sit on an advisory panel revising the ethics guidance for a national plant-protection service — the body that licenses insecticides, approves biological control releases, and funds integrated pest management programs. Before we assess the frameworks that have been put to us (welfarist consequentialism, rights-based views, ecocentric and ecosystem-integrity views, relational and virtue-oriented views, and the case-by-case precautionary default our agency currently follows without having chosen it), I want the specification first. Treat adequacy as relative to our purpose, which is to issue decisions we can defend in public, apply consistently across dissimilar cases, revise when the science moves, and reach within a single season rather than after a decade of further study. I am an entomologist by training with no formal philosophy background, so keep the vocabulary plain; the depth should sit in the requirements, not the jargon.

Give me the desiderata any adequate framework for this area must satisfy, as a table with these columns:

- ID (R1, R2, ...)
- Requirement — one line stating what the framework must deliver
- What it must actually produce — the test it must pass, or the output a regulator could extract from it
- Case or consideration behind it — name the test case below (or the general consideration) and identify the feature that generates the demand
- Status — non-negotiable, negotiable, or camp-specific (naming the camp whose preference it is)
- Framework families it excludes, and why

Anchor the requirements in the panel's test cases, which come from dossiers already before us:

1. The Resurgence Case. A spray is highly effective against the target moth and also kills the generalist predators that ordinarily suppress it; the following season the moth is worse than before. Assume this is established for the case.
2. The Untargetable Cousin Case. The only effective agent at the required dose also harms a protected butterfly in the same family, and no application window discriminates between them.
3. The Sterility Case. A proposal to release engineered males that sterilize a pest population, with a real prospect of local elimination of the species and a small but nonzero chance of spread beyond the release area.
4. The Baseline Case. In the untreated field the pest is held down by parasitoids that kill hosts slowly from within, and most larvae die of starvation or disease. Leaving the field alone is therefore not leaving the insects unharmed.
5. The Numbers Case. Any per-individual welfare accounting will be swamped by insect counts; a framework that counts individuals one by one makes nearly every agricultural practice look catastrophic, and one that counts only species or ecosystems makes individual suffering invisible.
6. The Resistance Case. High-dose spraying kills nearly all exposed individuals quickly but selects hard for resistance and requires refuges; low-dose spraying produces widespread sublethal impairment — disorientation, failed foraging, reduced reproduction — and selects slowly. Assume welfare and resistance management pull in opposite directions here.
7. The Relational Pest Case. The same species is a managed pollinator in one region and a damaging invader in another, and one grower's pest is another's livelihood. Our remit covers pests, so the framework must say whether pest is a biological category or a role fixed by human interests.
8. The Specificity Case. A sequence-specific spray is proposed that is designed to silence a gene in one species only. The dossier presents this precision as a new kind of ethical permission.

Then, in a second table, the conflict register — columns: Pair (IDs) | The tension | Why joint satisfaction fails, or where the trade-off must at least be made explicit | Which requirement you would weaken, and how you would reframe the survivor.

Close with a JSON object with these fields: most_exclusionary_requirement (string), why_it_excludes (string), camp_prejudices (array of strings, each naming a demand and the camp that treats it as obvious rather than argued for), excluded_families (array of strings), and one_line_summary (string). Argue from the cases; we are drafting our own document, so do not supply citations, reading lists, or attributions, and do not lean on any particular author's formulation.
