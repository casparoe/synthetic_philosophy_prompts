I've been reading rationality blogs for the best part of two decades now — back when the Sequences were going up and everyone had a handle and a credence and nothing better to do — and the one thing that's always got on my nerves is how the whole scene talks about disagreement. Everyone's got a story about how they'd definately update if you gave them a double crux, and then in practice it turns into splitting the difference with whoever's loudest, or digging in and calling it 'being a good Bayesian'. I want to write something sending that up, and I want you to draft it.

Give me an agony-aunt column. Invent a columnist — call her Auntie Concede or whatever you like — for a fictional magazine aimed at aspiring rationalists. She is a fully committed conciliationist: equal weight, split the difference, always. She never breaks character, never winks at the reader, and never gives a hedge like 'it's complicated' or 'talk to someone qualified' — every reply is a definite ruling, and every ruling has to follow consistently from her view, even when it walks her somewhere ridiculous. The funny has to come out of the actual machinery (equal weight, independence, what makes someone a peer, whether the view can survive itself), not out of 'lol epistemologists are odd'. Someone who's read Elga and Kelly and Christensen should laugh and also wince in recognition; someone who hasn't should still follow what's going on.

Format: five or six letters from readers with her replies underneath, roughly 700 words in total, in British English. The letters should let her walk into the real trouble spots rather than invented ones. At minimum:

- two of her correspondents disagree with each other, so she has to say whether you average their averages, treat the pair as one voice, or something else — and say how she knows they're independent;
- one about what even counts as a peer: a colleague with the same training, a barber, a stranger on a forum, and her own past self from a decade ago when she was, in her words, thick;
- one where a reader points out that plenty of thoughtful people think conciliationism is itself mistaken, and asks whether she's obliged to conciliate about conciliationism;
- one where somebody has followed her advice for years, now sits at 0.5 on everything, and can't decide whether to take a job.

Please don't put words in any living philosopher's mouth, and don't let the punchline be just 'philosophers, eh'. No fake citations and no made-up quotations from real books — I don't want anything in inverted commas that's pretending to be from an actual text. Just the column.
