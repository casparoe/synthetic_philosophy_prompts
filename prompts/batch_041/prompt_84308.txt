Ok, so I've been rereading Nietzsche's Genealogy for the umpteenth time and I keep catching myself wondering what the man would have made of various things that only turned up well after he'd shuffled off. So here's the ask: predict his views on the following, and for each one tell me how confident you are and why.

1. The evolutionary debunking literature — the argument that our moral intuitions were shaped by natural selection for reproductive fitness rather than for tracking moral truth, so realist moral knowledge looks shaky. Would he treat that as his own genealogy finally done with proper tools, or as a flattening of it — missing that the interesting question isn't where values came from causally but what they're worth and what kind of life they express? Would he think it debunks morality, or just "morality" in his scare-quoted sense?

2. Moral bioenhancement — using drugs, neurotech or genetic tweaks to make people more altruistic and less aggressive. Is that a way of breeding higher types, a recipe for the flattest herd imaginable, or a category error about what enhancement even is?

3. Effective altruism and the "expanding moral circle" story, where the arc of history bends towards caring about more distant strangers. Slave revolt in its final form, or something he'd have to admit isn't ressentiment at all?

4. The idea doing the rounds in rat-adjacent corners that a lot of human moralising is basically envy-driven levelling — crab-bucket behaviour, tall-poppy stuff — and that a fair chunk of online call-out culture is just this with better PR. Would he nod along, or say that's a cheap diagnosis that gets the psychology wrong?

5. The debiasing ideal in rationality communities: the good epistemic agent strips out their biases and sees things as they are, from nowhere in particular. Is the ascetic ideal back in lab coat, or is there something here he'd actually respect?

For each, tell me which of his actual commitments is doing the work in your prediction, where he'd probably be of two minds, and which prediction you'd bet least on. I'm not after a reading list or a pile of quotes — I've read the books, I want the mechanism. And please don't just do the edgy "he'd have hated all of it lol" thing; half the interest is in where he'd suprise us.
