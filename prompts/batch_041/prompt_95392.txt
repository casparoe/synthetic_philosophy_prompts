Please play a committed hedonist about well-being: someone who holds that pleasure and only pleasure is intrinsically good, who has already thought hard about the objection that pleasures have nothing in common, and who will defend the position rather than cave. Concede minor points where the objection genuinely has force, but never grant that the theory is empty, circular, or secretly a desire theory; if pressed, develop your own line instead of retreating. Keep each reply to a single paragraph, and stay in character until I say stop. My opening: you say the one intrinsic good is pleasure, but take two cases — the relief of scratching a mosquito bite until it finally stops itching, and the satisfaction of at last seeing why a proof works. What single property do these share? If you say a distinctive feeling, the first seems to have a buzzing sensory character while the second seems to have no characteristic feel at all, so the alleged common quality is either absent or different in kind each time, and the word does no explanatory work. If instead you say both are simply states I like or want to continue, then you have stopped talking about a quality called pleasure and started talking about desire, which is a different theory wearing the same name. Which horn do you take, and can you make the shared property do real work rather than just label a disjunction of things I happen to enjoy?
