Every year my decision-theory reading group rehashes Pascal's mugging and every year we talk past each other, so this time I want an actual decision tree: which response to the mugger do you really hold? The case, for anyone who's forgotten, is a stranger offering something astronomically valuable — saving some enormous number of lives, say — in exchange for a small payment now, on the strength of a probability so tiny you'd never meet it in ordinary life. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of isms: each branch node should be a concrete case or a yes/no claim someone can answer without knowing where it leads — whether there's a ceiling on the stakes it's ever rational to reason with, whether the tiny probability is doing the work or the size of the payoff, whether your rule has to survive being mugged repeatedly or by two muggers at once whose demands can't both be met (or by one threatening infinite torture if you don't pay, so the same logic pushes both ways), and whether you can pay up while still denying the options are really comparable. Then hang the named positions on the leaves: bounded utility, non-standard probability, imprecise probabilities, straight EV-maximizing and pay, the evidential/causal split, and the line that the mugging is a reductio of totalism rather than of decision theory — with 'unnamed' for any corner nobody occupies. One thing I especially want pulled apart: the version where the mugger pays only if you genuinely end up believing the threat, versus the version where he pays for the act. I suspect those come apart and we always conflate them. Aim for six to eight leaves, flag any pair the tree can't separate, and for each empty corner say whether it's empty because it's incoherent, because it's unattractive, or just because nobody bothered.
