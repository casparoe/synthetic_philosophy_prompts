I've recently begun to think of myself as religious while being unable to believe in a personal God, and a friend gave me a popular book on Einstein to help me make sense of the position. The book summarises his views roughly as follows: Einstein believed in Spinoza's God — the God of the philosophers, who reveals himself in the order of the universe; he rejected the personal God of the Bible and any afterlife, but insisted he was not an atheist. He also held that free will is an illusion, since the universe is strictly deterministic, and that praise and blame are therefore groundless. And he thought science and religion could never genuinely conflict, because science tells us what is and religion tells us what ought to be, so the two occupy separate territories. I am drawn to this picture, which is precisely why I want it checked. How much of it is fair? Which parts would a careful reader of Einstein accept, which are popular simplifications, and where does the book combine his views in a way he would have rejected? I am especially keen to know whether his denial of free will really commits him to the conclusion about blame, and whether the science-and-religion division the book describes is actually his.
