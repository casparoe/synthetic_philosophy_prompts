Write the underwriting and claims manual for a small insurance company that sells policies against miracles -- the dry sort of document with numbered definitions, exclusions, a claims procedure, a witness-credibility schedule, written by a very earnest risk department that has somehow never heard of David Hume and has arrived at his conclusions entirely on its own. Deadpan all the way; the humor has to come from the machinery being applied literally and in good faith, not from wacky surrealism. I want it to include: a clause defining what counts as a violation of the ordinary course of nature and who gets to decide; a clause covering claims backed by competing miracle reports from different religions; a schedule of witness-risk factors (stake in the outcome, education, reputation, how credulous or incredulous the times are); and a worked example where the adjuster reasons impeccably and the claim still dies in the file. It should never quite notice that its own actuarial furniture -- the premium table, the claims reserve, the fact that payouts have historically been zero -- is doing work the argument is supposed to be doing. 600-700 words, formatted like a real policy.

Then add a short note at the end, outside the manual, saying how confident you are in the Humean reading you built on -- in particular whether the argument is meant to make belief in a miracle irrational in principle or only to say that no testimony has actually met the bar -- and which bit of that your least sure of.
