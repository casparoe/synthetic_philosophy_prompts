I'm putting together a handout for a weird reading group I run — half the people work in ML, half came out of art school — and we keep talking past each other about whether image generators actually make art. I want a decision tree (taxonomy-ish is fine, but I want branches, not just a list) that takes someone from stuff they'll actually agree to and lands them on a named position. Don't just list positions — build the tree so the order of questions does some work.

Each branch node should be a concrete question a person can answer without already knowing where it leads, and each leaf should be a position with a name plus one line on its ugliest commitment. I'd say 5–7 nodes deep-ish and 5–9 leaves, but use your judgment.

The distinctions I care about, roughly in the order I think they should cut: (1) whether making art requires a mind that meant to make *that* thing; (2) whether the value lives in the product or in the process — the struggle, the choices, the risk of failure; (3) whose causal contribution counts — the prompter's, the model's, or the corpus of training images; (4) whether "creativity" is a natural kind we're discovering or an honorific we hand out and could revise.

I want leaves for at least: the tool view (model is just a fancy brush), the co-author view, the "the prompter is the artist" view, and the "nobody is, it's a folk concept" view — plus whatever third options fall out of the tree that I haven't thought of.

Two things I don't want. Don't make any node a straight yes/no on "do you think AI can be creative" — that's the thing we're trying to sort, not a question that sorts. And don't pretend the tree separates positions it can't: if two leaves come out behaviourally identical on every node, say so, and say whether that's because the views are really the same or because my nodes are bad.

Also flag the empty combinations — the answer patterns no real position seems to occupy — with a one-line diagnosis of each (incoherent, merely unattractive, or just nobody's bothered).

I don't need cites or reading, just the instrument. End the whole thing with a single last line beginning "Summary:" that says in one sentence what the tree reveals about the debate.
