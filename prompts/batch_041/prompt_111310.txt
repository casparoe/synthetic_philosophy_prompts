So I've got a running argument with my two roommates about which markets should just be flat-out illegal, and when I actually write down my gut calls they don't obviously hang together. I'm a backend engineer, never took a philosophy class past the one gen-ed requirement, so I'm probably missing whatever the standard answer is here. Anyway, here's where I land. An adult sells a kidney for cash: I'd legalize it tomorrow. Selling your vote: jail, no question. A guy with a truck of bottled water selling it for $20 a bottle after a hurricaine knocks out the power: should be illegal, that's gouging. Somebody buys 40 concert tickets and resells them at 5x face value: eh, that's fine, the market's the market, I don't care. Paid surrogacy: fine, she's a grown adult making a deal. A company pays new parents to name their kid after its brand: feels gross, I'd ban it. Paying a stranger to serve your prison sentence for you: obviously a crime, and not just because of the paperwork. Paying your own kid $50 a grade: fine, whatever gets them to study.

What bugs me is I can't tell if these add up to one thing or if I'm just being inconsistent. The kidney call and the water-gouging call seem to point opposite ways, since both are somebody in a bad spot paying a lot for something they badly need. The ticket thing vs the water thing look like the same move at different stakes. Meanwhile the baby-name ban and the kidney green-light both seem to be about selling something kind of personal, so why does one gross me out and the other not? And surrogacy vs baby-naming is close enough that I worry I'm just going on vibes.

So: is there a principle, or a small set of principles, that actually gets my eight calls right, and for each clause of it, which call is that clause there to explain? Then run it on a case I haven't decided yet: a hospital auctions off a single spot in a scarce experimental treatment to whoever bids highest, and the losers get nothing. Is that the kidney case, the water case, or its own thing? Please start your answer with your bottom line in one sentence and then give the reasoning -- no preamble, and don't restate what I asked.
