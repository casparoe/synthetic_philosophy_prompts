I keep flip-flopping on algorithmic fairness and I'd like you to work out what I actually beleive. My gut verdicts:

- A lender scores loan applicants. In both groups the score is calibrated (of those scored 0.2, about 20% default). Group X defaults more often, so fewer X applicants clear the cutoff. Fine by me — the same score means the same thing.
- A recidivism tool, calibrated the same way, but with a much higher false-positive rate for the group that's already over-policed. Not fine. I genuinely can't say why that differs from the loan case — maybe it's that the error costs someone their liberty rather than some money, or that the group carrying the errors is the one already behind.
- A hiring model that ignores race but leans on postcode and shopping data, and rejects one group at twice the rate. Not fine — feels like laundering.
- A scholarship that reserves slots so both groups are picked at equal rates, even if it means taking some weaker candidates from the better-off group. Fine — the point is opportunity, not prophecy.
- The same parity trick for a model predicting who has a rare disease. Absurd; you can't force equal positive rates onto a fact.

I suspect my rule is something like "match the target to the model's job: predicting a fact, calibrate; allocating an opportunity, equalise rates" — but the recidivism case breaks it, since that model's job is prediction and I still call it unfair.

I know there's a family of impossibility results here; I don't want the lecture, I want to know which horn I'm actually on and what the cleanest principle is that saves most of my verdicts. Test it on a case I haven't given, tell me which of my verdicts it makes me give up, and say where the principle is most likely to be wrong and what would change your mind.

Format: JSON with "principle" (one sentence), "clauses" (each with the clause, the case that forces it, and the verdict it yields), "new_cases" (two cases I didn't give, each with verdict and reason), "verdicts_i_lose" (list), "confidence" (0–1), and "what_would_flip_it".
