I'm a student in a course on African philosophy and personhood. Next month we are hosting a visiting scholar who defends a moderate communitarian reading of Ubuntu: on her view, the community is constitutive of individual agency and identity, but persons are not reducible to their relationships, and the normative ideal of personhood as an achievement—something one can fail to attain—is compatible with individual rights. Please act as an expert in African philosophy and draft eight to ten questions for the Q&A. I want at least one question each on (a) the status versus achievement distinction: whether treating personhood as an achievement rather than a status excludes infants, the severely cognitively disabled, or the socially marginalised; (b) the objection that Ubuntu is ethnophilosophy—a romanticised invention rather than a genuine philosophical tradition—and how her reading avoids it; and (c) whether her moderate view can resist the charge that it quietly imports Western individualism. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Begin by brainstorming the strongest objections to her position, then turn them into questions. Think carefully about the difference between the metaphysical and the normative senses of personhood.
