I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I've run into a disagreement at work that I can't adjudicate on my own. My company has two engineering teams of the same size, with the same tooling, the same deadlines, and the same bonus structure; one ships on time almost every quarter and the other slips almost every quarter. When I asked my manager why, he said it comes down to culture: the first team has a culture of finishing things and the second one doesn't. I objected that this looked circular, since the only evidence I can see for the culture is the very behavior it is supposed to explain. He disagreed, and said something I keep turning over: the incentives and the processes are identical on paper, and the culture is what determines how people read those incentives in the first place, so the culture is doing real causal work rather than merely labeling the outcome.

Two other people have given me flatly opposed reactions. A friend with a master's in economics says the whole idea is confused: once you have explained each engineer's individual decisions in terms of that engineer's own reasons and circumstances, there is no work left for the culture to do, because it is a summary of individual facts rather than an additional cause of them. A colleague who used to work in evolutionary biology shrugged and said that maybe the culture is not what makes the team ship, but what makes shipping practices persist once someone stumbles into them, and that this is a different claim from the one my manager is making.

I can't tell whether these positions actually conflict, whether one of them is confused, or whether they are answers to different questions, and I have no idea what evidence would settle the matter; every test I propose (swap the two teams onto each other's projects, watch what a new hire does, change the incentives and see what happens) strikes me as something I would not know how to interpret.

Please answer with exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. In whatever order makes sense to you, I would like: what the disagreement is really about once it is stated precisely; whether the culture can be a cause at all given that the individual-level account is complete; whether my circularity objection is fatal or answerable; whether the persistence-versus-origin suggestion changes the picture; and what the tests I described would and would not show, along with what would make my manager's claim false. I am after the structure of the problem, not management advice or reading recommendations.
