Then the carrying example. I'm tempted by the demon (the one who'll destroy something you care about unless you come to admire him), and I'm also tempted by something closer to home for this crowd -- a museum whose endowment depends on the trustees sincerely coming to admire a mediocre painting, or a firm that pays analysts a bonus for finding a report convincing -- and I want you to pick which one carries the session, say what it has to have and what it can't have (if the room reads it as just self-deception, or just prudence versus evidence, I've lost them before we get to the interesting part), and tell me why not the demon if you'd go the other way. The misconception map matters as much to me as the order, so give it to me in the order they'll actually form, with the point in the sequence where I should head each one off.
