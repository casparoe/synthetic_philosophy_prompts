ok so I just got hired as a public defender and I've been assigned to Judge Kessler's courtroom, which apparently is the courtroom everyone warns you about. My supervisor told me to read through her sentancing opinions before my first plea deadline, and there's four of them — well, five — that I can't square with each other:

- Texting driver runs a red, kills a 15-year-old on a bike. Max: 12 years. She spends two pages on how he "chose his phone over a child's life."
- Same crash, someone dies, except this guy had a stroke at the wheel — no warning, nothing he could have done, the doctors agreed. Probation, no jail. She writes that punishing a man for something that wasn't his doing accomplishes nothing.
- Guy empties a revolver at his ex outside a bar, misses every shot, nobody's hurt. Nine years, basically what he'd have gotten if he'd hit her.
- 19-year-old with two shoplifting priors steals a car and wraps it around a fence. Nobody hurt. Diversion program, and she says a prison term "would only make him worse."
- Woman shoots her husband after 20 years of beatings. His brother writes in begging the court not to lock her up. Probation and counseling.

New one, and I need to know what to tell my client before he decides whether to plead: he's 52. At 22 he killed a guy in a bar fight, panicked, ran, and has been a model citizen ever since — married, coaches soccer, zero arrests in 30 years. DNA from the old scene finally matched him. He's not dangerous, everyone agrees, and locking him up now costs the state a fortune and prevents nothing. The victim's family has been on the local news every week saying they've waited 30 years and want him to die in a cell.

What's she gonna do with him? And the part I actually need: what rule is she running — is she sentencing people to what they deserve, or to whatever the sentence will accomplish, or to what the victims' families need? Which of those fit all five earlier cases just as well? If they come apart on my guy, what other kind of case should I watch her docket for that would tell me which rule she's really on? If there's a name for the thing she's doing, tell me, and if you had to bet on my guy, which way and why?
