I've also been reading some philosophy-of-action stuff about akrasia / weakness of will and I keep having the feeling that everyone is describing the same thing in a worse language. So here's what I want. Take the main positions in the weakness-of-will debate and restate each one in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can:

(a) the Socratic / Hare-style line that genuine akrasia is impossible, because a real all-things-considered judgment about what's best is supposed to settle what you do;
(b) the Davidson-style line that it *is* possible, because the all-things-considered judgment and the unconditional judgment you actually act on are two different things;
(c) the revisionist line I half remember, that weakness of will is really about abandoning your resolutions too easily, and not about acting against your better judgment at all.

Put each one into beta-delta / intrapersonal-game / commitment-device terms.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. What translates cleanly? What only translates if you fudge it? What has no counterpart in my vocabulary whatsoever? And for each bit of residue, tell me whether it's my framework missing something real, or the philosophers' claim turning out vaguer than it sounded once you try to operationalize it.

Things I specifically want you to be honest about rather than smooth over:

- In the multiple-selves picture there is no single agent who judges one thing best and then does another; there are just temporally indexed orderings and whoever is at the controls wins. Is that a translation of Davidson's point or a rejection of the question? Say which, and don't hedge.
- My machinery seems to need a notion of the agent's true or long-run preference (the delta-discounted one) before it can even call present bias a *bias*. Is that the same thing as the philosophers' better judgment, or is the normativity different in kind?
- Do I have any counterpart of a resolution or an intention -- a state whose job is to stop you re-opening a question you've already settled -- or does everything like that collapse into an external commitment device (i.e. something outside the agent doing the work)?
- Socrates and Hare say akrasia is impossible; hyperbolic discounting says it's not just possible but pretty much inevitable. Are those even answering the same question, or is one a claim about rationality and the other a claim about mechanism?
- Does any part of the philosophical debate need akrasia with no delay at all -- you judge e best and do f right now, same instant, no timing difference? If so my model may not have room for it, since the whole apparatus is built out of timing. Tell me if I'm wrong there.

And at the end give me your own call: does the discounting picture explain akrasia, dissolve it, or quietly change the subject?
