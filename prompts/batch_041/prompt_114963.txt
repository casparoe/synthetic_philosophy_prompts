In De Corpore, Hobbes presents geometry as the paradigm of demonstrative science and locates its demonstrative character in construction: because the geometer himself draws the lines and figures he reasons about, their causes lie within his power, so geometry can proceed from principles that are known rather than merely assumed. Yet Hobbes also treats the geometer's definitions as stipulations—names imposed upon constructions—rather than as reports of antecedently given essences. Please compare these two strands of his view and assess whether they can be held together.

Begin by separating the epistemic role of construction, which is what makes geometry demonstrable and thus the model that civil philosophy is supposed to emulate, from the semantic role of construction, which is what makes geometrical definitions arbitrary impositions of names. Then press on the tension. On the stipulation reading, does a rival geometer who defines his terms differently err, or does he merely disagree? If he merely disagrees, Hobbes's insistence that his own quadrature of the circle is a genuine discovery about the circle—and that Wallis and his allies are simply mistaken—looks unmotivated. If he errs, we need an account of the constraint on definitions that the stipulation reading appears to deny Hobbes. Consider, as a test case, a geometer who stipulates a definition of the circle under which his quadrature succeeds: is Hobbes committed to granting that the construction is correct relative to that definition, and if so, what remains of his claim to have found the true quadrature?

Finally, consider whether the demonstrability criterion is better read as a criterion of intelligibility than as a criterion of truth, and whether that reading rescues the coherence of Hobbes's position at the cost of reducing geometry's necessity to something conditional on our definitions. Conclude by saying which of the two strands you take to be Hobbes's more fundamental commitment, and whether the circle-squaring controversy is evidence for your answer or merely a symptom of his mathematical limitations. Be explicit about where you are reconstructing a position rather than reporting one.
