I'm in a seminar on AI and society, and there's a pattern I keep noticing that I'm definately sure has a name, only I can't find it. A friend of mine started a newsletter to explain research carefully; now it's mostly dunking on people, and if you say that, he says he's just following the arguments where they lead and that objecting is nostalgia. Nothing he did at any single point was indefensible. At the food co-op I work at it went feeding people, then serving more people, then hitting our numbers, and now everyone inside agrees that hitting the numbers is serving people. And the AI version from class: a system rewarded for being helpful ends up optimizing for the reward. In all three cases each step gets judged by the standard the previous steps produced, so the drift is invisible from the inside, and from the outside you can't tell it from genuine improvement, because the only people who could have flagged it are the ones who already changed. Is there a name for that? Value drift doesn't sound right -- that sounds like a system's goals changing while it's busy doing something else -- and Goodhart's law is about a proxy getting gamed, not about a person or an organization becoming something else with no moment where it happened. What I'm after is the structure where nothing goes wrong at any particular moment because every step is endorsed by the very thing that's drifting. Is that one phenomenon, or am I lumping three things together, and is there any real work on when an agent's self-endorsement counts as evidence rather than a rubber stamp? Don't just hand me the label -- I want the edges, what counts as the same thing and what doesn't. Also say how confident you are and which claim you're least sure of.
