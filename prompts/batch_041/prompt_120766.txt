Two jurists are arguing a single case, and I want the argument itself. The case: after a coup, an assembly enacts the Restoration of Order Act. Section 1 bars any investigation or prosecution of acts done between 1974 and 1990 "in defence of the constitutional order". Section 2 provides that such acts "shall be treated as lawful at the time they were done". Section 3 provides that the Act "shall be construed and applied in conformity with the requirements of legality and the rule of law". The assembly also purports to entrench the Act, so that it can be amended only by a two-thirds vote of the assembly itself. The constitution, for its part, guarantees that a person injured by an unlawful act shall have a remedy. A survivor of torture in 1978 now sues a former officer for damages, and the courts must decide whether the claim can proceed.

Write a dialogue of exactly five numbered exchanges between a legal positivist and a natural lawyer, each equally competent, each answering the other's actual arguments rather than restating their own. Neither may rest on a slogan: the positivist not on "it was validly enacted, so it is law", the natural lawyer not on "it is unjust, so it is not law". Both should work through what Section 3 does to their position, and through whether they disagree about what the law is or about what a judge should do.

Exactly five numbered exchanges, with no introduction and no closing remarks. If the case as I have set it up makes one of the two positions untenable, or collapses the dispute into a merely verbal one, say so plainly — even if that means the five exchanges cannot be written as I have asked.
