Dan and I spent two hours last night arguing about whether psychiatric diagnoses pick out anything real, and he left me with an arguement on the back of a receipt that I can't stop chewing on. Tidied up it goes like this:

(1) A category is real only if its members would have been there anyway, whether or not we'd invented the label — the classification has to be discovering a division that was already in the world, not drawing one.
(2) Psychiatric diagnoses don't sit still. Hand people a label and they read up on it, take on the symptoms, build identities and support groups around it, and the presentation itself shifts — so much that the next edition of the manual has to redraw the category to fit the people the last one made. Think shell shock turning into combat fatigue turning into PTSD.
(3) So for these categories, who's in and where the edges are is partly produced by the classifying.
(4) And if the membership is produced by our classifying, then the category isn't tracking anything that was there independently of us.
(5) It also never converges. Every redrawing makes new people who don't fit the new box, so unlike, say, the elements, these categories never settle into something that stays put under investigation.
(6) Therefore psychiatric kinds aren't real kinds, and psychiatry isn't a science of anything — it's a machine that manufactures the people it then claims to have discovered.

The parts I want to keep are (2) and (5). That's the most interesting thing anyone's ever said to me about psychiatry and I dont want to give it up just to win. But (6) can't be right. The same reasoning chews up gender, money, being a teenager, being left-handed, being unemployed — and when I said so he just shrugged and said fine, none of those are real either, which is the point where I stopped believing him. He also says the same thing happens in economics, where the theory changes the behavior, and I've never heard anyone conclude that there are no economic facts. He says there's a name for this, the looping effect, and that it's some philosopher of science's whole thing, which I've never read.

So which step goes, and what does dropping it cost me? If I keep (2) and (5), what's the cheapest thing left to give up, and can I still keep the interesting part? And is there a sense of 'real' that's weak enough to let psychiatric kinds in but strong enough to still be worth wanting? I'd rather not pay with (5).
