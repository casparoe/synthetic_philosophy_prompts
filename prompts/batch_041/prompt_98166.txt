Okay so I got into a fight with a friend about that finding where the more equal a country is between men and women — you know, the ones that top those gender-gap rankings — the more men and women seem to split apart in what they study and even in personality. She says it settles something big. I think her argument proves way too much but I can't find the seam. Here it is as she gave it:

(1) In the countries where men and women have the most equal rights and opportunities, they choose the most different fields, and score the most differently on personality tests.
(2) If the sorting were caused by culture — patriarchy, stereotypes, parents steering girls off math — then the most equal countries, where that culture is weakest, should show the *smallest* sorting.
(3) They show the biggest. So culture isn't what's causing the sorting.
(4) Whats left is nature: the preference differences are built in, and equality just lets them show up instead of being squashed.
(5) So a country where men and women end up in very different jobs is a country where people are free to be themselves, and pushing for 50/50 representation is basically fighting biology.

My problem is that if (5) is right, then the *least* equal countries — the ones near the bottom of the rankings — are the ones where men and women come out most alike, which would mean the most oppressive societies are the most biologically authentic and that equality is a machine for manufacturing difference. That can't be the lesson. But I can't find the step where she cheated, and honestly I do think there's *something* to the idea that freer choice produces bigger differences — I just don't think it gets her all the way to (5).

Which premise do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? If you tell me to drop (2), what am I left saying about culture? And is there a version of the argument that keeps the interesting part — that equality and difference can move together — without the absurd ending?
