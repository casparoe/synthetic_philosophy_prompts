Hey — I could use your help drafting something for a meeting on Thursday and I'm too close to it.

Quick background. I'm the resident philosopher on the grants committee of a mid-sized foundation — call it $40M a year, mostly global health, some climate. There's a proposal in front of us from a group that wants us to carve out 5% of the annual budget for what they're calling "existential resilience" work. The concrete ask is $2M over three years: some seed-bank sites in weird places, an archival project, and a small policy team working on a treaty I won't pretend to understand. The details of the projects aren't the issue. The argument for them is.

Here's their pitch. They put the chance that this cluster of work meaningfully lowers the risk of an unrecoverable collapse of industrial civilization at about one in a billion (1e-9). But if it does work, they say it safeguards a long-run future of roughly 10^16 human lives. Multiply it out: 10 million expected lives for $2M, i.e. about five lives per dollar, against our bednet portfolio at roughly one life per $4,000. They're not shy about it — their line is that "you don't get to wave off a number because it's small when the payoff is big enough, and refusing is also a bet."

Half the committee thinks this is Pascal's wager with a spreadsheet and wants to reject on sight. The other half, including our chair, thinks we'd be morally negligent to say no. I have to write the decision memo and I don't want to just split the difference and fund 2.5%.

So draft it for me. It needs to land on a concrete recommendation — fund, don't fund, or fund with conditions, with a specific dollar figure and a review date — and the reasoning has to survive the strongest version of the losing side, not a strawman. A few things I want you to actually engage rather than gesture at:

- Whether a 1e-9 estimate is even the kind of thing a funder can evaluate, and what our posture should be toward probability numbers that nobody could ever be shown to have gotten wrong. The people giving us the number are also the people who'd recieve the money, which the chair says is irrelevant and I say isn't.
- The fanaticism problem in general. If we buy the logic here, what stops it from licensing literally any grant with a big enough cosmic payoff attached, including from people who are transparently making the numbers up? I want an actual screening rule I can put in the memo, not a vibe.
- Whether it matters that the beneficiaries are in the far future and uncountable, versus our usual beneficiaries who are alive now and can be counted. If you think it doesn't matter, say why; if it does, say what weight it gets.
- If you recommend against, you owe me the rule we're adopting instead and an answer to "so you're just refusing to think about the far future." If you recommend for, you owe me the mechanism that keeps this from metastasizing into funding every long-shot anyone pitches us next cycle.

Keep it to a page or so, in the voice of a committee that will have to defend this to donors. Don't give me a literature review, I know the literature — I want a document with a bottom line.
