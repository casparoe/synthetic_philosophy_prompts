Background: I'm an infectious disease epidemiologist — did TB for a few years, now I work on vaccine hesitancy — so my working vocabulary is R0, herd immunity thresholds, SIR-type models, waning immunity, super-spreaders, competition between strains, coverage targets. Philosophy: I've read basically none of it, and I'm honestly not sure what counts as a position in it.

There's an argument I keep hearing and I want to actually understand it rather than nod along. The version you always hear is: if a society is tolerant without limits, the intolerant will use that tolerance to take over, and then there's no tolerance left, so a tolerant society is entitled to be intolerant toward the intolerant. People then say either (a) that's a practical dilemma, not a paradox at all; (b) it is a real paradox, because it makes tolerance self-undermining; (c) the move is illegitimate, because intolerance isn't one single thing and a group's refusal to extend basic protections to others isn't just another opinion sitting alongside the others; or (d) it's fine in principle, but everything turns on who gets to decide who's intolerant, and that power is the actual problem.

What I want: take that whole thing — the argument and each of those four responses — and restate it as carefully as you can in my vocabulary. Norms as things with transmission rates. Tolerant norms and intolerant norms competing in one population. Susceptibles, carriers, herd immunity as the threshold below which a norm stops reproducing itself. Tolerance as something with waning immunity, because people get tired and forget why it mattered. The tolerant society's willingness to suppress intolerance as... I don't know, a vaccine, a culling program, some intervention that acts on the population carrying the competing norm. Where the analogy is strained, don't paper over it, say where.

Then the residue, which is the part I care about alot. What carries over cleanly, what only carries over if you bend it until it means something else, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. And two specific things I want you to take a position on rather than just lay out as open questions:

1. My vocabulary has no 'should' in it. It has will-spread and won't-spread. When the philosophers say a tolerant society is entitled to suppress the intolerant, is there anything left of that claim once it's translated, or does it evaporate and leave only a prediction about who wins? If something survives, tell me exactly what it is, in my terms.

2. Is the paradox genuinely a self-reference problem — a rule about rules — or is that just a fancy way of describing a feedback loop I already have a name for? If self-reference is doing real work, say what extra thing it gives you that a feedback loop doesn't. If it gives you nothing extra, say that plainly.

Pick a side rather than handing me the menu. If you think the residue is just fog — that the philosophical version is vaguer than it sounds and my framework isn't missing anything — say so and defend it. And if you think it's the opposite, that my vocabulary is structurally blind to something here, name the thing and don't hedge it into mush.
