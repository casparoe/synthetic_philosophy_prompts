I started volunteering at a hospice two months ago and somethings been bugging me that I don't have a word for.

Here's the pattern. When I'm actually in the room with someone who's dying, what moves me is completely particular: this person, this hand, the fact that he's scared. If I caught myself thinking "I'm doing this because it's my duty as a volunteer," something would have gone wrong — that thought, in that moment, would be a contaminant. But then the family shows up and asks why I stayed, and I say "it's part of the volunteer role, we dont leave people alone at the end," and that's... fine? It's true, it's a good answer, and it does something useful: it takes the weight off them, it stops them from owing me anything personal. Nobody's being deceived. I'm not misreporting my motives, because that sentence was never offered as a report of my motives — it's a justification, and as a justification it's exactly right.

So the same proposition — I did it because it's my job — would be corrupting if it were what was actually driving me, and correct, even virtuous, if it's what I offer in answer to a why-question. The two roles seem to be governed by opposite standards. Same thing at home: my kid asks why I read to her every night, and "because I love you" is the honest answer; "because nightly reading correlates with vocabulary gains" would be monstrous as a motive, and yet if her teacher asks me why I read to her, the vocabulary point is a legitimate part of the answer and the love part is nothing the teacher can act on.

I know the Williams case about the man saving his wife, where if his deliberation had to include the thought that saving one's wife is permissible, that's one thought too many. But that's about a thought that shouldn't be in the agent's head at all. Mine is weirder: the thought is fine, it's true, it's even the right thing to say out loud, and it's still true that if it had been doing the motivating it would have wrecked the action. And I don't think it's just rationalization, because rationalization is giving a false or self-serving reason for what you did; here the reason is genuinely good, I genuinely endorse it as a justification, I just wasn't acting on it.

Is there a name for this? I keep reaching for self-effacement, but that seems to be about a theory being true while it's better that people not believe it, which is about belief, not about the gap between a motive and a justification. And the integrity objections in the Williams/Stocker vein seem to be about theories demanding the wrong motive, not about one proposition being right in one role and wrong in the other. I'd be happy to be told this already has a name, but mostly I want the carving: what exactly is the asymmetry, what's the nearest established concept and how does it fall short, where's the line between this and plain hypocrisy or rationalization, and does it generalize past motives — can a justification be correct and also be correct that it never be anyone's reason? If there's no name, give me one, define it, and tell me what falls outside it.
