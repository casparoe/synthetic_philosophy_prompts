I've recently retired from a career in ecology -- thirty-odd years of counting things in quadrats, mostly -- and since then I've been reading alot of moral philosophy, which is a different beast entirely, but I think I've definately got a real objection to something and I'd rather test it against a good opponent than a straw man.

So here's what I want: play a committed defender of what I'll call the bargaining picture of moral compromise -- the view that when two value systems collide, there's almost always some split of the difference, some package deal, some trade that both sides should rationally prefer to fighting, so the only question is how to find it, not whether it exists. Give this character real resources: they can lean on the point that value pluralism doesn't obviously mean values are incomparable, can talk about logrolling across separate issues, integrative versus distributive bargaining, trading on what each side cares about less -- the whole apparatus, whatever they've actually got. Stay in character until I say timeout. Don't caricature the position; if my objection is weak, say so and push back hard. Keep your replies to a paragraph or two so it feels like a back-and-forth, and lean on concrete cases rather than just names of positions, because half the names I won't know.

I'm the skeptic, and here's my opening move, which comes out of my old line of work. In ecology, when two closely related species meet and interbreed along a contact zone, you get what's called a hybrid zone. Sometimes it's a stable, productive band. But sometimes the hybrids are sterile, or the whole zone collapses and one species just swamps the other. I think moral compromise between value systems is like that, and I suspect your gains-from-trade story only ever describes the happy case. When two value systems meet, the surplus you want to split might be real -- or it might be a phantom, like averaging two quantities that don't share a unit. So my question is this: what plays the role of the sterile hybrid in a moral compromise, and how could anyone tell, from the inside of a case, which kind they're in? Because if you can't answer that, I don't think you've shown compromise is always available -- you've just assumed the cases where it is.
