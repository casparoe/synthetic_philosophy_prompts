I am presenting a paper next month to a joint statistics and machine learning reading group. My thesis is that Solomonoff induction does not justify Occam's razor but quietly presupposes it: the prior is fixed only up to the choice of reference machine, the invariance theorem buys an additive constant in log-probability, and that constant is precisely the size of the inductive bias the argument was supposed to deliver, so the reply that machine-dependence washes out is a claim about the limit rather than about the sample sizes anyone actually has. The audience is three Bayesian nonparametrics people who regard minimum description length as Solomonoff done properly, two empirical reinforcement learning researchers who think the whole framework is a distraction from what works, and one philosopher of science who writes about curve-fitting and simplicity. Prepare my Q&A prep sheet: the objections I will actually get, in the order I will get them, phrased the way each of these people would phrase them, ordered by how much damage they do, each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft, plus the one question I am hoping nobody asks. Give me a definite verdict rather than a survey: tell me which objections are fatal, which I should concede up front, and which rest on a misreading of my claim, and if you think the central thesis is wrong, say so plainly. Write in American English.
