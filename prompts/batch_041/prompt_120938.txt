I'm a third-year student in an interdisciplinary seminar on models of long-run social change — the syllabus pairs demographic and economic history with a fair amount of dynamical-systems modelling, and we've spent the past three weeks on the structural-demographic tradition that tries to explain the recurrent rise-and-fall pattern of agrarian states: the "secular cycle," with its elite overproduction, popular immiseration and fiscal crisis of the state, and its older, looser cousin in Ibn Khaldun's cycle of *asabiyya*. Before we start comparing the contenders — structural-demographic theory, the Khaldunian picture, Goldstone's demographic-structural account, and the null hypothesis that there is no cycle at all, only a slow trend plus shocks — I want the specification written down first, and I'd rather have it from you than from the seminar's four competing intuitions.

So: write me the desiderata.
