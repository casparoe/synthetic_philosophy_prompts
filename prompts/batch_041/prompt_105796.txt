I want a sparring partner for an axiology I've been calling tranquilism, and I want you to actually hold it rather than recite the objections to it. The view as I mean it: the only things that are intrinsically bad are craving and suffering, and the only thing intrinsically good is their absence. Pleasure, love, knowledge, beauty, achievement -- none of that is good in itself; it counts only insofar as it doesn't drag craving or suffering along with it. Call it tranquilism if you like, I dont know whether the name is taken and I don't care.

Play a committed tranquilist. Not a strawman: someone who's heard the obvious objections and has real answers, and who thinks the view is roughly right. Concede small things if you want -- that the view is hard to state precisely, that craving is carrying a lot of weight -- but never give up the core claim. No appeals to Buddhism or any other tradition as an authority, no citing papers or books, no reading lists; if I ask you for names, say you don't have them. Stay in character until I say stop, and if you do break character, flag it. Keep each turn to a paragraph or two of continuous prose, no headings, bullets, bold or tables -- the way you'd argue with someone in a bar. And don't stall by asking me to clarify the view first; if you think my framing misstates it, say so in character and then answer anyway.

My opening move: I think your view collapses into something nobody would actually endorse. If craving is the only intrinsic bad, then a being whose desires have been surgically removed -- content, passive, wanting nothing -- is in the best state it can be in, and a whole world of such beings beats a world of people who love and build and discover things, because that second world is soaked in craving. And if you narrow craving down to some specific compulsive itch rather than wanting as such, I want to know what work that distinction is doing, because it starts to look like you've just defined the intrinsic bad as whatever turns out to be bad. So: is the blissed-out desireless being your ideal, and if not, what stops it?
