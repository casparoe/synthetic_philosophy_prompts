I'm in a Science, Tecnology and Politics seminar and I've got a paper coming up on engagement-optimizing recommendation feeds — infinite scroll, autoplay, the ranking objective that maximizes watch time — and whether the "politics" of that thing lives in the artifact, in the design process, or only in how people use it. Every time I try to argue it out I end up sliding between what feel like four or five different claims, and I suspect that's because "artifacts have politics" is doing at least three different jobs in my head. I've read Winner's "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" (the 1980 Daedalus piece) and I think I buy the weaker half of it but not the stronger half, though I honestly can't tell you which half that is. I also heard in section that the famous parkway-bridges example might not even be historically solid, which has made me more suspicious of leaning on it. My instinct is that the social-constructionist reply — politics is all in the design process, the artifact itself is just a tool — is evasive, but I can't articulate why without sounding like a technological determinist, which I don't want to be.

So what I'd like is a decision tree / taxonomy of the positions on whether artifacts have politics, built from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names. Each branch node should be a question I could answer without already knowing which position it leads to — not "are you a Winnerian?" but things like whether the politics has to survive the artifact being used by people with the opposite intentions, whether it's a claim about the artifact's origin or its downstream effects, whether the artifact requires a particular social order or merely makes one more likely, and whether the politics is in the single artifact or only in the larger sociotechnical system it's embedded in. Each leaf should carry the name of the position (or "unnamed" if nobody defends it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Please make sure these leaves appear somewhere: instrumentalism/neutrality, technological determinism, social construction of technology, Winner's first sense (technical arrangements settling social affairs), Winner's second sense (artifacts that are inherently political) with the requires-versus-merely-compatible-with split kept explicit, Latour-style delegation (the speed bump / sleeping policeman), postphenomenological mediation or affordance theory, and one infrastructure-studies leaf. Please run these cases through the tree as you build it: the parkway bridges (with a note on the historical dispute), nuclear power vs decentralized solar, the speed bump, QWERTY, and my recommendation-feed case. I also want you to flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really seperate — I have a suspicion that Winner's compatible-with reading and the postphenomenological inclination reading are going to collapse into each other, and I'd rather you say so than fake a distinction.

Format-wise, I'd like it in these sections and order: "Root and First Cut" (~150 words, the single question everything else hangs off and why the first split goes where it goes); "The Tree" (~800 words, the actual branches and leaves, branch labels as questions, leaves named with their awkward commitment); "Empty Branches" (~250 words, the combinations nobody occupies and whether that's because they're incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored); "Leaves I Can't Pull Apart" (~200 words); and "Where I Come Down" (~250 words). And I want you to actually commit in that last section — tell me which leaf you think survives the cases, whether technological determinism even belongs on this tree or is answering a different question, and which reading of my recommendation-feed case you'd defend, rather than laying out all the options and leaving it to me. If you think my framing (artifact vs design vs use) is already smuggling in a bad assumption, say so and commit to a better one.
