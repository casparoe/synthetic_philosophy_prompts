I've been reading a popular introduction to Marx, and its chapter on Capital summarizes the critique roughly as follows. Marx's indictment of capitalism has two parts. The first is exploitation: since all value comes from labor, and capitalists pay workers less than the value their labor creates, the surplus is pocketed as profit — so capitalism is at bottom a system of theft, and the labor theory of value is what proves it. The second is alienation: under capitalism work becomes meaningless and estranged, and Marx treats this psychological suffering as the deeper harm, with exploitation as its merely material shadow. The remedy, on this reading, is to pay workers the full value of their product — or better, let them own the firm — after which exploitation and alienation dissolve together.

Then say whether the chapter's concluding inference, that exploitation and alienation stand or fall together, survives once the summary is corrected.
