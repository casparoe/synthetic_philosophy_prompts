For most of my career I have covered science and ideas, and for a long stretch my beat was measurement: calibration laboratories, uncertainty budgets, conformity assessment, and the endless committee arguments over how a specification limit becomes an accept-or-reject decision. I am fluent in that vocabulary the way a beat reporter gets fluent — I know the difference between the measurand and the measurement result, I know what a coverage interval is for, and I know that a tolerance is usually a number somebody negotiated.

I am now writing a feature on the philosophical debate about vagueness, and I want to understand it through the only technical vocabulary I actually command. The two positions I am trying to get straight are these. Epistemicism: a borderline case — a man with 4,999 hairs on his head, a pile of sand that is neither clearly a heap nor clearly not one — has a determinate answer, bald or not, heap or not; we simply cannot know where the line falls. Supervaluationism: in a borderline case there is no fact of the matter, though the sentence may still count as true if it comes out true on every admissible way of sharpening the predicate, and false if it comes out false on every such sharpening.

What I want from you is this. Restate that disagreement as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary of a measurement lab and its regulator — measurand and specification, tolerance and acceptance limit, measurement uncertainty, guard band, decision rule, false accept and false reject, conformity assessment — and then give me the residue report. Which commitments of each position carry over cleanly into that vocabulary, which carry over only if I distort them, and which cannot be said in it at all. Treat the residue as the payoff rather than as a footnote, and be specific about where the mapping breaks. Is the lab's ignorance about a borderline part the same kind of thing as the epistemicist's ignorance, or is the tolerance limit simply not the sort of thing that could have been true or false in the first place? Does the sorites reasoning — the step-by-step slide from the clearly bald to the clearly hirsute — have any purchase in a framework where the cutoff is stipulated rather than discovered? And does anything in the debate, such as the further question whether the boundary of the borderline is itself vague, correspond to anything I would recognise as uncertainty about the decision rule rather than about the object under test?

Please do not hedge into a survey of possible mappings. Commit: tell me which of the two positions the metrological vocabulary actually supports, and whether the leftover — whatever will not translate — is a defect in the philosophical debate or a defect in my vocabulary. And keep the translation honest: do not quietly import the lab's conventions and then report that the philosophers were confused about something that was a convention all along.
