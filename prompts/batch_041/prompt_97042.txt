Write me a short story that puts the "what is an emotion" question under real pressure, in a concrete way rather than as a debate. Setup: an ordinary workplace — an office, a hospital ward, a kitchen, pick one — nothing sci-fi, no neuroscience lab — and three coworkers who each, without ever announcing it, quietly stand in for one of the three big answers: emotions are judgments/evaluations, emotions are perceptions (of the body, or of value), emotions are just feelings. I definately don't want them naming any theory or citing anyone; I want the disagreement to come out through what each of them notices and what they say about one specific episode.

The episode: someone on the team gets hit by a strong emotion that's weirdly detached from any belief — a dread or a mood with no object, just a grey pressure that shows up before anything happens — and then later the same person has an emotion that flatly contradicts what they consciously judge (they know perfectly well the thing is fine, and still recoil). Make both of those vivid. And make me feel the pull of all three views without letting any one of them win: the objectless one should be the case the judgment view has to sweat for, and the contradicts-your-own-judgment one should be where "it's just a feeling" starts to feel thin, because the emotion seems to be presenting something, tracking something, even if it's getting it wrong.

Constraints: under ~800 words, present tense, dialogue carrying more weight than description, no lecture-y paragraphs where a character explains the philosophy at the reader, and don't resolve it — end on something concrete, not a verdict. Also skip the spider/phobia thing, thats the example everyone reaches for. And I'd rather the three coworkers not be clean mouthpieces — they should each be a little wrong about the others, and about themselves.
