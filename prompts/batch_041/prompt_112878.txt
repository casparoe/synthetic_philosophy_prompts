So here's my situation. I got drafted onto this small working group — four of us, no budget, meeting for maybe six weeks — and we have to hand a short memo to a city council. The council is trying to decide what to do about an old endowment and two buildings downtown that were financed back in the 1700s off the slave trade. The money's long gone, the people are long dead, the buildings are full of tenants. And every time we sit down, someone raises the big question and we all go quiet: can the city be guilty of something that happened before anybody alive was born? Jaspers is what everyone reaches for — I think it's four kinds of guilt, criminal, political, moral, and metaphysical, or something in that neighborhood — but honestly at this point I think we invoke him to avoid deciding anything.

What I actually want from you is a prioritized list. Of the open questions in this territory — what guilt even attaches to when the agent is a collective and the acts are generations back, whether what's inherited is guilt or debt or just responsibility, what inheritance does to identity, whether "the nation" is even the right unit versus some institution that never dissolved, all of it — which ones should a group like ours sink our six weeks into, and in what order? Defend the ordering. I care about the reasoning more than the list: which questions are upstream of the others, which are answerable at all in six weeks by four people with day jobs, which ones sound deep but wouldn't change a single line of our memo, and which are the ones where the councils decision actually hinges on the answer.

Two things I keep going back and forth on, so factor them in. First, I've been trying to split the whole thing into three seperate ledgers — who owes what to whom, who's to blame, and what makes the current residents of a place the same agent as the people who did the thing — and I genuinely can't tell whether that third one is a real distinct question or just the other two wearing a hat. Second, one of us keeps saying the nation framing is a trap and that we should be talking instead about institutions that never dissolved: the endowment still exists as a legal entity, the buildings still stand, somebody still signs the checks. Is that a genuine dissolution of the problem or just relocating it somewhere we find more comfortable?
