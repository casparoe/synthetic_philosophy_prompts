I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of mind, and for the past year I've sat in a reading group with two colleagues — one an illusionist about phenomenal consciousness, the other a phenomenal realist — whose exchanges have hardened into mutual incomprehension. I want the document that a genuine adversarial collaboration between them should have produced, written so that both could put their names to it.

To make the exercise bite, adopt the following three-way disambiguation of what illusionism might mean, and have each side locate itself and the other within it, or explain why the trichotomy is itself misleading:

(i) eliminativism: there are no phenomenal properties, and phenomenal consciousness names nothing at all;
(ii) fictionalism: phenomenal discourse is a useful fiction rather than a description of anything real;
(iii) no-further-fact (deflationary) illusionism: introspective reports are about real, reportable mental states, but those states carry no phenomenal properties over and above their functional and representational ones — the illusion is only that we take there to be such a further property.

Then produce the following.

1. A numbered set of agreed claims: the substantive propositions both can sign. Cover what both accept about the existence and character of introspective judgments, about the meta-problem (why creatures like us come to think and talk in phenomenal terms at all), about which explanatory-gap and no-go arguments do and do not establish what, and about what would count as progress. Keep these non-vacuous — a claim any position would accept does not belong here.

2. The precise residual disagreements, each phrased in the words its own side would use, and each flagged as substantive or merely verbal; if you judge part of the dispute verbal, say which part and why.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each, state a case, argument, or empirical result both sides would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction about it in advance.

Throughout, separate what is settled among people who work on this from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus. Note explicitly where the illusionist side is not one view — the three readings above already pull it apart — and where the realist side is likewise a family rather than a single position.

End with a final line of exactly the form \answer{...} containing a short phrase naming the single deepest remaining crux.
