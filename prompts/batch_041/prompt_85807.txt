A colleague who works on European integration leans on the bicycle analogy: the EU, she says, is like a bicycle — it stays upright only while it is moving, so any pause, any opt-out, any "variable geometry" is a fall. On that basis she argues that ever-closer union is a structural necessity rather than a political choice, that the EU earns its legitimacy by delivering results rather than by being chosen, and that complaints about a democratic deficit are a distraction, since a bicycle that stops to redesign itself has already toppled. Audit the analogy for me. Give the correspondence table — what plays the rider, the pedals, the momentum, the road, the fall — then the places where the mapping breaks: is a political order really unstable when stationary the way a bicycle is, does the EU have a single rider with a single heading, and is falling a discrete catastrophe or merely a slower pace? Tell me which disanalogies matter for her conclusion and which are idle, and whether the necessity claim survives once they are on the table. If it does not, say what analogy would carry the point she wants better.
