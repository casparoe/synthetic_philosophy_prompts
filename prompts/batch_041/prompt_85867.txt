I am assembling a short piece of sustained deadpan satire for a humanities-humor venue, and I want it written as a mock referee's report of the kind a journal sends back to an author. The manuscript under review is a reconstruction of the historical Jesus that proceeds entirely by the standard criteria of authenticity — dissimilarity, embarrassment, multiple attestation, coherence, traces of Semitic idiom, the tendency of the developing tradition, and whatever else you judge to belong to that toolkit. The referee is a careerist bureaucrat of the discipline: patient, humorless, entirely without malice, and takes the criteria far more seriously than their own proponents ever did. The comedy should arise from applying them consistently.

A few constraints. Build the report as a numbered evaluation, one criterion per section, with a scoring rubric and a final recommendation, so that the parody is also a demonstration: by the end, the criteria should have stripped the author's Jesus down to nothing, and then stripped the referee's own report down to nothing by the same logic. Let the reductio do the work. The referee must never wink, editorialize, or announce that the method is absurd — never say the criteria are broken, only that they are being applied. Effects worth engineering if they fall out naturally: independence collapsing because the Gospels share sources; embarrassment cutting both ways, since the early church had motives to preserve humiliating material as well as to invent it; coherence turning vacuous once the authentic core is empty; the preference for the more difficult saying rewarding worse Greek, with a recommendation that the author's prose be made less idiomatic; and the one datum the criteria cannot touch being the crucifixion, so that the committee's advised remedy is a manuscript consisting of that sentence alone, expanded to length. Keep it legible to a reader who knows only that there were once rules for telling what Jesus really said. Refer to the criteria by their ordinary names; do not attribute them to individual scholars, cite literature, or supply a bibliography — assume no access to sources.

About 700 words for the report itself. Then, on a separate line, an appendix of no more than 150 words in your own voice rather than the referee's, stating which features of the criteria debate you have exaggerated or played straight, where the parody leans on a contested reading, and what the most effective reply from a defender of the criteria would be. Close that appendix by naming the consideration that would make you abandon the joke's verdict — the argument or evidence that would show the criteria can bear the weight the referee places on them — and say which criterion, if any, you think survives the parody.
