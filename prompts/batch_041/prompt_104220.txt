I teach a graduate seminar on the semantics–pragmatics interface, and before we start adjudicating the live disputes — embedded implicature, the boundary between implicature and presupposition, whether the maxims earn their keep — I want the standard of adequacy laid out in one place. Please write me the desiderata: the specification any theory of conversational implicature and speaker meaning must satisfy to count as adequate for our purposes. Fix the purpose as follows: the theory has to be usable as a court of appeal for settling disputes about which effects belong to what is said and which are implicated, and about whether an implicature survives when it is embedded. That is the bar; adequacy is relative to it.

Organize the specification under four headings, and under each requirement give the case or consideration that motivates it and a verdict on whether it is non-negotiable or merely the preference of one camp:

1. The phenomena the theory must explain — the full range of things we call implicature (particularized, generalized, scalar, relevance-driven, ironic, and the implicatures carried by figure and by indirection), and how it draws the line between implicature and neighboring phenomena: entailment, presupposition, and mere hinting.
2. The mechanism constraints — the properties an implicature is supposed to have (that it be recoverable by reasoning from what was said plus the context plus something like the cooperative assumption; that it be defeasible or cancellable without contradiction; that it be detachable or not depending on type) and whether each is a genuine test or a byproduct of one particular machinery.
3. The distinctions the theory must not collapse — between what a speaker means and what a sentence means, between meaning something and merely causing a hearer to believe it, between the implicature a speaker intends and the one a hearer recovers.
4. The accounts of speaker meaning it must be consistent with — in particular any intention-based analysis on which meaning something to someone involves intending that they recognize the intention, and what that analysis has to say about cases where there is no expressed proposition to work from.

Then the part I most want. I have five cases I intend to use in the seminar, and the specification must make clear what an adequate theory owes each of them:

- A speaker reads aloud a passage she is plainly quoting — a statute, a weather bulletin — and the point of her utterance is carried by the fact that she chose to read it now, not by its content. Where does the theory locate the implicature, and does its machinery even apply when the flouting is done on behalf of someone else's maxims?
- A speaker tells a hearer something both parties know the hearer already knows. What is implicated is about the speaker's stance toward the hearer, not about the world. The effect is not detachable by paraphrase and does not seem to flow from the truth-conditional content. Is this implicature at all, or a separate category the theory must still accommodate?
- In one and the same context, two competent hearers agree on what was said and diverge on what was implicated, and neither has misheard. Must the theory treat the implicature as determinate, as a range, or as hearer-relative — and which choice does least damage to the rest of the specification?
- An implicature normally carried by a bare assertion appears to survive under negation, in the antecedent of a conditional, or inside a question, behaving as though it were part of the content rather than a defeasible addition. Say exactly what the theory owes here and whether this case can be handled without giving up the defeasibility requirement.
- A speaker implicates something by conspicuously declining to say anything — leaving a direct question unanswered, or omitting an item from an otherwise complete list. There is no expressed proposition. Does an intention-based account of speaker meaning apply, or does this case show that the specification must be broadened beyond what is said?

Flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement, if you had to keep only one, does the most to exclude otherwise popular theories. Close with a brief pass over how a maxim-based Gricean account, a relevance-theoretic account, and a truth-conditional-minimalist treatment fare against the list — keep this short, since the criteria are the deliverable and the scoring is the part I can do in class.

Along the way, state how confident you are that each item is a genuine desideratum rather than a prejudice of one camp, and single out the one requirement you are least sure belongs on the list at all.
