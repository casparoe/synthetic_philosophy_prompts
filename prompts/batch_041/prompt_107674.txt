I am writing a novel about a philosopher of mind, and I want her climactic turn stated with real precision. Her argument, as I have written it, is that she knows from the inside what it is like to be her and that no physical inventory of her brain captures that, and that she can set down in full detail a twin who is physically identical to her yet feels nothing whatever — the novel itself, she says, is her proof that such a being is possible. Physicalism, she concludes, is finished. What follows is that she must decide what to believe instead, and I do not want her to waffle. Should she come out a property dualist or a substance dualist, and how should she phrase the choice so that a philosopher reader will not wince?
