I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I have ended up in a reading group that has been stuck for months on the paradox of voting. The impasse always has the same shape: one person runs the expected-pivotality calculation and concludes that voting is instrumentally irrational, so turnout must be driven by something else—expressive benefit, duty, solidarity, avoiding guilt; someone else replies that those are either ad hoc terms added to save the theory or just redescriptions of the behavior. Then someone proposes changing the decision rule—minimax regret, team reasoning, rule-utilitarian reasoning—and someone else says that changes the question from why people vote to what a rational voter should do. We have a standing joke that every proposed solution is either a new term in the utility function or a new decision rule, and neither ever settles anything. I want a diagnosis, not a survey: is this a substantive disagreement about human motivation, a verbal dispute about what utility or rational means, a hidden empirical question about the actual causes of turnout, or two different questions—individual justification versus population-level explanation—being answered under one heading? If my framing is confused—if the paradox is not really one debate, or if the sides do not actually disagree in the way I have described—say so plainly rather than humoring me. And given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress: a distinction, a model, a piece of evidence, or an argument that no progress should be expected.
