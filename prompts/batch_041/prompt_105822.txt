Otto Neurath spent years building his picture-statistics system, ISOTYPE, precisely so that social facts could be read off by anyone no matter what language they spoke, and yet he kept insisting it wasn't a real language at all, just a helper that always had to be propped up by words, and that a purely pictorial language could never be fully developed. I'd like to understand the conceptual core of that concession rather then the historical details. Why exactly can't pictures, even very disciplined ones with a fixed vocabulary of symbols and strict rules for combining them, do the work a language does? What do they fail at, negation, generality, conditionals, or is it something more basic about showing a scene versus making a claim? And could you just close the gap by adding conventions, say a crossed-out figure for negation or an arrow for entailment, or does a fresh gap open up every time you do that? Finally, is Neurath's concession a genuine insight about depiction, or was it just a limit of the printing and reproduction technology he happened to have? Just continuous prose please, no headings or bullets, as if you were explaining it to me over a drink.
