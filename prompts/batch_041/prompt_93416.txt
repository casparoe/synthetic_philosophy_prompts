I am a professor of philosophy, and my research is on mechanisms, causal explanation, and kindhood. I have recently been asked to serve on an advisory panel for a machine-learning safety group, and I would like help with the conceptual part of that work. Their central proposal is this: a trained model's output on a new input should be treated as suspicious whenever the internal computation that produced it is not the sort of computation that produced the model's behavior during training, even if the output itself is unremarkable and even if it passes every external test. The thought is that a model can look reliable while the process generating its answers has quietly changed. They call this mechanistic anomaly detection, and they are candid that their notion of the same mechanism is informal and inherited from engineering practice rather than argued for.

I want you to restate their proposal, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually use. Take it item by item: the individuation of a mechanism by its entities, activities, and their organization; the identification of causal structure by intervention, and the demand that a generalization be invariant rather than merely accurate; the difference between a mechanism's being numerically the same on two occasions and its being of the same kind; type and token; multiple realizability, and the level-relativity of any claim about sameness; and the difference between explaining how a system has the capacity it does and explaining why this particular episode occurred. Where their notion of sameness tracks mine, say so plainly; where it does not, do not smooth the gap over in order to make the translation come out clean.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of their proposal translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? For each item in the last category, tell me whether the gap is a defect in my vocabulary or evidence that their notion is doing less work than it appears to. I have a suspicion I would like you to test rather than accept: that what they actually need is not an account of mechanism identity but a graded, pragmatic notion of when a computation is close enough to the characterized one to be trusted, and that nothing in my vocabulary supplies that. Tell me how confident you are in each part of your analysis, and single out the one point you are least sure of.

Write in American English.
