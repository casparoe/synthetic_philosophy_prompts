I keep running into the same situation and I've never found a word for it, so maybe you can help me out.

Here's one instance. I do a bit of machine learning on the side. You train a net, then you want to know what it learned, so you go looking for a feature -- some direction in activation space that tracks something like "this input has a stripe" -- and you find one that predicts the output really well. Great. Then someone else analyzes the same net a totally different way, finds a different feature that also predicts the output really well, and the two features don't line up with each other. Neither one is just a decoding artifact, both survive held-out tests, and yet they seem to carve up the same computation into incompatible parts. So is the stripe-feature real or not? It feels like the honest answer is "real as long as that way of describing the net keeps earning its keep," which is definately not the answer I was taught for whether something is real.

Second instance, less techy. In biology people talk about the gene for eye colour, and for practical purposes that's a real thing -- you can predict with it, you can breed for it -- but if you look at the actual molecular goings-on there's no clean little object sitting there that is the gene for eye colour. The useful description and the basic description don't match, and the useful one still works fine.

What bugs me is the pattern across these cases. Something behaves like a real part of the world for predicting and explaining; you can be right or wrong about it; it's not just a convenient fiction you could swap out for any other story that's equally tidy; and yet it isn't in the world the way an electron or a table is, and whether it's there at all seems to depend on which way of describing the system you've adopted. And sometimes two incompatible ways of describing the same system are both like that.

Is there an established name for this class of thing -- the entities that are real, but only relative to a way of describing, where the describing is judged by how well it predicts and compresses rather than by matching some bedrock? I'm not satisfied with "it's just instrumentalism," because these things seem to be true or false in a way fictions aren't, and I don't think "emergent" or "higher-level" is it either, since that still sounds like a fact about the world rather than about a description of it. If there's a name, tell me what it is and exactly what it covers. If there isn't, coin one, and give me the boundary cases -- what's in, what's out, and how it differs from the nearest things people already say. Dont just hand me a label; I want to know where the edges are.
