Ok I need help with something that's been bugging me for a couple weeks. A colleague of mine has this little arguement he wheels out whenever anyone in a seminar says "we decided" or "the committee intends," and I keep opening my mouth to object and then closing it again because I can't quite say whats wrong. Here it is:

(1) Whenever something does something, there is an agent that does it.
(2) An agent is the kind of thing that can have intentions, and having an intention is being in a certain mental state.
(3) Mental states are states of a single mind -- they belong to one subject.
(4) A group -- a team, a committee, a couple -- is not a single mind. It's just a bunch of individual minds.
(5) So a group is not an agent.
(6) So a group cannot act.
(7) So everything we call joint action is really just several individuals each doing their own thing, each with an ordinary individual intention, one that may happen to mention the others.
(8) So collective intentionality is at best a way of talking and at worst a category mistake.

He takes (7) and (8) to just follow, and honestly the first few steps look fine to me -- I'm not a fan of group minds and I don't want to posit some spooky collective consciousness. But (8) strikes me as proving way too much. On this arguement two people can't even take a walk together, and "we" is never the subject of anything, which seems crazy. Still, I don't want to weasel out of it by smuggling a group mind back in through the side door, and I also don't want to just wave my hands and say "emergence."

So: which step is doing the illegitimate work, and why did it look so plausible until you push on it? Then tell me what it costs to reject it. In particular, if I want to keep (1)-(3), am I forced to give up (4), or can I keep (4) and still block the slide to (6) or (7)? I'd rather keep (4) than (1), if it comes to that, but I want to know what I'm actually paying for -- i.e. what picture of shared intention I'm signing up for, and what the main reductive and non-reductive options each have to give up to stop this arguement.
