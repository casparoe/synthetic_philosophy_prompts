I sit on the comittee that decides who gets to vote in our city's referendums, and there's one coming up that I can't get straight in my head. Our track record so far: for the council tax rise we let everyone on the local register vote — non-citizens, 16 and 17 year olds, the lot — but not the people who own flats here and live abroad. For the incinerator on the eastern edge we wouldn't let the neighbours over the border vote, even though the smoke was going to land on them. Now there's a referendum on a levy on wages earned in the city, which would fall on the people who work here but live in the suburbs; they're in the city five days a week, but they've never had to obey our bylaws and they don't pay us anything at the moment. Should we let them vote? Tell me what rule we've actually been running, what the other rules that fit our record just as well would predict here, which of our past calls is the closest analog, which call you'd make and why, and what next case would pin down which rule we're really on. British English please, and finish with a last line starting 'Verdict:' in the form yes/no — the rule it rests on.
