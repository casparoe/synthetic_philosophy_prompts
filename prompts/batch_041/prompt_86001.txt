I've gotten convinced by Dennett's Quining Qualia line — the Chase and Sanborn case, the two veteran coffee tasters who both report that their experience of the same brand has changed, but one says the coffee changed and the other says he changed. Dennett's point, as I take it, is that their introspective reports can't prop up qualia in the heavy sense: ineffable, intrinsic, private, directly and incorrigibly known to the one having them. I think that package is an incoherent posit adn we should drop it. What I want from you is what would actually count against me — not the usual but you can't deny experience stuff, since I'm not denying experience, I'm denying that this particular theoretical package is a coherent thing to posit. Give me concrete defeaters: cases where introspection reliably discriminates something the functional or representational story can't capture, results that make the intrinsic or ineffable bit do real explanatory work, or arguments showing that Chase-and-Sanborn-style cases are the exception and that subjects can in general tell changed-me from changed-object in a way that tracks a real, projectible property. What about a variant where the two reports predict different downstream behavior — different discrimination thresholds, different consumption patterns? Does that give the qualia posit traction or just show the reports are functional states in disguise? Sort the defeaters by how decisive they'd be, say which ones a committed defender of qualia would themselves concede counts against them, and tell me honestly whether my position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — if I just say that's not real qualia whenever a candidate shows up. If it has, is that a problem here, or is the concept so confused that unfalsifiability is the right response? American English, please.
