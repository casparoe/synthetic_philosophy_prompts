I have no formal training in philosophy, but over the last few years I have been working through, on my own, what my own choices amount to when the harm they seem to be part of only comes about through the choices of very many others — the flights I take, the meat I eat, the single vote I cast. I would like to apply for a small grant for independent researchers, and I need a proposal of roughly two pages.

My half-formed idea is that the familiar standoff over whether one person's act can matter has been run together from three cases that are not really the same. In the first, my act is one of a great many that add up to a harm. In the second, my act is part of what keeps a harmful practice or market going even though no particular outcome turns on it. In the third, my act is one vote in a procedure whose result is insensitive to it but whose standing as a procedure may not be. The standard objection — that no single act changes anything, so the individual is off the hook — appears to assume all three share one structure, and I suspect its force is lost or gained depending on which of the three we are actually talking about. I would like the proposal to turn this into a question sharp enough to have an answer: is there a single account of individual contribution that handles all three cases, or does the objection come apart into distinct objections, one per structure?

Please write the proposal with a precise statement of the question; why the positions people usually take leave it open; the method — what I would actually do with the time, month by month, and why this way of proceeding rather than the obvious one of just re-arguing the standard cases; what a real result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and a scope that is honest about what can be done in six months of part-time work. I will add the references myself, so please do not include a bibliography or citations, and please do not lean on any particular author's name or book — I want the argument to stand on its own.
