I am a PhD student in the philosophy of history, and I have come to hold the following view: what distinguishes pseudohistory from history is not any intrinsic evidentiary or methodological defect but a refusal to recognize the discipline's authority to adjudicate historical claims; on this view, pseudohistory names a stance toward institutional epistemic authority rather than a class of false or irrational beliefs. Tell me what would count against this position. Which cases—Fomenko's New Chronology, Atlantis narratives, ancient-astronaut literature, but also heterodox claims such as continental drift or the re-dating of the Greek Dark Age—would a defender have to count as genuine history, or would have to exclude from the category of pseudohistory, and would either result be a reductio? What features of actual historiographical practice would a critic say the view cannot accommodate? Please rank the defeaters from decisive to merely awkward, separate what defenders would themselves concede from what only critics allege, and assess whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability—and whether that is a problem for a view of this kind.
