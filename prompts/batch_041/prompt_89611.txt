ok so im putting together a handout for the last meeting of a reading group — its a weird mix of stats people and philosophers — and i want to open with a poem about simpsons paradox, the thing where a trend holds inside every subgroup but flips when you pool them. the poem has to actually perform the flip, not just name it. i want a reader going top to bottom to feel the verdict reverse on them, and i want it to be clear that nothing weird is happening inside any single subgroup: every number in the poem is honest, and the reversal lives in how the groups get weighted when theyre added up, not in any one row.

my rough idea, steal it or do better: a two-part poem, 16 to 20 lines total, no fixed rhyme needed, where the second part regroups or reweights the same material as the first, so the same facts come out naming the opposite winner. the thing doing the weighting — severity, age, who showed up sickest, who was already ahead when they walked in — should be visible in the poem rather than hidden, since thats the whole machinery. you can use the term once if it helps but the poem shouldnt lean on it; someone who has never heard the name should still get it by the end. dont tack an explanation on afterwards either, the poem has to carry it.

please invent your own example — no berkeley admissions, no kidney stones, ive used those in every handout for years. and dont close it with correlation isnt causation or a line about lying with statistics; the interesting thing is that nobody in the poem is lying. a final couplet giving the pooled verdict is fine, i just dont want it to read like the poem is scolding anyone for being confused, and its ok if it doesnt resolve which number you should actually act on.
