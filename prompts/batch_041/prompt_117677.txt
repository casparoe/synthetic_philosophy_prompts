I have been trying to decide whether to join a political party — a local branch of one whose platform I largely agree with — and a friend who knows I have been reading philosophy on my own told me not to bother, quoting Simone Weil at me: "Un parti politique est une machine à fabriquer de la passion collective", a political party being a machine for manufacturing collective passion. He also cited her description of a party as an organization built so as to bring collective pressure to bear on the thinking of each of its members, and her claim that a party's first and, in the end, only aim is its own growth, without limit. I cannot tell whether this is a real insight or just an arresting way of saying that any group of people will end up sharing feelings. Please set out one by one the distinct claims that one-liner could be making, ordered from the harmless to the radical, and sort them by what they are claims about: how parties tend to behave, what a party is by definition, what belonging to one does to a person's thinking, or what a collective is worth in itself. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible thesis, or an exaggeration or outright falsehood, and say which of them would actually give my friend a reason not to join, and which of them Weil would have needed for her conclusion that parties should be abolished rather than reformed. Where a reading turns the one-liner into something true by definition, and so impossible to argue against, say so plainly.
