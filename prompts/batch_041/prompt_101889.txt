Write a mock referee report, about 500 words, for a fictional journal that has received Hume's essay on miracles as a submission. Adopt the deadpan voice of an anonymous reviewer recommending a major revision, with a brief summary of the manuscript, a numbered list of major concerns, and a short section of minor comments. The satire should aim at the evidential policy the essay defends rather than at Hume himself, and its force should come from the reviewer's demands being genuine philosophical objections in disguise. The report must press at least three of the following tensions: the slide between treating an event as merely contrary to experience and treating it as contrary to the laws of nature; whether a maxim that is itself founded on experience can consistently be used to disqualify all contrary testimony; whether the essay's own reckoning of probabilities makes a miracle not merely unproven but in principle unprovable; and whether the argument assumes the very naturalism it purports to establish. Close with a two- or three-sentence dissenting note from a second referee. A philosopher of religion should find the objections fair rather than strawman, and a general reader should still be able to follow the joke.
