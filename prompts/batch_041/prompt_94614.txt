In a graduate seminar on history and myth, I keep encountering a move that I can describe but cannot confidently name, and I would like your help in deciding whether it is an established concept, a species of one, or a confusion that deserves a new term. The move is this. A narrative is treated by the seminar as legend rather than history: its central episodes are wondrous, its chronology is schematic, its characters are types, and its function is etiological or ideological. Then a mundane substratum is produced—a burned layer at a site, a local dynast behind a legendary king, a population movement behind a migration story. The substratum is not evidence for the central episodes; it is usually compatible with their being invented. Yet once it is in place, the whole narrative is said to be historical in outline, and anyone who denies its historicity is asked to explain away the substratum. The narrative has not been confirmed so much as reclassified. I have been calling this kernel-washing, but I suspect that is not the right term. Think of the recurring debates over Troy, Arthur, and the Exodus. Is there an established concept for this? The nearest candidates seem to be euhemerism, rationalization, and the ordinary source-critical inference from a later legend to an earlier event, but none seems to fit exactly. What I want is not a label but an anatomy: what is the minimal structure of the move, how does it differ from legitimate inference from a distorted memory, and when does it become a category error? If no established term picks it out, please coin one and give it a definition with boundary cases. I am not asking for a bibliography; I am asking for the carving.
