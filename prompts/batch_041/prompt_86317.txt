Ok, this is going to sound like a weird mashup, but bear with me. I'm a psychometrician by trade — factor analysis, IRT, SEM, latent variable models all day — and I've been reading about the problem of universals for fun. I keep having the feeling that I already work with this stuff, which probly means I'm fooling myself, so I want you to check me.

Take the main positions on universals — Platonism, Aristotelian immanent realism, and the nominalisms (class nominalism, resemblance nominalism, trope theory) — and restate each one as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary. Factors and latent variables, observed indicators, loadings, measurement models, identification, the difference between a latent variable and a composite score that's just a sum of items, reflective vs formative indicators, measurement invariance, model fit. One position at a time, and be honest: don't relabel things just to make the translation look tidy.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually want. For each position: what came across cleanly, what only came across because you fudged it (say exactly where the fudge was), and what had no counterpart in my framework at all. A few things I'm already suspicious of, tell me if I'm off base:

- In my world the factor is a parameter that generates the indicators — two people are similar because they were drawn from the same distribution. That feels like the opposite direction from 'many particulars share one thing'. Real difference, or am I overreading?
- A latent variable with no indicators is unidentified; you literally cannot estimate it. Plato seems perfectly happy with universals that nothing instantiates. Does that mean my framework can't even state his position?
- In factor models the identity of a factor is partly up to me — rotation, scaling, which items I include. Is there anything in the universals debate about who gets to decide when two properties are the same property, or is identity just given?
- What about the third man? If I can always ask for a factor of factors, why doesn't my framework spawn the same regress? Is the regress an artifact of how the philosophers set the problem up?

And the meta question: is the residue a sign my framework is too thin to hold the metaphysics, or a sign that some of what the philosophers are arguing about is less substantial than it sounds? Don't give me a tidy 'both' — say which, and say why.
