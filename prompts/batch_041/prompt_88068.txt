I am a doctoral candidate in comparative ethics, working on reconstructions of karma as a theory of moral desert, and I am trying to determine the decision rule that a contemporary teacher I have been corresponding with is implicitly running. I have put five cases to her, and her verdicts are as follows. First, a woman gives a large sum to a stranger out of genuine compassion; the stranger uses the money to drink himself to death. Her verdict: good karma for the giver. Second, a man gives the same sum to the same stranger purely in order to be seen as a benefactor; the stranger uses it to obtain treatment and recovers. Her verdict: bad karma for the giver. Third, a man puts rat poison in a rival's tea, believing it lethal; the rival happens to be immune and drinks it unharmed. Her verdict: bad karma. Fourth, a woman gives to a shelter partly out of compassion and partly to impress a colleague; the shelter is helped. Her verdict: good karma. Fifth, a man, under threat of death, drives a getaway car and bears the victim no ill will. Her verdict: no bad karma.

My working hypothesis is that she tracks only the quality of the agent's intention at the moment of action, which I call the act-desert rule. But I cannot tell whether she is instead tracking what the act does to the agent's own character, which I call the agent-desert rule, because in all five cases the two rules happen to agree.

The new case, on which I want a verdict, is this. A woman, out of genuine compassion and with no thought of harm, repeatedly shields her brother from the consequences of his addiction, paying his debts and covering for him at work. Each individual act is well-intentioned, and she expects each to help. Over several years the shielding makes him worse and turns her into someone controlling and self-righteous. Does this case force her off the act-desert reading, and what would she say the karma of these acts is?

I want, in order: (a) the candidate rules that fit her five verdicts, including any that are not desert rules at all, since I have named only two; (b) what each candidate predicts about the new case, stated as a verdict; (c) your best guess at her verdict, with the reason for preferring it; (d) the further case that would separate the surviving rules, specified precisely enough that I could put it to her; and (e) whether the case exposes a tension in the very idea of karma as desert, in particular whether character is a proper object of desert at all if she goes with agent-desert, and whether karma can still be a matter of justice rather than a mere causal trace if she goes with act-desert. If the case as I have described it leaves her free to give either verdict, tell me which missing fact would decide it rather than quietly adding that fact to secure the prediction. Assume I have no interest in what any historical school officially holds; I want the rule to which her own verdicts commit her.
