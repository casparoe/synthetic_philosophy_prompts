I clerk for Justice Renata Solberg of the Constitutional Court of the Republic of Vela, and I must prepare the bench memorandum for the case we hear next month. Her votes on the validity of challenged statutes over the past decade have settled into a pattern, and I want to predict her vote on the new statute as carefully as I can. My colleagues call her a positivist, but I suspect that label conceals more than one rule.

Her rulings, in order:

1. A statute enacted by the Assembly with the required majority but never published in the Official Gazette — the enrolled text remained in the presiding officer's safe — was held to have no binding force.

2. A statute, validly enacted and published, that empowered a minister to define the offences by unpublished internal circulars was held invalid to the extent that it authorized offences no one could read in advance.

3. A statute, validly enacted, published, and general in form, that applied retroactively to punish conduct lawful when it was done, was held invalid.

4. A statute, validly enacted, published, general, and prospective, but drafted so vaguely that no one could determine what it required, was held invalid.

5. A statute, validly enacted, published, general, prospective, clear, and consistently applied, that imposed a mandatory life sentence for the late filing of a routine registration form, was upheld and applied.

6. A statute, validly enacted, published, general, prospective, clear, and consistently applied, that confiscated a fixed share of the property of a named ethnic minority and restricted its members' movement within the country, was upheld and applied. Her opinion stated that the court's office is to apply the law as it stands and that the injustice of a statute is a matter for the Assembly, which remains free to repeal it.

Next month we hear a statute that is validly enacted, published, general, prospective, clear, feasible to comply with, stable, and applied congruently by officials, and that authorizes the enslavement of the members of a religious sect, the seizure of their property, and the removal of their children to state institutions.

Please do the following. (a) State the candidate rules that fit her six rulings, as rules — each with the features it treats as necessary for legal validity — rather than as summaries, and note which rulings each rule explains well and which it strains to explain. (b) Say what each rule predicts about next month's statute. (c) Give your best guess at her vote, with the reasons for preferring one rule over the others; if you think her record genuinely underdetermines the outcome, say so and say which way a tie ought to break. (d) Name a further case, different from the one before us, that would most cleanly separate the surviving rules. Throughout, mark clearly which parts of this are settled among people who work on legal validity and which are genuinely contested, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
