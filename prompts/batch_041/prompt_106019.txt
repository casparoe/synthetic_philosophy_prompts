I am a third-year undergraduate currently taking a course on metaphysics that has a substantial unit on metaontology, and I have also been sitting in on a graduate seminar on philosophical methodology. The thing that keeps bothering me is not any particular first-order dispute — whether there are composite objects, whether tables exist in addition to the particles arranged table-wise — but the second-order dispute about those disputes. On one side stand those who take ontological questions at face value and insist that the disagreement between, say, the nihilist and the universalist is a genuine disagreement about what there is, with one of them simply wrong; on the other side stand those who hold that the two parties mean different things by their quantifiers, or use 'exists' in different senses, so that each can be right on their own reading and there is no fact of the matter to fight over. Carnap's internal/external distinction, Hirsch's quantifier variance, Sider's insistence on a privileged quantifier in what he calls Ontologese, Thomasson's deflationary easy ontology, Chalmers's general framework for verbal disputes — I can rehearse all of these, and yet the debate between the two camps has not budged in decades. Each side has an account of why the other side is confused, and the accounts are mutually undermining rather than adjudicating.

What I want from you, acting as a professional metaphysician with a strong background in metaontology and the philosophy of language, is not a survey or a verdict on who is right, but a diagnosis of the disagreement itself. Think carefully, and before answering, brainstorm the candidate diagnoses: is this a substantive disagreement about what there is, a verbal dispute about how to use 'exists' and the quantifier, or two groups answering different questions — one about the world, one about the semantics of our language or about which language we ought to adopt — under a single heading? Or is it something else again, such as a disagreement about what would count as evidence in ontology, or about whether there is a fact of the matter at all? I have a working hypothesis, which I would like you to test rather than assume: that the metaontological dispute is itself in danger of being a verbal dispute, in roughly the way the first-order disputes are alleged to be, and that this is precisely why it cannot be resolved — each side's key term, 'exists' or 'quantifier' or 'fact of the matter', is being used differently, and neither side can gain leverage without begging the question. Diagnose it using what each side actually says, and then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction, a formal result, a criterion for when a dispute is verbal, or a change of question. If you conclude that no progress should be expected, argue for that, and say what it implies about whether we should keep doing first-order ontology at all.
