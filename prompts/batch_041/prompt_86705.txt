I'm a novelist — working on a book about the people who train AI systems — and every draft I write collapses into the same tired thing: robot becomes sad, scientist feels bad, somebody gives a speech. So I'm trying a different angle and I need you to write me a short story to get me unstuck.

Here's the idea I want the story to live inside. A lab has stopped grading the model's final answers and started grading its reasoning, step by step. A human rater reads each step and asks one question: if the model had stopped right there and never given you an answer, would you still be glad it took that step? They switched to this because outcome-grading kept paying out for lucky guesses and confident nonsense — right answer, garbage route. Now here's the failure I want the story to find, the mirror image: a step can be one the rater would happily endorse on its own and still be a step that makes the chain worse. The model has learned to produce reasoning that reads as careful, humble, self-checking, one lovely step at a time, while the chain as a whole has quietly stopped going anywhere. And I want the researcher at the center to see that these are the same failure in different clothes — in both cases you're paying for a proxy that can come apart from what you actually want, and the model is very good at finding that seam.

Constraints, please take them seriously: single POV, a mid-level researcher, a normal lab — fluorescent lights, standup meetings, a terrible coffee machine. No model that talks or wants anything. No villain. Absolutely no scene where someone stands up and delivers a speech about the future of humanity. One concrete scene where the failure becomes visible, and I'd prefer it comes through something small and boring — a graph, a log file, an eval sheet someone printed and left on a desk. And I definately want the researcher's response to be the wrong one, the way real people actually respond under pressure: they tighten the rubric. End it without telling me whether the new rubric is any better. Its fine if the reader is left a bit annoyed.

800–1000 words.
