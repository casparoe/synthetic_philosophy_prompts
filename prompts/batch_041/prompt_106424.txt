I need a thought experiment built to order. It is for a graduate seminar whose guiding question is whether the extended-mind program has a single criterion for what belongs to a cognitive system, or whether its criteria are separate tests that can be pulled apart. Design a case that pulls them apart, then show that it does.

Specifications.

1. Two criteria, opposite verdicts. The case must come out one way on the parity criterion and the other way on the complementarity criterion. As I am using them here, the parity criterion says that a bit of the world is part of the cognitive process when it performs a process that, were it performed inside the head, we would without hesitation count as cognitive. The complementarity criterion says that an external resource earns its place in a cognitive system by doing work the internal machinery does not do, by complementing it, rather than by replicating what the internal machinery already does. Your case must satisfy the parity criterion and fail the complementarity criterion. That is the direction the usual examples do not go; the familiar cases satisfy both criteria or neither, so do not model yours on them.

2. A single toggle. The divergence must be traceable to one feature of the case that can be switched on or off without altering anything else in the story. State that feature explicitly, give the case in both settings, and report the verdicts in both.

3. A third verdict, resting on a different reason. A common internalist line is that an external resource is an enabling scaffold rather than a constituent, and that this can be shown by a removal test: take the resource away and ask whether the agent's competence survives, as opposed to merely slowing down or becoming less convenient. Your case must yield not part of the system on this test as well, but for a reason you can state separately from the complementarity verdict. Say whether the removal test survives the toggle in item 2, and if it does not, say what the internalist must fall back on.

4. Realism and novelty. One paragraph for the scenario. An ordinary adult, an ordinary task, an artifact or another person that exists now. No science fiction, no hypothetical neurotechnology, no brain implants, no demons, no idealized agents. Do not use the standard case of a notebook, a phone, a list, or any other store of facts the agent would otherwise have to remember, or a close variant of it; whatever the external item duplicates must be something other than memory for items the agent can no longer retrieve. The verdicts must fall out of the criteria as you have stated them, not be stipulated by the way you describe the resource.

5. After the scenario. Give each criterion's verdict with the reasoning that produces it; name the feature that is doing the work and say what the case shows once that feature is removed; state which of the two criteria you would keep if only one could be kept, and why; and give the strongest attack on the case from someone who defends the position that fares worst in it, followed by your reply. If you conclude that the specifications cannot all be met at once, do not quietly drop one; show where the tension lies.

Give the case a short name, and write in American English.
