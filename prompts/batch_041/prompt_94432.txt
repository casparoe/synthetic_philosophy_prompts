I've been keeping a note on my phone of times when somebody turned out to be right and I couldn't work out whether they actually knew it, and I'd like to know if there's one rule behind how I react to them.

- My tracking said the parcel was delivered and it had been, so I told my flatmate it was here. But the app was actually showing a different parcel with a similar number, and I'd have said the same thing if mine was still sat in the depot. I dont think I knew it had arrived.
- I knew my scratchcard was a loser the second I saw the odds printed on the back. It was a loser. I still don't think I knew it though — everyone loses, I was just right about the likely thing.
- A bloke in the pub told me the road up ahead was flooded, and it was. I found out later he's a compulsive liar who happened to tell the truth that one time. I don't think I knew the road was flooded.
- My flatmate said she knew the kettle was boiling because the little light was on. The light was broken and always on — but the kettle really was boiling, and she could also hear it rumbling and see the steam coming out, she just didn't bother mentioning that bit. I think she did know.
- My mum says she knew my grandad was going to have a bad turn that night because she just had a feeling, and she claims she's had that exact feeling before every time and always been right. I want to say she knew, but it makes me uneasy.
- My cousin reckons he knows there's no biggest number, purely because a psychic told him so. There's no biggest number, so he can't be wrong about it. But I don't think he knew it at all.

The rule I keep coming back to is this: you know something if you couldn't easily have been wrong about it. That gets most of them — me and the parcel, the scratchcard, the liar, all cases where I'd have said the same thing even if it had been false. And the kettle one works too, since she'd have been right either way.

But the psychic one wrecks it. There's no way for "there's no biggest number" to be false, so by my rule he knows it, which is obviously rubbish. Is there a version of the rule that keeps the kettle verdict and still says the psychic doesn't know? And what would it say about the one I keep going back and forth on: my mate said he knew Tom would turn up at the party because he'd invited him and Tom never misses one. Tom did turn up — but only because his car broke down outside and he came in to use the phone.
