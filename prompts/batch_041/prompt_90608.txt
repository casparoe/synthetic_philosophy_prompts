For a novel I'm writing about a painter who is losing her sight, I've been reading popular neuroscience, and one phrase keeps surfacing: perception is controlled hallucination. A science writer I spoke with used it to tell me, flatly, that what my painter sees is no more real than a dream — which felt too quick, though I couldn't say why. Set out the distinct, precise claims that phrase could be making: that prior expectation shapes perception; that the sensory signal serves only as a correction to a prediction; that perceiving and imagining share their machinery; that perceived content is mind-dependent or constructed; that the world is not as it appears. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive empirical hypothesis, a conceptual or definitional claim, or simply false, and note what would have to be true for it to hold. Then tell me which reading the science writer must have been relying on to reach the conclusion that my painter's vision is as unreal as a dream, and whether any of the readings actually licenses that conclusion. I want a menu I can keep beside the manuscript, not a verdict on which reading is correct.
