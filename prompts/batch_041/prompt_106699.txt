Leibniz advanced his own version of the ontological argument, grounded in the notion of perfection and the compossibility of perfections. Suppose he were shown Gödel's modal version, in which God is defined as a being having all positive properties, necessary existence is counted among the positive properties, and the conclusion that a God-like being exists in every possible world is derived in the modal system S5. What would Leibniz have made of this proof? Would he accept the S5 step from possible necessary existence to necessary existence, or would he insist that the argument must first establish that the divine perfections are compossible? How would his principle of sufficient reason bear on the proof's treatment of positivity and necessity?
