I teach a seminar on the suspicion that some of philosophy's most durable disputes — over whether a statue and the lump of clay are one thing or two, whether there are tables, whether a tomato is a fruit, whether a burrito is a sandwich under the terms of a lease — are disagreements about our words rather than about the world. The seminar has a practical aim: to be able to take a live dispute and decide whether to pursue it, dissolve it, or reframe it. Before we read any particular proposal, I want the specification. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of verbal disputes must satisfy.

Set them out as a numbered list, each requirement stated as a requirement and followed by the case or consideration that motivates it. The list should cover, at least: the range of cases the theory must classify correctly, from harmless taxonomic disagreements through the ontological disputes to legal and political disputes in which the contested term is the only vocabulary in which a real grievance can be voiced; the difference between a dispute that is verbal and one that is merely verbal, and between verbal disputes in which one party is at fault and those in which no one is; whether the diagnosis requires the parties to agree on all the non-linguistic facts; what the diagnosis is for — what follows, practically, from finding a dispute verbal; and the requirement of neutrality, that the theory not earn its keep by deciding in advance which side of a dispute is right.

Then the two things I most want. First, the self-application requirement: the theory must be able to say whether disputes about its own criteria are themselves verbal, and I want to know whether it can meet that requirement without swallowing itself. Second, flag the pairs of desiderata that look jointly unsatisfiable — above all whether the demand for neutrality can be reconciled with the demand that the diagnosis have critical bite against parties who reject it, and whether a theory that treats verbalness as a defect can accommodate the cases in which a verbal dispute is worth having. Close with a brief assessment, for each of the main families of view you know of, of which requirement it fails or strains to meet.
