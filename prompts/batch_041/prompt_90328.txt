I'm a teaching assistant grading a short-answer exercise in an undergraduate history-of-ethics unit on Augustine. The question put to the class was this:

"Augustine concludes that no lie is ever permissible. (a) Give his definition of a lie and explain what he means by the 'double heart.' (b) Explain how that definition underwrites the absolute prohibition, and (c) explain why a lie told for a good end remains a sin even when it harms no one. Answer in one paragraph."

One student submitted the following:

"Augustine defines a lie as a false statement made with the intention to deceive. For him the essence of lying lies in the falsity of what is said, so that whoever utters a falsehood lies and whoever utters a truth does not; the lie is judged by the words rather than by the mind of the speaker. The 'double heart' is his image for the two things present in the liar: the true thing he withholds and the false thing he puts in its place. Because deception is essential to the definition, a lie told for a good end is still a sin -- Augustine will not do evil that good may come -- and he refuses to allow the apparently harmless lie that helps someone while hurting no one. He develops the point most fully in the treatise he wrote at the request of Consentius, where he argues that it is right for Catholics to feign Priscillianism in order to expose the heretics, since the truth of the faith is served by it. He grants only that a joke is not a lie at all, since no deceit is intended."

Please grade this answer and supply the comments that justify the grade. I want a point-by-point assessment rather than an overall impression: identify which claims are correct, which are wrong, and which are borderline or need qualification, and note which errors a hasty reader might pass over. Assign a grade out of 10, and keep the whole response to 300 words or fewer.
