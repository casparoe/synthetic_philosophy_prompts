Hi, I'm a high school junior and I'm in this civics/Model UN thing where we keep getting stuck on a weird problem that I can't find the right word for. It's about how any group that decides something has to already exist before it can decide, but the thing it's deciding is often who gets to be in the group. For example, in Model UN we were doing a resolution about a region's right to self-determination, and the whole committee got stuck on who counts as 'the people' of the region. Everyone in the region? Everyone in the current country? People who live there now, or also people who used to live there and got pushed out? And it felt like whichever answer you pick basically decides whether the resolution passes, so the rule about who votes is doing more work than the vote itself.

Then in civics we simulated a city annexing a suburb. The city council votes on whether to annex, but the suburb doesn't get to vote because it's not in the city yet. If the suburb votes first, the city residents don't get a say. If the suburb is split in half, each half votes seperately, and the outcome can flip depending on where you draw the line. And a neighborhood that got annexed last year now gets to vote on annexing another neighborhood, so the group that decides is always a product of earlier decisions that some other group made. I also saw this on a Discord server: a server votes on whether to merge with another server. The other server's members can't vote, but if they could, they'd outnumber the current members. Then after merging, they vote to un-merge, and now the original members are outvoted. It's like the membership rules are a constitution that can be amended by the very people it creates, and there's no neutral starting point.

Is there a standard name for this exact circularity? I've heard 'boundary problem' and 'constituent power' and 'all affected interests' thrown around, but I don't know if one of those is exactly it or if they're all slightly different things. I don't just want a label, though. I want to know what the phenomenon actually is, what it isn't, and where its edges are. Like, is it the same as gerrymandering? Is it just secession under another name? Is it a special case of social choice, or is it something else entirely? If there's no single established name, can you coin one and define it so it covers the cases above but not ordinary rule-making inside a group whose members are already settled? And what would you say are the boundary cases, like does it apply to a club deciding its own membership fees, or only to territorially defined groups, or to things like future generations who can't vote at all? Thanks, this has been bugging me for weeks.
