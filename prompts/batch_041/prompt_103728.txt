I've been trying to get my head round Hofstadter's careenium (I think it's in I Am a Strange Loop, though it might be an earlier book of his — I've half forgotton). The setup as I remember it: a big sphere packed with millions of tiny magnetic balls, and the balls stick together in clumps, and the way the clumps shove each other about is supposed to be what a mind is — symbols, concepts, meanings, all of it living at the level of the clumps rather than at the level of the individual balls. What I can't get past is the causality. If it's the clumps doing all the shoving, then the symbols aren't shoving anything; they're just a handy label for what the balls are up to, and meaning never actually moves anything, which sounds like plain epiphenomenalism to me. But if the symbols really do shove the clumps around, then that's the symbols exerting a causal power over and above the balls, and no physics I know of is going to hand you that. And if you tell me the two descriptions are just the same events under two labels, then "the symbols pushed the clumps" adds nothing at all to "the clumps pushed each other", so it's either false or empty. So which is it — do symbols push the substrate around or not? (And does it even matter which clump you point at, seeing as they're constantly swapping balls in and out?)
