I am trying to write up a seminar discussion that keeps collapsing into two familiar slogans, and I would like you to produce the document the two sides should have written together rather than another round of point-scoring. On one side is a defender of a unified big-history synthesis in the spirit of Harari or Diamond: human history has a broad directional logic, and the accumulation of energy, information, and organizational scale explains why hierarchy, states, and durable inequality became so common. On the other side is a critic in the spirit of Graeber and Wengrow: that story is a retrospective ratchet imposed on an archaeological and ethnographic record full of alternative arrangements, reversals, and deliberate experiments, so the apparent direction is at least partly an artifact of what survived and what later narrators chose to emphasize. I want a joint statement of an adversarial collaboration that both could sign in good faith, not a verdict about who is right and not a dramatized dialogue.

To keep the exercise from recycling the usual talking points, please organize it around a distinction I have been calling the ratchet view versus the switchyard view. The ratchet view treats cumulative scale as the master variable that makes hierarchy increasingly likely and hard to reverse; the switchyard view treats scale as opening a set of possible tracks, with institutions, coalitions, and contingent choices determining which track a society takes. The document should set out, in continuous prose and with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the claims both sides can accept about what a grand narrative is legitimately supposed to explain and what the evidence can and cannot show, as well as each side's own statement of what would count as a genuine anomaly for its position. It should then give the exact remaining disagreements in wording that each side would accept as a fair statement of its own view, identify the cruxes -- especially whether the archaeological record underdetermines the ratchet, whether hierarchy is a default consequence of scale, and whether freedom and domination are structural variables or interpretive categories -- and, for each crux, describe a case or type of evidence both sides agree would bear on it while recording what each side predicts about that case.

Please write in American English, as a single polished letter to two colleagues, with no formatting beyond ordinary paragraphs, and please avoid citations or reading lists; I care about the structure of the disagreement, not the bibliography.
