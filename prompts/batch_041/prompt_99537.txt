Here's my opening. I'll give you more than Mises would: I'll grant that your board can solve the equations, and that with enough clerks it can even keep up with them. But your managers are the ones who have to *tell* you the production functions, and the only person who knows what a given factory can really do is the person running it. So what you get is the set of numbers that makes next quarter's target easy, and since nobody at the center is spending their own money on the mistake, nobody has both the means and the motive to find out what things actually cost — which means your trial-and-error process converges on whatever the managers find it convenient for you to believe, not on real scarcities. And if you try to patch that by letting a manager keep a share of whatever his factory saves, haven't you just reinvented the profit motive, and with it the capital market and the inequality you set out to abolish? So which is it — a board that doesn't know, or a bonus scheme that quietly hands the whole thing back to the capitalists?
