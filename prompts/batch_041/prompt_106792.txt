That got us reading about the whole designer-baby thing, and I keep running into this idea — procreative beneficence, I think it's called — that if you're already in a position to choose among the children you could have, you should pick the one likely to have the best life, and that this is basically continuous with what parents do anyway.

Here's what I actually want help with. My brother-in-law, who reads a lot of this and is definately sharper than me, says the whole fuss is overblown because picking your child's traits is just like picking a school for your kid. Same kind of decision, he says, just earlier — so liberal eugenics is a scary name for something completely ordinary. He also throws in that the kid can't really complain later, since with a different embryo it wouldn't have been that kid at all. I'm not convinced, but I can't put my finger on why.

What I really want to know is whether the nothing-to-see-here conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the whole argument the entire time — and if it was, what a better comparison would be.
