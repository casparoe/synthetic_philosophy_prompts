I chair the grants committee of a small environmental foundation, and we vote next week on a proposal to buy and protect a tract of species-rich forest that would otherwise be cleared for farmland. One of my fellow trustees has circulated a note urging us to decline: the tract supports an enormous number of short-lived animals — insects, small mammals, nestlings — most of which die young, by predation, exposure, or hunger, so if their lives contain more suffering than wellbeing, then protecting the tract preserves a vast quantity of suffering that clearing would end, and the humane vote is to let it go. Two trustees call this sophistry and want it ruled out of order; I think there is more to it than they allow, but that its conclusion does not follow. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the vote: recommend a specific course — fund, fund with conditions, or decline — with the reasoning that bears on it, giving my colleague's argument its strongest form and the best grounds for rejecting or outweighing it. Write it as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a letter to my fellow trustees.
