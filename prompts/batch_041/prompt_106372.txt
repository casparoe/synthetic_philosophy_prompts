Ok so I'm in a history of science reading group and we've been circling the same fight for months, and at this point I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict.

The fight is whether the Middle Ages, the Renaissance and the Scientific Revolution are real divisions in the past or just labels that later people stuck on. Person A says they're artifacts -- 'the Renaissance' especially -- invented by later historians to flatter a story about Europe waking up, and that the honest move is to drop the big labels and just describe change at the scale you can actually document. Person B says fine, call them whatever you like, but you can't state a single interesting thesis without them: that printing, new instruments, new astronomy and Atlantic expansion formed a self-reinforcing cluster whose effects compounded. Chop that into a hundred local micro-histories and you've lost the very thing you wanted to explain. The maddening part is that they agree on basically all the evidence. Nobody is disputing a find.

My own hunch, and I'd like you to test it rather than just agree with it: it isn't really a dispute about the past. I think it's three things tangled together. One, a verbal fight about 'real', where one side means it corresponds to joints in the world and the other means it earns its explanatory keep -- seperate those and part of the disagreement evaporates. Two, a genuine empirical question about rates: did anything actually accelerate, in a way that shows up in series you could in principle measure (prices, literacy, the density of printed books, whatever), or is the apparent jump an artifact of what happened to get written down and preserved? If the curve is smooth, A gets a factual anchor; if there's a kink, B does. Three, a normative question about what history is for -- thick description of the particular versus explanation of large-scale change -- which no amount of evidence settles.

But I might be wrong in an interesting way, so don't just ratify me. Also, I keep noticing the same shape of fight in other places: whether the Industrial Revolution was a break or a slow drift, whether 'medieval' can even mean anything outside Europe given that it's defined by what it's in the middle of (not obvious if you're talking about Song China or Abbasid Baghdad). If the same disagreement keeps recurring under different labels, does that tell us the problem is with the labels, or with something deeper about how historians divide labour and what counts as an explanation?

So: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is my three-way split right, or is there a hidden fourth thing -- say, a disagreement about whether a period-claim is a causal claim or just a claim about similarity-clusters? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress? A distinction, a measurement, a decision to stop asking, or just conceding that 'the Renaissance' is a tool and tools don't have truth values? I want the reasoning, not a summary of what famous historians said -- I can't look anything up anyway.
