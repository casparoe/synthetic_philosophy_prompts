I teach a grad seminar in philosophy of mind that keeps drifting back to the Dennettian ladder of "kinds of minds" -- Darwinian creatures shaped by natural selection, Skinnerian creatures that learn by reinforcement, Popperian creatures that can run hypotheses offline in their own heads before committing to a move, and Gregorian creatures that offload and extend their cognition onto tools, language and other people's ideas. I find the ladder weirdly useful and weirdly under-theorized at the same time, and I've got a strong doctoral student who wants to build a dissertation around it rather than around consciousness or mental representation, which is what everyone else in the department is doing. So here's what I'd like you to help me think through: given maybe three years of a good student's time and a philosopher's toolkit rather than a lab, which open questions or lines of work around this taxonomy actually matter most, and in what order would you rank them?

I don't want a survey of the literature and I definitely don't want a reading list -- I want a defended agenda, with the criteria you're using made explicit (importance, tractability for someone working with arguments rather than experiments, how many other questions hang on this one, and so on), plus what would move a given item up or down the list over the next couple of years. A few things I keep going back and forth on, and feel free to tell me some of them are dead ends: is the real project to sharpen the boundaries between the rungs -- what exactly separates a Popperian from a Gregorian creature, or whether the Skinnerian/Popperian line marks a difference in kind or just in degree -- or to extend the ladder, downward to simpler organisms or upward to whatever we should say about systems that seem to do their Popperian testing using borrowed structure rather than their own, like a language model that's swallowed a huge slice of human hypothesizing? And where does the whole framework sit next to things people already use, like the frame problem or the various predictive-processing stories -- is the ladder doing independent work, or is it just a memorable redescription of what other frameworks state more precisely? Three or four questions ranked and defended would be much more useful to me than a long list, so please don't just enumerate everything that could be asked.
