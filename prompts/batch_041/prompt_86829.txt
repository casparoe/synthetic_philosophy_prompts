Ok so I need advice on a weird situation and I keep flip-flopping on it. I'm on the board of a small food co-op — about 400 member-owners, one store, always broke. We have a rent increase coming and the board has been deadlocked for three meetings on two options: raise shelf prices about 8%, or cut staff hours and shrink the deli counter. Everyone agrees the real goal is "still be open in five years with roughly the same members," but we can't agree on which option gets us there, and the arguments have gotten personal.

Then one board member, Dana, drops this proposal. She says we should stop arguing about which option is better and split the question in two. First, we all vote on which outcome we'd prefer — like, do we prefer a world where prices went up and we kept the deli, or a world where prices stayed and the deli shrank. That vote just picks the target. Then, once we've picked the target, we open a market: anyone can put money down on which of the two options (raise prices / cut hours) actually ends up producing that target outcome, and we commit in advance to just doing whatever the market says. She calls it "skin in the game for the facts, democracy for the values," and half the board thinks she's a genius and the other half thinks it's insane.

I'm the swing vote, so I actually have to decide. My gut says there's something clever here — it does seem to separate the thing we disagree about for real (what we want) from the thing we're just guessing about (what will happen). But I can't shake a few worries and I don't know which ones are the real problems vs. me being a luddite. Like: who's allowed to bet? If our landlord's management company can put money on "cut hours," aren't they just buying the outcome instead of predicting it? And what if the bettors aren't trying to be right but trying to make the thing they bet on come true? Also — and this is the one that nags me — even if the market is a great way to pick the best means, it doesn't seem to tell us anything about whether "keep the same members" is really the thing we should be optimizing; we picked that by fiat. And there's a boring practical thing: do we even have enough people, and enough money on the line, for any of these bets to mean anything?

So what I want from you is a straight read on this. Is Dana's scheme actually a good idea for a broke 400-member co-op, or is it the kind of thing that only works in a thought experiment? If it's worth trying, how should we set it up so it doesn't turn into a mess — who should be allowed to bet, what we should do about people betting to manipulate rather than predict, and whether there's some way to keep it honest when we're this small. If it's not worth trying, tell me exactly which part breaks and whether there's a cheaper fix for the underlying problem, which is that we can't tell good-faith disagreement about the facts from people just pushing what they want. And be concrete about the values thing — if the market can only optimize whatever number we hand it, does that make the whole proposal less radical than Dana thinks, or does it not matter?

Just write it back to me as one continuous reply, please — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just normal prose like you're emailing me, since I'll be reading this on my phone at the store.
