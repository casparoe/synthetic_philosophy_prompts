I host a science-and-ideas interview series, and next week's guest is a philosopher who defends heterophenomenology as the proper method for a science of consciousness: the investigator suspends the metaphysical question of whether first-person reports correspond to any phenomenal reality and instead builds a heterophenomenological world out of the subject's utterances, treating those utterances as texts to be interpreted. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the method. I want at least one question on whether the procedure can avoid collapsing into a sophisticated behaviorism, one on how it can distinguish a subject's confabulation from a genuine report, and one on what it can say about creatures that cannot give verbal reports, such as infants or non-human animals. For each of those three, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Also include a question on the reflexivity problem: the heterophenomenologist is herself a conscious subject whose reports would need the same interpretive treatment. Keep the questions concrete enough for listeners who have not read the literature, and do not ask for citations or reading lists.
