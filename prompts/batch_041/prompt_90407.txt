I am putting together a proposal for a small interdisciplinary grant (six months, roughly one day a week of my time) on whether it is true that the world is becoming less violent, and I need it written up as a formal research proposal of about 1,500 words. My half-formed idea is this: the disagreement is usually treated as an empirical dispute about the numbers, but I suspect the disputants are often not measuring the same thing, because the word 'violence' has drifted. It now covers, depending on who is speaking, only direct bodily assault, or also structural and institutional harms, or slow and cumulative harms such as pollution or neglect. If the extension of the term shifts with the theory, then the comparative claim that the world is less violent than it once was may not have a single truth-value: it may be true under a narrow reading and false or indeterminate under a wide one, and the interesting question is what follows from that.

I want the proposal to do five things. First, state the central question so that it has a determinate answer and so that a defender of either side would recognise it as the real crux — not simply 'has violence declined?' but something sharper about whether that claim survives a change in what is counted as violence. Second, explain why the existing positions leave this open: why the empirical and the conceptual strands of the debate have been kept apart, and why each side can concede the other's points without abandoning its conclusion. Third, set out the method. I imagine it as conceptual and measurement work rather than data collection: distinguish violence as a kind from violence as a magnitude; separate the question of how much there is from the question of what falls under the term; and work through the interaction between three variables — the breadth of the concept, the choice between absolute counts and per-capita rates, and the choice of denominator (total population versus population at risk, or versus some measure of the opportunities for violence). Fourth, say what a result would look like: what would count as showing that the comparative is well-defined after all, what would count as showing that it is not, and what a reasonable third outcome might be. Fifth, assess honestly what could make this too large for six months and where I would have to draw the line.

Please do not include a bibliography or a literature review by citation; refer to positions and arguments in prose and I shall add the references myself. Pitch it at the level of a mixed panel of philosophers and social scientists.
