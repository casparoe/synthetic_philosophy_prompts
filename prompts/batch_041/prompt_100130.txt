Next guest is a philosopher who's been defending origin essentialism for years, i.e. the Kripke-ish line that you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg, your actual origin is baked into who you are. She's got a wrinkle though: lately she's been pushing the same idea onto trained neural nets, arguing a model has an essential origin in its particular training run, so two nets that end up computing the exact same function are still different models if they got there via different seeds / data orderings. I want 8 to 10 questions that actually press her, not softballs.

Please make sure at least one question lands on each of these, and give me a follow-up for each keyed to the answer she's most likely to give:

- the identical-twins problem — if twinning happens after fertilization the two of them arguably share an origin, so does origin turn out to be necessary-but-not-sufficient for identity, and does that weaken the whole thing?
- the IVF / embryo-selection version — whether her view implies your existence swings on stuff that looks morally and metaphysically irrelevant, like which embryo the clinic happened to implant
- the Lewis-style counterpart objection, and whether she's quietly assuming haecceitism
- how she thinks we could ever know which of the origin facts are the essential ones, vs just the ones we happen to fixate on
- the AI extension specifically: does she mean sameness of weights or sameness of function, and what happens to her view if the answer is function

And end on one question that makes the AI extension do real work — something where the net case either earns its keep or exposes a crack in the original view. Casual is fine, these are just my notes to you.
