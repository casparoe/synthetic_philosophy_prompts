I have no formal training in philosophy, cognitive science, or artificial intelligence, but for several years I have been reading and thinking on my own about what it would take for a computation — a pattern of information processing running on some physical substrate — to matter morally, and about where the boundary of sentience ought to be drawn. I am now trying to decide where to put a year or two of solitary study, and I would rather spend it on whichever open question would make the others easier to answer than on whichever one happens to be most discussed. So I want you to tell me which open questions in this area most deserve that time, in order, and why.

To be clear about what I am after: not a survey of positions and not a reading list — I could not get most of the literature even if you named it — but a defended agenda. For each question, tell me why it is upstream of the others rather than beside them, how far someone working alone without a lab or funding could actually get on it, and what evidence or argument over the next couple of years ought to move it up or down the ranking.

Here is the knot I keep running into, which may or may not be the right way to cut it. It seems to me that three questions get run together. First, which physical systems are sentient. Second, which computations a given system implements — and here I am stuck on the thought that the same physical process can be described as implementing many different computations at once, so that "the computation" may be partly a matter of how we choose to describe the system rather than a fact sitting inside it. Third, of the computations a system implements, which ones are the ones that carry moral weight: all of them, only those that play a certain role in the system's own control of itself, or only those with the right kind of causal history. I have a suspicion that most disagreement in this area is really disagreement about the third question while everyone argues about the first, but I cannot tell whether that suspicion is a genuine insight or just the shape my own confusion takes.

I have also been bothered by a boundary question. When I try to say where a sentient system ends, I can describe the relevant processing either as bounded by a skull or as a pattern spread across several systems that interact over time — and I cannot tell whether the moral unit is the pattern or the thing that carries it, or whether that distinction even makes sense.

Give me your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question. And be explicit about how confident you are: say where your own reasoning is most likely to fail, and what argument or consideration would lead you to rank the questions differently.
