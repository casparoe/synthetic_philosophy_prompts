I teach a masters seminar on suffering and value, and I'm writing teh take-home final. I want one essay question on lexical threshold negative utilitarianism -- the view that there's some level of suffering above which no amount of pleasure can compensate, even if below that level pleasures and pains trade off. The question should force them to get into whether the threshold is a genuine incommensurability claim or just a very steep exchange rate. To keep it from being a book report, I've been using a distinction in class between the 'floor' reading (the threshold marks a category change: below it the scale is cardinal and comparable, above it pleasures don't even enter the ranking) and the 'cliff' reading (there's one scale all the way up, but the marginal rate at which pleasure buys off pain goes to infinity at the threshold). Ask them to say which reading makes better sense of the view, whether either can avoid arbitrariness in where the threshold sits, and what each implies for a case where you can either prevent a huge amount of threshold-level suffering for one person or give a massive but finite amount of pleasure to many people. Also make them address whether the view can still call itself negative utilitarian if it needs a positive-value scale to say what counts as compensation below the threshold. Please write the question, a model answer of about 550 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points out of 20 marks, plus a one-line note on the common mistake for each point and a line on what separates a first from a 2:1. Keep the whole thing under 950 words. No citations or reading lists -- I want to see if they can do the argument, not the bibliography.
