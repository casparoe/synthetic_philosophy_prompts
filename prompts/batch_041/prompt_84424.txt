I hung up the lab coat a few years ago after thirty-odd years as a marine geochemist, and since then I've been trying to catch up on the philosophy I never got to. Lately it's been Keynes on probability — the Treatise, plus the later essays where he sounds like a different man entirely. Here's roughly where I've landed and I'd like a sanity check before I inflict it on a reading group.

(1) Probability claims are objectively true or false. A probability is a logical relation between two propositions — the degree to which one partially entails the other — not a report on my degree of belief and not a fact about how often something has happened.

(2) But the probabilities I actually care about — will this patient respond to the drug, will this treaty hold, is this model's projection any good — usually aren't numbers and can't be measured. On a good day I can say one is larger than another, and that's it.

(3) Weight of evidence is a real thing and it matters over and above probability. Two hypotheses can carry the same probability, but if one rests on a mountain of evidence and the other on one thin argument, the well-evidenced one is the better basis for acting, and that difference isn't a difference in probability.

(4) Then there's the late stuff, where you simply don't know — no probability to be had, not even a rough ranking — and we get by on convention, on what everyone else is doing, on animal spirits.

(5) When I do have a probability, even a crude one, I should maximize expected value. When I don't, I shouldn't invent a number and then maximize on the number I invented.

(6) And yet, as a lab man to the bone: if a probability can't in principle be checked against frequencies — if I can't imagine any data that would bear on it — I get itchy calling it objective.

Is this a coherent position or just six things that each sound nice on their own? If something's pulling against something else, tell me which pairs, how bad it is, and which one I should give up, roughly ranked by how much it would cost me. And please be straight with me about which of these are settled and which are just my amateur reading — where the people who actually know the Treatise and the later essays disagree, don't hand me one camp's line and call it the consensus.
