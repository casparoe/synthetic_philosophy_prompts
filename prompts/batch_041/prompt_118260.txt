Q3. An agent's credences over the algebra generated by H and E are P(H) = 0.4, P(E|H) = 0.8, P(E|¬H) = 0.3.

(a) Compute P(E) and P(H|E).

(b) The agent now receives uncertain evidence that raises her credence in H to 0.7, and tells her nothing new about how likely E is given H or given ¬H. State the update rule you would use, compute her new P(E) and her new P(H|E), and say precisely what assumption you are making.

(c) Suppose instead she first learns E for certain by ordinary conditioning, and only then receives the uncertain evidence that raises her credence in H to 0.7. Does the order matter? Work out both orders.

(d) Two students each end up with credence 0.7 in H. The first applies the rule from (b) relative to the partition {H, ¬H}. The second applies it relative to the partition {H∧E, H∧¬E, ¬H}, taking the new credences of those three cells to be 0.2, 0.5 and 0.3. Must they agree about P(E)? About P(H|E)? What does your answer show about the rule?

Student's answer:

(a) P(E) = P(E|H)P(H) + P(E|¬H)P(¬H) = (0.8)(0.4) + (0.3)(0.6) = 0.32 + 0.18 = 0.5, so P(H|E) = 0.32/0.5 = 0.64.

(b) Bayes' rule doesn't apply directly here, because the evidence isn't a proposition she learns for certain. The right rule is Jeffrey conditionalization (probability kinematics). The assumption is rigidity: her conditional probabilities given H and given ¬H don't change, so P'(E|H) = 0.8 and P'(E|¬H) = 0.3. Then P'(E) = (0.8)(0.7) + (0.3)(0.3) = 0.56 + 0.09 = 0.65, and P'(H|E) = P'(H∧E)/P'(E) = 0.56/0.65 ≈ 0.86. Sanity check: if her new credence in H had been 1 rather than 0.7, this would collapse into ordinary conditionalization, which is what we'd want.

(c) The order makes no difference. Both orders leave her with the same two constraints -- credence 0.7 in H, and the original conditional credences for E given H and given ¬H -- and Jeffrey conditionalization is a linear operation on the probability vector, so the two updates commute. Conditioning on E first and then shifting P(H) to 0.7 therefore gives P(H|E) ≈ 0.86 as well.

(d) It doesn't matter which partition you use, as long as it really is a partition of the space and the constraint on H comes out right. Jeffrey's rule just combines the new probabilities of the cells with the old conditional probabilities inside them, and {H∧E, H∧¬E, ¬H} is just a refinement of {H, ¬H}, so the same constraint P(H) = 0.7 has to give the same posterior for E. Both students get P(E) = 0.65 and P(H|E) ≈ 0.86.
