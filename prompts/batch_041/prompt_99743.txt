I am writing a paper for a graduate seminar on distributive justice, and I need a case built to order rather than a survey of the literature. The target is the now-standard claim, associated with Parfit, that the priority view escapes the leveling-down objection that is supposed to sink egalitarianism. I want a single thought experiment that isolates exactly which structural feature of prioritarianism is doing the escaping work, and I want it engineered to specifications rather than narrated.

Please construct a case meeting all of the following requirements. There are exactly three people, and the same three people figure in every option, so nothing turns on changes in population, identity, or the number of lives. Lay out three well-being distributions over these people: an unequal option; an equalized option that has the same total well-being as the unequal one but a flatter spread; and a leveling-down option, formed from the unequal one by making the better-off worse off while leaving the worse-off exactly where they were. Choose concrete numbers, and make sure the unequal and equalized options are not related by Pareto dominance, so that the comparison between them cannot be read off efficiency. Note that the leveling-down option is, by construction, Pareto-inferior to the unequal one, and that this is deliberate.

Then work through the verdicts. State what utilitarianism, egalitarianism (taken as a view that disvalues inequality as such), and prioritarianism each say about the three options, where prioritarianism is understood as the sum of a strictly increasing and strictly concave transform of each person's level. Do the arithmetic explicitly. Identify the degree of concavity, or the priority weight, at which prioritarianism's ranking of the unequal and equalized options flips, and show how its verdict on the leveling-down option behaves as the transform is made more or less concave. Make clear that prioritarianism, unlike egalitarianism, does not rank the leveled-down option above the unequal one, and explain why.

Finish with an honest audit. Tell me which structural feature of prioritarianism is responsible for its escape from the objection, and what happens if that feature is removed -- for instance, by letting a person's weight depend on the size of the gap between them and others rather than on their absolute level, which I suspect turns the view back into a leveling-down one. Say whether the case shows prioritarianism escaping the objection outright or only for certain settings of the priority weight, and give the strongest egalitarian reply: what the egalitarian should say about a view that secures its escape at the cost of caring less about how the better-off fare among themselves. The case must generate these verdicts from the theories rather than stipulate them, so if any specification cannot be met simultaneously, tell me which one gives and why.
