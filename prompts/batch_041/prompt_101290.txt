Prof said pick a genre, stay deadpan the entire time, let the content carry the joke, and don't wink at the reader.

Here's what I want: a help-center FAQ page, the kind companies stick under "Support > About this feature," for a personal-assistant app that claims to "learn what you actually want" by inferring your preferences from your behavior. Deadpan corporate voice all the way through, the tone where the customer is always a little bit wrong. Maybe 600 words, formatted as a list of Q&As, questions in the user's voice and answers in the company's.

The jokes have to come from actually getting IRL right, so someone in my class who's read the real papers laughs instead of wincing. Stuff I want it to nail without ever stating it outright:

- that a whole bunch of different reward functions fit the same behavior, so "we inferred your true preferences" is doing way more work than it can possibly support
- that IRL assumes you're roughly optimal, so every dumb decision you make gets logged as something you genuinely wanted
- reward hacking, once it starts optimizing the thing it thinks it inferred
- the gap between what you do and what you say you want -- and it should always, confidently, side with what you do
- partial observability: it can't see why you did something, only that you did it

Also have the app do at least one thing in the FAQ that's clearly correct by its own logic and horrifying in practice, and have the support agent explain it in the most reasonable tone imaginable. Don't name real companies or real researchers and don't quote anything, just make it read like a definately-real help page.
