ok this is maybe a weird request but bear with me. im not a philosopher, i just read stuff at night on my own and ive gotten kind of obsessed with Al-Ghazali's thing about causation. the idea that the fire doesnt actually burn the cotton, God does, and all we ever really witness is that the burning keeps happening right after the touching. the fire is just the occasion. i keep explaining it to people with a vending machine: you push the button, the can drops, everyone says the button did it, but all you ever actually saw was the button and then the can, over and over, in that order. he thinks thats true of absolutely everything. nothing in the world pushes anything else, God just keeps re-making the whole world each moment out of habit, and the habit is why it looks like laws.

what i cant stop chewing on is the "each moment" part. if the world is remade fresh every instant, am i remade too? why is the person who wakes up me, and not some stranger who just happens to have my memories and my handwriting? "habit" seems to be doing an awful lot of work there and i cant tell if it holds.

i want a poem about this and i want it to be a pantoum. the reason is the form: the lines keep coming back but never in the same place, nothing new ever gets added, the poem just keeps re-making itself out of its own leftovers, and the last stanza usually loops back to the first. thats the whole idea, the re-creation and the no-progress and the loop that never actually ends because theres no last moment, just the next one. so please lean on that. the recycled lines should each read a little differently in their new spot, like the same thing happening again but as a new event, because thats what im after.

it needs to be accurate enough that someone whos actually read the arguments would nod and not wince, and clear enough that someone whos never heard of any of this still gets a good poem out of it. keep the fire/cotton kind of image if it helps, or the vending machine, whatever earns its place. dont let it turn into a sermon about everything being a miracle, and dont let it turn into a smug little ending about how science settled it either. and please dont wrap it up. i want it to end on the loop, mid-breath, still going, since thats honestly where i am with it. 4 or 5 stanzas is fine, maybe more if it needs room. im gonna read it out at the little group i go to so it should sound ok aloud. thanks.
