I'm in a reading group on Frege and we've hit a wall. Two camps have formed. The first—call it the seepage camp—thinks Frege's diary, the one with the antisemitic and authoritarian political remarks, isn't just an embarrassing biographical appendix. They say the same habits of mind show up in the philosophy: the craving for purity, the sharp hierarchies (concept over object, sense over reference, the third realm over the mental, logic over psychology), the contempt for anything muddy, democratic, or merely psychological. On their view, if you read the logical works without the diary, you're missing the point; the politics and the philosophy are cut from the same cloth. The second camp—the firewall camp—thinks that's a category mistake. Even if Frege was a nasty piece of work, the logical doctrines stand or fall by argument. The diary tells you about the man, not about whether logicism is true, and using it to reinterpret the philosophy is just ad hominem with extra steps.

We want to stop shouting past each other. So please draft the joint statement that both camps could actually sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. To keep us honest, we've been using a little homemade taxonomy of ways the diary might bear on the philosophy: truth-bearing (it makes some philosophical claim less likely to be true), warrant-bearing (it undermines Frege's entitlement to the claim, even if the claim is true), interpretation-bearing (it changes the best reading of what the claim means or is doing), and pedagogical/ethical-bearing (it changes whether or how we should teach or cite him, without touching the truth of the doctrines). Use that taxonomy if it helps, or replace it if you can do better. I want the agreed claims first, numbered, including what both sides can say about the diary itself, about the relation between biography and doctrine, and about which of the four bearing relations are actually in play. Then a disagreements table: for each remaining disagreement, give the continuity/seepage camp's formulation in wording they'd accept, the firewall camp's formulation in wording they'd accept, the crux—the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side—and for each crux a concrete case or argument that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Some cruxes I suspect are lurking: whether there's any defensible mechanism from political ideology to logical doctrine, or whether the alleged parallels are just mood music; whether the diary counts as evidence about Frege's epistemic character and thus about the warrant of his logical claims; whether the private/unpublished status of the diary matters; whether the third realm's objectivity is undermined or actually reinforced by his politics; and whether the diary changes the best interpretation of specific doctrines or only our feelings about him. Don't just take my list—if a better crux is hiding, include it.

Finally, say explicitly which parts of the dispute, if any, are merely verbal, and what it would take for each side to change its mind. No bibliography, citations, or reading list—I don't have web access and I don't want you guessing at scholarship. Just the document, in plain prose, with the table at the end. And please don't make the agreed claims so thin that they're vacuous; the whole point is to find formulations that are neither slanted nor empty.
