He finished with exactly what he started with, 30p lighter. He wasn't bothered about the 30p, he was bothered about what he thought it proved.

Rowan: that shows the loop is irrational, full stop. If a set of preferences lets someone drain you like that, there's something wrong with them. The cure is to have your preferences stack up into a single ranking with a number on each option, so you can just maximise the number.

Bethan: it shows nothing of the kind. It shows a patient trader who knows your preferences can take your money a small step at a time. Being trickable by a clever salesman isn't the same as being irrational.

Rowan: that doesn't rescue him. The reason he says yes at each step is one he'd still endorse at the end — every swap is an improvement by his own lights at the moment he makes it. And if he turns down the first swap only because of what's coming later, then his choice depends on what else happens to be on the menu, which is exactly the sort of thing that shouldn't matter. Two options are two options whether or not a third is lurking.

Bethan: of course it should matter. Turning down a trade that's part of a package leaving you worse off is what a sensible person does — you're just assuming that only pairwise comparisons count. So it's a claim about being vulnerable to a certain kind of patter, not about coherence.

Rowan: then take the belief version, which is stronger.
