A friend and I spent last night arguing whether the kalam cosmological argument is a genuinely distinct argument or merely a temporal special case of the contingency argument, and I would like a careful comparison rather than a verdict by label. Please compare the two on the work done by their central premises: the kalam’s premise that whatever begins to exist has a cause, and the contingency argument’s principle that everything contingent has an explanation. Then test them against two cases: an eternal universe with no first moment but still contingent, and a universe with a first moment that is nonetheless necessary. Which argument survives which case, and what does that show about whether the kalam needs the contingency argument or the contingency argument needs the kalam? Finally, say whether the necessary being at the end of the contingency argument and the cause at the end of the kalam are the same entity under different descriptions or different theoretical posits, and which of the two arguments is more vulnerable to the objection that its key principle is either too weak to rule out an infinite regress or too strong to be plausible.
