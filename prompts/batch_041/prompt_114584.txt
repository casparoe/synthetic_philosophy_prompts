I write about science and ideas for a general readership, and for months now I have been going back and forth with a friend of mine — a working physicist with a weakness for philosophy — about Hume's argument concerning miracles. What I want from you is not an exposition of that argument but your own credence in a single, sharply stated claim, together with the decomposition that actually produces it. The claim is this: that no possible body of human testimony — however vast, however mutually corroborating, however scrupulous the witnesses — could ever make it rational for a suitably informed person to believe that a miracle has occurred, where 'miracle' is understood strictly as a violation of a law of nature. Give me a number between 0 and 1 for that claim, and then break it into the sub-questions it truly rests on, assigning each a rough probability and one line of justification before you combine them. I expect those sub-questions to include at least: whether 'miracle' remains a coherent category once one accepts that the laws of nature may be statistical or defeasible rather than iron; whether the prior probability of a law-violation should be treated as exactly zero, as undefined, or merely as very small; whether Hume is best read as making an in-principle claim or a merely in-practice one, and how much your final number turns on that interpretive choice; whether testimony is even the sort of evidence that could in principle swamp a very small prior; and the reflexive worry that our knowledge of the very laws in question is itself substantially testimonial, so that a report of a miracle would be doing double duty — at once evidence against a law and an event defined by its violation. Then tell me which sub-question the number is most sensitive to, and what argument or evidence would move it most.

I would also like to see how much of the disagreement between my friend and me is really about the argument and how much is about the definition. So please give a second number for the proposition that our quarrel would survive the substitution of a different construal of 'miracle' — say, an event that exceeds the explanatory resources of any natural law we shall ever possess, rather than one that violates a law now in force — and tell me which reading makes your original number higher and why. Where a term is doing load-bearing work, define it as you go rather than leaving it to the reader; I would rather have your stipulations on the table than have to infer them.

Throughout, please write in British English — spellings, punctuation and idiom — and keep the prose in the register of a serious essay rather than a bulleted list, even though the decomposition will of course have parts.
