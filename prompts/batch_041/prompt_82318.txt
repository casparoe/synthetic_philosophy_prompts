After forty years as a field ecologist, I have spent my retirement reading philosophy of technology, and I am stuck on the idea of low-impact AI. In my old lab we distinguished direct from indirect effects relative to a stated model; we never needed a complete theory of the ecosystem to do it. I have written out an argument that seems to show the idea is incoherent, and I cannot see which step to give up:

(1) For a change to count as a side effect of an action, it must be possible to distinguish the changes that constitute the action's intended outcome from those that are merely collateral.
(2) That distinction requires a specification of the agent's values complete enough to determine, for every possible change, whether it belongs to the intended outcome or is collateral.
(3) No real agent, and certainly no general-purpose AI, can be given such a complete specification of values.
(4) Therefore no change can be identified as a side effect.
(5) An agent can avoid side effects only if side effects can be identified.
(6) Therefore no agent can avoid side effects, and any research program that aims to build low-impact AI is incoherent.
(7) But low-impact AI research has produced apparently usable measures, and the instruction to avoid unnecessary disruption seems meaningful. So something must be wrong.

Which step should I give up, and what does giving it up cost? Please answer in no more than 400 words, beginning with the step you reject.
