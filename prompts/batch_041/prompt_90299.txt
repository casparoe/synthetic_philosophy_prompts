I am setting the final examination for an upper-level philosophy of medicine course. The unit is on concepts of health and disease, specifically the dispute between naturalism and normativism. I need one essay question that cannot be answered by reciting the usual contrast between the biostatistical theory and its normative critics. The question should turn on a case that forces students to decide whether a condition counts as a disease when the biological dysfunction is statistical and environment-relative and the harm is merely predictive.

Use this case. Let us call a condition constitutive if the condition just is the biological dysfunction, and prognostic if it is a statistically defined risk state that predicts future dysfunction but does not itself constitute current dysfunction. Consider subclinical dysregulation (SD): a biomarker profile that is rare in the population, arises from a mechanism that reduces the organism's capacity to maintain homeostasis under stress, and produces no present pain, disability, or distress. Longitudinal data show that 30% of people with SD develop a serious disease within ten years; 70% do not. An intervention normalizes the biomarker and reduces ten-year risk, but it has side effects and is costly. A health system must decide whether to classify SD as a disease for coverage, research priority, and stigma. The naturalist says disease is biological dysfunction; the normativist says disease is a harmful condition constituted by social values. The only evidence for dysfunction is a statistical deviation from species-typical functioning in a particular environment; the only evidence for harm is a probabilistic prediction of future harm.

Write the exam question, a model answer of about 700 to 800 words, and a marking scheme. The exam question should be self-contained, include the case and the constitutive/prognostic distinction, and be answerable in 75 minutes. It should require students to: (1) state whether SD is a disease under the strongest naturalist account and under the strongest normativist account; (2) explain why the constitutive/prognostic distinction puts pressure on both; (3) propose and defend a criterion or decision procedure that could settle the classification without merely stipulating a definition; and (4) say whether the remaining disagreement is substantive or verbal, and what the case shows about the limits of the naturalism/normativism debate. The model answer should take a clear position on SD and defend it; it should not merely summarize positions. The marking scheme should total 20 marks, should consist of five must-hit points with the marks for each, and for each point should include a one-line note on how candidates typically get it wrong. It should also include a short statement of what separates an A answer from a B answer, naming the specific moves each must contain. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or verbatim quotations. After the marking scheme, end with a final line in the form \answer{<short phrase naming the single crux the question is designed to isolate>}.
