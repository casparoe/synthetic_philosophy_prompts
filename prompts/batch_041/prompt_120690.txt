I'm giving a talk at a small conference on moral change next month and need a Q&A prep sheet. My thesis: moral revolutions don't happen because arguments win or because material interests shift; they happen because a practice flips from being a costly signal of high status to a costly signal of low status, and arguments mostly supply the vocabulary for a flip that the signaling environment has already driven. I'm extending Appiah's honor-centered account, but I think he underplays two things: honor is a portfolio of costly signals with substitutes, so a practice loses its honor value once a cheaper or more legible substitute signal becomes available; and metropolis and colony are separate honor markets, so abolition can be a status play by metropolitan elites against planter elites. I use dueling in England, footbinding in China, and the abolition of the slave trade and slavery in the British Atlantic as my cases.

The audience is mostly historians of Britain and the Atlantic world, several of whom work on the economics of slavery and are either sympathetic to the Williams thesis (abolition came when slavery was ceasing to pay) or to its critics (abolition came at the peak of profitability); two Kant scholars who think natural-rights arguments did independent work; a philosopher of social science who is hostile to functional explanation; and one anthropologist who thinks honor is too blunt a variable to do the work I'm asking it to do. Give me the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this room — not the ones I'd get from a rationalist meetup — ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each phrased the way that particular objector would phrase it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. I've spent enough time in the old rationalist blogosphere to be allergic to hand-waving, so keep the mechanisms concrete. Write in American English, tell me how confident you are in your read of this audience, and single out the one objection you're least sure I'll actually face.
