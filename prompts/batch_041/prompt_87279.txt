My opening: here's what I definately don't get. You say there's a fact of the matter, before God decrees anything and before any creature exists, about what I would freely do if I were in some situation C. Fine. But what makes that fact true? It can't be God's will, since you say it's prior to any decree. It can't be my essence, since you say the choice is free and my nature doesn't settle it — if my essence settled it you'd have natural knowledge of a necessary truth, not middle knowledge of a contingent one, and I wouldn't be free anyway. And there's no me yet to do the settling. So the truth just hangs there in the void with nothing holding it up, and a truth with no truthmaker isn't a truth, it's a sentence you happen to like. So which is it — do you drop the priority of middle knowledge over decree, drop creaturely freedom, or admit you've got a truth that nothing makes true?
