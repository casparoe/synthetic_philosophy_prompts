Something thats been bugging me for a while and I dont have the words for it. I mess about with little physics toys, and I built my own version of that careenium idea -- a few thousand tiny magnetised balls in a shallow dish, where now and then a clump of them sticks together loosely and the clump bounces around like a single bigger ball. So two clumps collide and bounce apart. I can describe that as one clump hitting another, and that description tells me what happens next; and if I want to change what happens, I act at that level -- flick a clump with a paddle, slide a wall into its path -- and it works, reliably. But when I zoom in there is no push coming from the clump. Every push is one tiny ball on another. The clump is just a name for a shape that a bunch of balls happen to be in. So at that level it does nothing, and yet it's the only level at which I can say anything useful about what's going to happen.

Two features of my setup might be the whole story or might be red herrings: the same clump is made of different balls from one moment to the next, and two quite different sets of balls can be the same clump, so the levels dont line up one-to-one; and my paddle-flick, which feels like acting on a clump, is also secretly just balls on balls.

Does this have a name? I know there's a big argument about whether your thoughts can cause anything and it feels related, but there are no minds anywhere in my toy, and I want the phenomenon itself named and separated from its neighbours. If it turns out to be one of the standard things, which one is it, and what does my version add or leave out -- in particular, would the puzzle be any different if each clump were permanently tied to one fixed set of balls? And if there is no name, coin one and tell me where its edges are, because I dont care about the label, I care about what this is and what it isnt.
