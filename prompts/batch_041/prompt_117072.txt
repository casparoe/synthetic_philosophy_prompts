Suppose William James — who died in 1910, decades before the idea that the brain might literally be a computer became a serious theoretical option — were revived and asked to assess that idea as it is deployed today. Reconstruct the assessment you judge he would have given.

Organize the reconstruction around four claims, kept distinct throughout, since they are often run together:

1. Implementation. The brain realizes some specifiable algorithm or program.
2. Explanatory sufficiency. Citing the algorithm the brain runs explains cognitive capacities at least as well as citing its physiology, and in some respects better.
3. Level autonomy. The computational description constitutes a level of explanation in its own right, rather than a convenient redescription of the neural one.
4. Triviality. Anything with the right causal organization computes, so the question whether the brain is a computer is settled by stipulation rather than by evidence.

For each claim, say whether James would have accepted it, rejected it, or held that it was not yet a determinate question, and identify which of his standing commitments drives the verdict — his account of the stream of thought, his warning against the psychologist's fallacy, the theory of emotion he developed with Lange, his radical empiricism, his pragmatism, or his naturalistic sympathy with evolutionary explanation. Paraphrase his positions; do not supply quotations, page references, or citations, and do not invent views for him. Where his writings genuinely leave the matter open, say so, and mark that portion of your answer as extrapolation rather than record.

Then address four further matters:

(a) James would be likely to apply his own pragmatic method here, asking what practical difference the answer makes. State what he would conclude, and then say whether that is a fair application of his method or a misapplication of it in this case.

(b) One part of the modern package is not new to him: the thought that perception involves something like inference was already in circulation in his lifetime, in the Helmholtzian tradition he engaged with. Say which components of today's computationalism are genuinely novel relative to what he knew and which are continuous with positions he had already taken.

(c) Name the kind of evidence or argument that would have moved him off the claim he is most likely to reject, and say whether that evidence was available in principle in his own time.

(d) Audit your own answer: identify the single place where you are most likely projecting a present-day debate onto James rather than reconstructing him, and say what would settle the matter.

Close by noting any conflict between his predicted verdict on computationalism and something else he is known to have maintained, and say which commitment he would probably have surrendered.
