Draft the joint statement that two philosophers of material constitution could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the statue and the lump of clay: one who holds that the statue and the lump are two numerically distinct objects in the same place — the lump constitutes but is not identical to the statue — and one who holds that there is only one object there, described in two ways. I want a numbered agreed-claims section (what the case involves; what the options are; what any view must accommodate, including the apparent difference in persistence conditions, modal properties such as being essentially a statue, and what is true of each when the lump is squashed or remolded; and which moves would beg the question), followed by a disagreements section stating each side's position in wording its own side would accept. Then lay out the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — including whether composition is identity, whether objects can be counted independently of a sortal, what grounds the difference between the statue and the lump if they share all their parts, and whether the reasoning that yields two coincident objects does not equally yield indefinitely many. For each crux, give a case or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If you judge part of the dispute to be merely verbal, say which part and why. Close by stating how confident you are in the accuracy of each reconstructed position and singling out the one formulation you are least sure of.
