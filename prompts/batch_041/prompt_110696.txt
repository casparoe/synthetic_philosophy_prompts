I never studied philosophy — I drive a delivery van and read in the evenings — so I'm putting this to you rather than to my friend, who teaches history at a secondary school, is a serious Catholic, and takes Anscombe's case against Truman over Hiroshima and Nagasaki, and her protest when Oxford wanted to honour him in the 1950s, as the last word on the subject: Truman, he says, was a murderer, not a man who faced a hard call and got it wrong. The rule he repeats is that choosing to kill the innocent as a way of getting what you want is always murder; he also says a man who merely signs the paper is the man who did it.

I have been testing him with cases, and here is his record so far. A bomber crew sent to wreck one particular factory, knowing the houses around it will probably come down on the families inside: not murder — they are not out to kill the neighbours, and if the factory is worth wrecking, that is that. A general who blows a dam to flood the valley and force a surrender, saying he has nothing against the people who will drown: murder, because the drowning is what makes the enemy quit. A sheriff who could save five hostages by framing and hanging one innocent man: murder, and he must not. A scientist who designs the bomb but has no say in its use: not a murderer, though mixed up in something filthy.

Now the case I have just put to him. A country invades its neighbour, and the invaded country's minister sets up a programme, run by a targeting system rather than by people, to cut the invader's supply lines. One of the strikes it selects is certain, not merely likely, to bring down a rail tunnel and the town sitting on top of it; the system's own analysts say so. The minister approves the settings and lets it run. He says he does not intend a single one of those deaths — what he intends is to stop the invasion — and that it was the system, not he, that picked the tunnel. He also insists the deaths achieve nothing: the tunnel is what matters, and the town merely happens to be above it.

What will my friend say about the minister? Which of his earlier verdicts is the nearest model for the new case? Which rules fit everything he has said so far, which rule would you bet he is really running, and what single further case would show whether it is that rule or a rival?

Begin with your verdict in one sentence, then give the reasoning. No throat-clearing, and do not restate my question.
