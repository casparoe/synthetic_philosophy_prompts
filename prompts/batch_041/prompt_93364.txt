I run a small grant program (think low seven figures a year, three-year grants) and I get to set the research agenda for the next cycle. The topic I've been handed is the distribution of consciousness across animals: which creatures are conscious, how confident we should be about each group, and what would actually justify drawing a line somewhere. Most of what crosses my desk is either philosophy-of-mind throat-clearing about the hard problem or yet another invertebrate nociception study, and I dont think either is where the marginal dollar does the most work.

So: give me an agenda. Which open questions in this area most deserve the next three years of a handful of researchers, and in what order? What I care about is the reasoning, not the list. For each one, tell me whether it's upstream of other work or downstream, whether it's tractable with methods that actually exist right now, and what concrete result over the next few years would move it up or down my list.

Some things I'm already chewing on, so react to them rather than ignoring them: whether the field can converge on behavioral or anatomical markers that are at least somewhat theory-neutral, as opposed to ones that just presuppose a favorite theory of consciousness; whether the whole framing should treat consciousness as binary or as graded, and what turns on that; how the competing neuroscientific theories (recurrent processing vs global workspace vs higher-order, etc.) make genuinely different predictions about where the line falls, and whether that disagreement is a feature or a bug for someone trying to spend money; and whether questions about the function of consciousness (what it's for, if anything) are load-bearing here or a distraction. Also, "who decides" matters to me: if the line is going to be drawn partly on pragmatic or ethical grounds rather than pure science, say so and tell me which decisions that pushes upstream.

I want you to separate what's actually settled among people who know this area from what's genuinely contested, and dont hand me a contested view dressed up as the consensus. Where you're unsure, say you're unsure.

Format: lead with a markdown table of your ranked top priorities. Columns: Rank | Question | Why it's upstream (what else depends on it) | Tractable how / with what methods | What would change its rank in ~3 years. Then, after the table, a few paragraphs defending the ordering and naming the single objection that would most make you doubt it. Keep the whole thing under about 900 words.
