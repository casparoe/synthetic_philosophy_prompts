I am building a filter rather than a survey, so I need the specification before I can use it: set out the desiderata that any adequate account of the unity of the proposition must satisfy.

The target phenomenon, so that we are talking about the same thing. A proposition is something that says that such-and-such and is thereby assessable as true or false; its constituents are the items it is about and the items it applies to them. The unity problem is to say what distinguishes such an item from the mere aggregate of its constituents, and in particular what distinguishes the proposition that a bears R to b from the proposition that b bears R to a when both are built from the same three items. Bradley's regress is the standing threat: if the difference is furnished by a further item — a copula, a relation of predication, a tying relation — that item stands in need of being tied to what it ties, and so on; if no further item furnishes it, it is unclear what does.

I want the requirements list, with the consideration behind each. For every requirement, state the case or result that motivates it, and keep separate three kinds of demand that are easily run together: (1) phenomena the account must explain — what unity is, why it is not merely complexity, why some combinations are propositions and others are not; (2) cases it must classify correctly — converse pairs, category mistakes, propositions containing no saturated constituent, propositions embedded as constituents of further propositions, false and impossible propositions; (3) results it must not contradict, including the compositionality that lets a finite stock of constituents yield unboundedly many propositions.

Then do four things with the list. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of a particular camp (psychologism, Platonism, deflationism about propositions, nominalism about properties, and so on). Flag every subset you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied and say which member of the subset has to go; this is the part I most want, since a list with an unsatisfiable core is itself a result. Name the single requirement that excludes the most otherwise attractive accounts. And say whether the list, taken whole, can be met by a view that dispenses with propositions altogether or whether it forces them.

Two constraints on how the requirements are stated, since I will use the list to assess accounts that are not in the literature. First, no requirement may be stated as fidelity to an author's text or terminology; I want criteria a competent reader could apply without having read anyone. Second, the list must be sharp enough to decide a candidate it has never seen, so I am giving you one.

Candidate. Constituents come with slots: a property or relation is unsaturated in a fixed number of places and demands saturation, while a particular is saturated and saturates. A proposition is what results when every slot of every constituent is saturated by another constituent, and unity is nothing over and above the exhaustion of slots. There is no tying item, hence no regress. Variant A: slots belong to the constituent's identity, so a relation just is a thing with ordered places. Variant B: slots are assigned by a separate mode of combination that is itself unsaturated. Variant C: only the arity is intrinsic, and the order of saturation is imposed by a further convention.

Assess the candidate and its variants against the list, and note where the list is silent: if it does not decide the candidate, that silence is a defect in the list and I want it recorded as one.

Also press the list against these cases, chosen because they cut across the usual camps:

(i) the proposition that this very proposition is unified, so that the unity of the whole depends on the unity of a part;
(ii) a proposition with no saturated constituent, such as one consisting entirely of quantifiers, variables, and a relation;
(iii) a pair of propositions with the same constituents, the same arity, and the same slots, differing only in the order of the slots, where one is true and the other false;
(iv) a proposition embedded under negation, or under an attitude no one holds, so that its unity cannot be the product of any act of judgment;
(v) a category mistake, where the question is whether it is a false proposition or not a proposition at all, and what unity could mean in either case;
(vi) a purported proposition whose content is that there are no propositions, or that unity is an illusion.

Finally, one paragraph and only one: the standard families — Fregean concept and object, Russellian structured propositions with a multiple-relation account of judgment, and the reduction of propositions to sets of possible worlds or to inferential role — scored against the finished list, with each failure traced to the requirement it violates. Do not let this displace the list; the criteria are the deliverable.
