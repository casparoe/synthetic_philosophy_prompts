Ok so I have to hand in a philosophical profile on supererogation for my seminar and I want a consistency check before I get torn apart in front of everyone. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Supererogation is real — there are acts that are morally better than what I'm required to do and that I'm not required to do. The saint who gives away everything isn't doing her duty, she's exceeding it, and that's a different thing.

2. I think the mirror image is real too: some acts are permitted but genuinely objectionable — the sort of thing you had every right to do and that I can still hold against you. You didn't do anything wrong and you're still kind of a jerk about it.

3. The line between what's required and what's merely nice is fixed by what other people can demand of me. And a demand has to be reciprocal — I owe you roughly what I couldn't refuse to justify to you if you pushed me on it.

4. The reasons that bear on what I'm allowed to do are agent-neutral. Suffering is suffering, whoever's it is and whoever has to pay to fix it. When I'm allowed to put my own projects first, that's not because my reasons somehow weigh more to me — its just a permission not to maximize, not a difference in the reasons themselves.

5. Missing a chance to be supererogatory is never blameworthy (you were within your rights), though it can leave you a smaller person than you could have been. Doing the suberogatory is blameworthy even though it was permitted.
