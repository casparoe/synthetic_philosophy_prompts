I've spent a fair amount of time in the worst-case-versus-average-case debates in AI alignment theory, and I keep running into the same frustration: everyone agrees that a guarantee is only as strong as the class of adversaries it quantifies over, and everyone also suspects that a guarantee strong enough to be interesting is either vacuous or impossible, because you cannot quantify over "all possible adversaries" without either trivializing the claim or quietly smuggling in a distribution over adversaries that you had no right to assume. In a reading group recently we realized we were conflating at least three distinct targets that get called worst-case guarantees: (a) a guarantee that holds against every input in a formally specified set, (b) a guarantee that holds against an adversary that is itself bounded — by compute, by information, or by some criterion of what counts as a realistic optimizer — and (c) a guarantee that holds with high probability over a family of environments we cannot fully specify but can constrain. My suspicion is that most of the apparent disagreement in the field dissolves once you say which of these you mean, and that the choice among them is upstream of nearly everything else, but I can't tell whether that's a genuine crux or just a verbal confusion I've talked myself into.

So here is what I'd like from you. Suppose you had to direct a small theory group — a handful of people, three years, no compute to speak of — and you had to decide which open conceptual questions in this area are worth their time. Which questions come first, and in what order? I care much less about the list than about the reasoning: for each, why it is upstream of other work rather than downstream, how tractable it is for people doing conceptual rather than empirical work, whether the answer would actually change what anyone builds or funds, and what would have to happen over the next year or two for you to move it up or down the ranking. Please be explicit about the criteria you're trading off — importance, tractability, neglectedness, how many other questions depend on this one — and about where they conflict, because I expect the honest ranking to require sacrificing one of them. And if you think the (a)/(b)/(c) trichotomy I sketched is itself the wrong cut, say so and give me the cut you'd use instead, since I'd rather be corrected on the framing than get a tidy answer to the wrong question. What I don't want is a survey of who has said what; I want a defended agenda, with the load-bearing assumptions exposed, so that I can argue with it.
