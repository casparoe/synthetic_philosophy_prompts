Please describe the most important implications of the philosophical debate over paternalism and nudging for a decision my community fridge committee is stuck on. I have no academic background; I have simply been reading and thinking about this on my own, and I would like the answer pitched at someone in my position rather than at a specialist.

Some context. I help run a volunteer-run community fridge on our street. We want more of the people who take food from it to also sign up for a cleaning shift, and the committee keeps going back and forth between two proposals. The first is to rearrange things so that the sign-up sheet is the first thing a visitor sees and the default box on the form is already ticked, and to post a notice saying that most of the neighbours who use the fridge also take a shift. The second is simply to explain, in plain terms, why the fridge needs the help, and then let people decide for themselves.

The more I think about it, the more it seems to me that these two proposals are not the same kind of thing at all, even though both would usually be called "nudges." The first changes what a person believes about how other people behave; it works by handing them a picture of the social norm. Rearranging the shelf, by contrast, works on how easy or how salient the options are without touching what anyone believes about anyone else. What strikes me as odd is that the paternalism objection people usually raise seems aimed at the rearranging, whereas the notice about what "most people" supposedly do seems to me the more troubling of the two, because it does not merely tilt the field -- it inserts something like a fact into the person's reasoning, and if that claim is only roughly true, then we are getting the result by something hard to distinguish from a polite lie. One committee member's view is that none of this matters as long as the sign-up sheet fills up.

Please describe the most important implications of the paternalism-and-nudging debate for how I should think about which of these two things we ought to do, and for how I should defend the choice to the rest of the committee. Keep it to no more than 600 words.
