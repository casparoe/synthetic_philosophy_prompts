I have been asked to write out my metaethical position in a form my colleagues can attack, and before I circulate it I want to know whether it actually hangs together. Here is where I have landed.

(i) There are no framework-independent moral truths: a moral judgement is true or false only relative to a moral code, and there is no standpoint outside every code from which such judgements could be assessed.

(ii) A moral code is individuated by its constitutive commitments rather than by its surface norms — by the things its adherents could not abandon and still be following that code. Two communities whose members behave identically may therefore be following different codes, if what underwrites the behaviour differs.

(iii) Because of (i), the only legitimate way to criticise someone who belongs to another code is immanent critique: showing them that a surface norm they accept contradicts a constitutive commitment of their own code.

(iv) Codes can nonetheless be ranked. One code is better than another when it is more internally coherent and when its adherents can reflectively endorse it as a whole.

(v) There is no standpoint from which to condemn a whole code as such — only particular norms within it.

(vi) Toleration is the right response to codes we find repellent.

I suspect these do not all fit together, but I cannot see where the seams are. Tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension and which pairs only appear to be; rank the real tensions by severity; and for each, say what would have to be given up and what that would cost. I would rather have the cheapest single revision that relieves the most pressure than an undifferentiated list of everything that could be trimmed. Please answer in British English.
