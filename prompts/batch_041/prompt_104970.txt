So I moved to Amsterdam last year for work, my girlfriend is Dutch, and I've got this recurring argument with myself about politeness, directness and lying that I'd like you to settle for me. I keep giving verdicts on cases and I can't find the rule behind them.

- My Dutch colleague, right after my pitch, says flat out "I don't think this works, and here's why." I feel fine about this — more than fine, I'd definately rather have it.
- Same colleague says the exact same words in front of the whole team and my boss. I think that was wrong, and not because it was blunt. The audience is the thing.
- My Japanese friend, when I ask if she can help me move, says "that might be a bit difficult." I read it as a no, I don't feel lied to, it actually seems kind.
- Same friend says "sure, no problem," then cancels the night before with a vague excuse. Nothing she said was false, but this one bothers me more than the "difficult" one.
- I tell my grandmother the soup is delicious when it's pretty bland. Fine.
- I tell my partner I love a gift I don't really love. Fine.
- A colleague asks if their draft is ready to submit, it isn't, I say the methods need another pass. Fine, and I'd feel gross saying "looks good."
- My doctor tells me a scan is "nothing to worry about" when it's actually ambiguous. Not fine, not remotely.
- I tell a student who's falling behind that they're doing great, to keep their morale up. I genuinely can't decide whether this is fine, and it's the one I keep flip-flopping on.

The closest thing I have to a rule is: what I say is OK exactly when the person doesn't have a right to the truth of that particular thing. Grandma has no claim on my opinion of her soup; my doctor has a claim on my scan. But that doesn't touch the public-versus-private colleague case, and I've no idea what it does with the student.

So: is there a principle that gets these, or close? Take my rule, test it against the cases, repair it where it breaks, tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed, and be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save — I'd rather drop a verdict than keep a rule that's been contorted to preserve it. And please don't just lay the options out and leave the choice to me; actually come down on a principle and defend it.
