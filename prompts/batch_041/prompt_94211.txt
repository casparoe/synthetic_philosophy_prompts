I serve on the grantmaking committee of a regional arts foundation. We have one unrestricted award of $500,000 left this cycle, and the committee has narrowed the field to two finalists, both of which would occupy the same decommissioned municipal hall in a small city of about 40,000 people. The first is a professional repertory company that would renovate the hall, hire a resident ensemble, and run a paid subscription season of classic and contemporary plays. The second would keep the hall in near-daily public use through free indoor and outdoor festivals, a community chorus, amateur productions, and open town meetings, with small stipends for the organizers rather than a salaried company.

A committee member I respect has circulated a memo urging us to fund the second proposal on grounds she draws from Rousseau's critique of the theater: that a professional stage trains citizens to be spectators who feel virtue vicariously instead of practicing it, that it flatters the audience's image of itself, and that it would quietly displace the participatory culture the hall was built to host. I am not persuaded this gets us all the way to the second proposal, but I also do not think we can wave it away, and the deadline for announcing the award is in three weeks.

Draft the committee's written decision for me to circulate. Pick one of the two proposals, give the reasoning in a form the losing side could actually engage with rather than simply overrule, and state what we should attach to the award — conditions, an evaluation plan, and a time frame. Engage the Rousseauan objection at its strongest, including whether it really favors the festival model over the theater, and be candid about what the evidence cannot settle.
