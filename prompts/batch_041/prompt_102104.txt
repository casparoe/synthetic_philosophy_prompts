I run a monthly "philosphy for programmers" night at a co-working space, usually 10 to 14 people, a mix of backend engineers, a couple of data folks, one guy with a maths degree who never lets us forget it, and my flatmate who mostly comes for the snacks. We get 90 minutes and it works best when I keep the setup short, spend most of the time on some activity, and leave a good chunk for a debrief, and when I don't lecture first but let them walk into the problem themselves. Last month we did the heap / bald-man thing and it landed really well, so for next month I want Zeno, specifically the one where you can't cross the room because you keep having to cover half of what's left first. I want them to feel two things at once, and I think a game can do it. First, that the endless list of half-steps actually adds up to a finite distance, so the usual "the limit is just the whole length" answer feels like it shuts the case. And second, that this doesn't obviously answer the nagging bit about how you ever finish doing infinitely many things, because the very same move applied to a lamp that gets flicked on and off with each flick taking half as long as the last tells you it's been flicked infinitely many times by the two-minute mark and leaves you with no answer about whether it's on or off. That second half is what I really want them to sit in, the feeling that the maths hands you a number but not an account of the finishing.

Can you design the whole session for me? Treat yourself as the expert here, someone who knows both the old arguments and the modern maths of infinite series and of tasks that get completed in infinitely many steps, and please think it through carefully before you commit, maybe sketch two or three candidate games first and then pick the one that will actually put people in the position rather than just illustrate it to them. I'm imaging something physical or semi-physical: a race across the room where every move is capped at half the remaining gap so nobody can ever reach the wall, or a version with a token sliding along a strip of tape, or a little bit of code if you think that plays better. But I don't want it to be just the halving race, because that only teaches the comforting half. I want the rules arranged so that at some point I have to cheat to end the game, like the only way anyone crosses the line is if I let someone take a full step, or if we all agree the finish "happens" at the limit even though nobody takes a last step, and then that cheat is the thing we dig into afterwards. Add roles if you need them, a token or points scheme if it helps (nothing involving real money), the materials I'd need, a rough minute-by-minute timeline, and a debrief that walks back through what actually happened and lines it up with the premises of the paradox and with where the limit story does and doesn't help.
