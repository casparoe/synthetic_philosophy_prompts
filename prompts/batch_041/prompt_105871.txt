I am applying for a twelve-month research fellowship and need a proposal of about 1,500 words. The topic is the alleged moral asymmetry between lying and merely misleading, and I have a half-formed idea I should like you to turn into something with a determinate question and a workable method.

The familiar framing runs the moral difference through the assertion/implicature boundary: a lie is a false assertion, whereas a misleading utterance is true but conversationally implicates something false, and the received view is that the second is the lesser wrong. I am unconvinced that this boundary is where the moral action lies. My suspicion is that the asymmetry, to the extent that it is real, tracks a difference in who authors the hearer's false belief. In a lie, the speaker hands the hearer the false content directly; in a misleading, the speaker arranges the materials and the hearer draws the false conclusion for herself. If that is right, the operative variable is not falsity or implicature as such but what I have been calling engineered inference: the speaker's exploitation of a reasoning disposition the hearer is entitled to rely on, together with the speaker's advantage in predicting the hearer's inference better than the hearer can.

Please write the proposal as follows. First, state the question so that it has a determinate answer, along these lines: is there a moral difference between lying and misleading that survives once the induced belief, the harm, and the hearer's reasonable expectations are held fixed, and if so does it track authorship of the false belief or the breach of a truthfulness convention? Second, explain why the existing positions leave this open, taking seriously the possibility that the received view conflates a linguistic distinction with a moral one. Third, set out the method: a taxonomy of misleading devices (implicature, presupposition, selective quotation, framing, and the case in which a speaker says something true in order to induce a false belief the hearer will act on), then matched cases that vary authorship and convention independently of falsity and harm, then the competing accounts to be tested — the double-wrong view that lying breaches a norm of truthfulness as well as inducing error, the equivalence view that both are equally wrong when they induce the same belief, and the exploitation view that misleading can be the graver wrong. Give a month-by-month plan and name the result that would refute the authorship hypothesis. Fourth, say honestly what would count as the project failing, and give a realistic scope for twelve months and a single paper.

Do not supply a bibliography or citations; I shall add those myself. Refer to positions and arguments by description rather than by name wherever you can.
