Here's the rough shape, and I'd like you to turn it into something actually runnable. At the very start, before any framing, I offer anyone in the room a small reward — a candy, or a chip they can trade for coffee, nothing that needs real money — if they now sincerely intend to drink a shot of watered-down vinegar at the very end of the session. The reward is paid immediately and unconditionally, and the slip they sign says only that they intend to drink it; it says nothing about actually drinking. The catch I want to build in: before they sign, a student volunteer who knows the class well writes down, for each person, whether they predict that person will actually drink at the end, and earns a little bonus for each correct prediction. So the reliable-predictor assumption from the original isn't me claiming I can read minds — it's a real classmate making real, incentivized bets about what their friends will do. Then we run the rest of the session (some other discussion, doesn't matter what), and at the end anyone who signed is handed the vinegar and gets to decide.
