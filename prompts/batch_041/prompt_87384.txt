I'm putting together the final exam for my graduate seminar on anthropic reasoning and decision theory. I've been discussing with a colleague who thinks the standard presumptuous philosopher thought experiment is too well-known to test whether students really understand the difference between the self-sampling assumption (SSA) and the self-indication assumption (SIA). I want a question that forces them to apply these assumptions to a novel strategic scenario, and also to confront the presumptuous philosopher objection in a new guise. Here's a scenario I've been toying with: Alice and Bob are philosophers in a pre-universe void. They are each offered a choice between two possible universes to create. Universe A contains 10 observers, each with utility 100. Universe B contains 100 observers, each with utility 50. They will each be reincarnated as a random observer in the universe that is created. The rules are: if both choose Universe A, then Universe A is created. If both choose Universe B, then Universe B is created. If they choose differently, then a third universe, Universe C, is created: it contains 1,000 observers, each with utility 1. They cannot communicate, and both know the rules and each other's rationality.

Please write the exam question (a single essay question answerable in 90 minutes), a model answer of about 800 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points, each worth 4 marks (total 20), and a one-line note on how students typically get each wrong. The question should ask students to determine what Alice should choose under SSA and under SIA, to explain how the possibility of Universe C affects the analysis, and to assess whether the presumptuous philosopher argument applies here. In the model answer, please include a short paragraph on what would change your mind about the verdict, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
