I'm a backend engineer, no formal philosophy training, but I've been listening to alot of libertarian stuff on youtube and now I cant tell if they're onto something or just loud. I want to stop arguing about it in the abstract and look at how tax actually works, then ask what that machinery quietly assumes.

Some things that are just normal to me: my employer takes the tax out before I ever see the paycheck, so I never hold the money and then decide to hand it over. Tax avoidance is legal while evasion is a crime, and there are entire firms whose job is to get your bill as close to zero as the law allows, and nobody treats that as shady. If I dispute what I owe, as far as I can tell it's mostly on me to prove the state got it wrong rather than on them to prove I owe it, which is backwards from what happens if someone accuses me of stealing. And if I just refuse to pay, they can take it out of my account or my wages, and in some places lock me up.

What does that whole setup presuppose about who owns my money and about what the state is? Could someone hold the whole thing in place and still say taxation is theft, or does the design already assume the state has a prior claim on my earnings, like a co-owner or a senior creditor or the thing that creates property rights in the first place? I'd also like to know which parts are actually forced by the practice and which are just habits we happen to have, and what would have to change if the theft view were right.
