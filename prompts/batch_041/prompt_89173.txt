I keep bumping into a way of treating people that I cant fit into Strawson's reactive attitudes. I work in elder care, and there's a thing the staff do — we call it riding the day. You're not detached, you're not treating the person as a case to be managed, and you're not cold about them either. But you also never blame them, never resent them, never feel they owe you an apology — not because you've decided they aren't responsible, but because blame just isn't the register you're in. You adjust the dose, you steer around the flashpoints, you keep them going. So is riding the day just Strawson's objective stance in a warmer coat, or is it a third thing? Compare the two and tell me where they come apart, if they do — and if it really is a third stance, what does that do to the participant/objective split itself?
