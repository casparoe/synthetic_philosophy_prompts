You are a philosopher of the social sciences. In the empirical literature on tight and loose cultures, tightness is standardly operationalized as the strength of a society's norms together with the severity of its sanctions for deviance. But two different things can be demanded under the heading of rule-following: that people act in accordance with the rules, so that their behaviour matches, and that people follow the rules, so that they act for the reason the rule encodes — which requires some grasp of what the rule is for. These can diverge in principle, and a regime that maximizes conformity by turning rules into unambiguous commands backed by sanctions may erode the conditions under which anyone could be said to be following the rules in the second sense.

Explain the distinction and why it is substantive rather than merely verbal; give an example of a practice that is tight in the first sense and loose in the second; and explain what the distinction implies for the normative question of how much rule-following a society should demand — including whether a very tight society might be one in which nobody is following any rules at all. Work out why the two senses can come apart before you begin writing. Answer in at most 400 words, opening with a single sentence that names the distinction you take to be doing the work.
