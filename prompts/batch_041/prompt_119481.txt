I'm putting together a short set of jokes about Ian Hacking for something I'm working on, and I want one that's actually about the looping effect instead of just "lol pyschiatry makes it up as it goes". The part I want to hit is the mechanism: a classification gets applied to a bunch of people, those people find out about it and start living inside the description, or pointedly living against it, so the population shifts; then the next edition of the manual re-writes the criteria, which shifts the population again. And the bit that makes this interesting rather than cynical is that it doesn't make the condition fake -- it makes it a kind of kind that a quark can't be, since quarks don't read the textbook and adjust. There's also the thing about these conditions needing some kind of niche in the culture before they catch on, which I guess is why you get a rash of one diagnosis in one decade and a different one the next.

Heres a hook if it's useful, but throw it out if you'd rather invent your own: for a sketch I came up with a disorder called "Echo Syndrome", and one of the official criteria is that the patient has read the criteria and felt personally addressed by them. So reading the entry is the symptom and the diagnostic test at the same time, and the more the label gets passed around the more people qualify, so prevalence climbs for reasons that have nothing to do with anything getting worse. The other criterion I gave it is that the symptoms have to be inexplicable by any currently recognized condition, which I think is the funniest part, because the second the manual recognizes Echo Syndrome it stops qualifying and every patient it just created falls back out of it. So the condition is created by being named and spread by being cured, or something like that.

Write the joke. Two-liner, dialogue, fake dictionary entry, whatever you like. The punchline has to turn on the loop itself, so that someone who knows the difference between the kinds you can loop and the kinds you can't will laugh, and someone who's never heard of Hacking can still follow it. Please don't do the "the manual keeps changing so who even knows" thing -- that's the joke I'm trying to get away from.
