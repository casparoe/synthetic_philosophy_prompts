My friend and I had a stupidly long arguement last night about the bed trick in Measure for Measure, and I want an actual reading of the play out of it, not a plot summary. She's a law student so she came at it through consent doctrine: Angelo dangles Claudio's execution over Isabella's head, she says no, the disguised Duke talks her into pretending to say yes, Mariana takes her place in the dark, Angelo sleeps with a woman he thinks is somebody else, and by the curtain he's married to Mariana, Claudio's reprieved, and the Duke is proposing to Isabella, who says absolutely nothing. Her line was that Mariana is the only person in the play who genuinely consented and she got exactly what she wanted, so basicly the play is fine with the whole business. If a lie about who your partner is counts as the kind of lie that voids consent, then that isn't a seduction, it's a violation, of the very man who's trying to extort sex out of a novice who's already refused him twice.
