I want to put a position about acausal threats and surrogate goals under pressure, and I would like you to map its exposure.

Here is the setup. An agent faces a threat that cannot reach it by any causal route: nothing sent to it will arrive in time, no contract can bind the threatener, and there is no future interaction to make retaliation rational. What the threatener has instead is structural. It runs the same decision procedure, or solves the same abstract problem, so its action is correlated with the agent's own choice even though nothing passes between them — the threatener moves against the agent, say, if and only if it predicts the agent will refuse to pay, with the prediction read off the agent's own algorithm. A threat of this kind has force only if acausal influence is real: only if an agent can affect what another does by being correlated with it rather than causally connected to it.

Granting that much, the position I am inclined to hold is this. Such threats are genuine and should be taken seriously; and the correct defense for an agent exposed to them is to genuinely adopt a surrogate goal — a stand-in objective it does not intrinsically care about, chosen so that it ceases to be a profitable target. I am aware of the rival view that all of this is either empty (no correlation is ever tight enough for the threat to bite) or derivative (it is merely precommitment reasoning with new vocabulary).

What would count against my position? I want the considerations that would tell against it, not merely the rival's complaints: which intuitions, which arguments, and which formal results would be decisive and which merely awkward; what a committed defender would have to concede as counting against them; and whether the insistence that the surrogate goal be genuinely adopted leaves the view exposed at all or insulates it from every counterexample. Give the response as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, ordered from the consideration that most threatens the position to the one that least does.
