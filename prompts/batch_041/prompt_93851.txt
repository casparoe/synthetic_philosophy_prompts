I write about science and ideas for a living, and I've been circling a long piece on memory and the timing of experience. Memento keeps pulling me back in, but I can't get the reading to sit still, so I'd like you to help me think it through properly — a real reading of the film against the philosophy, not a plot recap.

The philosophy first, in my own fumbling words so we're on the same page. There's an old lab trick — I think psychologists call it color phi — where you flash a dot on the left, then a dot on the right a fraction of a second later, different color, and people swear they watched one dot travel across and change color roughly halfway. Except the halfway color was never on the screen; it could only be known once the second dot appeared, and yet the change gets reported as happening before that. Dennett liked to frame what the brain might be up to as a fork. One story, Orwellian: you really did see a static red dot and then a static green dot, and the record got rewritten afterwards, Ministry of Truth style, so the memory you keep is a doctored one. The other, Stalinesque: the brain holds everything back, edits first, then screens it for you, so the moving dot is what you genuinely experienced and no post hoc revision ever happened. His punchline, if I've got him right, is that your report can't tell the two apart, and once you drop the idea that conscious content gets published at some single moment, the fork stops being a real fork.

Memento feels like it runs that fork at the scale of a life instead of a tenth of a second, and I want to know whether the analogy holds or whether I'm just seduced by it. Which horn is Leonard in — Orwellian, living it and then having the record destroyed and rebuilt, or Stalinesque, with the edit happening upstream so what he gets is already the finished cut — or does the film show that the question is malformed for a whole life the way Dennett says its malformed for a flash of light? The form seems to be doing something: the color scenes run backwards and the black-and-white scenes run forwards, so as a viewer I'm handed the corrected version before I've seen the events, while Leonard gets the events and loses them. Does that put us in the editing room and him in the world, and does anything actually differ between our two positions — do I learn something he can't, or only learn it in a different order?

Two things I want you to take seriously as objections to my own framing. First, the film seems to hand us a record nobody could have: Teddy's account of the past arrives as though it were the unedited archive, which is exactly the privileged standpoint Dennett says doesn't exist for consciousness. Is that a cop-out, or is the film using it to show what the stakes would be if there were a fact of the matter? Second, Leonard's last move looks deliberate — he manufactures a fact and then chooses to forget — and I suspect voluntariness breaks the parallel with sub-personal perceptual editing. The Sammy Jankis business nags at me too: an insurance man running a behavioural test to find out whether a mind is really doing what it claims is basically the phi experiment with a paycheck, and in both cases all we ever have is the report.

So give me the reading. Where does the film dramatize the fork, where does it complicate it, where does it quietly trade on sliding from milliseconds of perceptual editing to years of personal-level confabulation, and what does it say that the argument alone can't? Which scenes should I actually point readers to? I'm not a philosopher and I may have mangled Dennett, so correct me where I have. No citations or reading list needed — and don't worry about quoting the script, I'll pull the lines myself.
