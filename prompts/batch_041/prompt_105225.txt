I run policy for a mid-size marketplace app — people post offers to do things for each other, we take a cut, and my team has a 48-hour turnaround on any new listing category, because trust-and-safety won't let anything go live without our written sign-off. We've had a run of proposals that all feel like the same problem in different clothes: someone wants to sell their place in a line for a limited-edition sneaker drop; a woman wants to list herself as a paid plus-one and fake relative for weddings and funerals (she says she's been doing it informally for years and just wants to go legit); a landlord wants to let tenants bid to jump the waitlist for a rent-controlled unit in his building; a guy wants to auction a tattoo ad slot on his forehead; and someone keeps re-posting a listing offering to pay a stranger for "a kidney, travelling expenses negotiable," which we keep pulling but never have a principled reason for pulling beyond it being illegal where the seller says he lives. Our general counsel is fine with anything not statutorally banned, which isn't a standard, and last month the board adopted a one-line principle — any transaction conveying a claim on a person's body, identity, or standing is presumptively off the table, anything transferring a fungible good or a service anyone could have performed is presumptively fine — which is lovely as a slogan and useless at 4pm on a Thursday.

Can you turn this into an actual decision procedure my three-person team can run? Nobody here has a philosophy degree, we can't afford to deliberate for a week, and we have to justify every call in writing to legal and to the trust-and-safety lead who signs off.
