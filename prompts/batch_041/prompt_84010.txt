I host a podcast on social choice, and next week's guest is a game theorist who argues that the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem has been badly overread. Her position is that the theorem only rules out mechanisms under which a voter can gain by misreporting whatever the other voters do, a bar she thinks no real institution should be asked to clear, and that the defensible requirement is weaker: no voter should gain by a misreport whose payoff she can work out without modelling the others' strategies. On that reading, the theorem leaves ordinary plurality and runoff voting untouched. Draft eight to ten interview questions that force her to make the weakened requirement precise rather than restate the headline. Include at least one question on what it means, operationally, for a manipulation's payoff to be visible without modelling the others; one on whether the weaker requirement survives voters learning from experience, since a manipulation opaque this season may be transparent the next; one on whether restricting the domain of admissible preferences, say to single-peaked ones, is a more honest route to her conclusion, and why she prefers her route; and one on what the theorem still tells us about real elections once her weakening is granted. For each question, add the follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. The questions should not ask her to cite or summarise the literature; she should be able to answer from the position as described.
