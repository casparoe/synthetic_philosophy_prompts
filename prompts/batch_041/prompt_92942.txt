Context for the brainstorm. I am a software engineer, no formal philosophy training, and next month I have forty-five minutes plus twenty minutes of questions in front of our internal responsible-AI review group and three outside guests. The system in question is unremarkable by the standards of the field: a large language model with tool access, deployed internally, able to read production data and write to a few of our systems. It drafts emails, writes SQL against our analytics warehouse, files tickets, and updates a small set of configuration records.

My thesis is that most of the safety benefit we can realistically get this year comes from ordinary software engineering rather than from any new insight into alignment, and I want to argue for five specific mechanisms: (1) a bounded action space, where every tool the agent can call has a typed schema and a declared reversibility class of reversible, compensable, or irreversible; (2) an irreversible-action budget, a small shared daily pool of such actions that the agent draws from and that, once exhausted, forces a handoff to a human rather than a retry; (3) provenance-typed inputs, where everything the agent reads is tagged as user-authored, tool-authored, or third-party-authored, and any instruction arriving inside third-party content is never executed at the agent's own privilege level but only surfaced to a human; (4) a shadow reviewer, a second model that is deliberately weaker and not distilled from the main one, which must concur before an irreversible action commits, with disagreements blocked and logged; and (5) a plain-language audit log, one line per action, written for a non-engineer, containing the model's stated reason and the input that triggered it. My claim is that none of this requires progress on alignment as a research problem; it is the same discipline we already apply to payment systems and database migrations.

The audience is not uniform. Two of them do interpretability work and regard anything you cannot inspect inside the weights as a placebo.
