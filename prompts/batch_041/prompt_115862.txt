Over the last year I've gotten sucked into longtermism, and the objection that keeps me up at night is the fanaticism one — the idea that these astronomical future values make everything else look trivial, Pascal's-mugging style. A friend tells me the simulation argument is the antidote: if there's a serious chance we're already simulated, then the giant futures we're supposed to optimize for either aren't ours to steer or are themselves just part of the sim, so you multiply the astronomical payoff by some small factor and the fanaticism deflates. Another friend says it cuts the other way — that if we're simulated, the simulators are the audience that actually matters and we should be trying to influence them somehow, which would make the stakes bigger, not smaller. I can't tell if either of them is right.

I've got maybe three years of spare evenings to throw at one question in this area and I want to spend them where it counts. So what are the real open questions at the intersetion of the simulation argument and far-future fanaticism, and which ones are actually worth someone's time? I care more about the reasoning than the list: which questions are upstream of the others, which are tractable for one person doing philosophy and decision theory rather than empirical work, and which sound deep but wouldn't change what anyone does if they got settled. And tell me where your own reasoning is most likely to be off, and what would change your mind about the ordering.
