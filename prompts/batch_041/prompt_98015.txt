I'm drafting a position paper on sex-work law and I'd like a consistency audit before I commit to it. Here is where I've landed, stated as baldly as I can.

(1) As a matter of principle, the criminal law has no business punishing consensual sexual conduct between adults. Criminal liability should be reserved for force, fraud, coercion, or demonstrable harm to non-consenting third parties.

(2) On policy, I favour the sex-purchase-ban approach: selling sexual services is lawful, but buying them is a criminal offence.

(3) Sex work is work. Sellers should be covered by ordinary labour law — occupational health and safety, contract and wage protections wherever an employment relation exists — and should have an unambiguous right to organise collectively.

(4) Third parties who organise, manage, or take a cut from other people's sex work — brothel operators, agencies, drivers — should remain criminally liable.

(5) In this area I am consequences-first: a regime should be judged by how it affects the people who actually sell sex, above all the most marginalised among them, and not by the expressive or symbolic message it sends about commodified sex.

(6) I want aggressive anti-trafficking enforcement, but it should fall on coercers and on buyers of coerced sex, not on sellers. No one should be prosecuted or deported merely for having sold sex, migrants included.

I'm sceptical of the German and Dutch licensed systems; my impression is that they have grown a large commercial sector without clear gains in safety or in reducing trafficking, which is why I'm not drawn to full legalisation.

Please go through these and tell me which commitments are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and — for the real ones — what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and be specific about what would have to give. Please also say how confident you are in each of your verdicts, and single out the one point you're least sure about.
