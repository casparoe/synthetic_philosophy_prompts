ok so I want a mock employee handbook section from a company that's gone full eliminative materialist. Someone in HR read up on it and now the whole workplace has to stop using belief/desire/intention talk, because on this view those things are just posits of a bad folk theory and they dont exist. Write it as numbered policy clauses, deadpan, around 700 words, sounding like an actual compliance document, no winking at the reader.

The humor has to come from getting the position right, so a few things the memo should be careful about: this is NOT behaviorism -- internal states are fine, it's the propositional attitudes specifically that are being eliminated, so employees can still report a toothache or a visual experience but not a belief; there should be a clause covering "I didn't mean to do it," which the company can no longer process; a grievance procedure for staff who keep insisting they do have beliefs; a note that "we value your preferences" was retired too, since preferences were part of the same folk package; and somewhere a paragraph on why the memo is asking you to accept a claim, in the flattest corporate tone available, given that the memo is in the business of changing what you think.

It should be funny to someone who knows the debate -- intentional stance, theory-theory, the difference between eliminating a posit and reducing it, the self-refutation complaint -- and still readable to someone who's never heard of any of it. No citations, no names, just the document.
