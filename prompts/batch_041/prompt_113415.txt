Consider the statue and the clay. Two influential responses to the puzzle are the constitution view, according to which a statue is constituted by a lump of clay but is not identical with it, and the sortal-relative identity view, according to which statements about the same lump and statements about the same statue deploy different identity relations rather than competing answers to one and the same relation. Compare these two responses. Then apply them to the following case. A sculptor makes a statue from a single lump of clay. Over many years a conservator replaces the statue’s clay molecule by molecule with a synthetic material that is perceptually indistinguishable from the original, storing the removed clay. When the replacement is complete, the conservator re-moulds the stored clay into a statue of the same shape as the original. There are now two candidates for the original statue: the synthetic object and the re-moulded clay object. Which view gives the better account of how many statues there are, which candidate, if either, is the original, and whether the original lump survives? Where do the two views agree, and where do they genuinely diverge? Which view is more faithful to ordinary counting practices, and which is more metaphysically economical? In answering, explain how each view handles the modal difference between the statue and the lump, for example that the lump but not the statue can survive being squashed, and say whether either view is committed to coincident objects and whether that commitment is a cost or a benefit. Finally, state which view you find stronger and identify one consideration that would lead you to abandon it. Write your answer in British English.
