I am a PhD student in cognitive science working on computational theories of self-representation, and I am moderating an upcoming session in our departmental works-in-progress series with a philosopher of mind who defends a strong reading of the Gödelian analogy in Gödel, Escher, Bach. Her position is not the familiar Lucas-Penrose claim that Gödel's theorems refute mechanism. It is narrower and more constructive: she argues that any system that counts as self-aware must contain a self-representational map with a fixed point structurally analogous to the diagonal lemma, and that the resulting self-model is necessarily incomplete in a way that explains both the peculiar authority and the peculiar fragility of first-person thought. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press on the actual content of this position rather than letting her restate it. I want at least one question each on the distinction between the diagonal lemma and the fixed-point construction she invokes, on whether her view inherits the soundness-versus-consistency problems that plague the Lucas-Penrose argument, on whether the fixed point must be syntactic or can be realized in a dynamical or connectionist architecture, on the threat of trivial fixed points, and on whether the incompleteness she attributes to the self-model is constitutive or merely contingent. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give; for example, if she says the fixed point is not syntactic, ask whether the diagonal lemma still does any explanatory work, and if she says the incompleteness is benign, ask whether the same move would make the original anti-mechanist argument benign as well. Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or attributions, and avoid generic opening questions. The questions should be answerable from the position itself, not from external sources.
