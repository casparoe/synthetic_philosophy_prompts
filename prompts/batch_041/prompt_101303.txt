ok so I got money-pumped for real last month and now I can't stop thinking about it. I'd paid a fee to move my flight to the earlier one, then paid again to move it back to my original flight, so I ended up exactly where I started minus $150, and the depressing part is I'd make each of those swaps again tomorrow. It's got me wondering if my views about this kind of thing actually hang together. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Your past self's preferences have no authority over you now. Each time-slice gets to decide for itself what it wants.
2. Rationality is entirely a matter of what you do at a time, given what you want then. There's no extra requirement that your preferences stay stable over time.
3. Relatedly, I'm a reductionist about personal identity -- there's no deep fact about which future person is "me" that could ground any special obligation to that person.
4. But I definately think getting money-pumped is a real defect. Someone who'll pay to swap A for B, then B for C, then C back to A has done something wrong, even if every individual swap looked fine when they made it.
5. And I think intransitive preferences at a single moment are irrational -- you need a cycle-free ranking before you even have a coherent thing to maximize.

Which of these are actually pulling against each other, and how bad is it? I have a hunch at least one of them only looks like a conflict. Rank the real tensions by severity, tell me the cheapest one to give up, and be honest about what I'd lose by giving it up.
