I have spent a decade covering psychology and the sciences of the mind — the replication crisis in particular — and the beat has left me fluent in a vocabulary I never expected to acquire: construct validity, latent variables, reflective versus formative measurement, the difference between an indicator and the thing it is meant to indicate, discriminant validity, and the twin measurement traps in which one word quietly covers several distinct things or several words quietly cover one thing. I would like you to use that vocabulary as the target of a translation exercise from a field I read with real interest and real incomprehension.

The debate I have in mind is the one in the philosophy of well-being over what, if anything, all pleasures have in common. Hedonism holds that pleasure is the one thing good in itself, and its critics press what is sometimes called the heterogeneity problem: the pleasure of a warm bath, the pleasure of hearing a phrase resolve, the pleasure of a friend's good news, the pleasure of winning an argument, and the allegedly pleasant quality of certain pains and certain cruelties appear to share no single felt quality and no common phenomenological core. The hedonist has a stock of replies — that pleasure is a determinable with many determinates; that the common element is a way of being directed at something rather than a raw sensation; that some pleasures are qualitatively higher and rank above the rest — and the critics have replies to the replies. I can follow each move in isolation and still never quite see what is at stake.

Restate that debate in the vocabulary I actually have, as faithfully as you can, one position at a time. Then give me the residue report. Which of the hedonist's moves and which of the critic's moves survive the translation intact — where, say, the claim that pleasures share a common nature becomes a claim about a single latent factor, or about whether the category is reflective or formative? Which survive only in a distorted form, and what precisely gets bent in the process? And which have no counterpart at all in how I think about measurement — I suspect the felt quality itself, the very thing hedonism is about, and possibly the normative claim that this quality is what makes a life go well, though I want your judgment rather than mine.

I am most interested in whether the residue is a defect of my vocabulary or a confession about theirs. If the psychometric apparatus can settle whether pleasures behave like one construct but cannot settle whether they are one, is that because my framework is descriptively thin, or because the philosophers' question was never as unitary as their word pleasure suggests? Do not smuggle in the assumptions of the measurement framework — that constructs are only as real as their indicators, that a failure of unidimensionality is a failure of the thing rather than of the test — and then present the result as translation. Where the two frameworks pull apart, I want to see the seam.
