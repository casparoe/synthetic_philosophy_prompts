I run a monthly discussion group at a community center, about 14 people, mixed ages, nobody with a history degree, and we have 75 minutes. I've been reading about how nations are supposed to be ancient but a lot of national traditions are actually pretty recent inventions, and how a nation is a community you imagine yourself into even though you'll never meet most of its members. I want an activity that makes the group feel this in their bones, not just hear me say it. Something where they have to build a national tradition from scratch—maybe they're a committee in a made-up country that just got independence—and then at the end they see that they've done in 40 minutes what real states did over a century, and that the feeling of ancientness is manufactured. But I don't want it to be just a cynical gotcha. I want them to also feel the positive side: that imagining the community is what makes it real, that it's not fake just because it's invented. And I want them to feel the difference between a tradition being invented and a community being imagined—those seem like two different claims to me and I don't want to mush them together. You're an expert in nationalism studies and in designing experiential learning. Think carefully and start by brainstorming a few different activity structures, then pick the one that works best and give me the full plan: setting, roles or teams, materials, rules, a timeline for 75 minutes, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the ideas. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle that. Be honest about which part of the idea the activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to explain myself. I'm definately not an expert, so if this is a bad idea—like if the activity would just make people cynical or if it can't actually produce the feeling I want—say so straight instead of humoring me.
