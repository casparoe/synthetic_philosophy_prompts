My friend has a philosophy PhD and is now insufferable about it. Over the years I've collected her verdicts on borderline cases. Baldness: no hair, bald; ten hairs, bald; a hundred thousand hairs, not bald. Heap: two grains, not a heap; five hundred grains, a heap. On the ones in the middle — five thousand hairs, fifty grains — she refuses both labels: "I can't say it's bald and I can't say it isn't," "you won't get a yes or no out of me there." She will say a fifty-grain pile is "more of a heap" than a forty-grain one. And when I asked whether there's some exact number of hairs where baldness stops, she said "I don't think you'll find one" and changed the subject. I genuinely can't tell whether she thinks there's an answer we can't reach or no answer at all.

She's now helping me build a sorting machine that has to drop every pile into either a "heap" bin or a "not heap" bin — so it needs one exact cut-off, and she has to choose it. Predict what she'll say and why. Lay out the rules that fit everything she's told me so far (at minimum I'd expect epistemicism, supervaluationism, contextualism, and some degrees-of-truth view — tell me if there's a fifth I'm missing), what each predicts about the machine, which one she's most likely actually running and why, and what single further case or question would finally seperate them.

Sections: "The rules on the table" (a short list, one line each), "What each predicts about the cut-off" (a few lines each), "My money's on" (one paragraph), "The question that settles it" (short).
