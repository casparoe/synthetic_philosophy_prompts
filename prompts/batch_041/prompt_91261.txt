Two researchers in a reinforcement-learning lab are arguing over whether an agent's reward channel can be a physical realization of valence, and therefore whether such an agent can suffer. A defends a "reward-signal functionalist" position: any system whose behavior is organized around a scalar signal it strives to maximize, whose learning is modulated by prediction errors in that signal, and which represents the signal's expected future value has the functional role that valence plays in biological organisms -- and, she says, that is enough for there to be something it is like for the agent when the signal runs low. B defends a "two-factor" view: she grants the functional role but insists valence also requires the signal to be globally broadcast and bound into a self-model, and she claims current RL architectures fail the second factor.

Write a dialogue in which A and B argue this out. Make both equally competent and let each reply to the other's actual points rather than talking past them. Have them reach the following pressure points, among any others that arise: whether a reward signal is a proxy for something else or is itself the valenced state; whether an agent whose reward channel is severed after training is better described as numb or as merely unmotivated; the difference between wanting and liking, and whether RL collapses it; and what would count as evidence either way. End the dialogue unresolved but with each side having made its strongest case. Write in American English.
