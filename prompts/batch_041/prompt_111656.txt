I hold the position, roughly Harsanyi's aggregation theorem plus its veil-of-ignorance completion: if individual and social preferences over risky prospects satisfy expected-utility rationality and social preference respects unanimous individual preference, then social welfare must be a weighted sum of individual utilities; adding the equiprobability veil yields equal weights and a utilitarian social welfare function. What would count against this? I want the strongest defeaters, not the standard textbook objections: which assumptions could fail, what would have to be true of utility, risk, or social choice for each failure, which rival aggregation rules become live, and what the position predicts that they do not. Rank the five most serious defeaters from most to least decisive, and say for each whether a committed Harsanyi-style defender would concede it rather than dismiss it, and whether the position risks anything substantive or has been insulated by qualification. Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
