I'm a fourth-year PhD student (econ-ish, though I work on science and tech policy) and I've somehow ended up running next month's session of our interdisciplinary reading group — mostly first-years, mixed backgrounds, a few from outside the social sciences entirely. I got handed Goodhart's law and I really dont want to do the standard version: the usual when-a-measure-becomes-a-target line plus the cobra bounty story. Too many of them have heard it, and the ones who haven't will walk out thinking they've understood something.

What I actually want them to leave with is that the name is a junk drawer for at least four different mechanisms: (1) the proxy is just noisy, so when you select on it you're also selecting on the noise and the correlation sags; (2) the proxy-goal relationship simply isn't the same out in the tail you're now pushing into; (3) it's the regulator's own intervention that collapses the link, not noise and not cheating; (4) agents who don't share your goal actively game it. These have different signatures and completely different fixes, and I'd bet half the confusions in the literature are people arguing about (4) when the case is really (1) or (3). So plan me the session. What has to come before what, so the four-way split feels like something that falls out of the problem rather than a taxonomy to memorise? What single running case should carry the whole thing — ideally one where you genuinely can't tell which mechanism is operating until you look at the counterfactual — and what does that case need to have, and not have? Where in the sequence do I head off the misconceptions I'm expecting ("this just means people cheat" and "so metrics are useless"), and is there a third I'm not seeing? And give me one closing question that would actually tell me whether they can diagnose a fresh case rather than just recite the four.

Then be straight with me about where the plan is likely to fail: what would make you reorder it or drop my four-way split for a different cut, which of my assumptions about the audience would sink the session if it's wrong, and whether some case could fit two mechanisms at once and make the neat split look silly.
