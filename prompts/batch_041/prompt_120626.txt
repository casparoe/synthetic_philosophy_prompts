OK so I've got a WIP talk in two weeks and I need to prep for the Q&A and I'm already dreading it. Can you help.

Heres the thesis I'm defending: the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — a backrub, the click of a proof finally going through, scratching a mosquito bite, what do these even have in common — is not the fatal objection everyone treats it as. My line is that 'pleasure' is a role kind rather than a natural kind. What unites pleasures isn't a shared feeling tone, it's the job they do: they're the states we intrinsically want to be in, are disposed to repeat, and that present themselves as good to feel. And that role is multiply realized — sometimes by a distinctive feeling, sometimes by the attitude of taking pleasure in something, sometimes by whatever the brain's 'liking' machinery turns out to be. So hedonism about well-being should be a thesis about the role, not about one shared quality. I call it role hedonism and I get some mileage out of the jade case (jadeite and nephrite are both jade and share basically no composition) and out of the wanting/liking neuroscience — dopamine doing wanting, opioids doing liking.

Now the audience, this is the part that matters. Small workshop, maybe 15 people, mostly empirically-minded phil of mind, but these are the ones I'm worried about: two people who hold a distinctive-feeling-tone view of pleasure and think I'm just changing the subject; a hedonic neuroscientist who studies liking in rodents and thinks the whole philosophical notion is a mess; two attitudinal hedonists who think the attitude does all the unifying work and that my role kind is circular; one Mill person who will want to know what happens to higher and lower pleasures on my view; and a Kantian-ish person who thinks pleasure isn't the only good and will try to make the whole thing look parochial.
