ok so heres my epistemic profile, and I'd like a consistency check before I go around acting on it:

1. The outside view is the default. Base rates, reference classes, the planning fallacy — if I catch myself reasoning from the particulars of my own case, I treat that as a warning sign rather than as evidence.
2. Epistemic modesty is a virtue. Concretely: when I learn that people in my position are systematically biased about X, the right response is to lower my credence in X.
3. But there's an opposite failure mode that I think is just as bad, where someone invokes the base rate as a way of never updating on anything they actually know. I've watched that eat people's lives.
4. Calibration is the whole ballgame. Modesty isn't a feeling or a personality trait, it's a property of a credence, and someone well calibrated at 90% is being immodest and is right to be.
5. I do think I'm well above average at a few things, and I don't think that's the above-average effect, because the relevant reference class is people who've put the hours in, not people in general.
6. And none of this means ignoring my own evidence — if I have information that nobody in the reference class had, I should obviously use it. The outside view is just an inside view that somebody else had first and then froze in place.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and which one should I give up? Rank the tensions by severity rather than telling me they're all equally bad, say what the cheapest fix is for each, and then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. Also: tell me what would change your mind about that ranking — which consideration, if it turned out the other way, would make you name a different item as the one to drop.
