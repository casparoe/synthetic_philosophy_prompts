I want a pantoum about Boltzmann brains — the worry that if the universe lasts long enough to reach thermal equilibrium, a lone brain briefly assembling itself out of the noise, complete with false memories of a childhood and of having done cosmology, is a much cheaper fluctuation than a whole ordered galaxy with someone in it, and so should vastly outnumber the likes of us. The form has to do the work: in a pantoum the second and fourth lines of each stanza come back as the first and third of the next, and the final stanza reuses the opening lines, so the poem ends where it began — I want that loop to land as a fluctuation running down into equilibrium rather than a cosy return, and the closing echo of stanza one to be the point where you genuinely can't tell whether the cosmos came first or the brain did. Keep the technical bits visible and correct: the real argument is about how you count observers in an infinite universe, which nobody has a principled way of doing, so the swamping is softer than it sounds; and the sting is reflexive, because if the numbers really do favour the brain then the reasoning that produced the numbers was probably a fluctuation too, so the argument cuts off the branch its sitting on. 4 or 5 quatrains, in the voice of someone who has just given a talk on this and is now alone with it. Don't resolve it — the loop is the point.
