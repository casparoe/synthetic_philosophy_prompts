I keep flip-flopping on whether the features people find inside trained networks are really representations or just handy summaries for us. I lean realist, but only on a specific line: a state represents some property P only if there's a downstream consumer whose behaviour is counterfactually sensitive to P under intervention, and the network could be wrong about P -- misrepresentation has to be possible, not just decodability. So it's a three-place thing: state, content, consumer. What would count against this? Give me the observations and arguments that should actually move me, ranked, including stuff that's arguably already in hand -- probes that recover the same structure from randomly initialized nets, units that fire for unrelated things, features that decode cleanly but nothing downstream reads, the same property recoverable from several incompatible directions -- and tell me which of these a realist can just absorb by tightening the criterion. I'm most worried about the obvious failure mode: that I've qualified the view until nothing could count against it. If so, say so, and say whether that's fatal or just the price of any decent account of representation. I'd be most moved by a case where every box on my checklist is ticked and it still seems wrong to say the net represents anything, so try to build one. And tell me how confident you are in each bit, and which bit your least sure about.
