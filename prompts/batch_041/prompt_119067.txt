For a while I've thought the heterogeneity problem for hedonism is the most underrated problem in the well-being literature: the taste of honey, the warmth of a bath, the relief of a diagnosis that comes back clean, and the satisfaction of finally getting a proof to work seem to share no common phenomenal feature, so it is unclear what hedonism could even be claiming when it says well-being consists in pleasure. I want a purpose-built case to serve as the load-bearing example in a long post on whether hedonism survives this. Build me a thought experiment to the following specifications.

- One case, one ordinary person, ordinary circumstances. No brains in vats, no aliens, no fictional neurochemistry, no new kinds of experience stipulated into existence. If the case can only be described in technical vocabulary, it is not what I want.
- The case must contain two episodes that our ordinary concept of pleasure sorts together but that come apart under the two standard answers to the heterogeneity problem. Call them the felt-quality answer -- an experience is a pleasure when it has a positive hedonic quality that is part of what the experience is like -- and the attitude answer -- an experience is a pleasure when the person likes or welcomes it in the relevant way, whatever it is like.
- I want the flip in the harder direction: an episode that the attitude answer counts as a pleasure and the felt-quality answer does not. Liking has to outrun feeling.
- The flip has to fall out of the theories' own criteria rather than being built in by stipulation. If the case works only because you have asserted that an experience feels like nothing, that is cheating, and it is precisely the move I am trying to avoid.
- Barred furniture: masochism, addiction, brain stimulation, and any case whose punch comes from the person being wrong about the facts. Those are the rehearsed cases, and I already know how they go.
- One paragraph of setup, at most.

Then do the work. Walk through what each answer says about each episode and why. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens if you remove it -- in particular, whether the case collapses into one where both answers agree. Give me the strongest version of the attack available to whichever answer comes out worse -- a steelman, not a strawman -- and say where it lands. Finally, say whether the case bears on hedonism's definitional claim, that well-being consists in pleasure, or on its aggregative claim, that pleasures can be summed and compared, and which of those two is in worse shape.

Do not hand me a menu. Take a position: on your construction, is the heterogeneity problem fatal to hedonism, survivable by going pluralist about pleasure, or something in between, and why? If you think no case can meet these specifications without smuggling in the verdict, name the specification that is the culprit and show it -- I would rather learn that than get a fudged example. Flag anything in the case that leans on a contested reading of either answer.
