So I've somehow ended up on the advisory board of a small foundation that hands out about $1.5M a year, split between philosophy of wellbeing and the more empirical side of things (pain scales, quality-of-life instruments, animal welfare metrics). They've asked me which conceptual questions they should be pushing on over the next three years, and I'd like to give them an actual agenda instead of a list of topics. This is where I get stuck.

Hedonism as a theory of wellbeing seems to need 'pleasure' to be one thing, or at least one kind of thing — otherwise you can't compare a pleasure against a pain, weigh them, aggregate across people, or even say clearly what the theory is claiming. But look at what actually gets called pleasure: the taste of a peach, an ache going away, the punchline of a joke, being praised, a cigarette, the specific relief of finally sitting down. Hard to say what they have in common. So is 'what do pleasures have in common' even the right thing to fund, or is the money better spent somewhere slightly upstream or downstream of it?

A friend in my reading group floated an idea I like: treat 'pleasure' like 'jade'. One word, and it turned out there were two minerals under it. She says the interesting question isn't which one is the real pleasure, it's what the word was doing for us before we looked. Someone else said it's a determinable/determinate situation — 'pleasure' is like 'red', which is real without being any particular shade, and the various pleasures are its determinates. A third person said fine, but hedonism doesn't need a natural kind, it just needs a measurable one, and the empirical people can tell us whether there's a common currency — some single quantity that scales with how good an experience feels.

So: what are the open questions in this area, and which ones should a foundation with three years and maybe four or five researchers actually prioritize? I want the ordering defended. For each: why is it upstream of the others, can philosophers make real progress on it without waiting on the neuroscientists, is it neglected because it's unglamorous, and would its answer actually change anything downstream — what the measurement people do, what a hedonist is committed to, how a funder splits the budget? Tell me which ones sound deep but are basically idle. And tell me what would move an item up or down the list: a formal result, a good argument that some pleasures are incommensurable, a failure to find any common currency, whatever. If you think the framing is wrong and the heterogeneity problem is a pseudo-problem, definately say so and tell me what you'd fund instead — but then actually defend it, don't just wave at dissolution.
