Write me a Kickstarter page, around 600 words, for a startup that wants to build a giant computer and run an astronomical number of happy simulated people — and whose whole pitch is an anthropic argument: you're probably already one of theirs, so pledging is really investing in yourself. Deadpan, and give me the full pitch-page furniture: headline, funding tiers, stretch goals, a risks-and-challenges section, an FAQ. All the jokes should come from the anthropics being *right*, not from the founders being dumb — so dont let them run together the assumption that you're a random observer drawn from all the observers there are with the assumption that your own existence is evidence that more observers exist (they should lean on one, quietly need the other, and have an FAQ answer that fudges the difference). Somewhere in there I want a stretch goal that's only justified under one of the two assumptions and not the other, and a pledge tier that gives away what the whole thing is really trading on — the one where a tiny gain to each of an enormous number of people beats a big gain to one person. Someone who's chewed on the doomsday argument should laugh; someone who hasnt should still follow it.

Then tack a table on at the end: one column for the specific anthropic move the page makes, one for which assumption it actually needs (self-sampling / self-indication / neither), and one for the objection that bites.
