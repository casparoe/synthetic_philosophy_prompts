Ok so I've been arguing with a friend about process-based vs outcome-based supervision for training reasoners. The standard line is: if you only reward final answers, the model can land the right answer via garbage steps and that blows up out-of-distribution, so you should reward each step instead. My friend thinks that's mostly cope and that outcome-only plus a good verifier gets you nearly all the way there.

Here's the specific thing I want a number on. For tasks where the final answer is cheaply checkable but the intermediate steps aren't, does step-level supervision (a learned process reward model) actually buy a real, non-artifact OOD generalization advantage over outcome-only supervision? Give me your credence, as a number — not "it depends."

Then decompose it. I want the sub-questions the number hinges on, each with a rough probability and one line of justification, e.g.: the chance that a model's written steps are actually causally upstream of its answer rather than a post-hoc rationalization of it; the chance a process reward model can be trained to grade whether a step is *correct* without just grading what *looks* like a good step to the human labelers; the chance that "step is correct" even tracks "step is load-bearing" for the final answer (a step can be true and doing no work); the chance that any gain is just the extra supervision signal rather than the step granularity itself; plus whatever else you think belongs. Combine them, give me the number, and then tell me which single term it's most sensitive to and what evidence would move it most.

Don't hedge on me. I want you to definately land on a number and defend it, not lay out the considerations and hand the decision back to me.
