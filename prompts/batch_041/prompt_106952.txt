I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy of mind (cogsci-adjacent, mostly), and I've somehow ended up defending a version of functionalism I can't shake. Roughly: mental states are individuated by their causal role; the role in question is the one our cognitive science actually picks out, not just any old input-output mapping; and I treat multiple realisation as a live empirical bet, since the reason psychology gets to be autonomous from neuroscience is that the same role really does get implemented in different stuff. I want to know what this is actually exposed to. What would count against it -- not only the usual thought experiments (the nation-of-people case, absent qualia, inverted spectra), but which findings in comparative neurobiology or evolutionary convergence would genuinely tell against the empirical bet, and which arguements or formal results would show the role-based individuation collapsing either into identity theory or into something so liberal that nothing could fail to have a mind. Sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones functionalists themselves would concede, and be honest about whether the view has been quietly insulated by the role/realiser distinction -- and whether that insulation is a problem or just what any decent position looks like. Please answer in American English.
