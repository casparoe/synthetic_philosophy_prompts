I host a small philosophy interview series — part reading group, part podcast — whose listeners are mostly graduate students and serious amateurs, and next month's guest works on the philosophy of pleasure. Her position, which I want the interview to probe rather than merely advertise, is that the heterogeneity problem for hedonism is real but soluble. Pleasures of taste, music, sex, conversation, and intellectual discovery admittedly present no single introspectible quality; on her account that is exactly what we should expect if pleasure is a determinable and its instances are determinates. Just as scarlet, teal, and mauve share no shade in common yet are all hues, the particular pleasures share no feeling in common yet all possess hedonic valence, which she treats as a genuine determinable property rather than a mere label for a disjunction. She is not an attitudinalist: she thinks locating the unity in an attitude of taking pleasure in something, rather than in how the experience feels, changes the subject from pleasure to liking. And she thinks the determinable view matters for the axiology as well, because hedonism can claim that pleasure is a single intrinsic good only if there is something single for it to be good in virtue of.

Please draft ten to twelve interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and no soft openers. I want at least one question that forces her to say precisely what hedonic valence is over and above the disjunction of determinate pleasures — the objection that the determinable is just an abstraction doing no explanatory work — and at least one that presses the colour analogy from the other side, asking whether hue is a model of unity or a model of how little the members of a kind need share. I also want a question that puts the attitudinalist alternative to her seriously, one that asks her to answer the charge that introspection reports the taste of the coffee and the shape of the melody and no separate pleasantness, and one that draws out the axiological stakes: what becomes of hedonism if the unity turns out to be mere family resemblance rather than a shared property. The follow-ups should be the ones that make the standard reply harder to repeat — for instance, if she says valence is introspectively detectable, the follow-up should raise mixed feelings, or the masochist who reports taking pleasure in something he also dislikes, or the possibility of a hedonic spectrum inverted with respect to ours; if she leans on the determinable/determinate relation, the follow-up should ask whether every determinable earns its keep and how we could ever tell when one does not.

Close with a question about what would count against her view — what observation, case, or argument would make her abandon the determinable and concede that pleasures share nothing at all.
