Here's the position. Gödel's incompleteness results don't only tell us something about formal systems; they tell us something about minds. Gödel gives us a sentence that's true but unprovable in F, and the second theorem gives us that F can't prove its own consistency. Now I — a human mathematician — can look at F, see that it's sound (or at least consistent), and thereby see that the Gödel sentence is true even though F can't prove it. And I can do that for any such F you hand me. So whatever I'm up to when I do mathematics can't be captured by any single formal system of that kind. Hence mechanism is false, at least in the crude sense that the mind is just one formal system. This is the Lucas–Penrose sort of line, if you want a label for it.
