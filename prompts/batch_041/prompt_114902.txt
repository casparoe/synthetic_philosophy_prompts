I sit on my city's welfare appeals panel (its a volunteer thing, we hear maybe 30 cases a month). We've been pretty consistent, or at least I thought so, but now we've got a case that's splitting us. Here's our track record from the last year, stripped down:

- Missed a mandatory job-search appointment because the bus never came: we overturned the sanction.
- Missed the same kind of appointment because he overslept: we upheld.
- Missed because of a panic attack, no formal medical exemption on file: overturned.
- Refused a job offer that paid less than his old wage and meant a 90-minute commute each way: upheld.
- Refused a zero-hours contract because the shifts clashed with caring for his father, no formal carer's exemption: overturned.
- Missed a training session because she was at a protest outside the training provider's office: upheld.
- Missed because a job interview ran long and she didn't call ahead: overturned.
- Missed because his phone was dead and he didn't have a charger: upheld.
- Missed because the online portal locked him out and the phone line was closed for a holiday: overturned.

New case: A claimant missed a mandatory work-focused interview because she was in police custody after being arrested at a protest outside the job centre. The charges were dropped the next day. She didn't notify us in advance. She has a clean compliance record otherwise. The department wants to sanction her for 4 weeks. She says she was exercising her political speech and that the arrest was the police's doing. The department says she chose to be there and could have rescheduled.

What should we decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Which of our earlier cases is the closest analog? If our precedents genuinely underdetermine it, what principle would we be adopting with each choice? And what further case would best tell apart the rules we might be running?
