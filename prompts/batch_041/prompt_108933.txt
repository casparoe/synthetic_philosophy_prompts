Write me the desiderata a theory of the value of information must meet: what an adequate account of what information is worth to a decision-maker has to deliver. State each requirement with the case behind it — that the theory reproduce the result that free information never lowers expected utility, and yet explain why the same information can leave an agent worse off once it arrives, as when a patient learns a prognosis that cannot change her treatment and is diminished by the knowledge; that it keep the expected value of information apart from the value of the information actually obtained; that it say when information is worth its cost; that it fix whether the value is agent-relative or belongs to anyone who could act on it; and that it not simply collapse into the value of true belief. Say what the theory must do once the assumptions behind the free-information result fail — costly information, information the agent cannot interpret, information that shifts her preferences. Mark which requirements are constitutive and which are optional, and name the single one that excludes the most candidates; if some pair of them cannot be jointly satisfied, tell me, since that is the part I most want. Leave the scoring of the received positions to me.
