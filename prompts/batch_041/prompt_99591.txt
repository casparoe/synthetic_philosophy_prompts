I have been assembling my views on reference and modality for a paper, and before I commit them to print I should like a consistency audit. Here is the package I have settled on. (1) Proper names and natural kind terms are rigid designators: each refers to the same individual or kind in every possible world in which that individual or kind exists. (2) Consequently, a true identity statement whose two terms are rigid designators is metaphysically necessary, and some such identities — that Hesperus is Phosphorus, that water is H2O, that heat is the motion of molecules — are knowable only a posteriori. (3) The content of a declarative sentence just is its truth conditions across possible worlds: to grasp the proposition a sentence expresses is to know the worlds in which it is true. (4) Learning one of these necessary a posteriori identities is genuinely informative; it extends what one knows in a way that recognising a tautology does not. (5) Reference is fixed by causal-historical chains of use within a community, not by any descriptive content in a speaker's head; what a term means is not settled by what the speaker is inwardly entertaining. (6) Conceivability is a fallible but genuine guide to possibility: if I can coherently conceive of a scenario, that gives me real, if defeasible, reason to think it metaphysically possible. (7) Where a name's reference is fixed by a description — say, a name introduced as "whoever invented the zip" — the name is nonetheless rigid: the description fixes which object we are speaking about, but it is not part of the name's meaning.

I suspect that some of these pull against one another, but I cannot see where, and I should rather find out now than in review. Please audit the package. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be; rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad; for each genuine tension, identify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and say what that revision costs me elsewhere; and finish by telling me which single commitment, if I abandoned it, would resolve the most while costing the least. Be specific about what commits me to what, and do not simply reassure me that the package is a familiar one — I want to know where it is unstable. Answer in American English.
