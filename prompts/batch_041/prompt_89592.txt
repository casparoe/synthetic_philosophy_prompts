I have no training in philosophy, but my partner and I have been having the same argument for the better part of a year, and it has drifted from being an argument about our family into an argument about something much larger, so I would like an outside view. We have two young children, and I am close to certain that I want to stop there; he would like a third, and perhaps a fourth. His reasoning is not sentimental, and he states it cleanly: each additional child would be another life, that life would be worth living, and a world containing more lives worth living is, all else being equal, a better world than one containing fewer — so, all else being equal, we should have more. I cannot find the flaw in the argument, and yet I am convinced the conclusion is wrong. My own position is harder to put into words. What I want to say is that two children who get the best of what we have to give — our time, our patience, our attention, our money — is a better outcome than four children who each get a quarter of it, even though I would never claim that any of the four would have a life that was not worth living. He says this is mere sentiment, and that if I really followed it through I would have to accept something even stranger.

And when I do follow it through, I end up somewhere I find genuinely repugnant. If his principle holds, then a world crowded with an enormous number of people whose lives are only barely worth living — people who would not choose to die, but who have very little — would count as better than a smaller world in which everyone lives splendidly, simply because the sheer number of lives outweighs the difference in quality. He does not flinch at this; he says the conclusion only seems monstrous because I am not really imagining the numbers, and that my revulsion is a failure of imagination rather than a philosophical insight. I suspect he may be partly right about the psychology, and yet I do not think that settles anything.

What I want to know is whether one of us is making a mistake, and precisely where it lies. I am not asking for parenting advice — I can decide what I want for my own household. I am asking whether the principle that more lives worth living make for a better world, all else equal, can be resisted at all, and if so, at what cost. If I reject it, do I have to accept that it would be better if two blissfully happy people existed and no one else did? And if I accept it, do I have to swallow the crowded world of barely-worth-living lives? Is there some third option that preserves what seems true on both sides, or is this one of those cases where I simply have to give up an intuition I cannot argue for? If I must give one up, I would like to understand which one, and why.
