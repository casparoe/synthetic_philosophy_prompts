Ok so I've been reading rationalist blogs for like ten years and I thought I had this whole updating thing figured out, but I keep running into something that doesnt fit the framework and I can't tell if it has a name or if I'm just confused.

Here's what started it. A friend walked me through why she's convinced a certain popular supplement does basically nothing, and I came away genuinely more confident that she's right. Then later I realized she hadn't told me a single fact I didn't already know. Every ingredient had been sitting in my head for years. What was new was the argument — the way she chained the pieces together. And my credence actually moved, like a real update, not just a warm feeling about her being sharp.

That shouldn't happen though, right? If I already knew all the pieces then there's no new evidence, so conditioning on it should do nothing, P(E) was basically 1 already. But I did update. And honestly it felt like the most legit way I've ever changed my mind — more legit than being shown a new study.

Then I noticed the exact same structure in a case I think is garbage. A guy I know got deep into one of those everything-is-connected theories, pharma and the food supply and whatever, and when I pressed him on what actually convinced him it was never a fact, it was always that "it all fits together", "it explains everything". Same move, and he couldn't point to a new fact either.

Third one, which might be a different thing: someone proves a theorem to me from axioms I already accept. I already believed the axioms, so did I learn anything? Sort of, but that case feels different, because there the conclusion is supposed to follow with certainty and in the supplement case it doesn't.

So, three things, and I care way more about 2 and 3 than 1:

1) Does this have a name? I have a vague sense it's cousins with the thing in confirmation theory about evidence you had before you even thought of the theory — but that seems to be about a theory being supported by stuff that was already known when the theory was invented, and mine is weirder, because nothing about the theory changed either, only the argument got better. Although maybe that's just the same problem wearing a hat, I can't tell.

2) If it's legit — and in my friend's case it really seems legit — where does the confidence come from, mechanically? What did I learn? That the argument exists? Is that evidence about the world or just about my own head?

3) What's the actual difference between my friend and the conspiracy guy, said in a way I could apply to the next case I run into? "It all fits together" seems to be doing the same work in both, and I want something better than "mine is true and his isn't". Everyone I know says the answer is falsifiability, but hers is definately not more falsifiable than his and I don't think that's the real line anyway.

If there's an established concept here, fine, tell me what it's called, but mostly I want to know where it stops applying — the boundary cases, what it's not, what looks like it but isn't. If there isn't one, coin something and tell me what it rules out. A bare name won't help me, I already know what the thing feels like, I just don't know what it is.
