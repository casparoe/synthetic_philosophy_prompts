ok so I'm in this seminar on thermo + information (phil dept, cross-listed, which is why the reading list is such a mess) and the final assignment is to bring in a poem that does actual work on one of the topics instead of just naming it. I've got Maxwell's demon and I want to do it as a sestina, because I think the form and the physics are secretly the same shape and I want to see if someone else sees it too.

Here's my observation, and please check it because I worked it out on the back of a handout: the sestina permutes its six end-words by a fixed rule each stanza, and if I've got it right that permutation is a single 6-cycle -- so across the six stanzas every end-word lands in every one of the six slots exactly once, and a seventh stanza would be identical to the first. The form is periodic with period six. It just stops one stanza short of repeating itself. Which is the demon's register: it cycles through its states, comes back around, and the cycle looks free as long as you never add the stanza where the cost shows up. Then the envoi is the three lines where the whole six-stanza cycle gets collapsed and all six words come out in one breath at once -- which is the erasure.

So: a sestina, 39 lines, using end-words from this set (or the same words in whatever grammatical form the line needs): door, bit, memory, heat, cost, again. I picked heat and cost on purpose -- the pun space between thermal accounting and money accounting is where I think this poem should live, and if you don't use it the poem will be worse.

Stuff I want it to actually get right:

- The demon isn't beaten by entropy always increases. He's beaten by bookkeeping: his own ledger balances only because the term he never enters is the state of his own memory. The poem should make you feel that, not state it.
- Somewhere in there: the Szilard one-molecule engine, one bit, kT ln 2 out of a single particle, which is either the most beautiful thing in the area or the most suspicious, depending on the day.
- If you anthropomorphize him, make him a clerk. Records, filing, decades of service, never once counting himself. No cartoon imp with a tiny gate and a grin.
- The envoi should be the erasure. Door and bit and memory and heat all swept into one sentence and the bill finally landing. If the reader feels the bill arrive in those last three lines without you tacking on a line about the second law surviving, thats the win.
- Accuracy bar: a philosopher of physics definately shouldn't wince. The cost sits in the logically irreversible reset, not in the measurement as such, and a demon who simply never reset would just be a piece of a bigger system whose total entropy you'd have to count. Don't turn that into a thesis statement though -- the form should carry it.

His voice or third person, your call. It doesn't need to rhyme or scan outside the form. I'm reading it out loud to the seminar so it has to sound good spoken. And it can't be a physics lecture in verse -- someone who's never heard of the demon should still think it's a good poem.
