My friend and I play a dumb game when we're bored: you have to explain a word without pointing at anything or naming anything you can taste, touch or smell. So "sour" comes out as "the taste that isn't sweet or bitter," and "bitter" as "not sweet or sour," and after four rounds we're going in circles, laughing. Lately I keep thinking thats what a chatbot is doing all day, and then whether it's what I'm doing too, since I also got every word I have from other people's words.

I want to write a poem out of this. A proper sestina, all 39 lines, the kind where six words keep coming back in a fixed shuffle and you can never climb out of them. Pick the six so they're words you could never learn by pointing: word, means, other, same, again, name, something like that. Let it be in the voice of the machine, or of a word that only exists because other words keep using it. It should come across that it can answer anything about the words and still never touch anything -- and at some point it should try to say what "rough" is and only manage "not smooth." Then in the last lines, after all six have come back one more time, end on one plain physical word -- wet, grit, salt -- and leave me honestly unable to tell whether that word finally puts the whole thing on the ground or is just one more word in the circle. Someone who's read about this should nod; my friend should still get it.
