Although I have no academic background — I never went to university and I came to this from arguments with friends and strangers on internet forums, plus whatever I could find in the public library — I have spent several years trying to get straight a question that keeps defeating me: whether there is any reason not to bring a child into the world that has no counterpart reason to bring one into it. Nearly everyone I argue with seems to accept some version of that thought, that it would have been better for someone never to have existed than to exist in misery, while a happy life is not something the world was missing before it turned up, and then they disagree, sometimes violently, about what follows. I would like to be able to sort these disagreements properly instead of talking past people, so please build me a decision tree.

I want it to work as a sorting instrument, not a summary. Someone should be able to walk down it by answering questions about cases and commitments, never being asked which position they hold, and arrive at a leaf that names the position their answers commit them to. I would also like to be able to walk down it myself and find out where I actually land.

Please do the following:

- Build the tree out of the logical space rather than out of a list of names: first set out the questions that carve the space and the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the positions in that space.
- Every internal node must be a question a person can answer without knowing what lies beyond it, and it must be phrased as a concrete case or claim rather than a label. No node may simply ask whether the person accepts the asymmetry, or whether they are an antinatalist.
- The tree must contain branch points separating, at a minimum: what is being evaluated (the act of conceiving, the state of the world, or the person who results); whether a life can be better or worse for the person living it than that person's never having existed at all; whether the asymmetry is a claim about reasons for acting or about the value of outcomes; whether the question is whether to conceive at all or how many children to have; and whether the view is prospective, a reason not to conceive, or retrospective, a complaint the created person could make.
- Work these cases in somewhere: a couple who could conceive only a child with a substantial chance of a painful, incurable illness; a couple who could conceive now a child with a difficult life, or wait and conceive a different child with a good one; a policy change that would make conception slightly more likely across the whole population; someone who says their life has been worth living but that they wish they had never been born; and whether the same reasoning applies to bringing animals into existence.
- The leaves should include, if these are positions rather than confusions: the view that all procreation is wrong; the view that procreation is permissible only when the resulting life is expected to be good enough; the view that there is no asymmetry, because if there is a reason not to create suffering there is equally a reason to create happiness; the view that the asymmetry runs the other way, so that there is a reason to create happy people but none to avoid creating unhappy ones; the view that existence and nonexistence are not comparable at all and the question is therefore a mistake; and the view that the whole matter is a personal preference with no moral content. Add whatever others the space requires.
- Where a leaf corresponds to a position with a standard name, give the name. Where you believe nobody has defended that combination, mark the leaf as unoccupied and say briefly whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
- State explicitly which leaves the tree cannot separate, rather than forcing a distinction between them.
- Each leaf should carry one sentence on the most awkward commitment of the position it names.

Keep it to roughly ten branch nodes, and please leave out citations, reading lists, and the titles of books or articles: I have no way of checking any of them, and I would rather have the structure than the references.
