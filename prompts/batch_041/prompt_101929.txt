A friend of mine thinks the standard "ignore sunk costs" advice is a slogan that proves way too much, and he built this argument to show it. (1) A cost is sunk when it's the same under every option I still have -- you eat it either way. (2) What I should do now depends only on what the options still ahead of me will bring about. (3) So the size of a sunk cost never gives me a reason to pick one option over another. (4) The three years and the money I've poured into my failing restaurant come out identical across every option I have left -- sell, close, or keep going. (5) So none of it is a reason to keep going. (6) Same deal for the twenty years I've put into a marriage I'm unhappy in, the promise I made last spring, the debt of apology I owe someone I wronged, the sentence I'm halfway through serving. (7) So nothing about what I've already done can be a reason for anything -- my past self has no claim on me, my promises dont bind, and the wrongs I've done have no bearing on what I owe now. (8) But thats obviously false. (9) So (2) is wrong, and some facts about the past do give me reasons.

Where's the screw loose? If I have to give up a step, I'd rather keep (2) than (7) -- I don't want to say my history is normatively inert -- but I want to know what that costs me. And if the rescue is just to say promises and past wrongs were never sunk costs in the first place, tell me whether that's an actual fix or whether he's defined the problem away.
