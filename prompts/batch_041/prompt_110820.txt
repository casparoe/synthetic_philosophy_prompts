Retired schoolteacher here, three years into a history of the small town I grew up in, and I keep running into something I don't have a word for. Three things, actually, and I'm not sure whether they're one thing or three. I always assumed the historian's job was just to show what actually happened, which is maybe why this bugs me so much.

One. I write that a certain mill owner "started the town's decline." Nobody in 1894 would have said that, and nothing he did at the time looked like a beginning. The sentence is still true, I can document it, but its truth seems to depend on stuff that hadn't happened yet. What do you call a statement about the past that only becomes sayable later?

Two. My friend is writing the same book from the same documents, and every fact in her version is in mine. But mine is a story of decline and hers is a story of a place that kept reinventing itself. The difference isn't in any of the sentences, it's in the arrangement -- what I put in chapter one as background and what I put in the last chapter as a consequence. When I try to say what my book asserts that hers denies, I can only point at the shape. And yet last year a new document turned up and I had to scrap a whole chapter and rebuild the shape around it, which makes me think the shape is answerable to the evidence even though it isn't *in* the evidence. Is there a name for this, or for the claim that an arrangement of true statements can itself be right or wrong?

Three, possibly just my paranoia: the mill owners wrote letters and the mill hands didn't, so my narrative has alot more turning points for the people who wrote things down. The workers' lives just sort of continue. Same problem or a seperate one?

I don't need a reading list. I want the names, and more than the names I want the lines -- are one and two the same phenomenon or two? Is two just "interpretation," or is it something more specific that people have argued about? If there's an established term that's close but not exact, tell me exactly where it falls short; if there isn't one, coin it and tell me what it wouldn't cover.
