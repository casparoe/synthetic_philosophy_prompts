Her line is that generations are astrology for HR departments — "Gen Z" is a horoscope column with a birth window instead of a zodiac sign. My line is that she's right about the pop version and wrong about the general thing, and I want you to build the argument I keep failing to make.

So here's the position, and I'd like you to argue for it as hard as you can. Generations are real kinds, but almost everyone on both sides is looking for the reality in the wrong place. Not shared values, not shared formative events — everyone alive has formative events. The reality is that a cohort can be a self-reproducing transmission channel: if the people in a birth window mostly came of age on the same communication technology at roughly the same age, then the norms, jokes and habits they picked up got copied peer to peer inside the cohort, rather than handed down from parents or broadcast out to the whole culture. That's a mechanism, not a vibe, and it's the only sense I can find where a generation label names something with causal teeth.

Two things I actually want you to deal with. First, the standard statistical complaint: age, period and cohort move together — if you know how old someone is and what year it is, you know when they were born — so you can't pull a cohort effect out of survey data without an extra assumption. I don't think that kills the position, but I dont know which assumption does the work, so tell me. Second, if the mechanism is platform-during-the-formative-window rather than birth year, my view has an awkward consequence I can't dodge: the birth-year bins everyone uses are probably the wrong units, and "Gen Z" might only be a real kind accidentally, as a lumpy proxy for something narrower. Take that seriously instead of patching it.
