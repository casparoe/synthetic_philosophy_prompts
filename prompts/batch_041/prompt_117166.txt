I argue to much on the internet, and lately I keep running into a shape of argument that I don't have a word for. I'm not a philosopher, so if this is some famous thing with a boring name, fine, but the name isn't really what I'm after.

Here's the shape. Somebody says that something is possible *in principle* -- any truth could in principle be found out, any physical process could in principle be simulated, whatever -- and then a few moves later the "in principle" has quietly evaporated and they're on the hook for the thing actually happening, already, everywhere. The possible gets eaten by the actual. It always smells like a trick to me, but I can never put my finger on which move is the trick.

Here's the instance that's been bugging me. Take the claim: any true fact could in principle be known by somebody. Sounds modest, right? It's not saying anyone actually knows everything, just that nothing is off-limits in principle. Now take some true fact that nobody actually knows -- say, the exact number of hairs on Julius Caesar's head on the morning he was killed. Call it N. It's true that the number is N, and it's also true that nobody knows the number is N. So the compound statement "the number is N and nobody knows it's N" is a true statement. By the modest claim, it could in principle be known. But suppose somebody did know it. To know a compound statement you have to know both halves -- you can't know "A and B" without knowing A and knowing B. So this person knows the number is N, and also knows that nobody knows the number is N. But knowing something means it's true, so if they know that nobody knows it, then nobody knows it. Except they know it. Contradiction. So that compound statement isn't knowable after all. But it's true, and the modest claim said every true statement is knowable. And this seems to work for *any* unknown truth whatsoever, which would mean there are no unknown truths, which would mean everything true is already known by somebody. Which is obviously nonsense.

So: does this particular argument have a name? And is there a name for the general shape -- the move where a claim about what's possible in principle turns into a claim about what's already the case? I've also got a half-remembered thing from a logic class that feels like the same animal: something about a system that could prove that everything it proves is true being able to prove everything, full stop. Same shape or a differant one?

Please don't just hand me a label and walk off. What I want is the name if there is one, the nearest named things to it and exactly how they differ, and then a committed verdict on which step in my argument is the one that has to give. Is it "knowing a compound gets you both halves," or "every true statement is knowable," or "there is some fact nobody knows"? Pick one, say why that one and not the others, and tell me whether the claim can be patched into something defensible that doesn't collapse. If you think the whole thing dissolves and isn't really a paradox at all, commit to that instead -- don't lay out five options and tell me it depends on my logic.
