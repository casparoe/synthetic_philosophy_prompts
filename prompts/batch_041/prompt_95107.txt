I recently received an unusual letter from my university's alumni office, and I am one of exactly twenty people to whom it was sent — apparently they pulled the names of twenty graduates who had, at some point, shown a taste for puzzles, and I suppose I qualified. The offer is this. The office has earmarked a considerable sum for whichever single one of us twenty writes back to claim it, but only on the condition that exactly one of us does so. They decline to say how large the sum is, hinting only that it would change a life outright. If two or more of us write back, or if none of us does, the money returns to the endowment and no one receives anything. The letter is emphatic that the twenty are anonymous to one another and that we are forbidden to try to discover one another's identities or to coordinate in any way; if the organizers learn of any contact among us, the offer is void. So each of us must decide, entirely alone and without knowing what the others will do, whether to write back.

The trouble is that I can talk myself into either answer and back out of it again. If the prize is as large as they hint, then surely the other nineteen are as tempted as I am, all nineteen will write in, and nobody will win — so why should I bother at all? Yet a friend points out that this very reasoning, if all twenty of us indulge in it, guarantees that no one writes and the money is simply thrown away, which strikes me as a worse outcome than if we had all somehow refrained from the greedy calculation in the first place. I cannot tell whether my friend has found a real flaw in my reasoning or is merely being sentimental, and I would like to settle the matter rather than go with my gut.

Please tell me what I should actually do, and why — not a survey of positions or a list of considerations on both sides, but a definite recommendation with the reasoning that supports it. Keep your answer to a single paragraph of no more than 300 words.
