Hey, I'm a PhD student working on Wittgenstein and social epistemology, and I'm trying to get my commitments straight for a chapter. I've got a bunch of views that I think are compatable, but I'm not sure. Here they are:

1. I'm a hinge epistemologist: there are certain commitments — hinges — that are exempt from doubt and that make inquiry possible.
2. I think hinges aren't propositional beliefs; they're more like practical certainties or rules of a practice.
3. I think hinges are socially sustained: they're held collectively and maintained by trust and testimony.
4. I think hinges can be harmful — e.g., hinges that sustain racist or sexist practices — and we can be epistemically blameworthy for holding them.
5. I think we can rationally criticise and revise harmful hinges.
6. I think all rational criticism must be immanent: there's no neutral, practice-independent standpoint from which to evaluate a hinge.
7. I think epistemic justification is externalist: a belief is justified if formed by a reliable process, whether or not the subject can give reasons.
8. I think hinge commitments aren't justified in that externalist sense, because they're not beliefs and they're not formed by a reliable process; they're the background that makes reliability attributions possible.
9. I think knowledge requires justification, so hinge commitments aren't known — but that's fine, knowledge isn't the only epistemic good.

I'm worried some of these pull against each other. Can you audit the set? Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and how severe is each tension? For the genuine ones, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. And please write in British English. Thanks!
