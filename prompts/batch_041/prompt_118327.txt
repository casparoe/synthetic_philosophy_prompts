I write features for a science-and-ideas magazine, and I'm putting together a reader quiz to run alongside a big piece on the consciousness wars. Not a Buzzfeed thing. The thing I keep running into is that everyone I interview argues straight past each other about access vs. phenomenal consciousness, and about whether global workspace theory is even in the business of explaining the felt part. So what I want is a quiz that sorts readers onto real positions in *that* fight, rather than letting them pick whichever label they already like.

Can you write it? Eight to ten questions, each one a concrete case or a bare claim, two or three answer options each, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. The positions I want to be able to land on, at minimum: (a) the global-workspace line where being globally available just is being conscious, and the felt quality isn't a further fact on top; (b) two-kinds realism, where access and phenomenal consciousness are seperate things and workspace theory is only ever a theory of the first; (c) the illusionist line that the phenomenal side is a misfire in our self-model and the workspace story is the whole story; (d) the view that the workspace is at best a correlate, that something else does the real work and broadcast is downstream of it; (e) the quietist option, where the science of access proceeds and the phenomenal question is left open. Add anything else that's genuinely a distinct spot in the space, and tell me if I've named something that's really two positions.

The cases are where I need you to be careful, this is the part I actually care about. Use ones where the views come apart for real, and phrase every question so a reader can answer it without knowing which position it leads to — no questions that just ask 'do you think the felt part is real?' Things I'd like in there somewhere: blindsight; the partial-report/overflow result, where people seem to have more in front of them than they can report; the no-report setups, where you get the brain's ignition signature with no report to go with it; dreaming; a split-brain patient; and — the one I care most about — a stipulated system that broadcasts and reports exactly the way you do but has, by stipulation, nothing it's like to be it. And maybe the reverse case: a system with the felt quality and no global broadcast at all. For each one, ask what the reader thinks the case shows.

Two more things. First, don't build this out of a list of famous theories — build the logical space first (what are the independent questions here, what combinations of answers do they allow), then place the positions in it. Where a combination comes out empty, say so and say why: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And flag any two positions your instrument can't actually pull apart; I'd rather have a hole in the quiz than a fake distinction. Second, this has to be self-scoring by hand, so keep the key simple enough that a reader with a pen can do it.

I don't need citations or a reading list — I'm not printing them, and I know you can't check them anyway.

Finish with a line like \answer{2} giving the number of leaf positions that no combination of answers ever lands on (name them in the prose too).
