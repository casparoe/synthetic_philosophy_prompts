I run underwriting at a small mutual insurer — farms, small businesses, the odd one-off — and I sit on the risk committee, so I'm the one who has to say yes or no on the stuff we have no loss history for. E-bike battery fires in apartment blocks. A marina that wants cover for a new kind of floating dock. Drone spraying outfits. Maybe two or three a month, and anything over a certain size goes to the board with my written reasoning attached, so whatever I decide has to be defensible on paper a year later.

Here's my problem. I think I keep making two different decisions and filing them under one word. Sometimes I know roughly what the odds are and I don't like them. Sometimes I have no idea what the odds are and I don't like that either — and I say no to the second kind far more often, even when the premium is good and the worst case is survivable. I read about the Ellsberg thing with the two urns years ago, where people happily bet on the urn that's half red but won't touch the one where nobody knows the mix, and I've never stopped thinking about it, because its exactly what I do at my desk. I've also noticed I'm much braver when everyone in the room is equally in the dark than when it's only me who doesn't know the answer. And I've heard the two-urn choice is supposed to be a mistake somehow, that you can't hold both halves of it at once, which bugs me because both halves feel right to me.

I want a procedure I can actually run in about twenty minutes on a single submission that keeps those two things apart: what I don't like about the odds, and what I don't like about not knowing the odds. It should tell me when my caution is doing real work and when it's just costing us business, and it should be something I can paste into the file. I dont have any econ training so keep the machinery out of it, but don't water down the part that actually matters — I'd rather have a step I have to sit and think about than a fake number that makes the form look complete.

Write it in these sections:

- "What I'm actually deciding" — plain English, about 150 words, on the difference between the two kinds of unknown, and why the difference isn't just 'how confident I am'.
- "The procedure" — numbered steps in order, each with the question it asks, what counts as an answer, what to do when I can't answer it, and which steps hand the decision to a person rather than producing one. About 800 words.
- "When 'just call it 50/50' is fine" — the tests for when I'm allowed to treat an unknown as a coin flip, and when that move is just me hiding. About 200 words.
- "Three submissions run through it" — the e-bike one, the marina, and one where the procedure tells me to write the policy and I won't want to hear it. About 500 words.
- "Where the procedure stops and I have to decide" — the parts that are values rather than facts. About 200 words.
- "What this can't do" — honest limits, including anything about that two-urn choice you think I've got wrong. About 150 words.
