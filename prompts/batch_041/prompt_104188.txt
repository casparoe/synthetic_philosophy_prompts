Suppose the John Stuart Mill of his final years, the Mill who had come to expect that the labor question would be settled in the direction of greater equality of ownership, who saw the cooperative movement as a school of self-reliance and a possible way beyond the wage relation, and who carried to the end both his Malthusian anxieties about population and his fear of the despotism of custom and majority opinion, is revived and asked to assess a legislative package from our own time, one whose particulars he could not have anticipated. The package, called the Commonwealth Settlement, has four parts:

1. A universal, unconditional monthly payment to every adult, financed by a tax on the unimproved value of land and on the assessed market value of personal data.
2. A rule requiring every firm above 250 employees to transfer twenty percent of its equity per decade into an employee-owned trust with full voting rights.
3. A ceiling on inherited wealth, with the excess flowing into the fund that finances the dividend.
4. A one-year civic service obligation for all eighteen-year-olds, with a noncombatant alternative.

Give me Mill's considered verdict on each part and on the package as a whole, presented as an argument rather than a summary, and observe the following requirements:

- Sort your verdicts into three classes: those that follow from commitments of his that transfer cleanly to our circumstances; those that depend on contestable empirical beliefs he held (about population pressure, the educability of the working class, the moral effects of competition, the likely conduct of a democratic electorate) and how each verdict changes once those beliefs are corrected; and those on which his own writing pulls in different directions, where you should say which pull you weight more heavily and why.
- Identify the single premise of his to which your overall prediction is most sensitive, and state what would falsify your reconstruction, that is, what Mill could plausibly have written that would show you have projected a contemporary politics onto him.
- Say explicitly where the exercise is anachronistic, and whether any of the four parts raises a question he lacked the conceptual vocabulary to answer at all.
- Close by stating your confidence, on a scale of zero to one, that your reconstruction resembles what Mill would actually have concluded, with one sentence on what drives the number down.
