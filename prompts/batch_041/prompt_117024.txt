For some months now I have been trying to settle a question about famines that keeps nagging at me, and I do not have the background to work it out on my own. The way I have always heard it put is that some famines are natural disasters and others are political crimes, and the cases that get called political crimes — the Bengal famine, the Irish famine, and the famine during the Great Leap Forward — have never looked that way to me. The Bengal famine came out of a cyclone and wartime upheaval, the Irish one out of a potato blight, and the Chinese one out of dreadful weather layered on top of a bad farming policy. If the thing that actually set the crisis in motion was the weather, why is it not simply a natural disaster? What I want is a clean rule I can carry with me and apply to new cases I read about: when a harvest fails for reasons no human being caused and people starve, blame nature; when a government deliberately diverts, hoards, or blocks food, blame the government. Amartya Sen's observation that famines do not occur in functioning democracies seems to fit this picture, since the democratic part strikes me as being about how a government responds after nature has already done the damage, rather than about what caused the shortage in the first place.

Two things keep tripping me up, though. First, a friend insists that in at least one of these famines food was still being shipped out of the starving region while people were dying, which I had assumed was merely a detail about how badly the relief effort was handled. Second, I invented a test case to check my rule: a drought strikes a region that mostly grows cotton for export, the farm laborers lose their wages, grain prices jump, and food keeps flowing to the cities and abroad, so the laborers starve even though the region as a whole is not short of food. By my rule that counts as a natural disaster, because the drought came first, yet calling it that feels plainly wrong. Would you take the three real famines and my made-up one, sort each into the natural or the political bucket exactly as my rule would sort it, and then tell me plainly whether the rule holds up? If it does not, I would like to understand precisely where it breaks and what a better way of framing the natural-versus-political question would be. Please write your answer in American English.
