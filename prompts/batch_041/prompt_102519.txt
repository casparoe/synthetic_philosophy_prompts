I spent forty years as a physiologist and have lately been teaching a small upper-level seminar in philosophy of mind. I am writing the final exam and want one essay question on pain asymbolia and the badness of pain. Please act as a professional philosopher and assessment designer: think carefully about the crux before drafting, and brainstorm two or three possible ways to frame the question before choosing the one that best separates understanding from recall.

The question should not be answerable by reciting the standard sensory/affective distinction, nor by listing the usual accounts of pain’s badness. It should make students confront a case in which a patient reports feeling pain but is not distressed by it, shows no withdrawal, and may even laugh. I want them to ask what, if anything, remains bad about the pain in such a case, and to distinguish at least three candidate sources of pain’s badness—for example, its intrinsic phenomenal quality, its motivational or imperative force, and its role as a signal of bodily damage—and say which survives asymbolia. They should also distinguish pain asymbolia from congenital indifference to pain, and explain why that distinction matters for the argument. Do not require citations, reading lists, or bibliographies; the question must be answerable from conceptual reasoning alone. The students are upper-level undergraduates with some background in philosophy of mind but no specialist knowledge of pain research.

Please provide: (1) the exam question, pitched for a 90-minute essay; (2) a model answer of about 600–700 words that takes a clear position rather than merely surveying the literature; and (3) a marking scheme out of 20 that names five must-hit moves, assigns marks to each, and includes a one-line note on how a typical B-level answer would miss each move. The marking scheme should make clear what separates an A from a B: an A should show why asymbolia threatens some accounts of pain’s badness but not others, and should say whether the asymbolic patient is still worse off and in what sense.
