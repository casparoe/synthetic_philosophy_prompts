alright, I'm writing up my views on miracles for a seminar and I want to check they actually hang together before I put them on paper. Here's where I've landed:

- I'm a theist, and I think at least one miracle report is true — the resurrection of Jesus is the one I'd stake most on.
- I accept the core of Hume's argument: you shouldn't believe a miracle report unless it would be more surprising for the report to be false than for the event to have actually happened. The evidence has to beat the prior improbability, basically.
- I think laws of nature are just patterns — the best systematization of what actually happens. No governing glue, no necessary connections between distinct events.
- I also think laws are ceteris paribus — we always state them with implicit provisos, and no single observation ever refutes one outright; it just tells us we've hit the edge of its scope.
- Testimony is a basic source of knowledge as far as I'm concerned: we're entitled to believe what people tell us unless we've got some specific reason not to, and we don't have to first compute the prior probability of the kind of event being reported.
- And I think history is the right method for the resurrection: what the historian can establish is that the tomb was empty and that his followers had experiences they took to be of him — not that God raised him.

I've got a nagging feeling that at least two of these are pulling against each other, but I cant tell which.
