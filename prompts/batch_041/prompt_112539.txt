I've been listening to a lot of history podcasts on my runs and its made me insufferable, so apologies in advance. There's an argumentative move I keep running into and I want a name for it, because I'm pretty sure it isn't just hindsight bias. Here's the shape. Someone says some outcome was bound to happen, given deep causes: the alliance commitments, the cost structure of the industry, the soil and the rainfall. I ask about one small thing that could easily have gone otherwise -- a telegram that arrived a day late, one investor changing his mind, a late frost. The reply isn't 'no, that wouldn't have mattered'. It's softer than that: 'sure, maybe that one thing goes differently, but something like it was going to happen; the pressures were too strong.' So a claim that this event was necessary quietly turns into a claim that some event of this kind was probable, which is a much easier claim to defend, and the original claim never gets withdrawn. And the standards are lopsided: if I propose a counterfactual I'm told to specify a mechanism, step by step, or else it's idle speculation; nobody ever has to specify the mechanism by which the outcome was guaranteed -- pointing at the pressures is treated as enough.

My friend who does geology and I have a version of this about extinctions (was it the asteroid, or was something like the asteroid coming anyway), and I hear it in arguments about whether a particular company was always going to win its market, and about whether some revolution could have been avoided by one concession arriving a week earlier. So it isn't specific to history writing, which is part of why I suspect there should be a name and I just haven't found it.

What I want carved out. First, is there an established name for that specific slide from 'this outcome was necessary' to 'something of this kind was coming'? I know about hindsight bias, but that's a claim about the psychology of the judge, and this move gets made by people who've read all the revisionist literature and know perfectly well that contemporaries were surprised and split. I know the word overdetermination, but that's supposed to be a claim about the actual causal structure -- two causes each sufficient -- not a swap of one modal claim for a weaker one halfway through the argument. Second, is there a name for the asymmetry in evidential demands, mechanism required for the counterfactual and nothing required for the inevitability claim? If neither has a name, coin one for the pair, since they seem to arrive as a package, and tell me the boundary cases. How does this differ from a legitimate argument that really does rest on a statistical model and really does license 'something of the kind was likely'? And what's left of inevitability talk once that slide is blocked -- is there a respectable version of it, or does the whole notion go?
