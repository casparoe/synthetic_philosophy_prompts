Ok so my friend Marta (she did a philosophy degree and never lets anyone forget it) has spent months telling me my views on manipulation are a pile of leftovers from three different meals, and I want an audit from someone who isn't her. Here's where I've landed, mostly from arguing about this at work and thinking in the shower: (1) what's wrong with manipulation is that it gets you to do something without going through your own judgement — it treats your reasons as an obstacle to route around instead of something to answer to; (2) that doesn't mean emotion is off limits — if someone shows me photos from a factory farm and I go vegetarian, or a mate tells me about his life and it changes how I see something, that isn't manipulation, that's persuasion working; (3) the test I actually use in practice is: would I be ok with the person finding out exactly what I did and why I did it? If I'd want the method hidden, it was manipulation; (4) but I'm completely fine with opt out organ donation, with the supermarket putting the milk at the back so you have to walk past everything, and with a website putting the expensive plan first. Nobody's judgement is engaged in any of that, and honestly I'd rather not have the trick spelled out to me, and yet I think it's fine, sometimes even good; (5) I think it's genuinely wrong to guilt trip your partner into changing their mind or to sulk until they give in, even though there's no lie in it and they can see exactly what you're doing; (6) I'd tell a burglar the wrong room my flatmate is sleeping in without blinking, and that doesn't feel like manipulation in the bad sense at all; (7) and I don't buy it when someone says they were manipulated into it — if you did the thing, you did it, you don't get to hand your agency back like a rented car.

Where I'm stuck is that some of this has to be pulling against some of the rest, and I can't tell which, because every time I try to fix one bit another bit goes weird. Marta reckons a couple of these can't be mine at the same time, and she keeps bringing up the placebo thing — a doctor giving you a sugar pill and calling it a strong painkiller — as if it proves the whole thing collapses, but I have a feeling some of what she's pointing at is a fake problem, like with the burglar. So: go through the set and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how bad each one is, and which pairs only look bad.
