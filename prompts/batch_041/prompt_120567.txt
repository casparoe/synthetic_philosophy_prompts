I'm a philosopher of cognitive science stuck in an engineering department, and I've talked my way into a small pot of money -- one postdoc, two years -- that I get to spend on basically whatever I want as long as its conceptual work on von Neumann's picture of the brain. You know the one: the unfinished book that came out of his Silliman lectures, brain as computing machine, the analog/digital mess, the reliability problem. No wet lab, no new data, no hardware. Just a smart person, a whiteboard, and two years. It has to be defensible to the neuromorphic people down the hall, though, or they'll eat me alive at the seminar.

So which open questions here actually matter? Assume I've read the book and dont need it summarized. The candidates I keep coming back to all feel either picked over or unfundable:

- analog vs digital, the claim that the nervous system is a mix of both -- everyone agrees it's a real question, nobody seems to move on it
- reliability from unreliable components -- he clearly cared, but my impression is that error-correcting codes and then overparameterized nets basically ate this one, and it's a footnote now
- memory: how the brain stores anything at all, distributed vs addressed -- also feels like it's been farmed out to neuroscience proper and I'd just be redoing their work badly

And then my own hunch, which I genuinely can't tell is deep or dumb: I think the thing he kept worrying at wasn't the components at all, it was the language -- that the nervous system's signals are statistical and aggregate rather than logical, so the unit of computation isn't the neuron or even the spike but some population-level thing, and that describing it would need a mathematics nobody had yet. Everyone I float this to says it's just connectionism, or the statistical mechanics of learning, or predictive coding with a new hat on, and that it's already been absorbed. Is it? If it hasn't been, is that the one?

What I want from you is a defended ranking, not a list. For each live question -- mine, the three above, and anything I've missed that you think is the real heir to his project -- tell me: is it upstream of other work or downstream of it, can one theorist plausibly move it in two years from an armchair, has it secretly been answered and we just didn't notice, and what would have to happen to move it up or down my ranking. And please flag the zombies: questions that keep getting restated because they sound foundational but that no possible result could settle. I'd much rather fund something that could turn out to be wrong.
