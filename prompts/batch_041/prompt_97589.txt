I'm defending a paper at our work-in-progress seminar next Friday and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The thesis I'm defending: Pascal's mugging isn't a reductio of expected-value maximization at all — it's a reductio of the assumption that you can assign a single real-valued probability to a claim with infinite stakes. My fix is a two-tier rule. Screen offers by probability first; infinite or enormous value only gets to speak once the probability clears a floor, and the floor is supposed to be set by something decision-relevant rather than by taste. I've been calling it value-after-probability, which may or may not survive contact with the room.

The room is the problem. It's mostly formal epistemologists and decision theorists — the imprecise-probability, infinite-ethics, fanaticism corner of the department — plus two philosophers of religion who think the mugging is a genuinely live argument for theism and who will definately hear my "floor" as me giving myself permission to set the bar wherever it's convenient, and one longtermist who'll read the whole thing as a hit job on caring about the far future.

So: give me the objections I'll actually get from these people, in their vocabulary not mine, ordered by how much damage they'd do. For each one, a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. I don't need the stock objections every decision theorist already knows — I need the ones this specific room will reach for, including the ones that come from inside my own camp. Tell me what I should concede up front, what I should hold no matter what, and the one reply that has to land or the paper's dead. And finish with the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
