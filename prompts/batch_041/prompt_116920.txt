I'm a third-year PhD student in decision theory — my dissertation is on the causal/evidential divide in Newcomb-style cases, so I'm not exactly a rube about this stuff — and there's one arguement a friend in my program keeps hitting me with over drinks that I can't seem to get any traction against, so I want to see if you can help me find the hole in it (or in my head). The argument, as best I can reconstruct it, goes roughly: the universe is probably enormous, so there are lots of other agents out there who are relevantly like me — same values, same kind of decision procedure, same general situation — and who are facing their own one-shot prisoners dilemmas and public-goods problems. Since they're like me, what I choose here is evidence about what they choose there. So I should cooperate in my one-shot PD, and generally act the way I'd want all my 'copies' to act, even though I'll never meet the other player and she can't possibly observe me. He calls it something like cooperating in a large world.

Here's my problem with it, and I'm worried it's a pretty basic one. In the actual PD sitting in front of me, the other player has already moved — or is moving right now, in a room I can't see — and there is no channel of any kind running from my choice to hers. So how can my picking cooperate possibly be evidence that she cooperated? It feels like evidence has to run forward in time, or at least along some causal wire, and here I've got neither: her action is fixed and sealed off from me, and mine comes after. Which makes me think the argument has to be doing one of two unacceptable things: either it's smuggling in backwards causation (my present choice reaching back and telling me about her past choice), or it's quietly assuming that she and I are literally the same agent underneath, which is just false — she's a seperate person in a different room. So, seriously, which is it? Put on your decision-theorist hat and think it through carefully for me — feel free to start by brainstorming the different ways the evidential link could be supposed to work, then tell me which one actually holds up. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence (am I right that it needs backwards causation, or not?) and then give me the reasoning; no preamble, and don't bother restating my question.
