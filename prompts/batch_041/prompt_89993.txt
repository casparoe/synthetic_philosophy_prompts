(1) Hedonism — net felt pleasure is the only thing that's good for you in itself. (2) There are no higher pleasures in Mill's sense: when people call some pleasures higher they just mean they prefer them, and the preferring doesn't show the pleasure is worth more per unit. (3) The experience machine intuition is tracking something real — when people say a life in the tank would be worse for them even if they never found out and never wanted out, I don't think they're just status-quo biased or scared of the unfamiliar. (4) Autonomy and authenticity are only instrumentally good: they matter insofar as they tend to produce pleasure or prevent pain, full stop. (5) Posthumous harm is real — having your last wishes ignored, or your name dragged through the mud after you're gone, is a genuine wrong to you. (I'm not religious, I don't think anything survives death, for what it's worth.) (6) I'd give up a chunk of my own well-being to help a stranger if the numbers were big enough, and I don't think that makes me irrational.

Which of these actually clash, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest way to fix the real ones? Rank them by how bad the clash is rather than telling me they're all in tension, and for each one that has to give, tell me what the fix costs me.
