I clerk for a justice on our state's highest court and must predict her vote in a case that reaches us next term. Her record on statutes challenged as pure morality legislation:

- a ban on private, consensual sexual conduct between adults: struck down;
- a ban on selling sexual material to willing adults: struck down;
- a ban on dueling, upheld even as applied to a consensual duel between competent adults in which neither party was hurt;
- a motorcycle helmet requirement: upheld;
- a ban on possessing a small quantity of a drug for personal use: struck down;
- a ban on paying a living person for a kidney: upheld;
- a ban on an act performed in a public place that injures no one but that most citizens find degrading: upheld.

Her opinions in these cases argue in the register of what a community may ask of its members and what kind of society it means to be, and are thin on the language of harm, consent, and rights. The statute now before us criminalizes the private, consensual creation and possession, by competent adults, of highly realistic simulations of the torture and killing of human beings, where no actual person is depicted, harmed, or depicted without consent, and nothing is shared or sold. Its preamble says the practice degrades those who engage in it and erodes the community's shared conviction that persons are inviolable. Our constitution has no bill of rights, so nothing here turns on free expression.

Predict her vote. I care far less about the outcome than about the rule she has been running: give me the rule that best fits all seven verdicts; two or three rival rules that fit them about as well; what each predicts here; your best guess at which is really hers, with reasons; the single case that would best separate the rivals; and which of her past verdicts she would have to give up or reinterpret to reach each result. The relevant tradition is the one running from the Hart–Devlin debate about whether the law may enforce a shared morality, so keep separate what is settled in that literature from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested position as the consensus.
