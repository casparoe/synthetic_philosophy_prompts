ok so I teach a seminar on consequentialism and every year the students hit the same wall: the cluelessness thing, where any action ripples out so far that you can't actually say whether it did more good than harm overall. I want something that makes the problem vivid instead of just asserting it.

Could you write me a parody "guidance for applicants" document — the kind of FAQ a grantmaking foundation sends out — for a fictional foundation that has fully absorbed the cluelessness worry and built it into its own application process. Deadpan bureaucratic voice, no winking at the reader. The application should require applicants to trace the consequences of their project out to absurd distances (effects on people who don't exist yet, on the far future, on the broad trajectory of civilization), and the guidance should keep conceding that nobody can actually compute any of this while still demanding the applicant attest that they've tried. The comedy has to come from the philosophy being right: the difference between ordinary uncertainty and genuine cluelessness, why a chaotic causal chain makes expected-value comparison not just hard but arguably meaningless, and the pressure this puts on you to either abandon consequentialism or quietly pretend the problem away.

Around 500-600 words, formatted as numbered FAQ items with fake question headers. Don't name any real philosophers or real foundations — invent the foundation and its officers.
