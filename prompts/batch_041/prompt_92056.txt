For most of my working life I was a chemist, and since retiring I have been reading my way into the philosophy of mind. I have found myself drawn to the suggestion that the physical, design, and intentional stances are not competing theories of one and the same system but different predictive strategies, and that the entities they posit — beliefs and desires among them — are real patterns in something like the sense in which a coarse-grained regularity in thermodynamics is real. For a small reading group (two other retired scientists, a graduate student in cognitive science, and one philosopher of science who is politely sceptical of the whole idiom), I would like a decision tree that sorts the positions one can hold on the question: are the patterns posited by the intentional stance real, and if so, in what sense?

Please build the tree from the logical space of answers rather than from a roster of names. Every internal node should be a question that someone could answer without already knowing which position it leads to — for example, whether the reality of a pattern depends on the existence of some possible observer who could compress the system's behaviour; whether the predictive success of a stance licenses any ontological commitment at all, or only a licence to keep using it; whether the reality of a pattern admits of degrees; and whether a pattern that is real at a coarse grain survives, without loss, when the system is described at the finest available grain. Each leaf should carry the name of a position, or the word unnamed where you judge that no one has occupied it, together with one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Where two leaves turn out to be indistinguishable in your tree, flag them rather than inventing a distinction; and where a branch leads to a combination no one has defended, say whether you think that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

I would particularly like the tree to locate a position I have been trying to state for myself and have not seen put in quite this way: that beliefs and desires are real as temperature is real — genuine, and not observer-dependent in the way a useful fiction is, yet defined only relative to a coarse-graining and a choice of macrovariables, so that they are neither fictions nor recoverable, without remainder, from the physical stance. Show me what separates this from the more familiar claim that patterns are real but mind-dependent, and from the claim that the intentional stance is merely a convenient instrument. Use concrete systems as the test cases at the leaves wherever you can — a chess engine, a thermostat, a bacterium, an ant colony, a market, a large language model — since I trust those more than I trust my intuitions about people. Keep it to six or seven leaves, and let the philosopher of science's own view, that the dispute is merely verbal, appear as one of the leaves, with a note on whether it is really a position or a refusal to take one.
