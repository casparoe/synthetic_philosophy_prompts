I've been chewing on the Gricean toolkit — speaker meaning vs. sentence meaning, the cooperative principle and its four maxims (quantity, quality, relation, manner), conversational implicature with its supposed calculability and cancellability — and I keep circling back to Clinton's 1998 grand jury testimony. You know the bit: he argued that whether one of his earlier statements was true depended on what the word "is" meant in it. I already know the standard Gricean read — he leaned on a narrow sense of what he'd literally said while implicating something else, and the perjury charge had alot of trouble getting purchase because perjury targets what you said, not what you implicated. That part I've got.

What I want is the harder pass. Treat the whole thing as a real case for Grice and push the theory until it creaks. Map it properly first: who's the speaker and who's the audience (the grand jurors, the prosecutors, the public reading the transcript months later — does that split matter?), what Clinton's actual options were at each turn, what the shared background is that the maxims would have to draw on to generate an implicature at all, and what exactly the hearer is supposed to calculate from. Then tell me where Grice breaks. Its built for exchanges whose point is to be understood; a witness coached by lawyers has roughly the opposite aim, the exchange is compelled and adversarial, and the transcript gets read by people who weren't in the room. Does the CP even apply, or does this need something Grice didn't have — strategic ambiguity, a non-cooperative pragmatics, a hearer-side notion of implicature? Does the said/implicated line survive in a setting where not-saying is precisely the winning move? And is the law's reliance on "what is said" a principled distinction or just a doctrinal accident, given that Grice himself thought implicatures are genuinely communicated and not mere decoration?

Then run it backwards: is this a confirmation of the theory, a case Grice never anticipated, or an anomaly showing the framework was idealizing all along? And don't force Grice to have an answer where he's just silent — say where he's silent.
