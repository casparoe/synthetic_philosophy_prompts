I have no formal training in philosophy, but I have been trying to work out a position of my own about reasons that no one has. I call them orphan reasons: rationales that explain why something happens or why a trait persists even though no mind ever entertains them. I mean reasons in the broad sense, not only moral reasons. I would like you to audit the following package for consistency. Please treat it as a set of commitments, not as a request for a survey of the literature.

(1) Natural selection is a reason-recording process. When a trait persists because it solved a problem for ancestors, there is a fact about what the trait is for, and that fact is independent of any organism or biologist representing it.

(2) To give a trait's function is to give the reason for its presence. So there are reasons in the world that are not anyone's reasons.

(3) A reason, properly speaking, is a consideration that counts in favour of something. Reasons are normative: if R is a reason to do X, then R makes X fitting or required in some sense, not merely caused.

(4) Normativity requires a subject. A consideration can count in favour of an action only if there is someone for whom it can count, a creature that can be guided by it, even in a thin sense.

(5) Nevertheless, I think a bee's dance, a tree's phototropism, and an immune response can be explained by orphan reasons. No subject in those systems represents the relevant consideration; the rationale floats free.

(6) Reasons must be weighable. If two considerations count in favour of incompatible outcomes, it must make sense to ask which has more weight, even if no one ever does the weighing.

(7) But weight is always weight-for-someone. Nothing has weight except relative to a standpoint or a project.

I suspect the package does not hang together, but I cannot tell whether the fault is in (2), (3), (4), or (6) and (7), or whether I am just using reason in two different senses without noticing. I also hold, separately, that a river has no reason to flow downhill, even though its flow is intelligible and not arbitrary; I do not know whether that commitment helps or hurts.

Please tell me which commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and which are underdescribed. Rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each, say what would have to be given up or revised and what the cost of that revision would be. Then say which single revision would resolve the most while doing the least violence to the rest. I am not asking for a reading list; I want an audit of this particular package. Finally, please state how confident you are in your audit and identify the point you are least sure about.
