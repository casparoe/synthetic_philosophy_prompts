My friend Nadia is doing a PhD on Kierkegaard and has gotten really into his late stuff about the press, the public, and the single individual. She keeps saying the problem with modern media isn't lying, it's that it conjures up a public that is nobody and everybody, so no one can be held responsible for anything. Anyway, over the years I've collected her verdicts on media cases. She thinks the old scandal sheets that published anonymous gossip about private people were poisonous even when the gossip was true. She thinks a harsh satirical attack on a politician's policies is fine, even if it ruins his career, because he put himself forward as a public figure. She thinks a newspaper that runs a poll asking readers what they think about an issue and then reports the result as the voice of the public is doing something worse than just being wrong. She thinks state censorship of a vulgar paper is still wrong, because the press has to be free to be vulgar. She thinks an anonymous author who writes edifying essays under a pseudonym is fine, but an anonymous op-ed that accuses a named person of a crime is cowardly. And she thinks a social media pile-on is worse than a newspaper campaign, because in the pile-on everyone is a public and no one is an author.

Now a new thing has come up. A platform lets users post anonymous confessions about their personal lives, and the most liked ones get promoted to a front page called the public square. The confessions are often about other people, sometimes true, sometimes not, and the people they're about are usually not public figures. The platform says it's just a neutral space for the public to speak, and it doesn't take responsibility for what's posted. Nadia hasn't seen it yet. What's your best guess about what she'll say, and what rule is she actually running? Give me the two or three rules that fit her earlier verdicts equally well, say what each predicts about the confession platform, tell me which rule you'd bet on and why, and name the further case that would best tell those rules apart. The verdict matters less to me than the reasoning.
