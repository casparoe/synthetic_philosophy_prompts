hi — I'm a junior and I'm in a political theory seminar this term (mostly about democracy and its discontents) and I've got a situation I can't stop chewing on. I'd rather get an actual argument than the "thats just politics" I keep getting from people.

In September I started volunteering for a party's local branch — canvassing, the group chat, all of it. I definately believed in the platform going in. Two things are eating at me. First: at the monthly meeting I asked whether our position on the housing measure actually added up, since our own policy person's numbers seemed to point the other way. The chair shut it down — said we don't litigate that in front of people, we need a united front until the vote. When I pushed he said you change the platform from the inside, after we have seats. I can live with message discipline, honestly. But the second thing is what scares me. I've started believing things because we believe them. Last week I was arguing with my dad and heard myself making a case I would've called dumb in August. And there was a specific moment — I was writing a comment defending something I privately still thought was a bad idea, and I felt nothing. Not "I'm lying and I hate it," just nothing. That's the part that got me, because I didn't feel like I was being dishonest at the time.

My friend Maya thinks I'm being precious. She's in a tenant union and says any group that wants to win needs discipline, and the alternative is a bunch of individuals with no power. But the union people argue constantly and nobody gets shamed for it, so I don't buy that it's just "groups." Maybe it's parties specifically? Or maybe my branch is just a bad one and I'm generalizing from one dysfunctional chapter, in which case I should just switch branches and stop moralizing.

So: is this a fixable-people problem or something built into what a party is? If it's built in, is there a way to be in one without it doing that to me, or is it a straight choice between being effective and being able to think? And is quitting a cop-out, or the only honest move? Sorry for the essay, I just keep going in circles on it.
