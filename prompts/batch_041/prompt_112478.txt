I recently became a naturalized citizen of a country where I will soon be voting for the first time, and, by a strange coincidence, my name was drawn by lot to serve on a citizens' assembly that will spend several months deliberating on a local issue and issuing recommendations. Both experiences sent me back to Spinoza. I read the Theological-Political Treatise years ago and have lately been working through the unfinished Political Treatise, and I keep finding myself wanting to ask him questions that his own century never put to anyone. I would like your help thinking through what he would have said.

Please take the role of a historian of early modern philosophy and political theory with a specialist's command of Spinoza's Ethics, his Theological-Political Treatise, and the unfinished Political Treatise, as well as the Dutch republican setting in which he wrote. Think carefully, and work in stages rather than delivering a verdict immediately.

Begin with a brainstorming pass: lay out, in your own words, the handful of commitments in Spinoza's metaphysics and psychology that actually do work in his politics — his account of natural right and power, his identification of right with might, his treatment of the passions and of superstition, his insistence that the purpose of the state is freedom rather than obedience, his subordination of religious authority to civil authority, and his argument that freedom of philosophizing is inseparable from the peace of a republic. For each, note in a sentence how load-bearing it is for his political conclusions.

Then, on that basis, predict his positions on things that did not exist, or were not yet live questions, in his lifetime. I am especially interested in: universal adult suffrage, including women; the secret ballot; compulsory voting; mass political parties with disciplined memberships, professional staffs, and ideological platforms; proportional representation, ranked-choice voting, and the selection of officials by lot; judicial review, by which courts strike down legislation passed by an elected assembly; referendums and citizens' initiatives; paid career politicians and a permanent administrative bureaucracy; campaign finance regulation and disclosure requirements; coordinated disinformation, foreign propaganda, and algorithmic micro-targeting of political advertising; transnational democratic federations in which hundreds of millions of citizens elect a common legislature; and the welfare state, including the question whether material security is a precondition for the freedom he prizes.

Sort your predictions into tiers: (1) positions that follow fairly directly from his principles; (2) plausible extensions of his principles, where he would likely have landed but with some strain; (3) issues on which his framework gives no determinate answer, or on which he would probably have found the question unintelligible; and (4) issues on which his principles would push him somewhere we would not expect a seventeenth-century thinker to go. Flag throughout where you think his Dutch context, rather than his philosophy, would be doing the explanatory work.

Two puzzles deserve extended treatment. First, the Political Treatise breaks off just as he turns to democracy, and the one passage in his corpus where he takes up women's political standing reads as an exclusion. Would his own principles have forced him past that exclusion had he lived to finish the chapter, and can a faithful Spinozist retain the exclusion while keeping everything else? Second, would his defense of free expression protect deliberate manipulation and coordinated falsehood, or does his psychology of the passions give him grounds to distinguish speech that aids understanding from speech that merely moves the passions?

Please paraphrase Spinoza rather than quoting him, and do not supply citations, chapter numbers, page references, or titles beyond the two treatises named above; where you are uncertain about his texts, say so plainly rather than inventing support. Close with the prediction you are most confident in and the one you think would have surprised him most.
