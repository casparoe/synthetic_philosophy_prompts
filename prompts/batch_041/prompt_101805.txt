I am a law clerk for Justice Mira, and I need to predict how she will vote in a pending case. Justice Mira has spent two decades writing about precommitment, and her votes are remarkably consistent. In Case 1, a patient with a history of episodic psychosis signed an advance directive refusing antipsychotic medication during future episodes; when he later became competent and sought to revoke the directive, Justice Mira voted to enforce it. In Case 2, a person signed a contract with a casino banning himself from gambling for five years; when he later wanted to gamble, she voted to enforce the ban. In Case 3, a state constitution included a charter of rights whose repeal required a supermajority; when a simple majority of the legislature sought to repeal it, she voted to enforce the supermajority requirement. In Case 4, a state constitution included a provision defining marriage as between one man and one woman; after public opinion shifted, a simple majority of the legislature sought to repeal the provision, and Justice Mira voted to strike down the provision, explaining that it entrenched a denial of equal standing. In Case 5, a city charter required a supermajority to alter a zoning rule that protected a historically marginalized neighborhood from displacement; when the city council sought to alter the rule by simple majority, she voted to enforce the supermajority requirement. In Case 6, a state legislature passed a statute granting a tax cut and provided that it could be repealed only by a supermajority of a future legislature; when a later simple majority sought to repeal it, Justice Mira voted to strike down the entrenchment, explaining that one legislature cannot bind another in that way.

The pending case is this. A state constitution contains a provision requiring a supermajority vote to repeal a set of tax credits that benefit a particular industry. The industry is not a historically marginalized group, and the tax credits are a contested distributive policy rather than a protection of anyone's autonomy or equal standing. After a change in public opinion, the legislature wants to repeal the credits by a simple majority. What should Justice Mira decide if she wants to be consistent with her own precedents? Which earlier case is the closest analog? If her precedents genuinely underdetermine the pending case, what principle would she be adopting with each possible choice? I am less interested in the bottom line than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, which of those cases would best tell them apart, and what further case would settle the matter.
