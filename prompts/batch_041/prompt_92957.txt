Take the 1815 eruption of Tambora and the 'year without a summer' that followed -- failed harvests across Europe and New England in 1816, food riots, families pulling up stakes and heading west, and that cold, gloomy summer by Lake Geneva where Mary Shelly dreamt up Frankenstein. I want you to run the episode through the idea -- call it vital materialism, or 'thing-power', or in actor-network terms the 'actant': anything that makes a difference to a state of affairs whether or not it has intentions -- that non-human things are genuine actors in history rather than mere background conditions. Do it in both directions. First, what does the vocabulary actually buy us here -- in what sense did the volcano 'act', and where does calling it an actor do real explanatory work that plain old 'cause' doesn't? Then the harder half: where does this slide straight back into the environmental determinism its supposed to get us out of -- does making the volcano an agent just re-run the old 'climate made history' story and quietly write out the human decisions (poor relief, grain markets, emigration, empire) that turned a bad harvest into a catastrophe? And finally the sceptic's objection: isn't it a category mistake to hand a volcano agency, and if it is, is the sceptic right, or is there something the sceptic is missing? British English, and no reading lists please.
