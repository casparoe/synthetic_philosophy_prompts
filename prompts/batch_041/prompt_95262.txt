Writing up my view for a seminar and I want a consistancy check before I say it out loud. The view, which I've been calling lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism (no idea if that name is taken):

(1) welfare is the only intrinsic good, and welfare is just pleasure and pain;
(2) pleasure and pain are commensurable — one single scale, so in principle some amount of pleasure can be weighed against some amount of pain;
(3) but there's a threshold of suffering above which no amount of pleasure to anyone can compensate, i.e. severe suffering has lexical priority over pleasure;
(4) below that threshold I'm an ordinary utilitarian, small pains get traded off against bigger pleasures all the time;
(5) the threshold is vague — there's a borderline zone where it's indeterminate whether you've crossed it;
(6) and the badness of suffering just is its intensity and duration, there's no point at which suffering stops being one kind of thing and becomes another.

Something in there is doing double duty and I can't see what. Go through these and tell me which ones actually pull against each other and how bad it is, which pairs only look like they clash, and for the real ones what the cheapest repair is and what it costs. Rank by severity rather than just listing everything.
