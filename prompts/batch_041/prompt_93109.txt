I'm a novelist, about two thirds of the way through a book about the abolitionists, and the Garrison/Douglass falling out is the spine of it. Heres my problem: they argued for years, in public, in print, and neither of them ever wrote down the thing they actually agreed on, because thats not how people work. I want to write that missing document as an appendix to the novel — the joint statement the two of them could have signed if somebody had locked them in a room for a weekend. So draft it for me.

What I need in it. First, a numbered section of claims both men would sign: about the text of the Constitution, about the framers, about what the Union is, about what an abolitionist owes it, and about the concrete stuff — the counting clause, the clause about people held to service, the slave-trade clause, the fact that the document never once uses the word slavery, the Preamble. These have to be claims a Garrisonian and the later Douglass would both put their names to, so nothing vacuous and nothing slanted. Then a disagreements table: one column for each, each position phrased the way that man would phrase it himself, not the way his opponent would.

Then the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling it would actually move one of them, and I want you to find the ones that are real, not the ones that just sound deep. For each crux, give me the case, arguement or consideration that both sides would concede bears on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance. Two things I especially want handled. Whether Garrison's real objection is to the text or to the Union — and if the two of them are partly arguing about different things, say which part of it is verbal. And whether Douglass's reading needs the framers to have meant it, or only needs the words to bear it; he kept saying you read the document as it stands, and I've never been sure how much that move costs him.

Don't put invented quotations in their mouths. I'm not going to defend fake quotes in a book. Paraphrase the positions.

And be honest about what the document can't settle. My suspicion is that one thing neither of them could know is which strategy actually ends slavery, and another is whether a Union that includes slaveholders is worth having at all, which is a value question and not a factual one. If the statement has to concede something that makes one of them look bad, let it. I'd rather have a document with a real wound in it than a polite one. Aim for something I can read aloud at the end of a chapter without wincing.
