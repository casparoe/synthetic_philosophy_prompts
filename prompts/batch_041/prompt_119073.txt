I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about Sleeping Beauty, and I came away unable to tell whether my position is a coherent package or a set of claims that quietly undermine one another. Please act as a philosopher of probability with a specialty in self-locating belief and audit it for me. Work through the probability bookkeeping explicitly before you commit to a verdict; I would much rather have a slow, careful audit than a brisk one.

Here is where I stand.

1. In the standard case — fair coin, one awakening on Heads, two on Tails, amnesia in between — the correct credence in Heads upon waking is one third. I am a thirder, not a halfer.
2. I take the generalization seriously. If Tails produced a hundred awakenings rather than two, my credence in Heads on waking should be one in a hundred and one, not one half. My friend treats this as a reductio; I do not.
3. The only legitimate way for a credence to change is conditionalization on new evidence. I have no patience for updating rules that are not some version of Bayes.
4. I reject the self-sampling assumption — the idea that I should reason as if I were randomly drawn from all the observers in some reference class. Its most celebrated product is the doomsday argument, and I regard that argument as absurd.
5. If, upon waking, I were told that it is Monday, I would move to one half. My friend says this is incoherent, since I knew all along that Monday was one of the possible days on which I could be woken. I cannot see the incoherence, but I also cannot explain why it is not there.

Give me exactly five numbered items, ordered from the most serious problem to the least, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no closing summary. Each item should name the two of my commitments that pull against each other, say whether the clash is genuine or only apparent, and give the cheapest repair together with what that repair costs. Make sure each of my five commitments receives a verdict somewhere across the five items.
