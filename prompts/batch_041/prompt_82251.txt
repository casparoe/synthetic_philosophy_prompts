i'm trying to settle an arguement with a friend about whether credences have to be single numbers. she thinks a range is not just allowed but sometimes mandatory, i think that's indecision dressed up in interval notation, and we keep going in circles because each of us argues from the one case where the other side's view does something ugly. so before i pick a side i want the actual spec. write out the desiderata any theory of credence-under-ambiguity has to meet: the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the structural facts it can't contradict, and the consideration behind each requirement. then tell me which of those are non-negotiable and which are just house rules of one camp, and flag any two that you suspect can't be satisfied together -- that's the part i actually want. the purpose is narrow: i want a standard good enough to tell me when a range is *required* versus merely permitted, and when my friend is just avoiding a number.

some things i want on the list, because they're what we keep tripping on:

- the case where two quantities are independent of each other once you condition on a shared unknown (a coin with an unknown bias, say), and then learning something about one of them makes your range for the other *wider* than it was before you learned anything. is that a requirement, or an artefact of representing the state as an interval?

- the case where the imprecision sits one level up: i'm not unsure about the chance, i'm unsure which of two ranges of chances i'm in, and there's no fair coin to average over. do i get to have a non-convex set of probabilities, and if you tell me to just take its convex hull, what exactly did i lose?

- the decision side: i keep hearing that any rule for acting on a set either collapses back to a sharp number or lets a bundle of bets i'd each accept add up to something i'd reject. if that's a real result, put it on the list as a requirement; if it's a confused complaint, say why.

- whether more evidence is allowed to make things worse, i.e. whether the range is required to shrink as i learn.

- what the theory says the range *is* -- a psychological fact, a summary of the evidence, a set of sharp opinions i haven't chosen between, or a fiction for deciding. i dont see how you pick desiderata without settling that first.

no reading lists please. a table at the end is fine if it helps -- the usual families (sets of probabilities, plain intervals, belief-function-ish things) against the requirements -- but the list is the deliverable.
