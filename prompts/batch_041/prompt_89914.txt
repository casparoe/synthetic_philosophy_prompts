ok, I've been going in circles on a question about letters and I want you to write me an essay that actually takes a side, so please no "both views have their merits" ending.

Here's the thing. Every typeface draws an 'a' differently. Blackletter, Futura, Times, some scrawl on a birthday card — the shapes have almost nothing in common, and yet we all agree they're the same letter. So letterhood isn't in the shape. Fine. Then where is it? Is an 'a' an 'a' because of what it does (readers read it as 'a'), because of its place in a system (it contrasts with 'b', 'd', 'q' and so on), because of where it came from historically, or because of some social convention that could in principle have settled on anything?

I've got three cases I keep chewing on, and I want your criterion to handle all of them instead of hand-waving:

1. A typeface that swaps the glyphs for 'a' and 'e' and changes nothing else. So wherever the letter 'a' belongs, you see a shape that looks exactly like an ordinary 'e'. Read a paragraph in it and after a few lines you stop noticing. Is that swapped glyph an 'a'? Does it matter whether the reader ever notices the swap?

2. Someone takes Times New Roman and relabels every glyph: the shape that used to be 'a' now stands for 'm', and so on. They call it a typeface. Is it? Or is it a cipher wearing a typeface as a disguise, and if so, what's the actual difference?

3. A brand new glyph — a shape that has never been a letter anywhere — gets adopted by some small community for the sound /a/, and their kids grow up calling it 'a'. At some point does it just become an 'a'? If yes, when, and what changed?

I want the criterion to do real work, so don't just say "family resemblance" and stop. And I'd rather you commit: if your view implies the thing in (2) is a genuine typeface, say so and defend it instead of softening it. If you think the whole question is confused and there's no fact of the matter, that's a conclusion too, but you have to argue for it rather than gesture at it.

Also, one thing I definately want addressed: whatever criterion you land on, does it need a human in the loop? Like, is letterhood partly about how a human perceptual system processes the mark, or could a glyph be an 'a' in a world with no readers at all?
