My dad got diagnosed with ALS last year and last month he signed up for this trial where they don't upload you all at once — instead they replace your brain cells with tiny chips slowly, a little patch at a time, over about two years. A chip takes over the job of a clump of neurons, runs alongside them for a bit, and then the neurons get switched off and dissolve. He keeps telling me 'it's still me, its gradual, there's no single moment where I die.' I'm not so sure, but I don't know how to argue with it either. I've been reading forums about this for a year, I'm not a philosopher, but I want something better than vibes. I know there's a whole field of people who've thought hard about this, so use whatever they actually know — just don't hand me a bibliography, I can't look anything up.

What I want is a procedure I can actually run. Like a checklist, questions in order, to decide whether the person after each stage is still the same person as before — or, if it isn't, whether that even matters for the decisions I have to make. I'm the one who signs the consent for each stage and I'm the one who'd have to decide about his advance directive and about a promise he made me, and I usually have like a day to decide, no committee. So: what counts as a good enough answer to each step, where does the checklist stop and just say no, and where does it have to stop and say a person has to decide this because it's a values question and not a fact question. And be honest about where the procedure is going to give a dumb answer.

Things that trip me up and that the procedure has to survive:

1) There's a 72-hour overlap where the chip and the neurons are both live and they have to agree on every signal or the chip shuts itself off. The company says that's the proof it's the same mind. Is that doing any real work, or is it just a light show?

2) At the end, if you pay extra, they'll also keep the original brain alive in a tank for six months 'just in case.' Now there's two of him and both say they're him. My dad's take is the tank one is the real one and the chip one is a copy. Is he right? Does your procedure agree with him?

3) What if the chip version comes out nicer than my dad ever was — less angry, more patient with me. Does 'he's still him' survive that, or is being a better person actually a reason to say it's somebody else?

4) The one I actually dread: doing the whole thing in one shot instead of gradually, which they also offer for people who can't wait.

Run the procedure on all four and show me the steps, not just the answers. At least one of these should come out in a way I won't like — don't soften it. And at the end tell me plainly what the checklist can't do and where I'm just going to have to make a call and live with it.
