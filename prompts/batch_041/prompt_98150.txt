I am preparing a position paper for an internal standards body that is attempting to write a single definition of an autonomous system for use across several of its instruments, and I have run into a puzzle I cannot resolve alone.

The body does two things with the same class of software, and does not see any tension between them. In its procurement and liability arrangements it treats the system as a tool: a clinician or analyst remains the decision-maker, must record assent or dissent before acting on any recommendation, and carries the responsibility when the recommendation is wrong. In its safety and evaluation arrangements it treats the same system as an agent: red-teamers probe whether it will pursue ends it was not given, obscure its own reasoning, resist being switched off, or accumulate resources beyond its task, and the results are reported as facts about the system. The tool vocabulary governs accountability; the agent vocabulary governs testing. When I have asked, nobody has been able to say whether this is a division of labour or a contradiction.

What I want from you is a philosophical reconstruction of the practice: what conception of agency, and of the relation between an agent and the responsibility that attaches to it, the practice would have to rest on in order to be coherent. My own half-formed thought is that the practice only works if agency is treated attributively, as a relation between a system and the decision procedure it is embedded in, rather than as a capacity residing in the system; but I am not confident that this survives the evaluation side, where the reported findings look like claims about the system's dispositions and not about anybody's procedure. So I would like you to do the following.

First, state precisely what the practice presupposes about agency and responsibility, separating what it strictly requires from what it merely fits comfortably with. Second, identify which rival accounts of agency it quietly rules out, and which it leaves untouched, so that I can see where the practice is underdetermined and several theories would license it equally. Third, say whether the two halves of the practice presuppose consistent things or whether the appearance of consistency depends on sliding between an attributive and a capacity-based reading of the same word. Fourth, consider the consequences of adopting each of the two readings consistently across the whole body of instruments: what would have to change in procurement, in liability, and in safety evaluation if agency were taken to be purely attributive, and what would have to change if it were taken to be a capacity of the system alone. Where you think neither reading can be applied consistently, say so, and say what the body would have to give up.

The paper is for readers who are not philosophers, so keep the vocabulary doing work rather than decorating. Assume no access to literature: refer to positions and arguments by description, not by citation, and do not supply references or reading lists. Write in British English, and please reply in British English as well. Aim for something around two thousand words, organised so that each of the four tasks above is separately identifiable.
