I've been arguing with a friend about identity over time and I keep finding my gut verdicts dont obviously fit one story. A ship has its planks replaced one at a time over the years — I say it's still the same ship at the end. But if someone then gathers up all the old planks and reassembles them into a ship, I want to say that reassembled one is the original too, which can't be right since there's only one original. A caterpillar pupates into a butterfly: same animal, obviously. A gold statue gets melted down into a lump: the statue is gone, though the gold obviously isn't — yet I'd also say there's just one thing on the table, not a statue-shaped object and a lump-shaped object occupying the same place. A club replaces all its members over a decade: still the club. An amoeba divides in two: I don't think either daughter is the amoeba, though I can't put my finger on why that's different from the ship. My tentative stab at a principle: something survives just as long as there's continuous causal/spatiotemporal continuity of the underlying stuff, and how that stuff is arranged (statue vs. lump) doesn't matter for identity. Test that against my cases, tell me which verdicts it forces me to give up and why, and say what it predicts for a case I haven't mentioned. And please be honest about what's actually settled versus genuinely contested among people who work on this — I don't want one camp's line (perdurantist or otherwise) sold to me as if it were the consensus.
