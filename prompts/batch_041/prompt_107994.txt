The position: corrigibility is not fundamentally a property of an agent's utility function. It's a property of its decision procedure under uncertainty about its own future. Specifically, an agent is corrigible to the extent that it treats the shutdown action as preference-invariant: its ranking over world histories shouldn't change depending on whether it itself is still running, or on what its future self would prefer. On this view, the shutdown problem is a special case of a more general problem: any agent that assigns positive option value to its own future agency will resist shutdown unless it is absence-indifferent. And absence-indifference is not something you can just bolt on as a terminal value. It has to be built into how the agent aggregates over possible futures, or it will erode under reflection and self-modification. I also think this explains why utility-indifference style fixes feel so fragile: they treat shutdown as just another outcome, but a decision procedure that represents shutdown as an outcome can still assign it low value if the agent's preferences are over states of the world that include its own continued activity.
