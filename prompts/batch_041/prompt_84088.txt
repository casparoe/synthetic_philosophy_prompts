I'm writing a poem to close a seminar on consciousness and the self in Indian and Western thought, and I want it to do philosophical work rather than summarize. Write a villanelle in Schrödinger's voice defending the Vedantic claim that consciousness is numerically one against an interlocutor who insists there are as many minds as bodies. The two refrains should be the pluralist's count and the unity claim, and their returns should track the arithmetical paradox Schrödinger develops in Mind and Matter: any count of consciousnesses presupposes a standpoint outside consciousness, so the many can only appear within the one. Let the closing quatrain bring the refrains together without collapsing them — Schrödinger never fully resolved how the one relates to the apparent many — and let one line register the ethical cost: if the boundaries between minds are appearances, what becomes of the difference between my suffering and yours? Use the image of a single light through many windows, and make it accurate enough that a reader of Mind and Matter recognizes the position while someone who hasn't read it still finds a poem.
