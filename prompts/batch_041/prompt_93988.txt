So a few months ago my flatmates and I started playing Nomic, the game where the rules themselves are what you vote on, and it has eaten my life in a way I did not sign up for. Our game has been frozen for about six weeks over one vote and I've started to suspect the deadlock isn't really about the game.

Here's the mess. Our starting rules include R4: any rule can be amended by a simple majority of players present. And R5: R4 and R5 can only be amended by unanimous consent. Someone moved to amend R5 by simple majority, on the grounds that R4 licenses it. Someone else said no, R5 requires unanimity, so the vote was void, so R5 never changed, so the vote was void... and we go round. Then we made it worse by passing a tidy-up rule saying that any rule whose text contradicts another rule is void to the extent of the contradiction. Now the voiding rule looks like it can void itself, because it was passed by a procedure that another rule says was invalid, so it contradicts that rule, so it voids itself, so it doesn't void anything. One of us wants to just declare the game reset and start over, but that feels like losing on purpose.

I've been reading around and it turns out this is a whole area — Hart's rule of recognition, Kelsen's basic norm, self-amending constitutions, something people call the paradox of self-amendment. The strange thing is that literature reads to me like it's stalled in exactly the same way. Nobody is missing a fact. Everyone agrees on what the rules say and on what happened at the vote. The camps just re-describe the same situation in different words. One side says an amendment is only valid if its authority traces back to a rule that was in force at the time of the vote, so the amendment was never valid and R5 stands. The other says validity is a matter of what the system is like afterwards, all things considered, and there's no contradiction here, just a rule that turned out to be changeable in a way it claimed it wasn't. A third position floating around is that we've simply generated an inconsistent rule set, inconsistent rule sets have no consequences, and the game is over — which nobody accepts but nobody refutes either.

My working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the two main camps don't mean the same thing by rule. One means something like a premise you reason from, the other something like a norm you're bound by, and the disagreement is a verbal one dressed up as a paradox. But I can't make that stick, because the tracing-back camp seems to be saying something substantive about authority and not just about a word.

So tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, substantive, two questions under one heading, or an ill-posed question in the family of can God make a stone so heavy he can't lift it — and what would move it. A distinction, a formal result, or just picking a decision procedure and admitting the rest is philosophy? I'd like reasons, not a survey of positions, and an honest answer about wether my flatmates and I are wasting our time.
