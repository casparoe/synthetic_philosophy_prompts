You are a philosopher of science with expertise in explanation, reduction, and the rhetoric of scientific controversy. I need help turning a half-formed suspicion into a short research proposal — six months, one paper. Please think the idea through carefully first, sketching two or three ways of sharpening it, and then write the proposal itself.

The suspicion concerns the vocabulary of cranes and skyhooks that Dennett introduced in Darwin's Dangerous Idea (1995), together with his contrast between good reductionism and greedy reductionism: the good reductionist explains apparent design without skyhooks, the greedy one tries to explain it without cranes. Both charges have become standard furniture in disputes about sociobiology, genetic explanations of complex human traits, deflationary accounts of consciousness, and adaptationist explanations of religion. My suspicion is that both of them — the charge that an explanation hangs from a skyhook, levelled at those who posit irreducible higher-level facts, and the charge of greedy reductionism, levelled at those who explain too much away — are presented as epistemic verdicts but are in fact pragmatic ones: they register what it is appropriate or useful to say, to whom, and to what end, rather than any error in the explanation. Note that the standard criterion for greediness, namely skipping the intermediate cranes, does not make an explanation false — which is exactly what my suspicion predicts.
