I'm a novelist — third book out next spring, which is neither here nor there — and for the last couple of years I've been collecting the way stories change as they get passed around, partly out of self-interest, since I keep stealing other people's versions of things. Here's a pattern I've noticed and can't find a word for. I have a family recipe that four generations have made and nobody has the original of; every time someone makes it "the way grandma did" it comes out basicly the same, even though if you ask three aunts for the recipe you get three different lists. Same thing with an urban legend that circulates in my city — the details wander (it's a hotel, no it's a car park, it was a freind of a friend), but the load-bearing beats are always identical, and if you tell it wrong people correct you instantly and with total confidence. And a joke my dad tells: everyone tells it slightly differently, but the bad versions die off and the punchline keeps snapping back to the same shape. What strikes me is that the thing isn't being preserved by anybody's memory — nobody can recite it. It's being pulled back into shape from the outside, and the pull seems to have a direction: the surviving versions are the tidier ones, more symmetrical, more morally pointed, more quotable, as if whichever version is easiest to be sure of is the version that wins.

I've read Dawkins on memes and it doesn't quite do it for me, because his picture is a copy that gets copied accurately and then either survives or doesn't, whereas what I'm looking at is a copy that gets copied badly but in a consistent direction, so the error itself is what keeps it stable — the thing sits at the bottom of a bowl and every knock rolls it back to the middle, instead of being a billiard ball that goes wherever it's hit. I genuinely don't know if I'm describing one phenomenon or two, or whether the bowl is in the idea or in us.

So: you're a philosopher of biology or a cognitive anthropologist, take your pick, and please think carefully before answering. Is there an established name for this — for the property of an idea that makes its corrupted copies converge on the same form, and for the shape they converge on? If there is, tell me what it's called and how it differs from the nearest things people usually reach for (memes, natural selection of ideas, telephone-game degradation, the vague thought that memorable things survive). If there isn't, coin me one and define it, and be precise about the edges: is the convergence a matter of the content of the idea or of the minds receiving it, does it need a population or does it happen down a single chain of tellers, does it require the errors to be random or can deliberate distortion do the same work, and what would count as the same phenomenon in another medium entirely — a tune, a slogan, a rumour, a scientific result that everyone half-remembers. I don't want a label, I want the carving, so start by brainstorming three or four candidate concepts and then tell me which one actually fits and exactly where it breaks.
