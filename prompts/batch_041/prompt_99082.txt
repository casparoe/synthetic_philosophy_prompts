I host a small cognitive-science podcast, and the next episode is given over to concept induction; my guest is a researcher who has spent several years defending a deliberately unorthodox reading of Bongard problems. Her thesis, which she will be pressed on at length, is this: Bongard problems do not measure the general capacity to form concepts, but a much narrower capacity to detect what she calls contrastive redundancy between the two panels — the degree to which the positive and negative instances overlap in every respect except the one that matters — and she claims that the felt difficulty of a problem tracks the number of feature-dimensions an observer must explicitly discard, rather than the abstractness or holism of the target concept. She adds that the received difficulty ordering of the classic corpus is partly an artifact of a single designer working in a single visual idiom, so that what presents itself as a scale of conceptual depth is in part a scale of stylistic familiarity.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that do more than let her restate this position. I want the sequence to press exactly where the literature presses, so build in the following, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give:

- at least one question on what she takes a feature to be, and whether her account quietly presupposes a fixed feature vocabulary of the kind that Bongard problems are often said to test the ability to invent;
- at least one on how contrastive redundancy is measured, and whether the measure is observer-relative or can be defined without appeal to a particular perceptual system;
- at least one on the corpus-bias claim: what evidence would establish that the difficulty ordering is stylistic rather than conceptual, and what would refute it;
- at least one that isolates a problem whose solution seems to require inventing a new feature rather than discarding an old one, and asks how her metric can accommodate it;
- a closing question on what her account predicts about machines that solve Bongard problems by brute search, and whether such solutions should count as concept induction at all.

No generic opening questions; the first question should already be doing philosophical work. For each question, indicate in a sentence the direction the follow-up takes if she answers in the way she usually does.
