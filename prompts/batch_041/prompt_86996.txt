I'm in the second year of an MA and I've got a year-long writing fellowship coming up (one big paper, no teaching), and I've gotten weirdly obsessed with the leveling-down objection to prioritarianism. Not the standard "does prioritarianism escape it?" question -- I think it clearly does in the crude statement of the view, where you just never rank a world lower merely for having more inequality in it. What I'm after is whats left over once you grant that, i.e. where the action actually is. My supervisor has a pet line that if you state prioritarianism in terms of improvements rather than total levels -- the currency being gains and losses to people, not how well off anyone ends up -- then the objection doesn't just fail to land, it inverts, and the view gets hit by a mirror-image complaint: a tiny gain to the worst off can license arbitrarily large losses to the better off, so the prioritarian ends up as the one who'll level *up* at any cost. A friend in the seminar thinks that's a red herring, and that the whole leveling-down debate is downstream of population ethics and the person-affecting restriction -- it only has teeth if you've already bought something you maybe shouldn't have. A third person keeps insisting the real fork is ex ante vs ex post: do prioritarianism ex ante under expected value and it collapses toward something like plain expected-welfare maximization, do it ex post and you get a view with no clear story about what to tell people behind a veil or in a gamble. Also floating around in my head: how much priority (the weight parameter, and whether it's even the right kind of thing to be a parameter), whether the view is indifferent to inequalities among the better off, and whether the currency should be welfare at all rather than something like claims or responsibility-adjusted desert. I genuinely don't know which of these is the live one and I'd rather not burn a year on the wrong one.

So: rank the open questions in this little corner for someone in my position and defend the ordering. Which are upstream of which, which are actually tractable for one person with a year and no co-author, which are neglected vs. hashed over to death, and for each, what would move it -- what argument, result, or counterexample over the next year would make me say oh, *that* one was the important one after all. If some of what I've described is just the old debate in new clothes, or is verbal and would dissolve under better wording, say which and why. I don't want a reading list, I want the agenda and the reasoning behind it.
