I wanna test a game theory idea on a real episode. Program equilibrium is the idea that in a one-shot prisoners dilemma, if each player can submit a program that the other can read before either plays, cooperation can be stable: each program can be written to cooperate only if the other program cooperates. Now take the 1914 Christmas truce on the Western Front -- the unofficial ceasefire where British and German soldiers came out of their trenches, swapped cigarettes, and in some places played football. Analyze it as a case of program equilibrium. Who are the players, what counts as a program, and what made the cooperative program readable to the other side? Why did the truce hold in some sectors but not others, and why did the high commands crack down on it? Then tell me where the analogy breaks: soldiers aren't source code, the game wasn't one-shot, and the high command was a third player who could rewrite the programs. Does the episode make program equilibrium look like a useful model or just a toy?
