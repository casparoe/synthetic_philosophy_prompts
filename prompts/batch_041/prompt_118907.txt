Construct a thought experiment that exhibits the unilateralist's curse in as pure a form as you can manage: several agents each independently have the option of performing an action whose value depends on how many of them perform it — beneficial if few do, harmful if many do — with the harm falling on everyone the moment the threshold is crossed, so that no individual's restraint protects anyone. Constraints: a mundane setting (a hospital, a research group, a neighbourhood association); three to five agents; no doomsday devices, no exotic technology, no buttons; full symmetry among the agents, who must be equally informed, equally rational, and identically motivated, so that disagreement, overconfidence, and unequal evidence are doing no work; and the action must carry some private incentive for the agent who takes it, so that the case cannot be dissolved by observing that acting is costless. The bad outcome must be generated by the agents' own expected-value reasoning rather than stipulated — the case is meant to show that the curse is a structural artefact rather than a failure of character or information, so any construction that secures the harm by making an agent reckless or ignorant fails the brief. Keep the scenario to a single paragraph.

Then: (i) run the expected-value calculation explicitly for one agent, and name the structural feature that makes restraint pointless; (ii) say what happens if the agents act in sequence rather than simultaneously, and what happens if each agent's action is independently necessary for the harm — does the curse survive either change, and why; (iii) say whether the case shows the agents are rationally required to adopt a rule of acting only when some collective decision procedure would endorse acting, or merely that they would be better off doing so; and (iv) explain how a critic who holds that each agent should simply act on their own best judgement would attack the construction, and what such a critic must deny about the reasoning in (i).
