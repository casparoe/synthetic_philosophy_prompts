The book says this was Dennett's experiment, and that it proves the brain literally fills in the frames the way a cartoon animator draws in the in-between drawings — it paints in the moving dot and its color — so that what you end up "seeing" is really a story your brain cooked up after the fact. Then it says there are two ways to explain the trick: an Orwellian one, where you really do experience the change and then your memory gets quietly rewritten afterwards, and a Stalinesque one, where the editing happens before it ever reaches your awareness, and the book says Dennett shows the Orwellian version is the correct one — which is why he thinks consciousness is basically an illusion and that we're always living a little bit in the past.
