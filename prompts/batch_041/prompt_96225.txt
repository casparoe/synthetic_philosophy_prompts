Ok so I need someone to talk me out of my own head here.

Five years ago I started converting an old warehouse into a climbing gym. It's mine on paper but my brother put in half the money, my uncle loaned me the rest, I sold my car, and I signed a ten-year lease with a break clause so ugly that leaving in year three would've cost me nearly as much as staying. I did that on purpose. I wanted to make it stupid to quit.

Its... fine. We break even most months. Now a big chain is opening two exits down the highway, and my brother just told me he's out — he wants his money back or he wants me to sell. Everyone I talk to says the same thing: the money's gone, the years are gone, none of that comes back, just look at next year on its own terms and decide. Which sounds right, and also sounds like it's telling me to throw away the only information I've got.

Two things I can't stop chewing on. First, my brothers pulling out feels like the most honest signal I'll ever get about whether this thing actually works, and it's a signal that comes from the past, from what's already happened. So when people say the past is irrelevant to the decision, are they saying his exit doesn't count either? That can't be right. Second — the town has this rule where small businesses get a big tax break once they've operated seven straight years. So years one through five aren't just memories, they're a countdown. Does that change the math, or am I just finding a respectable costume for the thing everyone tells me not to do?

So: is there any version of "I've come too far to stop now" that's actually sound, or is every version of it the same mistake wearing different clothes? And if some versions are sound, how do I tell which one I'm actually doing?
