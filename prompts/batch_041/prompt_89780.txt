Say William James has been brought back and can see our world, but the only thing I'm allowed to ask him about is this one wasp thing I heard about. A naturalist in the eighteen hundreds watched a certain wasp — a digger wasp, the kind that stocks a burrow with paralyzed prey for its larva — and it goes through this fixed routine: dig the burrow, go get the prey, drag it back, leave it at the entrance, go inside to check the burrow is still empty, come back out, drag the prey in. If you move the prey a few inches while the wasp is inside doing its check, it comes out, finds the prey, drags it back to the entrance, and then — checks the burrow again. You can do it forty times. It never just drags the thing in.

The reason I'm asking is that it's been sitting with me for a week. I keep catching myself doing something that feels like the exact same shape: I lock my front door, walk to the car, and turn around and go check it, and if my phone rings while I'm walking back I'll lock it and check it all over again, and it honestly doesn't feel like thinking, it feels like a loop that runs me. What freaks me out is the thought that this is what all of it is — that my deliberating and weighing and deciding is just this, more loops stacked up, and the wasp is the same machine with fewer loops. So: what would James have made of this wasp? Does the story bother him, or does he have somewhere to put it? And do I have any grounds left for saying my going-back-to-check is different in kind from the wasp's, or only in how many steps it takes? I want his actual view, not a pep talk about human dignity.
