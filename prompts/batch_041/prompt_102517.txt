I'm writing a chapter on 'low-impact' AI for my advisor, due Friday, and I keep hitting the same wall. Everyone in this area talks about making an agent 'avoid side effects' as though that were a value-neutral thing you can bolt onto a task specification, and every time I push on it people either say 'well, just don't break anything' or 'that's the precautionary principle with extra steps'. I'd like the spec nailed down before I write another word, because I suspect the notion definately can't be cashed out at all and I'd rather find that out now than at the defense. So put on the hat of someone who works on decision theory and on the philosophy of measurement and also knows this small AI-safety literature, and think carefully before you answer -- maybe start by privately listing the cases that make you squirm, then write it up properly.

What I want is the desiderata list: what must any definition of 'impact' or 'side effect' get right in order to count as a definition of that rather than of something else -- the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results it can't contradict -- with the consideration behind each requirement, and a mark on which ones are non-negotiable and which are just the taste of one camp. Some of the things I'm chewing on: impact has to be relative to a baseline, but the baseline could be the agent's own default policy, or what a human would have done, or the optimal policy, or simply inaction, and inaction is itself an intervention with consequences, so I don't see how to choose one without already having decided what matters; any measure needs a zero point and a scale, and both of those smell like value choices; the intended/side-effect distinction seem to depend on how the goal is described, so the same event flips category when the description changes; whether the agent being shut down, or editing its own weights, counts as an impact; whether the thing has to be invariant under a re-description of the state space, since two agents can carve up the same world differently and I don't want a definition of impact-in-my-model rather than impact; and the fact that whatever the agent optimizes against becomes a target, so must the measure work as a constraint rather than an objective?

Then the part I most want: are there pairs of requirements on your list that can't be jointly satisfied? My suspicion is that 'definable without a full specification of value' collides with 'action-guiding in every situation', but tell me if I've got that wrong, and tell me which single requirement does the most work at ruling out the proposals people actually make (reversibility, keeping the agent's options open, distance from a baseline in some feature space, don't touch anything you weren't told to touch). No reading list or citations please -- I'm not at that stage, and you couldn't check them anyway. And if you think the whole framing is confused, that 'side effect' is just a folk notion we should drop, say so in a line, but still give me the list.
