I'm putting together a collection of satirical pieces for a philosophy-and-physics reading group, and I'd like you to draft one for me. The conceit is a small direct-to-consumer company — call it Demonic Systems Ltd., or invent a name you prefer — that sells a countertop appliance advertised as a genuine working Maxwell's demon: a unit that, in the marketing copy, "separates the fast molecules from the slow ones so you can heat your coffee with the cold you'd otherwise throw away." What I want is that company's help-center FAQ page: roughly 700 to 900 words, formatted as numbered questions with headings and answers, written throughout in the flat, unflappably courteous register of a corporate support site, in which a sequence of increasingly exasperated customer queries ("My kitchen is not getting colder." "Your technician told me the machine 'pays for itself.'" "Is this a perpetual-motion device?") are met with answers that deploy real thermodynamics with mounting elaborateness. The humor has to come from the physics being *right* and the bookkeeping being shameless: the answers should invoke Szilard's one-molecule engine, the cost of acquiring the measurement, Landauer's bound of roughly kT ln 2 per bit erased, and above all the demon's finite memory as the true bottleneck — with the entropy always relocated somewhere convenient (into the demon's forgotten record, into the customer's own coarse-graining, into the observer's ignorance) so that the ledger balances no matter what the customer observes.

The target I want satirized is not the second law but a habit of discourse: the way the information-theoretic resolution of the demon is often retold as a loophole that "keeps the demon alive" rather than as a proof that it cannot win, and the related move of saving the law by attributing entropy to a memory or an observer in a way no experiment could ever settle. So the piece should be a satire *from inside* that position — the support agents should be sincere, even zealous, believers that entropy is fundamentally a fact about what someone knows, and every answer should be internally consistent with that conviction rather than collapsing into a straightforward admission that the machine cannot work. Keep it deadpan: no winking, no narrator breaking in to tell the reader what is absurd, and no false physics presented as fact outside the fiction — the joke should be that the physics is stated correctly and then recruited to absurd ends, so that a thermodynamicist laughs and recognizes the argument rather than wincing at an error.

Please don't quote or cite any real papers, and don't name living researchers or attribute specific published claims to anyone; where the literature is useful, gesture at the ideas in plain terms rather than leaning on titles or dates. A few details you can feel free to seed and riff on: an "Entropy Offset Ledger" through which customers purchase "erasure credits," a warranty clause covering "observer-dependent performance," and a compliance note referencing a fictional "Second Law Compliance Board" that has, the FAQ insists, reviewed the product favorably. By the end, a reader who knows the demon literature should be able to tell exactly which resolution is being sent up, and a reader who doesn't should still find the escalation funny on its own.
