I'm TAing a seminar on consent and I've got a stack of midterms to get through. One question is worth 20 marks and I want to make sure I'm grading it consistently. Here's the question as set, then one student's answer. Can you give me a grade out of 20 and then the comments I should write on it — point by point on the five parts, not a general impression? The five parts are 4 marks each.

Question. Our working conditions for valid consent are: (i) competence, assessed relative to this particular decision rather than the person's general capacity; (ii) information, meaning the consenter understands the facts material to their decision, where a fact counts as material if it bears on the concerns they've actually voiced or that a reasonable person in their position would have, and where it makes no difference whether the consenter was misled by a lie or simply left in the dark; (iii) voluntariness, meaning the consenter's options have not been made worse than the baseline they're entitled to, regardless of whether the threatener is acting lawfully; (iv) scope, meaning a valid consent authorises only what was consented to. For each case below, say whether the consent was valid and whether the other party acted permissibly.

(a) A patient tells her surgeon that what she cares about above all is avoiding a particular nerve injury; the surgeon doesn't mention that the operation carries a 1-in-5000 risk of precisely that injury; she consents and it happens.
(b) A woman has sex with a partner she believes to be infertile because he had a vasectomy; he never had one, and never says so.
(c) A landlord threatens a tenant with an eviction he is legally entitled to bring, in order to get the tenant to accept a lower buyout; the tenant accepts.
(d) A patient consents to surgery on his left knee; the surgeon finds the right knee worse and operates on both.
(e) An 82-year-old with mild dementia who can no longer manage her own finances understands this operation, its risks and its alternatives perfectly well, and consents to it.

Student's answer:
(a) Invalid. The risk was exactly the thing she'd said she cared about, so it's material, and the surgeon's silence means she didn't understand the facts. The size of the risk doesn't matter.
(b) Valid. The information condition is about being deceived, and there was no lie here, just silence about something that isn't part of the act itself; and a reasonable person would have gone ahead anyway. So nothing was done to her consent.
(c) Valid. The eviction was lawful, so the tenant was never worse off than his baseline — the baseline is just whatever the law entitles him to, and the landlord stayed inside it.
(d) Valid for the left knee. The right knee is outside the scope, but the surgeon was acting in the patient's interest and had consent to surgery, so he acted permissibly.
(e) Invalid. Dementia means she fails the competence condition; competence is a threshold about the person, not about the decision.

Grade it out of 20, and give me the specific coments I'd write — what's right, what's wrong, and what a full-mark answer would have said instead.
