Write an essay on the following controversy. Bernard Williams's integrity objection to utilitarianism claims that a utilitarian morality alienates agents from their deepest commitments by requiring them to treat those commitments as mere inputs to an impartial calculus, while his related one thought too many criticism claims that a person who saves his wife from drowning should not have to think that saving her is morally permitted. Consider a variant case: an agent whose deepest identity-conferring commitment is itself impartial beneficence, so that utilitarian morality appears to express rather than undermine her project. In your essay, distinguish the audit model of deliberation, on which the agent must consciously represent the impartial justification as the operative reason at the moment of action, from a background-conditions model, on which impartial justification need only constrain the agent's commitments without being occurrently entertained. Weigh whether Williams's objection refutes utilitarianism as such or only the audit model, and defend a verdict. Present the essay as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion; each point should be a distinct step in the argument, the fifth should state your verdict, and together they should constitute the essay.
