I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy of mind, and I'm trying to fix my position on Dennett's three stances before my prospectus defense, because my committee keeps pressing me on whether I'm a realist about the mental or just dressing up instrumentalism in realist clothing. Rather than another literature summary, what I want is a hard-nosed consistency audit of the specific package I've been defending.

Here it is. (1) I'm a scientific realist about the entities cognitive science posits: beliefs and desires are as real as electrons, not useful fictions. (2) I accept the real-patterns criterion of existence: a pattern is real just when describing a system in its terms compresses the data — lets you predict more with less — and unreal when it is a projection we could have skipped without predictive loss. (3) I think the intentional stance is something we adopt for predictive convenience, and that a system's being an intentional system consists in its being reliably predictable from that stance; there is no further fact about whether it really represents the world. (4) I think there is nevertheless a fact of the matter, independent of which stance happens to be useful to us, about what a creature believes: a creature that tracks the world in the right way has beliefs whether or not anyone finds it convenient to predict it that way. (5) I think the design stance is more basic than the intentional one — you cannot get intentionality without a mechanism whose design explains why the intentional description works, so the physical and design stances have explanatory priority. (6) I think the Game of Life glider is a genuine real pattern while a thermostat's 'belief' that the room is warm is not, and the difference is that the glider earns its keep by compressing the physical dynamics, whereas the thermostat's 'belief' is real only relative to the intentional stance.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict, and how much would each repair cost? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than telling me everything is in tension to some degree; tell me which single commitment is doing the most damage and what I would have to give up to fix it; and if you think the package is incoherent at the root — that I am trying to hold both stance-relativity and a stance-independent fact of the matter about belief — say so plainly instead of humoring me.
