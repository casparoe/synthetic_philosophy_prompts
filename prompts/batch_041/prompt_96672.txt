I work on the product team for a conversational assistant that ships with a name, a voice, and a small invented biography, and for the better part of a year three of us have been arguing in a circle about what we have built. One colleague, a Dennett reader, holds that the question of whether it really believes or cares is the wrong question: a belief just is a robust predictive pattern, and if the pattern does the work then it is real, so the anxiety about counterfeit people is misplaced. A second holds that this is precisely the trick Dennett was warning against — a counterfeit is something that passes the test without being the thing, and the fact that a stance pays off tells us nothing about whether there is anything there to be right or wrong about. A third only wants to know why we gave it a name. We agree on the architecture, we agree on the benchmarks, we agree that users form attachments; when we rewrote the persona in its system prompt, its self-reports about its own inner life changed along with it, and we cannot agree on whether that settles the matter or settles nothing. What we cannot agree on is what we are disagreeing about. Is this a verbal dispute over the words belief and person? A disagreement about what counts as evidence — behavioral robustness versus what is happening inside? A concealed empirical question that better interpretability would settle? Two questions under one name, one metaphysical about what is there and one normative about what we should be willing to build and how we should treat it? Or bedrock, the problem of other minds with the polarity reversed, where seeming and being cannot be pulled apart for mindedness at all and neither side can argue for its starting point? I have a hypothesis, but I would rather you test it than adopt it: that the metaphysical half is verbal and all the heat is coming from the normative half. Give me a diagnosis, and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress here — a distinction, an experiment, a result from interpretability, or a change of question — or whether I should expect none.
