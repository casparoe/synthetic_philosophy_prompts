Write a short story set in a fictional but plausible society about a historian who has been commissioned to produce the definitive account of how that society was founded. Almost nothing survives from the founding period except two things: a long, morally charged narrative written years afterward by a founder who had every reason to make the outcome look inevitable and just, and a bare ledger of dated transactions with no causal or evaluative commentary. The commission asks for an account faithful to what actually happened — one meant to settle the question for good.

The story should stay inside the historian's working life rather than summarize her conclusions. Show her reading the ledger against the narrative and the narrative against the ledger, and show her realizing that the ledger is compatible with several incompatible stories, each of which carries a different verdict about who was right. Then let her discover that the ledger's own categories — the words it uses to classify each transaction — are themselves contested, so that even the supposedly raw record is already an interpretation, and there is no neutral ground from which to arbitrate. Bring in the pressures of the present: a patron who wants a usable past, a public that wants a founding it can be proud of, her own sympathies. Let the story turn on one concrete decision about how to render a single disputed episode, and let the ending resist a clean resolution.

Two constraints. First, dramatize rather than explain: the story should make a reader feel the pull between the ideal of recovering the past as it actually was and the suspicion that any narrative imports a shape, a moral, and a point of view that the evidence does not contain — and that the very demand for a definitive account is itself a product of the present. Do not name or summarize the philosophical positions, and if the story takes a side, let it do so through what the historian does rather than what she announces. Second, avoid closing on a paragraph of commentary. Aim for roughly 1,500 words.
