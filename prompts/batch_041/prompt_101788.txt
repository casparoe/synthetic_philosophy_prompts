Okay so I've gotten into two things at once and I want to smash them together. I've been reading up on information cascades — you know, the herding thing where after a couple of people act, everyone downstream stops weighing their own evidence and just copies what the people ahead of them did, and the strange part is the cascade doesn't have to be right, and it can shatter the moment a little genuine public information shows up. Separately I've fallen hard for pantoums, that form that came to French poetry from the Malay pantun, where lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next, and the last stanza ties back into the first, so the whole poem is a chain where every step is built out of the previous step's second half. It hit me that a pantoum is basically a cascade in verse: each stanza only "knows" what the last one handed it. So write me one — four or five quatrains, free verse is fine, but the repeated lines should come back more or less word for word so I can hear the chain.

Set it somewhere small and specific: a fishing village where the sea has pulled back off the reef, which one old woman recognizes as the wrong kind of quiet, and the poem moves house to house as person after person sees the same wrong sea but looks instead at what the neighbor is doing — the boat still tied up, the kettle still on — and stays. I'd love it if the recurring lines are the public, observable "everyone's acting normal" record, so that a line which started out as somebody's private unease, when it comes back around two stanzas later, has been flattened into mere crowd-noise. And I know the form forces every line to return, which is kind of a lie about a real cascade, since in a real one your private signal just gets dropped and never comes back — so let that friction show, or at least don't pretend its not there. Put in one line where someone almost says the true thing out loud and doesn't, and let the final stanza closing the loop onto the first line feel like the cascade sealing itself shut. Someone who studies herding should be able to read it without wincing — the fragility, the way two or three early movers can outweigh a dozen private signals, the way the outcome can be flatly wrong.

And since I might be wrong about something: tell me where the poem takes liberties with the cascade model or leans on a contested reading of it, and which piece of the theory — if it turned out to be false, say if real-world cascades turned out to be far less fragile than the textbook treatment suggests — would force you to rewrite the poem rather than just tweak a line.
