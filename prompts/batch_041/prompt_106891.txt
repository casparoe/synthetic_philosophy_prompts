I am a novelist writing a near-future thriller. My protagonist is a government negotiator who will face several acausal threats over the course of the book. An acausal threat, as I am using the term, is a threat whose execution does not depend on any causal channel between threatener and threatened: for example, a promise to punish simulations or copies of her, or other instances of her, unless she complies. The threatener may be a single agent, a predictor, or a coalition of instances. She also suspects that the stated demand is a surrogate goal, meaning a proxy adopted for some deeper objective rather than the objective itself.

You are an expert in decision theory, acausal trade, game theory, and narrative plausibility. Think carefully, and begin by brainstorming the distinctions that matter here: causal versus evidential decision theory, precommitment, credible threat versus acausal correlation, acausal trade as cooperation or threat based on correlation between decision procedures rather than causal contact, and surrogate versus terminal goals. Then write a decision procedure my protagonist can actually run. It should be a checklist or decision tree in words: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a refusal, where it stops with compliance, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is one of values rather than facts. She has only a few hours, no way to contact the threatener, and her recommendation must be signed off by a minister. Do not cite sources or give a reading list; define any term you use that a non-specialist would not know.

Apply the procedure to two cases. First, the threatener promises to instantiate and punish a million copies of her unless she destroys a research programme. Second, the threatener promises to punish a third party she cannot identify or verify unless she leaks a document. Include one case where the procedure gives an answer she will not like, and say clearly what the procedure cannot do. Write in British English. At the end, state how confident you are in each major recommendation and single out the point you are least sure of.
