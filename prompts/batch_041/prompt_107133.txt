The claim: human extinction is bad in a way that goes beyond the deaths and suffering, because the value of our present projects and achievements depends on there being a human future — if we knew we were the last generation, writing a book or curing a disease would lose a kind of point. So it's about us now, not just about future people's welfare.

What would count against it? I want the actual defeaters ranked by how decisive they'd be, not a survey of what critics say. Specifically: (a) people who sincerely believe the world ends soon, or that they're the end of their line, and still value things — does that bite, or does the argument just wave it off by saying it's about the fact and not the belief? Because if it needs to be remembered, that's a stronger and shakier claim.

Also: what does this predict that a plain extinction-is-bad-because-of-the-deaths view doesn't — is there a case where they actually come apart?
