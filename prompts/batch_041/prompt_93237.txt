A metaphysics reading group I sit in on has spent several sessions circling the special composition question — when, if ever, do some things compose a further thing, so that there is some one object of which they are the parts? One member proposes to settle it by analogy with a wave. A wave, he says, is nothing over and above the water: no extra entity arrives at the shore beyond the molecules that move there. Yet we speak the truth when we say the wave is three feet high, that it broke early, that it is the same wave that passed the buoy an hour ago even though none of its original molecules remain. Composition is like that, he concludes: 'composite objects' are patterns we trace in what is really just an arrangement of simples, not further things that come into being when the arrangement is right. Hence the special composition question has no fact of the matter to answer, and the standing quarrel between the universalist (everything composes) and the nihilist (nothing does) is a dispute over a distinction without a difference.

I want an audit of the analogy, not a verdict on the debate. Set out the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of the molecules, of the medium, of the wave itself, of the wave's height, of its identity across the buoy, of its interference with a second wave. Then say where the mapping holds and where it breaks, and which of the breaks matter for the use to which the analogy is being put. I am especially concerned with what the wave picture quietly imports that the target may lack: a wave is a form imposed on a substrate that is itself already a composite, it is defined by energy in transit rather than by any intrinsic unity, and the 'real but derivative' status we readily grant it is not obviously the same as the nihilist's 'not a thing at all.' Note also that waves interfere and erode cliffs — that they do causal work — and ask what that does to the inference from 'a wave is not an extra entity' to 'there are no composite objects.' Finally: does his conclusion — no fact of the matter about when composition occurs — survive the disanalogies, or does the analogy support only the weaker claim that composites are real but thin? And if there is a better analogy for the restricted compositionist's position, name it and say why it does the work the wave does not.
