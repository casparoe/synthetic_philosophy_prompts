I've been chewing on the phrase "Western values" for a couple of years now, and my settled view is that it doesn't pick out a coherent category at all — it's a rhetorical bundling device, a way of claiming credit for a grab-bag of things (rule of law, individual rights, free inquiry, democracy, maybe capitalism, maybe secularism) that don't reliably travel together and don't share a single source. Tell me what would count against that.

Background on where I'm coming from: I spent most of my working life as a biologist, so the distinction I keep reaching for is homology versus convergence. If Western values are a real lineage — one package inherited from a common ancestor, whether you want to call that ancestor Greece, Rome, the church, the Enlightenment, or some seperate thread — then the category has something like common descent going for it. If instead they're convergent adaptations, seperate societies arriving at similar arrangements because they're all solving similar coordination problems, then the bundle isn't distinctive to the West and my skepticism looks right. So one thing that would genuinely move me is a common-garden style thought experiment, or better, actual history: strip the supposed ancestral inputs and ask whether the cluster still shows up. If it reliably does, that's a defeater for me. If pulling the inputs reliably pulls the cluster, I should probably fold.

The other thing I want is some sense of whether it's even a bundle. Do the members co-occur, or does every real case come with an asterisk — this country has the rights but not the democracy, that one the free inquiry but not the rule of law? And I'm aware there's a middle position I may be steamrolling: maybe the category is a family-resemblance cluster with a prototype rather than either a lineage or a mere slogan, in which case "incoherent" is too strong and I'd need to say what kind of coherence I was denying. Sort that out for me.

So: which observations, arguments, historical cases, or conceptual results count against my view, ranked from decisive to merely awkward? What do the people who think the category is real concede as counting against them, and what should I, on my side, concede? And be honest about whether my view risks anything at all or whether I've insulated it by definition — if I have, tell me whether that's a problem for a claim of this kind.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble, no restating what I asked.
