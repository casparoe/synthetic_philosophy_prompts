I'm in a second-year seminar on Buddhist philosophy and we've spent the last two weeks on whether Buddhist ethics is really a form of consequentialism — the reading where right action just means action that reduces suffering, and karma is a long-run causal feedback system that eventually hands your intentions back to you. I mostly buy it. But my roommate, who does formal logic and has no patience for any of this, helped me build an argument against the bodhisattva ideal and now I can't find the exit. Here it is, with the steps numbered so you can point at one.

(1) The bodhisattva vows to keep working, life after life, until every sentient being has been freed from suffering — she postpones her own final liberation until the last one gets there. (2) The number of sentient beings is infinite: the cosmos is beginningless, and beings are beyond counting. (3) If the number is infinite, and any finite stretch of work frees only finitely many beings, then at every moment the set of unliberated beings is still infinite, so the task is never finished. (4) So the vow, taken at face value, commits her to something that can never be completed, which means her own liberation is postponed forever — the opposite of what a Buddhist is supposed to be aiming at. (5) And if we judge her work the way a consequentialist judges any work, by how much of the good it secures, then a finite amount of work against an infinite task secures a share of zero. (6) A share of zero makes no difference to the total, and if it makes no difference to the total, her efforts are worth exactly what doing nothing is worth. (7) So either the vow is unfulfillable, and the ideal is incoherent, or on the consequentialist reading her efforts are worthless, and the ideal is pointless. (8) Neither of those can be right, so something has to give.

I'd rather keep (1), and I'd rather keep the thought that compassion is actually obligatory — that idea is honestly why I signed up for this class. If the price is dropping the consequentialist reading of Buddhist ethics, I'll pay it, but I want to know what I'm buying and whether I'm paying for the right reason. So: which step do I reject, why did that step look so natural that I walked right past it, and what does each of the alternatives cost me? Two more things. Some people in class claim the word usually translated as "countless" might not mean actually infinite, and I can't tell if that's a real escape hatch or a distraction — if it matters, tell me why. And please say how confident you are in your diagnosis, and single out the point in your answer you're least sure of.
