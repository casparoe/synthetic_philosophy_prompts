I have just come out of a long argument with a friend about Kelly betting, and I want to settle it properly by taking it to someone who has thought harder about it than either of us. My friend's line was that any bet with a positive edge should be taken for as much as you can stake — offered even money on a coin he puts at 60/40, he would put up everything he owns. My guest on the podcast next week defends the opposite view under the name geometric rationality: the thing to maximize, she says, is not expected value but the growth rate of your wealth over time, which is why the right stake on that coin is a fifth of your bankroll and not all of it, and why refusing to bet big is arithmetic rather than risk aversion — the average over the ensemble of possible worlds is not the same as the average over the one life you actually live. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that get past letting her restate this. I want at least: one that forces her to say whether geometric rationality is anything more than expected-utility maximization with log wealth, and what actually turns on the difference; one about the single-shot bet, where there is no long run for a growth rate to be a fact about; one about which quantity is supposed to be doing the compounding, and whether she has any principled reason to pick wealth rather than the logarithm of wealth as the thing whose time average matters; one that grants her the long-run argument and then asks what she tells the person offered the 60/40 coin exactly once; and one about whether the theory generalizes beyond repeated multiplicative stakes — to irreversible losses, to things that cannot be rebought, to lives. For each of these, give me the reply she is most likely to make and the follow-up that reply invites, and close with the question most likely to make her commit to the strongest version of the view rather than the safest one.
