I'm a novelist, mostly fiction, but I've got a fellowship application due and I want to pitch a short nonfiction research proposal off the back of the book I'm writing. Heres the half-formed thing I keep circling: in my novel the character who gets cancelled isn't actually deprived of anything — no job lost, no money gone, nobody's even really yelling at her. What happens is she stops being addressed. People stop quoting her, her testimony stops counting, she's in the room but not in the conversation. And it struck me that we argue endlessly about cancel culture *as punishment* — is it proportionate, does it need due process, is it cruel — when maybe the thing isn't punishment at all. Maybe it's closer to what some old legal systems called civil death, or outlawry: not a penalty for a wrong, but removal from the community of address, the loss of standing to be heard at all. So the question I want to put: is deplatforming / social sanction better modeled as punishment, or as loss of social standing — and does that distinction change what we owe the sanctioned person?

Turn this into a two-page proposal.
