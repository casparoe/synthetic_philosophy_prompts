ok, context: im a novelist, about 90 pages into a book whose narrator is a logician, and i need a poem she writes for herself late at night after her sister dies. so the poem is doing double duty.

the thing shes stuck on is what i think is called the knowability paradox, and since im not a logician let me say it back to you so you can fix it in the poem if ive got it wrong. some truth is true and nobody knows it. that whole package -- the truth, plus the fact that its unknown -- is itself true. now if every truth can be known, then this one can be known too. but you cant know "p and nobody knows that p", because knowing it means knowing both halves, and the two halves eat each other: if you know p then you dont get to also know that nobody knows it. so that truth isnt knowable, which breaks the rule that all truths are. unless you take the other exit, which is that there are no unknown truths at all -- everything is already known. and thats the horrible part: the rule that says everything can be known turns into the rule that everything is known. i think thats right, but check me.

i want a villanelle, nineteen lines, two refrains. one refrain is the everything-can-be-known principle, the other is the everything-is-known it collapses into. let them seperate, swap places down the tercets the way the arguement swaps them, and then in the last two lines let them stand side by side so the reader sees they were the same sentence the whole time, just wearing different coats. i also want that moorean thing in there somewhere, the "p but nobody knows it" bit -- maybe thats the hinge the final stanza turns on. the rhyme should sound tired, not chirpy.

and the grief has to be real, not decoration. she keeps listing the truths that went into the ground with her sister: what the last night was actually like for her, what she was afraid of, whether she knew. the paradox is cold and she cant stop noticing that both doors say the same thing -- nothing is ever lost because everything can be known, nothing is ever lost because it was already known -- and she cant tell if thats comfort or the same trick twice. dont let the poem resolve that. no famous philosophers named, she wouldnt. someone who knows the paradox should be able to point at where the proof is hiding, and someone who doesnt should just think its a good poem about a person who cant sleep.
