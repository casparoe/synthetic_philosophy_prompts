I'm a science writer, mid-feature on free will, and I want to come at it sideways — through Christopher Nolan's Memento rather than another reheated lap around Libet. I've been chewing on this alot, so tell me if the reading holds up. The setup I'm working from: Libet has people watch a fast clock and report the instant they feel the urge to flick a wrist, and the readiness potential in the EEG shows up a few hundred milliseconds before the reported urge, which itself beats the twitch by a couple hundred. There's also a revisionist camp now saying the readiness potential might not be a decision signal at all, just slow background noise drifting up till it crosses some threshold — which would deflate the whole your-brain-decides-before-you-do line rather than confirm it.

Now Leonard Shelby. He can't lay down new memories, so he outsources his intentions to tattoos and Polaroids, and the film runs backward, so we get the act before the reason — which is exactly the temporal structure the Libet data is supposed to reveal. Where does that map cleanly and where does the film cheat? The thing I keep circling is that Leonard isn't a puppet of some pre-conscious decision; he's a guy who deliberately writes his own reasons after the fact, and the Sammy Jankis business — the man who couldn't be conditioned, which is a different lesion from Leonards — makes it worse. So is Memento an argument against the Libet reading rather than for it? Which scenes should I lean on, and what would a working neuroscientist say the film gets right that the lab can't show? Don't send me a reading list, I've got the papers.
