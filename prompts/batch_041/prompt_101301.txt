I'm a third-year student taking a metaethics module and I've gotten weirdly fixated on the internal/external reasons distinction — the one usually credited to Bernard Williams — but not the standard version. The standard version I get: a reason for you has to hook onto something already in your subjective motivational set, otherwise it's only an external reason, which is really just advice or pressure in disguise. What's eating me is a distinction our seminar's guest speaker floated and then left without defending properly. She said we're conflating two completely different senses of internal: reasons that are internal to a person (the Williams kind) and reasons that are internal to a practice or a role — the norms you're bound by just in virtue of being a doctor, or a promiser, or a judge, even when nothing in your motivational set lines up. She called the second kind role-internal reasons and insisted they're not reducable to the first, because you can be bound by them while actively resenting them. My friend thinks that's nonsense and that every role-internal reason secretly smuggles in a desire (to be a good doctor, to keep your self-image intact, whatever), so it collapses back into the Williams picture. I half see her point, but I also think the doctor who's stopped caring still owes her patient something, and that owing doesn't feel like a desire at all.

Could you compare these two notions of internality for me — agent-internal reasons in the Williams style versus role- or practice-internal reasons — and tell me which one you think is doing the real work, or whether the second really does collapse into the first? I'd especially like you to test the collapse on a hard case: a lawyer who's convinced her client is guilty, has no desire to help him, and yet seems bound by something (the duty of zealous representation, or whatever) that isn't just her wanting to keep her licence. And maybe say what the two views actually disagree about that would change how someone acts, rather than it being a verbal dispute over the word internal. Please write it as continuous prose — no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, just the way you'd write an email.
