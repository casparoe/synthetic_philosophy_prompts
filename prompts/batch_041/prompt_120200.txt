An essay I recently read summarizes the Hobsbawm-Anderson position on nationalism like this: 'Hobsbawm and Anderson showed that nations are invented rather than discovered. Hobsbawm demonstrated that the paraphernalia of nationhood -- anthems, flags, "traditional" costumes, commemorative ceremonies -- was manufactured in the nineteenth century by elites who needed to bind the masses to the new states; his criterion is datability, since a practice that can be traced to a single founding moment is exposed as spurious, while one that cannot be dated is a genuine survival. Anderson explained why people are willing to die for a fabrication: the nation is an imagined community, which is to say that it exists only in the imagination, a fiction believed in much as God was once believed in. Between them the two books prove that national identity is an illusion, which is why nationalism's persistence today, in Scotland, Catalonia, and elsewhere, shows how thoroughly the original manipulation worked.'

How much of that is fair? I would also like your view on whether the passage's two central claims -- that national traditions are manufactured and that nations are modern -- are one thesis or two that can come apart, since the passage treats them as a single result.
