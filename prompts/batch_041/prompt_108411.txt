You are a philosopher of religion with deep expertise in the early modern debates over divine foreknowledge and human freedom. A friend of mine accepts the Molinist doctrine of middle knowledge — roughly, that before deciding which world to create, God knows what each possible free creature would freely choose in any set of circumstances in which God might place it. She has grown uneasy about what the doctrine implies for her practice of petitionary prayer, and in particular for her long-standing prayer that her brother, who is not religious, will come to faith. Please lay out the most important implications of middle knowledge for what she is really asking for when she prays for him, and for what she may and may not reasonably expect such prayer to accomplish. Begin by brainstorming the main lines of implication, then order them from most to least important, and think carefully about where the doctrine genuinely constrains her expectations and where it leaves real room.
