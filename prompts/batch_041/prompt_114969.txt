I have been keeping a list of the verdicts on supertasks — tasks with infinitely many steps compressed into a finite time — of a philosopher of mathematics I correspond with. The lamp switched on and off at 1/2, 3/4, 7/8... of the interval, asked for its state at the end: no fact of the matter. The vase into which ten balls go at each step and from which the lowest-numbered ball is removed, so that ball n is gone by step n: at the end it is empty, and determinately so. A ball that covers half the remaining distance to a wall at each step: it is at the wall. A specification that adds a further instruction to be carried out once the infinitely many steps are complete: the specification is incoherent, rather than its outcome indeterminate. I would like her verdict on a case of my own. A lamp is on exactly when the angle of its dial exceeds 45 degrees. The dial turns continuously, and at 1/2, 3/4, 7/8... of the interval it stands at 46, 45.5, 45.33... degrees, so that at the end of the interval it stands at exactly 45 degrees — the value that no step attains. Is the lamp on, off, or neither at the end, and on what grounds? I care less about the verdict than about the rule she is running: which rules fit her four earlier verdicts equally well, where my new case pulls them apart, which one I should expect her to be running and why, and what further case would best settle the matter — would her verdict on the same dial turned to exactly 45 degrees in finitely many steps be decisive, or is there a better case?
