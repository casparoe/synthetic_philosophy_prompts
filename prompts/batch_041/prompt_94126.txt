ok so I have to bring a position paper to my phil of language reading group and I want to make sure my commitments actually hang together before I get shredded. Here's roughly where I've landed on the gavagai thing:

1. The indeterminacy is real. Given every bit of behavioral evidence there could ever be, more than one translation manual fits the natives, and those manuals disagree about what gavagai picks out — rabbit, undetached rabbit parts, rabbit stage, take your pick.
2. What makes this different from ordinary underdetermination in physics (which I also believe in, and which obviously doesn't mean there's no fact about electrons) is that here there is genuinely no further fact that could settle it. The manuals are empirically equivalent and that's the end of the story.
3. I'm not a behaviorist, though. I think there are real facts about what a speaker means, and a good semantics would be a real theory of the speaker's internal states. It's just that no radical translator could ever read those facts off the evidence.
4. Reference is inscrutable even given a perfect manual, since you can always swap in a proxy function and keep the truth value of every sentence the same.
5. Truth is a different matter. Sentences are objectively true or false and none of the above touches that. If we both accept a sentence and it's true, it's true, period.
6. And I think all of this generalizes to our own language. The home language doesn't get a free pass just because we happen to be standing inside it.
7. Oh, and I'm an instrumentalist about belief and desire. Attitude talk is indispensable for predicting people, but I don't think it carves nature at the joints.
8. Also I think the cleanest way to model a translator is Bayesian — you start with a prior over reference assignments and the evidence just never moves you off it. So the indeterminacy is really a fact about the prior, not about the world.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each real clash? I'd definately rather get them ranked by severity than a flat list telling me they're all in tension. For the real ones, what's the cheapest thing to drop, and what does dropping it cost me? And if there's a single revision that would clean up the most, tell me that too, because I have ten minutes and can't rewrite everything.
