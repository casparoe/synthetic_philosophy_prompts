My aunt filled out one of those advance directive forms a couple years ago, right after her diagnosis, back when she was still sharp as anything. She named me the one to make the calls, so Im the one getting yelled at now.

Here's the part I can't get my head around. The form isn't just a checklist of medical stuff. She wrote her own paragraph at the top, and her lawyer says it's valid. Roughly: "I know that later on I may seem calm, even happy. I want it on record that this happiness is not me, it's the illness wearing my face. Don't let my contentment overrule the instructions below, and don't take my later objections seriously, because they won't be mine."

She's in the later stages now. And honestly? She's lovely. She doesn't remember writing any of it. She says she's happy, she likes her aide, she's painting again, and she has zero interest in the "do everything, tube, hospital, the works" plan she insisted on back then. She mostly wants to sit in the garden and eat cake. When the nurse brought up the feeding tube she waved it off and said she'd rather be let go.

My cousin says the document is her, the real her, and that I'd be betraying her if I ignored it. My gut says the woman in the garden is also her, and it's creepy to let a version of her who's basically gone boss around the version who's actually sitting in front of me.

The thing that really gets me is that she wrote it so that nothing she said later could count against it. If that works, the earlier her can never lose, no matter what happens to her. Is that fair? And who am I actually taking orders from here?
