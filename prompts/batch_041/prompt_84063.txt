I somehow got talked into giving the keynote at the annual fundraising dinner for the aid org I've been involved with for years, it's in three weeks, and I'm stuck. Last week my friend Priya, who does political philosophy, spent about two hours over dinner taking apart how I think about all this, and honestly I think she won. Her line was that the whole debate about how much the rich owe the poor — the drowning-child argument, giving away some percentage of your income, all of it — is asking the wrong question, because it still leaves the rich as the ones deciding. Charity, on her view, isn't just too small; it's the wrong relation, because it puts the poor in the position of depending on the goodwill of people who can cut them off whenever they feel like it. What's actually owed isn't a bigger check, it's the poor getting to set the terms. I can't shake it. Wich is a problem, because I have to stand up in a room where half the people are donors who've given us millions and want to hear that they're heroes, and the other half are our field staff from the countries where we work, who will hear every drop of condescension if there's any in there. Write me the speech. It has to raise money — I can't not do that, the clinic runs on it — but it can't be a lie, and it can't turn into a seminar on political philosophy. She'll be in the audience and she'll know instantly if I chicken out. Six minutes or so.
