I've been reading around the whole "was the Enlightenment the source of progress or the engine of domination" fight for a seminar, and I'm struck by how little the two camps actually engage with each other. One side (the Pinker-ish, progress-studies crowd) says the ideals of reason, science and universal rights are basically why we got abolition, the end of judicial torture, the long decline in violence — take those ideals away and you get the twentieth century's worst. The other side (the Adorno-and-Horkheimer lineage, plus a lot of postcolonial writing) says the very same ideals were the alibi: a "universal" reason that licensed civilizing missions, "rights of man" that coexisted quite happily with slave empires, a science that ranked the races. Both sides cite real history and real examples. And the arguement just goes nowhere — everyone retreats to their favorite cases and nobody's position shifts an inch.

What I want from you isn't a verdict on who's right but a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a plain empirical dispute about causes — did Enlightenment ideas cause the progress, or did economic and imperial interests cause both the ideas and the outcomes? Is it two groups answering different questions under one label, one asking what made modernity materially better and the other asking what made it morally self-satisfied? Or is it a fight about what "the Enlightenment" even picks out, so that they aren't really talking about the same object at all? Here's my tentative hypothesis, and I'd like you to test it rather than just nod along: that the whole thing turns on an equivocation between the Enlightenment as a body of doctrines and "the Enlightenment" as a label we stick on a period after the fact, and that once you prise those apart, half the disagreement dissolves and the other half becomes a fairly ordinary historical question about which ideas did what work, where. If that's too neat, say why — and either way, tell me what would actually move this forward: a distinction, a kind of evidence, or a reason to think it's not the kind of question that can be settled at all.
