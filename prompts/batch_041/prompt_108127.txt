ok so I fell down a rabbit hole last week on that CEO experiment from experimental philosophy. The setup: a VP pitches a program that will bump profits and also harm the environment. The CEO says he doesnt care about the environment at all, he just wants max profit. Program goes ahead, environment gets harmed, and when you ask people whether he intentionally harmed the environment, 82% say yes. Then you swap one word so the program helps the environment instead -- CEO still says he doesn't care about the environment, still just wants the money -- and now only 23% say he intentionally helped it. Same guy, same indifference, same knowledge of the side effect, only the good/bad word changed, and the verdict flips. Its been bugging me for days.

Write me a villanelle about it. Make the two refrains the two verdicts -- one that says he meant it, one that says he only ever meant the money -- as close to identical as you can get them, so they keep coming back and land right next to each other at the end and you feel both of them be true about the same guy at once. Somewhere in the middle I want the same line twice with only the help/harm word swapped, so a reader can see the evidence never budged. Dont let it turn into a lecture about the folk being irrational; leave it open whether people are confused or whether theyre tracking something the philosophers forgot to ask about. Someone whos read the papers should think the poem is fair to the experiment.
