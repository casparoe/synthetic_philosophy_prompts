Ok so here's a thing I've been chewing on and can't find a word for. Everyone has a word for the person who does more than they had to — the guy who jumps in the river, the coworker who stays late to fix your mess. Going above and beyond. I'm pretty sure there's even a proper philosophy word for it that I can never remember (super-something?). Fine.

But I can't find the word for the mirror image. My old landlord raised the rent by the maximum the law allowed every single year, never fixed the radiator, and sent back my deposit minus every penny he could justify. He broke no rule. Or: a company that follows the letter of an environmental law while visibly doing the least it possibly can. Or the person who takes the last seat on a crowded bus and then very slowly puts their bag away while an old man stands there. Nothing illegal, nothing forbidden — but "nothing wrong" feels like a lie. And it's not the same as just being a boring neutral choice like vanilla vs chocolate. We hold it against them, we just don't have a charge to file.

Is there a name for that second thing — the act you're allowed to do but that we still quietly hold against you? And where exactly is the line between "within your rights and that's the end of it" and "within your rights but come on"? I don't just want a label, I want to know what the thing actually is and where it stops being it. I'm also honestly not sure whether this is really the opposite of going above and beyond, or whether it's just the absence of it — those feel like different claims and I can't tell which one is right.

I never studied any of this so please don't just hand me a name and walk off. Lay it out as a JSON object with these fields:

- "has_name": true or false — does an established term already exist for this?
- "established_terms": array, each item an object with "term", "what_it_means", "how_it_fits_my_cases", "where_it_falls_short"
- "best_fit": the single nearest established concept, or "none"
- "coined_term": if nothing fits, an object with "term" and "one_sentence_definition"; otherwise null
- "precise_definition": your best stab at defining the phenomenon I described, in a couple of sentences
- "not_this": array of things it's easily confused with, each with a one-line difference
- "boundary_cases": array of short cases, each with "case", "verdict" (in / out / borderline), "why"
- "opposites_verdict": short answer to my last question — is it the true opposite of above-and-beyond, or just its absence, and why
