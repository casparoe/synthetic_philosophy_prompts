Quick recap so we're on the same page: KS picks the unique feasible payoff point sitting on the straight line from the disagreement point to the ideal point, where each player's ideal is their max feasible payoff given the other player's, and it's the solution characterized by Pareto efficiency, symmetry, scale invariance, and individual monotonicity (the monotonicity axiom is what does the work separating it from Nash, which swaps that out for IIA).

So map the episode onto it. Who are the two players, what's the disagreement point (status quo, or war, or something both sides were actively trying to shift), what does the feasible set actually look like once you account for Sinai being basically indivisible and the settlements-versus-normalization trade, and where does KS land relative to where the deal actually landed -- was the real split closer to the KS point, the Nash point, or neither?

Then the part I actually care about: where does the model break, and be honest about it. It's three-plus players not two (the Palestinians got deferred, not included), the feasible set looks non-convex, the disagreement point was contested and being moved by both sides, and Israel's and Egypt's ideals were nowhere near symmetric. Does KS survive any of that, or does the case show the axioms -- individual monotonicity especially -- were carrying more weight than they can bear? Don't need citations, just the analysis.
