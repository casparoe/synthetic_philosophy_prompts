Okay, so I'm a novelist and there's a pattern I keep running into and I dont know what to call it. Every time I try to look it up I get dumped into the same old argument about whether anyone could ever have done otherwise, and that isn't quite what I'm after.

Here's the pattern. I write a scene where somebody does something awful at a moment when they weren't really in control of themselves. Say he's on a sleeping pill that turns him belligerent, or she's been up for two days straight and screams at her kid, or a guy who's been in a cult since he was 19 gives up a friend's address. Readers come after the character anyway. "He chose to take the pill." "She chose to stay up." "He chose to stay in the cult." Fine. So I say: he was prescribed it, she was working two jobs because the rent went up, he was a kid whose parents were already in it. And the answer is always some version of "at some point you have to stop." Except nobody can tell me where. They'll happily walk the chain back a link or two, but the tracing has to land somewhere, and there's no rule about where, so it lands wherever the person doing the blaming already wanted it to land.

Two things about it that make me think it's its own thing. First, it doesn't need determinism to run: even if some choices are genuinely up to us, this one wasn't, and the one it's riding on wasn't either, and so on down. And it cuts across both camps -- people who think we're puppets and people who think we're not use the exact same move. Second, it only ever runs one direction. Nobody in these fights uses the same chain to get to "so really it's his father's fault" or "so nobody's to blame." It's always used to keep the blame exactly where it already was, and the chain is just the excuse for keeping it there.

So: is there a term for this, the specific move of rescuing blame for something you weren't in control of by pointing at something earlier you were, together with the fact that the chain never bottoms out in anything you authored from scratch and the stopping point is basically arbitrary? If there's a real concept, tell me what it is and where it stops being the same thing -- I've had people tell me "oh that's just X" before and X turned out to be about a slightly different question, so I want the boundaries more than the label. And if there's nothing, give me one, with a definition that says what's in and what's out. Is the brainwashed guy in? Is the guy who just has a short fuse out? Where's the line, and does the line have anything going for it besides how the verdict feels?
