Right — I'm 67, retired last year after forty years in sensory physiology (my whole working life was measurement: calibration, reference standards, deciding when two numbers were the same number), and for the past year I've been sitting in on a philosphy of economics reading group. Last week I said something and got a look, and I'd like to know before I open my mouth again whether the set of things I believe actually hangs together or whether I'm just holding two incompatible half-views. Here's the pile, as honestly as I can state it:

1. Numbers are only as meaningful as their admissible transformations let them be. If your claim survives rescaling it's about the world; if it dies when you change units, it was about the units. That's the only discipline I know.
2. Inside one person, welfare is ordinal at best — a ranking, like the Mohs scale. I prefer A to B; the size of the gap is not a fact.
3. So interpersonal comparisions of *levels* are empty. "Ann is happier than Bob" is like "this room is twice as hot as that one."
4. But interpersonal comparisons of *differences* strike me as perfectly real: that Ann's relief from the operation outweighs Bob's irritation at the tax seems like a claim about them, not about my scale. I keep thinking there ought to be a fixed reference stimulus — some analogue of the triple point of water, the Fechner idea that equal ratios of stimulus give equal steps of sensation — and then we could all be measured against it.
5. Prioritarianism: a given improvement matters more the worse off the person already is. I'm fairly confident of this.
6. The veil of ignorance is the right justificatory device for distributive questions.
7. Maximin is daft — behind the veil I'd take the gamble, and in the clinic I'd spend the money giving ten thousand people a small improvement rather than curing the one dreadful case. The awful case doesn't get a veto.

What I want is not a lecture, it's an audit. Which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they do, and how bad is each real one? Order them by severity. For each genuine conflict, tell me the cheapest single thing to drop and what I'd be buying by dropping it — and if the cheapest fix just moves the problem onto another item on the list, say so.
