For a graduate seminar I am assembling a scored taxonomy of theories of historical change, and I want evaluation rather than exposition. Take these four frameworks: Polybius's cycle of constitutions, Ibn Khaldun's cycle of dynastic rise and decay, Spengler's life-cycle of cultures, and Toynbee's challenge-and-response. In ordinary usage "cyclical" conflates at least three distinct commitments: (a) recurrence of event-types, (b) return of a system to a state it previously occupied, and (c) recurrence that nonetheless carries a net directional drift, a spiral rather than a circle. Rate each framework from 0 to 10 on each of (a), (b), and (c). Then give each a single overall score for how far it genuinely earns the label "cyclical" rather than merely asserting that things repeat, and rank the four by that score. Justify each placement in two or three sentences. Finally, say which of the three sub-scores does the most work in your ranking, and whether any of the four would be misclassified by a plain cyclical-versus-linear dichotomy. No citations are needed. Write in American English.
