Two people on my team have been going at each other for months about whether frontier AI should be open-sourced. One is dead set that if you don't open the weights, the data, and the training code, you just hand the whole field to three labs and nobody else can check them. The other thinks past some capability level, putting the weights out is basically arming whoever asks, and that the open camp never prices in the oversight you give up. They've stopped hearing each other, so I want to draft the joint statement they could both sign and get the argument moving again.

Include: what they can actually agree on -- I'd start with the idea that "open-source" isnt one thing but a bundle (weights, training data, code, eval results, safety tooling) and they don't have to take the same line on each; the places they really split, each worded so the person who holds it wouldn't wince; and the cruxes, i.e. sub-questions where settling one would force one of them to move. One crux I suspect is whether releasing at the frontier permanently lowers the capability ceiling or whether the ceiling just keeps moving anyway. Another is whether the thing we're worried about spreading is capability or oversight -- an open release can spread both. For each crux, give a case or piece of evidence both would agree is relevant, and write down each side's prediction in advance. Number the agreed claims, then a table for the disagreements, and if part of the fight turns out to be just about words, say which part.
