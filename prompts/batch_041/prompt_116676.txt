I should like a focused piece of conceptual explanation concerning the metaphysics of material constitution. I am not asking for a survey of the literature, and I do not want names, dates or references; what I want is the problem itself, the arguments on each side, and your own verdict. Please write in British English throughout.

Consider two cases.

Case one. A potter takes a lump of clay and shapes it into a statue, which is exhibited for a year. She then squashes the statue back into a shapeless lump. Some weeks later, using the same clay, she shapes a second statue with a quite different form. Nothing is added or removed at any stage; the clay is conserved throughout.

Case two. A potter takes a lump of clay and shapes it into a sculpture that is lump-shaped: it looks exactly like a lump of clay, of the same size and colour, and is intended as a sculpture of a lump. Here the sculpture and the lump begin at the same moment and have exactly the same career; there is no asymmetry between them.

For each case, I want you to explain:

(a) the counting question — at a given moment, how many material objects are present, and what turns on the answer;

(b) the coincidence question — if there are two objects sharing all their matter at every moment, what exactly stands in need of explanation, and why is it thought that this cannot simply be taken as a brute fact (I have in mind what is sometimes called the grounding problem);

(c) the modal question — why does the suggestion that the statue and the lump are the same object, but only contingently, appear to conflict with the principle that if a is b then it is necessary that a is b, and what reply is available to someone who wishes to hold that they are the same object nonetheless?

Then lay out the main options available — for instance, denying that everyday composite objects exist at all; holding that an object is identical with its parts and cannot survive any change in them; treating objects as four-dimensional wholes with temporal parts; holding that constitution is not identity; and relativising identity to a sortal or kind — and say what each of them costs, in particular what each has to say about the squashing in case one and about the absence of any asymmetry in case two. Finally, tell me which option you think handles case two best, and why the others do worse.
