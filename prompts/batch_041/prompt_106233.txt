The thing I keep circling is that Briony's whole catastrophe comes from doing what historians and detectives do: she takes a couple of ambiguous moments (the fountain, the letter she shouldn't have read) and retro-fits them into a story with a culprit and a motive, and once the story's set every later detail just confirms it. So I'd like a reading of the novel as a study in hindsight bias and the narrative fallacy. Some things I want you to press on. Is Briony's mistake really hindsight bias, or is it the narrative fallacy, i.e. imposing causal shape on noise, and does the novel treat those as one failure or two different ones? Does McEwan use the mystery structure to indict that hindsight, or does he end up reproducing it? Where does the novel resist the reading, is there anything in it hindsight bias cant explain?
