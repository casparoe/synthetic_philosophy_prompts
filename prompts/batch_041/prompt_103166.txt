Alright, so I've been rereading Hofstadter's Fluid Concepts and Creative Analogies — the Copycat chapter, where you take a letter string like abc and transform it (abc becomes abd, or abc becomes mrrjjj, whatever) and the whole point is that no single rule is 'the' rule, just ones that are warmer or colder. Anyway it's got me wanting to build a decision tree / sorting quiz for my blog: which theory of conceptual slippage do you actually hold? The kind of thing where you answer concrete cases and it lands you on a named position, instead of just asking "do you think concepts are definitions or prototypes?" up front.

Constraints, cause I've been burned by these before:

- Microdomain: stay in the letter-string world, it's the cleanest. But don't just do abc -> abd. Use at least these cases: (1) abc -> abd, then ask what happens to mrrjjj; (2) abc -> abd, then ask about kji; (3) a string that's also a readable word or abbreviation, so that "reading it as meaningful" competes with "reading it as a pattern"; (4) two transformations that both fit, one obvious and one a stretch but somehow more apt; (5) a case where someone gives an answer that fits no rule you could state, yet still feels like it gets it — the apt-nonsense / pun case.

- Every branch node has to be a question a reader can actually answer without knowing which label it leads to. No "are you a realist about concepts?" — that's cheating. Make the nodes be about the cases.

- I want one node that seperates deliberate pun / playful extension from honest mistake from genuine new generalization. That feels like the real crux of slippage, so the tree should force people to commit on it.

Leaves I need in there, whatever names make sense: (a) the "there's a fact of the matter, slippage is just error" realist; (b) the "any consistent rule is as good as any other, we just pick, it's all convention" one; (c) the Hofstadter-ish fluid-concept view, where a concept just IS the space of its possible slippages, graded by temperature, no sharp line between using it and stretching it; (d) the prototype/exemplar similarity view, slippage as low similarity to the centroid or nearest neighbor; (e) the "concepts are tools, slippage is just repurposing a tool, ask what it's for" pragmatist; (f) the Wittgenstein family-resemblance one, where there was never a same-concept relation to begin with. If you think two of these can't actually be told apart by any case, say so rather than fudging it — flag the pair.

- Also note which combinations of answers nobody occupies, and diagnose why (incoherent? nobody's bothered? genuinely unexplored?).

Keep it to like 7-9 nodes and 5-6 leaves. Audience is my readers — mostly rationalists who've done the Sequences but haven't read Hofstadter, so don't assume they know alot of the vocabulary. And no reading lists or citations please, just the tree and the reasoning; I'll add my own links after.
