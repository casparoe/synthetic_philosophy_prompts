I run a small reading group at work (all engineers, no philosophy training) and we keep going round in circles on path dependence. Some of us think that once a system picks a direction it's basically locked in, others think the same sort of outcome was going to turn up anyway and the particular direction is just scenery, and I keep loosing track of which claim we're actually disagreeing about. So: can you build me a decision tree that starts from a couple of ordinary commitments and ends at a specific position on whether history is locked in? Each branch should be labelled with the question that seperates it from its siblings, and each leaf should carry the position's name (or 'unnamed' if nobody defends it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Please build the branches out of the questions first and drop the labels in afterwards — not a list of schools with questions bolted on. Two things I care about that the usual discussion seems to mush together: first, whether the lock is about reversal being impossible, reversal being merely expensive, or the alternatives having been destroyed outright; second, whether 'the same outcome anyway' means this exact outcome (this standard, this constitution) or just the same type of outcome, in which case it's not obvious that it matters which one won. I want the tree to pull those apart rather than lump them.

Use cases a non-philosopher can actually answer: a technical standard beating its rival, a revolution, the same body part evolving in unrelated lineages, a team's architecture decision everyone now has to live with. Ten to twelve leaves at most, and no reading list or citations — I want the instrument, not the literature. Flag any pairs of leaves the tree can't really separate, and note any combinations of answers that nobody seems to occupy, with a line on whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. British English please.
