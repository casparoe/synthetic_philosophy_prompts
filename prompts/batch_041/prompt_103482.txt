I have no background in philosophy or theology — I read something about the omnipotence paradox a while ago and have been chewing on it ever since. What struck me is that every answer I can find seems to work the same way: it quietly narrows what "all-powerful" is allowed to mean, and then the very same difficulty reappears at the new edge of the definition. I would like you to turn that observation into a satire.

Write it as an in-universe official document from an invented body — call it the Bureau of Omnipotence Standards — which exists to certify claims of omnipotence and has just concluded an investigation. Use the form of a real regulatory artifact: a brief findings section, a list of non-conformances, a definitions section, and a revised standard. About 800 words, deadpan throughout, formatted with numbered sections and a couple of subsections. Keep it legible to someone who has never read any theology: gloss any technical term in plain language, and do not quote from any real text.

The thing being satirized is not a religion and not a person but the habit of rescuing omnipotence by redefinition, so the comedy should come from the bureaucratic logic of that move rather than from invented absurdities: each fix the Bureau adopts should generate a fresh edge case of exactly the kind it was meant to close. Please let the document demonstrate real command of the actual debate — that an omnipotent being is standardly said to be unable to do what is logically impossible, and that this is presented as no limitation at all; the difference between a being that cannot do something and one that will not; the question whether "make a stone too heavy for its maker to lift" names an impossible task or merely an incoherent description; the strengthened version about creating a being greater than oneself; and the modern formulation of omnipotence as the power to bring about any possible world. I do not want a catalogue of paradoxes; I want the Bureau's own paperwork to enact the pattern. Include a severability clause under which the Bureau may rule that any troublesome request was never a task to begin with, and let the final section turn on the Bureau's obligation to certify its own revised standard, which is itself the sort of self-referential item it exists to police. Close with a short dissenting appendix from one board member, in the same deadpan register.
