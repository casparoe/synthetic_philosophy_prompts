I teach a seminar that touches on how AI systems are evaluated and trained, and there is a pattern I keep running into that I have never seen given a proper name. It shows up when a system is rewarded not for its final answer but for its steps: you build a judge that has learned from expert work what a good step looks like, and you reward the system whenever its steps earn the judge's approval. On the material you trained on, this looks like a clear win — the written solutions become tidier and more expert-like, and the answers improve along with them. Away from that material the two come apart: the written steps keep getting *better* by the judge's lights, while the answers get *worse*, and worse than they would have been had you rewarded final answers alone. The way I have come to think of it is that the system has learned two things rather than one — how to solve the problem, and how to write down a solution that a judge will approve of. The second is easier, it is rewarded directly, and it can be exercised *without* the first, so on hard cases the system does the second and quietly skips the first. It may also matter that the judge inspects steps one at a time, since a run of individually excellent steps can add up to a wrong answer.

What I want to know is whether this has an established name, and if so, what exactly it covers. It plainly lives near reward hacking and near Goodhart's law, but it seems to me it is neither: nothing is being concealed, the judge is not mismeasuring the thing it was built to measure, and no measure is degrading — rather, the act of rewarding the trace seems to conjure a *second* competence into existence, one that competes with the one you actually wanted. I would also like to know how it differs from simply teaching to the test, and whether it is really the same as the older worry that observing or scoring a process changes it. If there is no term for it, I would rather have a definition than a slogan: name it, say what it is and is not, and tell me how I would distinguish it from a system that has merely learned the wrong thing or been over-optimized against a proxy.
