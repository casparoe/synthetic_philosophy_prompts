I write about science and ideas for a living, which means I sit through a lot of talks where somebody says that one kind of thing is really just another kind of thing. Minds are really just what brains do. A species is really just a population of interbreeding organisms. A queue is really just a bunch of people standing in an order. Money is really just a promise with a printing press attached. I nod along, and then the same little snag shows up, and in fifteen years I have never gotten a straight answer about what its called.

Here is the snag. Ask which of the lower-level things you are actually pointing at, and the answer always seems to mention the higher-level thing you were supposed to be getting rid of. Sure, a belief is a brain state, but which one? The one that means its going to rain, which is to say the one that plays the role of the belief. Sure, the statue is just the lump of bronze, but which lump? The one shaped like the general, which is to say the one that already counts as the statue. The person doing the reducing then says, fine, it is the arrangement that matters, and the arrangement turns out to be describable only in the vocabulary we were supposedly reducing away.

So is there a name for that? I know about grounding and supervenience and multiple realisation, and I know the hermeneutic circle is a thing in some other tradition, but none of them seems to be quite this. What I am after is the specific situation where the allegedly more basic facts can only be picked out by their relation to the less basic ones, so that the claim that A is nothing over and above B comes out true but cannot be stated without help from A. And honestly I care about the edges more than the label. Why does the same trick not sink water and H2O? Is the snag a fact about our vocabulary or a fact about the world? And is it the same snag running in the other direction, when the whole looks like what makes its parts the parts they are, rather than the parts making the whole?
