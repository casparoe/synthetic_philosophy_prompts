ok, I'm filing a piece on moral foundations theory -- Haidt's thing from The Righteous Mind, where the moral mind is like a tongue: you come with a fixed set of receptors (care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, and the liberty one he bolted on later), and cultures are just different cuisines that hit them in different combinations. I've been using this to make the conciliatory point in the piece: if liberals and conservatives are basically tasting different things, then the psychology of left and right can't tell us who's right, so everyone should get off their high horse. my editor loves the framing.

but a friend of mine who does philosophy of biology read the draft and says the taste analogy is quietly carrying my whole argument, and that it's also just wrong about taste. now I'm worried.

so what I want: an actual audit of the analogy, not a vibe check. lay out the correspondence -- what maps onto the taste buds, what onto the cuisines, the tongue, the cook, the hungry diner -- and then tell me where the map breaks. I care most about which breaks matter for the specific conclusion I'm leaning on (that the psych of left/right tells us nothing about who's right) versus which are just pedantry I can wave off in a sentence. the biology friend's gripe is that taste receptors aren't arbitrary -- they're detectors, they evolved to sort nutritious from toxic, and a cuisine can genuinely make you sick -- so if anything the analogy points toward there being a standard after all. is that a fair hit? does it matter that nobody gets harmed by preferring bitter greens but somebody gets harmed by a purity code?

and then the verdict: does the no-one's-right conclusion survive the disanalogies, or was the analogy doing all the work? if the taste picture is a bad fit, what's a better image for what MFT actually shows -- or is the honest answer that the theory is just descriptive and the analogy was never supposed to get me to a normative place at all? (also, while you're in there: the same book leans on the rider-on-an-elephant image for reason versus intuition. do the tongue and the elephant even fit together, or am I mixing two pictures that pull in different directions?)
