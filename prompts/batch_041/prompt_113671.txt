I'm a PhD candidate in philosophy and the graduate representative on my college's committee reviewing alcohol policy for the senior common room. The Master wants to introduce a weekly supervised wine hour next term — a designated sober convener, sign-in, a hard stop, no spirits — and has asked for a decision memo before the first meeting. I have argued in committee that this is not merely a risk-management matter but a chance to take seriously the proposal Plato's Athenian makes in the Laws: that wine, drunk collectively and under a sober commander, is a drug that dissolves shame and lets a person's settled character show, so that a well-run drinking party can serve at once as a test of and a training in temperance, with one rule for those under eighteen, a looser one for the under-thirties, and full participation reserved for the over-forties. My colleague's objection is that if Plato is also right, in the Timaeus, that excessive pleasure and pain are themselves diseases of the soul, then the colleague who disgraces himself is not disclosing a culpable self we may judge but displaying a symptom — which makes the test-of-character rationale either cruel or incoherent. Draft the memo I shall circulate: recommend a concrete policy with a start date and a review point, give the dissenting view its strongest form, and state plainly which argument or consideration would change your recommendation and where your reasoning is likeliest to fail. Write in American English.
