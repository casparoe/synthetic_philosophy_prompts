I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, so forgive the laymans terms. I'm writing a book about two strangers who do each other a good turn in a situation where they'll never see each other again and nobody else is around to see it. I've been trying to figure out why that's even possible, and I keep noticing a pattern in real life that I don't have a word for. It's not the usual "they'll meet again so they have to play nice" thing, and it's not "someone's watching so they have to protect their reputation." In the cases I mean there's no future interaction and no audience — like the guy who returns a wallet he found in a cab with no ID in it, or someone who keeps a promise to a friend who's dead. What there is instead is a third party who isn't in the situation at all: a dead parent, a god, a future reader, the kind of person they'd like to be. The two people cooperate because each is imagining being seen by someone who can't do anything back. I've been calling it "the invisible witness" in my notes but that's just my own tag. Is there an established name for this? If there is, how is it different from plain reputation, or from the idea that you're nice to people because it gets back to you through other people, or from the Kantian rule about only acting on a principle you'd want everyone to follow? If there isn't, coin one for me — and be precise about what it includes and leaves out. I don't just want a label, I want the shape of the thing: what makes it different from each of those, and which of them it's closest to. Take a definate position — don't give me a menu of options and leave me to pick. Give it to me as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no intro and no conclusion.
