I just got voted onto the board of our local land trust — small operation, we manage a few hundred acres of woods and meadow that an invasive shrub has been eating alive for a decade. Our stewardship director wants us to introduce a leaf-eating beetle that's been tested in a lab and looks like it'll knock the shrub back within a few years. The wrinkle is that three other land trusts in the county are looking at the same beetle, and none of us is talking to the others. If one of us releases it, great, problem basically solved. If all four of us do it, the beetle breeds up past what the shrub can feed and starts in on a rare native shrub we actually care about, and we can't easily pull it back out once it's out. The director says our case for releasing is strong no matter what the others do, and one board member keeps grumbling that this is the unilateralist's curse and we're about to be the sucker who blinks. Another member thinks thats just paranoia and we should pick up the phone. There's a regional invasive-species council that could coordinate everyone, but it only meets twice a year and the next meeting is five months out, well past the spring release window. There's also a real chance that if we don't do it, one of the smaller trusts does it anyway, sloppier, with no monitoring at all.

I have to circulate a short written opinion before Thursdays vote: recommend release, hold off, or release under conditions. Give me a concrete recommendation with a time frame and the reasoning behind it, and please take the strongest argument on the other side seriously instead of just waving at we-should-coordinate. Most of the board aren't philosophers, so keep the jargon out of it without dumbing the problem down. And say something about what we should tell the other trusts, because half the board thinks reaching out first is a sign of weakness.
