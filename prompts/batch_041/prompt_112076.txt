I'm a retired chemist who's been reading history of science for fun, and I keep running into a pattern that isn't just bad history. Take Fomenko's new chronology, or the Atlantis people, or the ancient-aliens crowd. The thing I notice isn't the specific claim; it's the move where the absence of the evidence you'd expect is treated as positive evidence that the evidence was destroyed or hidden, and where the conventional timeline is itself the thing to be explained away. So every gap in the record confirms the theory, and every actual find either gets ignored or gets absorbed as another layer of the cover-up. It feels different from ordinary pseudohistory, which usually just gets facts wrong. Is there an established name for this exact evidential structure -- not the content, the structure? I know about Popper and falsifiability, and I know conspiracy theory is nearby, but those don't seem to carve it right. If there's no name, can you give me one and say where the edges are: what seperates it from a normal research programme that has anomalies, and from a conspiracy theory that just happens to be about history? I'm not after a bibliography, just the concept.
