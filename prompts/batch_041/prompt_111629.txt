Okay, this is half a rant and half a real request, so bear with me.

At my company's benefits meeting last month someone from HR shut down a question about extending paid parental leave with some version of 'you can have it all' -- plenty of people juggle a career and kids, her sister did it, you just have to be organized. Everyone in the room knew the reply was bad but nobody could say exactly what was wrong with it, and I've been chewing on it ever since.

What I want is not an essay about work-life balance. I want you to take that one sentence, 'you can have it all,' and lay out the different precise claims it could actually be making. It seems to me it's either a boring truism, an empirical prediction, a policy position in disguise, or something that quietly assumes an invisible army of other people. For each reading I want to know what exactly it asserts, whether it's true or plausible or false, and whether it would actually get HR to her conclusion, which was that we definately don't need to pay for more leave. I have a suspicion that the readings which are obviously true get you nothing, and the ones that would do the work are the ones that fall apart.

Things I'd like you to push on: who the 'you' is (is the sentence even addressed to fathers, or is the whole thing about mothers?); what 'all' is supposed to contain, and whether that list is fixed or just whatever the speaker happens to have; whether 'having' means at the same time or over a lifetime, and whether the 'just not all at once' version is a rescue or a retreat; whether the subject is a person, a household, or a society; and whether 'can' means nothing formally blocks you, or you can do it without paying a price. Also whether 'plenty of people manage' is even the right kind of claim to answer a question about an individual, and whether a possibility that exists only because someone else absorbs the cost (a grandmother moving in, a nanny, a partner who quits) counts as the kind of possibility the slogan promises.

Don't pick a reading and defend it -- give me the menu, with verdicts, and tell me which one HR was probably operating with even though she'd never spell it out. American English, please.
