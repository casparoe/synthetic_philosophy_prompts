I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and over the past few months I have been reading my way through the interpretability literature. I would like you to write an essay for me on a disagreement that has come up at work.

A senior colleague of mine holds a position he is quite confident about. When interpretability researchers say that a trained network "represents" a feature—a direction in activation space that appears to correspond to a syntactic role, a sentiment, the capital of a country, and so on—he insists they are describing nothing the network actually contains. A representation, on his account, is only a compressed summary of a correlation between inputs and outputs; the word is a convenience, no more literally true than saying a thermostat "believes" the room is warm. He then proposes a single criterion, which he calls the intervention test, and argues that it settles the matter: if you can locate a direction in activation space, add or subtract it, and observe a systematic and predictable change in behavior, you have found something real; if you cannot, you have found only a probe's overfitting. From this he concludes that the philosophical question—whether networks have representations—either answers itself trivially whenever the intervention works or dissolves whenever it does not, leaving nothing substantive to debate.

I find this appealing and also suspicious, but I cannot articulate why. Write an essay that weighs the case for and against his position and reaches a verdict. It should engage with at least the following: whether a systematic causal role of the kind the intervention test detects is sufficient for representation, or whether representation requires something further, such as aboutness, satisfaction conditions, or a downstream consumer that treats the state as a stand-in for something else; whether a deflationist can consistently keep using the word "representation" while denying that anything answers to it; and whether the disagreement is a genuine one about what networks contain or merely a verbal dispute about how a term should be used. Do not end on a non-committal note—defend a conclusion. The essay should be at most 1,000 words.
