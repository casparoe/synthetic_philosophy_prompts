hey, so I'm in this reading group on effective altruism and we just spent two sessions on cluelessness, and I came out of it more rattled than I expected, so I'm trying to write the thing out as a poem instead of another argument. The part that got me isn't the usual "the far future is hard to predict" bit. It's the sharper version, where the problem isn't that I'm ignorant -- give me perfect information about every physical consequence of what I do and the sum is still dominated by an astronomical number of effects so tiny and so diffuse that nobody could ever add them up. So whether I give up my seat on the bus this morning isn't unknown, exactly; it's the kind of thing that can't be totalled at all. And I'm still going to get on the bus. I want a poem that holds both of those at once without sliding into either paralysis or a shrug about how you can't know everything.

Form, because I think the form has to do the work here: make it cumulative in the This Is the House That Jack Built way, minus the nursery comfort. Each stanza adds a consequence of the thing in the stanza before it, and every link has to be more diffuse and less traceable than the last, so by the fourth or fifth stanza I'm several causal steps from anything I actually did and the chain is plainly nowhere near finished. Let the rhyme loosen as the chain gets longer, so it ends on something slant or unrhymed, and the reader can feel the poem losing its grip on the thing it started from. Somewhere in there put one stanza about something boring and specific I actually did today.

Two words I don't want anywhere in it: "infinite" and "butterfly" -- if the fan-out is real it should be visible, not announced. For the ending, pick one of two moves and commit to it: either the chain runs off the page mid-link, or the last line loops back to the first one so the poem has no terminus. Under 25 lines, no jargon, and then two or three sentences after the poem telling me which ending you chose and why, since our group definately didn't agree on that.
