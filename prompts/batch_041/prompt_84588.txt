I sit on the accommodations panel of a publicly funded secondary school — the body that rules on requests from pupils and staff to be exempted from, or accommodated within, the school's general rules. Our decisions over the past four years, with the reasons we minuted at the time:

1. Granted: a pupil was excused from the end-of-term concert, which always includes carols, and given a written alternative assignment. Reason recorded: she withdrew from an activity and nobody else was affected.
2. Granted: a pupil was excused from the mixed-gender swimming unit on grounds of religious modesty, again with a written alternative and no extra staffing or facilities.
3. Refused: a request that the school create and supervise a dedicated prayer room at lunchtime. Reason recorded: it would consume a room and staff time that other pupils use.
4. Refused: a request that the cafeteria serve only halal meat, so that Muslim pupils would not have to check labels. Reason recorded: it would change what every other pupil eats.
5. Refused: a religious exemption from the vaccination requirement. Reason recorded: unvaccinated pupils would put others at risk.
6. Granted: a pupil was excused from the dissection lab on ethical, non-religious grounds, with an alternative assignment.
7. Refused: a request to hand out religious leaflets in the cafeteria. Reason recorded: the audience could not easily walk away.
8. Granted: a pupil was excused from the mindfulness-and-yoga unit, which her family regarded as religious practice, with an alternative assignment.

The request before us next week: a pupil asks to wear a head covering during lessons, which her faith requires whenever she is in the presence of boys or men. She proposes a plain cloth wrap in the school's uniform colours, and offers to remove it for PE and for her identity photograph. The uniform code forbids head coverings indoors, but the school already waives that rule for a pupil receiving chemotherapy and for a pupil with alopecia. Nothing else would change, and no member of staff would take on any duty.

Tell me how we will rule, and more importantly what rule we have been running. Two colleagues have offered readings — one that we have simply been applying a harm-to-others test, another that we have been keeping religion out of the school — but I suspect the record is consistent with more than these two. I want the full menu of rules that fit the eight decisions, what each of them predicts about the head covering, which of them the record supports best and why, and which further request — one we have not yet faced — would most cleanly separate the survivors from each other. If the eight decisions genuinely underdetermine the ninth, say so, and state what principle we would be adopting with each of the defensible rulings.
