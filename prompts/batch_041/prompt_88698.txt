You are designing a single session for an upper-level undergraduate seminar in rational choice and social choice theory: roughly eighteen students, ninety minutes, in a room with movable tables so they can work in pairs and then combine into quartets. Design the activity so the conflict arises from the rules, the payoffs, and the roles, not from anything I announce. The participants should commit to something — a division rule, a split, a principle — and then be led, by the structure of later rounds, into a case where holding to it forces them to abandon an intuition they endorsed a few minutes earlier. I want the room to disagree about which intuition to give up, because that disagreement is the whole point. Please give me: the materials (assume I can prepare tokens or point sheets and a one-page worksheet); a payoff scheme in points with no real money, including the disagreement payoff and how the outside/utopia options are generated; a round-by-round timeline that fits ninety minutes; the roles or pairing structure; and a debrief that maps specific moments in the session onto Pareto efficiency, symmetry, scale invariance, independence of irrelevant alternatives, and individual monotonicity, then shows how Nash's solution and the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution each buy consistency by paying for it with one of those principles — the product-maximizing point versus the intersection of the Pareto frontier with the line from the disagreement point to the utopia point.
