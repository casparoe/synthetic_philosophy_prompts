I'm in a metaphyiscs course and we've just finished the unit on dispositions, finks, and masks. I mostly followed the readings, but our TA's handout gave a summary that I think might be smoothing over some stuff, and I want to know how much of it I should trust. Here's what the handout says:

'Dispositions are really just conditionals in disguise. To say a glass is fragile is just to say that if it were struck, it would break. That's the simple conditional analysis. The problem is that the conditional can fail even when the disposition is present. Fink cases are ones where the object would lose the disposition if the trigger happened, so the conditional comes out false even though the object is disposed. Mask cases are ones where the disposition is still there and the trigger happens, but something external blocks the manifestation, so the conditional comes out false too. The difference is that finks are internal and masks are external. What finks and masks together show is that dispositions are intrinsic causal powers that don't reduce to any counterfactual at all; a disposition ascription just reports the presence of a power, and the counterfactual is at best a symptom. So once you've got finks and masks, the conditional analysis is dead, and the right view is dispositional realism: powers are basic.'

I have a hunch the last bit is doing more than the examples support, but I can't tell where the line is. Can you go through it claim by claim -- what a philosopher working on dispositions would accept, what they'd wince at, and what's just wrong? In particular I'd like to know whether the internal/external way of drawing the fink/mask distinction is right, whether "the conditional analysis is dead" is fair, and whether the conclusion that dispositions are irreducible basic powers actually follows from the fink and mask cases or if it needs a further premise. Also, if the handout is wrong about the distinction, what would be the better way to put it? I don't need citations or reading recs, just the audit.
