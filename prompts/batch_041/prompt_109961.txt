I'm writing a response paper for my concepts-and-categories seminar on the Letter Spirit project, and I've gotten stuck on something that I suspect may be a confusion of my own rather than a genuine difficulty. As I understand the architecture, a letter's style-independent "essence" is represented separately from the style that a particular font imposes, so that once the system has grasped a style it can work out how all twenty-six letters should look in it. What I can't see is how the essence of a letter is supposed to be pinned down. If the essence of 'a' is whatever every 'a' shares no matter the font, then it looks like it should be statable as an inventory of features — a bowl, a stem, their arrangement — with the essence being whatever survives once the style is subtracted. On that reading, the single-story 'a' of a geometric sans and the double-story 'a' of a traditional serif would have to share that feature core, and a shape lacking the core would be a mark that merely resembles an 'a' rather than an 'a' at all. I realize the program is confined to a coarse grid microdomain, but I take it the point about essence is meant to outrun that. So my question is whether Letter Spirit treats being an 'a' as a matter of matching a fixed, style-free set of features, with style as a separable layer laid over the top. Because if it does, then what makes every 'a' an 'a' has one determinate answer, and all the genuine creativity would lie in the styling.
