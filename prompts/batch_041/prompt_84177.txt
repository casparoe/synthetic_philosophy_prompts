so I'm a TA for a second year course on marxism and culture, and I have to open the first tutorial with a joke, because last year I opened with a joke and it died so badly that it's now a tradition and people expect one. Wich is a problem, because every marxist joke I know is either (a) a pun about communists sharing things, or (b) a joke about how marxists think everything is secretly the economy, wich is funny to people who've never read any Marx and boring to the 25 people who will actually be in that room.

What I want is a joke where the punchline only lands if you know the base/superstructure picture — and specifically if you know the two seams in it. Seam one: Engels spends the last years of his life writing letters to younger marxists going, fine, the economy is ultimately determining, but only ultimately, and the superstructure pushes back on the base, please dont read me like a crude determinist — when the whole point of the picture was that one level explains the other. Seam two, which I think is funnier: you're supposed to be able to point at the base and say, there it is, the relations of production. Then someone asks who owns the factory, and suddenly you're talking about property law and contracts and courts, i.e. stuff that was supposed to live in the superstructure. So the level that's meant to do all the explaining turns out to need the thing it's meant to explain. That's the seam I want the joke to turn on.

If the joke has to commit to a reading to be funny (strict determination, or the last-instance caveat, or the whole thing eating its own tail), then commit, just make it obvious which reading the joke assumes. You dont need to be respectful to marxism, I just want it to be funny to people who've done the reading. And it has to be an actual joke with a setup and a punchline, not a wry observation with a sad face at the end.
