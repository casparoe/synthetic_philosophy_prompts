I've got a question I can't settle and it's been bugging me for years, so I'm throwing it at you. I've spent about a decade doing data and performance work in public services -- a hospital trust first, now a local authority -- and I keep watching the same shape of thing happen with targets, and it doesn't quite match the story people tell about targets. The story everyone knows is the one where the number gets gamed: the target is hit, the thing underneath it gets worse, and there's an obvious gap between the number and the good it was supposed to stand for. That's not what I keep seeing, or not mainly. What I keep seeing is that over four or five years the number stops being a stand-in for anything, because the thing it stood in for quietly stops being available. The people who used to argue about what good looked like in terms of the actual work leave or get filtered out, the arguments stop, the vocabulary goes, and what's left is a practice organised entirely around producing the number. Ask anyone in the building what the number was for and you get the number back. Its not cynicism and it's not lying -- they sincerely think the target is the job and they can give you a perfectly coherent account of why. And when the target finally does get dropped, nothing reverts, because there's nothing left to revert to; the old way of working survives only as a rumour that some people were a bit slack about it. Same pattern in a school I know of after the league tables, same pattern in a software team after they switched to points.

I know about Goodhart, and the version everyone quotes about a measure ceasing to be a good measure once it becomes a target, and the cousins of it -- Campbell, teaching to the test, the cobra bounty, the whole literature on the unintended consequences of performance indicators that I only half know. But all of those seem to need a fixed goal sitting behind the measure, so that you can say the measure has drifted off it. Here the goal doesn't sit still: it gets replaced by the measure, gradually and sincerely, and the replacement is hard to undo because the only surviving record of the old goal is in the heads of people who've gone.

So: is there a name for this specific thing -- the point at which the proxy stops being a proxy and becomes the referent, with the old referent no longer available to anyone? If there is, tell me what it is and what it commits you to, and if the closest candidates are the goal-displacement and metric-fixation stuff, say exactly where those stop and this starts, because I don't think they get the self-sealing bit. If there's no name, coin one, and give me the boundary cases rather than a definition in the abstract: does it still count if the number was never any good to begin with? Does it count if the staff privately still hold the old goal and just can't act on it? And is the irreversibility part of the phenomenon or a separate thing I'm bundling in by mistake? Honestly I'd rather have the carving than the label -- a bare name won't help me.
