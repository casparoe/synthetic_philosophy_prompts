Ok so this is a bit of a shot in the dark. I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy, my thesis is on virtual worlds (metaphysics of virtual objects mostly, though I keep getting pulled into the game studies and law stuff), and there's a pattern in how people talk about whether something in a virtual world is 'real' that Ive been noticing for about a year now and I can't find a name for it. I'm starting to think there isn't one and I need to make one up.

Here's the shape of it. A guildmate dies and the guild holds an in-game memorial; nobody thinks that's silly, and people will say the friendship was real, the loss was real. Fine. But when the same person tells their department they need a few days off because a friend died, and the friend is someone they've only ever known as an avatar, it's suddenly 'well, an online friend'. Real enough to grieve, not real enough for bereavement leave. Or the other direction: someone spends two years building a city in a game, the studio shuts the server down, and the studio's position is that it's just data, no property interest, nothing was taken from you — but the same studio will happily sell you those items for money and will definately sue a gold-farming site for 'stealing' them. Real when the claim is theirs, not real when the claim is yours. Or harassment: the platform is fine saying the abuse was real enough to justify a permanent ban and at the same time that it wasn't real enough to make the platform liable for anything.

What gets me is that each of the individual moves is defensible, and the people making them are usually sincere, so it's not just self-interest or motivated reasoning in the obvious sense. It's that 'is this real' seems to get settled by what's downstream of the answer — who owes whom, who can be blamed, whose claim is on the table — rather than by any fact about the virtual thing, and then the ontology quietly shifts back and forth as the stakes change, with nobody flagging the switch.

I know it's near a few things. It's not just 'the magic circle is blurry', because that's about the boundary being fuzzy, not about the boundary being redrawn in the direction of whoever benefits. And it's not plain inconsistency either, since the attributions are usually made by different parties at different times.

So: is there an established name for this specific thing — the reality-status of an object or harm getting assigned on the basis of what the assignment is needed for — and if there is, which existing concept is closest, and where exactly does it come apart from what I'm describing? If there isn't one, coin one, but define it properly, including the edges. Like: does it still count if the same party holds both attributions at once? What if the asymmetry runs the other way, i.e. something declared unreal when that hurts the claimant? Is there an innocent version where the ontology just tracks the practice and nobody's gaming anything? And does the same pattern apply to virtual objects themselves, not just to harms? I care way more about the carving than the label, so a bare name won't help me. No need for a reading list, I can find the literature myself once I know what I'm looking for.

Please write it as continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. Just write it the way you'd write an email.
