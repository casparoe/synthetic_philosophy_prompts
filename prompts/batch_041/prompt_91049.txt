I am closing a radio essay on Gödel's rotating-universe solution to Einstein's field equations and his argument that relativity leaves the objectivity of time in ruins, and I want to end with a sestina—six six-line stanzas and a three-line envoi—rather than a summary. Please write it. Use six end-words that carry the argument—rotation, curve, light, simultaneity, order, now—and let the form do the philosophical work: the way the same words return in altered positions across the stanzas should enact the closed timelike curves that thread through that cosmos. The physics should be right enough that a relativist will not wince: a rotating dust universe, through every point a closed timelike curve, no global division into past, present, and future. The philosophy should be Gödel's rather than a science-fiction gloss: the trouble is not that time travel would be fun but that the objectivity of temporal passage is undermined, which he took to favor an idealist reading of time. Let the envoi also face the obvious objection—if time is not real, what is a poem doing taking time, line after line—without resolving it too neatly. No citations, no reading list; the poem should stand on its own.
