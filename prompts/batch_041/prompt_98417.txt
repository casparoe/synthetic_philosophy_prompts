My old officemate has spent years on a theory of which systems are sentient enough to matter morally, and her verdicts have been pretty stable. As best I remember them: thermostat, no; a plant that leans toward light and fires off stress signals, no; a feedforward net that labels photos of human pain, no; a small RL agent in a gridworld that learns from reward-prediction errors and steers away from states it was shaped to avoid, no; a mouse, yes; a honeybee, she waffles but usually no; and a recurrent net that carries a model of its own body and its own uncertainty and broadcasts its states out to a pile of downstream modules, yes. Now a group down the hall has built somthing and I want to know what she'll say about it: it's a single all-to-all attention bottleneck that every submodule routes through and gets re-broadcast from, so you could call it a global workspace, but it has no representation of itself as an agent, no self-model at all, and it's trained purely to predict the next frame of video. Does she count it? I care less about the yes or no than about the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, which of those the new system actually splits, and what further case would tell them apart for good.
