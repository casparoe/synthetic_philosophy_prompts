Problem is I keep circling the same cluster of questions around the paradox of tolerance and I can't tell which one is worth the six weeks. Here's what I've got so far:

- Is it even a paradox, or just a fancy name for the fact that "tolerant" obviously can't mean "tolerates literally everything"? Our lecturer made a distinction I keep chewing on: tolerance as restraint vs. tolerance as a rule of the game. Under the first, tolerating something means you had the power to stop it and chose not to; under the second, the rules forbid you from stopping it, so there's no restraint happening and maybe no paradox either. Is that a real distinction or just relabelling?

- Does any of this change when the thing being tolerated is speech vs. conduct, and is the words/deeds line stable, or does it collapse once you notice that organising, funding and platforming are all conduct anyway?

- The who-decides problem: any rule that says we won't tolerate the intolerant has to be applied by somebody, and the application starts looking like ordinary politics rather than a principled test. Fatal regress, or just what any rule looks like up close?

- Whether the whole thing is really about tolerance at all, or about power and whose norms get enforced.

What I want is a prioritisation, not a survey. Out of the open questions in this area — mine plus the ones I'm not seeing — which two or three should I actually spend six weeks on, given that my tools are argument and not data (I can't run experiments, and I can't get library books over the break, so please don't just point me at readings)? Defend the ordering: which question is upstream of the others, which is tractable with pure argument, which is neglected because everyone keeps making the same move in week one and then stopping, and what would move something up or down the list.
