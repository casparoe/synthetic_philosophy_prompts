The peacock's train is usually given as the textbook case of the handicap principle: it is costly, and that cost is what makes it an honest signal of quality. But the train is also what gets the peacock chosen by peahens, so it plainly raises his reproductive success, which makes it an advantage rather than a handicap. And since a low-quality male could grow just as large a train and pay just as high a price, the cost does not seem to make his signal any less credible than a high-quality male's. What is the handicap principle actually claiming about the relationship between cost and honesty, and is the peacock's train really an example of it?
