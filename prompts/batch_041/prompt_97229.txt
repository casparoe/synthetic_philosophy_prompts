so me and a friend have been arguing for weeks about Hofstadter's careenium — the thought experiment where you've got this huge arena with countless tiny marbles (simmballs) bouncing off each other, and a "symbol" is supposed to be just a stable pattern in all that bouncing. His line is: fine, but who shoves whom around in there? do the symbols actually push the simmballs, or do the simmballs do every last bit of the pushing while the symbols ride along as decoration? I want a poem out of it, specifically a sestina, and I want the form to do the arguing for me. So: all six end-words have to be dumb physical things from the simmball level — clack, bounce, grit, floor, rim, echo, or whatever six concrete words you pick, but no abstractions — and you're not allowed a single abstract noun in the six stanzas; the whole thing stays down at the level of things hitting things, and it should read stanza by stanza like pure billiards. Then in the envoi, when those same six words come back one more time, the poem should arrive at wanting or meaning or thinking, and it should be clear that this arrived out of nothing but the rearrangement of the six dumb words — no extra ingredient, no ghost, but also not a illusion; the pattern is real and it's doing work. Someone who knows the careenium should find the actual claim in the poem and not think I cheated; someone who doesn't should just get a good poem about a lot of balls bouncing that turns, at the end, into a mind.
