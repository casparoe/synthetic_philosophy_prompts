You are a philosopher of decision theory. I want you to argue for a position that is both technical and normative: that risk-weighted expected utility (REU) should be interpreted as encoding rank-relative stakes sensitivity, not merely a taste for or against variance. In REU, a gamble with outcomes ordered from worst to best is evaluated by starting with the utility of the worst outcome and adding, for each better outcome, the utility increment times a risk function applied to the cumulative probability of obtaining at least that outcome. Assume the risk function R is continuous, strictly increasing, and maps [0,1] to [0,1], with R(0)=0 and R(1)=1; a convex R corresponds to risk aversion. Argue that this rank-dependent structure gives the correct account of risk aversion as a rational preference rather than a bias, and that the independence axiom should be rejected because it wrongly treats the value of an outcome improvement as independent of the rank and probability of the outcomes it improves upon. Then confront the strongest objection: that REU is dynamically inconsistent or can violate stochastic dominance. Argue that the dynamic inconsistency objection fails when the risk function is applied consistently to the same cumulative probabilities at each choice node, and that any remaining violation of stochastic dominance is either not a violation under the correct formulation or is an acceptable price for capturing rank-relative stakes. Use a concrete three-outcome example with numerical utilities and probabilities to show how a convex risk function can reverse a choice that expected utility would make. Do not cite any literature or use quotations; explain the formalism in your own words. Before writing, brainstorm for a few sentences about the difference between probability weighting and risk weighting. Then write a polished argument of no more than 650 words. Begin with a one-sentence thesis.
