Write a satirical FAQ page of the kind a direct-to-consumer genomics company posts under "Our Science," for a fictional firm called Variance Partners, LLC, whose flagship product — the H-Score — is described in its own marketing copy as "the share of your child's trait that is written in the genes." The page should run about 700 words as numbered questions with answers, and it should hold a straight face throughout: no winking asides, no narrator stepping outside the fiction, no disclaimers that break the spell. The target is a practice rather than a person — the selling of a population variance decomposition as an individual's destiny — so no real company, product, or living researcher should be named or thinly veiled, and no real study should be cited. The only quotation in the page should come from an invented methodologist. The usual furniture of such a page (a "Reviewed by our Science Advisory Board" line, a last-updated date, a pointer to "the peer-reviewed literature") is welcome, provided nothing real is named.

The comedy has to be load-bearing: the technical content should be exact enough that someone who works on behavior genetics finds the parody fair rather than merely wrong. Without ever labeling any of these as an error, the page should commit, and then half-conceal, each of the following:

- reading a variance proportion computed in a studied population as a property of one child's trait;
- treating the estimate as a constant of the trait rather than something that shifts with the range of environments in the sample;
- sliding between broad-sense and narrow-sense heritability whenever the sales pitch is better served by the other one;
- leaning on the equal-environments assumption of twin designs as though it had been measured rather than assumed;
- arguing from high heritability to the futility of intervention;
- borrowing a figure from one cohort and applying it to a different population, with a footnote that concedes the problem and is written to be skipped;
- treating a heritability near zero as evidence that genes are irrelevant to the trait;
- folding gene-environment correlation and gene-environment interaction into a single line of small print;
- promising that whatever "missing heritability" remains will be recovered by the next product release.

Somewhere in the middle, quote the invented methodologist — described as "broadly supportive" — in a sentence that, read carefully, undercuts the product. Include one number that cannot be squared with another number earlier in the document, and one clause about what the company does not offer, phrased so that it gives away more than it intends.

Then give the response three sections, in this order and under these headings:

1. "The FAQ Page" — the satire itself, roughly 700 words.
2. "Notes on the Satire" — roughly 350 words. Say which of the satirized claims are simply false or confused and which touch on matters that are genuinely contested among people who work on this: how much weight twin designs can bear, how to read the missing-heritability gap, whether an estimate from one population carries information about another, what a heritability coefficient is even a coefficient of. Keep the settled and the contested apart, and do not let a contested position pass as consensus — including any position the satire itself has taken.
3. "Three Answers, Rewritten Honestly" — roughly 200 words. Take three of the FAQ's answers and rewrite them as someone who understood the material would write them, keeping the plain register of a company page.
