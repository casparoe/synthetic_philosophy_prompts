I am assembling a taxonomic backbone for a biodiversity database that has to reconcile records from bacteriologists, botanists, ornithologists and palaeontologists, and before I settle on a species concept I want the full specification of what one would have to deliver. Set out the desiderata for an adequate theory of species: the cases it must get right (ring species, obligately asexual and apomictic lineages, freely hybridising trees, prokaryotes that exchange genes laterally, fossil sequences with no sharp break), the distinctions it must not collapse (between the species category and a particular species taxon, between the concept and the operational criteria for applying it, between species as classes and as concrete historical lineages, and between there being species and our being able to delimit them), and the findings from evolutionary biology it cannot contradict (that species are lineages, and that monophyly, reproductive isolation and ecological cohesion can come apart). Add the constraints my own project imposes: the concept must be workable for non-specialists, must keep species counts comparable across the disciplines, and must sit alongside the nomenclatural codes, which tie names to type specimens. Then say which of these are genuine desiderata and which are artefacts of my database, which pairs cannot be jointly satisfied — that is the part I care about most — and which single requirement rules out the greatest number of candidate theories. I do not need the contenders scored against the list; the criteria are the deliverable.
