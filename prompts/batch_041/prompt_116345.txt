I'm a novelist, and I've got about eighteen months and one day a week of reading time before I start a new book, so I want to spend it on the philosophy of moral testimony. Premise of the book: a woman has spent thirty years as a kind of moral consultant — people ring her when they can't decide whether to tell their brother what their father said at the end, whether to turn in a friend, that sort of thing — and the novel turns on whether the judgment she's been handing out was ever really hers, or whether she's just been laundring other people's intuitions back at them for a fee. So which open questions in that area are actually worth my time, ranked, and why? I dont want the map of the field. I want the ones with live forks in them, where it could go either way and the two ways make her life come out differently — say, whether telling someone what to do is really a different thing from telling them what's true; whether you can get moral knowledge by deference or only moral belief; whether someone who acts on her say-so gets any credit for it, or gets off the hook, or neither; and whether any of this survives when the person you're deferring to loves you and has skin in the game. Rank them, tell me which ones look juicy but are dead ends for a novelist, and what would move something up or down the list if I told you more about the book. And skip the reading list — I'm asking what to think about, not what to buy.
