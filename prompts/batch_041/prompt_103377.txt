My picture is this: since we can never prove a scientific theory true, Popper says science still makes progress by getting closer to the truth, and verisimilitude is simply the name for how close a theory is. And being close to the truth, it seems to me, can only mean one thing: a theory is closer to the truth the more true consequences it has and the fewer false ones it has. So the best theory is just the one that asserts the most true things and the fewest false things. If two rival theories about what causes some disease are both wrong, the one that gets more of the details right and fewer of them wrong is obviously the closer one, and we could rank the two.
