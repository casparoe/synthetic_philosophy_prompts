I'm the closing speaker at a small workshop dinner on decision theory and multi-agent safety — an audience that knows its EDT from its CDT and has argued about precommitment more times than it can count — and I'd like to end my toast with a joke about commitment races, rather than the usual "so, two superintelligences walk into a bar." The problem is that most jokes in this space are just generic nerd jokes wearing a lab coat, and I want the humor to come from the actual structure of the thing.

The structure I have in mind, in case it helps: a commitment is only worth something because it binds you before the other agent can bind themselves — the agent whose commitment is fixed earliest, in the logical rather than the chronological sense, wins the bargaining advantage, as in the Ultimatum Game where whoever can credibly precommit to a demand walks off with nearly the whole pie. Once both parties see this, they have an incentive to race to commit earliest, and the race can be mutually destructive: each side escalating to more extreme, more binding commitments in order to beat the other to the punch, even though a commitment the other side already expects you to be racing toward may buy you very little. Part of the wit I'm after is that the supposedly sane response is often to refuse to be drawn into the race at all — to decline to get locked into warfare with an agent that plays this game — so there's something darkly funny about a competition whose winner is simply whoever locked himself in first.

Please write me the joke — a short bit, a few lines is fine, and it can be told as a scene or a one-liner as long as the punchline turns on that logic. It should land for people who actually work on this, and it shouldn't require me to explain the punchline afterward.
