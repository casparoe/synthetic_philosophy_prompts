I am presenting a paper at a departmental epistemology workshop next month, and I need a question-and-answer preparation sheet, not a defense of the paper itself. My thesis is a two-tier tracking account of knowledge. On the lower tier I retain the ordinary, evidence-relative notion of belief that most of my audience already accepts; on the upper tier I hold that knowledge requires Nozick-style tracking — sensitivity and adherence — and that because sensitivity is not preserved under known entailment, knowledge itself is not closed under entailment. What remains closed, I argue, is the lower-tier notion of evidence-based belief: whoever has that is entitled to its known consequences. I use this split to defuse the charge that denying closure licenses what is sometimes called the abominable conjunction, the utterance of the form "I know I have hands, but I do not know that I am not a handless brain in a vat." I apply the machinery to the grandmother case and to the skeptic's premise, arguing that the air of absurdity comes from running the two tiers together.

The room: about fifteen people in a department whose work centers on closure, safety, and hinge epistemology. Most are closure-friendly. Several are safety theorists who think sensitivity is simply the wrong modal condition; two are Wittgensteinian hinge epistemologists who think any premise-relative tracking framework has already missed the point; one works on the lottery and on concessive knowledge attributions; one is a formal epistemologist who will want the subjunctive conditionals given an explicit semantics and will press me on how I individuate the relevant possibilities; and there is exactly one person in the room who is sympathetic to Nozick.

Prepare me for the objections this room will actually raise — not the ones I would get from a general audience — ordered by how likely each is and how much damage it would do if I fumble it. Phrase each one in the vocabulary that person would actually use; give me a two-minute reply and a candid note on where the reply is softest; and tell me which objections I should concede rather than fight and which I must hold. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks.

Two constraints on the answer. First, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not hand me a contested view of what closure requires, or of whether safety or sensitivity is the better modal condition, as though it were the consensus. Second, give me arguments and replies, not a bibliography; I cannot use reading lists or citations, so leave them out.
