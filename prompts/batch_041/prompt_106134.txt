I retired a few years back from a career in protein crystallography and have been mainlining philosophy books ever since — no formal training, so fair warning, I probably mangle the vocabulary. Anyway I've ended up genuinely persuaded by something I take to be Nozick's tracking account of knowledge, and I'd like you to try to talk me out of it. Here's the view as I hold it, so you can see if I've even got it right: knowledge is true belief that "tracks" the truth in the sense that (a) if the thing weren't so, I wouldn't believe it, and (b) if it were so, I would believe it — and both of those are evaluated against the way I came to the belief, not against some ideal method. The payoff I like is that it lets me know ordinary things (I have hands) while refusing to let me know I'm not a brain in a vat, and it does that by giving up closure — I can know P and know P implies Q and still not know Q. That trade seems to me worth it, and honestly it feels like the kind of move a scientist makes all the time, giving up a clean general principle to save the phenomena.

So: what would count against it? Not just the famous objections, but which specific cases, which intuitions, which results elsewhere it stands or falls with. I want them sorted by how decisive they'd really be — fatal, awkward, or cosmetic — and I want to know what a committed tracker would themselves concede counts against them, as opposed to what critics just assert. A few things I'm already suspicious of and would like you to weigh in on: the whole business of evaluating sensitivity relative to a "method" seems to let you gerrymander the verdict by how you describe the method, and I can't tell if that's a fixable detail or a hole in the hull; I also gather there are cases where somebody's belief is true and arrives by a perfectly respectable route but wouldn't budge if it were false, and the theory has to say they don't know, which strikes me as the wrong verdict. And I've heard the sensitivity condition swapped out for something like a "safety" condition instead — tell me what that buys and what it costs, and whether the tracking view even survives having its center of gravity moved there.

One more thing, and it matters to me: I know you can't look anything up, so don't bother with who-said-what or reading lists, I just want the ideas. And please be straight with me about which parts of this are actually settled among people who work on it versus which are just one camp's preferred line — I don't want a live dispute dressed up as the consensus, and I don't want you to hedge into mush either. If the honest answer is that the view has been patched so many times it can't really be refuted anymore, say so, and tell me whether that's a defect or just the normal fate of a decent theory.
