Explain how the no-free-lunch theorems bear on Hume's problem of induction, and take a position on how tightly the two are connected.

Start by being precise about what each claim quantifies over. The no-free-lunch results are comparative and average-based: they say that no learning or optimization procedure outperforms any other when performance is averaged over all problems under a uniform weighting, and sharpenings of the theorem extend that same indifference to any collection of problems closed under permutation, meaning any collection that is invariant under arbitrary relabelings of the problem space. Hume's problem is categorical and local: it asks what justifies trusting some inductive method on the problem we actually face, not whether one method beats another on average across all problems. Say whether either claim entails, supports, or merely resembles the other.

Then treat two attempted escapes from the theorem as attempted answers to the skeptic rather than as technical footnotes.

First, the non-uniformity reply: real problems are not drawn uniformly from the space of all functions, so the symmetry does not bind on the problems we care about. Does this answer Hume's question, or does it relocate the assumption? If it relocates it, state exactly what the relocated assumption is and whether it can be supported without circularity.

Second, the universal-prior reply: a prior that is not uniform, such as a universal prior over computable sequences of the sort Solomonoff introduced, escapes the theorem and converges on the truth across a large class of problems. Explain in what sense this is an evasion of the no-free-lunch result and in what sense it is not, being explicit about the class of problems the theorem ranges over and the class the prior covers.

Close by saying whether the no-free-lunch literature changes the dialectic of the problem of induction at all, or whether it is a result about the comparison of methods that leaves the skeptic's question untouched. State the strongest objection to your own conclusion and respond to it. I am after the conceptual structure rather than a literature survey, and I do not need citations.
