The claim I'm defending is roughly this: when two anomaly-detection systems flag the same episodes we're tempted to say they've found the same mechanism, and when they disagree we're tempted to say one of them is simply wrong. I want to argue that both moves are mistakes. My proposal is that 'same mechanism' should be cashed out as agreement on an intervention profile — two detectors target the same mechanism only if they respond the same way to the same set of perturbations, i.e. you nudge this channel or this sensor, does the score move, and does it move the same way in both systems? Where the profiles come apart, the question of which one found the real mechanism isn't an open empirical question waiting on better data; it's ill-posed until you say what you want the mechanism for — prediction, control, or explanation — and those three can pull apart. I have a toy case I'll build the talk around: a recurrent seizure-onset detector and a gradient-boosted detector on the same recordings that agree on about 90% of flagged events but diverge wildly when I do leave-one-channel-out ablations, and I keep insisting the divergence is the interesting part and not a bug, which is exactly the claim I expect to get hammered on.
