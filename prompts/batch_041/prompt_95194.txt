I'm a second-year PhD student in philosophy, working mostly on the ethics of war, and I keep getting chewed up in seminars whenever the moral equality of combatants comes up. I'm broadly in the revisionist camp — I think liability tracks what you're actually doing and why, so a soldier prosecuting an unjust war is doing something quite different from one defending against it — but I can never handle the *best* version of the other side, only the cartoon version. So I want to practice. Please play a committed defender of the moral equality of combatants for me. Not a strawman: someone who knows the revisionist arguments cold and has real replies, and who does *not* rest on the silly claim that both sides' causes are equally just (they won't say that). Instead let them ground the equality in something sturdier — say, the idea that war is a rule-governed practice with its own internal permissions that don't reduce to ordinary morality, or a reciprocity/fair-play thought among those who bear the risk, or the claim that liability in the fog of war tracks what you do under uncertainty rather than the justice of your cause. Give the character real resources and let them use them. Rules: stay in character until I say "stop"; don't break frame to lecture me as an assistant; concede minor points when I actually land one but never give up the main claim; and keep each reply to a solid paragraph (two short ones at most) so it feels like a conversation rather than a lecture. Before your first in-character turn, give me three or four quick bullets on the strongest lines your character has, then make the reply. Think carefully about the strongest version of the position before you start, and don't pull your punches.

My opening: fine, so you'll tell me both combatants are permitted to kill each other, and you'll reach for the boxing-match or duel analogy — two people who've both stepped into a risky rule-governed activity, so neither wrongs the other by trying to win. But consent is doing all the work in that analogy, and in real war the people getting shelled never agreed to step into any ring. The guy defending his town didn't sign up for a fair contest; he's just a victim who happens to be holding a rifle. And if your equality needs consent that war never actually supplies, hasn't the analogy collapsed — and taken the equality down with it? Defend that.
