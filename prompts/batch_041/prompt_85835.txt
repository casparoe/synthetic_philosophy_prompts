I'm a third-year PhD student and I have to hand in a two-page proposal for a small internal grant by the end of the month (roughly £12k, one year, mostly buys me out of a semester of teaching). I've got a half-formed idea and I want you to turn it into the actual proposal.

The topic is the combination problem for panpsychism. But I definately don't want to write another survey of the standard responses, everyone's read those. My hunch is that the whole debate quietly assumes something nobody bothers to argue for: that for any given physical system there's a fact of the matter about how many subjects it contains and which experiences belong to which one. The usual question, how do all the little experiences get together into one big subject, already presupposes a determinate count — one, not zero, not three, not something that shifts depending on how you carve up the system. I want to know whether that presupposition can be defended. And the part I find interesting is that it cuts both ways: if subject-count turns out to be vague, or relative to our interests, or something that comes in degrees, then the combination problem as usually posed starts to look ill-formed — but the panpsychist also loses something, because they can no longer say what the combination is a combination of.

So the proposal should make the question sharp: is there a fact of the matter about how many subjects a physical system realises? And then, for each way of answering, what happens to (a) the combination problem and (b) the panpsychist's claim to have an advantage over the physicalist? I also want the option on the table that experiences don't need an owner at all — there are old contemplative traditions, and some recent work, that say something in this vicinity, and I don't want the proposal to treat that as a cop-out.

- say why the existing families of response leave it open — I think they all just assume a determinate count, but make that case properly instead of asserting it, and please don't fall back on saying the literature has overlooked this, that's the lazy move
- the method: what I'd actually do, month by month, and why the work is conceptual rather than, say, throwing neuroscience at it.
