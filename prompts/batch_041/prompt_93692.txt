So my friend is in her second year of a neuroscience PhD and we ended up arguing for like two hours last night about pain asymbolia, and now I honestly don't know what to think. The thing she kept pushing is this: in asymbolia patients will say, totally calmly, that yes they feel the pain, they can point at it and describe it and rate its intensity, but it just... doesn't bother them, and they won't pull their hand back or reach for the painkiller. Her read is that pain is really three separable things stacked up — the raw sensory signal, the "this is awful" affect, and the urge to make it stop — and that asymbolia is basically the clinical proof that the first layer can come apart from the other two. Which, if it's true, is kind of a big deal, because then the badness of pain lives in that second layer and not in the sensation at all. And then she went further: if that's right, half the animal welfare debate is confused, because maybe fish or insects have the sensory signal without the awfulness, and so nothing bad is really happening to them. I pushed back that "feels bad" might just be what pain is, and that the patients might be misreporting or just coping, but honestly I didn't have a good answer. We also got stuck on a thought experiment she made up — a "hedonic dial" that leaves the pain sensation perfectly intact but turns the awfulness down to zero — because I couldn't tell whether the person is still in pain, or whether anything bad is still happening to them.
