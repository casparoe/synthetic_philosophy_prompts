I got into politics properly about a year ago — joined my local party, knocked on doors, the whole thing — and then a few months back I was at a branch meeting about a housing vote and I said the thing I actually thought, and someone I like a lot told me, very kindly, that it maybe wasn't helpful to say that right now. I couldn't stop chewing on it. Someone lent me Simone Weil's short essay about abolishing political parties and it's definately rearranged something in my head. The gist as I've got it: a party is a machine for manufacturing collective passion; it's built to put collective pressure on the thinking of every one of its members; and its only real end, in the end, is its own growth, without limit. Which means the problem isn't villains or weak characters — even a member who is personally honest gets pulled toward saying what the party needs said, and Weil goes as far as saying that belonging to a party constrains you to lie. Her frame is that the good is truth and justice, and those are impersonal: owed to everyone, not to us.

Anyway. The branch secretary has asked me to do the big ideas slot at next month's meeting: about fourteen of us in the function room above the pub, forty minutes, a mix — a few members of thirty years standing, a couple of new ones like me, one branch officer, nobody who reads philosophy. Don't explain Weil to me, I've read the thing three times. Plan the session for me. What has to come first for the room — do they need the impersonal-good bit before the party bit, or can you back into it from their own experience? What single example carries the whole thing (should I use my meeting story, and if so what does it need and what does it need to not have — I worry it makes my branch look bad and lets everyone else off)? The misconceptions I expect: that she's a crank who wants one party or no politics, that this is just a nicer way of saying don't be biased, that it's about the other lot and not us, and that she's against disagreement itself. Where in the sequence do I head each of those off, and what will they believe instead if I don't? Then one question to put to the room at the end that shows me whether it landed, and what I should openly say I'm not going to cover in forty minutes in a pub. No reading list please, I can't hand anything out and I'm not citing anyone.
