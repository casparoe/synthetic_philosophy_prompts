Putting together notes for a seminar on merit and desert, and before I inflict this on students I want a sanity check on my own position, because I suspect it doesn't hang together and I can't see where the seam is. Here's what I hold: (1) nobody deserves their talents, and — going further than most people are willing to go — nobody deserves the diligence that lets them use those talents either, so the control condition for desert is never met and the whole notion bottoms out; (2) all the same, I'm perfectly happy to talk about desert locally, as in "she deserves that promotion for the hours she put in," and I don't think (1) should force us to give that up; (3) I'm comfortable with markets setting pay, since wages track what other people actually want, and that seems as good a proxy as any; (4) I still think a society that sorts people into winners and losers along these lines is corrosive — it leaves the losers feeling like garbage and the winners insufferable; and (5) on a concrete case, a lab has one postdoc slot, one applicant is a natural who'd breeze through it, the other is mediocre but has fought for every inch. I'd take the fighter. Which of these can I actually hold together, which only look like they clash, and which one am I going to have to give up? Be specific, and rank them by how bad the damage is rather than telling me they're all arguabley in tension.
