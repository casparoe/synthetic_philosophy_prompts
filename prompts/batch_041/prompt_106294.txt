So this is a slightly weird request but it's been on my mind. I got a big promotion at work a couple months ago and it's made things strange with basically everyone I know. My friends back in the US are all delighted for me, which is nice, but my partner is Danish and when I told her family there was this whole thing of like... my mother in law said something along the lines of dont get too big for your boots, and her brother basically changed the subject. And then a friend from Sydney said I was getting the tall poppy treatment. So now I'm walking around feeling like I'm supposed to be proud of this and also supposed to shut up about it and I genuinely don't know which instinct is the right one, or whether they're even answering the same question.

I write a small newsletter and I want to turn this into a quiz for it: which attitude toward other people's success do you actually hold? Like a personality-quiz format but the questions are real cases, so it sorts people by what they actually say about situations, not by which word they like (I don't want any question that's just do you believe in the American Dream or do you believe in Jantelagen, that's useless).

Nine or ten questions please. Each one a concrete case with two or three answer options. Some cases I'd love to see in there, use them or swap in better ones:
- a younger sibling lands a job paying twice what yours pays
- a friend posts a smug announcement about their book deal
- a coworker who you know coasts gets promoted over you even though you outwork them
- your own kid wins a national prize, do you put the trophy where guests will see it
- a guy in town builds a huge house on the hill
- a former classmate who was born rich now runs a charity and keeps getting praised for it
- a neighbour's startup sells and they retire at 35
- you yourself do something genuinely impressive, do you bring it up at dinner
- someone you love is struggling while you're doing great, what do you do with that

Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the actual positions. The ones I want as possible results: the straightforward meritocratic one where success is earned and should be celebrated openly; the leveling one where standing out is a threat to the group and should be quietly cut down; the version where cutting the tall poppy is itself the problem and the envy is what needs calling out; the one where success is mostly luck so you celebrate the person but want the winnings shared; the old aristocratic one where excellence deserves honor and most people are just mediocre; a structural one where the trouble is that success is a zero-sum race so nobody should be celebrating; and an indifferent one where neither celebrating nor resenting is worth your time. If you think there's an obvious position I'm missing, add it, but keep the list from getting bloated.

Two things I really need from you on top of the quiz. First, flag any two of those results the quiz cant actually tell apart with these questions, and say what extra case would seperate them. Second, tell me which answer combinations are just empty, like if someone gives a set of answers that doesn't match any real position, is that because the combo is incoherent, or because it's ugly, or because nobody's bothered to occupy it.

Phrase every question so a reader can answer it without knowing any of the labels. Plain language, I'm not a philosopher.
