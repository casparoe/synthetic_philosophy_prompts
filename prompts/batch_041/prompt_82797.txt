Please lay out the most important implications of pain asymbolia — the condition in which someone reports that a stimulus hurts while being entirely unbothered by it, with the location and intensity of the pain preserved and its aversiveness gone — for the question of what makes pain bad. I have three candidate answers in mind and want to know what asymbolia does to each: that the badness is intrinsic to the sensation itself, that it belongs to the affective reaction the sensation normally triggers, and that it lies instead in pain's capture of attention and its overriding of whatever else the subject was doing. Say also what asymbolia implies for whether a creature that registers tissue damage without minding it can count as suffering, and for how much evidential weight pain reports, pain scales and pain behaviour should carry in patients and animals who cannot tell us how the pain feels. Rank the implications by how much they should move someone's view rather than by how familiar they are, and flag any that would survive asymbolia's turning out to be a reporting or motivational deficit rather than a genuinely hedonic one. Keep the whole answer to a single paragraph of at most 300 words.
