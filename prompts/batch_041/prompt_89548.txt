I keep running into a situation and I don't know if it has a name, and honestly it's bugging me more than it probably should. The shape of it is this: there's a choice I have to make more than once in a row, and the right call at each one depends on which one I'm at -- early on I should push forward, at the end I should stop -- but in the moment I can't tell which one it is. So the best plan, worked out in advance, ends up telling me to do something that looks like a mistake right now, and to do it randomly, like flipping a coin at each stage. If I knew I was at the last one I'd stop; if I knew I was at the first I'd keep going; but because I can't tell, the plan says I should sometimes bail out at what might be the very first stage, which feels premature, and sometimes plough ahead at what might be the last, which feels reckless. Two little examples. A friend is selling a car and gets two rounds of offers -- reject the first and a better one comes, reject the second and he gets nothing -- and when the phone rings he genuinely can't tell which round it is, so "hold out for at least X" is bad advice, because the same number means different things depending on the round. Or a thing at work where there are two chances to pull the plug on a project and both decision points look identical from the inside.

Here's the part that definately gets me though. In some of these cases I could find out which stage I'm at -- I could keep a tally, set an alarm, leave myself a note -- and it seems like the best strategy might be to deliberately not find out, or even to arrange to forget. Because the second I know which stage it is, the coin-flip plan falls apart and I'll just do the locally sensible thing, which is worse overall. Which is bizarre, since normally knowing more can't hurt you. And the whole thing only seems to make sense if you treat the me-who-planned and the me-who-acts as one person whose expected payoff gets averaged, but also as if they're different people who each want to do the best they can where they stand -- and those two readings pull in opposite directions.

So, is there a name for this exact thing? I have a vague memory of a puzzle about a driver who can't remember whether he's already passed the exit he meant to take, and I suspect that's nearby, but I don't think it's quite my case, because there the guy just can't tell, whereas mine is about ignorance I could cure but maybe shouldn't. I'd like to know what the closest established idea is, what it fails to capture about my version, and how all this differs from (a) plain weakness of will or being tempted, (b) the Ulysses-and-the-sirens move where I tie myself down because my future self will want different things, and (c) just being uncertain about the world. And if there's genuinely no name for the exact thing, please coin one and tell me where the edges are -- a bare label won't do it for me, I want to know what the phenomenon actually is and what it isn't.
