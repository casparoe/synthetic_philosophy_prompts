My cousin Mara and her old roommate Dev argued about this in the car for like two hours on a drive last month and I was stuck in the back seat with nothing to do, so I got the whole thing. Mara runs a small nonprofit, Dev is a programmer who reads alot of philosophy on the side. It started when Mara said she felt weird about a bonus she'd gotten, because the org just happened to have a good year and she didn't feel she'd really earned it in any deep sense. Dev said that was muddled, and off they went. Roughly how it went:

1. Dev: deserving is always relative to something — you deserve X for Y. Whether she did the work is one question; whether the work was worth that much is another; she was running them together. Mara: fine, but Y is exactly where the luck lives. She did the work, but she has the temperament that makes her do that kind of work, the health, the upbringing that made her conscientious, and the org was sitting in the right place when the grant money showed up.

2. Mara pushed harder: every candidate basis for desert — effort, talent, sacrifice, willingness to take risk — is itself something whose distribution you didn't choose. Strip out everything that's a matter of luck and there's no residue left over to be the basis. So desert has no basis, and we should just drop the talk.

3. Dev: that argument eats everything. If being the ultimate author of yourself were the price of desert, you'd have to give up blame, praise, gratitude, resentment too, since they all run on the same kind of attributability. And desert doesn't need you to be self-made out of nothing, it needs the outcome to be attributable to your agency in the right way. Mara: then desert is just the name we give to the practices we've already decided to keep, and calling people deserving adds nothing to the explanation of why we keep them.

4. Mara added a second thing: even granting some real residue of desert, the payoffs are a step function of a continuous variable. Two people can be within a hair of each other in skill and one of them captures the entire market. So the gap between first and second can be a hundred to one while any plausible desert gap between them is tiny. Dev: that's an argument that our institutions are badly designed, not that desert is unreal. And he floated something — maybe all desert is institutional, there's no pre-institutional desert at all, what you deserve is just what you could be justified in expecting the rules to give you. Mara: then a slave society's rules make the slave-catcher deserving, and desert collapses into "whatever the rules say," which means it's doing no work. Dev: no, whether the rules are justified is a separate question from whether desert exists; desert is what's left on the downstream side, and that's what explains why resentment is apt and why taking someone's legitimately earned thing is a wrong and not merely inefficient.

Neither of them would give an inch and I honestly couldn't tell who was winning, and I'm definately still not sure. Can you go through it move by move and say who's right about what? Not just who won overall — rate each step, and for the ones that are partly right, say which part. Then tell me what both of them are missing, cause I have a feeling there's a distinction neither of them has hold of.
