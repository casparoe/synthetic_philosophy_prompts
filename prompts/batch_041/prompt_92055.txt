Ok, I've been going back and forth on cognitive penetration for months and I want an actual number, not a vibe. Here's the claim I want your credence on: that in ordinary adult humans, at least some of the effects people cite as cognitive penetration are genuinely changes in the phenomenal character of a perceptual experience — the experience itself is different because of a belief, desire, or expectation — and not merely changes in judgment, response bias, or where attention happens to land. Take 'phenomenal character' to mean the what-it's-like of the experience; if you think that's too mushy to have a truth value, don't bail out, just price that skepticism in as its own term.

Number plus decomposition, please. Mine is around 0.6; a friend I argue with is around 0.15. Here's roughly how I'd carve it up, though override me if you'd cut it differently:

- the paradigms are real: the memory-color stuff (a banana looking yellower then it actually is), the expectation/taste stuff (the same wine rated better when you're told it cost more), and the social-category effects on perception (an out-group face looking more threatening or angry than it is) survive the 'it's just demand characteristics and response bias' critique;
- conditional on the effect being real, it lives in the experience rather than downstream, in judgment or response criterion;
- conditional on it living in the experience, it's penetration by a cognitive state and not just attention — and whether attention-mediated effects should count as penetration at all, since I think a lot of people silently exclude them;
- and whether any of it generalizes past ambiguous or degraded stimuli to crisp, clearly visible ones, because I suspect a chunk of people's confidence quietly rests on it being pervasive rather than local.

Give me a rough number for each with one line of why, then how they combine, then tell me which term my number is most sensitive to and which one is most likely where my friend and I actually disagree (my hunch is the third — that we mean different things by 'penetration' — but you tell me). Then the big one: what single piece of evidence, an experiment or a case or a conceptual argument, would move your number the most, and which way?

And keep it honest about what's definately settled among people who actually work on this versus what's genuinely contested — I don't want a contested reading of the experiments sold to me as the consensus.
