My friend and I have been going back and forth about whether the whole verisimilitude / truthlikeness project in philosophy of science is salvageable, and I want an actual number from you, not a both-sides tour. The setup as I understand it: we want to be able to say that one false theory is closer to the truth than another false theory — that's the whole point, since on the realist picture most of our best theories are strictly false — and Popper's original definition (more true consequences, fewer false ones, roughly) got demolished in the mid-70s by the Tichý–Miller results, which show that under that definition no false theory can be closer to the truth than any other false theory. People have since tried all kinds of rescues: factoring truthlikeness into a truth factor and a content factor, demanding that the consequences doing the work be "relevant", and the whole similarity/likeness family where you rank theories by how close the possible worlds that make them true are to the actual world. My friend thinks its a rigged game — that even if you get a formal metric, the language-dependence problem lets you re-describe a theory and flip the ordering, and that any weighting of different respects of similarity is just a free parameter dressed up as a result, so the whole thing is an impossibility theorem waiting to happen. I half-agree, but I think you might rescue it by narrowing the target: don't try to rank arbitrary theories, only rank rival answers to the same well-posed question, and require the ranking to be estimable from the evidence a scientist actually has rather than only from a God's-eye view of the truth. I'd put my own credence at about 0.4 that a workable account exists.

So: what's your credence that there is a workable account of comparative verisimilitude — where "workable" means (a) it can rank false theories against each other, (b) it dodges the trivialization results, (c) it survives the language-dependence objection, (d) it's at least sometimes estimable from evidence, and (e) it isn't just a relabeling of something we already have like predictive accuracy or explanatory power? Break the number down into the sub-questions it actually rests on — can the trivialization be dodged at all by a formal fix; does any fix survive the language-invariance worry; is there a non-arbitrary way to weight respects of similarity; is the notion epistemically accessible; is truthlikeness even the right target for science — give me a rough probability for each with one line of justification, show me how they combine into the final number, and then tell me which single sub-question the number is most sensitive to and what arguement or result would move it the most.
