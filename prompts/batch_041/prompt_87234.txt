Help me settle an argument a friend and I built and now can't agree on. The setup: God's whole aim, if there is one, is supposed to be a loving relationship with each of us, and the relationship has to be entered freely or it doesn't count. My friend runs it like this.

(1) A loving relationship only counts if you enter it freely.
(2) If God's existence were as plain as the sun, you couldn't help but believe in Him.
(3) A relationship where belief is forced on you isn't one you enter freely.
(4) So a loving God has to stay at least a bit hidden.
(5) And there's no non-arbitrary place to draw the line -- whatever amount of evidence He gives, someone can always reasonably want more.
(6) So no amount of apparent absence counts against a loving God; even a world with zero religious experience anywhere is exactly what we'd expect from one.
(7) So the fact that sincere people can't find Him tells us nothing at all about whether He's there.

My friend says this dismantles the hiddenness argument for atheism. But (7) bugs me -- it seems to make "God loves everyone" compatible with literally any experience we could ever have, and a claim that can't be wrong about anything feels like it's stopped saying anything. Still, I'd rather not give up (1) through (4); that free-relationship stuff seems like the whole point of the defense. So where's the bad step, and what do I pay by cutting it? If it helps, tell me separately what it costs to drop (5) versus what it costs to attack (3) instead.
