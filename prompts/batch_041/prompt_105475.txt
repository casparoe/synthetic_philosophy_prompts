I spent most of yesterday afternoon arguing with an old friend about whether reality has a bottom, and we ended the evening further apart than we started. She holds that the grounding relation — the in-virtue-of relation by which one fact obtains because another does — must be well-founded: every chain of dependence has to terminate in something that depends on nothing further, so there is a fundamental level. I hold that this is a prejudice, and that a world whose dependence structure descends forever, with no floor, is at least as coherent as ours. The trouble is that we both know the standard moves, and knowing them made things worse: we kept firing the same objections past each other and never pinned down what we were actually disagreeing about. I would like to write, with her, the document we should have produced instead of arguing.

Please draft that document: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a defender of the well-foundedness of grounding and a defender of the possibility of a bottomless dependence structure. It should open with a numbered list of claims both sides can sign — about what the grounding relation is being asked to do, about what a fundamental level would and would not have to be, about which of the familiar objections we both regard as cheap shots, and about what each of us concedes is a live option for the other. It should then state precisely where we still disagree, with each side's position rendered in wording its own adherent would accept as fair rather than in the caricature the other side enjoys knocking down. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would genuinely move one of us, as opposed to merely restating a starting point.

For each crux, identify a case, thought experiment, or argument that both sides would agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts about that case. At least one crux should turn on a structure we kept circling without ever naming: a dependence structure that is neither well-founded nor circular, in which every fact is grounded in further facts and between any fact and its ground there is always a finer ground still — no bottom, no loop, only ever-denser descent. State what each side should say about whether such a structure is even possible, and about whether a world with that structure contains anything that deserves the name fundamental.

Somewhere in the document, make the definitional dispute explicit: whether fundamental is to be defined as the ungrounded, as that on which everything else depends, or as something else again, and say which of those definitions the two of us can agree to fix by stipulation and which we cannot. Where it turns out that part of our quarrel is verbal — about which of two things to call fundamental rather than about what there is — say so, and say which part. Where a familiar argument does no work, say that too; I have in mind the thought that if nothing is fundamental then nothing is real, and the thought that an infinite regress is simply unintelligible. I would rather the document be honest about the residue than paper it over with a consensus neither of us believes.
