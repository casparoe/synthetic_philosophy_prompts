I run a small insights team at a mid-size company and every couple of weeks someone pitches something that leans on a generation: Gen Z wants constant feedback so redo our reviews, Boomers won't touch the app so build a stripped-down version, Millennials care about purpose so lead with that in recruiting. I need a decision procedure I can actually run on a pitch before we spend money on it, and I want it built on what's genuinely known about whether generations are real things or just bins we made up. Questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it stops with a hard no, and where it stops and hands the decision back to a person because it's values or strategy and not a fact question. It has to work when all we've got is a hunch and no data, and it should be honest about which steps are judgment calls in a lab coat. My director signs off on the budget, so the output needs to be something I can defend in a five-minute conversation.

Two things I specifically want it to handle. First, the obvious rival explanations: whatever looks generational might just be age (twentysomethings act like that in every era) or just the times (everyone went online in the same decade, it's not a Gen Z thing) — and I gather there's a real identification problem where you can't seperate birth-year, age, and period from each other with the data people usually have, so tell me what the procedure does about that rather than pretending it's solved. Second, the gap between a cohort (just people born in some window, and the cutoffs are basically arbitrary) and a generation in the stronger sense (these people share a formative experience in youth that durably shaped them) — I think a lot of pitches quietly assume the strong version and never say so. Also, does it change anything if the people being labelled have started using the label about themselves?

Then run it on the two pitches, the Gen Z feedback one and the Boomers app one, and make one of them a case where the procedure tells me to drop something I'm actually fond of. Finish with what the procedure can't do.
