I am applying for a small internal grant—three months, roughly half-time, no collaborators—and I need a two-page research proposal that a reviewer from outside my area can assess. The topic is the standard defence of Occam's razor that runs through Solomonoff induction: a simplicity-weighted prior over hypotheses is supposed to be justified because the choice of reference machine is harmless, since any two universal machines assign complexities that differ by at most an additive constant, and that constant is said to wash out as the data accumulate.

My half-formed idea is that this defence answers a question adjacent to the one that matters, and that it looks unassailable mainly because a single word—simplicity—is doing three jobs. I want to separate three currencies in which simplicity can be paid. First, code length: the bits needed to write the hypothesis down, which is what the prior charges. Second, compression: the bits the hypothesis saves when the data are described through it, which is what a two-part, fit-plus-penalty accounting charges. Third, translation: the bits needed to get from the hypothesis as a working science states it into the vocabulary the machine will accept. My suspicion is that the invariance result secures stability for the first currency only, that the second is where the epistemic work is actually done, and that the third is not invariant in any interesting sense, because the vocabulary of a working science is a historical artifact rather than a formal convention. A conservation principle is one line in a formulation that has the concept of conservation and an open-ended list of special cases in one that lacks it; I want to know whether that difference counts as a difference in simplicity, and under which of the three currencies.

Please write the proposal. It should state the question so that it has a determinate answer rather than a family of them; explain why the standard defence leaves the question open, referring to positions and arguments rather than to what any particular author is supposed to have said; set out the method I would actually pursue and a schedule I could keep to, including whether the work is conceptual, formal, or both, and what I would do in the first fortnight if the formal part turns out to be intractable; say what a result would look like, and separately what would count as the project failing; and give an honest assessment of scope for three part-time months, including what I should drop. I would rather the proposal say plainly that the contribution is a distinction plus a diagnosis than overclaim a theorem, if that is what the material supports.

Two constraints. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; describe the relevant positions in your own words and I will supply the references. And do not rest any load-bearing part of the proposal on an attribution, since I cannot verify one while drafting.

Finally, tell me how confident you are in the substantive claims the proposal makes, and single out the one point you are least sure of.
