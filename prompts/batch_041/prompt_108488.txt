Write a dialogue between two co-founders of a hardware startup that's 18 months in and still hasnt got a working prototype. One of them keeps pushing the outside view: if you want to know how this ends, ignore your gut about *our* situation and look at the base rate for companies at this stage with this much runway left. The other thinks that's just a fancy way of refusing to think -- the base rate is an average over companies that aren't ours, and the whole reason we know anything is that we know the details of this particular case. Don't make either of them a strawman, and have them actually go at each other's points instead of talking past each other. In particular I want them to fight about the reference class: our company belongs to a lot of reference classes and they'll hand you very different base rates, so who gets to pick, and on what grounds that isn't just picking the answer you already wanted? And if the outside view is supposed to be the humble one, what happens when you point it at itself -- choosing a reference class is itself an inside judgment, and the evidence that base rates beat expert intuition comes mostly from cases where the expert intuition was overconfident. Let them land somewhere, even if it's just a sharper statement of what they still disagree about.
