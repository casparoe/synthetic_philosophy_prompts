I chair the acquisitions committee at my university's special collections library, and we have to vote Thursday on a donation that's turning out to be a headache. The artist's estate is offering us the only durable thing she left: a single handwritten sheet of instructions for a large wall drawing, plus a codicil saying the drawing itself must be re-made each year on a different wall by whoever we pick, and that last year's drawing gets painted over. So in principle the wall is temporary and the sheet is forever. Half the committee says we're buying a document, full stop -- catalog it, vault it, done. The other half says the sheet isn't the work at all, the work is the yearly realization (or maybe the whole ongoing practice of realizing it), and if we only keep the sheet we've preserved the recipe and thrown away the meal. We need to decide what we're actually accepting, what we're promising to preserve and how (do we photograph each wall before it's erased? do we lock the sheet away and let nobody touch it? do we commission the drawings ourselves or just license the right?), and what we tell the estate.

I don't want a shrug of "it's all in the eye of the beholder." There's a specific distinction I keep coming back to from Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach, where he takes something like a phonograph record and splits its message into three layers: the frame message, which is all the stuff that tells you this is a record and here's roughly how you're meant to use it; the outer message, which is the actual fine-grained instructions encoded in the grooves for getting the sound out; and the inner message, the music you actually hear. His point, roughly, is that the inner message only comes alive when frame and outer are both right, and that the three layers can drift apart from each other.

Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before the vote. Give me a concrete recommendation -- accept, accept with conditions, or decline -- with the reasoning, say explicitly which layer we think the work's meaning lives in and why, and give the strongest version of the side you're rejecting an honest hearing. Keep it tight enough that I can read it aloud in ten minutes.
