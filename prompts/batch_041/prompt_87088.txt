I teach a final-year option on decision theory — 14 students, 75 minutes, and they've already done causal vs evidential, so I don't need the basics again. What I want is an activity where they feel the pull of updateless decision theory in thier gut, rather than just hearing me say "evaluate policies, not acts". The shape I have in mind is the counterfactual-mugging one: a coin is flipped out of sight; on one outcome I turn up and ask you for a tenner, on the other I pay you a thousand only if you would have handed over the tenner in the branch that didn't happen. Half the room finds that obviously fine, half finds it obviously insane, and I want the game to produce that split by itself, before anyone names anything.

So design it: rules, roles, materials, a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money, and a running order. They should hit the moment where the act that looks right in the situation you're actually in is the wrong policy, and vice versa, before the debrief. Then give me a debrief that maps what happened onto causal, evidential and updateless reasoning, tells me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and what to do about that, and is honest about which part of updatelessness the activity simply can't reproduce and I'll have to supply myself. Cap it at 900 words, in British English, and lead with the one-sentence version of the game so I can see straight away whether it'll fit the session.
