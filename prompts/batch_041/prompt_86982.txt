I've been reading a popular book on the philosophy of mind, and its chapter on the extended mind thesis offers the following summary:

"Clark and Chalmers argued that the mind spills out of the skull whenever we rely on an external resource. Their central claim is the parity principle: if an external process plays a role that, were it performed inside the head, we would call it cognitive, then it just is cognitive. The favourite illustration is Otto, whose Alzheimer's forces him to record everything in a notebook; because he consults it automatically, the notebook literally is part of his memory, just as the biological memory of his friend Inga is part of hers. The upshot is that anything you habitually use -- a notebook, a phone, a search engine -- thereby becomes part of your mind, so that cognition is smeared across whatever tools a person happens to carry. The obvious objection is that this cannot be right: a notebook is not conscious, and thinking is something the brain does."
