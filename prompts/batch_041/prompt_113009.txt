I keep going arround in circles on the Euthyphro question and I'd like to actually pin down what I think, so here's my gut on a bunch of cases:

If God commanded us to torture a child for fun, it would still be wrong — so his commanding it isn't what makes it wrong.

A being who knows everything and is perfectly compassionate: if it approved of some act, I want to say the act really is right, and not just that this being happens to like it.

A whole community that approves of enslaving its enemies: doesn't make it right.

Me, after a pill that makes me approve of factory farming: doesn't make it right either.

Me, after ten years of thinking hard and settling on a view: here I do feel like my settled approval carries some weight, more than a stranger's offhand one.

A language model fine-tuned on human approval — basically how they get made these days — approving of something. I don't think that makes it right, though I'd expect a good one to track what's right.

My tentative principle is that an approval counts when it comes from someone in a position to know and care, but I can see this just lets the ideal observer back in, and I can't say what separates it from God. So: is there one principle that gets all six, or do I have to give one up? What does it predict for a superintelligence's considered verdict, or for a species whose emotions are wired completley differently from ours? And is the ideal observer case anything other than divine command theory with a different owner?
