ok, I'm trying to get my own head straight before a seminar on machine translation and I keep half-suspecting my views dont hang together, so I want an audit before I say any of this out loud. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) Translating is preserving meaning, and meaning is use — a word means what it does in the lives of the people who use it. So you can't translate out of a practice you don't understand.
(2) I use DeepL and the like constantly, and for French and German it's as good as I need. I often genuinely can't tell its output from a decent human translator's, and I don't think that's just me being sloppy.
(3) I think the Chinese Room is basically right: shuffling symbols fluently, however well, isn't understanding.
(4) But I don't think understanding is all-or-nothing. It's graded. There's no magic threshold where a system goes from zero to understanding.
(5) I think a translation is only right relative to a purpose. Legal, poetic and tourist renderings of the same sentence can all be correct and disagree with each other, so there's no single "the meaning" sitting there waiting to be preserved.
(6) When someone asks whether a machine really understands, my instinct is that this isn't a real question — it's about our attitudes toward the thing, not a fact about it.
