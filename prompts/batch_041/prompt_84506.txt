I am assembling a handout for a graduate seminar on anthropic reasoning, and I want a decision tree rather than a lecture. The tree should take a student from the starting commitment "I accept that the bare fact of my existing, or of having the evidence I do, can bear on which world I am in" and route them, by their answers to concrete questions, to a named position in the self-sampling versus self-indication debate.

Every branch node must be a question the student can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and every node must turn on a concrete case rather than on a slogan. Use at least the following cases: the standard Sleeping Beauty problem with a fair coin; the Doomsday argument as it is ordinarily stated; the case in which one is asked to bet on a hypothesis that implies vastly many more observers than its rival because it posits extra universes or extra copies, and which therefore seems to be favoured against all antecedent sense (the presumptuous philosopher); and one case of my own devising, which I want you to place correctly in the tree: a biased coin decides between a world containing exactly one observer with my evidence and a world containing a billion observers who each have exactly that same evidence, the coin being weighted heavily in favour of the single-observer world, and I must say what I ought to believe on finding myself one of the observers.

Leaves should be named positions: the self-sampling assumption in its various reference-class versions, the self-indication assumption, any self-sampling assumption restricted so that it cannot license the presumptuous philosopher, the view that one should conditionalise only on one's full non-indexical evidence rather than sample observers at all, and the deflationary view that no such update is ever warranted. Where a position has no settled name, give it a descriptive label instead. For each leaf, add one sentence naming its most awkward commitment.

Build the tree from the logical space first and attach the labels second: begin with the questions that actually separate the views — what precisely is being sampled, whether the sampling is over observers or over worlds or over observer-moments, whether bare existence carries self-indication weight, and whatever else you judge load-bearing — and only then locate the named positions within the space you have drawn. Mark any combination of answers that no one occupies, and say in a clause whether you take it to be empty because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any two leaves your tree cannot separate, rather than papering over the gap. Eight to twelve nodes and no more than eight leaves.

I do not want a reading list, citations, or references to particular authors; I want the structure of the debate, not its bibliography. Please write in British English.
