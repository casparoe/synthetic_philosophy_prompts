I am presenting a paper at an interdisciplinary seminar next week and need a Q&A prep sheet. The paper argues that the widespread assimilation of akrasia to hyperbolic discounting is a mistake. Hyperbolic discounting, properly understood, generates preference reversals that are internally rational: the agent's preferences at each moment are coherent, the reversal is foreseen, and nothing in the model requires the agent to act against her own considered judgment about what she now has most reason to do. Akrasia, I claim, is not that. It is an evaluative conflict rather than a temporal one: the agent judges that, all things considered, she ought to do A now, and yet does B now, with no change in her ranking. I therefore propose to model akrasia not with a discount function at all but as a gap between a person's standing evaluation and her engaged evaluation at the moment of action, a gap that can persist even where there is no present bias and no preference reversal.

The audience is a department seminar with three distinct constituencies. Roughly half are behavioral economists: they treat hyperbolic discounting as the default explanation of every intertemporal lapse, they are allergic to talk of the will, and they will want a formal model or nothing. A third are philosophers of action in the Davidsonian tradition, sympathetic to the thought that akrasia is a genuine phenomenon but suspicious that my standing-versus-engaged distinction quietly reintroduces exactly the faculty psychology they take to have been dispensed with. The remaining few are clinicians who work with addiction and believe the philosophical literature ignores what patients actually report.

Tell me what to concede up front, what to hold, and which single exchange I must win for the paper to survive the session. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks.
