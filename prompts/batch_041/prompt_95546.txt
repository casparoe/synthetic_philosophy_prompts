I want a short story, maybe 1500 words or so, for a collection I'm putting together about Gödel's theorems and the way people wave them around outside of math. Audience is a general reader who's heard of the theorem and vaguely thinks it says something about truth being relative or minds escaping machines, and I'd like the story to quietly cure that without ever lecturing.

Heres the setup. Use it, but rename people and places however you like; the mechanics are what matter. In some city, the legal code is administered by a Bureau whose entire pitch is that the code is *provably* consistent: they issue a certificate swearing that no two articles can ever be made to yield contradictory verdicts. The code has a small fixed set of derivation rules, so a clerk can always check in finite time whether a given ruling actually follows from the articles. And the articles are rich enough to talk about the code itself: one of them says a ruling is binding only if it was derived by the Bureau's own procedure, and another says the Bureau is required to issue the consistency certificate. A new clerk starts trying to actually derive the certificate, article by article.

No lectures in anybody's mouth. I want the limits to show up as plot. Along the way please make sure the story gets across all of these: that the trouble isn't the code being broken or secretly self-contradictory; that the clerk can't just declare the missing ruling unprovable, full stop; that tacking the missing ruling on as a new article fixes that one case and does nothing for the general problem; and that the Bureau's actual escape is to weaken what it advertises -- maybe from "this code is consistent" to "everything we've ever issued was properly derived" -- which is less impressive and still true.

Also give me one character who over-reads the whole thing, selling it as proof that no system of rules can be trusted, or that human judgment transcends any code, and one character who explains precisely why the analogy to a real legal code is strained (real codes are vague, theyre amended by the same body that interprets them, there's no fixed proof procedure you can point at) without being a killjoy about it.

Setting and voice are yours. Just don't let anyone say "incompleteness" more than twice, and don't end on a speech.
