Ok so I'm a second-year econ PhD student and I keep tripping over something that I'm sure is dumb but I can't get past it. Revealed preference, the way I learned it, is that we never need to talk about anything going on inside the head: a person prefers A to B just in case they choose A over B in the relevant choice situation, and "utility" is whatever function represents that ranking. Which I took to mean that utility is *defined* by choice, so choice and preference literally can't come apart. But then I sit in on a behavioural seminar and everyone is completely comfortable saying stuff like "the subjects were choosing against their own preferences" or "this default nudges people toward the option they'd endorse later," and there's all this talk of "experienced utility" versus "decision utility." How is that not a flat contradiction? If preference is choice, there's no gap for a mistake to live in, so "choosing against your own preferences" is like saying "a bachelor who is married." So either revealed preference is just false, or the behavioural people are being incoherent, and since everyone else seems fine with both it's probably me.

The other thing that's bugging me: a friend in the theory group told me that any finite set of choices can be rationalized by *some* utility function, and concluded that "utility maximization" is unfalsifiable and therefore not really a theory at all. But that can't be the whole story, because people in my department spend months running experiments on whether subjects violate GARP, and they clearly think there's something to test. So which is it — unfalsifiable or not? And relatedly, if there really is a gap between what someone chooses and what they prefer, who gets to decide which one was the mistake? I worry it's just the researcher deciding they know the subject's interests better than the subject does and dressing it up as science. I have a feeling there's a distinction I'm missing between the "utility" in the representation theorems and the "utility" that policy people throw around, plus something about cardinal versus ordinal that I never properly got (I thought RP was supposed to get rid of cardinal utility entirely, but then how does anyone say one outcome is *much* better for a person than another?). I'm definately missing something here — can you untangle what I'm actually confused about?
