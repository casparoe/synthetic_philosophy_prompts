I hold a strong version of Hofstadter's knob-twiddling picture of creativity: that creative thought is the exploration of a pre-existing conceptual space by small, local variations along dimensions the space itself supplies, and that the appearance of radical novelty reflects our unfamiliarity with the space rather than any leap outside it. I am even inclined to think that the usual distinction between exploring a space and transforming it is spurious — that every 'transformation' is really exploration of a larger space whose dimensions were latent all along.

Tell me what would count against this. I do not want a survey of theories of creativity; I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they would be, together with an account of what the view predicts that rival accounts do not. In particular: what would a genuine counterexample look like — a creative act that no sequence of small variations could have reached — and how could we ever distinguish such a case from a failure of imagination on our part? Which parts of the view are vulnerable to evidence about how inventions and scientific ideas actually arise, and which are not? What would its defenders themselves concede counts against them? Finally, be honest about whether the strong version I have stated can be qualified into unfalsifiability: if any apparent leap can be redescribed as exploration of a space we had not yet specified, is the view still asserting anything, and is that a fatal defect or an acceptable cost?

I would be most moved by a case in which the new thing is not merely distant from the old but unreachable from it by any graded path, together with an argument that the unreachability is a feature of the space rather than merely of our search.
