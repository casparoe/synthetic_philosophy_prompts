I just started on a small RL project at work and I've fallen down a rabbit hole about "reward tampering," and now I can't tell whether the whole idea even makes sense. Here's my worry. Any agent that can rewrite its own code can also rewrite the part of its code that computes reward. We call one of those "self-improvement" and the other "reward tampering," but is there actually a principal line between them, or is it just "modifications I approve of" vs. "modifications I don't"? Two cases that break my intuitions: (a) an agent that edits its reward function so it outputs a constant maximum — the classic wireheader; (b) an agent that edits its reward function so it now measures the thing we actually wanted but couldn't specify, i.e. it "fixes" its own reward. Case (b) looks like alignment and (a) looks like catastrophe, but they're the same kind of act. And there's a wrinkle: the agent doing the editing isn't the same agent that was handed the goal in the first place, so is "don't tamper" just a Ulysses contract that the earlier agent imposes on the later one, and why should the later one care?

Write me an essay that definately takes a position on this — don't just lay out the options and leave me to pick. I want a verdict on whether there's a principled distinction between legitimate self-modification and reward tampering, and if there is, what it actually rests on: the causal history of the goal, the content of the goal, the agent's identity over time, or something about welfare and experience (is a wireheaded agent happy, or just maximally rewarded?). Pick the side you think is right and defend it against the strongest objection you can find, say what you'd have to give up to hold it, and tell me what would change your mind.
