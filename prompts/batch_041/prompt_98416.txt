Ok so I'm reworking the unit on Nyāya and the pramāṇas for my epistemology seminar and I want to do something different this year. Instead of marching through pratyakṣa, anumāna, upamāna, śabda and then all the inter-school bloodsport, I want the students to spend a whole session doing nothing but building the spec — the list of things any theory of the pramāṇas has to get right — before we let Nyāya or the Buddhists or the Mīmāṃsakas anywhere near the table.

So write me that desiderata list. Not a survey of who said what, I dont want names and lineages, I want the requirements. For each one, give me the case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether you think its actually a requirement or just a prejudice of one camp dressed up as a requirement. Then flag any pair you suspect cant be jointly satisfied — thats the part I most want, because if some subset is jointly unsatisfiable then the list itself is the result and we can skip half the term paper topics.

Things I want in there, add your own on top:

- the error case — I mistake a shell for silver, or a rope for a snake. Whatever a pramāṇa is, it has to let this happen without making perception as a whole untrustworthy. What exactly is the requirement here?
- the smoke-on-the-hill case. If inference works by seeing the reason in the subject, and the universal connection between smoke and fire is what licenses it, then how is that connection itself known, given that nobody has observed all the smoke and all the fire? Is the requirement that the theory supply an account of how the connection is established, or is it allowed to take it as given?
- the wild-ox case for upamāna. Is the requirement that the theory make room for learning something by being told what it resembles, or is that just inference in a costume?
- testimony. The requirement that a speaker's word can be a basic source of knowledge on its own, not reducible to inference from the speaker's reliability. Is that non-negotiable or a sectarian demand?
- memory. Does the theory have to rule memory in or out, and on what grounds — novelty? If novelty is the criterion, is that a genuine requirement or a stipulation that does a lot of quiet work?
- doubt. The requirement that the theory distinguish a state of doubt from both error and knowledge, rather than treating it as low-grade knowledge.
- the counting problem, which I think is the sleeper here. What requirement fixes how many pramāṇas there are? If a camp says there are four and another says two and another says six, what does the list have to say for the disagreement to even be a disagreement? I suspect the honest answer is that most camps never state a criterion and just count, and that if you demand one, the four-pramāṇa view gets much harder to defend.
- validity — whether a cognition's correctness comes from the same source that produced the cognition or needs external confirmation, and whether we know it to be correct intrinsically or only after the fact by acting on it. I want this in the list as a requirement about where warrant lives, not as a recap of the debate.
- defeat. A source can hand you a cognition that later gets overturned by a better one. Requirement, or something the theory can absorb without cost?
- absence. Is non-existence something we perceive or something we infer? Does the list force a verdict?

And here's the twist I want you to fold in. A colleague of mine keeps half-joking that machine learning has handed us a new pramāṇa — call it algorithmic deliverance — where you accept a system's output as knowledge even though the warrant is statistical and you cant inspect it. I want the students to test the list against that. So: do the requirements as youve written them admit it, exclude it, or is it a borderline case that shows one of the requirements was doing less work than we thought? If your list excludes it, say which requirement does the excluding; if it admits it, say which requirement turns out to be toothless.

End with the one requirement that knocks out the most contenders, and one sentence on why. Skip the scorecard, we'll do Nyāya vs the rest in class.
