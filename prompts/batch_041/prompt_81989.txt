I'm a third-year philosophy student taking a course on practical rationality and the will, and my final assignment is a position paper on weakness of will that I then have to defend in seminar. Before I write it up I want an audit of the package rather than a defence of it, because I would rather find the seam myself now.

Here is what I hold.

1. Weakness of will is real. An agent can judge, at the moment of action and all things considered, that she ought to do A, and then intentionally do B, without having changed her mind.

2. What makes the weak-willed act irrational is that it goes against the agent's own authoritative judgement about what she has reason to do, not that it frustrates her desires — often it satisfies them.

3. A sincere evaluative judgement does not by itself move the agent. Judging that I ought to get up now does not make me get up.

4. Practical rationality consists in satisfying one's preferences. Decision theory supplies the standards of coherence, and it takes preferences as input rather than adjudicating their content.

5. The reversals of preference that hyperbolic discounting produces — the near reward looming larger as it approaches — are a defect, not a legitimate feature of a standpoint. Preferences should be stationary: an agent whose ranking of two rewards can reverse merely because time has passed has irrational preferences, even if she is consistent at every moment. I take this to be the same kind of requirement as the ban on a preference that depends on the order in which options happen to be presented to the agent, since in both cases the ranking is being driven by something other than the options themselves.

6. Yet in many particular cases the reversal is the right response. Someone who declines to defer a reward because she has no reason to expect the person she will be in ten years to be continuous enough with her present self to be worth sacrificing for is not thereby making a mistake.

7. I think what looks like a bare preference for the present is usually better described as a discounting of the credibility of the future self rather than a discounting of the value of the future reward, and that these come apart: the first is not obviously a bias, and it is the second that the irrationality charge really targets.

Which of these pairs are actually in tension, and how severe is each — ordered, not just listed? Which pairs only look like they conflict? For each real tension, what is the cheapest revision that restores consistency, and what does that revision cost me elsewhere in the package? I would rather be told that two of these cannot both be had than be reassured that the set is fine, and I would like to know which single revision would clean up the most.
