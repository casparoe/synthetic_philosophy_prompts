I'm a PhD student in the philosophy of AI, writing the criteria section of a chapter on instrumental convergence, and I want the specification settled before I commit to any particular formulation. Give me the desiderata: what an adequate account of instrumental convergence must deliver — the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it has to respect, the observations of current systems it can't contradict — with the consideration behind each requirement, marking which are non-negotiable and which are commitments of one camp. Fix adequacy to the job the safety arguments actually ask of the thesis: licensing the claim that a system's pursuit of its goal gives us no assurance of benign behavior.

I want the list to address at least the following. Claims of the form "most goals lead to X" presuppose a measure over goals, and the standard statements never supply one. There is a choice among convergence as a fact about optimal policies, as a fact about what training dynamics reinforce, and as a byproduct of an agent's uncertainty about its own final goal; an adequate account should say which it is claiming, or show the three collapse. The drive list — self-preservation, resource acquisition, goal-content integrity, power — needs something to make it closed rather than merely suggestive; test it against a satiable one-shot agent whose terminal value is that a particular number be written on a wall on Tuesday. Say what convergence owes to the orthogonality thesis, and what it would take to have convergence without it. And say what to do about the gap between convergence in an agent's instrumental values and convergence in its actual behavior.

If some subset of these is jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect substantiveness with measure-freedom, and a priori austerity with predictive bite — say so; that is a result in itself. Tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude existing formulations, and where your reasoning is likeliest to fail: which counterexample, or which resolution of the measure problem, would lead you to a different list.
