Ok so I've lived in a housing co-op for about four years now — we cook together, garden together, fix the boiler together, argue about whose turn it is on dishes — and it's turned me into someone who thinks about "doing things together" way more than is probably healthy. I've been trying to write down where I've landed so my housemate (who's doing a masters and keeps telling me I contradict myself) can poke at it, and I'd rather find the contradictions myself first. Here's the list:

1) Only individual people have intentions and beliefs. There's no group mind and no "we" that has mental states of its own. When someone says "the co-op decided," that's a manner of speaking.
2) Even so, some of what we do is genuinely joint. When Priya and I cook dinner together, that's one action the two of us do, not two separate actions that happen to overlap. And it isn't just me intending to cook plus me believing she'll cook.
3) I don't think you can build a shared intention out of individual intentions plus beliefs about each other, because then my intention has to be conditional on hers and hers on mine, and that loop never closes — there's no bottom to it.
4) I'm a naturalist, in the boring sence that I don't believe in spooky entities: if something is real, it should turn up in a causal story somewhere.
5) When a company poisons a river, saying "the company did it" is shorthand for a bunch of individuals doing things. I don't believe in collective responsibility over and above the responsibility of the individuals involved.
6) And yet everyone in a city driving on the right-hand side of the road feels like a joint action to me — it's coordinated, it's something we all do together — but nobody intends the whole pattern, so there's no shared intention anywhere in it.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad is each real one? Please rank the tensions by severity instead of telling me they're all equally problematic, and for each one say what I'd have to give up to fix it and what that would cost me, then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Also say how confident you are in your verdict, and flag the one point you're least sure about.
