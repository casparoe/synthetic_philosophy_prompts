I have been trying to locate exactly where Moore's proof of an external world fails, and I want help with the argument itself rather than with what to read. I have read enough of the surrounding discussion to know the standard moves by name, but I have never seen the reconstruction spelled out, so let me state the problem as I see it.

In the 1939 British Academy lecture, Moore holds up two hands and argues that here is one hand, here is another, so at least two hands exist, and therefore external things exist. Everyone grants that this is a valid argument, but it looks question-begging in the plainest possible sense: the premise already contains the conclusion. The proof can only move someone who has already conceded that there are external things, which is the very point at issue. So it does no work against the skeptic, and it seems to do no work even for a neutral party, since a neutral party will ask how Moore knows the premise.

The obvious repair is to rebuild the argument on premises the skeptic cannot deny, namely facts about the contents of my present experience, the sort of thing that survives the demon hypothesis. Those premises are more certain than the conclusion, which is what I take a proof of this kind to require, and they are neutral between the realist and the skeptic. What I cannot work out is the middle. From the fact that I am now having a visual experience as of a hand, what is the step that delivers a hand? Every version I try either smuggles the conclusion back in or stops at the experience.

One person in my reading group suggested that the hand claim is a hinge rather than a premise, something that stands fast rather than something argued for. But a hinge still looks like an assumption you have simply decided not to defend, so this reads to me as a relabeling of the gap rather than a fix.

Two things, then. First, what is the correct reconstruction of the repaired proof, and if there is none, what precisely blocks the step from experience to hand? Second, is this failure the reason the proof is treated as a curiosity rather than a refutation, and if not, what actually keeps it in circulation?
