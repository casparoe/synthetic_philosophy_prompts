I'm a PhD student working on the hole argument, and I want to rehearse the anti-haecceitist line before my supervisor gets to it. Please play a committed substantivalist: you hold that spacetime points are genuine individuals, that the hole argument is sound as it stands, and that its conclusion — indeterminism in the initial-value problem — is a genuine cost of the view rather than a verbal illusion. You regard the sophisticated reply, which denies that diffeomorphic models represent distinct possibilities, as a dodge, and you will not give it up unless I can show you exactly which premise of the argument fails and why; concede minor points freely, but hold the main line. Stay in character, keep replies to a paragraph or so, and break character only if I say stop.

My opening: you say the two hole models agree on everything observable and still differ, so substantivalism leaves you with indeterminism. But the difference you have in mind is a difference in which point plays which role, with all the fields and all the point-coincidences held fixed. That is precisely the difference between two set-theoretic reductions of the natural numbers, and we do not conclude that arithmetic is indeterministic or that the number 1 is a mysterious extra object over and above its position in the structure; we conclude that numbers are positions, not little things. So tell me what the substantivalist about spacetime has that the structuralist about numbers lacks — otherwise the hole argument looks like the permutation problem wearing a manifold.
