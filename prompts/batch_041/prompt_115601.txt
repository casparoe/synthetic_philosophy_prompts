In a seminar on the moral status of animals and machines, a colleague repeatedly uses the line 'every thermostat is a little bit sentient' to argue that if insects are granted some moral weight, thermostats should be granted some as well, however small. Please lay out the distinct precise claims that line could be making, from the trivial to the radical. Separate at least: (i) whether sentience itself admits degrees or only our evidence for sentience does; (ii) whether the degrees are ordinal or cardinal, and whether there is a threshold below which the value is zero; (iii) whether moral status is a separate scalar that tracks sentience linearly, nonlinearly, or not at all; and (iv) whether an artifact can occupy any point on the scale. For each reading, say whether it is plausible, whether it is the reading the colleague would need for the thermostat conclusion, and which reading is most likely doing the work when the line is used to close the argument. Present the readings as a list I can take to the next meeting.
