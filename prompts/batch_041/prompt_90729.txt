I teach a third-year undergrad course on epistemology and metaphysics — 16 students, one 90-minute session — and next week I'm doing transcendental arguments. Last year I just lectured them through the regress, the "you can't coherently deny P, so P must be true" move, and the Stroud-style complaint that this only gets you how we must think rather than how things are, and it completely bounced off. Nobody felt the pull of the move, so the objection landed like a technicality instead of a threat.

So I want a runnable activity, not a lecture, where they hit the shape of a transcendental argument with their own hands before I name it. The thing I want them to feel is the specific vertigo of trying to deny something and finding the denial quietly presupposes the very thing — and then, later, the gap between "I can't deny it" and "it's true."

Heres my half-baked idea, wreck it if you've got something better: swear the whole room into a society whose founding oath is that nothing said in the room is true or justified. Then give them actual business to do — elect a chair, pass one motion, keep minutes — while bound by the oath. The bet is that they can't run the society, or even follow the oath, without treating some claims as true, and that collision is the lesson.

Give me: the rules and materials (cheap, no tech), a timeline for the 90 minutes, the roles if you want roles, and what I do minute to minute. Then a debrief that maps specific moments of what just happened onto specific parts of the idea — the presupposition, the pragmatic self-defeat, the modal gap, and what would have to be added to close it. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (I assume someone just refuses to play, and someone else tries to turn it into a logic puzzle) and how to handle that without rescuing them too early. And be straight with me about the one thing this activity can't reproduce and that I'll have to supply myself, because I suspect the leap from "can't deny" to "must be true" is exactly the part no game can force.
