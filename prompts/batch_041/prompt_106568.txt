I have been following the argument between people who want worst-case guarantees from AI systems and people who are content with good average-case behaviour, and it strikes me that neither side ever says what a guarantee would have to amount to before it should count. I would like the specification first. Set out the desiderata that any adequate account of AI safety guarantees must meet: the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it must not collapse — for instance between a guarantee that holds whatever the system is trying to do and one that holds only because the system is trying to be good, and between safety treated as a property of a model and safety treated as a property of the whole arrangement of model, users, and institutions around it — and the facts about how these systems are built, trained, and deployed that it cannot contradict, with the consideration behind each requirement. Then say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are merely the preferences of one camp, and whether any pair of them cannot be satisfied at once. That last question is the one I care about most, along with which single requirement rules out the most proposals; I do not have a technical background, so keep the requirements in plain terms and let me do the rest.
