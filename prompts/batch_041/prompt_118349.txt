I have been trying to put a name to a certain shape of problem, and, that failing, to work out where its edges are. I have no training in this area, so it may be that the word exists and I have simply never met it.

The shape is this: a choice is open to each of a great many people at once, what any one of them does is by itself almost nothing, and what all of them do together is decisive. I keep running into it in three flavours.

The exactly-one flavour. A radio station promises to play a song only if exactly one listener requests it, and cancels the request if two do. I want the song played. Should I write in? If I stay quiet and no one else writes, it never plays; if I write in and someone else does too, I have spoiled it; and if I imagine everyone in my position reasoning as I am, we all write in and it never plays.

The divided-pot flavour. A settlement, or a giveaway, pays a fixed sum split among everyone who claims it. Claiming is individually sensible — I get a share. But if everyone claims, and above all if everyone claims as much as they can, the share shrinks toward nothing; and once each person expects the others to claim more, their own best reply is to claim more still, and the pot runs away.

The threshold flavour. A strike, a boycott, a petition that succeeds only if enough people join. My joining is invisible in the total, yet the total is the only thing that matters, so everyone waits to see whether everyone else will come.

In all three, sooner or later someone makes the same move: don't ask what happens if I do this; ask what would happen if everyone in my position did this, and act so that the everyone-version comes out well. I learned a version of this as a child — what if everyone did that — and I know it is often rubbish: if everyone studied what I study there would be no plumbers, and that is no reason for me to study something else. So the move looks sound in my three cases and false in the plumber case, and I cannot say what the difference is.

I am not after a reading list. I want to know whether this class of situations has an established name, and whether the three flavours are really one class or several; whether the reasoning move has a name of its own, distinct from the situations it is applied to; and above all where the line falls between the cases where the move is sound and the cases where it is the fallacy. I suspect it turns on whether the others are like me in a way that makes their choices track mine, but I cannot make that precise without going in a circle.

Please say how confident you are in each part of the answer, and single out the point you are least sure about.
