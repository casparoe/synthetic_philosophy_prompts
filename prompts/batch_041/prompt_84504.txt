I've been trying to work out what I actually believe about taking the safe option when the odds are murky, and my own gut keeps contradicting itself. Here's what I'd really choose, case by case, if the bets were real money.

1. Jar A has 50 red and 50 black balls. Jar B has 100 balls, red and black mixed, but nobody's ever counted them. Bet $10 on red, draw one ball. I pick Jar A. Now the bet is on black instead — I still pick Jar A. I know that combination is supposed to be incoherent, but it's honestly what I'd do.

2. Same two jars, except now B's contents were set by flipping a fair coin 100 times (heads, red). So it's not that the odds are unknown — the odds are exactly a coin flip, I know the coin is fair, and red in B is a clean 50/50. I *still* want Jar A. This is the one that bugs me, because there's nothing I'm actually ignorant about here.

3. Now B's mix gets chosen by a friend who wants me to win, versus by a rival who wants me to lose. Against the friend I'll take B without blinking. Against the rival I'll take the boring 50/50 jar. So it looks less like I'm dodging unknown odds and more like I'm reacting to whoever is behind them.

4. Different flavor: I'd rather bet on whether a specific hard thing in my own field pans out in five years than on a coin, even though I couldn't give you a number for the field bet to save my life. So sometimes I run straight at the unquantifiable.

5. And a case where the fuzzy part is the payoff: I'd take a job with a known salary over one that's "up to double, depends how it goes," even when my own rough guess says the second one is worth more.

Is there a principle that actually gets all five, or do I have to give one up? I keep reaching for something like "I dodge ambiguity when someone else is holding the odds, but not when it's my own judgment" — except case 2 doesn't fit that (nobody's holding anything, it's a fair coin), and case 5 doesn't fit either (nothing's hidden, it's just genuinely open). So which call has to go, and whats the cleanest principle left standing? And tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't thought about: a coin I'm allowed to bias myself, where I get to flip it a bunch of times and watch how it lands before I bet.
