I am putting together an application for a one-year faculty fellowship — modest money, no laboratory, no co-investigators, just me and a term of teaching relief — and I want to build the project around a puzzle that a graduate student put to me in my formal epistemology seminar and that I have not been able to set aside. We were working through Jeffrey conditionalization, and I had given the standard account of its virtues: strict conditionalization demands certainty about one's evidence, and Jeffrey's rule relaxes that demand by letting an agent learn a new distribution over the cells of a partition and renormalize within each cell, so that evidence which leaves one less than certain can still move one's credences. What the student pressed was whether 'uncertain evidence' names one thing or several. She distinguished at least three cases: (i) I am unsure what my evidence is, though there is a fact about it — the dial sits somewhere within a range and I can read it only coarsely; (ii) my evidence has probabilistic content — the instrument reports a chance or a frequency or a sample statistic, and there is no proposition about some particular outcome that I could be more or less sure of; (iii) my evidence is unspecific — I have a perceptual state I cannot attach to any proposition, or an impression of a kind for which I have no predicate. She then observed, correctly as far as I can tell, that the standard apparatus is built for (i) and quietly presupposes both a partition and a set of new cell-values that only (i) supplies, and that this connects to the fact that the rule is order-sensitive in a way strict conditionalization is not: two Jeffrey updates on different partitions can be run in either order with different results, which suggests the rule is being asked to do two jobs at once, describing the input and revising on it. My own suspicion — the half-formed idea I would want the proposal to test rather than assume — is that the three cases require three different treatments, that (ii) is handled not by the update rule but by whatever chance–credence bridge principle one accepts, and that the general theory is better stated in terms of a standing partition that belongs to the agent's evidential situation rather than to any particular learning event. Whether that survives contact with the cases I do not know, and that is the point of the project.

What I need from you is the proposal itself, two pages or so. State the central question so that it has a determinate answer — I would rather have a sharp question I can fail to answer than a broad one I can only gesture at — and make clear why the obvious replies leave it open: the orthodox Bayesian line that the input is simply a conditional probability and nothing further; the line that the input is the experience rather than any proposition, so that no propositional treatment is owed; the imprecise-probability line that indeterminacy in the input should be represented as a set of distributions rather than a single one; and the deflationary line that one should decline to use the rule outside the cases it was built for. For each, say exactly what it does and does not settle about (i)–(iii), and where I would be entitled to press it.

Then lay out the method as work I could realistically do in a year with no collaborators: the cases I would construct; the constraints against which I would test candidate treatments, with particular weight on order-invariance, on avoiding any stipulation that has no independent motivation, and on recovering strict conditionalization as a limiting case; the shape of the deliverable, which I imagine as a criterion for when a Jeffrey-style update is well defined together with a small catalogue of cases where it is not and an account of what the agent should do instead; and an honest statement of what would count as failure. Failure would include the three-way distinction collapsing under pressure — in which case I want the proposal to say so and to redirect rather than to paper over it — or the fix turning out to require a partition that can be identified only by already knowing the answer, or the whole apparatus proving to be a notational variant of something already on the table. Finally, give me an honest scope: what is genuinely a year's work, what should be cut down to a single paper, and what I should concede from the outset that I will not settle. Do not include a bibliography or any references; I will supply those myself, and I would rather the proposal speak of positions than of names.
