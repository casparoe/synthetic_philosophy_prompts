ok so I need a satirical piece for a philosophy-ish dataset. Setup: a made-up frontier AI lab (invent a name, dont use a real company) has shipped an assistant that won't take moral positions by fiat — instead every contested question gets routed through an internal "Values Aggregation Engine." The comms team has just published a user-facing FAQ about it. Write that FAQ.

Form: numbered Q&A, deadpan corporate voice, no winking anywhere. Around 650-750 words, with the usual FAQ furniture — a "known issues" section, a "last updated" line, at least one answer that's obviously a non-answer. The satire has to live inside the individual answers; don't let it drift into essay mode.

It should be funny to someone who's spent real time on social choice theory and on the liberal-neutrality debate, and still readable to someone who hasn't. So the jokes need to come from the actual machinery of the problem, not from generic tech-company silliness. Woven in, not lectured:

- why the engine aggregates preferences rather than values (preferences are observable), and the follow-up worry that this is less humility than a category error
- that each user counts as one no matter how much they care, plus the obvious next question about the user who cares a thousand times more — answered evasively
- a Condorcet-style cycle among three user blocs over three policy questions, presented as a "known issue" they're "monitoring"
- the fact that some short list of obviously-desirable properties can't all be satisfied at once, and the FAQ's refusal to say which one they dropped ("a theorem, not a bug")
- the user whose central value is that other users' values shouldn't count, and what the engine does with them
- order effects: same question, different order of consideration, different answer
- the difference between a weight and a veto, and whether anything gets a veto (they say only what's in the system card, and don't notice the circularity)
- why "we're neutral" isn't the same as "we have no position," delivered so the reader sees the gap even though the FAQ definately doesn't

No real names, no real papers, no reading lists, no footnotes to actual literature. The target is the practice — claiming neutrality by aggregating, and the corporate register that dresses it up — not any person. End on a "what if I disagree with the answer the assistant gave me?" question where the answer is basically "that's the system working as intended."
