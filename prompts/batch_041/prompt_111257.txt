ok so we spent basically the entire seminar this week on the torture vs. dust specks thing and I've been chewing on it way too much, I think I might do my final paper on it. the problem is my gut has opinions that don't line up and I want to know if there's one principle that gets them all or if I have to give one up. here's what I've actually got, going case by case:

- one person tortured for fifty years vs. one dust speck in the eye, once, for any number of other people you like: I take the specks, every time, at any number. even at 10^30 people I'd still say give them the specks. I know that's supposed to sound unhinged but that's where I land and I can't talk myself out of it.

- but flip it: one person tortured for fifty years vs. every one of 10^30 people losing one second off the end of their life. here I take the torture, no hesitation. 10^30 seconds is something like 3 billion years of life, and that's obviously worse than one person's fifty years, even though each person only loses a second, never notices, and wouldn't trade a penny to get it back.

so those two bullets are pulling in opposite directions and I can't tell if that's a real distinction or just me being a hypocrite.

- concentrating the same harm in one person: 10,000 specks to one person over the course of a minute, vs. one speck each to 10,000 different people. the concentrated version is way worse, not even close. a single speck is nothing; a solid minute of eye irritation is genuinely miserable. so inside one person the specks obviously aren't negligible, they add up to something.

- a pinprick given to someone in deep sleep, which they don't feel and never find out about: still a harm, just a tiny one. it doesn't become zero.

- one person with a 1-in-a-billion chance of fifty years of torture, vs. a billion people each certainly getting one speck: I'd rather take the chance of torture, which I think means I'm treating a billion certain specks as worse than a billionth of a torture. this one I'm least sure about and it might just be me hating the idea of knowingly hurting a billion people.

- the benefit mirror: one person getting fifty years of bliss vs. a billion people each getting one second of mild contentment. I feel zero pull toward the billion here. benefits don't seem to add up for me the way harms do, which is probly its own can of worms.

what I'd tentatively say is something like: a harm counts for nothing if nobody who has it would trade anything at all to be rid of it, and that kind of nothing-harm stays nothing no matter how many people have it, except if you pile those same nothing-harms into one person until they notice, at which point they're suddenly real. but that makes the second-of-life case come out wrong, since nobody would trade anything for one second either, and yet I'm sure 10^30 seconds is a huge deal.

so: is there a principle that gets most of these, or a couple of candidate principles that split them differently? state it precisely, tell me what it says about stuff I haven't listed (tiny harms to people who die immediately after, harms that only exist as an increased risk, harms to people who wouldn't exist otherwise, that kind of thing), and be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save and which one you'd make me drop.
