Two of my friends have been going at each other for about a month now over whether Sweden or Norway is the more liberal country, and I keep getting pulled in as the ref. The annoying thing is that every time I'm sure one of them has landed a point, the other says something that flips me back. I'd definately like to be able to settle it next time. Roughly how it goes:

Mira grew up in Malmö; Jonas is from Stavanger.

Mira: It's Sweden, obviously. We took in more refugees per head than basically anyone in Europe back in 2015 — that's what liberal means, actually opening the door. We're one of the most secular countries on earth. You lot hung onto an official state church long after we'd dropped ours.

Jonas: That's not liberalism, that's just being generous with other people's money. Liberalism is the state leaving you alone. We're not even in the EU — we told Brussels no twice. You signed a big chunk of your sovereignty over to them and we didn't.

Mira: We kept our own currency, we just never joined the euro. And "the state leaving you alone" — your government owns a giant sovereign wealth fund and a state oil company. That's about the least liberal arrangement I can think of. We've got way more startups and a much bigger private sector.

Jonas: The fund's the opposite of what you think — it's the state saving up so it doesn't have to come after the next generation. And you keep doing this thing where "liberal" means socially permissive when that helps you and free-market when that helps you. Pick one and stay there.

Mira: Fine, let's do it your way. Count everything a normal adult is legally barred from doing in each country. Longer list = less liberal country. I'll bet Norway's list is longer.

Jonas: That test's broken. It weights a rule about where you can buy wine the same as a rule about whether you can say what you think. Ten petty bans aren't one big ban.

Mira: Then what's your test?

Jonas: You'd ask what it's actually like to be a random person dropped into each country. Not the average person — a random one. You don't get to know if you'll be rich or poor, healthy or sick, born there or not. Whichever country you'd rather be dropped into blind is the more liberal one.

Mira: That doesn't measure how liberal a country is, it measures how nice it is to live in. Those come apart. A place could leave you completely alone and still be miserable to be dropped into.

Jonas: And your test just counts rules on the books, which also isn't the same as freedom, since half of them nobody bothers enforcing anyway.

Then they both looked at me. Can you go through it move by move and rate each one — clearly mistaken, defensible, or clearly right — say who's right about what, and point out where they're just talking past each other? And then the big one: is there any test that would actually settle which country is more liberal, or is that not even the kind of question that can be settled? Don't just hand me a winner, I want the moves graded.
