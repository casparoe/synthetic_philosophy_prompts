I am a graduate student in a cognitive science reading group that has spent the term arguing about the free energy principle, and I would like to commission the joint statement that our two factions should have produced as an adversarial collaboration. The first faction, the free-energy realists, holds that the free energy principle (FEP) is a substantive, unifying law of self-organising systems: any system that maintains its integrity against dispersion must, under a suitable description, minimise variational free energy, and this fact explains why brains approximate Bayesian inference. The second faction, the free-energy deflationists, holds that the FEP is either a mathematical truism dressed in thermodynamic language or an unfalsifiable modelling framework, and that its apparent explanatory power comes from the fact that almost any persistent system can be described as if it minimised a free-energy functional. Both sides accept that active inference is a useful engineering paradigm; they disagree about whether the FEP earns the title of a theory of the brain. A colleague has also proposed a distinction I would like the statement to register: between blanket-relative formulations, on which the FEP is always indexed to a chosen Markov blanket and therefore cannot be stated without already fixing the system's boundaries, and blanket-free formulations, on which the principle is supposed to apply to any system whatsoever, with the blanket derived rather than assumed.

Please draft the document they could both sign. It should open with a numbered list of claims both sides accept -- about the formalism (variational free energy, expected free energy, Markov blankets), about what the principle does and does not entail, about the status of the dark-room problem, and about the relation between the FEP and Bayesian brain hypotheses. Then provide a disagreements table with two columns, one for each camp, where each row states a precise disagreement in wording that the camp in question would accept as fair to its own view. I am especially interested in three cruxes. First, whether the FEP is a constitutive principle (any persisting system must be describable as minimising free energy) or a regulative one (it is a normative constraint on modelling, not a description of mechanism). Second, whether the Markov blanket apparatus is doing explanatory work or merely redescribing the boundaries of a system. Third, whether the FEP makes any prediction that a well-confirmed alternative -- say, a predictive-coding model with a fixed generative model and no explicit free-energy objective -- does not also make. For each crux, identify a concrete test, thought experiment, or empirical case that both sides agree would bear on it, record in advance what each side predicts, and say what each side would count as evidence against its own view. Finally, say whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal, and if so, propose a stipulation that would dissolve it without begging the question. Please do not give me a reading list or citations; I want the argument and the formulations.
