ok so for my school's global-issues zine (it's a little online thing, mostly op-eds and one short story per issue) I signed up to write the satire slot, and I've been kind of obsessed with this topic since debate regionals — our motion was something like "corruption is a universal evil and Western anti-corruption standards should apply everywhere," and the whole round fell apart because nobody could agree on what even counts as corruption. My friend's dad works for a big logistics company and he was telling me they have to sit through this online training every year about "gifts and hospitality," where basically a box of chocolates from a client is fine but a watch is a bribe, and there's this whole flowchart, and it's the same flowchart for every country they operate in. That seems insane to me and also kind of perfect. So: write me a parody of that — a fake employee FAQ / compliance-training page from an invented multinational (call it something plausible, the kind of bland name a real company would have), in the deadpan voice of an internal "Global Ethics & Hospitality" team, formatted as numbered Q&A, maybe 600-700 words. The jokes should come from getting the actual arguments right, not from making up fake facts: the line between a gift and a bribe, whether a gift economy is really corruption or just a different way of doing favors and obligations, whether the policy's baseline is universal or just one culture's custom dressed up as neutral, the "it oils the wheels / it sands the wheels" thing about whether informal payments help or hurt an economy, petty vs grand, and the awkward fact that the same document has to pretend a wedding gift from a supplier and an envelope to a customs officer are the same kind of act. I want a specialist to read it and go "ok yeah, they get it" instead of wincing.
