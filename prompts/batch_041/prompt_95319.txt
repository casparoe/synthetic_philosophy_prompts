I sit on the heritage committee in my city — we're the ones who hand out the culture grants — and next week we vote on an application from the Midwinter Lantern Walk people. They want forty grand to stage what their application calls "a tradition unbroken since the 1650s." Our resident historian (retired archivist, does this for free) has dug up that the walk was actually assembled in 1974 by a schoolteacher and a tourism officer out of scraps of a Victorian thing, and that the Victorian thing was itself a revival somebody cooked up for a mill opening. She wants us to reject on the grounds that funding it means the city printing a lie. Two other members say who cares where it came from, three thousand people show up and the old folks cry, so rejecting is just vandalism with a filing cabinet.

Then I got into it with my friend Nell at the pub. She did a module on Hobsbawm and Anderson at some point and kept saying the whole question is confused, because all traditions are invented — christmas, the monarchy, weddings — so the historian's "authenticity" line is just snobbery with a footnote. I said fine, but then what is the historian actually measuring, and if nothing is authentic then the word "lie" has nothing to bite on, which can't be right either. She said that's just me wanting there to be real traditions under the floorboards. I said if we ran her test consistently we'd have to slap a disclaimer on half the city's civic furniture, and she said yes, good, do it. We went round for about an hour and a half.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote. I want a concrete recommendation — fund, fund with conditions, or reject, and say exactly what the conditions or wording would be, including whether they get to keep the "unbroken since the 1650s" line on the posters — plus the reasoning, and an honest account of the strongest case on the losing side. And say something useful about the Nell problem: what is this committee supposed to be measuring when it asks whether a tradition is real, does it matter whether the people doing the walking believe the 1650s story or would cheerfully admit it's from 1974, and should any answer to that actually move the vote.
