Ok, weirdly specific question, but this has been bugging me for about a month and I can't find the right words for it.

I'm on the board of a small housing co-op. A few years back we passed a bylaw: nobody can spend the reserve fund without a two-thirds vote of the whole membership. It passed because an earlier board had drained a similar fund on alot of stuff nobody remembers approving. Fine, whatever. Fast forward to now and everyone agrees the rule was a good idea, nobody wants to raid the reserve, and at the last meeting somebody said, well, if we all agree then why do we need a supermajority, it just makes us slow, lets repeal it. And I couldn't articulate why that felt wrong.

Heres what I keep circling. The rule was supposed to protect us from a future us that would want to spend the money. And it looks like it worked, because we don't want to spend the money. But the only evidence that the rule is doing anything would be a future us that wants to spend and cant. The success of the rule is exactly what destroys the evidence that we need it. And if we repeal it and nothing bad happens, that reads as proof it was never needed, even though it might have been needed precisely to keep us in the state where nothing bad happens. Your never able to run the other branch.

Same shape with my own dumb stuff, by the way. I moved my savings into an account with an early withdrawal penalty, and now that I never touch it I genuinely can't tell whether the penalty is doing anything or whether I just got better with money.

I know about the Ulysses thing with the ropes and the sirens and I guess this is in that family. But he can hear the temptation and knows it's coming; my case is the temptation is gone and I can't tell if that's because of the rope or because there was never anything to tie myself against. Is there a name for this specific bind? If there is, I want the actual term and what it's normally used for. If there isn't one, can you name it and mark the edges, because I care way more about the boundaries than the label: how is this different from the ordinary tied-my-own-hands case, and from the thing where people just convince themselves they never wanted the thing anyway, and from the plain fact that you can't prove a counterfactual?
