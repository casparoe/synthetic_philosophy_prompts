I sit on my university's ethics committee, which has to approve or reject a proposal from dining services to replace beef with a mealworm-and-cricket protein blend in all campus dining halls. I don't have a philosophy background, but I've been thinking about this on my own. The climate case for the switch is strong. The problem is the insects: our entomology department's internal review says there is no consensus on whether insects feel pain, though it puts the chance at something well above zero, and the switch would involve billions of insects a year. Some committee members say that even a small chance of suffering multiplied by that many insects makes this morally riskier than beef; others say we cannot let speculative insect minds block a concrete climate benefit, or we will never act on anything. Our committee's charter says we should minimize ethical risk but does not define it. Please draft the short written opinion I can circulate before the vote. Lead with a concrete recommendation -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions -- then give the reasoning, including the strongest point on the losing side. Also propose a general rule for how our committee should weigh uncertain moral status in future decisions. No citations or reading list. Maximum 600 words.
