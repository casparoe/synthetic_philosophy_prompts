I somehow ended up on the hardship fund committee of our housing co-op — six of us, about forty grand a year to hand out, and I'm the one who has to write up the reasoning every time we meet, which is how I've noticed we've never actually written down a policy. Our track record, roughly: we paid for the whole of a member's ongoing care for a condition she was born with; we paid full relocation for a family whose building got condemned; we covered four months of rent for a member after the factory he worked at shut down; we gave nothing to a guy who lost about fifteen grand on a poker habit he'd always been open about (he kept his job and his flat, just had no savings); nothing to a member who quit a solid job to write poetry and then couldn't make rent (he had family he could move back in with, and we all knew it); and nothing to someone who took out a loan for a motorbike, crashed it, and wanted us to cover his physio — though we did pay for the ambulance and the emergency surgery. Nobody has ever seriously pushed back on any of these. People just nod and say yeah, that's fair.

Next week's application is making everyone twitchy. A member is genuinely destitute, can't cover his arrears, and if we say no he's looking at a hostel or worse. It's down to a two-year gambling problem — he says it started after he was put on painkillers for a back injury and he's never really been able to stop — plus he turned down two jobs last year that he says weren't for him. Half the committee says we've already decided this kind of thing: he chose it, he eats it, we don't bail out people who make bad decisions. The other half says the man is going to be on the street, and whatever he did, we are not the kind of body that lets that happen. I need to know what our own precedents actually commit us to.

So: what rule have we really been running, and what other rules fit every single thing we've done just as well? What does each of them predict for this application, which precedent is the closest analog, and which rule do you think we should own up to adopting if we have to choose? Also, does the ambulance and surgery thing for the motorbike guy change the analysis, or is that just a way of dodging — someone on the committee said it's the whole answer in miniature and I can't tell if that's clever or a cop-out. And tell me what future case would force us to admit which rule we're actually running, because I'd rather find out on our terms than on someone's eviction notice.
