The UK's Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022 recognized sentience in law for the first time, extending protection to all vertebrates and to certain invertebrates — cephalopods and decapods among them — while leaving insects outside its scope. Analyze that legislative choice through the lens of the scale argument for insect sentience, treated as an explicit decision rule rather than a slogan: rank taxa by the expected moral value of their members, taking the product of population size and the probability that members of the taxon are sentient, and treat those probabilities as deeply uncertain rather than known. Given that the number of insects alive at any moment exceeds the recognized invertebrates by many orders of magnitude, work out what the rule actually implies for the insect case, and then assess whether the line the Act draws can be defended as an implicit probability threshold, as a reasonable response to uncertainty, or only as scope neglect. Run the analysis in the other direction as well: what does the episode show about the scale argument itself — does the exclusion of insects count as evidence about the probability of insect sentience, or does it show that the argument's expected-value structure is not how such decisions are, or should be, made? Be explicit about where the argument is silent, and indicate where a competing rule — say a precautionary or a lexical threshold on the probability of sentience — would deliver a different verdict.
