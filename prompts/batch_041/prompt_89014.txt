Ok so I've been reading rationalist blogs and adjacent phil-of-language forums for like fifteen years now (old LessWrong, the Slate Star Codex comment section, all of it), and there's one argument I keep seeing recycle without anyone actually landing a blow. It's about slurs — the words themselves, not the politics. Two camps, and they talk straight past each other.

Camp one: the word's derogatory punch is part of its literal meaning. On this view a slur isn't just a rude name for a group; the ugliness is baked into what the word contributes to the sentence, so a sentence using it says something false or at least expresses something derogatory as a matter of content.

Camp two: no — the descriptive/referential content of the slur is basically the same as the neutral word for the group, and the derogation is something the *speaker* does: a sneer you attach, an implicature, a presupposition, a tone. Not part of the word's literal content. On this view two people can utter the "same" word and only one of them is doing anything derogatory.

People in each camp seem to have a different pile of data they think is obviously decisive and another pile they wave away. What I want from you is the document these two camps could actually both sign after a good-faith adverserial collaboration. Not a dialogue, not a verdict on who's right — the joint statement.

Include:
1. A numbered list of agreed claims — what both sides accept about the data (how slur uses behave, where the neutral counterparts do and don't line up, what happens when you embed them) and about what exactly is in dispute.
2. The points of disagreement, each phrased in wording that the side holding it would accept as fair — so not a strawman version of it.
3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one camp.
4. For each crux, a concrete case, contrast, or argument that both sides would agree bears on it, plus what each side predicts about how it comes out, written down in advance.

Some stuff I want you to fold in, because I think it's where the standard back-and-forth goes soft: the fact that a slur's ugliness seems to survive embedding under negation, in conditionals, and in questions (that "still there" quality); whether the derogation can ever be fully canceled; slurs that don't have a clean neutral counterpart; and reclaimed / in-group uses, which I suspect strains both camps in different directions. Also tell me if some chunk of the fight is just verbal — the two sides using "meaning" or "content" differently — and if so, which chunk.

And please: if the honest answer is that one camp's pile of data really is decisive, say so in the cruxes section instead of faking symmetry. I want the real document, not a polite one. Reason it out from the phenomena — no citations, no reading lists, I don't need you to gesture at who said what.

Write it in American English.
