I sit on the health-and-safety committee at the hospital where I work as a nurse, and over the past few months I've been reading about how other countries have fought over working time — the push for a thirty-five-hour week, and the Japanese practice of officially recognizing death from overwork as a compensable condition — because my committee keeps relitigating staffing ratios and mandatory overtime. What has come to interest me is not the politics of these fights but the recurring shape of the arguments, and I have not been able to find a word for what I keep seeing.

Here is the pattern, with two instances from our own meetings. Nobody on either side will let rest stand on its own as something owed. When we proposed protected rest breaks for the night-shift residents, the proposal went through the moment a colleague framed the breaks as reducing medication errors and burnout. When the contract cleaners later asked for the same breaks, they were told the schedule could not absorb it and that the breaks were something you earn by finishing your rounds. So far that is only the familiar point that everything gets justified by what it produces. What I cannot name is that the very same premise — that rest pays for itself in better work — appears to yield opposite conclusions depending on who is asking. For the salaried and the senior staff, rest is treated as an investment: they are told they need to recharge, and their time off is defended as protecting their performance. For the hourly and the contracted staff, rest is treated as a reward: they are told that if they want it they must earn it first, and their time off is treated as a cost that everyone else absorbs. One justification, and it lands on two classes of worker in two opposite directions.

Is there an established name for this specific move — a single instrumental case for rest that gets used to grant leisure to some workers and to deny it to others, so that a limit on work is converted into a further demand on work for the people who already have the least? I have heard of the Protestant work ethic, and of a distinction between the deserving and the undeserving, but the first seems to concern the moral worth of working and the second whether you merit help at all, whereas what puzzles me is how one and the same argument for resting gets sorted by who you are. I do not want a bare label; I want to know what the nearest concepts are, how each one falls short of this case, and where exactly you would draw the boundary, with a definition and a few cases that sit on the edge. If there is genuinely no term, coin one and defend it against the obvious rival names. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — whether such a term exists, and if not, what you would call the thing — and then give your reasoning, without preamble and without restating what I have asked. And tell me where your carving is most likely to be wrong: what example, or what competing classification, would lead you to revise the definition or the name you propose.
