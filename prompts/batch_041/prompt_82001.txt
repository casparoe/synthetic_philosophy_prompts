ok so context: I'm a fourth-year PhD student in political science, but my whole dissertation is quantative — latent variable models, IRT, factor analysis, mixture models, that toolkit. I keep getting into arguments with the theory people in my dept about whether 'left and right' is a real distinction or just a lazy label, and about what actually holds each side together (shared ideology? coalition of groups? a pile of correlated but separate grievances?). I can never say anything useful back because I don't think in their vocabulary and they don't think in mine.

So here's what I want. Take that whole debate — the 'is it real' part and the 'what unifies a side' part — and restate it as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use: latent variables, indicators, measurement models, dimension reduction, clustering, model fit, out-of-sample prediction. Be honest about the translation, don't smuggle my assumptions in and call that faithfulness. Some specific machinery I'd want you to actually use, because I suspect that's where the interesting stuff is:

- factor solutions are only identified up to rotation, so 'the' left-right axis is partly a convention you pick, not a discovery — and even a perfectly real dimension doesn't tell you which end should be labelled which.
- reflective vs formative measurement: is the latent variable meant to cause the positions people hold, or is it just a compact summary of them? These are different claims and I don't think the theorists distinguish them.
- one general dimension vs a bifactor-ish thing: a general left-right factor plus separate economic and cultural ones. Does anything in the debate turn on which of those is true, or is that just a modeling choice?
- unimodal-with-a-hollow-middle vs genuinely two clusters. People talk about polarization as if a gap in the distribution were the same as two separated groups, and I don't think it is.
- the realism question about latent variables — is the factor a cause, or a convenient description of the covariance? — since I suspect 'is left and right real' is basically this question wearing a costume.
- the operationalization trap: the axis is defined by whatever indicators you happened to include. If your battery never asks about X, X isn't on your axis, and that looks like a finding about politics when it's a fact about your survey.

Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. What in their debate has no counterpart at all in this vocabulary? And for each thing: is that because my vocabulary is missing something real, or because the original claim was vaguer than it looked and the translation just exposed it? Is there anything they're arguing about that survives the translation completely intact and that I should therefore be able to settle empirically — and if so, what would the test even look like? Also tell me the other direction: what does my vocabulary make look easier or more tractable than it is? I have a suspicion that 'what holds each side together' is a question about causal structure and coalition dynamics that a factor model will happily answer with a number regardless of whether it's the right kind of answer, and I want to know if that suspicion is right or if I'm just being defensive about my own tools.
