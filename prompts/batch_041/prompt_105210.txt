I am trying to determine whether there is an established concept for a pattern I keep encountering in moral and historical argument, and, if there is not, to have it named and defined rather than merely gestured at.

The pattern is this. Someone sets two historical horrors side by side — this famine killed more than that genocide; this atrocity was more systematic, more prolonged, more deliberately aimed at a people than that one. The reply is rarely that the figures are wrong or that the comparison is misleading. It is that the comparison ought not to be made at all: that ranking such things is itself indecent, a second injury added to the first. Once that is said the argument is over, and the person who made the comparison is treated as having done something shameful rather than as having made a mistake.

Three things about this puzzle me. First, the same people who forbid the comparison will make it without hesitation when something they care about turns on it — deciding whom to memorialize, which history to teach first, whom to compensate. Second, when the comparison is resisted, the objection is often not that one horror outweighs the other but that they are not of the same kind at all, so that the comparison is not merely tasteless but category-breaking. Third, the prohibition is applied unevenly: it is loud when a comparison would diminish a horror the speaker identifies with, and quiet when the comparison would diminish someone else's.

I take it that at least three distinct things are tangled here: a claim about whether such evils admit of measurement against one another at all; a norm forbidding the measurement; and a rhetorical move that deploys the norm to close an argument. I suspect the first has a home in value theory, that the second may be a special case of a more general ethics of refusing to trade one good against another, and that the third may have no name. What I want is not the label but the carving: which of these is the in-principle claim, which the prohibition, which the move; how each differs from its nearest neighbours; and, if the selective invocation of the prohibition has no established name, a proposed term with a definition and the boundary cases that would test it.
