I've been lurking on LessWrong since the Sequences were coming out, and now I'm in a law-and-ethics seminar on autonomous systems, and the phrase responsibility gap is doing way too much work. Half the room uses it to mean no one can be blamed, half to mean no one is liable, and a couple people seem to mean the system itself should be a moral agent. Before we start grading theories I want the spec. So: write me the desiderata any adequate theory of responsibility gaps for autonomous systems has to meet. Not a survey, the requirements list, and I want your own carving of it rather than a summary of what people say. What phenomena must it explain? Which cases must it get right -- the autonomous weapon that misidentifies a target, the self-driving car that chooses between two harms, the trading algorithm that flash-crashes a market, the hospital allocation model that a human committee rubber-stamps, the swarm where each drone only does a tiny part and the commander sets a broad objective? Which distinctions can't be collapsed -- moral vs legal vs causal, blame vs liability vs answerability, gap vs mere diffusion vs displacement, individual vs collective vs system-level, ex ante vs ex post? What empirical or institutional facts is it not allowed to contradict -- automation bias, opacity of learned systems, the fact that humans are bad at supervising things faster than they can think, the way liability regimes already spread risk? For each requirement give the case or consideration that motivates it, mark which ones are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp (retributivists, tort lawyers, AI safety people, roboticists, etc.), and flag any pair you think can't be jointly satisfied -- e.g., if you require real control and also want to hold someone responsible for opaque learned behavior, or if you want responsibility to track causation and also to be forward-looking. I especially want the requirement that knocks out the most popular theories. And if some requirement is just a camp's prejudice dressed up as a desideratum, say that too.

One more thing: I suspect there's a hidden assumption that a gap is always bad and should be closed. Maybe a theory should be allowed to say some gaps are appropriate, or that the right response is to redesign institutions rather than find a guilty person. So include a requirement about what the theory is for -- allocating blame, assigning liability, guiding design, or preserving moral agency -- and tell me if the desiderata change depending on that purpose. Also, if you can, give a quick scorecard of the usual contenders (human-in-the-loop, strict liability, personhood for the system, collective responsibility, responsibility as answerability) against the list, but keep it short; the list is the main thing. Oh and dont give me a reading list, I can't search and I dont want citations, just the conceptual spec.
