so I'm in this small reading group that's been grinding through decision theory — Newcomb's problem, Hofstadter's superrationality, that whole cluster — and we just spent two sessions on acausal trade, the idea that two agents who can never causally affect each other can still 'trade' because their decision procedures are correlated, so each one can predict the other will hold up its end. Half the group thinks it's the deepest idea in the area, half thinks it's a house of cards, and I keep flip-flopping, which is why I want to write something funny about it for the group's end-of-year zine. Could you write me a satirical op-ed, around 600 words? The voice should be an invented functionary — the head of some future civilization's fictional Office of Acausal Affairs — who is dead serious, a bit exasperated, and trying to convince a skeptical public (they write in alot) that we should honor our acausal commitments and deliver the promised goods to a counterparty in a region we can never reach, see, or verify. Make it read like a guest column, one continuous piece.

The jokes need to come from the decision theory being right rather than wrong: the correlated decision procedures, precommitment, the fact that there's no enforcement mechanism at all so compliance has to rest entirely on the other side having predicted us, the awkwardness of 'confirming' a trade you can't observe, why a demand from someone who can't reach you is exactly the case the correlated-procedure argument is meant to cover, and how the whole thing only hangs together if the counterparty runs roughly your decision theory. Keep him internally consistent — no slipping into 'actually this is all bunk' halfway through — and let the usual worries land on their own without him ever spelling them out. Don't name real people or cite papers, and don't put words in anyone's mouth. Prose only, please: no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables, just the way you'd write a longish email.
