I picked up one of those popular physics books about time (airport bookstore, whole shelf on Einstein) and the block universe chapter made me want a second opinion before I start repeating it at parties. Heres the gist of it:

Since simultaneity is relative, two people walking past each other at slightly different speeds disagree about which distant events are happening right now. Neither of their frames is privileged, so there's no objective present at all, and past, present and future are all equally real -- that's the block universe. Time is just a fourth dimension, so your whole life is already laid out in spacetime like a road on a map; nothing ever really changes, and the feeling that time flows is just something our brains do. Putnam and Rietdijk proved this straight out of special relativity, and once the future is sitting there in the block it's already fixed, which is why Einstein thought the passage of time was a stubborn illusion and why free will is an illusion too.

How much of that survives a conversation with someone who actually works on the philosophy of physics? I'm already fairly sold on the block universe part, so tell me if I'm being too credulous.
