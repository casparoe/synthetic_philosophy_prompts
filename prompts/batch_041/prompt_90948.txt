I have no academic background, but I have spent a few years losing the same argument with myself, and there is a poem I can't write.

Two things happen to me that everyone treats as one thing. The first: a month out, I rank the two options one way; a week out, the other way; the morning of, the first way again. Nothing in me changes its mind at any point. Each moment's preference is sincere; the earlier self isn't lying or caving, he's simply standing somewhere else, and there is no moment at which he is of two minds. The second: I look at what I am doing, judge right then that I should not be doing it, and do it anyway. A friend with some economics says the first is just a fact about the shape of the discount curve; a friend who reads philosophy says the second is the old problem of weakness of will. I suspect the two labels are not doing the work they're asked to do, but I can't say what the difference is.

What I want is a loose pantoum, five or six quatrains, in which the repeated lines are the two options themselves — the second and fourth line of each stanza returning as the first and third of the next — so that the ranking visibly inverts down the page without a single word of either line changing. The form should do the work of the reversal: the reader should see that no line contradicts any other, only reappears in a new position. Somewhere in it, the note you leave for the version of yourself who will find it and ignore it. Let the last stanza or two bring in the other figure, the one who betrays a judgment he is making at that very moment, so that the poem holds both without collapsing them, and let it end without deciding whether they are two things or one. It should be accurate enough that someone who has thought hard about this doesn't wince, and it should not turn into advice.

After the poem, a short note on where it takes liberties and which reading of that difference it is leaning on, separating what is actually settled among people who study this from what is genuinely contested. I would rather know which parts are load-bearing than be flattered.
