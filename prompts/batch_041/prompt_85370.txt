I run a philosophy club at my high school, and I have about 15 students for a 45-minute session. I want to design a game that makes us feel the force of David Lewis's idea that conventions are arbitrary solutions to coordination problems—regularities that persist because everyone expects everyone else to follow them, not because they are intrinsically better or because anyone agreed to them. I want to keep it a pure coordination problem, with no conflict of interest, so the difficulty is not temptation but how an equilibrium gets selected and then keeps itself alive. I am especially interested in the difference between a convention and a mere regularity, and in how a convention can get stuck even when everyone would prefer a different one. Please design the activity: setting, materials, rules, a point-based payoff scheme that does not use real money, and a debrief that maps what happened onto coordination, precedent, common expectation, and stability. I would like the rules to produce a convention on their own, without any upfront lecture, and then to put pressure on it—perhaps by rotating players in and out so the norm has to survive new members—so that we discover how self-reinforcing it can be. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle that, and be honest about which part of Lewis's account the game cannot reproduce and that I will have to explain myself.
