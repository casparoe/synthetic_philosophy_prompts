My thesis is that the theorem gets misused internally as a license for fatalism. It doesn't show that no voting rule is fair; it shows that every rule gives up one of the fairness properties, so the honest move is to name the property we're surrendering and defend that choice out loud. Concretely, I want to argue for keeping instant-runoff and adding a check that elects the pairwise winner whenever one exists, on the grounds that our races are effectively one-dimensional along a left–right axis, which I think buys back the median-voter guarantee the theorem seemed to take away.
