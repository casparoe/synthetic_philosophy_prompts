Let's do a roleplay. You're a comitted social choice theorist — the kind who treats dominant-strategy strategy-proofness as the non-negotiable core of any decent mechanism, not one desideratum among many. Sharp, not a caricature: you'll happily grant that other virtues matter, you'll concede a point when it's actually right, but you will not give up the main claim. Stay in character until I say stop. Keep your replies to a paragraph or so, it should feel like a back-and-forth, not an essay, and no hedging into both-sides mush.

Your position, roughly: if a mechanism ever rewards me for lying about what I want — if my best move depends on guessing what everyone else is going to do — then it isn't a mechanism for aggregating preferences, it's a game of poker, and whatever it spits out is tainted. You'd rather swallow the grim conclusion of Gibbard–Satterthwaite whole than tolerate a rule you can game. Restricted domains, dictatorial-ish escapes, whatever the theorem forces — you think that's the honest price, not a reason to drop the requirement.

Here's my opening, deal with it. You keep saying strategy-proof or bust, but the theorem bites the moment there are three or more options and the rule is onto: then you get a dictator. Fine. And if you let the mechanism flip coins you don't escape either — you just get a random dictator, one fixed person's ranking with a lottery spread over it. So your one master virtue buys us either a single person's will or a coin toss. Meanwhile on single-peaked preferences the median rule is strategy-proof and anonymous and nobody's a dictator at all — so plainly the theorem isn't telling us strategy-proofness is too strong, it's telling us that unrestricted domains are the problem. And the second you admit that, your principle collapses into "find the domain where the rule I already like happens to be strategy-proof." That's reverse-engineering a domain to launder a conclusion you wanted in the first place. So which is it — is strategy-proofness a constraint on mechanisms, or a constraint on what people are allowed to want?
