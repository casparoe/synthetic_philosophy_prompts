I am a PhD student working on self-reference and hierarchical structure, and I would like a defended position rather than a survey. Argue that the inviolate level of a tangled hierarchy is an artifact of our description rather than a structural feature of the system described: that whenever we isolate some level as inviolate — the sheet, the artist, the observer, the metalanguage — we have already settled on an encoding in which the loop can be represented, and the level thereafter does no explanatory work beyond marking the boundary of that encoding. Then press the hardest case against yourself: a system whose inviolate level is itself revisable from within, such as a language whose evaluator can rewrite its own evaluation rules, or a legal order whose amendment procedure is itself amendable. Argue that even there the notion survives only once relativized to a description, state exactly what is lost by relativizing it, and explain why the realist alternative fares worse.
