ok so I'm putting together a humour page for a phil-adjacent mag and I want a Which?/Consumer Reports-style buyers guide review of the competing theorys of free will, written as if they were appliances you can actually buy. call it something like 'Free Will: Six Models on the Bench'. 650-750 words, deadpan consumer-magazine voice the whole way through, no winking at the reader. structure: a short 'how we tested' box at the top, then one entry per model with a model name, a price, a star rating out of five, 'the good', 'the bad', and a one-line verdict. end with a Best Buy and a short returns/complaints column.

the tests have to be the real pressure points, not gags bolted on:

- the determinism test: does the unit still run at all if determinism is true, or does it just shut down?
- the just-deserts test: can you actually deserve praise or blame on this model, or are you only ever a fitting target of certain attitudes?
- the inspection test: does the unit need a part nobody can see or test - anything like an unconditional 'could have done otherwise'?
- the assembly test: standard reliability check, the unit gets built in a lab by a technician who rigs the wiring from the start. say which models only pass if the history of the unit is allowed to matter, and which only pass if it isn't.
- the bypass test: the unit sometimes behaves perfectly normally even when it's main switch is bypassed. say which models survive this and what they have to say for themselves.
- the service-manual test: can the owner ever be wrong about their own freedom, and is there anything they can do about it if they are?

please cover these six: a hierarchical model ('you're free when your higher-order desires endorse your first-order ones'), a reasons-responsiveness model, the plain libertarian model with the ultimacy part, the deflationary model that says free will was never a natural kind worth wanting in the first place, hard incompatibilism, and a budget 'wanton' model that registers no verdicts at all (0 stars, no returns accepted).

the thing I want sent up is the consumerist framing itself - the idea that we shop around for a free will on the basis of whether it preserves the practices we already have. so the running joke is that the reviewer keeps sending units back because the just-deserts feature is defective, and by the end it should be obvious that the reviewer's own criterion (does it let me deserve things?) is the thing being mocked - but don't say that, let it fall out of the ratings.

sidebar, 100 words or so: two customer complaints, one from someone who can't stop taking the objective stance toward their brother-in-law, one from a happy compatibilist whose model the returns desk won't take back because it 'was never supposed to do that' in the first place.

funny to someone who knows the literature, readable to someone who doesn't. no cites, no 'as X argued'.
