Hey — I'm in a seminar on assertion this term and I have to lay out my own position next week, so before I get torn apart I want to know whether what I've got is a consistent position or just a pile of things I happen to like. Here's where I've landed, roughly in order of how confident I am:

(1) Assertion is governed by a single constitutive norm — one condition that makes an assertion proper *as* an assertion, as opposed to rude or unhelpful or whatever else.
(2) That condition is knowledge. Truth is necessary but not sufficient — asserting a truth you don't know is still improper — so I'm not a truth-norm person.
(3) I also hold a belief norm (don't assert what you don't believe), though I've never been sure how that sits with (1).
(4) 'Knows' is context-sensitive: the bar moves with stakes and with conversational context.
(5) But the norm of assertion is invariant — whether a given assertion is proper doesn't depend on who happens to be assessing it.
(6) The reason the norm is knowledge is that assertion's job is to transmit knowledge, to put it into circulation; that's the deep motivation for the knowledge account.
(7) I think someone who asserts what she justifiably but falsely believes is asserting properly, not merely excusably.
(8) And I think you can properly assert something to someone who already knows it — a reminder, a confirmation — even though nothing gets transmitted there, which makes me worry that the transmit-knowledge story in (6) can't be the whole story.

The seminar is split between people who think the knowledge account is just obviously right and people who think the whole single-norm picture is a fantasy, and I keep nodding along to both, which is definately not a good sign. What I want is an audit, not a survey. Tell me which of these actually pull against each other and which only look like they do; for the real ones, say how bad it is and what the cheapest repair would be; rank the tensions by severity rather than dumping them all in one pile. Then give me one committed verdict — which single commitment has to go if I want the rest to hang together, and why that one rather than the others. Please don't tell me it depends on my other commitments; I'm asking you to pick and defend it.
