I want your credence in a thesis about determinism, decomposed rather than asserted.

Here is the setup I have in mind. Any indeterministic theory can be made deterministic by enlarging its state space to include whatever it treats as chancy — put the coin's outcome into the state and the dynamics become a function. Any deterministic theory can be made indeterministic by coarsening its state space until distinct microstates are no longer distinguished. Call this the inflation/coarsening symmetry. The thesis is that, because of this symmetry, the claim that the world is deterministic is not a substantive empirical claim: there is no fact of the matter about whether the world is deterministic, only about which descriptions we find natural.

Give me your credence in that thesis. Decompose it into the sub-questions it rests on — whether determinism is properly a property of theories or descriptions rather than of the world; whether some non-arbitrary constraint (the state space of our best fundamental theory, physical salience, or a requirement that the added variables be independently measurable) breaks the symmetry; whether the inflation move is legitimate or ad hoc; whether the coarsening move makes indeterminism equally cheap and so cuts the other way; whether the empirical inertness of determinism is confirmed or undercut by the fact that deterministic chaotic systems are unpredictable in practice, so that determinism's alleged empirical signature — predictability — is missing precisely where determinism is most confidently asserted; and whether the no-fact-of-the-matter conclusion is itself a coherent, non-verbal claim. Give a rough probability and one line of justification for each, then show how they combine into your number. My own credence is near 0.7 and a colleague's is near 0.25; tell me which sub-question we most likely differ on, which sub-question your number is most sensitive to, and what argument or result would move it most.
