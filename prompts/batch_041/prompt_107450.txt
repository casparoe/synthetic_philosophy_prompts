I have been a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, and I keep encountering a move that I can never quite pin down. In a discussion of resource limits, demographic stagnation, or technological rescue, someone will say that Malthus was right, just early, and treat that as having settled the matter: the prediction has not failed, it has merely been deferred. I want a careful disambiguation of what that one-liner could actually be claiming. Lay out the distinct precise theses it might express, from the trivial to the radical, and give a verdict on each: which are tautologies or near-tautologies, which are substantive empirical claims that could in principle be tested, which are unfalsifiable rescue devices, and which are simply false. Include at least the following axes, and add any others you think are needed: first, the difference between a point prediction and a claim about the distribution of the time at which a threshold is crossed; second, the difference between the core mechanism, such as diminishing returns to fixed factors or feedback loops, being correct and its historical timing being correct; third, the distinction between a theory being falsified and a theory being deferred, including whether an open-ended not-yet can ever count as evidence for the theory; fourth, the possibility that right but early is not a claim about the world at all but a stance toward a research programme, in something like the sense in which a hard core is protected from refutation; and fifth, the normative reading, on which the slogan licenses present action regardless of whether the prediction is true. Then tell me which of these readings the people who use the slogan in arguments like the one I described probably mean, and which of them would actually do the work they want it to do. I am especially interested in the case where the prediction is not merely late but is embedded in a theory whose auxiliary assumptions can be adjusted indefinitely; I want to know whether early is doing any epistemic work there or is just a way of changing the subject. Please write this as a menu of readings with verdicts, not as a defense of any one of them, and do not assume that I have access to the original texts or to the secondary literature.
