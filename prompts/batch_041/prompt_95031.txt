I'm trying to work out what I actually think about when two people are doing something together, going off gut reactions to cases. Two flatmates both get up to make tea at the same moment, each fancies a cup, each knows the other's up and doing it too: that's two separate things happening, not something we did. Same two flatmates, but there's a standing unspoken thing where whoever's up first makes the pot for both: that one's together. A load of strangers form a bucket chain at a house fire, nobody orginizes it, people just start passing: together. Same crowd when the alarm goes and everyone bolts for the one exit, shoving: not together, however closely they're all reading each other's movements. A string quartet: together, obviously. Two rival supermarkets that have matched each other's prices for years, each watching the other and each knowing they're watched: not together, feels like the opposite of together. Two people who happen to be walking to the same shop, fall into step, chat the whole way: I think they walked there together, even though neither needed the other and neither would have changed their route. And two coauthors who write their halves in separate rooms, never talk, but each writes so it'll fit what the other's section needs: I want to say together, and that one worries me. The case I keep going back and forth on is the Mexican wave at a match -- everyone stands up when their neighbour stands up, no plan, no goal beyond the wave itself.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? Tell me what it says about a case I haven't listed: a couple where she cooks and he does the washing up, every night, never discussed, and each would just keep doing their bit even if the other stopped. If your principle saves every verdict of mine, tell me what it costs in complexity; if a simpler one overturns one of my verdicts, say which and why I should give it up. Please don't just name-drop the standard theories of shared intention at me -- state the thing itself, precisely enough that I can apply it to new cases.
