ok so this is a bit of a weird one but its been bugging me all week and the TA just shrugged. in my concepts and categorization seminar we did a unit on this old AI project (Letter Spirit? something like that) where the whole point was to get a program to design a font: you hand it two or three letters drawn in some new style, it works out the style, and then it generates the other 20-something letters so the whole alphabet looks like one family. what the prof kept hammering on is that the project needs two seperate things to come apart cleanly: the essence of each letter — what makes every 'a' an 'a', whether it's blackletter or handwriting or some geometric sans — and the style, which is whatever the 26 letters of one particular font share with each other. the essence is supposed to be the bit that stays fixed while the style varies.

so then in section the TA put this on the board, said "find the mistake," and moved on. I've been staring at it:

(1) If letters have essences, then for each letter there's some feature (or bundle of features) that every instance of that letter has, and that no other letter has.
(2) The essence is supposed to be style-independent — it's what's left when you strip the style away.
(3) Take any feature you'd name as the essence of 'a': a closed bowl, a stroke on the right, the double-story shape (the one with the little hood, vs the single-story 'a' that's basically a circle with a stick). For every one of them there's a perfectly readable 'a' that lacks it — single-story 'a's, blackletter 'a's where the bowl barely closes, sloppy handwriting, etc.
(4) And any feature that really is shared by every 'a' — some roundness, some stroke — is also shared by 'o', 'd', 'q', so it can't be the thing that makes an 'a' an 'a'.
(5) So no feature is necessary, and none is sufficient.
(6) So there's no essence (by (1)).
(7) If there's no essence, there's no fact of the matter about which letterforms are 'a's.
(8) So 'a' isn't a real category, nothing is really an 'a', and same goes for every other letter.
(9) And the argument doesn't care that it's letters — run it on dogs, chairs, games and you get the same result, so there are no categories at all.
(10) But there obviously are letters (I'm looking at a page full of them right now) and obviously there are chairs.

I'd rather keep (10), and I'd also like to keep something like (1), because I don't want to end up saying 'a' is just whatever we happen to call an 'a' — that feels like giving up the game entirely. my gut says the problem is (2), but (2) is basically just what "style-independent" means, so that can't be where it breaks, right? so: which step do I give up, why did it look fine when the TA wrote it out, and what does giving it up actually cost me? specifically, if the essence can't be a checklist of features, what else could it even be, and does the essence/style picture from that project survive at all or is the whole thing dead?
