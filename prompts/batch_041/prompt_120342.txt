I chair the bylaws committee of my professional association and we have to bring a recommendation to the board next month on a permanent rule for ranking candidates in multi-candidate races; right now we just use plurality and everyone grumbles about it.

Two cycles ago our vice-presidential race had four candidates on the ballot and the winner was not the person who beat each of the others head to head. A candidate who dropped out ten days before the deadline is widely thought to have decided it: had she stayed on, the result would have flipped, even though hardly anyone's ranking of the top two changed. The board is split. One faction wants the winner to be whoever takes their pairwise contests, with a written tiebreak for when we get a majority cycle; they say head-to-head is the only defensible standard and anything else lets a fringe candidate act as a spoiler. The other faction wants a points rule, because it's simple, always returns an answer, and they dont see why a candidate who can't win should be able to change who does.

Two things are making this harder than the board realizes. First, our bylaws currently say that the ranking of any two candidates is to depend only on how members rank those two against each other, and I don't think that clause can survive alongside the other things the board wants (majority rule, no single member dictating, everyone free to rank however they like). Second, our procedure settles the pairwise questions one at a time in a fixed order, and I've watched the order alone flip an outcome.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the meeting: a specific recommendation (which rule, how to break cycles, whether to fix or randomize the order of the pairwise votes), the reasoning, and an honest account of what the losing faction is giving up and why it's worth giving up. I expect the board will recieve pushback either way, so don't explain the impossibility results to me as though I haven't read them -- tell me which requirement our bylaws will have to abandon, why that one rather than the others, and whether members will have an incentive to misreport their rankings under whatever you recommend. Under 600 words, and put the recommendation in the first two sentences so the board can act without reading the rest.
