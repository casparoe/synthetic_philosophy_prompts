I'm putting together a handout for a grad seminar on moral trade and compramise, and I want a decision tree rather than a reading list. The starting commitment is: two people with conflicting moral views can sometimes both be better off by agreeing to a package neither would have chosen alone — e.g. I'll vote for your cause if you vote for mine, or we split the difference on two issues we care about unequally. Act as a philosopher who works on moral trade, value pluralism, and compromise, and think carefully before you write. Start with a short brainstorm of the logical space, then give me the tree.

Each branch node should be a question a student can answer without already knowing the label of the view it leads to, and each should be a concrete case or a claim about cases, not a definition. I want the tree built around the distinction between compromise as trade (each gives up something they value to get something else they value more), compromise as convergence (they come to share a reason or discover a deeper value), and compromise as capitulation (one side's values are simply overridden). Other joints I want somewhere on the tree: whether the values are commensurable enough to be traded at all; whether the compromise requires someone to do what they think is wrong or only what they think is less good; whether the gain is measured in each person's own values or in some common currency; whether the agreement changes what the parties value or only what they do; whether third parties or rights are affected; whether the agreement is issue-by-issue or a package deal; and whether the point of the compromise is truth-tracking, legitimacy, or just peace. Include positions like moral trade as a Pareto improvement, moral logrolling, compromise as a second-best modus vivendi, compromise as integrity violation or dirty hands, compromise as moral discovery, incommensurability blocks trade, rights as side constraints block trade, and democratic compromise as legitimacy rather than truth. If some combination of answers is empty, say so and diagnose it — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. If your tree can't seperate two positions, flag that instead of pretending it does. No citations or reading lists.

The response should be committed: don't just lay out every possible option and leave me to pick. Take a definite view about where the real joints are, which leaves are occupied, and where you'd put yourself, and give your reasons. Eight to twelve branch nodes, and every leaf should have the position name plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
