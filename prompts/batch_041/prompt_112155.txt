I host a small interview podcast on the philosophy of language, and next month's guest is a philosopher who has defended supervaluationism about vagueness for most of her career: on her view a vague sentence is true when it comes out true under every admissible way of making the predicate precise, false when it comes out false under all of them, and neither true nor false when the admissible ways of making it precise disagree. The reason I want her on is a twist she has been developing lately — she insists that the admissible sharpenings are not abstract predicates sitting in some platonic warehouse, but rather concrete ways a linguistic community could carry on using the word, so that vagueness is a matter of the open-endedness of a practice rather than a gap in the world. I need eight to ten questions that get past the usual exposition and press her where the view is actually under strain, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, so that I can keep her from settling into a restatement of the position.

I want at least one question on higher-order vagueness: if the line between the admissible and the inadmissible ways of carrying on is itself fuzzy, does her view simply relocate the problem one level up, and if so is that a stable resting place or the first step of a regress? I want one pressing the suspicion that her use-theoretic reading of the sharpenings quietly collapses into the epistemicism she rejects, since there may be a fact of the matter about how the community would carry on even if nobody could know it; and one asking whether she can make it come out true, rather than merely not false, that a vague predicate draws no sharp boundary anywhere. I would also like a question on the sorites: on her account the argument is valid and its conclusion is false, yet no single premise is false, so how does she diagnose the failure — a false premise, an equivocation, an invalid step, or none of the above? And one about the same word being vague in one community and precise in another: is that a change in the facts, in the language, or in neither? Please close the sequence with a question asking her what could count as evidence against the whole approach — something that could actually come out against it, not a rehearsal of its explanatory virtues.
