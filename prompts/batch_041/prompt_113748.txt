I'm a novelist working on a book about a philosopher who has three years to make lexical threshold negative utilitarianism defensible before a public debate. You know the view: suffering gets lexical priority up to some threshold, and past that threshold pleasures and pains are commensurable. I want her to spend those years on the questions that actually matter, not the ones that just look good in a seminar. So which open questions in this area should she prioritize, and why? I'm less interested in the list than the ordering: what's upstream of what, what's tractable for a philosopher rather than an economist, and what evidence or argument over the next few years would move a question up or down. Also, I've got a weird variant I want to use: what if the threshold isn't a fixed line but a ratio that shifts with the sufferer's baseline — the worse off you are, the more pleasure it takes to compensate you — does that change the priority list? And please no reading lists or citations; she can't look anything up in the novel anyway. End with \answer{...} giving the one question you'd put first.
