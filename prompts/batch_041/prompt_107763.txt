I'm taking a grad seminar on inverse reinforcement learning and preference inference, and my advisor is retireing after like 35 years. The department wants me to give the toast at his dinner next month. Half the room will be RL people who know the IRL stuff cold, and the other half is his family, who are lovely but think a reward function is something you get at a casino. Can you write me a 5-minute toast?

Here's the thing I want it to do: the whole reason his work matters is that behavior underdetermines preferences — you can watch a trajectory forever and still not know the reward, because any potential-based shaping term leaves the optimal policy alone. So a toast is basically an inverse inference problem, and I don't want to solve it by pretending I know what he really valued. But I also can't just stand up and say we can't know him, because that's a terrible toast. I want the speech to use that underdetermination as its structure: it should show the audience the behavior (the office hours, the terrible puns, the way he'd answer a question with a question) and then make a case for one reward function over the others without claiming it's the only one. Also there's a running joke in the lab that he's the only person who ever got reward-hacked by his own students, so if you can land something about specification gaming without it being mean, that'd be great. No citations, no reading lists, just the toast. And maybe don't make it too long, his family has a drive home.
