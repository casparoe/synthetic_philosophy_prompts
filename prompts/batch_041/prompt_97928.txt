I'm in a reading group that's been working through philosophy of math stuff, and we keep circling back to the same fight: the finitists/constructivists on one side (our one resident ultrafinitist, one guy who insists 'potential infinity' is the only honest notion, plus a couple of people who just find completed infinities spooky) versus everyone else who's perfectly happy with Cantor, Hilbert's hotel, ω, the whole apparatus. We've been at this for months now. The weird part is that nobody's short of arguements — both sides can do the diagonal argument in their sleep, both know the hotel, both know the Skolem-paradox point about countability from outside the theory — and yet every session ends in the same standoff: 'well obviously you can just define it that way' versus 'defining it doesn't make it real'. It's started to feel less like a disagreement about sets than about something upstream of sets.

My hypothesis is that the real split is about what a mathematical existence claim is *for* — whether 'there is a set with such-and-such a property' is a description that's true or false, or a licence to carry out a certain construction — and that the two sides are answering different questions under one heading. Alternative hypothesis: it's really a disagreement about which rules of inference are legitimate (the constructivists won't grant excluded middle over infinite domains, so of course they reach different verdicts), which would make it a disagreement about logic rather than about infinity. I can't tell which of these is closer, or whether the crux is somewhere I haven't thought of — the meaning of quantifiers, or what a foundation is even supposed to do, or just an unargued intuition about what 'really exists' means.

Could you take the role of a philosopher of mathematics who actually knows the foundations literature and diagnose this properly? I don't want a history of the debate or a survey of positions — I want a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is, grounded in what each side actually says (the finitist's real objection, the infinitist's best reply to it, and where the reply simply changes the subject), an honest verdict on whether my two hypotheses are on target or whether the impasse is better explained some other way, and then, given that diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or an argument that no progress should be expected and why. Start by brainstorming the possible diagnoses before committing to one. And please tell me how confident you are in your diagnosis and single out the point you're least sure about.
