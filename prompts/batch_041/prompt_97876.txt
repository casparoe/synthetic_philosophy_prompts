Design a decision problem for an agent whose probabilities are imprecise, engineered so that the standard rules for choice under ambiguity come apart in a specified pattern. The case should use at most three acts, at most three states, and a credal set given explicitly as the convex hull of at most three distributions, with all payoffs in a single common unit (money, patients treated, bushels) so expected utilities are commensurable. The setting should be ordinary — a farm choosing a crop, a clinic choosing between protocols, a small firm choosing a supplier — and the ambiguity should have a real source (conflicting expert estimates, a small sample, a model the parties know to be misspecified), not a stipulated interval. Ellsberg-style urns and ball-drawing are excluded.

Build the case so that: gamma-maximin and gamma-maximax select different acts; those two acts are exactly the E-admissible ones; and no act weakly dominates another across the credal set, so that maximality and E-admissibility happen to coincide here.

Then do the audit. Print the payoff table and the extreme points of the credal set, give each act's range of expected utility, and state the verdict of gamma-maximin, gamma-maximax, the Hurwicz criterion with α = 1/2, maximality, and E-admissibility. Identify which feature of the payoff structure is doing the work, and report what happens when it is smoothed away — first when the credal set collapses to a single distribution, then when the payoffs are perturbed so that the expected-utility lines no longer cross inside the set. Finally, say whether the coincidence of maximality and E-admissibility in your case is forced by the structure or an accident of it, and give the strongest objection a gamma-maximin defender would raise against admitting your third act.
