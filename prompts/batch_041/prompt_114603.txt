I've been going down a bit of a rabbit hole on free-floating rationales — the idea that there can be a perfectly good reason for something even though no one has ever had it. Stock example: a cuckoo chick shoves the host's other eggs out of the nest, and there's a real rationale sitting there (chick gets all the food, trait keeps getting made), yet nobody in the story entertains it — not the chick, not the host, not the genes. What makes it interesting to me is that the rationale is supposed to be genuinely part of the explanation, and even assessable as good or bad (a bird can do the wrong thing for a real reason, sort of), while there's no mind anywhere that could be holding it. My freind insists the whole thing is a metaphor dressed up as metaphysics; that's definately an argument worth having, but I want to see whether the idea survives contact with a joke first.

So: write me a joke about this. It needs to be funny to someone who actually gets the concept — the punchline should turn on the actual content, i.e. on reasons floating about with nobody to have them, or on the weirdness of a reason being bad when there's no one being unreasonable, rather than being a generic "evolution is blind, lol" gag. I'd be happy with something grim about the cuckoo chick, or a scene where the reason ends up on trial with its own defence counsel, or someone demanding to know the chick's reasoning while the reasoning is right there and the chick is not — but those are just to show the register, do whatever you think is actually funniest, and make it short enough to tell out loud. British English please.
