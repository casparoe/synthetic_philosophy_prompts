I'm in a reading group that has been working through the contemporary revival of Stoicism, and one of our members, a clinical psychologist, keeps returning to a comparison he treats as almost self-evident: that Stoicism is essentially cognitive behavioural therapy with a toga on, that the dichotomy of control is the ancient ancestor of cognitive restructuring, and that the whole thing is best understood as a therapeutic technology that happened to be discovered early. He draws a specific moral from this. Since CBT is a validated, secular, empirically supported method, he argues, we can and should help ourselves to the Stoic toolkit — the dichotomy of control, premeditation of evils, the morning and evening review, the view from above — while discarding the cosmology, the theology, and the metaphysics of nature that the original authors hung it on. The techniques, on his account, are detachable from the doctrine; the therapy is the part that works, and the rest is period costume.

I would like you to audit this analogy rather than simply agree or disagree with it. Begin by laying out the correspondence explicitly — a table would be welcome — saying what in Stoicism plays the role of what in CBT: which Stoic element corresponds to the cognitive model of emotion, which to the therapeutic exercises, which to the therapeutic alliance, which to the criterion of success, and which to the underlying theory of the mind. Then tell me where the mapping strains or breaks. I am interested in the asymmetries of aim (virtue and a life according to nature, as against the reduction of distress and improved functioning), in what the Stoics said about the emotions and whether that lines up with what a cognitive therapist means by a maladaptive belief, in the role of providence and the rational order of the cosmos, and in the way Stoicism was a communal and political philosophy rather than a private clinic. I also want you to say what the analogy quietly imports from the source side that the target side may not have — the presumption that Stoicism is a value-neutral technique, that it is individualistic, that it is apolitical, and that it is empirically vindicated because its descendant is. Finally, and most importantly: does my friend's conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table? Can the techniques really be lifted out of the system that generated them and still be the same techniques, or does the dichotomy of control, say, mean something different once the doctrine of indifferents and the providential cosmos are removed? If the analogy cannot carry that conclusion, say so plainly, and tell me what a better comparison would be — or whether the comparison is the wrong shape of thing entirely, and what work the claim that CBT descends from Stoicism was really doing in the first place.
