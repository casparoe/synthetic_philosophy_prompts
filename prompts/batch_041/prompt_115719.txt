I'm a philosophy student in a seminar on evolution and cognition, and our term project is an adversarial collaboration. The seminar has split into two camps over whether honest signaling requires waste, and I've been asked to draft the joint statement the two camps could actually sign. Here are the two positions as they've settled out in our meetings, and I want you to write the document from inside both of them.

The handicap camp: a signal is trustworthy only because it is differentially costly. What makes it honest is that producing it costs a high-quality signaler less than a low-quality one, so that low-quality signalers do better by not signaling at all. On this view the cost has to be a real fitness cost, paid in survival or reproduction, and the signal's informational content is parasitic on that cost: strip the cost away and the signal becomes cheap talk, however reliably it happens to correlate with the trait.

The index camp: honesty does not require waste, because honesty can be underwritten by a causal or physical link between the signal and the underlying quality. If the signal is a byproduct of the trait itself, faking it is not merely unprofitable but impossible, and cost is doing no explanatory work at all. Where no such link exists, honesty can instead be secured by contingent conventions backed by sanctions or by alignment of interests rather than by expense.

I want the document to take on four stipulations that go beyond what our readings cover, and I care more about these than about summarizing the standard positions.

First, a cost decomposition. Distinguish efficacy costs, what it takes for the signal to be produced, transmitted and detected at all, from strategic costs, what it takes to make the signal unprofitable to fake. Both camps should say which of the two the handicap principle is really about, and whether a signal with zero strategic cost can be honest.

Second, a trichotomy. Treat handicap, index and convention as three distinct mechanisms of honesty, and give, for each, a criterion that would let us classify a signal without circularity and without the label just restating the mechanism's conclusion.

Third, a discriminating manipulation. Suppose we sever the causal link between the underlying quality and the signal while leaving the cost structure untouched, so that the signal becomes cheap to fake but the differential marginal cost of producing it is unchanged. The index camp should predict honesty collapses; the handicap camp should predict it survives, since the cost differential is intact. Also consider a flat cost increase applied to all signalers regardless of quality, and a cost increase applied only to low-quality signalers. Write down both camps' advance predictions for each.

Fourth, an application. Consider a case from human life in which the signal is a costly personal commitment that is publicly observable but of no direct benefit to the observer, and say what each camp predicts about how such commitments should vary with the quality they purport to reveal.

The deliverable is the joint statement, in four parts. (a) A numbered list of claims both camps can sign: the formal core, the cost decomposition, the scope conditions under which each mechanism is supposed to operate, what would count as evidence for each, and what the empirical record does and does not settle. (b) A disagreements table, one row per remaining question, the question stated neutrally and each camp's answer given in wording that camp would accept as its own. (c) The cruxes: which sub-questions, if settled, would move at least one camp, each with both camps' predictions recorded in advance. (d) Your judgment about which disagreements are merely verbal and why.

Please state everything in plain terms without citing papers, authors or dates, since I have no way to check them. At the end, tell me which claim in part (a) you are least confident both camps would actually sign, and what argument or piece of evidence would change your mind about it. Finish with a final line of the form \answer{...} containing a short label for the crux you judge most likely to be settled empirically.
