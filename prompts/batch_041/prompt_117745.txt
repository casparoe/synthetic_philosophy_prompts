Two research groups have spent several years arguing past each other about insect sentience, and have now agreed to publish a single joint statement. You are to draft that statement.

The first group advances what we shall call the scale argument. On its view, because the number of insects and other small invertebrates is so vast, even a very low probability that any one of them is sentient yields an expected quantity of morally significant experience that dwarfs what is at stake in vertebrate-focused work; the uncertainty therefore damps the verdict without overturning it, and the rational priority is to act on invertebrate welfare now. The second group holds that the uncertainty here is not merely a low number but a different kind of thing, and that it defeats the arithmetic rather than merely depressing it: on this view the scale argument's verdict is an artefact of plugging probabilities into an expected-value calculation where they do not belong.

The statement must be intelligible to a reader who has followed neither side, so the following stipulated vocabulary is supplied and is to be used explicitly and consistently; do not substitute rival terminology for it, and do not assume the reader knows it.

- Token uncertainty and kind uncertainty: the difference between uncertainty about whether this particular individual is sentient and uncertainty about whether the kind to which it belongs -- a species, a life-stage, a guild -- is sentient at all.
- The common-cause problem: the observation that kind uncertainty is shared across all members of the kind, so that the individual-level probabilities are not independent draws but reflections of a single unresolved fact.
- The additivity assumption: the premise that moral value aggregates by simple summation across individuals of uncertain status, and the question whether that premise survives the common-cause problem.
- The sign problem: uncertainty not about whether there is a subject of experience but about whether a given intervention improves or worsens that subject's welfare, and what follows when the sign is unresolved and plausibly symmetric.
- Option value of research: the possibility that resolving the uncertainty is itself the highest-value intervention available under either side's own commitments.

Treat the underlying biology as genuinely unresolved: no settled account of which taxa are sentient, no settled view on whether sentience admits of degrees, and no agreed behavioural or neural marker. Do not appeal to particular studies, authors, publications or findings, and do not supply figures; where a quantity matters, write about the role it plays in the argument rather than its value.

Produce the statement in the following parts.

1. Agreed claims, numbered. These should cover what each side concedes to the other, what both accept about the structure of the scale argument, the conditions under which expected-value reasoning is and is not appropriate, and what the science does and does not currently establish. Include at least two claims that each side would find unwelcome but cannot deny.
2. Disagreements, each stated three times: once in wording the first group would accept as its own, once in wording the second group would accept as its own, and once in a neutral restatement both agree is accurate. Each self-formulation must be signable by its own side without the opposing side's vocabulary doing the work.
3. A cruxes table. For each crux give: the sub-question; the case, argument or thought experiment both sides agree bears on it; each side's prediction about how it would come out, recorded in advance; and whether the crux is empirical, conceptual or evaluative. At least one crux should be one both sides agree is in principle empirical but not presently resolvable.
4. A short section identifying any part of the dispute that is merely verbal, with the stipulation that would dissolve it, and saying whether what remains is a genuine disagreement.
5. A closing section on what each side would count as evidence or argument against its own position, and whether any of it is obtainable.

The document must be signable by both groups, so do not adjudicate which side is right overall; if one side's position proves unstable under its own commitments, say which step fails without declaring the other side the winner. Write in British English, in a register suitable for joint publication, at roughly 1,200 to 1,800 words. No bibliography, citations or references of any kind.
