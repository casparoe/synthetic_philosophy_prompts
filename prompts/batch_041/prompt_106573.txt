I am trying to decide whether to undergo a preventive procedure that my physician recommends. She tells me that among patients in my broad category — my age band and general health — the procedure reduces the ten-year risk of a serious event from roughly 4% to roughly 2%. A friend with training in biostatistics reviewed the same study and observed that if I am grouped instead by the handful of biomarkers that appear to drive the outcome, my baseline risk is closer to 0.5%, and the procedure would barely help me. Both numbers come from the same data; the only difference is how patients like me are defined. The broad group contains thousands of patients, while the narrow group contains only a few dozen. I understand that these figures describe groups rather than me in particular, yet I still have to make one decision as one person. Which grouping should guide me, and on what grounds? Is the choice something further data could settle, or does it ultimately reflect what I am trying to predict and why?
