ok so im not a philosopher or anything, i just read a lot and i've been going down a rabbit hole lately. I came across this condition called pain asymbolia — apparently there are people who, after certain kinds of brain damage (or sometimes other stuff going on), will say that yes something hurts, they can feel it, they can point to where it is, but it just doesn't bother them. Like the pain is there but the "ow, this is awful, make it stop" part is missing. They're not distressed about it at all. Which is such a strange thing to even imagine.

Anyway a friend of mine who's into this explained it to me with an analogy i keep thinking about. He said pain is basicly the body's alarm system, like a smoke detector. The hurting feeling is the alarm going off, and the reason pain is bad is the same reason the alarm is bad — it's loud and annoying and you just want it to shut up. So asymbolia, on his picture, is like a smoke detector that still goes off but you've somehow stopped being bothered by it. The signal still fires, you just don't mind anymore.

Then he made a bigger claim off the back of it. He said: if you can have the pain without the badness, that proves the badness isn't really part of the pain at all — it's just our reaction to it. So pain isn't bad in itself, it's only bad because we happen to dislike it. And if thats true then all the handwringing about pain being "intrinsically bad" is confused.

That feels too quick to me but i can't quite say why. Can you lay the analogy out properly — like a little table of what maps onto what (what plays the alarm, what plays the noise, what plays the annoyance, what plays the fire) — and then tell me where it breaks down? What i really want to know is: is the badness of pain supposed to be more like the badness of the alarm noise, or more like the badness of the thing the alarm is warning you about? And does his conclusion actually follow, or is the analogy quietly doing all the work for him? And if there's a better way to picture the whole thing that doesn't smuggle in a conclusion, what would that be?
