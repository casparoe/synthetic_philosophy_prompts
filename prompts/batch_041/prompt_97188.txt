I'm a machine learning person — model selection, minimum description length, rate-distortion, that whole neighborhood — and I keep getting into it with a philosopher friend about Dennett's real patterns and the three stances (physical, design, intentional). I think I basically already agree with him but we talk past each other constantly, so I want you to do the boring honest work: restate the whole position in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then tell me what didn't survive the translation.

Here's my instinct. A stance is just a model class you allow yourself over the same data. The physical stance is a low-level model that costs a lot to describe but pays off in fine-grained prediction. The design stance is a coarser model that says the thing has a function or purpose. The intentional stance is coarser still — treat the thing as having beliefs and desires. And a real pattern, on my reading, is just what's left when you pick the model class that gives the best trade-off between the description length of the model plus the description length of the data given the model — the low-complexity end of a rate-distortion curve. And real means it keeps paying off: re-encode the data some other way, or look at held-out data, and the pattern doesn't evaporate. If it evaporates it was overfitting, i.e. not real.

That's the story. Now translate the actual position into that and tell me where I've cheated. In particular:

- Which parts of the real-patterns claim go through cleanly as claims about description length and generalization, and which only look like they do.
- The intentional stance is where I get nervous. In my world, if a model class predicts well you just use it, full stop — you don't also need the entity to genuinely have beliefs. But Dennett seems to want more than predictive utility; there's a normative flavor, like the system has to be one whose beliefs ought to track the truth and whose behavior is broadly rational. Does that normative/teleological bit have any counterpart in compression or prediction at all, or is it exactly the residue?
- Is my reduction right — real = robust under re-encoding? Or does the original need real to mean something stronger, that the pattern is there in the world and not just a fact about which model I happen to find cheap to write down? I suspect the original is more realist than my version, and I want to know whether that extra realism is doing any work or is just decoration.
- One thing that bugs me: in MDL you can make almost any pattern look real by picking a stupid encoding of the raw data — the choice of what counts as the data is doing a lot of the work. Is there an analogue of that worry in the philosophy, or does the view have some way of fixing the data seperately from the modeler?

And at the end, the residue report: what can't be said in my vocabulary at all, and for each thing, whether that's because my vocabulary is missing something or because the original was vaguer than it looked. I'd rather you tell me the position is thinner than advertised than fudge a translation to make it come out clean — I'm definately not looking for a victory lap where my framing swallows the whole thing.
