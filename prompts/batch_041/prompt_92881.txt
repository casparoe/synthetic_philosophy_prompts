ok so I'm taking this aesthetics seminar and last week we did the whole fakes-and-forgeries thing, and I've come out of it with an argument I can't shake, and I'm starting to think it just dissolves the entire problem. Tell me if I'm being an idiot. Here's the argument. Take a really good forgery — not the kind that fools you for a minute, the kind where by hypothesis you could never tell. Same panel, same pigments, same craquelure, same brushwork, and if you want to make it airtight, imagine a machine that scans the original and reprints it molecule for molecule, so there's not even a forger and no intention to deceive anyone. Now every aesthetic property of the original — every colour, every relation of shapes, every expressive quality, every formal tension — is by hypothesis present in the duplicate too. But then they have the same aesthetic value. And if they have the same aesthetic value, then whatever extra value we hang on the original can't be aesthetic at all: it's investment value, or prestige, or the little frisson of being near the 'real thing', which is a fact about our psychology and not about the paintings. My friend calls this the sealed-gallery test — you get put in one room with one painting, you'll never be told whether it's the original or the machine-copy, and you'll never see the other one, so if nothing about your experience could possibly differ, then the difference in what the two are worth is a mistake we're making, not a property we're detecting.

And then the part that really sold me: there are forgers whose fakes are now themselves collected and shown under the forger's own name — an object that was basically worthless the second the reveal happened, and then decades later it's valuable again, now *as a forgery*. Same physical thing, value riding entirely on a label that changed. So isnt the honest conclusion that the whole literature on the value of originals is one long refusal to accept something obvious — that a painting is what you can see and touch, and everything else is market folklore and sentiment? Or am I missing something about what counts as 'the same painting' here? I dont want a reading list, I want to know where the argument actually breaks, because right now it feels airtight and that makes me suspicious.
