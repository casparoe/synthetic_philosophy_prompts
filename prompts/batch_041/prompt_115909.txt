I've been arguing with my roommates about this for weeks and I want someone to just tell me what I actually believe. Here's my gut on a bunch of cases. My one vote in the presidential election, in a state that's going to go the same way no matter what: I skipped it last time cause I was tired and I dont feel bad about it at all, though I'd still tell my friends voting matters. Me eating a burger: I feel genuinely guilty, and if I'm honest I think it's wrong, even though I know one burger kills nothing and changes no numbers anywhere. My round trip flight to see my sister in Portugal last spring: don't feel guilty, and when my coworker lectured me about it I thought she was being a little silly, even though the flight's emissions are obviously way bigger then the burger's. Tossing an empty bottle out the car window on the highway: I've done it, I feel bad about it, like actually bad, and I think it's wrong. Not rinsing a jar before it goes in the recycling: nothing, don't care, not wrong. Buying a cheap t-shirt I'm 90% sure came from a sweatshop: feels worse than the flight, worse than the burger, and I bought it anyway. Taking the car instead of the bus that stops right by my house: I feel like a jerk, mildly, in a way I don't feel about the flight. Leaving the faucet running while I brush my teeth: nothing. Eating the last of the chips my roommate was saving: not related, sorry, ignore that one.

What kills me is the pattern. Voting and the bottle out the window look identical on paper — one person, zero effect either way, and the bad outcome happens regardless — but I think one of them is fine and the other is wrong, and I can't defend that. Same with the burger vs the flight: the flight does more damage, I'm sure of it, but the burger is the one that feels wrong. I've tried a few principles on my own. "What if everyone did it" seems to get the bottle and the burger but it would also make my vote mandatory, which I don't buy, and it seems to make the t-shirt fine, which I also don't buy. "It's wrong when you're complicit in a system that does the harm" gets the t-shirt and the burger but then I don't see how the flight escapes, and anyway I'm complicit in the bus company too. The best I've come up with is something like: it's wrong when my action is a small piece of a harmful pattern that I'm knowingly participating in, unless my participation is basically symbolic or the harm is spread so thin there's no pattern at all — but I have no idea what "spread so thin" means and I suspect I'm just describing my feelings back to myself.

So: is there a principle that gets all of these, or close, and which of my gut feelings does it force me to drop? I'd like it stated precisely enough that I can apply it myself, with a note on which case in my list is doing the work for each part of it. And then use it on a case I haven't thought about, so I can tell whether it's real: say there's a lake with a limit of five fish per person per day, and I'm one of about ten thousand people who each take a sixth fish, and the fish population crashes. Or make up a better one. Also, just so I know what I'm signing up for, tell me which of my verdicts you'd call flat-out inconsistent with the others and why I should give it up, rather than smoothing it over.
