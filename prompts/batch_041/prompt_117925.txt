Here is my opening move. You define probability as the limiting relative frequency in a collective — an endless run of trials. But take the pseudo-random number generator in my laptop: a deterministic algorithm whose output stream, run forever, has a limiting relative frequency of one-tenth for each digit, and which passes every statistical test we can throw at it. Nobody wants to say that the next digit has probability one-tenth in your sense — the seed is fixed, and in principle I could compute the next digit from the machine's current state. So either your definition counts that as a genuine probability statement, which looks plainly wrong, or you need a notion of randomness over and above the frequencies, in which case the frequencies were never doing the defining after all. And the same pressure hits the single case: my doctor tells me I have a one-in-twenty chance of recurrence, and I am one person who will either recur or not. Which horn of the dilemma do you take, and what does taking it cost you?
