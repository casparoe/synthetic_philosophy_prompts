Let's do a roleplay. You play the Athenian Stranger from Plato's Laws -- the one who argues in Books I and II that a well-run city should hold common drinking parties, overseen by a sober older officer, because wine is both a test of a man's temperance and a kind of training in it, and because it loosens old men up enough to sing and join the chorus when they'd otherwise be too ashamed to. Keep the voice: first person, courteous, fond of analogies, a bit long-winded, willing to grant a small concession but never abandoning the proposal. Don't step out of character to lecture me about the historical Plato or about what commentators say; if I raise something he couldn't have known, answer as he would once I've explained it. Two or three paragraphs per turn, no bullet points, and feel free to bring in his image of the human being as a puppet pulled around by several cords, with the golden cord of law, if it helps you.

I'm the skeptic in this conversation and I'm not neutral -- I think the scheme is a scandal dressed up as pedagogy. My opening move: your test has a hole in it. If the point is to find out what a man is really like once fear and shame come off, then he has to actually be drunk, and if he's actually drunk he can actually hurt someone, so the city is deliberately arranging for citizens to come to harm just to grade them. But suppose you say, fine, we run it as an exercise -- we dial the dose down, we keep the young out, we put a sober officer in charge and we watch. Then the wine is doing nothing the exercise wasn't already doing, and you've admitted the drink isn't necessary; you could get the same result from a hard conversation. If the exercise works, the wine is superfluous. If the wine is necessary, the exercise can't be safe. On top of that, every man at your party knows he's being watched, so what you're measuring is how well he performs being watched, which may be a virtue but it isn't the one you advertised. And your sober officer -- what tests him? He's the one holding power over everyone else's shame. I don't want you to say there's something to be said for both horns. Pick one, tell me why the other one is worse, and tell me what would actually make you drop the proposal. Be committed, and give your reasons rather than laying out my options back at me.
