I'm in a political philosophy seminar that's knee-deep in Nozick right now (I have to present next week) and I've been trying to write down my own view on justice in holdings before my roommate, who's a Rawlsian and thinks my view just collapses into his once you push it, gets to ambush me. Trouble is I've ended up with alot of commitments that I like individually and I can't tell whether they hang together. Roughly: (1) full self-ownership — I own my body and my labour outright, and the state has no standing to use either as a resource, not even for good ends; (2) the justice of a distribution is entirely a matter of its history — how holdings were first acquired, how they were transferred, and how past wrongs get corrected — and never a matter of the shape it ends up having (I know that calling the current distribution in, say, the US unjust sounds like it contradicts this, but that's part of what I want you to sort out); (3) the Chamberlain case settles it for me: any pattern you favour will get broken the moment people spend their own money as they like, so keeping a pattern in place means forbidding voluntary exchanges, and a state may not do that; (4) the Lockean proviso is a real constraint and not a dead letter — appropriation is fine only if it doesn't leave others worse off than they'd have been had the thing stayed unowned, and I think it still bites today, e.g. a city selling its aquifer to a bottling company, or gene patents where it's hard to even say what the unowned baseline was; I've never managed to make the baseline precise though, and depending on whose position you compare (the people alive at the time of the taking? everyone afterward?) it comes out either toothless or wildly demanding; (5) a contract of voluntary slavery or indefinite peonage should be void, not merely unenforceable in some weak form — a guy can sign away twenty years of his labour and then just walk at year three owing nothing, because you can't actually alienate your future self; (6) and rectification: I think present holdings are so thoroughly the product of conquest, slavery, enclosure and expropriation that taking rectification seriously means enormous transfers, and concretely I'd tax bequests by subtracting whatever the bequeather themselves inherited, so you can only pass on what you actually added.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture on Nozick: which of these actually pull against each other, ranked by how bad the tension is, which ones only look like tensions and dissolve once you state them properly, and for each real clash what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs me. I'm definately not attached to any particular one surviving, so don't spare anyone's feelings — but if a single revision would clean up more than one clash at once, say so, and if two of these are really the same tension wearing different hats, say that too.
