I'm putting together the first session of a grad seminar on analyticity and I want the specification before I wade into the literature. So: write me the desiderata that any account of the analytic-synthetic distinction has to meet — the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse, the results it has to be consistant with — with the case or consideration behind each requirement, and say which of them are non-negotiable and which are just the house style of one camp. Purpose, so you know what I'm actually asking for: I need a notion that can do two jobs, (a) explain why denying a definitional truth sounds like changing the subject rather than making a mistake, and (b) explain why a proof gives a kind of warrant that testing doesn't. If either job is beyond any notion of analyticity, say so.

Cases I want on the list at minimum: 'no bachelor is married' sitting alongside 'cats are animals', which in turn sits alongside 'cats are mammals', which is supposed to be empirical; the fact that 'whales are fish' was once the first kind of thing and is now plainly false; a bylaw that defines 'vehicle' so that bicycles aren't vehicles (is that analytic? if not, what is it?); and the Pope counting as an unmarried man. On the consistency side, it has to survive the holism in Two Dogmas itself, the fact that we have revised things we'd have sworn were conceptual (absolute simultaneity, conservation of mass, distributivity in quantum logic), the indeterminacy of translation, and the psych findings that speakers treat these truths with prototype-ish slack and don't seem to know them in any different way from empirical ones. There's also the paradox of analysis, which I think is the requirement everyone forgets: an analysis has to be synonymous with its analysandum to be correct, and informative to be worth giving.

Then give me the bit I most want: whether some subset of these is jointly unsatisfiable — e.g. can you have a criterion a linguist could apply to a speaker's behaviour and still get the normative oomph out the other end, or does that pair go the way Quine says it does? Which single requirement excludes the most contenders? And is the demand for a usable criterion itself a prejudice of one camp — I suspect it is, and that the same goes for the demand that analyticity deliver an entitlement. One line each on how the Carnap-style postulates move, the Grice-Strawson-ish defence, and a deflationary inferentialist line fare against the list, and then I'll do the rest in class. Ta.
