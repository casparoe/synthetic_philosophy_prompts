I teach the second half of an MA course on moral theory and I'm writing the take-home for the moral-uncertainty week. By that point they can all chant "maximise expected moral value," so I want a question that starts there and then breaks it. The thing I want it to force is the regress: to use that procedure you've already picked a procedure, and picking a procedure is itself a normative choice made under uncertainty, so either it bottoms out somewhere or it spirals — and if it bottoms out, say where and why that stopping point isn't arbitrary. I also want them to seperate two things they keep merging: being unsure which theory is true, versus being unsure what a theory they already accept says about this particular case, and to say whether one decision procedure can carry both or whether the second is a different problem wearing the same coat. And I want the swamping problem in there as a must-hit: a tiny credence in an absolutist theory can swallow everything else, and they have to say whether that's an objection to the procedure or just a fact about morality. 90 minutes, 20 marks. Give me the question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme naming the specific moves a first-class answer has to make and the specific moves that should cost marks — things like treating intertheoretic comparison as a technicality you can wave away, or answering the regress with "just be reflective." Then, after the model answer, tell me how confident you are in what's in it and which claim you'd bet on least.
