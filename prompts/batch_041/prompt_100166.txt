For years I have read rationalist blogs and forums, and my instinctive vocabulary for social epistemology is theirs: common knowledge, legibility, Goodharting, Moloch, prediction markets, karma, moderation, trust networks, pseudonymity, and the contrast between a public feed and a private conversation. Imagine the forum has just adopted a public feed with anonymous posting, hidden karma weights, and a norm that downvotes must be justified. I want you to restate Kierkegaard's critique of the press and the public in that vocabulary. The position I have in mind is roughly this: the press does not merely report on a pre-existing public; it manufactures an abstract public that no one actually belongs to; anonymity and publicity dissolve the personal responsibility that attaches to face-to-face speech; and what Kierkegaard calls leveling produces a counterfeit equality in which no individual can be singled out, praised, blamed, or changed. Restate that as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary, then give me the residue report. Assume I know the rationalist vocabulary very well and the Kierkegaardian material only well enough to follow a careful paraphrase. Do not give me citations, reading lists, or historical background. Give me exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. In the first, translate the press; in the second, translate the public; in the third, translate leveling and anonymity; in the fourth, tell me what did not survive the translation and whether it is a defect of my vocabulary or a sign that the original claim was less clear than it looked; in the fifth, say what my vocabulary reveals that the Kierkegaardian one cannot, and what it hides.
