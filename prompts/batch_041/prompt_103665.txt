So my friend Priya and I got into it for like three hours last night — she's a sociologist, I do survey work — about whether generations are actually real. She thinks Gen Z is a real thing with real causal oomph. I think it's astrology for marketing departments, and that all the "Gen Z is different" findings are just age effects and period effects wearing a costume. We didn't resolve anything and both of us got kind of annoyed, so I want to do the thing we should have done instead: draft the joint statement that she and I could both actually sign.

Set it up as an adversarial collaboration between a generation realist and a generation skeptic. Use these three terms so we're not talking past each other the whole time: a cohort is just everyone born in some window; a thin generation is a cohort whose members, because they hit the same distinctive stretch of social/historical conditions during their formative years, tend to differ measurably from adjacent cohorts on some trait distribution; a thick generation is one where people also get labeled, recognize the label as applying to them, and start coordinating expectations and behavior around it. And a label loop is the feedback where the label itself shifts the trait distributions that were supposed to define the cohort in the first place.

I want: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims — stuff we both can sign. I'd bet a lot of it is about method: that age, period and cohort are formally entangled so you can't pull cohort effects out of a single cross-sectional series without assumptions; that within-cohort variance is huge and usually swamps between-cohort differences; that the same birth window gets different labels in different countries and different decades; that period effects get misread as generational all the time. (2) A disagreements table, with the residual disagreement stated in wording each side would accept for its own view — no strawmen. The ones I expect: whether thick generations are real causal kinds or artifacts of labeling; whether the label loop counts as evidence the kind is real (she'll say the loop makes it real, I'll say it's debunking) ; whether boundaries between generations are discovered or stipulated; whether "generation" is more like a homeostatic property-cluster kind or a purely nominal one; and whether cohort effects persist across the life course or fade.

(3) For each crux, the case, test or argument we'd both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. In particular I want a crux on the APC identification problem — what constraints each of us would accept on a cohort-effect estimate, and what we'd each predict about a cohort that was hit by a big period shock at labor-market entry but never got a generational label. I'd also like a crux on cross-national variation: whether self-identification tracks real trait divergence (her) or just how hard the media/marketing ecosystem pushes the label (me).

And if you think part of our fight is really just verbal — like if we mean different things by "generation" and one of us is answering a question about cohorts while the other is answering one about self-conscious groups — say which part and why. Format it as the numbered agreed-claims section, then the table, then the cruxes with predictions. No need for reading lists or citations, we're not writing a paper, I just want the document we could both put our names on.
