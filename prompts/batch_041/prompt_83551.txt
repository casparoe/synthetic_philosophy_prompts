I chair the theory panel of my institute's research council. Our charter says that funded work must be capable of being brought into contact with measurement, but it does not say whether that contact has to be possible with instruments we could plausibly build. At our meeting on the 14th we must decide whether to renew a three-year grant to a group that models our universe as one member of a vast ensemble of universes generated by a single underlying theory, the members differing in their vacuum energy; the group argues that the ensemble explains why the vacuum energy is far smaller than particle physics alone would lead one to expect, a fact their rivals can only take as brute, and that their probability distribution over vacuum energies, which peaks near the observed value, was derived from assumptions that could have been otherwise rather than fitted to the data. A rival group, whose competing proposal would instead fund a precision measurement that could constrain the ensemble's parameters, replies that no prediction has been made: a theory that guarantees that some observer somewhere will see something is not confirmed by the fact that we see something, the distribution is broad enough to contain the observed value whatever nature had done, and the peak sits where it does only because the value was already known. Renewing the grant would consume about a third of the panel's theory budget for the next cycle.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action — renew, renew with conditions, or decline — with a time frame; state a general criterion the panel can apply to comparable proposals next cycle; and give the rival group's objection its strongest form before explaining whether it carries the day. Say explicitly whether observer-selection reasoning of the kind the group uses counts as a prediction, and if not, what work it is doing in their case. Assume the reader knows no physics beyond what I have set out, and do not attribute arguments to particular books, papers, or authors; where an argument has a familiar name, use the name.

After the memo, add a JSON object with exactly these fields: decision (one of renew, renew with conditions, decline); criterion (one sentence, the general rule the panel should adopt); objection (the rival's objection in its strongest one-sentence form); conditions (array of strings, empty if none); confidence (an integer from 0 to 100); review_trigger (the future development that would force us to revisit the decision).
