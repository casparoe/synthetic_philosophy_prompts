I'm in a seminar on Marx and technology and it's got me stewing about my last job. When they put in the new automated line, management did the usual thing: nobody's loosing their job, we're redeploying people, here's a retraining budget, and the public story was that the machines just free us up for better work — tech is a neutral tool and we choose what to do with it. But the internal planning read like the opposite: the automation was a force of nature, "we have to do this or die, here's what it does to headcount by Q3." So one minute technology is something we shape, the next it's something that shapes us. What does that combo actually presuppose about technology? Is there a single consistent view that licenses both halves, or is the practice quietly committed to determinism in the planning and neutrality in the PR — and which parts are real presuppositions vs. just what the messaging happens to say? If I take seriously the Marx-ish line that machinery isn't the villain by itself, it's machinery as capital, under these property relations, that puts people out — what would the retraining/redeployment/bonus stuff have to become, and would it still be recognisable as the same practice? Also, does the practice actually need the old "machines eat jobs but spawn at least as many new ones" story, or does it just like telling itself that?
