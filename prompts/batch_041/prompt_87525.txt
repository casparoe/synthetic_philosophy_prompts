I'm putting together a handout for a reading group on social construction, and I want something more granular than the usual "race and gender are constructed, rocks aren't" business. So: rate each of the following kinds from 0 to 1 on how socially constructed it is, where 0 means it's a joint of nature we merely label and 1 means there's nothing there at all until we invented it — money, race, gender, species, pain, deafness, left-handedness, childhood, wilderness, quicksand, jazz, the number seven, the colour blue, teenager, obesity, natural (as in natural food), and cilantro tasting soapy.

For each one give me two sub-scores as well: (a) causal construction, meaning would the members of the kind actually have been different if our practices had gone otherwise, and (b) constitutive construction, meaning is the kind itself partly made up of our representations of it. The headline score shouldn't just be the average of the two, and I want you to say where they come apart and why that matters.

Then for each kind give me a removal test: describe the nearest possible world in which that kind doesn't exist at all, and say how far away that world is. If the nearest such world is one where the members still exist but the boundaries fall elsewhere, say so, because that's a different answer and I suspect a lot of people fudge it.

A few constraints, because I know how this goes. Don't let everything cluster between 0.4 and 0.6 — use the range, and if two items genuinely deserve the same score then say why they tie rather than nudging one to break it. I want the whole thing as one table sorted from most to least constructed, with a sentence or two of justification per row and one line on what evidence would move the score. If any item seems to you like a category mistake rather than a hard case — if asking how constructed it is doesn't even get a grip — flag it instead of forcing a number on it. And at the end, tell me which single item a thouroughly realist reader and a thouroughly constructivist reader would disagree about most, and whether that disagreement is empirical or just verbal. I'd also like to know which one you found hardest to score seperately on the two axes, and why.
