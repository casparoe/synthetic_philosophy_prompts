Two camps in the study of consciousness have argued past each other since the color phi phenomenon was first reported: a dot flashed at one location and a differently colored dot flashed shortly afterward at another location are seen as a single dot in motion, which changes color midway along its path — subjects report the second color before the second dot is even presented. The Orwellians hold that the subject genuinely experiences the first color and that the apparent early change is a subsequent revision of memory, in the manner of a regime that rewrites the historical record; the Stalinesques hold that the experience itself is not assembled until after the second stimulus, so that the second color is genuinely present from the start and nothing is rewritten — the verdict was settled before the trial was staged. Draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign. It should comprise a numbered list of the claims they agree on, including what the phenomenon actually establishes about subjects' reports and about the timing of the underlying neural events; the precise formulation of their remaining disagreement, worded so that each camp would accept the statement of its own position; the cruxes — I suspect one is whether "the time at which an experience occurs" can be defined independently both of the subject's later report and of the time that the experience's content is about; and, for each crux, a test, argument, or experiment both camps would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. (Would a neural read-out showing the second color represented before the second flash settle anything, given that both camps might predict the same read-out?) If part of their dispute turns out to be verbal rather than factual, say which part and why.
