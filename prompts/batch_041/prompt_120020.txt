I have been reading rationalist and EA blogs for years, and I keep encountering a pattern in cause prioritization that I do not have a clean name for. A cause is rated highly because it is neglected: few people are working on it, so additional resources seem likely to have high marginal impact. But sometimes the neglect is itself a symptom of something that should lower the rating. The area may be neglected because its effects are hard to verify, because past attempts failed in ways that are not widely publicized, or because it attracts people whose judgment is systematically off. In those cases, the same fact—few people are working on it—is treated as evidence of opportunity, when it may be evidence of a trap. This is not just the ordinary warning that neglectedness can be confounded with low tractability. It is a specific feedback loop: the cause is neglected because it is hard to evaluate, and its difficulty in evaluation makes it look more neglected, which makes it look more promising to a certain kind of optimizer. Is there an established term or concept for this? If there is, what is it and how exactly does it carve the phenomenon? If there is not, can you name and define it, and say how it relates to and differs from the standard importance-tractability-neglectedness framework, from the idea of a neglectedness proxy, and from the more general problem of Goodharting a metric?
