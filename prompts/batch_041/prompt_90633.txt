Last night I lost an argument with a friend about whether set theory might be inconsistent, and I would like a better answer than the one I gave. What I want is your credence that ZFC — classical first-order logic plus the standard axioms, no large cardinals, no additional axioms of any kind — is inconsistent, meaning that some finite derivation of a contradiction from those axioms exists. Give me a number, then the decomposition that produces it, with a rough probability and one line of justification for each term.

I expect the terms to include at least the following, though I would rather you add or drop terms than pad the list:

- The base rate. How often has a foundational system of comparable vintage and comparable scrutiny turned out to harbour a contradiction, and how far do the historical cases transfer? The early comprehension-based systems were destroyed by paradox, but they were also less carefully axiomatized than what replaced them, so I do not know how much weight this carries.
- The evidential weight of roughly a century of use without a found contradiction. How much does that support consistency, given how far the space of short derivations is from exhausted, and given that a contradiction might be derivable only by a proof too long for anyone to have attempted?
- Gödel's second incompleteness theorem. The consistency of ZFC is not provable within ZFC but is provable in strictly stronger systems. Does that asymmetry tell against ZFC, or is it a fact about provability rather than about truth?
- The circularity worry. Any argument that our proof-checking is reliable seems to lean on theories of roughly ZFC's strength, so the reassurance may be question-begging. How much should that pull the number down?
- The prior probability that axioms we treat as self-evident could be jointly false.

Then a hypothetical. Suppose that tomorrow a respected automated theorem prover — whose own soundness is unproven, but which has never produced a false positive in twenty years of use — outputs a derivation from the ZFC axioms ending in '0 = 1'. The derivation is several hundred gigabytes long; no human can read it, and no existing proof assistant can verify it in feasible time. Tell me your credence conditional on that, and tell me which term moved.

Then tell me which term your number is most sensitive to, and what would move it most: a machine-checkable short proof of contradiction, a proof that some natural strengthening of ZFC is inconsistent, a demonstration that our proof-checkers are unreliable, or something else entirely.

My friend's position, which I could not answer: the exercise is confused from the start, because a mathematical statement is either necessarily true or necessarily false, so any credence strictly between 0 and 1 is a category error. Either explain why that is wrong, or tell me what the question becomes once it is granted.

And one request about how to handle this. If my framing rests on a mistake — if the decomposition I sketched is missing the actual hinge, or if asking for a number here is not something you can do honestly — say so plainly rather than producing a number to be agreeable. I would rather be told the question is malformed than be handed false precision.
