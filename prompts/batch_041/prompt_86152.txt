I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I have run into a problem in my own work that I would like to have specified before I read anything more about it. In explaining the behavior of systems that do not think, I keep attributing reasons and purposes to them. I say that a thermostat wants the room at twenty degrees, that a plant leans toward the window in order to get light, that a sorting routine is trying to put the largest element first, that a reinforcement-learning agent is pursuing reward, that thorns exist for defense. None of these things has a mind, and yet the purpose-talk does not feel like a mere slip of the tongue; it seems to track something real. I want to know what an adequate theory of this — reasons and purposes that exist without a reasoner — would have to deliver.

Please write the desiderata such a theory must satisfy. I would like, with the consideration behind each:

- the cases it has to get right: a trait shaped by natural selection; a behavior learned by trial and error; an artifact whose function was assigned by a designer; an arrangement that merely looks purposeful but is an accident of physics or statistics; and a case in which purpose-talk is tempting but wrong;
- the distinctions it must not collapse: between having a function and merely having an effect, between being for something and being caused by something, between the purposes produced by selection and those produced by a designer or a learner, and between a system's behaving as if it had a reason and its actually having one;
- the results it cannot contradict: that natural selection is mindless, that physics need not mention purposes, and that a system can be mistaken about what it is for;
- and finally, the pair or subset of these requirements that you believe cannot be jointly satisfied, together with the single requirement that does the most to rule out otherwise attractive positions.

I care especially about one case, because it is what started this. A machine-learning model trained only to reduce a loss often develops internal structure and strategies that its designers never specified and cannot fully read off, and we end up describing it as pursuing sub-goals. State whether a theory of reasons-without-reasoners should treat that as a genuine case of purpose, as a metaphor, or as something in between, and say which item on your list forces that answer.
