I work on the philosophy of punishment, and I have been trying to find a name for a shape of sanction I keep running into. I would like your help in identifying it, or, if nothing established matches it, in coining something that does.

The shape is this. Someone says or does something that is taken to be a wrong. Over the following weeks they lose their job, their invitations, their colleagues' willingness to be seen with them, and much of their ordinary standing. But nothing has been decided. No body deliberated, no charge was framed, no finding was made, no sentence was pronounced. What happened instead is that a great many people, each acting alone and for their own reasons, privately withdrew — declined to hire, declined to invite, declined to defend — and the withdrawals added up. The person now stands under something that has the felt features of punishment: it is imposed because of a wrong, it is meant to hurt, and it is defended as deserved. Yet it has none of the machinery. No one is in a position to say how long it lasts, what would count as having served it, or whether it is over. Most strikingly, there is no one who can lift it. A convicted person can be pardoned, a suspended employee reinstated, an excommunicant absolved; an ostracised Athenian could go home when his term ran out. Here the person has no one to petition, because there is no authority that speaks for the sanction — only a public with no spokesperson, which cannot be addressed as a body.

Two features seem to me to be doing the work, and I would like to be told whether they are really the crux or whether I have fastened on the wrong thing. First, the sanction is the aggregation of many independent private refusals rather than the act of a deciding body; second, and I suspect more importantly, it therefore has no remitter — no office, no procedure, no recognised spokesperson with the standing to declare it discharged. The absence of a remitter is what makes it unlike anything I can find a name for.

I know the popular word for this, and I do not trust it: it covers everything from a mild rebuke to a criminal prosecution, and it names the mood of the crowd rather than the structure of the sanction. What I want is the structural kind, and I have been going through the neighbours without satisfaction. Infamy and civil death were legal statuses with legal authors. Outlawry placed a person outside the protection of the law, but even there an authority declared the status and could, in principle, undo it. Excommunication and the various practices of religious shunning involve a body that both excludes and readmits. Ostracism was a decision of an assembly and came with a term. Mob violence is a discrete act rather than a standing condition, and gossip damages a reputation without claiming to be deserved. Each of these is close, and each seems to me to fail at just the point I care about — the missing remitter — but I may be forcing the contrast, and I would rather be told that one of them fits than be flattered with a new coinage.

So: does this shape have a name? If it does, give me the name, and then say what the nearest rival concepts are and precisely where each stops short of it. If it does not, coin a term and define it, and then do the same work in reverse — state the boundary cases and say which side of the line they fall on. In particular I want your view on (a) a ban imposed by a single institution, which has an author, an appeal, and often an expiry; (b) a campaign that throws up a designated spokesperson or an open letter with named signatories, so that the crowd acquires something like an address; (c) a case where everyone agrees the matter is spent after a few years, so that a de facto term exists although no one set it; and (d) a case where the person is in fact guilty of what was alleged, set against one where they are not. I also want to know whether the missing remitter is essential or merely typical — whether there could be a sanction of this shape that is nonetheless liftable, and what that would look like — and whether the phenomenon is best classified as punishment at all, or as something else (a change of status, a forfeiture of standing, a standing permission for everyone to treat the person as though they were not there), given that the usual communicative accounts of punishment appear to require both a speaker and an addressee, and this shape supplies neither.

Please do not stop at the label. The label is the least of it; what I need is the carving: what exactly the thing is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.

End your reply with a single final line of the form \answer{...} containing, as a short phrase, either the established name you judge to fit best or the word none, followed by your proposed term if you coined one.
