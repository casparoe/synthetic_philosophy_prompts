OK, so here's a view I actually hold and I'd like it stress-tested. I think introspection is a reliable-ish guide to my own current mental life — not infallible, not magic, but when I attend carefully to what I'm experiencing right now, or what I'm currently wanting, I'm getting genuine information about myself rather than making up a story on the spot. I know the confabulation literature and the whole we-are-strangers-to-our-own-minds line, and I still basically believe this, which is why I'm worried I'm just attached to it.

So: what would count against it? I want the findings, cases and arguments that should actually move me, ranked from decisive down to merely awkward, and sorted by which introspective claim they hit. My suspicion is that most of the damning evidence is about mental states that function as causes or dispositions — why did I choose this, do I really believe that — and not about the phenomenal character of what's happening to me right now. Tell me whether that split survives scrutiny or whether the unreliability bleeds across it.

Second, is introspection even one thing? Inner perception, attention to your own mental goings-on, and inferring your own state from your behaviour and situation all get called that, and if they have seperate reliabilities then the question might be ill-formed until you say which one you mean.

Third, the circularity thing, which I can't get past. Any evidence about introspection has to be gathered at least partly through introspective reports, so what exactly is being compared to what, and can that comparison ever be non-circular? If the answer is not really, does that cut against the reliability claim, the unreliability claim, or both equally? What would move me most is a case where my careful, sincere report about a current experience gets contradicted by something I can't dispute — but I honestly can't picture what that something would be, and I suspect that inability is itself telling.

Fourth, and this is the real question: how much of this debate is people carving the claim up until it can't lose? Introspection is reliable about the coarse phenomenal character of present experience, but not about attention, not about causes, not about dispositions, not about anything that shows up only in reaction times... At what point is the position vacuous, and is that fatal here or is it just what a claim about a fallible faculty is supposed to look like? If the honest version is closer to introspection tells you almost nothing you couldnt get by inference from your own behaviour and circumstances, say so, and say whether that's a discovery or a definitional move. And give me the sharpest version of my view that's actually testable — what does it predict that the rival doesn't?
