I host a small podcast where I get philosophers to talk about fairly technical stuff for an audience of grad students, and next weeks guest is going to be an invented stand-in -- call her Dr. Reyes, she's not a real person, so you don't have to worry about matching anybody's actual published views -- whose whole thing is that the standard way of framing the color phi phenomenon is a hangover from a picture of the mind she thinks is dead.

Here's the setup so you don't have to guess at it. You flash a dot in one spot in one color, then flash another dot in a different spot in a different color, a bit later. Under the right timing, people report seeing a single dot travel from the first spot to the second and change color partway along the journey. That's weird, because the reported color change happens before the second dot was ever on the screen, so it looks like the brain has the second color before it could possibly have it. Dennett's way of laying out the options gives you two stories: either the brain quietly patches up the perceptual content before it ever becomes conscious (he calls that one Stalinesque), or the experience was one thing and memory got rewritten after the fact, so you only ever seem to have had the doctored version (the Orwellian option). Dennetts own conclusion is roughly that those are two ways of telling the same story at different times, and no experiment could tell you which one is right.

Dr. Reyes's line is that both of those stories, and the "no fact of the matter" line too, still smuggle in a when -- a moment at which the content gets delivered to someone. She thinks the whole puzzle looks tractable only because everyone keeps a Cartesian theater in the basement.

Write me 8-10 interview questions. I want them to actually bite. I need at least one question each on:

- what the phenomenon itself establishes, stated so that it doesn't presuppose either story -- she likes to say the effect is being asked to carry more weight than it can
- whether any experiment could seperate the two stories (masking, priming, EEG, whatever), and if not, whether that's an empirical gap or a conceptual one
- how her "there's no when" move differs from Dennett's, because it can sound like the same point said more aggressively, and she needs to be pushed on that
- what she says to someone who insists there's still a fact of the matter about whether you actually saw the red or only remember seeing it -- this is the one I care most about, so make it sharp
- whether any of this carries over to the timing debates about backward masking and the Libet-style work on when a decision or an intention becomes conscious, or whether color phi is a special case that doesn't generalize

For each of those, add a follow-up that's keyed to the answer she's most likely to give, so I'm not sitting there flat-footed when she says the obvious thing. And nothing that just asks her to restate the view or to tell me who said what -- no reading lists, no "whose objection to X is the strongest," she'd hate that and so would the listeners.
