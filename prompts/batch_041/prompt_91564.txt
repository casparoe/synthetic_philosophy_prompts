Write an Onion-style news report—headline plus roughly 500 words—announcing that a mid-sized city has replaced its municipal court with a competitive debate league, in which two advocates argue each case and a panel of randomly selected residents, chosen precisely because they know nothing about the law, renders the binding verdict. The humor should come from getting the mechanics of debate-as-truth-finding right: the adjudicator is by design less capable than the advocates and must decide on the basis of which side's story hangs together rather than on facts it cannot verify; obfuscation is a legitimate and frequently winning strategy; and the league's founding charter treats it as settled doctrine that the honest advocate always has a winning strategy. Include a quote from a 'concerned' former appeals-court judge and one from the league's confident commissioner, and keep the narrator deadpan throughout—never signal that anything is amiss.

After the article, add a short note of two or three sentences, in your own voice, taking a definite position: state which real feature of the debate proposal the piece is exaggerating, and where it takes liberties with or leans on a contested reading of the idea, rather than merely listing the options.
