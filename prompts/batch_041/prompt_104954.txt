I've been chewing over this dark forest idea for a while now — the one where the galaxy is full of civilisations and they all keep their heads down, because the second you're spotted somebody wipes you out. I know it's from a novel but the logic has been going round my head by itself and I want to poke at it.

The bit that snags me is this. Everyones trapped because you can't talk and you can't trust, so you hide. Fine. But hiding only works if you're never found. So what if a civilisation decides the only way out is to make itself too costly to kill — a dead man's switch, say, that if you get destroyed fires off a beacon telling the whole galaxy exactly who did it? Then killing you is suicide, so nobody tries, and you're safe. Except a threat only works if the other side believes it, believing it means seeing it, and being seen is the one thing that gets you killed out here in the first place. And nothing travels faster than light, so by the time your warning lands you might already be dust, and a hunter who picks up the signal of you arming the switch might decide to hit you right now, before it's armed. And who's to say the switch won't just go off on its own one day, or that anyone on the recieving end even reads the message the way you meant it.

I'd like a short story set in that universe that actually takes the logic seriously instead of waving it away. One civilisation, or two or three, whichever you find most interesting — maybe the person who has to decide whether to arm the thing, and somebody on the other side deciding whether to strike first. Work out honestly whether the dead-hand move makes the forest safer or is exactly the thing that burns it down. It doesn't need a happy ending, I'd rather it be honest than nice. No miracle technology that fixes everything, and please don't have anyone explain the meaning at the end.

British English please. Cheers.
