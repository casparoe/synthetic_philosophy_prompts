Here's the setup I want the question to be built around — please use exactly these little sentence-systems, don't swap in the textbook Liar:

The pair:
(1) "(2) is not true."
(2) "(1) is not true."

The triple:
(3) "(4) is not true."
(4) "(5) is not true."
(5) "(3) is not true."

I want students to have to notice that the pair and the triple come out differently under a plain classical two-valued treatment, and then to see what happens to that difference once you go to a three-valued gap account where sentences can be neither true nor false. And then I want the revenge point to bite — something like a sentence that says of itself that it isn't true (or isn't determinately true), which the gap account apparently mishandles.
