Construct a thought experiment in which a single, realistic episode of habitat destruction increases the net welfare of the wild animals in the affected ecosystem, but only under one specified theory of welfare, while under another equally respectable theory the same episode decreases net welfare. The case is for a unit on whether conservation should be justified by ecosystem health or by the welfare of individual sentient animals, so the destruction must not be stipulated to be good or bad: the verdict has to fall out of the welfare accounting.

Constraints: the episode should be one paragraph long and drawn from ordinary ecology, not science fiction; no appeals to human economic benefits, no domestic animals, no extinction of all sentient life, and no invoking a benevolent designer or a sudden change in animal psychology. The two welfare theories should be ones a competent philosopher would recognize, but you should state them in your own words rather than relying on labels alone. The case should turn on a variable that a real management decision could alter, such as which species are present, how deaths are distributed across ages, whether mortality is compensatory or additive, or whether the habitat is a source or a sink.

After the scenario, do four things. First, give the welfare ledger under each theory: which animals are born, which die, at what ages, and with what valences, and why the totals diverge. Second, identify the single feature of the case that does the work, and say what happens to both verdicts if that feature is removed or reversed. Third, state what a defender of the theory that comes out worse would say to resist the case, and whether that response is an ad hoc patch or a principled limit of the theory. Fourth, say what would change your mind: which empirical finding about wild-animal welfare, or which conceptual revision in the welfare theory, would lead you to reverse the verdict you have given. Be explicit about where your reasoning is most likely to fail, especially in any step where you have to estimate the number or intensity of experiences you cannot directly observe.
