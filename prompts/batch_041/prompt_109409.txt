Design a single-session classroom activity for a graduate seminar in applied ethics on paternalism and nudging. Setting: about 16 participants, 90 minutes, one ordinary seminar room, no budget beyond what is already in the room. The aim is that participants should feel, rather than be told, three things: that an intervention can do its work while leaving the person it moves unable to give the reason that actually moved them; that one and the same bit of environmental design can be a help to one person and an obstacle to another; and that learning how an intervention works does not make it stop working.

Arrange it so the group is on the receiving end before it is on the designing end. In the first phase, without announcing what is being tested, have participants make a handful of real, small choices in the room — which of two optional topics the session will actually take up, whether to take something from a table, what to write on a form they must all complete, where to sit — and after each choice have them record privately, in one sentence, why they chose as they did. The room should have been prepared in advance by you, the facilitator, so that each of those choices is shaped by a feature of the environment rather than by the merits of the options; the arrangements should be strong enough to move some participants, but not so theatrical that the reveal feels like a stunt. In the second phase, disclose the arrangements and have each participant compare the reason they recorded with the reason the arrangement was aimed at; the gap between the two is the substance of the exercise. In the third phase, participants design in pairs one arrangement for the seminar's next meeting, and the group must say in advance whether it will still work once it has been announced.

Provide: the exact pre-arrangements and, for each, which mechanism it exploits (at least one default, one ordering or salience effect, one friction, one social-norm cue); the wording of the reason-recording step; a phase-by-phase timeline for the 90 minutes; a debrief that maps what happened onto the difference between changing what someone does and changing what they want, and onto the case in which the same arrangement assists the well-organised and traps the disorganised; an account of what will predictably go wrong the first time it is run and how to handle it; and a frank statement of which part of the paternalism debate the activity cannot reproduce and that you will have to supply in words.
