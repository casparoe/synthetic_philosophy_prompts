Ok so I'm in a type design elective this term and I think it's quietly breaking my brain.

Context: last week I was sketching an 'a' and I pushed it way too far — the bowl sealed up, the stem went stubby, and honestly it read as an 'o' with a tail, or an 'e' that had been knocked over. I figured it was a dud. My instructor glanced at it and said "if it reads as an 'a', it's an 'a'," and moved on like that was the end of the matter. When I pressed him he added something like "the letter's in the reading, not in the shape." Then he went to get coffee.

I've been chewing on that for six days. On one reading it's a nothingburger — sure, if people read it as an 'a' then your font works, ship it. But he said it like it was a real claim, and I can't tell what the claim is, whether it's true, or whether it's just a nice-sounding way of letting himself off the hook for a bad 'a'.

Here's what's really eating at me: 'a' has two completely standard forms that share basically no skeleton — the two-story one you get in text faces and the single-story one you get in handwriting, in italics, and in half the geometric sans display faces out there. So if there's an essence of 'a', what on earth do those two have in common? Meanwhile mirror a 'b' and you get a 'd' — same shape, different letter, and the distinction matters enormously. So shape can't be doing all the work in the b/d case and then none of it in the 'a' case. Something's off in how I'm holding this.

(My roommate, who does cognitive science, says there was some old AI project where people tried to get a program to design a whole font in one coherent style, and the entire difficulty was pinning down what makes an 'a' an 'a' when every font's 'a' is different. Which is... exactly where I live now. Not asking for a reading list, I just want the idea.)

What I want: a menu of the precise things "if it reads as an 'a', it's an 'a'" could actually be claiming, ordered from the trivial up to the radical, with a verdict on each — true, false, or contentious, and whether it's a real thesis or just a platitude in a trenchcoat. Then tell me which of those readings would genuinely license my instructor waving through my wierd 'a', and whether "it's in the reading, not the shape" is a stronger claim than the first one or basically the same claim twice. And if two of the readings make different predictions about a case I could actually go test — a font where 'a' and 'o' are nearly indistinguishable, say, or one where 'a' is a rotated 'e' — tell me which case would actually pull them apart.

And go easy on the confidence: say how sure you are about each verdict, and flag the one you're least sure of, so I know where the soft ground is.
