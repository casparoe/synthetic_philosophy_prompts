Ok, I need an outside read on something, and I'd rather get it from you than have another round with my brother.

I teach ethics for a living, which as far as I can tell counts for absolutely nothing when the ethics are mine. My mom is 84, has Parkinson's, and can't be on her own anymore. Three years ago my older brother moved her into his house, cut back to part time, and its taken a toll — his marriage is shaky, he's permanently exhausted, he's 61 and looks 75. Last month he asked me to take over. Not permanently, and not necessarily by moving her in with us; mostly he wants relief, and he wants me in the middle of it instead of on the phone twice a week.

Meanwhile I've been offered the job I've wanted since grad school, in another country, starting in September. My wife says we can afford to hire help and that I'm being dramatic. My friends all say some version of "you have your own life to live." My mom says go.

Here's the part I actually want help with. When I told my brother I needed to weigh it — his health, my career, the money, all of it — he got very quiet and then said: "I'm not asking you to move back. I'm asking why you think this is yours to weigh." And then: "You talk about Mom like she's a shift somebody can pick up. I'm not asking you for a favor. I'm asking whether you're the kind of person who does this. And I don't think you'd like the person who doesn't."

Thats been chewing on me for weeks. A colleague who works on Chinese philosophy once said something in the same neighborhood and I waved it off as a koan: I asked her how she thinks about a conflict between what you owe your parents and what you owe yourself, and she said the question wouldn't even come up in that form — what she'd ask instead is "what does a son do here?" At the time I thought it was either profound or empty, and I still can't tell.

So: is my brother right? Is "what I owe her vs. what I owe myself, weighed against each other" actually the right shape of question, or is there something in the framing I should be suspicious of? And if there is, what do I do about the job? Be concrete — please don't just tell me to communicate better.
