Right, so a friend of mine in a seminar has an argument that hedonism isn't just false but can't even be stated, and I've been chewing on it for a week. Here it is, cleaned up:

(1) Hedonism says the only thing that ultimately matters is pleasure, so it has to be able to compare any two lives or outcomes by how much pleasure is in them — or at least to put them on one scale.
(2) For two things to be ranked on a single scale they have to share a common measure, i.e. there must be some one property in virtue of which each of them adds to the total.
(3) So all pleasures must share some one property, the property in virtue of which each one is a pleasure.
(4) Candidates: the pleasure of cold water when you're parched; the click when the last line of a proof closes; the little fizz of hearing that a guy you dislike got passed over for the job; a hot bath; a song in a minor key that makes you cry; scratching a mosquito bite till it bleeds; the relief when a headache lifts.
(5) There's no common feel. Some live in the body, some don't. Some have an object you're pleased about, some don't. Some you'd be embarrassed about, some you wouldn't. Some you chase, some just land on you. Strip out everything they don't share and there's nothing left — no single inner glow, no single tone. (If you say "well they're all liked," that's either circular or it just means "desired," and then hedonism has quietly turned into desire-satisfaction, which is a diffrent theory.)
(6) So there's no one property that all and only pleasures share.
(7) So (3) is false, and by (2) outcomes can't be ranked by pleasure, so hedonism can't even state its thesis, let alone be true.
(8) And none of this is special to pleasure — no two desires share a common feel either, nor beliefs, nor colours (there's no one physical property all red things share). So either the argument is bad, or almost nothing is a kind.

I think (5) is just true — pleasures really are a mess, and I don't want to give that up. I also don't want to give up hedonism, or at least I want to know whether it survives (5). So which step do I cut, and what's the bill? If the answer is "pleasure is a functional kind" or "a determinable," I want to know what that actually buys and whether what's left still deserves the name hedonism or whether it's just relabelling whatever we happen to value as "pleasure." And if (8) is right, then either hedonism dies or a lot of other things die with it, and I'd like to know which. I keep wanting to say he's cheating on (2) but I can't beleive that comparing things on one scale really needs them to share a hidden common ingredient.
