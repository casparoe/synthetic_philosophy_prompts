On my team, the comparison keeps coming up that if the laws of physics fix everything that happens, then a person is just a computer running a program: the brain is the hardware, your history plus the laws are the code, and what feels like deciding is the program producing its output. A colleague leans on this to conclude that free will is an illusion and that praising or blaming anyone is as misplaced as praising a calculator. I want an audit of the analogy rather than a verdict on free will. Give me the correspondence table first — what plays the role of the program, the hardware, the compiler, the specification, and the programmer, if anything does — and then tell me where the mapping breaks. I am especially interested in the features a program has that a deterministic universe may not: a designer with intentions, a spec it can be right or wrong about, an implementation that survives being moved to different hardware, and an outside observer who runs and evaluates it. Separate the disanalogies that actually bear on my colleague's conclusion from the ones that are merely decorative, and say which varieties of free will — which senses of the phrase "worth wanting" — the analogy threatens and which it leaves untouched, in particular whether it quietly trades on determinism in one place and predestination or design in another. Once the mapping is corrected, does the conclusion that free will is an illusion survive, or was the analogy carrying it the whole time? If a better comparison is available, give me that too. Please write in American English.
