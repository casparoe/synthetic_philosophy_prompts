ok so I run a small reading group on applied ethics and we keep going in cirles on the "does my steak matter" question — one person says one person's burger makes no difference to anything, someone else says obviously it does, and then we burn an hour on whether the first person is even denying the second person's claim. I want to hand out a decision tree / flowchart that sorts people by what they actualy think, not by which label they've heard.

Build me the tree. Every branch node should be a question with two or three answers that someone can answer without knowing where it leads; every leaf should be a position, named in plain words (stick the standard label in parentheses if there is one). It has to handle three cases: one person ordering a burger, one person voting in a state that's a foregone conclusion, one person taking a long-haul flight. Build it from the logical space first — what combinations of answers are even available — then place the actual positions in it, and point out the empty cells with a guess at why nobody's there (incoherent? unattractive? nobody's bothered?).

Things I suspect we keep blending and want separated: whether the act makes no difference to the harm at all, vs. whether it makes a difference only in conjunction with other people's acts; whether the complaint is about causation or about moral responsibility; whether the wrongness (if there is any) attaches to doing the thing, to what the doing expresses, or to what kind of person does it; and whether the duty is to do the act or to do something about the outcome (donate, organize, agitate). There's also the threshold idea — each act as one more straw on the camel's back — I want to know where that sits and whether it survives the point that the camel's back isn't a fact you can point to before it breaks. And the 'I'll do my part if enough others do theirs' move needs its own branch, plus whatever position says there's no individual duty at all because only the group can have one. Somewhere around 6-10 leaves, doesn't have to be exact.

If two leaves come out identical on all three cases, say so instead of pretending the tree separates them, and flag anywhere I've baked an assumption into a question that gives away the answer.
