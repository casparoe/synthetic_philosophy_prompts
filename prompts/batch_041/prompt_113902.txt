I am the model-risk lead at a company whose risk score is licensed to two public agencies: a large urban school district that uses it to rank applicants for oversubscribed seats, and a state child-welfare department that uses it to order referrals for investigation. Both agencies set their own decision thresholds, so whatever we certify about the score may not hold at the cutoff they actually operate. Our regulator has rejected our last submission as evasive — it said only that the score was 'not calibrated on any protected attribute' — and now requires a written statement, per release, of the fairness criterion the score is designed to satisfy, why the alternatives were rejected, and who bears the cost of the choice. I accept that equal calibration within groups, equal false-positive rates, equal false-negative rates, and equal selection rates cannot all hold at once when groups differ in base rate; I don't need that argued again. What I need is the procedure my team runs before each release: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, which steps are engineering and which are value decisions belonging to the agency rather than to us, and the point at which the procedure ends in a refusal to ship. It has to survive the case where the outcome label is a biased record of what it purports to measure — arrests rather than offences, substantiated reports rather than actual harm — and the case where a subgroup is too small for its error rates to be estimated with any confidence. Run it on both deployments, whose base rates and error costs differ, and tell me what the procedure cannot settle.
