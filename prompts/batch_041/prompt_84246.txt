You are a philosopher of technology — someone at home both in the analytic literature on causation, agency, and responsibility and in the empirical and historical study of how technical systems shape social life. Please work through this carefully rather than answering off the top of your head: begin by brainstorming several candidate concepts and diagnoses before settling on one, and close by flagging which parts of your answer you take to be well established and which are your own construction.

I have noticed a pattern in arguments about technology that I cannot find a name for. I would like to know whether it already has one, and if it does not, what it should be called and where its edges are.

Here is the pattern. The claim that a technology is neutral — a mere instrument, whose effects are entirely a matter of how people choose to use it — gets deployed selectively. It appears as a defense when the technology is blamed, and disappears when the technology is credited. Some instances:

- A firm defends its recommendation system against charges that it radicalizes users by saying the system is just arithmetic and ranking; it does not decide what anyone sees, people do. The same firm, in its investor materials, presents the same system as the thing that drove a measurable increase in engagement.
- Platforms are treated in law as mere conduits for what users post, and are accordingly shielded from liability for it; the same platforms describe themselves, in other contexts, as having transformed how a generation communicates, organizes, and consumes information.
- When a technology is said to have caused a large social change — a wave of polarization, a decline in some cognitive skill, a political upheaval — the causal claim is usually made about the artifact, and often accepted. When the change is a harm rather than a benefit, the causal claim is retracted in favor of user choice.

So the same artifact is a passive tool when it is accused and an active cause when it is celebrated. What makes me suspect this is not simply ordinary inconsistency is that the two halves are not symmetric in kind. Saying a technology produced a benefit is a claim about causal contribution; saying it produced a harm tends to slide into a claim about responsibility, and responsibility may require more than causal contribution — an agent, a decision, a point at which someone could have done otherwise. If that is right, the asymmetry might be tracking a real distinction rather than being a double standard, and I want to know whether that is the correct way to see it or a rationalization.

I am aware that there is a long-running dispute about whether technical objects can themselves be political, and I do not think that is my question: I am not asking whether a design can encode a value, but about the asymmetry in when the neutrality claim is invoked and when it is withdrawn.

What I need from you:

1. Does this pattern have an established name? If there are close relatives, name each and say precisely where it falls short — in particular, how the thing I am describing differs from plain inconsistency, from the general thesis that technology is value-laden, and from the mundane observation that people defend their own interests.
2. If nothing fits, coin a term, define it, and give boundary cases: what it is, what it is not, and which of my three instances (if any) it fails to cover.
3. Tell me whether this is one phenomenon or several. Is the rhetorical version — an individual reaching for neutrality when criticized — the same thing as the institutionalized version, where a legal or regulatory regime officially classifies an artifact as a conduit while its owners sell it as transformative? If they are different, should they have different names?
4. Is the asymmetry a fallacy — special pleading, or an equivocation on cause or on neutral — or does it track a genuine distinction between causal and normative attribution? Argue for a verdict.
5. What evidence would show that I am pattern-matching noise rather than describing a real regularity? Describe cases that would count against the pattern, and cases that would confirm it.
