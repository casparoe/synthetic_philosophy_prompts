A familiar defense of cultural tolerance runs through an analogy with taste: moral codes, on this view, are like cuisines. Just as no one can be argued out of finding a dish delicious, so no one can be argued out of the moral code they were raised in; disagreement about values is therefore like disagreement about flavors, and the fitting response is not critique but letting each palate alone. The lineage is usually traced to Montaigne, who set the customs of the New World's cannibals beside those of his own France and asked who was really the barbarian. I want an audit of this analogy, not a defense of either side.

Begin with the mapping. Lay out the correspondence explicitly — what plays the role of the palate, the dish, the cuisine, the taster, the incorrigibility of preference — and state plainly what conclusion the analogy is being used to license: toleration, the deflation of cross-cultural criticism, and the denial of any single correct moral code.

Then locate the joints. Which features of the source domain are being quietly imported into the target, and which of them does the target actually possess? Consider, among others: that taste is private and corrigible only from within; that it makes no claim to universality; that it is not addressed to anyone else's interests; that disagreement about it is neither resolvable nor consequential; that it is seated in the body rather than in reasons. Sort the disanalogies into those that bear on the intended use and those that are merely decorative.

Then the part I most want. Montaigne does not merely report the relativity of custom; he turns the cannibals into a mirror for European cruelty, which appears to presuppose some standard by which Europe can be found wanting. If that is right, the taste analogy may be working against the very passage it is invoked to support — the relativism that makes tolerance easy also disarms the critique. Assess whether Montaigne's own use of the cannibals is consistent with the relativist reading, and whether the tolerance conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the conclusion all along. Finally, note whether tolerance is better grounded in relativism at all or in some universal principle that the taste analogy obscures, and if a better analogy is available for the work actually wanted, propose it and say what it would fix.
