I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background, and I've gotten weirdly into this wasp thing. You probably know it: the digger wasp drags a paralized cricket to her burrow, ducks inside to inspect the burrow, and if you nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's in there, she just... starts over. Drops it, drags it back, inspects again, forever. Hofstadter coined a word for that stuck-in-a-loop behavior, sphexishness, and Dennett picked it up to talk about free will -- the thought being, if the wasp's whole life is a fixed program, how sure are we that our "choices" aren't the same thing nested a few levels deeper? (I also half-remember someone pushing back that the wasp story is basically folklore and the wasp does eventually adapt. I find that hilarious and want the doc to deal with it, not just assume the myth.)

A friend and I have been arguing about this for months and we're talking past each other, so let's do the adverserial-collaboration thing: write the joint statement we could both actually sign.

The two of us are:

- Continuity: there's no difference in kind between the wasp and us, only degree. Flexibility is just rigid sub-routines stacked deep enough to loop back on themselves, and "stepping back to examine your own routine" is itself one more routine. So sphexishness goes all the way up; free will is a story we tell about the deepest stack.
- Break: something categorically different happens in us. The wasp can't represent, notice, or override its own procedure; we can catch ourselves mid-loop. That self-catching isn't just more nesting -- it's the thing free will was always about.

Give me:

1. A numbered list of claims both sides can sign -- about what's actually established vs. folklore in the wasp case, about what rigidity and flexibility even mean, about what would count as a system genuinely "catching itself," and about what free will is and isn't asking.
2. A disagreements table: each live disagreement worded so its own side would accept the phrasing as fair rather than a strawman, the crux underneath it, and for each crux a case, experiment, or argument both sides agree would actually bear on it -- with each side's prediction written down in advance.
3. Somewhere in there, say whether any of it is just a verbal fight -- e.g. if "flexible" is quietly doing two different jobs.

If my setup is confused -- if sphexishness is being used in two senses at once, or if I'm just smuggling in an assumption that behavioral flexibility has anything to do with free will, or if the folklore point means the whole thing is built on sand -- say so plainly instead of handing me a tidy document that papers over it.
