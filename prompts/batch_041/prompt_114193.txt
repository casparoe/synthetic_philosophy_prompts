Before I read any more accounts of promissory obligation, I want the specification of what such an account has to deliver. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of why promises bind must meet: the cases it must get right, the puzzles it must dissolve, the distinctions it must respect, and the constraints it inherits from the nature of convention, communication, and personal identity. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it and mark whether it is non-negotiable or merely a commitment of one camp; where you suspect that a pair of requirements cannot be jointly satisfied, say which pair and what that would show. Keep the weight on the criteria rather than on scoring the familiar contenders, though if a single requirement does most of the excluding work, name it.

Make the list answer to these cases in particular, since they are the ones I cannot get to fit any single account:

- a promise whose words the promiser does not understand, having only repeated them;
- a promise the promisee has explicitly released the promiser from;
- a promise made to someone who has since died, where performance remains possible;
- a promise to do something independently wrongful;
- a resolution made only to oneself;
- a promise made inside a game or role-play, where no one expects performance;
- a promise whose intended recipient never learns of it.

Then say briefly which of the standard grounds for promissory obligation—the promisee's reliance, the promiser's exercise of will, a social convention, or the value of the relationship itself—survives the whole list, and which requirement eliminates the most rivals.
