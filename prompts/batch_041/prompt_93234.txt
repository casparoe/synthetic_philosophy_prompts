I'm a market designer by training — mechanism design, matching, auction theory — with only a tourist's grasp of moral philosophy. I keep encountering the claim that certain markets are *repugnant*, or that putting a price on a thing *commodifies* it, and I want to know how much of that objection my own vocabulary can actually carry. Take the repugnance/commodification objection as it is pressed against kidney sales, price gouging after a disaster, and vote selling, and restate it as faithfully as you can in the terms I actually use: a planner selecting a mechanism over a feasible set of allocations; participation constraints; incentive-compatibility constraints; an objective function defined over outcomes; side constraints that are deliberately not traded off against that objective; and preferences that may be defined over the mechanism itself rather than over the allocation it produces.

Work through the objection element by element and, for each, say where it lands in that apparatus — as a restriction on the feasible set, as a term in the objective, as a participation constraint, as a side constraint, or as a procedural preference over mechanisms — and flag every step where you had to choose a reading rather than simply translate. Do not assume that the objection is secretly about welfare, and do not let the apparatus quietly convert a claim about what a good *is* into a claim about how much people want it. Then give me the residue report: which parts of the objection survive translation intact, which survive only in a distorted form, and which have no counterpart at all in my vocabulary. For the residue, tell me whether the gap is a defect of my framework — something a well-built mechanism-design model ought to be able to represent but currently cannot — or evidence that the original claim was doing less work than it appeared to. I am especially interested in whether the distinction between objections that turn on consequences (harm, coercion, exploitation) and objections that turn on the meaning of the good being priced is a real distinction or one that dissolves once the mechanism is written down carefully.
