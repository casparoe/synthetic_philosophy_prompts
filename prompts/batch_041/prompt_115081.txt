Okay, so here's one I keep going back and forth on. October 2020, the FBI arrests a bunch of guys in Michigan for plotting to kidnap the governor. The whole defense at trial was entrapment, and their claim was that about a dozen of the people in and around the group were working for the government -- the meetings, the rides, some of the money, alot of the pushing. Prosecutors said the guys were already at it before the feds ever showed up. And the juries came out split: a couple aquitted, a couple hung and then convicted on a retrial, so the case never really settled what to make of it.

Federal entrapment as I understand it: the defendant shows he was induced, the burden flips, and the government has to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that he was predisposed -- ready and willing, and specifically before any contact with the agents. Which is a weirdly pure counterfactual test: strip the government out of the world, look at the guy as he was, ask whether he'd have done it anyway.

Now run that through Frankfurt. In the Frankfurt case Black sits there ready to make sure Jones does the thing, Jones does it on his own, and Frankfurt says the counterfactual doesn't matter -- the intervener never lifts a finger, so the act is entirely Jones's, and he's responsible even though he couldn't have done otherwise. So do the mapping properly: who's the intervener in the sting, what's actually doing the ensuring, and what is the defendant even claiming when he says they made him do it -- is that a PAP claim at all, or something else wearing PAP's clothes? Because the analogy looks like it snaps at the hinge: the informant isn't a counterfactual cause who never acts, he's an actual cause who builds the thing he's supposed to be merely guaranteeing.

Then run it the other way. Does a courtroom of people with no philosophical ax to grind reaching for would-he-have-done-it-anyway show that PAP was right all along and Frankfurt's intuition is an artifact of a rigged setup? Or is the law tracking a different question -- whose act was this, who authored the crime, was the state the real cause -- in which case Frankfurt is untouched and the interesting thing is that the same test is doing two jobs at once? And be honest about whether the Frankfurt case is simply silent when the intervener is also the producer, or whether that's a genuine counterexample. If duress fits better, or the version of entrapment that asks about the cops' conduct instead of the defendant's character, say so, and tell me what would have to be true of that sting for Frankfurt's conclusion to actually hold of it.
