I'm a second-year phil student and I'm in a mind and metaphysics course where we just did the Dennett piece, Where Am I? — the one where his brain gets taken out and kept alive in a tank in one city while his body is sitting in another one and he's remote-controlling it over a radio link (I can't remember which two cities, and our TA said the cities dont matter, but if they do, tell me). I follow the story fine, but my roommate and I have been having this arguement for a week and I don't think either of us has actually got it.

Here's the thing. I said that you go where your brain goes: the brain is the thing doing the thinking, so if your brain is in the tank then you're in the tank, and that's just true regardless of what the body is up to. He said no, you go where your body goes, because you see what the body sees and feel what the body feels and hurt where the body hurts, and if anyone asked where you were you'd point at the body — a brain in a tank isn't anywhere in particular, it's just meat in fluid. Then we started messing with the setup to test it. What if the radio link gets routed through a satellite with a two-second lag, so everything you see and feel arrives late — does the lag move you? What if the brain is cut in half and one half goes to each city, each half running one side of the body — then am I in two places at once, which obviously can't happen? My roommate says cases like that show that "where am I" is just a convention. I said that can't be right, because a convention is the kind of thing we could change just by agreeing to change it, and you can't relocate yourself by agreeing to.

So one of us has to be wrong, but when we asked our TA he just said we're both assuming something we shouldn't and wouldn't tell us what. Which of us is wrong, or are we both? And if my roommate is right that it's a convention, doesn't that mean there's no fact of the matter about where I am at all? That seems obviously false to me — there's definately a fact about where my experience is happening, I just don't know how to say where.
