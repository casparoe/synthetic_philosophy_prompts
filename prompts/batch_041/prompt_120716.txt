Construct a thought experiment for a graduate seminar on Aquinas and tyrannicide, designed to test whether his distinction between a legitimate ruler turned tyrant and a usurper does independent moral work or merely tracks expected consequences.

Specifications:

1. The setting must be realistic: a small polity with a functioning legal order, not a fantasy kingdom. No divine signs, no secret heirs revealed by convenient documents, no stipulations about anyone's private intentions, and no assumption that the actors are unusually wise or well informed.

2. Two scenarios must hold fixed the ruler's cruelty, the harm he inflicts, the expected consequences of killing him, the killer's identity and motive, and the likelihood of success. They may differ only in whether the ruler holds legitimate title or seized power illegitimately.

3. On Aquinas's reasoning the verdict should flip between these two scenarios: the same killing is permitted in one and forbidden in the other. Show the principle that produces each verdict rather than asserting the verdict.

4. Then vary exactly one further feature — either whether some public authority capable of acting against the ruler exists, or how much harm the disturbance would cause relative to the tyranny — so that the verdict flips back while the title variable stays fixed. If your reconstruction of Aquinas makes this impossible, say so and explain what blocks it; that is an acceptable outcome and should be argued rather than evaded.

5. The case must generate its verdicts from Aquinas's principles, not stipulate them. In particular, engage his claim that tyranny is defective because it is directed at the ruler's private good rather than the common good, his proviso that a tyrannical government may be disturbed only if the disturbance does not inflict greater harm than the tyranny, and his treatment of power that was never rightfully held. Do not cite or quote texts; reconstruct the reasoning in your own words, and flag any point where you are unsure what Aquinas actually held.

Format the response as follows: "The case" (one paragraph); "Verdicts, scenario by scenario" (each scenario with its verdict and the principle yielding it); "What does the work" (a short paragraph naming the operative feature and saying what happens if it is removed); "A Thomist's reply" (a short paragraph on how a defender of Aquinas would resist the case or deny that it is a counterexample).
