I have been turning over a loop in my own thinking for some months and cannot get out of it alone, so I want your credence in one specific claim, with the reasoning laid out rather than summarized. I have no formal training in philosophy; what follows is my own attempt to sharpen the issue, and I would rather have your derivation than a survey of who said what.

The loop. To know whether my eyes can be trusted, I do the natural thing: I look at something, expect it to be a certain way, and check that the expectation holds. But the checking is done with the very eyes under audit. It looks as though I may trust my eyes only if I already have reason to trust my eyes, which is no reason at all.

To keep the question from floating, here is the claim I want priced:

(C) A person can have a justified true perceptual belief that counts as knowledge, without first having a justified belief that her perceptual faculties are reliable.

Treat knowledge however you think best, but state what you are assuming.

I also invented a case to test my own intuitions, and I would like you to use it. Imagine a woman whose visual experience carries a faint extra signal — I call it the glow — which occurs, as a matter of fact, exactly on the occasions when her eyes are working correctly, and never otherwise. She has always trusted the glow and has never checked it, because the only instrument available for checking it is the glow itself. Two questions: does she know the things she sees; and, more important to me, is her situation relevantly different from mine, or is it the same circular structure wearing a costume?

Please price (C) by decomposing it into at least these sub-questions, adding any others you think belong:

(a) Must all justification be inferential, or are some beliefs justified immediately, without inference from anything else?
(b) If immediate justification is allowed, does it also license knowledge of the reliability of the faculty itself, obtained by using that faculty — and is that acceptable or vicious?
(c) Is the glow case genuinely different in kind from ordinary perception, or does it collapse into the same structure?
(d) Is the demand for prior justification of reliability a coherent requirement, or does it rest on an over-demanding principle that every presupposition must itself be backed by a reason?
(e) How much weight should our confidence that ordinary perceptual knowledge exists be allowed to place on the answer — that is, when the skeptical requirement and the plain fact of knowing collide, which should give way?

A friend of mine puts her credence in (C) near 0.15; I am nearer 0.65. Tell me which single sub-question our disagreement most likely lives in.

Return the answer as a JSON object with exactly these fields:

- claim: (C) restated in your own words, precisely enough to have a truth value.
- credence: your probability that (C) is true, a number between 0 and 1.
- decomposition: an array of objects, one per sub-question, each with the fields term (a short label), sub_question (the question being priced), probability (a number between 0 and 1), how_it_enters (one line on its role in the combination), and one_line_justification.
- combination: a string describing how the terms combine into your credence.
- most_sensitive_term: the label of the term the final number is most sensitive to.
- why_most_sensitive: one or two sentences.
- what_would_move_it: an array of objects, each with evidence_or_argument, direction (up or down), and rough_size (small, moderate, or large).
- where_disagreement_lives: the term most likely separating me from my friend at 0.15, with one line of explanation.
- what_would_count_as_resolving_the_question: one or two sentences on what would settle (C), if anything could.
