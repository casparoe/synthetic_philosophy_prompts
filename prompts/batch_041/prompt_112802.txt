I've been going back and forth with my brother about promises and I want to pin down what I actually think, case by case, then see if there's one principle behind it. My gut verdicts:

- I promise to give a talk at a friend's event. A better offer comes up. I still owe them the talk, or at least a real excuse. Binds.
- Same setup, but last week they told me they found someone else and I should forget it. Now I don't owe them anything. Released.
- I promise a mugger with a knife that I'll hand over my wallet. Doesn't bind. Obviously.
- I promise my partner, after a long fight where they cry and say that if I really loved them I'd swear never to see my college friends again, that I will. I don't think that binds either -- though I'm less sure about this one.
- But I promise my kid I'll take them to the zoo, then it rains and a work thing comes up. I feel like I really do owe them that trip, more than I owe the friend the talk.
- I promise my dying father I'll look after my mother. He's gone now, can't hold me to anything, and I still feel bound -- I'd feel like a different person if I blew it off.
- Yet: I promise to water your plants while you're away, and it turns out you don't care about the plants at all, you'd just as soon they died. That promise feels almost empty to me. I don't feel bound the same way.
- I promise to do something that turns out to be impossible. Nothing to do, no obligation left.
- Playing make-believe with my nephew, I "promise," as part of the game, that I'm the king and won't leave the castle. Everyone knows it's a game. That word doesn't bind anything.
- I promise to keep a secret, and the secret is that you're going to hurt someone. I'm not bound.
- I promise myself I'll quit smoking. If I break it I feel something, but it's not the same kind of thing as breaking a promise to a person.

The two that won't sit together for me: the dead-father promise feels binding even though there's no one left to be disappointed or to rely on anything, while the plants promise feels close to empty even though you're right there and could in principle care. If bindingness is about the promisee's expectations or interests, the plants case comes out weak (fine by me) but the dead case comes out zero (not fine by my gut). If instead it's about my own act of committing, the dead case works but then the plants case and the manipulation case should bind too, and they don't.

Is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? If I have to give one up, which one? Please state the principle (or a couple of candidates) precisely enough that I can tell what each clause is doing and which case forces it, say what your favorite version predicts for a few I haven't listed -- a promise to someone I later learn isn't who they claimed to be; a promise to do something I was already obligated to do anyway; a "promise" in a community that uses the word to mean nothing more than "I intend to," where both of us know that; and a promise I made to someone who has since become someone I'd cross the street to avoid -- and be honest about which of my verdicts your principle can't accommodate.
