I am preparing a lecture on middle knowledge and want to be sure I have the Molinist's position right, because my own reconstruction of it keeps generating a dilemma that I suspect is my error rather than the position's. As I understand it, God's middle knowledge consists in counterfactuals of freedom — what each possible creature would freely do in each possible set of circumstances — and these hold antecedently to any decree of his will, in the order of explanation rather than in time. But if they hold antecedently to the decree, they are not themselves something God has decided, and they are not necessary truths either, since they concern contingent free actions. It therefore seems to me that God, being omnipotent, could have had a different set of them: he could have made it true that this very creature, in these very circumstances, would freely choose the good; and having secured that across the board, he could have created a world containing no free wrongdoing. Since he did not, either he could have prevented that evil and chose not to — in which case he is answerable for it — or the counterfactuals were fixed independently of his will, in which case something other than God constrains what he can create. Where does my reconstruction go wrong, and what is it that Molinists say these counterfactuals are true in virtue of?
