Write a villanelle in British English, in five tercets and a closing quatrain, spoken by a citizen whose conception of the good has been fed into an AI alignment procedure. The procedure is designed to serve only values that can be justified without appeal to any comprehensive doctrine: it translates each citizen's ends into an ordinal preference ranking over policies, treats those rankings as interpersonally incomparable, strips away the reasons that made those ends authoritative, aggregates the rankings by a Borda count, vetoes any winner that cannot be endorsed by every citizen on neutral grounds, and then runs again on what remains. The two refrains must be the citizen's two positions, held together without reconciliation: first, that her good is not merely a preference to be counted; second, that under the procedure it can be counted as nothing else. Let the form carry the argument rather than merely illustrate it. The refrains should return not as a lyric sigh but as the procedure re-running, so that by the closing quatrain, which should end with the two refrains side by side, the reader feels that the AI is serving neither the citizen nor her preference but the neutrality constraint that keeps converting the one into the other. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader who works on social choice or liberal neutrality recognises the dilemma, and it should not depend on naming any theorist or citing any text; it should also work for a reader who knows none of that.
