My mate Ravi has been running a decision-making seminar for years and he's become the guy our group chat brings cases to. His verdict is always one of two things -- mistake, or not a mistake -- and he means the decision to keep going, not the decision to start. I've been keeping score:

- State DOT keeps pouring money into a half-built interchange that the new traffic models say nobody will ever use, because they've already spent $400m. Mistake.
- Pharma company keeps a phase 3 trial going after flat interim results, because they've already enrolled and paid for the patients. Mistake.
- Two guys at a party get into a dollar auction over a $1 bill: bids in 5c steps, top bidder pays their bid and takes the bill, second-highest pays theirs and gets nothing. They're at $2 and $2.05. Ravi says keep bidding -- dropping out means paying two bucks for nothing, and the next bid at least buys you a shot at the bill. And anyway neither of them knew what they were walking into, the guy who set the game up is the one who should feel bad.
- A founder keeps funding a product after a failed pilot, but each cycle the roadmap gets sharper and the next bet is cheaper. Not a mistake.
- My old landlord kept a dead laundromat open two more years because he'd sunk his savings into the machines. Block was empty, he was loosing $3k a month, and everyone including his brother told him to walk away. Mistake.

Now the new one. My aunt has gotten hooked on one of those pay-per-bid auction sites: you buy bids at 60 cents each, every bid nudges the price up a penny, the top bidder wins and pays the final price, and every bid you've placed is gone whether you win or not. She's spent about $900 in bid fees in three weeks, she's currently top bidder on a $650 camera at $9.75, and two other people on it outbid her within a second every single time. She's already spent more than the camera is worth. She says she can't stop now because she's in too deep, and she genuinely had no idea how these sites worked when she signed up -- she thought it was a normal auction with a weird fee.

What does Ravi say about her carrying on: mistake, or not a mistake? Give me an actual prediction. He's definately running a rule and I can't see which one. That line about the party game could be a rule about the maths, or a rule about who's to blame, or it might just be a rule about whether the person's reason is "I've spent so much already" versus "here's what's ahead" -- so tell me which one he's actually running and why, what the other candidates are, what each of them predicts about my aunt, which earlier case is the closest analogue and whether his verdict there really commits him here, and what one more case I should put to him to settle it. Pick one and stick to it -- if you hand me a menu I've learned nothing.
