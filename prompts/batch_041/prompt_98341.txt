I sit on our hospital's consent appeals panel and we keep having to decide whether a patient's agreement actually counted as consent. Track record so far (details changed, obviously):

- Patient agreed to surgery believing Dr. X would do it; Dr. X actually handed it to a junior he never mentioned. We voided the consent.
- Patient agreed to a blood draw and wasn't told leftover samples would go to a research biobank. We upheld.
- Patient agreed to a drug whose rare side effect (~1 in 50,000, mild rash) wasn't mentioned. We upheld.
- Patient was told a treatment was 'standard' when it was still experimental. We voided.
- Patient agreed to a procedure after being told the doctor had no financial stake when he did. We upheld, reasoning that his fee arrangement wasn't part of what she was agreeing to.
- Patient with mild cognitive impairment agreed to a low-risk dressing change; we upheld, though we said we'd have voided the same agreement for major surgery.
- Patient agreed to a risky procedure because the only alternative was losing her insurance; we voided, saying the pressure wasn't a threat in the strict sense but it left her no real choice.

New one on my desk: the patient is a professional singer, and the doctor knew it, and nobody told her about a ~1-in-10,000 chance of temporary hoarseness. A reasonable patient would shrug at that number; she's told us flat out she'd have refused. Everything else about the consent was clean, no coercion, no competence issue.

What do we do? I'd like your best guess at the verdict, but mainly I want the rules our past rulings are consistent with, what each of those rules predicts about this case, and which future case would actually tell those rules apart. Also please seperate what's genuinely contested among people who work on consent from what's basically settled, and don't feed me a contested view as if it's the consensus. Under 700 words.
