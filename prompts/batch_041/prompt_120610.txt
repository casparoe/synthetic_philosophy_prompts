A bunch of us got into this at a barbecue and it's still going a year later, so what I want is a diagnosis rather than an answer. It started with my friend Dana saying a cold sore is "a living thing living in you" and me saying a virus is more like a chemical that copies itself using your cells, and neither of us has budged since. The annoying part is that we agree on all the facts: viruses have no metabolism, no cells, no ribosomes of their own, they don't do anything at all on their own — but they have genomes, they mutate and get selected, they have a replication cycle, even that word. And once we start pulling in other cases the fight gets worse rather than better: prions, viroids (a bare loop of RNA and nothing else), mitochondria, which used to be free-living bacteria and now aren't, a tumour that evolves drug resistance inside somebody, those little robots made out of frog cells that scoot around and heal themselves, a computer worm, a crystal seeding copies of itself in a beaker, a beehive, the whole biosphere, an embryo in a freezer. Dana's sister works in a hospital lab and says we're being silly, that "alive" is just a word people use for convenience and there's no fact of the matter to get right; another friend insists there has to be a real answer, because astrobiologists will eventually be staring at some chemical soup on Europa and have to decide whether to call it life, and you can't do that with a word you've already agreed doesn't mean anything. A third friend says it's like "game" — nobody can define it and that's fine, we go by family resemblance — which sounds clever, but I notice he still says some things are alive and some definately aren't.

So: is this a verbal dispute about a word, one that would dissolve if we all signed up to a single definition? Or a real disagreement about a hidden fact of the matter — whether there's actually a threshold somewhere in nature, a phase transition, a point where a chemical system starts maintaining itself or starts dissipating energy in that special way I heard someone describe on a podcast? Or is it two different questions wearing one word, one being "what should we count as alive when we go looking", the other "what is it about a thing that makes it alive"? Or is the question itself broken, in which case the stall is exactly what you'd expect? My own hunch is that it's mostly verbal and Dana's sister is right, but then I can't explain why the argument keeps feeling like something is at stake. Whatever you conclude, tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis — a distinction, a new case that forces a choice, an experiment, a proof that no sharp line can exist, or nothing at all, and why. Don't just list proposed definitions of life; I've read a pile of those and they're part of the problem.

Bottom line first, please: your diagnosis in two or three sentences at the top, then the reasons. Cap the whole thing at 700 words.
