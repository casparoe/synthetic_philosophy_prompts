For the past several months I have been stuck between two courses of action — one that would let me take up work I find absorbing, one that would keep me near a family member who needs me — and I have come to believe the obstacle is not missing information but the absence of any common measure on which the two could be weighed. I have been tempted by each of the following ways of deciding, which I gloss so that we are discussing the same things: (1) lexical priority — settle once which value outranks the other and always serve the higher one; (2) maximin — choose the option whose worst outcome is least bad; (3) satisficing — take the first option that clears a threshold on every value at stake; (4) covering value — ask which option better serves some more inclusive value under which both fall, such as my family's flourishing over the long run; (5) parity plus commitment — treat the options as roughly on a par and let a deliberate act of commitment supply the reason the values themselves do not; (6) randomization — give each option an equal chance and let a coin decide; (7) regret minimization — choose the option whose anticipated regret is smaller. Please sort these into tiers by how much you would trust each to yield a defensible resolution of a genuinely hard choice between incommensurable values, giving one sentence of justification per item, and indicate which, if any, you regard as a real resolution rather than an evasion of the choice.
