Ok so I run a small consciousness reading group — mixed crowd, a few cog-sci PhD students and a couple of philosophers, everyone has read the standard stuff about the color phi effect and Dennett's Orwellian/Stalinesque split, and I want to push them past "there are two options and Dennett says we can't choose between them." I need a handout that does the sorting for them.

Quick gloss so we're on the same page, correct me if I mangle a detail: you flash a red dot on the left, then ~100ms later a green dot on the right, and subjects report one dot traveling across and changing color partway — with the color change seeming to happen before the second flash was even on the screen, which is the weird part, since the info about the second flash's color arrives after. The Stalinesque story: the system holds the experience back, edits the two flashes into one coherent movie, and only then presents it, so there was never an experience of the unedited version. The Orwellian story: you really do experience the red dot moving, and then the memory of it gets rewritten afterward, 1984-style. Same reports either way. Dennett's move is that the disagreement is only about where to draw the pre-/post-experiential line, and there's no principled place to draw it.

What I want: a decision tree (flowchart, whatever's cleaner) that takes someone from a starting commitment to one of the named positions on this. The logical space has to be bigger than Orwellian vs Stalinesque. Build it out of the questions first and then locate the views in the space — don't list names and draw branches between them.

You're a philosopher of mind who also knows the vision science, so be precise about the processing and don't hand-wave. Think it through carefully, and before you write anything, brainstorm in a scratch list the axes on which these views can actually disagree — I want to see that you've found the axes rather than the usual two.

The angle I care about: the tree should sort by two things at once. (i) What does the view say about whether the report can come apart from the experience — is the verbal report tracking the experience, or reconstructing it later, or neither? And (ii) does the view think the question 'when was the color experienced' has a determinate answer at all? I also want it to pull apart views on which the phenomenon is a fact about reports/narration from views on which it's a fact about experience, and views on which the whole thing is an artifact of how the two flashes interact in early vision (no interpolation, no revision, nothing to explain) from views that accept there's something to explain.

Every node should be phrased as a question a reader can answer without knowing which position it leads to — concrete cases, yes/no or 2–3 options, never 'do you accept the Stalinesque hypothesis?'. Use these as nodes where you can:

What each view predicts here is part of what the tree should expose;
