I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for a long time, and I run a small reading group that grew out of one. The group is comfortable with basic propositional logic and with possibility and necessity talk, but most members have not studied epistemology. Next month I am responsible for Fitch's paradox of knowability. I do not want to present it as the familiar gotcha that if all truths are knowable then all truths are known, because several of them have already seen that formulation and think the whole thing is a parlor trick about self-reference. I want to teach a variant that I have been chewing on, and I want you to plan the session rather than deliver the explanation.

The variant replaces knowledge with a factive attitude I have been calling registration. Registration is factive: if an agent registers a proposition, the proposition is true. It is also closed under conjunction: if an agent registers a conjunction, the agent registers each conjunct. The principle to be examined is that every truth can be registered by some possible agent. The proof is structurally the same as Fitch's. Take a truth p that no one registers. The principle says it is possible that some agent registers the conjunction that p and that no one registers p. Closure gives that it is possible that the agent registers p and also registers that no one registers p. Factivity gives that no one registers p, contradicting the first conjunct. So if every truth can be registered, every truth is registered. I want the group to see exactly which assumptions are doing the work and why the result is not about knowledge in particular.

Plan the explanation for me. What has to be introduced first, and what depends on what, so that the proof feels like a consequence of the assumptions rather than a magic trick? What single carrying example should anchor the whole session, and what does that example need to have and lack? What misconceptions should I expect, in the order they will arise, and at what point in the sequence should I head each one off? I am already expecting that they will conflate can be registered with is registered, that they will think the proof assumes an omniscient agent, and that they will treat factivity or closure as optional idealizations. Give me one closing question that would show whether they have understood the structure rather than merely followed the steps, and tell me what I should deliberately leave out at this level and say so. Do not give me readings or citations; I want the dependency structure and the misconception map.
