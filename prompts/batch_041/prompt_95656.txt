I've been lurking around the rationalist-adjacent blogs for years now (LessWrong, the old SSC archives, a bit of the EA forum when I'm feeling brave) and I keep circling back to the insect-suffering thing. The bit that fascinates me isn't the moral conclusion, it's the *machinery*: you take an astronomical number of insects, multiply it by a tiny and basically unmeasurable probability that any one of them is sentient, and the expected value comes out ahead of animals we're all extremely confident about. That's Pascal's mugging with the infinity filed off, and I genuinly can't tell whether the move is brilliant or a warning sign. I want a satire that takes the machinery seriously rather than just pointing and laughing at it.

So: write me the FAQ page (or "About Us" / donor-facing page, whichever lands better) for a completely fictional insect-welfare charity — please invent it, don't model it on a real org or name any real people, I don't want it reading like a hit piece on actual campaigners. Call it something like the Hexapod Trust, and let it have its own invented in-house metric, say a "Sentience-Adjusted Insect-Year" (SAIY), plus a couple of internal factions that are barely on speaking terms (the ones who want to abolish insecticide outright versus the reformists pushing for "humane" killing methods, and the research wing that just wants to fund work on whether a fruit fly has anything it's like to be one). Deadpan throughout — the humour has to come from the arguments being *right*, not from the writer signalling that they're in on the joke. A reader who knows their decision theory should recognise the expected-value arithmetic, the probability-weighting under deep uncertainty, the moral-uncertainty move where you split your credence across rival theories of consciousness, and the aggregation problem, and should laugh rather than wince.

Land the standard objections without ever stating them: who decides the probability, whether a headcount is even the right kind of thing to aggregate, whether a charity that can't tell you if its beneficiaries are conscious should be asking for your money, and the awkward fact that its own staff kill insects every day (they have an offset scheme, obviously). About 650 words, formatted as Q&A headings with answers underneath, and keep the irony legible without a wink at the end — no "and that's why insects don't matter" reveal, just let the page sit there being sincere. I'm using this as a prompt in a dataset of conceptual prompts, so it has to be self-contained: assume whoever's answering it can't look anything up.
