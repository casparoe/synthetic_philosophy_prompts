I'm a history PhD student, mostly early modern Europe, though I keep wandering into economic history, and I've spent the last couple of weeks (when I should have been writing about grain prices) reading around the Great Divergence stuff for a seminar paper -- the whole "why did industrialization take off in England and not in China / India / Japan" genre, plus the older puzzle about why modern science didn't arise in China. Theres a move I keep seeing and I dont have a word for it. Someone asks why X happened here and not there. They answer with a list of things "here" had: coal lying close to the surface, high wages, a tinkering culture, secure property, cheap credit, whatever. Then a commentator points out that the "there" had most of those too -- China had coal, the Yangzi delta had high wages, Tokugawa Japan had secure enough property -- so how can any of them be the reason this side pulled ahead, if the other side had them as well? And then the first person half-retreats: no no, I was only explaining why it happened here, I wasn't claiming to explain why it didn't happen there. Everyone nods, the conversation moves on, and I always feel like something just got swept under the rug but I can't say what.

What I want to know is what the requirement actually is that's being invoked, whether that retreat is legitamate or a cop-out, and what to call the whole thing. Is there a name for the demand that an answer to a "why here and not there" question has to point at something that genuinely differs between the two cases? Because I've also noticed what looks like the mirror-image mistake: someone takes that demand as a general rule for all historical explanation and then throws out any factor that was present on both sides -- even though a factor can be present on both sides and still be the difference, if it's present in wildly different degrees, or if the two sides had it for different reasons, or if one side could substitute for it and the other couldn't. So the thing I'm after seems to have two halves, and I suspect they need separate names. People keep waving "correlation isn't causation" and "you need a counterfactual" at me, and neither seems to be the actual point -- the first is about evidence, the second is about something else. I'm not after just a label: I want the carve. What exactly is the requirement, what is it not, why is the retreat sometimes fine and sometimes a dodge, and where are the edges? If nothing in the literature covers both halves, coin me a name and a definition for the demand and for its mirror image, and tell me which existing concepts come closest, so I can see what I'd gain and lose by using your coinage instead. (Bonus if it also explains why the same shape shows up when people argue about why the printing press spread faster in some places, or in biology when someone asks why one lineage and not its sister went in for some trick.)
