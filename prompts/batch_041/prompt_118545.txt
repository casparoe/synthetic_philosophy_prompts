My friend and I got into a three hour argument last night about how bossy a society is allowed to be -- he keeps calling it the tight versus loose thing, like some places are just wound tighter and that's that -- and I came out of it with a heap of gut calls that won't sit still. I want to know what principle, if any, they add up to.

Here's my pile. A town that fines you for blasting music at 2am: fine. A town that fines you for skipping the annual block potluck: genuinely oppressive, and I don't care that its only twenty bucks. Littering fines: fine. A law that you have to wear a helmet on a motorcycle: fine. A law that you have to get thirty minutes of exercise a day: absurd, and I'd say so even if it worked. A country where swearing in public earns you a dirty look: fine, even charming. Where it earns you a fine: not fine. A workplace that fires someone for how they treat waiters on their own time: fine. A workplace that fires someone for who they're dating: not fine. A village where you're expected to greet strangers on the street and people think you're cold if you don't: fine, I'd live there. A village where you're expected to laugh at the mayor's jokes: not fine. Same town being strict with visitors and easygoing with locals: not fine, and it feels different in kind from the rest, like the rule was never really a rule. Mandatory jury duty: fine. Mandatory voting in a local election: I honestly can't tell. And then the one currently eating me: a housing co-op where everyone takes a turn cleaning the shared kitchen and people who skip get docked from their deposit. My gut says that's fine and I can't for the life of me say why it isn't the potluck over again.

My half-baked theory is that tightness is fine wherever a violation dumps a cost on someone who never signed up for it, and wrong wherever the cost lands only on the rule-breaker or on people who could just walk away. I don't think that survives the helmet law, and I don't know if it survives the co-op, or the greeting norm, or whether cost is even the right currency -- some of these feel like they're about who we are rather than who gets hurt. My friend says it's really about whether a rule can be stated precisely and enforced cheaply, which sounds to me like it's describing bureaucracy rather than legitimacy, but maybe he's onto something. I keep noticing that the rules I hate are the ones nobody can actually write down but everyone somehow knows how to apply, and the ones I like are the ones you could print on a sign.

So give me the principle, or the two or three competing principles, and tell me which of my calls each one has to throw overboard to stay coherent. Say what each predicts about cases I haven't listed -- a rule that parents can't let an eight year old walk to school alone, a rule that you have to stand for the anthem at a public event, a rule that you have to carry ID, a rule that every household has to keep a bucket of sand by the door for fires that basically never happen. And please be straight about which parts of this are actually settled among people who study it and which are genuinely contested -- I don't want a contested view dressed up as the consensus, and I'd rather hear that a question is open than get a tidy answer that isn't.
