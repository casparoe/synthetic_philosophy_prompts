A pattern keeps turning up in the history and history-of-science I read, and I cannot tell whether it has a name or whether I am just redescribing hindsight bias. Take two accounts of the same body of evidence, written by the same historian with the same inferential steps: one explains why a revolution succeeded, the other why an apparently identical set of conditions produced a failed rising elsewhere. Readers call the first an explanation and the second a rationalization, even when the second is a strict mirror of the first. The outcome does not merely raise my estimate of how likely it was; it changes which conditions I am willing to count as causes at all. The same facts that figure as "the causes" in the successful case get demoted to "background" or "countervailing factors" in the failed one, and I seem to have no independent test for the promotion other than the outcome itself. I know hindsight bias and the narrative fallacy, but as I understand them the first concerns my sense of inevitability and the second the pull of a coherent story, whereas what bothers me is outcome-dependence in the standard of what counts as an explanation. Is there an established term for that? If there is, name it and say how it differs from those two and from the older charge of reading history backwards from the present. If there is not, coin one and define it, and tell me where its edges are -- in particular whether it applies to explanations with no narrative at all, such as a statistical model that fits the observed outcome and would have fit its negation equally well.
