I run a monthly philosophy interview series, and next month’s guest is a philosopher whose forthcoming book defends what she calls ethical forgetting: the claim that forgetting is sometimes not a failure of memory but a duty, particularly where public remembrance hardens grievance and forecloses repair. She distinguishes between memory owed to victims and memory conscripted for collective identity, and she argues that the latter often masquerades as the former. Draft eight to ten interview questions that force her to specify the position rather than restate it. Include at least one question on what a duty to remember is actually a duty to do—which events, in what form, for whose benefit, and for how long—one on whether forgetting can be owed to anyone, and one on how she answers the charge that forgetting wrongs the dead. For each of those three questions, add the follow-up she is most likely to need, keyed to the answer she is likely to give. The questions should be usable by an interviewer who knows her work but cannot look anything up mid-interview.
