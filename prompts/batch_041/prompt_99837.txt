So my flatmate and I burned most of last night arguing about probability and I've come out of it less sure what I actually think than when we started. I'm hoping you can tell me whether my gut calls add up to one rule, and what that rule does to the cases I couldn't settle. Here's what I actually think, case by case.

A coin I've flipped ten times that's come up heads every time: I don't think the next flip is even odds anymore, I'd put it well under, because ten in a row makes me suspect the coin is bent. But a die that's rolled six sixes in a row: I still say the next roll is one in six, and a run like that doesn't make me doubt the die at all. My friend says I can't have it both ways and honestly I can't tell if he's right.

My neighbour, a man in his forties, asks his odds of dying this year. I'd look up the figure for men his age and go with that. If I then find out he smokes and runs marathons, I'd shift it. But if I find out his name starts with J, I wouldn't budge it a millimetre, even though I'm sure someone somewhere has worked out mortality stats for J-names.

There's a horse race tomorrow with eight runners and I know nothing about any of them. I still feel fine saying each has a one-in-eight chance, even though no two races are alike and this particular race only happens once.

A woman tests positive on a screening test. I say her chance of having the disease is roughly the base rate for women her age adjusted by how accurate the test is, and I don't try to assemble a class of women exactly like her.

Last one, and this is the one we really went round in circles on. Someone asks the chance that a person pulled at random from the whole country is left-handed. I'd use the national rate. But if that same person is instead pulled at random from a room I'm standing in, and I can just count the left-handers in the room, I'd use the room, even when the room is tiny and weird and nothing like the country.

What I want to know is whether there's a principled way to pick the group in each case, or whether I'm just doing whatever feels natural and dressing it up. If some of my calls contradict each other, tell me which ones have to go, and say what the surviving rule predicts for a case I haven't mentioned. Please give it to me as exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro and no wrap-up.
