I want the specification on paper before I read another defense of any position. What would an adequate theory of material constitution — of the relation between a thing and the matter that constitutes it — have to deliver? Take the phenomenon to be explained as the ordinary one in which a lump of clay and the statue it makes appear to be a single thing in a single place and yet behave like two. Adequacy here is adequacy for a metaphysics that keeps ordinary object-talk largely true and contradicts no physics; if you think it should be relativized differently, say so and adjust. Write the desiderata, and attach to each the case or consideration that forces it. I do not want a list of slogans.

The cases it must get right, each with a note on what it costs to get it wrong:

- The squash. The lump survives flattening and the statue does not, although at the moment before the squash the two are microphysically indistinguishable. The theory must say what could ground a modal difference between things that differ in no physical respect, and whether that ground may be social or conventional rather than physical.
- The counting. How many objects are on the pedestal, and whether the answer is sortal-relative or absolute. It must locate both our ordinary counting and the multiplication of objects that its own machinery licenses.
- The many. A cat, and the many candidates that differ from it by a hair or a cell; the same problem in artifactal dress, a statue and the many things that differ from it by a chip. It must say whether the theory can leave the matter indeterminate, and whether it can do so uniformly in the two versions.
- Reassembly and replacement. Parts dispersed and rejoined; matter replaced atom by atom with the form held fixed. The statue and the lump must come apart in the right places, and the theory must say what its verdicts require of its account of persistence.
- Creation and destruction. Which events bring each thing into and out of being, and whether the statue's creation and the lump's creation may be the same event.
- Truth and reference. Our claims about statues and their matter must come out true, or the theory must own an error theory and say why it is tolerable.

Distinctions it may not collapse, and results it may not contradict: object versus matter; being a something versus existing; identity versus constitution; the artifact and the natural object, which are to receive the same treatment unless the asymmetry is earned; microphysical supervenience; Leibniz's Law, which forces any two distinct coincidents to differ in some respect and forces the theory to say what grounds that difference; and parsimony, which forbids coincidents that do no work.

Two cases of my own, because I suspect they break the standard moves.

First: one quantity of bronze is at once a statue, whose identity tracks its shape, and a counterweight, whose identity tracks its mass. Reshaping it destroys the statue and leaves the counterweight numerically the same. Say what there was before and after, and what the verdict costs the theory elsewhere — in particular, whether it can still treat the bronze as one thing.

Second: a town's memorial. After vandalism the figure is recast from the same metal to the same design, and the original dedication plate is reattached. If the theory lets the memorial survive, it must say whether an artifact's identity can be grounded in an institutional fact rather than in matter or form, and must then explain why an ordinary recast statue is a new statue.

Finally: which of these requirements are non-negotiable and which are the preferences of a camp; whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable — my own suspicion is that the pressure lies in the combination of microphysical supervenience, the demand that every difference between distinct things be grounded, and the demand that ordinary counting survive — and, if so, which of the three is cheapest to abandon; and which single requirement excludes the most otherwise plausible theories. A closing paragraph on which structural options (identity, distinctness with constitution, eliminativism about artifacts, sortal-relative counting, four-dimensionalism) each requirement bears hardest on is welcome; a survey of who holds what is not.
