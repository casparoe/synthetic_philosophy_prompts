ok I've been chewing on the location-of-meaning material in GEB (the frame / outer message / inner message bit) and I think I've landed somewhere, but I want a consistency check before I try to write it up. Here's the package:

1) Meaning is isomorphism. A message means whatever it's structurally parallel to -- grooves to sound, score to music. It isn't in the ink and it isn't in the head, it's in the shared structure.
2) But meaning is always relative to a frame, i.e. the set of alternatives the receiver is contrasting the thing against. Same grooves, different frame, different outer message. The outer message is the one the frame carries, not the one the sender meant.
3) The outer message is objective. A record's grooves really do carry "this is a recording of this performance", and they carry it whether or not anyone ever plays it or even knows what a record is. That's not something the listener projects onto it.
4) Theres no meaning without a mind. Meaning is an interpretive achievement, not a physical property -- marks in a language nobody reads are just marks.
5) If a computer spits out random bits that happen to spell out a perfectly good English sentence about someone loving someone, that's a coincidence, not a message, and it doesn't mean anything.
