"Jaegwon Kim's exclusion argument is the most influential argument for dualism in contemporary philosophy of mind. It starts from the causal closure of the physical -- every physical event has a sufficient physical cause. Kim grants that if mental states were identical with brain states there would be no problem. But non-reductive physicalists insist that mental properties are distinct from physical properties even though they supervene on them. Take a mental event, like deciding to raise your arm. Its physical basis is a brain event, and because the mental supervenes on the physical, that brain event causes the decision. But the brain event already has a sufficient physical cause, in the earlier brain events leading up to it. Since an event can only have one cause, the mental event is either identical to the brain event, or it's doing no causal work at all. Kim concludes that mental properties are epiphenomenal, and that the only honest way out for a physicalist is to stop pretending mental causation exists. He once proposed that mental properties have 'supervenient causation', but he later abandoned that. These days the interventionists have basically refuted the whole thing by showing that mental properties can be difference-makers in the interventionist sense, and since causation just is difference-making, exclusion never even gets off the ground."
