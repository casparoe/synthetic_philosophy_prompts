I have read rationalist and decision-theory blogs long enough that the absent-minded driver is lodged in my head: one road, two intersections the driver cannot tell apart, a modest payoff for pulling off at the first, a better one at the second, nothing if he sails past both, and a single policy that has to govern whichever intersection he happens to be at. Write me a joke built on that puzzle.

It should earn its laughs from the structure rather than from forgetfulness as a bit. In particular, the punchline ought to turn on the feature that makes the case genuinely strange: that the driver's credence about which intersection he is at cannot be settled before he settles his own policy, since the policy is what determines whether he is at the first or the second, and the ex ante best plan involves randomizing precisely where one would want a rule. Someone who has worked through the puzzle should find it funny; someone who merely knows that absent-mindedness is the theme should not. Keep it to a setup and a punchline, or a short exchange, and let it stand without names, dates, or a particular numeric payoff, so that it survives being retold without footnotes.
