I retired a few years ago after a long career spent mostly on combustion kinetics and then, by a route I still find mildly embarrassing, on theoretical population dynamics; the same differential equations turn up in both fields under different names, and I have come to suspect the methodological questions are the same as well. Since retiring I have been working through the philosophy of science I never had time for, and I have been attending a reading group at the local university, where last week I heard myself contradicting myself twice in the space of a minute. So I should like an audit rather than encouragement. Here is the package I have assembled piecemeal and never tried to reconcile. (1) I accept Friedman's central contention in his essay on the methodology of positive economics from the early 1950s, namely that the realism of a theory's assumptions is irrelevant to appraising it and that what matters is how well it predicts, together with the corollary that a theory may be excellent even though its assumptions are known to be false. (2) I hold that a model is trustworthy outside the regime in which it was fitted only in so far as its assumptions capture something real about the system, so that the warrant for extrapolation comes from the assumptions and not from past predictive success. (3) I think a model that reproduces the phenomena for reasons we know to be wrong explains nothing, however well it predicts. (4) I think idealised assumptions — the frictionless plane, the well-stirred reactor, the point mass — are not false claims about the world at all but stipulations that define a model system, so that the charge of unrealism simply misfires against them. (5) I think the F-twist is persuasive in physics only because we usually possess an independent theory of the corrections, some perturbation expansion or separation of scales, which tells us when the idealisation is harmless; where no such theory exists it strikes me as licensing wishful modelling rather than describing sound practice. And (6) I am a realist about the entities my models posit while remaining an instrumentalist about the models themselves.

What I want to know is which of these genuinely pull against one another, which pairs only appear to, and what the cheapest way out of each real clash would be and what it would cost me. Please order the tensions by how much damage they do rather than presenting them as equally bad, say explicitly which commitment has to give in each case, and tell me at the end which single revision would clear up the most. I would far rather be told that something has to go than be reassured that the whole thing hangs together.
