Write a dialogue between two philosophers who disagree about whether hypocrisy defeats the standing to blame. One holds that a hypocrite loses the standing to blame, because blame is a practice that requires the blamer to be free of the very fault they condemn. The other holds that hypocrisy does not remove standing but only reduces the weight or force of the blame. They are discussing a concrete case: a person who was once guilty of a serious wrong, has since sincerely reformed, and now blames another person for committing the same wrong. The reformed wrongdoer's blame is sincere, but they are also motivated by a desire to demonstrate that they have changed. The first philosopher argues that the reformed wrongdoer lacks standing; the second argues that they retain standing but their blame should be discounted. The dialogue should contain genuine back-and-forth: each character should respond to the other's actual arguments rather than talking past them. Neither should be a strawman; both should be equally competent and should press their positions to uncomfortable implications. They should also consider how their accounts handle related cases, such as blaming oneself, blaming a group of which one is a member, and the distinction between standing to blame and standing to punish. The dialogue should not cite any literature or mention any philosophers by name.
