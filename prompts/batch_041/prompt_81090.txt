I've been reading about societal collapse for years now, mostly in the rationalist corner of the internet where it's the reference class everyone waves at, and the archaeology side of it has been stuck in the same fight for decades with nobody moving an inch. The lineup: the standard story says a bunch of societies collapsed — Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean, the Classic Maya, Easter Island — and the revisionist story says nothing of the kind happened: the Maya "transformed", Easter Island maybe never had a big pre-contact population crash at all, the Bronze Age thing was a reshuffle of trade networks that plenty of people lived through. Both sides know each other's arguments cold, and I can't find the crux anyone is actually arguing over.

My hypothesis: "collapse" is doing three jobs at once. Did the population fall? Did the organizational complexity fall — cities, kings, writing, long-distance trade? And did the same society persist, same language, gods, identity? A case where the people and the language survive but the cities and the kings and the script are gone: collapsed, or just a change of government? The three cases score differently on each question, and I suspect each side is quietly using different criteria without saying which. Second, smaller guess: "collapse" is a thick word, it already carries the verdict that this was a loss and that the before was better, which is why "transformation" reads to me like a dodge rather than a reframing.

So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Contested evidence about dating and population estimates, a definitional fight about the extension of a word, a fight about identity conditions for societies that nobody has stated out loud, or an evaluative fight dressed up as an empirical one? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a criterion both sides could apply to the record, a case that splits them, a distinction — or should I take it that "collapse" isnt a usable category and stop using it in my own thinking? I don't want a survey of the archaeology, I've read alot of it; I want the diagnosis.
