I'm preparing the framing session of a comparative-politics unit on corruption, and I want the specification before the class meets any of the candidate definitions: what a theory of what counts as corruption must be able to do. Not a survey of existing theories, and no citations — I want the requirements themselves.

Anchor the list in the cases it must classify correctly: a payment to a customs officer to under-declare a shipment; a large gift to a doctor who afterwards gives the giver priority; the running, reciprocal exchange of favors between patron and client, where no single transfer is a payment; a lobbying retainer lawful in one jurisdiction and criminal in another; an informal charge for a service that is officially free; and the same diversion of resources that one community reads as an obligation to kin and an auditor reads as theft. Then the distinctions it cannot collapse: act versus system; corrupt versus merely partial or inefficient; the participant's normative framework versus the external auditor's; duty owed to an office versus duty owed to a person; individual deviance versus a stable equilibrium in which non-participation is self-defeating. Then the findings it cannot contradict: that gifts create open-ended reciprocal obligations rather than closed transactions; and that one and the same state apparatus can be experienced by different groups as a shared civic institution or as a foreign extraction machine.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, mark it non-negotiable or a commitment of one camp (the good-governance tradition, or the anthropological relativist one), and say what it rules out. Flag any pair that cannot be jointly satisfied — my guess is that the demand that the theory be usable to indict a practice from outside, and the demand that it not simply impose one society's line between public and private, are in conflict — and name the single requirement that excludes the most candidates.

Format: a table with columns Requirement | Case or consideration behind it | Status (non-negotiable or camp preference) | Candidates it rules out; then one line for the incompatible pair and one line for the most-excluding requirement.
