Okay, so I'm a features writer — I do the long science-and-ideas pieces, the kind where I shadow a philanthropist or a policy person for a month and then try to say something true about it — and there's a thing I keep running into that I've never found a proper name for, and it's starting to bug me.

Here's the shape of it. I did a piece on a big anti-malaria charity. The case for it is clean: bed nets, X lives saved, cost per life saved, done. Then a development economist tells me, off the record, that by cutting child mortality you also change fertility and population and long-run growth, and the net effect of the whole thing over a century could be positive, negative, or basically nothing, and nobody can know which. And here's the part that got me: the charity people didn't disagree with him about the facts. They said, more or less, "right, but that part is unknowable, so we judge the part we can measure." And he said, "the part you can measure is a rounding error next to the part you can't." Both of them are being completely reasonable and they've just talked straight past each other, and I realized I've seen this exact standoff maybe five times now in totally unrelated areas — a city banning a pesticide, a company deciding whether to open a factory in a poor country, a foundation funding a literacy program.

And it's not just "predicting the future is hard," which is what everyone tells me when I ask. It's sharper than that. It's that for any action you take, if you follow the consequences out far enough — and there's no non-arbitrary place to stop — the net value goes dark, and worse, you can make it come out either way just by choosing where you draw the line. So the whole project of "judge the action by its consequences" either dissolves, or becomes an exercise in picking a stopping point and pretending it was principled.

Is there a name for this? I feel like there has to be, because it's such a specific and reccuring move. If there is, tell me what it is and — more to the point — what it's *not*: how is it different from ordinary unpredictability, or from chaos-theory butterfly stuff, or from the thing where a policy changes which people end up getting born (I think there's a "non-identity" label for that one?), or from plain moral luck? And if there's genuinely no name, then coin one, and define it tightly enough that I can actually tell it apart from its neighbours and use it in a sentence without feeling like I'm bluffing.

One ask: don't just hand me a label and a vibe. Three clever people have now told me "oh, that's just the butterfly effect," and I don't buy it, or at least they're not answering the thing that's actually nagging me. Also — tell me how sure you are about all of this, and be straight with me about the single point you're least confident in. I'd much rather have one flagged uncertainty than a confident wall.
