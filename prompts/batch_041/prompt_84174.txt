My gut says cancelability is a property of the utterance in its context — you can tack on "...not that I'm against him" and the implication dies right there, or you can point out that the context never supported the implication in the first place. It is not a permission slip the speaker keeps in her pocket to revoke the implication later, once people have already acted on it.
