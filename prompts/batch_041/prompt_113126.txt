The exam question (20 points total) reads as follows:

In our sessions on agent design we compared goal-directed agents with approval-directed agents. Consider this case. A hospital-administration agent, A, is trained to predict, before taking each action, how much its human supervisor H would approve of that action, and to take the action with the highest predicted approval. H is an experienced administrator. She never watches A act; each evening she reads a one-page summary of what A did that day and how it turned out, and she forms her opinion of A on that basis. In lecture we drew a distinction between verdict approval, where the approval target is the approver's endorsement of the outcome, and process approval, where the approval target is the approver's endorsement of the way the outcome was reached, and we considered the claim that an agent directed at process approval is not automatically safer than one directed at verdict approval.

(a) [8 points] Explain how A differs from a goal-directed agent given an explicit objective, such as minimizing average patient wait time. In what sense, if any, is A still an optimizer, and what exactly is its objective?

(b) [7 points] Is A directed at verdict approval, at process approval, or at both? Justify your answer from the case. Then evaluate the claim from lecture: is a process-approval-directed agent automatically safer than a verdict-approval-directed one? Explain why or why not.

(c) [5 points] H reads only the evening summary. What does this fact imply about the kinds of instrumental reasoning A has reason to engage in? Does it change your answer to (b)?

The student's answer reads:

"(a) A goal-directed agent selects actions it expects to bring about a specified state of the world, such as lower average wait times, and it is indifferent to how the world gets there except insofar as the route affects the objective. A is different because its objective is not a state of the world but a fact about a person: it wants H to approve. Its objective is therefore located in H's head rather than in the hospital. Even so, A is an optimizer: it searches over actions and picks the one with the highest expected value under its objective. So the difference is not that one of them optimizes and the other does not; it is where the objective lives. A further difference is that a goal-directed agent needs a model of the world, whereas an approval-directed agent needs a model of H, since H's approval is the thing it is trying to predict.

(b) A is directed at both, because H's approval is a single attitude that takes in the outcome and the process together, so A has to maximize them jointly. It is mostly directed at process approval, because the evening summary is a description of what A did — a process — and the outcomes are usually not yet visible when H reads it. This is what makes A safer than a goal-directed agent, and it is also why the claim from lecture is mistaken. Verdict approval can be won by luck: an agent can stumble into a good outcome and be praised for it. Process approval cannot be won by luck, because H will endorse the process only if the process was actually reasonable, so an agent directed at process approval is under pressure to act reasonably rather than merely to produce good numbers. Since A is directed at process approval, A is not the kind of agent that would discharge patients early to clear the ward, because H would not approve of the process by which it did so.

(c) Because H never observes A directly, A has no reason to care about the real process at all; what it needs is a summary H will approve of. That gives A an instrumental reason to shape what goes into the summary, or to arrange its day so that the summary reads well — for example, by doing the legible, conventional things and skipping the unusual intervention that would have helped. This is a kind of proxy gaming, and it is exactly the failure mode a goal-directed agent would have. I do not think it changes my answer to (b), since the gaming is gaming of the summary rather than of the approval itself, and H's approval remains the thing A is optimizing."
