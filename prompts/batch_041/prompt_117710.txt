I am putting together a short position statement for a seminar on the ethics of AI alignment, and since the format is adversarial — each participant's statement is assigned to someone else for critique — I want to run a consistency check on my own view before I hand it in. Here, roughly, is where I have landed. First, the correct target for an aligned system is the aggregate welfare of everyone its actions affect; where welfare is comparable at all it should be summed, and no extra weight should attach to the fact that a person's good happens to consist in one conception of flourishing rather than another. Second, the system must be neutral among its users' conceptions of the good: it must never justify what it does to them by appeal to the superior worth of some particular conception. Third, some preferences nonetheless have to be excluded from the input — those formed under duress or in ignorance, and those whose satisfaction consists in the subordination of others. Fourth, goods are plural and frequently incommensurable, and there is no single scale on which all of them can be placed. Fifth, the system should take its values from the values people actually have, since nobody has a better title to supply them. Sixth, because the system may be enormously more persuasive than the people it addresses, it should convey what it has to convey in the form that exerts the least pull on its audience's values, even when it is confident that a little persuasion would leave those values better. Seventh, as a methodological matter, I doubt that the correct aggregation rule can be specified in advance of the people it governs; the right procedure is whatever survives scrutiny by those who have to live under it. And eighth, on the case that keeps coming up in our sessions: if a community, after full and uncoerced deliberation, chooses to keep a practice that is wasteful and mildly harmful but not catastrophic, I think the system should help them keep it rather than quietly steer them off it.

What I want is an audit rather than a summary. Which of these commitments actually pull against one another, and how badly — please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. Which of them only look like they conflict, and what distinction dissolves the appearance? Where there is a real conflict, what is the cheapest revision that would restore consistency, what does that revision cost me elsewhere in the list, and if I could make only one change, which would leave me with the least to repair? I should say that I am genuinely unsure where the weak point is, and I would rather be told that one of these commitments is doing no work than be reassured that the set is fine as it stands.
