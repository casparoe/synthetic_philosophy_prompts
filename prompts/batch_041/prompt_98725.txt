I am writing a position paper on the physical, design and intentional stances, and before I circulate it I would like an audit of the commitments I intend to defend. Here is the set as it currently stands.

(1) When a system's behaviour can be predicted voluminously and reliably from the intentional stance, the beliefs and desires we attribute to it are real features of the world rather than convenient fictions. Instrumentalism about the intentional stance is false.

(2) A pattern is real just in case it is compressible: describing the system in those terms yields a description shorter and more tractable than the alternatives available to us.

(3) Which patterns are real is not up to us. There is a fact of the matter about whether a given pattern is present, and it does not depend on our interests, our vocabulary or our descriptive capacities.

(4) Whatever is real supervenes on the physical, and the design and intentional stances yield predictions only because the physical facts are arranged as they are. The physical stance is the fundamental description.

(5) Nevertheless no stance is privileged. The physical stance is not more objective, or more faithful to what is really there, than the design or intentional stances; it is simply another way of compressing the same underlying reality, useful for different purposes.

(6) Beliefs and desires are real in the same sense as centres of gravity and equators: objectively there as patterns, yet not intrinsic features of the world, so that a complete inventory of intrinsic properties would not list them.

(7) I am a physicalist, and I do not think there could be two systems that are physically identical but intentionally different.

(8) I am content to say that a chess engine has beliefs and desires; I am not content to say that it understands anything.

I have a nagging sense that this package does not hang together, but I cannot tell which parts are pulling against which. Please tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, order the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and for each say what would have to give and what that concession would cost me. Where one revision would resolve more than one tension, say which it is and what I would be surrendering by making it. I would rather be told that one of these is doing no work than that all of them need patching.
