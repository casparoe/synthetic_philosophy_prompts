My flatmate and I have been going at it for weeks about Twin Earth and I'm starting to think we're not even disagreeing about the same thing. She's a chemist, I'm a philosopher (well, a philosophy dropout), and we both read the same thought experiment: a guy on Earth and his duplicate on Twin Earth, both say water, but one's water is H2O and the other's is XYZ. She thinks the whole thing is a cheap trick: if the two substances are indistinguishable in every possible test — same taste, same boiling point, same everything, even same behaviour in every conceivable experiment — then there's no sense in which they're different substances at all, and so the twins mean exactly the same thing. I think that misses the point, because the difference is in the world, not in the head, and meaning just isn't a matter of what's in the head. But then she says, fine, but if the difference makes no difference to any possible use of the word, why call it a difference in meaning rather than a difference in the stuff? And I say, because meaning is about reference, and reference is fixed by the environment. And we go round and round. I've tried to mix it up by asking: what if the difference is in the past rather than the present? Like, both communities used to use water for XYZ and now both use it for H2O, but the individuals don't know that. Or what if the difference is in the community's deferential practices: one community has experts who can tell the difference, the other doesn't. She says those are just different cases, not the same debate. I say they're the same debate. So: has this debate actually stalled, or is it just us? Is it a substantive disagreement about what meaning is, a verbal one about the word meaning, or are we answering different questions — one about semantics, one about epistemology or chemistry? And is there a version of the thought experiment that would actually settle it, or is the whole thing a case where no experiment could, because the disagreement is about what would count as settling it? I want a diagnosis, not a survey of the literature. And if you think one of us is just confused, say which and why. I'm definately not a philosopher, so don't assume I know the standard moves, but don't dumb it down either — this arguement has got properly under my skin.
