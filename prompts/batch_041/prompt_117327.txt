About 18 students. No real money, points and maybe a candy bar is fine. I have a printer and a pile of index cards and nothing else. I don't want a lecture up front, and I don't want the name of the idea anywhere in the room until the last ten minutes.

I've spent alot of time messing with this symmetric three-option game. Both players pick A, B or C simultaneously; payoffs are in points and the table is symmetric, so you can read it from either side. If you pick A you get 12 against A, 8 against B, 1 against C. If you pick B you get 8 against A, 4 against B, 5 against C. If you pick C you get 1 against A, 5 against B, 2 against C. As far as I can work out: C isn't beaten by any single option (A only loses to C when the other player picks C; B only loses to C when the other player picks B), but a 50/50 coin flip between A and B pays 10, 6 and 3 against A, B and C, which beats C's 1, 5 and 2 every time. And once C is off the table the A/B game is trivial, A beats B in both cells. Please check that yourself, because I've gotten this wrong before.

So design the session around that.
