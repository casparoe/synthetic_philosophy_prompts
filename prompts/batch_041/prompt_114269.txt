A few years ago I read a short piece — an undergraduate essay, I believe, whose author I could not have named even then — that set out the disagreement between Mencius and Xunzi over human nature, and I have carried its gist around with me since. I now suspect it is subtly wrong in ways I cannot locate, and I would like your help in deciding how much of it to trust. The gist, as best I recall it, was this:

"Mencius and Xunzi agreed about what human nature is — the endowments with which we are born — and disagreed only about the moral quality of those endowments. Mencius held that we are born with four moral sprouts — compassion, shame, deference, and a sense of right and wrong — which, if left unimpeded, ripen into the four cardinal virtues. Moral education is therefore a matter of shielding the sprouts from harm and letting nature take its course, and the source of evil lies in our circumstances rather than in us. Xunzi held that human nature is bad: we are born selfish, envious, and appetitive, and whatever goodness we attain is the work of deliberate artifice (wei) — ritual, law, and sustained effort that reshape us from the outside in. Since ritual is a human invention with no warrant in nature, wei amounts to a kind of beneficent fiction, a noble lie the sage-kings told to render an unruly species tractable. Mencius is thus the ancestor of the view that morality is innate, Xunzi of the view that it is manufactured; and this is why Xunzi's best-known pupils, Han Fei and Li Si, became the architects of Legalism, while Mencius's line fed into the Confucian mainstream."

A friend of mine who has read a good deal of the primary texts says the summary is roughly right in its outlines but makes the disagreement far too tidy, and that a specialist would balk at two or three of its sentences in particular. I suspect my friend is right, but I cannot say where the trouble lies.

Please audit the summary for me, opening with a sentence or two stating your overall verdict. Distinguish what is accurate from what is a defensible simplification and from what is a genuine distortion a specialist would flag; where the summary misstates one of the two positions, say what that position actually is and why the difference matters. I would especially like two questions settled. First, is it true that the two men meant the same thing by "human nature" and differed only over whether it is good or bad? Second, does the closing clause — that the Legalist careers of Xunzi's pupils follow from his psychology — express anything the texts support, or is it a later genealogical slur?

My friend also offers a test of his own, on which I would like your judgment: he says that a small child who spontaneously comforts a crying sibling is evidence that Mencius can accommodate and that Xunzi cannot. Is that a fair way to mark the disagreement, or does it smuggle in an assumption about Xunzi that he would reject?

Please answer in no more than 500 words.
