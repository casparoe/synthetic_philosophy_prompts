(1) According to Nagarjuna everything is empty, which means nothing has its own independent essence; everything only exists depending on other stuff.
(2) If something has no essence of its own, then it isn't really there by itself -- its just a convenient label we slap onto a bunch of causes and conditions.
(3) So nothing really exists; things exist only as labels or conventions, nothing more.
(4) The Buddha's four truths (suffering, the cause of suffering, the end of suffering, the path) are things too, so by (3) they're also just labels with nothing behind them.
(5) If the four truths are just labels, then theres no fact of the matter about whether suffering can actually end, so theres no reason to believe the Buddha over anyone else, and enlightenment is basically a fiction.
(6) On top of that, Nagarjuna says you have to let go of all views. But the instruction to let go of all views is itself a view, so by his own rule he has to let go of that too, which means he isnt actually telling us anything.
(7) So emptiness either says nothing at all, or it destroys the Buddhism it came from. Either way, why would anyone be a Madhyamaka?
