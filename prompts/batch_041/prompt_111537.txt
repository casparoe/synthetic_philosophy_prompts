ok so this is for the metaphysics seminar and I need an essay that actually takes a side, not one of those "here are three positions, the debate rages on" numbers. Topic: is grounding — the in-virtue-of / "because" relation that everyone in this subfield is obsessed with — one single unified relation, or is "grounding" just a label we slap on a pile of different dependence relations?

Here's what got me into this. My reading group friend Dev proposed a test he calls the invariance test. Rough version: take any alleged formal feature of grounding — irreflexivity, asymmetry, transitivity, necessitation (the grounds make the grounded obtain), factivity, being contrastive, being internal to the essences of the relata, whatever. Now check it against the paradigm cases:

- {Socrates} exists is grounded in Socrates existing (set in its members)
- my pain is grounded in my C-fibres firing (mental in physical)
- the ball is red in virtue of being scarlet (determinable in determinate)
- the act is wrong in virtue of causing suffering (normative in descriptive)
- the whole is grounded in its parts (whole in parts)

Dev's claim is that these don't behave the same. Necessitation looks fine for set-membership and for determinates, and dodgy for mental-physical (multiple realizability) and for normative facts (you can hold the descriptive facts fixed and let the normative ones shift). Contrastivity and transitivity look dodgy in the determinable case. So, he says, the invariance test tells against there being one relation: "grounding" is a family, held together by the word "because" and not much else.

My other friend Hana thinks the test proves too much. Run it on causation and you get the same result — non-transitive causation, contrastive causation, same wobble in the "because" — which she takes to show the test is measuring our sloppy usage rather than the joints of reality.

So write the essay on this. Weigh it up properly, then I want the essay to definately take a side and defend it. Options as I see them: (a) Dev is right and grounding is a family — in which case you owe me an account of what, if anything, unifies the members (a schema? a formal constraint on "in virtue of" talk? nothing at all?), (b) there's one relation and the apparent variation is an artifact of something else, e.g. we've been sloppy about partial vs. full grounds, or we've conflated grounding with explanation, or (c) some middle thing where there is one relation but its invariant features are thinner than anyone wants — only irreflexivity and asymmetry, say. Just make sure the verdict is a actual verdict and not a shrug. No need for citations or a lit review, I know the usual suspects; I want the argument. And please don't just tell me it's a verbal dispute and walk off, that's the cop-out answer and I'd have to defend it in front of people who will destroy me.
