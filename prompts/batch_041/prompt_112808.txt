I'm the shop steward at our plant and we vote next Thursday on whether to strike over the new automated line. Ive spent months reading Marx on machinery and I'm pretty sure the people in our hall who want to block the machines are aiming at the wrong target, but I also can't stand up and repeat the company's line that automation is just weather and we should all be grateful for the retraining money. I have to give the closing speech before the vote, to about a hundred members: half of them have twenty-five years in and hear "the robots are coming" as simple fact, the other half are younger and mostly want to know whether the buyout is any good. If I say the word Marx out loud half the room switches off, so whatever I've taken from him has to be doing the work without ever being named. What the company really doesn't want discussed is our sister plant, which automated a year ago: the guys there say they're more tired, not less, more watching, faster line, and they had to train their own replacements as a condition of their severance. Same clause is in our draft, so the old hands spend their last six months teaching the system everything they know and the knowledge ends up in the machine and walks out the door with them. Give me about five minutes that tells the machine-breakers they're wrong about the machine without telling them they're wrong about the fight, that doesn't pretend the company's plan is inevitable, and that leaves people with something concrete to vote for. Marx's actual view should be the spine of it, machinery isn't the enemy in itself, under capital it tends to lengthen the day and intensify the work instead of freeing us from it, and the real prize is who controls the deployment and who gets the time it saves, but it all has to be smuggled in, no quotes, no jargon, no "as Marx said". And it can't be pure doom either, part of his point is that this could be the good version, shorter hours, less drudgery. One thing I keep circling and can't work out how to say without sounding paranoid: the new line doesn't only replace hands, it replaces decisions, it sets the pace and the order and when you stop. Write it as continuous prose, the way I'd write a speech out in a notebook, no headings, no bullets, no bold, no tables.
