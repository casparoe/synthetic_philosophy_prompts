ok so for my sociology class I have to write down what I actually think about generations and then poke holes in it myself, but id rather have someone else do the poking. here's roughly where ive landed:

1. Generations are real kinds. When I say Gen Z is different from Boomers, I mean that as a claim about the world that can be true or false, not just a marketing thing someone made up.
2. But the exact years are made up. Whoever decided Gen Z starts in 1997 and not 1996 just drew a line somewhere. Theres no fact of the matter about where the boundary is.
3. What actually makes a generation is the conditions you grew up under - same tech, same big events, same economy, and they all hit you at the same age.
4. Comparing generations is how you explain social change. When someone asks why everyone is so anxious now, the answer is about which generation grew up how.
5. Within a generation people are way more different from each other than the average Gen Z is from the average Boomer, so sentences like "Gen Z thinks X" are almost always bad sentences.
6. Im a materialist about this stuff: to explain why a group of people acts a certain way you point at their conditions - money, housing, work, tech - not at some shared vibe or attitude. The attitude is the thing being explained, not the explanation.
7. The label itself does something. Being told you're Gen Z and treated like one changes how you act, so the line, once someone draws it, kind of makes itself real.
8. And I think blaming boomers for the housing market is fair. If a cohort votes for the conditions everyone else has to live under, it can be on the hook for that.
