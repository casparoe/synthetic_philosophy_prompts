I'm a longtime reader of the rationalist blogosphere (LessWrong, SSC/ACX, the old OB days, all of it) and I've somehow ended up as the token deflationist in a metaontology reading group. Next month I'm giving a 45-min talk to a room I know pretty well, and I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a defense. My thesis is a local quantifier-variance view: for a restricted class of ontological disputes -- composition (when do some things make a further thing), persistence (when is an object the same across time), and material constitution (when is a statue identical to its clay) -- the disputants are often using 'object', 'thing', 'part', and 'same' in different but internally legitimate ways, so many of their assertions aren't in direct conflict. But I don't want the pejorative 'merely verbal' reading: I think the choice between schemes can be assessed by explanatory, practical, and normative considerations, and some disputes survive once the semantic differences are made explicit. Two distinctions I lean on: semantic indeterminacy (no fact about what our term means) vs semantic plasticity (the meaning can shift with context or stipulation) -- I claim most alleged cases are plasticity -- and the object-language/meta-language distinction, since I think the quantifier-variance claim is a semantic hypothesis about natural language, not a first-order ontological thesis. The audience: two philosophers of langauge who work on quantifier semantics and think 'exists' is univocal; a Ted Sider-style meta-ontological realist who thinks there's a privileged joint-carving description; a historian of analytic philosophy who knows Carnap's internal/external distinction inside out and will accuse me of turning it into bland relativism; and three grad students who are Amie Thomasson fans and will think I'm not deflationary enough. There's also one older metaphysician who thinks verbal disputes are rare and usually a sign of bad faith.

Prep sheet, please: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this room, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that person would put it (semanticist vocabulary for the semanticists, Carnapiana for the historian, etc.), with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. Include what I should concede up front, what I should hold, and the one question I'm hoping nobody asks. I'm especially worried about the semanticist's objection that if 'exists' varies, translation between schemes becomes indeterminate and my own statement of the view becomes self-undermining, and the realist's objection that my meta-language either is just another scheme (so the view isn't general) or is privileged (so it contradicts itself). If you think one of those is the real killer, say so. Also, the historian will probably quote Carnap at me -- don't worry about exact quotes, just tell me the shape of the objection and how to answer without getting bogged down in exegesis.
