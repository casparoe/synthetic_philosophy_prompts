My bio lecturer gave us this gloss on the function debate and I wrote it down nearly verbatim, but something about it smells. She said: 'There are two theories of what a function is. The etiological theory says a trait's function is whatever it was selected for -- the heart's function is to pump blood, because pumping blood is why hearts got selected. The causal-role theory says a function is any causal contribution a part makes to a bigger system, so on that view what the heart's function is just depends on which system you stick it in. The etiological theory is teological, so it smuggles purpose back into biology, which is why most biologists dont buy it; and since it defines function by selection history, it has to say a brand new trait can't have a function, which is obviously silly. Honestly, the two theories only really disagree about how far back you're allowed to look.'

How much of that is fair? Go sentence by sentence: what's accurate, what a defender of each view would object to, what's just wrong, and whether her 'most biologists don't buy it' conclusion survives the corrections. Two cases I keep getting stuck on: a trait that arose as a side effect and now happens to help, and a trait that was selected for ages ago and no longer does anything. Also, is any of this maybe partly a verbal dispute -- are the two theories even answering the same question? I'm inclined to think the etiological view is just obviously right because hearts are for pumping, so maybe I'm the one who's off. (Not after citations, just whether the gloss holds up.)
