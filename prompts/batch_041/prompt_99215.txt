Im a TA for an upper-level aesthetics course and the prof asked me to draft one of the final exam questions on the paradox of tragedy. I want it to be unanswerable by reciting the usual catharsis story or the line that unpleasant emotions get converted into pleasure, and I also don't want students to just name-drop some famous solution. Instead, I want them to confront a case where the sadness is real and doesn't get converted: a documentary about a real person's suffering that you keep watching even though it makes you feel awful, and you don't even enjoy it in the moment. The question should force them to say what a successful solution to the paradox would actually have to explain, and whether the paradox survives once you seperate liking the experience from endorsing it or finding it valuable. Please write the essay question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must contain. Five must-hit points, 20 marks total, and for each point a one-line note on how students typically get it wrong. Keep the whole response under 1100 words, and lead with the exam question before the model answer and marking scheme. No citations or reading lists -- this is a closed-book exam, so I don't want anything that depends on having read a specific article.
