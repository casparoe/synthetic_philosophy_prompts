A colleague of mine who studies animal behavior sorts behaviors into the mechanically rigid and the genuinely flexible, and her verdicts so far are these. The sphex wasp that re-inspects its burrow every time it drags prey back to the entrance, though it has just inspected it: rigid. A jumping spider that detours around a barrier to reach its prey: flexible. A rat that takes a shortcut through a maze it has only ever run the long way: flexible. A honeybee that alters its dance after the feeder is moved: flexible. She is now shown an ant that has run a fixed outbound route to food and, when it is picked up mid-route and set down a few inches to the side, runs out the remainder of its original route and stops where the food would have been had it not been moved. What will she say about this ant, and why? I care less about the verdict than about the rule she is running: set out the two or three criteria of rigidity that fit all of her earlier verdicts, say what each predicts about the ant, give your best guess with your reasons, and name a further case that would force the criteria apart. Her ultimate concern, which is why the ant interests her, is whether any creature's behavior is ever more than mechanical. Answer in American English.
