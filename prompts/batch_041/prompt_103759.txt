I run a small reading group on the metaphysics of social kinds and we have been going around in circles on the race-and-gender construction stuff for months. Heres what nags at me. The constructionists say race and gender are socially constructed; the realists say no, there are real kinds there (maybe biological, maybe mind-independent, maybe just robustly projectible) and that construction talk is either false or a boring claim that our concepts have a contingent history. And every session someone trots out the same moves — the looping effect, the point that "constructed" doesn't mean "arbitrary", the reminder that calling something constructed isn't calling it unreal — and nobody budges. It has the feel of a disagreement where each side is right about everything it actually asserts.

My hypothesis, and I'd like you to either back it or shoot it down, is that this is two fights wearing one coat. The first is a semantic/metaphysical question about what "socially constructed" even predicates of a kind: does it mean the kind depends on social practices for its existence, or that the features of its members are caused by social practices, or that the kind term's extension is fixed by social convention rather than by natural similarity? Those three come apart, and I suspect each camp is answering a different one and then calling the other side wrong. The second is a genuinely substantive fight about explanation — whether citing social causes is doing the same explanatory work as citing biological or intrinsic ones, or whether they answer different why-questions, and which of those why-questions is the one worth asking about race and gender.

So diagnose it for me. Is the dispute verbal, is it about which cases count as the shared data (i.e. do the two sides even agree on what a paradigm "real kind" looks like), is it a hidden empirical or formal question doing the work, or is it bedrock disagreement about what stands in need of explanation? And then be honest about what would actually count as progress: a distinction, a change of question, some formalism about kind-hood and projectibility, or nothing at all because the question as posed is ill-formed. Don't just survey positions at me — I want your diagnosis and the reasons for it, and a verdict on whether this thing can be moved.
