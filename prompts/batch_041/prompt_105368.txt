Ok so this has been bugging me and I finally want to ask. I keep noticing a pattern and I'm not sure if it has a name or if I'm just re-inventing something.

Here's the shape of it. There are a bunch of people (or labs, or countries, or companies) who each could do some one thing on their own. Doing that thing is totally fine — maybe even a good idea — as long as only one or a few of them do it. But if lots of them do it, it turns into a disaster. And here's the part I care about: each individual, thinking it through, figures 'well, if I don't do it, there's a decent chance someone else will, and then my doing it is either pointless or actively makes things worse — but I also can't stop them from doing it, and me doing it alone isn't what causes the problem.' So they go ahead. The punchline is that the more people who are capable of doing the thing, the more likely at least one of them does it — even in cases where, looking at the whole group, the best outcome is that nobody does it or exactly one does. Nobody is being reckless; each person is reasoning correctly on their own, and I definately can't find the mistake in any single person's reasoning.

Concrete versions I keep bumping into: some tool or capability that's harmless if one person wields it and a mess if a thousand do (publishing some exploit, a new fishing technology, a new pesticide); or a bunch of friends each independently deciding whether to bring up a delicate topic at a dinner, where one person doing it is fine and five doing it ruins the night.

Now the second half, which might be the same thing or the opposite of it. Same setup — lots of people each able to act — except this time the good outcome requires someone to act (organize the block, report the leak, be the one to speak up). And here the more people there are, the less likely anyone does anything, because each one assumes someone else has got it.

So my question: does the first pattern have a name? I feel like I've half-heard one and can't place it. And is the second pattern literally the same structure with the sign flipped, or is it a genuinely different thing? I don't just want a label — I want to know where the line is. How is the first one different from a tragedy of the commons, from free-riding, from plain diffusion of responsibility? And is there one underlying concept that covers both halves, or are these two separate named things that people keep lumping together because they sound alike? If there's no name for the first one, give me one, and tell me what it does and doesn't cover — what it would rule out, and where it stops being useful.
