I teach a upper-level seminar on free will and moral responsibility and I'm writing the take-home final. The last unit is Strawson on the reactive attitudes, and the problem is that my students can already recite the participant stance versus the objective attitude in their sleep — they've read the same three summary articles as everyone else. So I need a question that actually makes them use the framework on something they havent seen before. Can you write me the exam question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme?

Here's the case I want sitting at the center of it, and please build the question around this rather than around a textbook example. Call the guy Rae. Rae does a string of genuinely cruel things — not psychotic, not coerced, just nasty and self-serving — and then the neuroscience comes in and shows that his cruel dispositions are fixed and permanent: no punishment, therapy, or incentive will ever change him. The students have to decide what Strawson's account implies here. Does the framework tell us to move to the objective attitude and stop resenting Rae, because he's beyond the reach of the demand for goodwill? Or does the participant stance survive, because the relevant question for Strawson is whether he can participate in the moral community at all, and being unchangeable is a different thing from being unable to participate? I want them to have to feel that distinction and pick a side with reasons, not just define terms.

The question should also force them to deal with the descriptive-versus-normative issue: Strawson says something like we can't really give up the reactive attitudes, and I want students to say whether that's a claim about human psychology that leaves the normative question open, or whether it's doing real justificatory work. That's the crux for me.

For the marking scheme, give me five must-hit points, 20 marks total, with the points weighted, and a one-line note for each on how candidates typically blow it. I want the A-versus-B line to be specific — name the actual moves an A answer has to make that a B answer will skip. And do the whole thing in American English please.
