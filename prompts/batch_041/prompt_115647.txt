Okay, so I keep going back and forth on the Luddites and I want to settle it with Marx's account of machinery instead of vibes. The setup, as I understand it: Marx is not on the side of smashing machines in any simple sense. In Capital he works out that machinery under capital is what cripples the worker — it turns him into an appendage, appropriates his skill into a dead mechanism — and he makes the point that it takes time and experience before workers learn to distinguish the machine itself from its employment by capital and to aim their attacks at the latter instead. In the Grundrisse he pushes further and argues that as large industry develops, real wealth comes to depend less on direct labour time and more on the general state of science and technology, i.e. the productive forces are outgrowing the value-form. And there's the blunt line: "Hence the workers' struggle against machinery." In the Manifesto, the early workers who smash machines and burn factories get filed under a primitive stage, before they've learned to aim at the relations of production rather than at the instruments.

But then the actual Luddites — the Midland frame-breakers and the Yorkshire croppers, 1811-13, the troops, the York trials, the hangings and transportations — Marx seems to treat with something closer to sympathy, and I cant tell whether that's just sentiment or whether it's consistent with the theory.

So here's what I want: use Marx's own account of technology and automation as the lens, and run the Luddite episode through it in both directions. Map it properly first — who the players are, what the frames actually are in his terms (the "working machine," fixed capital, the material form of the real subsumption of labour?), what the theory says the croppers and stockingers were facing, and whether they read their own situation correctly or not. Then the part I actually care about: where does the lens break? Is the machine-versus-its-employment distinction one the Luddites couldn't have drawn — and does the theory need them to have drawn it in order to count them as anything more than a primitive stage? Does it matter that these were skilled artisans defending a trade position rather than factory hands being deskilled in place, and does that fit the automation story or sit awkwardly with it? And what does the case do to the theory in the other direction — does it confirm the machinery/automation analysis, or expose that Marx's optimism about the productive forces is doing more work than the analysis can cash? Skip the narrative history, I know the events; I want the mapping and then an honest account of where his categories distort the case rather than illuminate it.
