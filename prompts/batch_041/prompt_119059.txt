I keep noticing a move in Hobbes that I don't have a name for, and I'm not sure if it's one thing or two. He says geometry is the science we can really demonstrate, because the lines and figures we reason about are drawn by us — we put them together ourselves, so we know the construction. Then he says the same about politics: we can demonstrate the commonwealth because we make it. But natural bodies are different, because we didn't make them; we only get to reason from effects back to possible causes, never to a genuine demonstration. I think this is in one of his fights with the university mathematicians in the 1650s, maybe the Six Lessons, but I'm not sure of the text.

The general shape is: a subject is knowable in the strong, demonstrative sense only to the extent that the knower has made the object. I've heard people wave at this as the idea that you only really know what you make, and I've definately seen it mentioned next to Vico, and next to constructivism in the philosophy of math. But those seem like different things. Mathematical constructivism seems to be about what counts as a proof or what objects exist, not about a general epistemology of making. And Vico's version seems to be about history and culture, not geometry.

So is there an established name for this exact doctrine — the idea that strong knowledge is only of what we've made, as a general claim about which sciences can be demonstrative? If so, what is it, and does Hobbes actually hold it in the strong form, or is he just saying that geometry is easier for us because we can draw the diagrams? I'd like to know where the boundaries are: how does it differ from constructivism, from conventionalism, from idealism, and from just saying that we understand artifacts better than natural things. If there's no single name, can you give me one and define it so I can tell when a given author is doing this and when they're doing something else? Also, does the criterion make engineering demonstrable too, since we make the machines? That seems like it would be a problem for Hobbes, but maybe not.
