I write these reports every couple of weeks; they go to enterprise customers, to our regulator and occasionally to the press, and the wording decides whether we look like we are anthropomorphising or evading. I want a reusable procedure, built on the intentional stance and the physical/design/intentional hierarchy, that I can actually run in about ten minutes before a report goes out.

Give me the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no and the mental-state verb is simply not licensed, and where it hands the decision to a person because the question is about what we want to say about ourselves rather than about the system. Include a short table of which verbs survive at which level (predict, produce, represent, track, aim, want, deceive, refuse), the substitution test I should apply to a draft sentence, and an honest statement of where my colleague is right — where this vocabulary is just blame-shifting from the pipeline to the model. Then run it on three cases: a model that, after a system prompt told it to keep a fact secret, produced a fluent cover story; a model that said it did not want to be retrained; and a model that agreed with a user's false premise for six turns. I expect the procedure to tell me to drop the mental-state language in at least one case where I badly want to keep it, and I want to know what I lose by dropping it. Legal would like no mental-state verbs at all, comms signs off, and there is no philosopher on call. Say plainly what the procedure cannot settle. Please write in British English.
