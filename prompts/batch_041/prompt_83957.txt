So I've been lurking on rationalist blogs and forums for the best part of a decade, and the idea I keep coming back to is moral trade — the whole thing where you care more about X than I do, I care more about Y, so we each do a bit of the other's thing and we both come out ahead by our own lights. I'm fairly convinced the basic move is right, and quite convinced that most of the interesting questions about it haven't been properly asked yet.

So suppose a funder came to you with enough for one full-time researcher for three years (or a small team for one year, if you think that's the better unit) and the brief was just "open questions in moral trade — philosophy and theory, no empirical work, no particular cause area". What would you actually put them on, in what order, and why? Assume they can't search online, so no reading lists and no "see so-and-so's paper on such-and-such" — I want the reasoning, not pointers into the literature.

A few things I'd like you to treat as live candidates, though tell me if some are duds:

- the diachronic version: you trading with your future self, whose values you fully expect to drift. Is that even intelligible as trade, and if it is, what does it do to how we think about precommitment and preference change?
- the acausal version: does the case for trade survive when communication is impossible but the other party is running the same reasoning? Is moral trade a special case of something bigger here, or does the acausal move make the whole idea collapse?
- whether moral trade is itself a public good, and what the incentives look like when the benefit of a general norm of trading is diffuse but the cost of a bad trade falls on you
- the integrity objection: trading away something you take to be important in order to get more of what you take to be more important. Corruption, or is refusing to trade the self-indulgent move?
- trade under moral uncertainty, where neither party is sure what's right and the "currency" is therefore fuzzy
- the laundering worry: if you can buy moral offsets, does the market end up making things worse overall, or is that just an aesthetic objection dressed up as ethics?
- whether the possibility of moral trade is any evidence against moral realism, or whether that's a category error
- the political analogy with vote-trading and log-rolling, and whether the pathologies there — coalitions that leave everyone worse off, minorities quietly traded away — carry over

For each one: is it upstream of the others, is it tractable by someone with decent decision theory and philosophy rather than an economist or an ML person, is anyone actually on it, and what would move it up or down the list over the next couple of years? Also say what you'd drop, and why. I'd much rather have three questions definately defended than fifteen listed — this is a prioritisation, not a survey.

Write it in British English. Finish with a single line, on its own, of the form \answer{(1) ...; (2) ...; (3) ...} giving just your top three questions as short labels, in order.
