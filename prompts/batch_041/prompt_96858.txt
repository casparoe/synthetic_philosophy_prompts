But then someone else pointed out that consequentialists themselves admit we're pretty much clueless about the long-run consequences of anything, so you can't even know which road leads where. Now I dont know what the proverb is even claiming. Can you lay out the distinct precise things "the road to hell is paved with good intentions" could be asserting -- an empirical claim that good intentions tend to backfire, a moral claim that intentions don't count toward rightness at all, an epistemic claim that we simply can't see where our actions lead, some claim about blame or responsibility -- and give me a verdict on each: trivial, substantive, false? Then tell me which of those readings actually does the work of shutting me down, and which ones quietly collapse into the cluelessness point (since if we can't know the road leads to hell, the whole proverb seems to lose its teeth). Lay it out as a menu I can bring back to them, not one reading explained.
