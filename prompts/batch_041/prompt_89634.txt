I need to take a stand on whether the internal states of a trained neural network are representations in a literal sense — states with genuine content and truth conditions — or whether that way of speaking is at best a useful fiction. Before I commit to a side, I want the criteria fixed. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of representation for neural networks must satisfy: the cases it must adjudicate, the distinctions it must respect, and the constraints it must not violate.

The cases it must handle include at least the following. A network that has plainly learned to track a latent variable in its inputs, whose behavior is sensitive to that variable. A network none of whose individual units means anything on its own, but whose distributed activity linearly encodes a feature that no single unit carries. A network that holds information about a property in the sense that a simple linear classifier can read that property out, yet whose behavior is provably unaffected by it. A network that computes a function so symmetrically that no assignment of content to its internal states is better than any other. And a network whose candidate representation can be intervened on, where the intervention changes behavior in exactly the way the representational claim predicts.

The distinctions it must keep apart include: representing a property versus merely carrying information about it; the content a state carries versus the causal role that state plays; representational content versus the physical vehicle that carries it; and the attribution of a representation to the system versus the attribution of it to an interpreter. The constraints it must respect include: that a system may have far more features than it has dimensions, so that features must be encoded in superposition and recovered non-trivially; that a single unit may participate in many unrelated features at once; and that a genuine representation must be the sort of thing whose presence or absence makes a difference to what the system does.

For each requirement, state the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark it as non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular camp — for instance, whether the theory must be naturalistic, must make representation a matter of function rather than of intrinsic structure, or must remain neutral on whether representation resides in the system or in the eye of the interpreter. Then identify the single requirement that does the most to eliminate otherwise attractive theories, and name any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — above all, whether the demand that representations be causally efficacious can be reconciled with the demand that they be attributed to systems whose parts are, at bottom, nothing but weights and activations. Do not score existing views against the list; I want the standard itself, made as sharp as it will go.
