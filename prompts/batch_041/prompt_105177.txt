I am constructing the objective for a system that is supposed to advance a single person's welfare over the whole course of her life, and before I commit to a formalization of her utility I want the specification of what any formalization would have to deliver. Write the desiderata: the full list of demands that a theory of utility must meet in order to serve as the target of a welfare-optimizing system, each stated as a requirement together with the case or consideration that motivates it, and each marked as either non-negotiable or a commitment of a particular tradition. Flag every pair of requirements you believe cannot be satisfied jointly; that is the part I most want to see, and I would rather have an honest impossibility result than a tidy list that hides one.

The list must handle, at a minimum, the following cases, and I want each addressed explicitly rather than absorbed into generalities. (a) The person who binds her future self, paying now to remove options later; when the two temporal selves disagree, whose utility is the target, and what does the theory say the disagreement is about? (b) The person whose ranking of two options reverses when a third is added to the menu, with no new information; is the preference determinate, and if so relative to what? (c) The person who has a first-order desire she wishes she did not have and would pay to be rid of; does her utility track the desire, her higher-order attitude toward it, or her reflective endorsement? (d) The person whose preferences are altered by the very fact that a system is optimizing for them; can the target remain stable, and how does the theory avoid the charge that it has merely redefined utility as whatever the system ends up producing? (e) The person who chooses what is bad for her; is her utility what she chooses or what is good for her?

Then, briefly, take the three families most often proposed — hedonic theories, desire-satisfaction theories, and pure revealed-preference theories — and say which requirement each fails first, and which single requirement on your list does the most to eliminate candidates. Keep the weight on the requirements and on the impossibility claims; the scorecard is secondary.
