I help run a reading group that spun out of a LessWrong-adjacent meetup — 18 people, mixed backgrounds, one 75-minute slot, no budget and no real money.

I want to do moral uncertainty as an activity instead of a discussion, because last time I tried it everyone just announced their favorite theory and talked past each other. What I want them to feel is the second-order problem: that "we don't know which moral theory is right" doesn't actually tell you what to do until you answer a question nobody wants to answer — how bad it is to be wrong by the lights of a view you only half hold, and how that trades off against a view you also half hold that says something completely different — and then that the answer flips depending on which rule you use to combine the views, so the combining rule ends up doing more work than any of the views.

To keep this from depending on anything anyone's read, build it on this raw material:

- 5 "constituency" cards, each with a one-line view and a stakes number from 1 to 10, where the number says how much a unit of harm counts in that view's own currency (so a view with a 10 cares ten times as hard about being on the wrong side of its own ranking). One or two of the views should be ones I've made up so nobody can just recite them.
- A slate of 4 policies that hit different constituencies differently (say: an animal-agriculture rule, a border policy, something about future generations, and a wild-animal intervention).
- 4 combining rules, introduced one round at a time: plain average of the cards' scores; average weighted by the stakes numbers; maximin (never let the worst-off card be wrong); and a bargaining round where constituencies can trade concessions across policies and someone has to decide what counts as a fair trade.

I want the winning policy to change when the rule changes, in front of them, and I don't want any of this named out loud until the debrief.

**The setup** (~150 words): how I frame it at the start without giving the game away, materials, and what they should walk out having felt.

**The rules** (~400 words): cards, scoring, the rounds, where the twist lands, and what the facilitator says and doesn't say at each stage.

**What goes wrong the first time** (~150 words): the predictable failure modes and how I handle each in the moment.

**The debrief** (~250 words): map the moments in the game onto the machinery, and be honest about which feature of the real problem the game can't reproduce and that I have to supply myself.
