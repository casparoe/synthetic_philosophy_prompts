Background: I'm in a mech interp group. We train small transformers on synthetic tasks, hunt for anomalies (behaviours that don't match what we thought the model was doing), and then try to explain them, mostly with ablation and activation patching. For about eight months now weve been re-litigating the same question: when do two anomalies count as the same mechanism?

Camp one, the functionalists: same mechanism iff same computation over the same variables, up to irrelevant implementation detail. If you can re-implement it differently and get the same input-output profile, it's one mechanism; two circuits that both compute the same thing are the same mechanism. Camp two, the structuralists: same mechanism iff the same causal pathway — same components, same mediating steps, same number of them, at the same grain. If the wiring differs it's a different mechanism, even if the outputs match perfectly. Then there's the third position that keeps ending the argument by fiat: 'same mechanism' is relative to an explanatory interest, so there's no fact of the matter, and the question is only well-posed once you say what the explanation is for. Someone once suggested 'mechanism' is more like a cluster concept than a natural kind and that didn't help at all.

My hypothesis is that this is mostly a verbal dispute, and that we should just say 'same behaviour' vs 'same implementation' and move on. My collegue thinks it's a real disagreement about individuation criteria, not words. I honestly cant tell anymore.

One more thing I suspect is upstream of all of it: our anomalies are only anomalies relative to a baseline — a task description, a set of inputs we're treating as 'the same kind of case.' So maybe the fight about same mechanism is downstream of an unexamined fight about same behaviour.

What I want from you: a diagnosis, not a survey. Is this a verbal dispute, a genuine disagreement about individuation, two groups answering different questions under one heading, or a hidden empirical or formal question? What role does the relativity of the baseline play — does it collapse the whole thing into pragmatics, or can there still be a substantive disagreement once the baseline is fixed? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a formal criterion, an operational test (something like: same mechanism iff interventions transfer, i.e. you can swap one for the other and everything downstream lines up), a distinction, or a change of question?
