I'm in a decision theory reading group and we keep circling the acausal-blackmail cluster -- counterfactual mugging, XOR blackmail, and their cousins -- without ever deciding what's actually worth working on. Before we sink a semester into it I want a defended priority order. To make it concrete, here's a variant we made up that none of us has seen written up: a blackmailer tells you that if you don't wire him $100 today, he'll have arranged for a coin to have landed against you in a branch you never see, a branch where you'd have been paid $1000 for having the cooperating disposition. The twist is that a second agent gets the mirror-image message, and the blackmailer only follows through if you both decline -- so it's counterfactual mugging crossed with a coordination problem where neither of you can observe the other or agree in advance.

Given all that, which open questions in this area are upstream of the rest, which are genuinely tractable by argument alone (no lab, no data, just thinking), and which are overrated because their answer wouldn't change what any decision theorist actually does? Rank them and defend the order -- why one question is prior to another, what would move it up or down the list. I care way more about the reasoning than the list itself. And please flag if you think the whole cluster rests on a mistake (the predictor/blackmailer is impossible, the setup is incoherent, whatever), since that would reorder everything.
