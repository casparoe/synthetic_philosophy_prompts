I'm a grad student TA and my supervisor has me drafting the final for our upper-level seminar, "Mechanism, Mind, and Freedom" (cross-listed with cog sci). One whole section is on sphexishness — Hofstadter's word for the way the digger wasp Sphex seems locked into a fixed routine: she drags a paralyzed cricket to the burrow, drops it, ducks inside to inspect the nest, and if you nudge the cricket a few inches while she's in there, she just hauls it back to the mouth of the burrow and inspects the nest all over again, apparently forever. Dennett leans on this in the free-will stuff as the picture of mindless mechanism, the thing we're supposedly not. My problem is every single student writes the same essay: wasp = robot, human = not robot, therefore freedom. I want a question that forces them to either earn that move or give it up.

Here's the wrinkle I want baked in, because it's what makes the thing hard. I've been reading around and there's a philosopher (Keijzer, I think?) who went back to the old ethology and argues the anecdote is shakier than the textbook version — that under some conditions the wasp does eventually change her routine after enough repeats, and that re-inspecting a nest you just left might be the sensible thing to do rather than a dumb loop, since something *did* just disturb the site. So the "endless loop" may be partly a lab artifact and a story that got repeated because it's rhetorically handy. I want the question to make them sit with that, and with the deeper methodological point underneath it: even if the wasp never wised up, what would a loop actually show? Can you read rigidity of *mechanism* off a piece of looping *behavior* at all, or is that an inference from a single perturbation that a little latent flexibility would completely undercut? And if you can't make that inference for the wasp, does "sphexishness" even draw a line between us and her — especially since we do the same thing (checking the stove twice, reopening the fridge, re-reading the same paragraph)?

So: write me one essay question for the 90-minute final (they'll write maybe 600 words), aimed squarely at whether "sphexishness" can do the work it gets asked to do, namely mark off mechanical rigidity from genuine flexibility / freedom. Then a model answer of around 600 words, and a marking scheme of 20 marks split into 5 must-hit points, with a one-line note for each on how students typically botch it. You're the course's most exacting reader here, so hold the A answer to a high bar: it has to name the specific inference that fails and say what would have to be true for the wasp example to actually support the conclusion it's wheeled in for. Take a beat to brainstorm the crux before you draft anything — the last thing I want is a model answer that's just a Dennett summary with nicer prose.
