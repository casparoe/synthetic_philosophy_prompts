Hey, I'm a third-year phil student and I'm in a seminar on well-being and the structure of value. Our final paper is basically "pick a view about how goods and bads compare, and try to either break it or fix it." I've gotten interested in the negative utilitarian family, specifically the version where the suffering scale has a threshold built into it: below the threshold, pain and pleasure trade off against each other at some exchange rate like ordinary goods; above it, suffering is lexically worse than any amount of pleasure, so no pile of happy lives can ever justify it. I'll call it LTNU.

I've written a draft and I want comments, but real ones -- mostly on whether the argument actually goes through and wether I'm describing the view fairly. Not looking for "nice structure, consider tightening your prose." And please don't give me citations or a reading list, I can't get to the library this week and I'd rather just have the argument assessed on its own.

My thesis: LTNU gets the shape right and the mechanism wrong. It locates the specialness of catastrophic suffering in the magnitude of the experience -- how far down the scale a pain sits -- when the intuitions that actually push people toward lexicality are about something else, namely whether the good that's supposed to compensate is of the same kind as the bad. Nobody thinks that relief, or repair, or a meaning the sufferer themselves draws out of the ordeal, fails to compensate for it. What nobody accepts is that a bunch of unrelated pleasure elsewhere can. That's a difference in kind, not in amount.

So the fix I'm proposing: scrap the single threshold and go kind-relative. Suffering of a kind can be outweighed by goods of its own kind -- relief from that very suffering, restitution, something the person can make of it -- and never by goods of a different kind, no matter how much of them you pile up. A million people being slightly happier doesn't buy off one person's torture; that same person's pain being resolved plus a decent life afterwards does.

Three things I want you to hit hard:

1. Is my diagnosis fair? Does LTNU really have to plant a threshold somewhere on a single scale, and is that a bug rather than just the cost of the view? If someone says "the threshold is where suffering becomes intolerable," have I actually refuted them or just re-described them?

2. Is "kinds of goods" doing real work or is it a fudge? My worry is that it either collapses into full-blown incommensurability (which I don't want, I still think some trade-offs are fine) or it just is ordinary value pluralism with a new coat of paint. Tell me which.

3. Test cases. Take the objection that if suffering is lexically bad then an instant painless extinction of all sentient life would be an improvement -- does kind-relative lexicality help, hurt, or is it just as vulnerable? And take a case where the only way to prevent one above-threshold horror is to impose a small below-threshold pain on billions of people; what does my view say, and does it say anything sensible?

Also, is any of this interesting, or is it a distinction that already exists under some other name and I just don't know it? Be honest with me on that one. Please write it up in American English. Thanks!
