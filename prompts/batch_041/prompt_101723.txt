Before we begin reading on whether famines are natural disasters or political crimes, I want the criteria settled. Set out the desiderata that any adequate theory of the matter must satisfy: the cases it has to classify correctly (the Bengal famine of 1943, the Irish famine of the 1840s, and the Chinese famine that followed the Great Leap Forward, alongside at least one episode that looks almost purely natural); the distinctions it cannot collapse (between what caused the deaths and who is answerable for them; between famine and ordinary chronic hunger; between a government that willed starvation and one that merely failed to avert it); and the findings it must accommodate (above all that mass starvation can occur with no fall in the food available per head). Give the consideration behind each requirement, mark which are non-negotiable and which are the commitments of a particular camp, and identify any pair that cannot be jointly met -- I suspect there is at least one. Finally, say which single requirement does most to exclude the most popular accounts, and test the whole list against this case: a harvest destroyed by weather, followed by a relief effort that was sincerely meant and utterly incompetent, leaving a death toll larger than any in the region's recorded history. State what the theory must conclude about it, and whether that verdict is a strength or a cost. Please write in British English.
