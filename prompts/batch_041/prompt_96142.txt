My roommate and I have been going around in circles about repugnant markets, and I want to figure out what I actually believe rather than just trading slogans. Going by gut: selling a kidney for cash is not fine, and I'd say that even when the seller is a fully informed adult and the money would genuinely change their life — so it's not just about coercion or desperation. But scalping concert tickets is fine by me, so is a hardware store charging $40 for a generator after a flood, and so is paying some guy to hold my place in the DMV line while I grab coffee. Paying a stranger to vote the way I tell them is not fine, and that one I feel in my bones. Paying a woman to carry a baby for me — commercial surrogacy, everyones consenting, she's paid well — I'm honestly torn but I lean fine. Paying a student for good grades is not fine. Paying a kid a dollar a book to read over the summer is fine. Paying someone to confess to a crime I committed is not fine. My tentative story is that you can buy goods and services, but you can't buy the exercise of something that's supposed to be done for its own sake or as a duty — a vote, a grade, a verdict — and you can't buy pieces of a person, which is maybe why the kidney bugs me. But I can't tell whether that's one principle or three duct-taped together, and the surrogacy case seems to slide right through it.

So: is there a single principle, or a small set, that gets all of these, or close? For each clause, tell me which of my cases forces it — I want the case that motivates each piece, not just the abstract statement. Then apply whatever you land on to a case I haven't settled: paying someone who's broke to enroll in a risky first-in-human drug trial, where the fee is about a year's income for them. And be honest about which of my verdicts the principle can't save — I suspect at least one has to go, and I'd rather know which one and why.
