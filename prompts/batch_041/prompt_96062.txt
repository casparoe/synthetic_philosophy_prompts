The subject is pain asymbolia, and I need a short research proposal for it: three pages, for a small foundation that funds a year of half-time work at the boundary of philosophy of mind and clinical neurology. Draft it from the sketch below.

The clinical picture is this. Patients with certain lesions can localize a noxious stimulus, rate its intensity as high, and call it pain, yet report that it does not bother them; the aversive reaction is muted or absent, some fail to withdraw or protect the affected limb, and a few are reported to smile or laugh during stimulation. The evidence is a small number of case reports, which is itself part of what makes the philosophical question hard. The familiar use of these cases is to argue that the sensory and affective components of pain dissociate, so that feeling pain and minding it come apart. I think that conclusion is too quick, and that the cases in fact bear on a different question: what is the bearer of pain's badness? Three candidates are usually run together — the intrinsic phenomenal character of the experience, its functional role (the dispositions to avoid, protect, and learn), and the bodily damage or threat that the state signals — and asymbolia appears to pull them apart in a way that ordinary pain does not. I am also uneasy that the standard clinical definition builds unpleasantness into pain itself, which may make the question partly verbal unless it is framed carefully.

Please produce the proposal. State the research question so that it has a determinate answer, and explain why the existing positions in the welfare and philosophy-of-mind literature leave it open rather than settling it. Set out the method — what I would actually do, month by month, given that this is an armchair project rather than a clinical one — including how I would use the reported cases, why that method is the right one rather than an empirical or a purely taxonomic alternative, and whether I should construct new hypothetical cases that the three candidate views treat differently. Say what the deliverable is, what a successful outcome would look like, what would count as the project failing or collapsing into a merely verbal dispute, and what would make it too large for the year. Assume no bibliography: I will add citations myself, so refer to positions and arguments rather than to authors or titles. Keep the register suitable for a review panel that includes clinicians, and be honest about how much of the work is conceptual clarification rather than discovery.
