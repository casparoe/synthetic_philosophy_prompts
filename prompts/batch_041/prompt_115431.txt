I keep running into the long-running dispute between Dennett and Searle over original versus derived intentionality, and I want a diagnosis of the impasse rather than a survey of the two views. The case that starts it is Dennett's vending machine: a drink machine with a coin detector is shipped by accident to a country whose coins are indistinguishable to the detector but are a different currency, so the same internal state now latches onto a different denomination with nothing inside the machine having changed; Dennett takes this to show that the aboutness of a state is fixed by environment and history, and that nothing separates us from the machine in kind. Searle's reply is that this establishes only as-if intentionality, since the machine's states are not intrinsically about anything, whereas genuine aboutness is a biological feature of brains bound up with consciousness. Once the behavioural and historical facts are agreed on, the two sides seem to restate their verdicts in different vocabularies, and I cannot tell whether they disagree about a fact, about what a representation is, or about what an explanation of representation is supposed to deliver. I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and the practical stake for me is that I have to decide how to describe systems I build that respond reliably to features of their environment and get repurposed when the environment changes. Give me a definite diagnosis, with reasons: what kind of disagreement is this, are the two sides even answering one question, and what — a distinction, a case, a formal result, or nothing at all — would move it? I suspect the whole thing turns on competing demands on explanation rather than on any fact about machines or brains, and I would rather be told I am wrong than have that guess politely repeated back to me; commit to a verdict, and say which of the two positions your diagnosis leaves standing.
