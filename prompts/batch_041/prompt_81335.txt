For the final exam in my upper-level philosophy of biology course I need a single essay question on the debate over biological function, together with a model answer and a marking scheme. The exam is closed-book and lasts sixty minutes, and the question has to be answerable from the case alone — no citations, no reading lists, no dependence on what any particular author said — so please build it around the following invented case rather than the standard heart-and-lung examples.

Case. Strain KT-7 is a marine bacterium carrying a gene cluster that builds a mineral shell around the cell. The cluster arose by mutation roughly forty million years ago and spread through the species after a founder event; for most of that period the shell made no measurable difference to survival or reproduction, and the genetic evidence indicates that it spread by drift rather than by selection. A year ago a researcher moved KT-7 into a high-UV, high-salinity tank. There the shell, entirely unmodified, raises survival by about thirty per cent, and the lineages now in the tank descend from cells that survived because they had shells; the researcher has also been serially passaging the population, so that over the last twenty bacterial generations cells with defective shells have been progressively weeded out. A second lineage in the tank, KT-7m, carries a mutation that makes the shell form only partially; KT-7m cells die at high salinity, while conspecifics with intact shells survive.

The question should force students to do three things: say what each of the two main accounts of function — the one that ties a trait's function to its history of selection and the one that ties it to the trait's current contribution to a capacity of the containing system — implies about the shell before the transfer, after the transfer, and after twenty generations of serial passage; say whether the partial shell in KT-7m can properly be called a malfunction and on what standard; and reach a verdict on which account the case supports, while answering the objection that the two accounts merely answer different questions and so cannot conflict.

Please write the question itself (a few self-contained sentences), a model answer of about 700 words that takes a definite line, and a marking scheme of five must-hit points worth five marks each, with a one-line note per point naming the error that typically costs students that mark, plus a short statement of what separates an A answer from a B answer. Characterise positions by their commitments rather than by the names of philosophers, and presuppose no literature.
