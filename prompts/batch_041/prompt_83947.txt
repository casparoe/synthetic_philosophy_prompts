I've been doing UX and interaction design for about ten years — agency work, so a lot of my job is arguing about mental models — and I keep bumping into a philosophical view I can't place. The view is that consciousness is a kind of user illusion, the way the desktop "is" a workspace when really its just files and pointers, or the way a folder "is" a place where documents live when there's no folder anywhere in the machine. On this view there's no inner glow, no felt quality the brain secretes; there's a system modelling itself in a way that makes the modelling invisible, and we mistake the transparency of the model for the presence of a thing being modelled. I think it's called illusionism.

Here's what I want from you. Take that position and restate it as faithfully as you can, entirely in the vocabulary I actually use at work — the real stuff, not the pop version: implementation model vs. represented model vs. the user's mental model, affordances, progressive disclosure, direct manipulation, transparency, leaky abstractions, the difference between a metaphor that's carrying weight and one that's just decoration. Then give me the residue report: what comes across cleanly, what only comes across if you fudge it, and what I simply have no way to say at all.

Three things I'd ask you to take seriously.

First, a house rule we actually work by, and I want to know if it does any work here: if the user can never in practice detect the difference between the implementation model and the metaphor, we ship the metaphor and stop worrying about it. Is illusionism the claim that consciousness is that kind of case? And if so, is "we can't detect a difference" a reason to think there isn't one, or just a reason to stop looking?

Second, we also distinguish a simplification from a lie. A simplification is a model where the user's predictions still come out right — they think the file is in the folder, and when they open it, there it is. A lie is a model where the predictions don't come out right and we're betting nobody will check. Where does phenomenal consciousness go on that scale? If it's a simplification — everything downstream of it works fine — that sounds like functionalism with extra steps. If it's a lie, I want to know who's being lied to.

Third, my vocabulary comes with assumptions I can't easily shed: that there's a user, that there's a mechanism under the metaphor that the user is wrong about, and that someone standing outside the system with the spec open can see both the metaphor and the mechanism and judge the fit. I suspect at least one of those is exactly what the view is trying to deny. Tell me which, and whether that means my translation is broken, or that the view is quietly borrowing the very thing it says doesn't exist. Dont be polite about it. If the honest answer is that "user illusion" is a bad fit for the theory and only looks apt because both sides use the word illusion, say so, and tell me which vocabulary would carry it better.
