I'm prepping for a seminar on pain asymbolia and I want a consistency check on my own view before I say it out loud. Here's roughly where I've landed: (1) Pain is a composite bodily state: a sensory component that gives location and intensity, and an affective component that I think is imperative in form—it presents the body part as to-be-protected, like a felt command. (2) The badness of pain is intrinsic to that affective component; it's bad because of how it feels, not because it signals tissue damage or because we want it gone. (3) Pain asymbolia is a real case of pain: patients can localize the pain and rate its intensity, but the affective component is missing, so it doesn't bother them and isn't bad for them. (4) I think the badness of pain gives the sufferer a reason to avoid it, and that reason doesn't depend on whether they desire relief. (5) But I'm also a desire-based reasons person: a fact gives you a reason only if it connects to something you care about. (6) I think asymbolic pain is still pain, just pain without badness. Do these hang together? Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, rank the genuine ones by severity, and for each say what would have to give. I'm not sure which one I'd drop, so don't assume. End with a single line \answer{...} naming the one commitment I should revise if I want the cheapest overall cleanup.
