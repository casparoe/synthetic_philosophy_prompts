Last night my friend and I got into it for like two hours about how a group is supposed to make up its mind, and she basically accused me of wanting a bunch of incompatible things at once, so now I want a real audit before I keep repeating this stuff in seminar. Here's roughly where I've landed, in no particular order. (1) I think a group can genuinely be right or wrong about a factual or political question — it isn't just "whatever we decided," there's an answer we're trying to track. (2) I think a group's verdict should be responsive to the reasons its members give: if most of us accept the premises, the group should end up accepting what follows from them, otherwise we're not really reasoning together, we're just tallying. (3) I'm a small-d democrat, so one person one vote, and each question decided by a straight majority. (4) I think the group's overall view has to be internally consistent — it can't end up believing P, believing that P implies Q, and still denying Q. And (5) I'm suspicious of any procedure that decides one question by peeking at the group's answers to other questions; each issue should stand or fall on its own.

She swears that single example proves my whole package is incoherent, and honestly I couldn't tell if she was right or if she was just pattern-matching. Rank the tensions by how bad they really are instead of treating them all as equally fatal, and for each one say what it would cost me to drop the weaker side.
