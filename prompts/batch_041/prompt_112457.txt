One of us (call her the friend) has an objection she calls the designer-machine objection, and she thinks it kills the project of extracting Occam's razor from algorithmic information theory. Here it is, as she puts it. The universal prior is defined relative to a choice of universal machine, and the invariance theorem only says that any two universal machines agree on complexity up to an additive constant. Fine. But that constant isn't small and isn't bounded in any useful way: for any string s you like, you can build a universal machine U that outputs s on a short input, so K_U(s) is tiny. Since U is exactly as legitimate a machine as any other, the "simplicity" of s is an artifact of a convention we chose, and the constant is precisely where the epistemic work was supposed to be happening. So SI doesn't show that simpler hypotheses are more likely to be true; it shows that hypotheses simpler relative to our machine are favored, which is a different claim and has no independent content.
