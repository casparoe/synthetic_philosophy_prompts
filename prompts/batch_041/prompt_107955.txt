I've got a pile of gut calls about fairness and I want to know which currency of equality they're actually tracking, if any. Same income, but one person was born with a condition that costs a fortune to manage: I think we owe them more, outright more resources, not just same money, tough luck. Same income, but one person has cultivated expensive tastes and is miserable without them: I don't think we owe them a penny more. My aunt who never had much and now genuinely doesn't want much: I don't think her low expectations mean we owe her less -- that feels like the deprivation doing the talking. A town where everyone gets richer but one guy gets vastly richer: not unjust, even though the gap widened. But a town where everyone's materially equal and one group is still treated as lesser, or one guy can quietly buy the election: that bugs me more than any of the money cases. And the one that breaks my brain -- a person with severe impairments who says they're thriving, versus a person with everything going for them who's miserable; my gut says neither is owed extra, which seems to clash with what I said about the first case.

My half-baked theory: what matters is the range of lives a person can actually choose to live, and we top people up when something they didnt choose shrinks that range, but not when they shrank it themselves. Does that get all my calls, or do I have to drop one? If it fails, is the fix a richer currency (welfare, resources, capabilities, some hybrid) or a different idea altogether -- I keep hearing the real point isnt equal shares but being able to stand as equals, and I want to know if that saves the aunt and the bought election or just relabels them. And tell me what your principle predicts for a case I haven't listed: two people with identical resources and identical capabilities, one cheerful by temperament and one gloomy, nothing external to blame.
