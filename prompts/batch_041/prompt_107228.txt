I'm putting together the first meeting of a grad seminar on moral responsibility, and I've realized I don't have a clean answer to the thing they'll ask in the first ten minutes: what is a theory of moral responsibility actually supposed to do? Not which theory is right -- I want the spec first. So give me the desiderata: what any adequate account of moral responsibility has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each requirement.

A few things I'm fairly sure belong on the list, so push back where I'm wrong. It has to make sense of the difference between the participant stance and the objective stance -- Strawson's distinction -- and in particular it has to say what the objective stance is *for* and why adopting it toward an ordinary adult isn't simply an error. It has to keep apart the two things we seem to do when we hold someone responsible: treating them as answerable (the "you owe me an explanation" part) and treating them as fitting targets of blame or punishment (the "you had it coming" part). I think alot of theories collapse these and then can't explain why we still demand an answer from someone we've stopped punishing, or the reverse. It has to handle the usual gallery -- the young child, the person with advanced dementia, the addict, someone acting under coercive manipulation -- and also a case that keeps coming up and that I don't think the list usually covers: someone whose character and values were deliberately engineered by another agent, so that the resulting person is morally ordinary in every respect except for how they got that way. I want the spec to say something about that case rather than treat it as exotic. It has to tell me whether responsibility can attach to a group or only to individuals -- I keep flip-flopping on whether corporate responsibility is the same kind of thing or a different one wearing the same word. And it has to be consistent with whatever the actual sciences of behavior end up saying, without just deferring to them.

Now tell me which of these are genuine requirements and which are just prejudices of one camp (compatibilists, Strawsonians -- I can't always tell which I'm importing). Flag any subset I can't have all at once; if two of the requirements pull against each other, that's the result I most want to see. And tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the popular theories. Be explicit about how confident you are in each claim, and tell me which one you're least sure about.
