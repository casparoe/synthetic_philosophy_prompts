I am a high school student trying to understand the Judy Benjamin problem in Bayesian epistemology. From what I gather, Judy Benjamin is lost and has a prior distribution over four possibilities: Red headquarters, Red second company, Blue headquarters, and Blue second company. She then receives the message that if she is in the Red area, then she is in the Second Company area. The problem is that conditionalizing on the material conditional version of this message gives one set of posterior probabilities, while using Jeffrey conditioning or minimizing relative information gives another. I do not understand why this is considered a deep problem for Bayesian conditionalization. Isn't the material conditional just the correct logical translation of the English conditional? If so, then the Judy Benjamin problem just shows that Jeffrey conditioning and infomin are wrong, not that Bayesian conditionalization is wrong. Could you explain why philosophers treat this as a serious challenge rather than a simple case where the material conditional is the right way to update?
