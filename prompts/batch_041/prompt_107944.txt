I'm in a third-year course on Marx's critique of political economy, and our seminar has split into two camps that keep talking past each other. The first holds that exploitation — the appropriation of surplus labour — is the fundamental wrong Marx identifies, and that alienation is at best a symptom of it and at worst a residue of the early, pre-scientific writings. The second holds that alienation names the primary injury, labour that is not one's own activity, and that exploitation is merely one of the mechanisms producing it, so that a fully just distribution of the surplus could leave the injury untouched.

Write the joint statement those two could actually put their names to after a term of good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want a numbered section of claims both accept, worded so that neither side is quietly conceding its position — including on what each side's central term is doing, on whether either needs a labour theory of value, and on what each thinks the other's term gets right. Then the residual disagreements, each phrased in the terms its own side would use, as a table. Then the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side. For each crux, name a case or argument both would agree bears on it, and record what each side predicts about it.

Two cases should be treated as bearing on the central crux, whether labour can be alienated without being exploited: a firm owned and run by its own workforce, with no external claimant on the surplus, in which the work is nonetheless fragmented and stultifying; and a workplace where the workers genuinely set their own pace and product but still describe the work as something they merely put up with. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, or to be two different questions sharing one word, say which part and why.

No citations, no reading list — I want the reasoning, not the literature. Write in British English, in no more than 900 words. Finally, say where the document is most likely to be wrong: which agreed claim is the weakest joint commitment, and what argument or case would show that the two sides cannot honestly sign the agreed section at all.
