I teach a graduate seminar on mechanism, agency, and the will, and the session I have never managed to bring off cleanly is the one on what Hofstadter named sphexishness: a creature executing a long, intricate, and seemingly purposive routine while remaining entirely deaf to changes that any genuine agent would register. The stock illustration is the digger wasp that provisions its burrow with a paralysed cricket, inspects the burrow before dragging the prey inside, and, if the cricket is nudged a few inches away during the inspection, retrieves it and then repeats the inspection from the top — again and again, without ever varying the sequence. (I know the empirical basis of that illustration has been disputed, and that the repeated inspection may even be adaptive; I have stopped caring, because the illustration was never doing the work. The concept was, and the concept does not need the wasp.) My students take the moral to be obvious — sphexishness is the mark of the machine, and to be free is simply to be not-sphexish — and I want to force them to defend the contrary. So I am asking you to make the strongest case for a position of mine, not to survey a literature and not to hedge.

The position is this. Consider what I call the sphex regress. Whenever we set out to rescue a system from a rigid routine, we do it by installing a further routine that monitors the first and intervenes when the situation warrants; but the monitor is itself a routine, and it too will be blind to some class of changes, so we install a monitor for the monitor, and so on without limit. At every level the flexibility is real but local: the system becomes sensitive to the counterfactuals we happened to care about, at the price of fresh insensitivities we did not think to care about. My claim is that this regress is not a defect to be engineered away but the necessary shape of any finite agent, and that the contrast between the sphexish wasp and the free human is therefore a difference in the distribution of insensitivity rather than a difference in kind. Argue for that claim. Say precisely what work the word "rigid" is doing once we grant that rigidity is always relative to a chosen set of counterfactuals and a chosen level of description; explain why the regress does not collapse into the trivial observation that every agent is limited; and tell me what, if anything, survives of the intuition that the wasp is unfree and we are not.
