I'm making a handout for a grad seminar on intentionality and I want a decision tree / taxonomy of the positions instead of the usual prose survey. The question it should sort on: when does a system have original intentionality, and when is its aboutness only derived or as-if?

Build it from the logical space first (the splitting questions), then drop the named positions into the leaves. Anchor it with these cases, all of which I'll put on the handout: the two-bitser (the coin-op machine whose whole job is sorting quarters from everything else, and which can be fooled) — Dennett's deflationary line that we can legitimately ascribe it a belief about quarters, Searle's that this is observer-relative and not real intentionality at all; a chess engine; a corporation that "decides" things in meetings; a slime mold navigating a maze; a thermostat; a current LLM; and one case I made up, a species that evolved a dedicated organ whose only job is to track whether something is edible, so its states are reliably caused by food and reliably drive eating, but there's no reason to think anything in there means or feels anything.

Branch nodes I want, phrased as questions a student can answer without already knowing where they land: does real aboutness require consciousness, or at least the capacity for experience? does it require the state to be capable of misrepresenting? does it require having been shaped by selection for that tracking job? does it require being a whole agent rather than a subsystem or a group? and is the original/derived distinction even coherent — or is all ascription observer-relative, in which case does that make intentional talk cheap-but-fine or cheap-and-empty?

Each leaf should carry the position name (or "unnamed" if nobody actually defends it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. I want the usual suspects in there — Searlean observer-relative / as-if, Dennett-style deflationary realism, instrumentalism about folk psychology, eliminativism, teleosemantics, and whatever else belongs — but flag any two leaves the tree can't actually pull apart, and for each empty combination say whether it's empty because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Roughly 6-8 nodes, 5-8 leaves, terse, its a handout.
