I am a Year 12 student at a school in England and I have been asked to give the opening speech for our charity week. The money we raise goes to a fund that buys antimalarial bed nets, so what it actually does is save anonymous lives at a published cost per life; yet the assembly always includes a short film about one named child whom the same charity treated, and there is a plaque in the hall that says a single life is priceless. I want the speech to take a real position on the question philosophers ask as 'should the numbers count?' — whether, if I could save one person or save five with no other difference between them, the five give me any more reason than the one — instead of dissolving into 'everyone can make a difference' filler. Please write the speech for an audience of students, parents and the head teacher, lasting about four minutes, and make definite choices rather than handing me options: commit to one answer to the numbers question, say plainly what the speech is claiming and why, and flag anywhere you have had to soften the argument to keep it honest for a mixed room. Write it in British English.
