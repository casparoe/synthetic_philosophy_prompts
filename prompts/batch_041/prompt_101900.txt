I recently had a long argument with a friend about evolutionary psychology, and I came away with a clearer sense of my own verdicts than of the principle behind them. I want you to help me find the principle, or the best small set of principles, that captures the pattern, and to tell me what it implies for a new hypothesis.

I treat a claim as scientific when it is a legitimate empirical hypothesis that could be confirmed or disconfirmed by evidence, and as a just-so story when it is not, or when its apparent support is too flexible to count.

My verdicts:

1. The claim that human beings possess a cheater-detection mechanism triggered by violations of social contracts, which can operate independently of general logical reasoning: scientific.

2. The claim that men and women evolved distinct modules for detecting sexual versus emotional infidelity, which explain sex differences in jealousy: just-so.

3. The claim that disgust evolved as a pathogen-avoidance emotion and produces specific avoidance and contamination behavior: scientific.

4. The claim that a general tendency to overestimate the prevalence of threats in ambiguous situations is an adaptation to ancestral danger: just-so.

5. The claim that the nausea and food aversions of early pregnancy are adaptations that protect the developing embryo from toxins: scientific.

6. The claim that rape is an evolved reproductive strategy: just-so.

7. The claim that childhood co-residence reduces later sexual attraction between siblings and that this is an adaptation against inbreeding: scientific.

8. The claim that a particular waist-to-hip ratio is a universal cue of female fertility and is preferred by men across cultures: just-so.

What principle, or small set of principles, best captures this pattern? Which of my verdicts would I have to abandon if I wanted a single criterion? I am less interested in a verdict on the next claim than in the rule I seem to be running, the other rules that fit my verdicts just as well, and which further case would best tell them apart. The next claim is this: human beings evolved a finite daily budget for strategic social interaction; the budget is replenished by solitude; and contemporary burnout is a mismatch between that budget and the demands of modern work. Is that scientific, just-so, or something in between, and what evidence or case would settle it?
