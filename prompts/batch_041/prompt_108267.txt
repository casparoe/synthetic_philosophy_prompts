Just so we're aligned on what I mean, since people use the phrase loosely: the frame problem in the narrow sense is McCarthy and Hayes's problem of how a system represents everything that does *not* change when an action is performed, without needing a separate non-change axiom for every fact and every action. The harder, philosophical version is the problem of relevance — how a system is supposed to know which handful of its beliefs bear on the question in front of it, and how it's supposed to give up a default (things persist, the info I've got is still good) exactly when it should and not a moment sooner. The Yale shooting problem is the classic embarrassment for the obvious fix — minimize the changes — where the turkey ends up alive because the minimizer is allowed to unload the gun instead. And there's the sibling qualification problem: listing all the conditions under which an action will actually do what you want.

Things I'd like you to actually address:

2. He pre-compiles his own frame axioms at design time and pays for it: he can't revise relevance on the fly, so whoever writes to the base controls him. I've got a half-formed thesis that this is the film's real contribution — that the frame problem bottoms out in a question of who holds authority over the relevance judgments, and that every solution has a social cost.
