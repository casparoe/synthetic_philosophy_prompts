Write a poem about a coin-flipping wager that I will specify here, since I do not want you to rely on remembering it. A fair coin is flipped until the first head appears; if that first head comes on flip n, the payoff is 2 to the n divided by n, with the sign alternating, positive when n is odd and negative when n is even, so that the n-th possible payoff has probability one over 2 to the n. The expected-value sum is therefore the alternating harmonic series. Summed in the order the flips naturally generate it, it converges to a small finite number; but its positive terms taken alone diverge and its negative terms taken alone diverge, so rearranging the terms can drive the sum to any value whatsoever. The wager thus has no order-independent expected value, and a decision theory that insists every prospect have one has nothing to say about it.

I want a poem of 20 to 30 lines, no fixed meter or rhyme scheme, in which the arrangement does the argument. Give me a recurring refrain that marks each new flip. Make the poem's affirmative lines, read on their own, feel unbounded, and its negative lines, read on their own, feel unbounded too, while the poem read straight through settles at a small, definite, almost disappointing sum. Then close with a brief coda that takes the same lines in a different order and lands somewhere else entirely, so that the reader feels the total is an artifact of the sequence rather than a property of the wager; the coda's lines should be recognizably the earlier lines, reordered, with no new material introduced.

Do not cite or gesture at any literature, and do not name any theorists. End with a short prose note separating what is settled about this wager from what is genuinely contested among people who work on it — for instance, whether an order-independent expectation is the right requirement, whether the natural order of the flips has any privileged claim, and whether the rational response is to refuse to play, to play for a modest fee, or to treat the question as ill-posed — and do not present any contested position as the consensus.
