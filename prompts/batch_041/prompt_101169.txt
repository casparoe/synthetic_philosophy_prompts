I hold the deflationary view that generational categories — Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z — do not pick out real kinds: the labels are conventional birth-year brackets imposed on a continuum, and the cohort effects they purport to name are either artifacts of where we draw the cutoffs or redescriptions of age and period effects. What would count against this? I want the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward: which cohort effects could survive the age-period-cohort identification problem, given that age, period, and cohort are linearly dependent and no cohort effect is identifiable without an extra constraint; whether the impressionable-years hypothesis can be turned into a genuine defeater rather than a heuristic; and whether any generational boundary tracks a shared causal shock rather than a labeling decision. Tell me what the view's defenders would themselves concede, what the deflationary view predicts that a view treating generations as real kinds does not, and give an honest verdict on whether it has been insulated into unfalsifiability — since any cohort difference can be relabeled an age or period effect, is there anything it actually forbids?
