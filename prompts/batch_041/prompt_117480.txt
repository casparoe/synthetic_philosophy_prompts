I am specifying what a theory of belief revision must deliver when the new information arrives as an indicative conditional rather than as a proposition learned outright — the setting of the Judy Benjamin problem, in which an agent receives a message relating two regions of her location and, on any reading, ought to come away with a shifted credence in the antecedent region, while conditionalizing on the material conditional and applying Jeffrey's rule are both supposed to fail to give the intuitive posterior. The theory is meant to work as a general-purpose update rule in a probabilistic reasoner, so I want the desiderata list before any candidate is defended. Give me the requirements, each with the case or consideration that motivates it: what the learning event must be taken to be, given that the input is not a proposition and cannot simply be conditioned on; which existing rules it must agree with where those rules are uncontroversial, namely conditionalization on a proposition learned for certain and Jeffrey's rule when the input fixes the probabilities over a partition; what the posterior must be in the Judy Benjamin case itself; and the structural invariances the update must respect — stability under refining the partition, independence from the order in which equivalent pieces of information arrive, and indifference to logically equivalent formulations of the message, since a rule that treats if A then B and if not-B then not-A differently has let syntax into the posterior.

Then the edge cases: what the theory says when the antecedent has prior probability zero and the conditional probability is undefined, and how it separates a message conveying a conditional probability from one conveying the probability of a conditional, since a theory that runs these together cannot say what was learned at all. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — I expect the pressure to fall on the demand that the Judy Benjamin posterior move together with one or more of the invariances — and say which single requirement does the most to narrow the field. Finish by saying briefly where conditionalizing on the material conditional, Jeffrey's rule, and the distance-minimizing rules proposed for this problem each fail the list; I care far more about the criteria than about the verdicts.
