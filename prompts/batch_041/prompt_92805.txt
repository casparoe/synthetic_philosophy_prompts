For the past year I have been a member of a political-economy reading group that has drifted, as such groups will, from the early chapters of Marx into the present arguments about automation and artificial intelligence. In our most recent session a member closed off a long and increasingly heated dispute about whether the coming wave of automation will inevitably deliver a particular social order — he favored something like a new feudalism of platform owners and rentiers — by citing the line from Marx that "The hand-mill gives you society with the feudal lord; the steam-mill society with the industrial capitalist." He treated the citation as decisive: on his reading, a technology simply hands you the society that goes with it, so the only real question is which technology we are getting. I was not persuaded, but I also could not articulate what he was actually claiming or whether the line, read with any care, supports him. What troubles me is the suspicion that the sentence will bear several quite different precise readings, that some of these are nearly platitudinous while others are much stronger and possibly false, and that my friend has quietly helped himself to the strongest reading because it performs the rhetorical work he wants.

I would like you to disambiguate that one sentence for me: set out the distinct precise theses it could be expressing, from the trivial to the radical, and give me your verdict on each. For each reading, say whether it is trivially true, substantive and defensible, merely contentious, or outright false; and, separately, say whether it actually licenses the inference my friend drew — that from the character of a technology we can read off the inevitable shape of the society it produces. I would also like you to address whether the sentence sits easily with Marx's fuller treatment of machinery, where he appears to insist that the machine in itself is not the culprit but only its capitalist employment, and whether that complication undermines the determinist use of the line or can be reconciled with it. Finally, tell me which reading the people who repeat the slogan in ordinary argument most plausibly mean, and what our exchange should really have turned on.
