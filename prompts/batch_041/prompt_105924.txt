Consider a robot that learns a person's preferences by watching her choose. Its inference engine embodies the standard inverse-reinforcement-learning assumption: the person has a single, stationary, real-valued reward function; each action is chosen with a probability that increases with its expected reward, governed by a fixed rationality parameter; and the robot recovers the reward function that best explains the observed choices, up to the positive-affine rescalings that this observation model cannot pin down (which include the confound between the reward's scale and the rationality parameter).

Now suppose the person is not a passive data source. She knows she is being observed, she holds beliefs about how the robot's inference works, and she will sometimes forgo an action she prefers because she predicts that taking it would lead the robot to infer a rule that would make it behave worse in situations she cares about more. She may also be uncertain about her own preferences until she sees which rule her demonstration induces, and may revise them in light of it.

I want your credence that, in this setting, the robot's inferred reward function converges on the person's actual preferences as observations accumulate — that there is a determinate target for the inference to reach and that the inference reaches it (matching her true preferences up to the unidentified positive-affine transformations, in the limit of unlimited observation). I don't want a survey of positions; I want a number with its derivation. Decompose it: how much rides on whether her preferences are representable by a single stationary scalar reward at all; how much on whether her demonstrative behavior is itself generated by those same preferences, so that the demonstration is a fixed point of her own prediction about the robot, rather than by a separate desire to teach; how much on whether the robot's model class contains the true generative process; how much on the possibility that her preferences are partly constituted by the demonstration and so have no pre-demonstration value for the inference to recover; and any other sub-questions you think belong. Give each a rough probability and one line of justification, then combine them. Finally, name the sub-question the total is most sensitive to, and the single observation or argument that would move it most.
