Please explain the integrity objection to utilitarianism — the complaint, associated with Bernard Williams, that an agent who first consults the impartial calculus before saving his wife has had one thought too many. Make clear why the objection is not merely a complaint about the psychological burden of calculating, but a claim about the kind of reason the theory says the agent has for acting, and explain why the reply that utilitarianism can be self-effacing does not obviously dispose of it. Then probe the objection's scope with a contrast case: an agent with no special relationship to any of the parties, who must simply allocate a scarce benefit among strangers. Does the objection bite there too, or does it depend essentially on the special obligations of intimacy? Separate what is settled in the literature from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus.
