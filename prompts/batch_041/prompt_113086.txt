The 40th anniversary dinner for the conflict-research institute is in two weeks and I've somehow ended up with the after-dinner speech. Half the room are the people who've spent their careers counting conflict deaths and can smell a bad denominator from across the room; the other half are donors and the founders' families who are expecting something warm. What I actually want to talk about is that "violence" isn't one quantity — it's a dial, and the direction of the trend depends on where you set it. Homicide vs. organized war; deaths per capita vs. raw body counts; bodies vs. years of life lost, since a war that kills mostly the old and a war that kills mostly the young aren't the same event even at identical counts; and whether you count the kinds of harm that never leave a corpse. And becuase a single bad decade can dominate a century's total, the average is hostage to the tail. I don't want "cheer up, it's getting better" and I don't want "it's all framing, so who knows" — I want a speech that makes the audience feel that the trend's sign really does move with the dial, and then still lands somewhere honest about whether there's a fact of the matter underneath all those choices. Five minutes, written to be spoken out loud, and don't name studies or researchers.
