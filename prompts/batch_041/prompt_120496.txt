Bit of an odd request, I know, but I've been going in circles on this and I could use an outside head.

I retired a couple of years ago after a long career in a physics lab, and since then I've been reading alot of philosophy on my own, mostly in the evenings. I keep getting snagged on things that I suspect aren't really the hard parts. Right now it's Buridan's ass — the donkey standing between two identical bales of hay, starving because it has no reason to prefer either. I know the story is old and probably started as a joke, but I think it's pointing at something and I can't tell what.

Here's what I've collected so far. First, deny the setup: no two real options are ever equal in every respect — one bale is a centimeter nearer, one smells slightly different — and preferring the better option is all rationality asks, so the ass only has to find the smallest asymmetry and take it. Second, say the option set is described wrong: the real alternatives aren't just the left bale and the right bale but also "pick one of them on purpose, right now," and that third option clearly beats starving, so the ass is never actually stuck. Third, flip a coin: if the options really are equal, any device that settles it is as good as any other, and nothing is lost by letting it decide. Fourth, think in policies rather than episodes: an agent who adopted the rule "when tied, go left" years ago never faces a tie at all, so the puzzle only bites if you insist on judging the single act. Fifth, say practical reason is simply silent when options are equal: there is no fact about what the ass ought to do, it starves because it keeps demanding a reason, and that's a defect in the ass rather than in the theory. Sixth — the one I like, and I'm sure this is just my old job showing — the ass is a physical system sitting at an unstable equilibrium, and a real system balanced perfectly doesn't stay balanced; it goes, in a direction nobody can predict from the setup, which is the sort of thing I watched happen on my own bench for thirty years. On that reading, asking which way the ass should go is like asking which way a pencil falls when it's balanced on its point, and the whole puzzle comes from treating the donkey as a calculating device rather than a body.

What I want is a tier list of those six, from the ones that actually solve the thing down to the ones that just push the bump under the carpet, with a sentence on each saying why it lands where it does. If two of them turn out to be the same move wearing different hats, say so. And tell me straight whether my symmetry one is doing any real work or whether I'm just pattern-matching to my old trade. Write it out as plain prose, like a letter to me — no headings, no numbered lists, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables. I read these on my phone on the bus.
