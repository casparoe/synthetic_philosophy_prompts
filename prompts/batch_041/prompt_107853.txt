I am a second-year PhD student in philosophy, and in a few weeks I have to open a decision theory seminar by stating my own position so that the others can take it apart. I would like an audit of it before I stand up, not a defence, and I should say at the outset that I do not know which of the following I would abandon if forced. (1) I am an expected utility maximizer, and I mean that as a claim about rationality rather than as a convenient representation: an act is rational just in case it maximizes expected utility relative to my credences and utilities, and no preference that cannot be so represented is one a rational agent may have. (2) My credences are answerable to my evidence, and where my evidence does not distinguish between two possibilities it cannot fix a difference between them; so in the familiar two-urn case, where one urn is known to contain fifty red and fifty black and the second contains a hundred balls each of which is red or black with nothing else known about the mixture, my credence that a red ball comes out of the second urn is one half, and so is my credence that a black one does. (3) In the case itself I prefer to bet on red from the first urn rather than from the second at even odds, I prefer the same way for black, and I would pay a small fee to exchange a second-urn ticket for a first-urn one. (4) I think that preference is defensible: the person who avoids the second urn is responding to a real difference between the two situations and is not merely making a mistake. (5) I am not, however, averse to variance or to unfamiliar odds as such. I would take a bet on red from the second urn at three to one, and I would rather stake ten pounds on a sixty per cent chance than on a coin flip at the same odds. And if I am offered a third urn whose contents are fixed by a fair coin I am not shown — heads yields sixty red and forty black, tails the reverse — I am indifferent between that third urn and the first, and I prefer both to the second. (6) I think my preferences should be dynamically consistent: if I decide now that I will bet on red from the second urn, and nothing relevant happens in the meantime, then I should still bet on it when the time comes; and the mere fact that an uncertainty is resolved for me in two stages rather than in one, when the payoffs are unchanged, should not by itself alter what I am willing to do.

What I want from you is a consistency audit of this package. Tell me which commitments are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, and be careful to distinguish the two, because I suspect some of the pull I feel is an illusion. For the genuine tensions, rank them by severity, identify which commitment is doing the forcing in each case, and say what the cheapest repair is and what it costs me — whether that cost falls on the commitments I must drop, on the cases I must then call irrational, or on the theoretical machinery I must adopt. I would also like to know which single revision would clear up the most for the least, and whether any of the six is doing no work at all and could be dropped for free. Where a tension is really a fork rather than a contradiction, say so explicitly and spell out the branches, since I would rather be told that my position is underdetermined than that it is incoherent when it is not.
