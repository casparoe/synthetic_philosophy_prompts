So the general reflection principle, the rule that says my own proofs are trustworthy, is off the table. From there he concludes that no agent can ever be justified in trusting its own reasoning, so a self-improving AI — one that has to reason about what its own future self will conclude and do — is mathematically impossible. Not hard, impossible. And all the research on safe self-modification is just people refusing to accept a proven result. I did two years of logic-adjacent CS, so I nodded along because each step sounded plausible, but the conclusion feels way too strong for a theorem about formal systems.
