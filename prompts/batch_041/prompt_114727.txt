I am a retired sensory physiologist who has lately taken up philosophy, and what I want from you is a number with its derivation, not a survey of the debate. Avicenna asks us to imagine a man created in an instant, fully formed, suspended in air with his sight shrouded and his limbs held apart so that nothing reaches him from outside, and he claims that such a man would still affirm his own existence; from this he concludes that the soul is immaterial and does not depend on the body. Give me your probability that the argument establishes that conclusion, decomposed into its parts, with a number and one line of justification for each: (i) whether the stipulated case is even coherent, given that a subject receiving no input of any kind, not even affect or proprioception, is nevertheless supposed to be having a thought; (ii) whether, if coherent, such a being would affirm anything rather than there being nothing it is like to be him; (iii) whether the step from self-awareness not requiring bodily input to the self not depending on the body survives, since those look like different claims to me; (iv) whether the argument quietly presupposes the very subject whose independence it is meant to prove; and (v) whether anything changes if the man is not created at a stroke but is instead an ordinary human who never received any input at all, which is the version my own training makes me want to test. Then give the combination, say which term the final figure is most sensitive to, and tell me what would move you: which argument or consideration would most change your number, and where your reasoning is likeliest to fail. Please answer in American English.
