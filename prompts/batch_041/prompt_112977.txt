Sort the main families of explanation for the side-effect effect — the finding that a foreseen side effect is judged intentional far more readily when it is bad than when it is good — into tiers, and give each a share of the total effect, summing to 100%. The families I have in mind are the moral badness of the outcome, the agent's own attitude toward it, the pragmatics of assigning and deflecting blame, the statistical abnormality of the outcome, and the violation of a norm of any kind (moral, conventional, or otherwise), but add, split, or merge families as the taxonomy requires. For each, state in a sentence what it explains that the others cannot and what result would shrink its share. Then say what each family predicts, and which family you expect to win, in three variants: (a) a foreseen bad side effect that the agent actively tried to prevent; (b) a foreseen side effect that violates only a conventional norm; (c) a side effect that foreseeably harms a few people and benefits many. Close with the single experiment that would most cheaply separate your top two families, and say what result would embarrass your own ranking.
