I write about science and ideas for a general-reader magazine, and I keep running into a phrase that's doing way more work in my interviews than anyone seems to notice: 'perception is theory-laden.' Last week a vision researcher used it to tell me eyewitnesses basically can't be trusted, because what they saw was already filtered through what they expected to see. A philosopher on a panel used the exact same phrase to say something much bigger -- that there's no such thing as observing a bare fact, all observation is interpretation. And a third person, a psych prof, used it just to mean that expectation and attention bias your responses on a lab task, which sounds almost trivial.

I want to write a sidebar that lays out the actual menu of things that one sentence could mean, from the boring to the wild, so readers can spot when someone's smuggling a big claim in under a familiar phrase. For each reading I need: what's actually being claimed -- and crucially, whether it's about the content of your perceptual experience, or about your judgement on top of it, or only about what button you press in an experiment -- plus your verdict on whether it's trivial, substantive, or just false, and whether that reading really gets you the conclusion the person was using it for (the eyewitness one above is the case I care about most).

Some things I specifically need pinned down, cause I keep getting tangled: does the claim require that the *content* of experience changes, or is it enough that your interpretation of the experience does? What's the real difference between 'seeing as' and 'seeing that'? When someone says a face 'looked angry' to them, is that a report about the experience or a hedged way of reporting a belief? And if a source insists the effect reaches all the way down to early vision, is that a different claim from saying it shapes what you notice or what you're looking for? One more: I've heard it argued that a photograph can't be neutral evidence either -- where does that land on the menu, if anywhere? I don't need citations or a reading list, my fact-checkers would kill me, just the claims themselves and which ones hold up.
