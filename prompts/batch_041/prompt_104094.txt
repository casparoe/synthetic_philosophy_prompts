I am a professor of philosophy whose secondary training is in causal inference: I teach the do-calculus and the potential-outcomes framework to graduate students, and I am fluent with structural causal models, interventions, d-separation, faithfulness, and the mediation literature. My metaphysics of mind, however, is rusty, and I would like to use the tools I actually command to get a grip on the causal exclusion argument. I am not asking for a reading list or citations; I am asking for a translation and an honest report on its limits.

First, restate the exclusion argument as faithfully as you can as a claim about a structural causal model. Treat the physical variables as fine-grained and the mental as a coarse-graining of them: for concreteness, let the mental variable M be a deterministic many-to-one function of the physical micro-variables P, and let behaviour B be determined by the physical variables. Render the argument's premises — the supervenience of the mental on the physical, the distinctness of the mental from the physical, the efficacy of the mental, and the denial of systematic overdetermination — as conditions on this model: as statements about functional dependence, about the parents of B, about what does or does not happen under an intervention, and about what the graph implies for the association between M and B conditional on P. State explicitly which premises are claims about the model and which are claims about the world the model is meant to describe.

Second, tell me what survives the translation and what does not. I am especially interested in three points. (a) Whether an interventionist reading of causation turns the argument into a dilemma — if M can be intervened on independently of P then exclusion fails, and if it cannot then M is idle — or whether that dichotomy is an artifact of reading the modularity of a structural causal model as a metaphysical thesis rather than a modelling convenience. (b) What multiple realizability becomes once the physical-to-mental map is many-to-one: whether a d-connection among distinct physical realizations is anywhere in the argument being mistaken for a causal relation. (c) Whether the idea that the mental is nothing over and above the physical has any counterpart in this vocabulary at all, or whether the nearest analogue — deterministic functional dependence — is being made to do work it cannot bear.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of the exclusion argument translate cleanly; which translate only under distortion, and what exactly the distortion is; and which have no expression in this vocabulary whatsoever. For each item in that last category, say whether the gap is a defect of the causal-inference vocabulary — something it ought to be able to say and cannot — or evidence that the original claim was less clear than it appeared. Keep the direction of translation honest: do not import the assumptions of structural causal models, such as modularity, faithfulness, and the identification of causation with difference-making under intervention, and then present the result as a neutral rendering of the argument. I want to know what the exclusion argument looks like from inside my vocabulary, and what my vocabulary cannot see.
