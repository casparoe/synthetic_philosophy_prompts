I am a third-year philosophy student and I help produce a small departmental podcast on topics in logic and language. Next week's guest is a philosopher who defends a view she calls partition-relative suppositionalism about indicative conditionals. Her central claim is that David Lewis's triviality results only show that no single, context-independent probability function can assign to an indicative conditional the value of the corresponding conditional probability. On her view, the probability of an indicative conditional is relative to a partition or a question under discussion; the same conditional can receive different probabilities relative to different partitions, and the triviality proof equivocates between them. She also wants to preserve a version of the equation that the probability of a conditional equals the conditional probability of its consequent given its antecedent, but only within a fixed partition. I need eight to ten interview questions that go beyond letting her restate the view. I want at least one question on how the partition-relative move is supposed to block the triviality proof without making the probability of a conditional merely subjective or arbitrary; at least one on whether the view can preserve the intuitive equivalence in ordinary cases where no partition is salient; at least one on how the view handles embeddings, especially conditionals under negation and conditionals in the antecedents of other conditionals; at least one on what happens when the antecedent has probability zero or when the partition is refined after the fact; and at least one on whether the view makes the probability of a conditional come apart from its assertability, and if so, how she explains the intuition that the two are linked. For each main question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and make the follow-ups press rather than merely invite elaboration. Avoid questions that could be asked of any philosopher of language. Please do not ask her for reading lists, citations, or a survey of the literature; the audience is listening and cannot look things up. Write the questions in American English and phrase them for spoken delivery.
