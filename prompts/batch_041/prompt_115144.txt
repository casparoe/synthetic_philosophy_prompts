Ok so I've got a weird request. My little reading group has spent the semester on metrics and incentives — Goodhart, Campbell's law, all the ways a number that was supposed to be tracking something stops tracking it the second people start steering by it — and the last session is next week and I want to close it with a poem instead of another summary email. Here's what I'm after.

A sestina about Goodhart's law. 39 lines, the usual six end-words, and the whole conceit is that the end-words are the metrics: the same six words come back in the fixed rotation, same letters every time, but by the last stanza they aren't measuring the same thing anymore. The form should be the failure. Someone reading stanza one and then stanza six should feel the drift without being told about it.

I want the first four stanzas to be the four flavours of the thing, in this order: one where you select on a noisy proxy so you're also selecting for the noise; one where optimizing carries you out into a region where the old correlation simply doesn't hold anymore; one where the act of measuring or regulating changes the system being measured; and one where somebody with different goals than you games it on purpose. Stanza five should be where those four stop being seperate — in real life they never show up one at a time — and stanza six should shift voice, maybe to whatever's being measured, or to the thing doing the optimizing. Then the envoi: three lines, all six words in it, and it should read like the dashboard line, the number at the top of the report, and it should land as either an epitaph or a threat. And it'd be nice if at least a couple of the end-words have an arc of their own — a word that starts out as a thing and ends up as a verdict.

Don't name any of the four in the poem and don't let a stanza explain itself. The end-words shouldn't be abstractions like truth or goodness; they should be the sort of word that turns up on a dashboard or in a contract or on a school report, and at least one should be a direction of movement rather than a thing. Nobody should need to know the taxonomy to like it, and someone who does know it shouldn't wince — so the four mechanisms have to be actually right, not just gestured at.

Formatting: just give me the poem. No title, no stanza numbers, no headings, no bold, no bullets, and definitely no table mapping stanza to mechanism. If you want to say something about a choice you made, put it in a couple of sentences of plain prose at the end, the way you'd write it in an email. Thats it.
