I am putting together a lecture for a final-year seminar on the moral standing of animals, and I want to use Robert Nozick as a case study in a methodological question: how far can a philosopher's settled commitments be projected onto problems that became live only after his death? Nozick died in 2002, and the four questions I intend to put to my students were scarcely on the agenda while he was writing — whether meat grown from a biopsy in a vat, with no sentient animal involved at any point, attracts the same objections as ordinary husbandry; whether we would be justified in releasing a gene drive that extinguishes a predator species in order to spare its prey; what to make of the industrial rearing of insects on a scale of trillions, given that we still do not know whether insects feel anything at all; and whether the suffering that wild animals inflict upon one another in nature gives us any reason to intervene.

Reconstruct the positions Nozick would most plausibly have taken on each of these. Work only from the resources of his own philosophy: the entitlement theory of justice, his insistence that individuals are ends and may not be treated merely as means, the experience machine, and the utility monster. Where his framework yields a determinate verdict, state it and show the derivation; where it falls silent or pulls in two directions at once, say so plainly and identify which commitment is doing the work on either side. I am particularly interested in the tension between his libertarianism about property, which would appear to leave an owner free to dispose of his animals as he pleases, and the anti-instrumentalisation principle he takes over from Kant, which would appear to forbid it. Note also that the Kantian principle is standardly grounded in rational agency, which would exclude most animals from its protection; consider whether Nozick could widen the ground of the principle without abandoning the rest of his position, and whether doing so is really available to him.

Close with an assessment of what he would have had to surrender in order to hold the resulting package together, whether that package is one he could have endorsed or merely one that bears his name, and which of your attributions you regard as least secure and what further consideration would settle the matter. Argue the case; do not supply a reading list or citations, as I shall add those myself. Please write in British English.
