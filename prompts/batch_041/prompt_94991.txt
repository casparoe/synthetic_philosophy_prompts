I'm trying to build a case that does for 'better than' what a sorites does for 'heap', except with all the usual escape routes nailed shut, and in a currency that isn't population ethics — I've read the one where you keep adding people living lives that are worse but still worth living about forty times now and I never want to see it again.

Here's the spec. Build me a chain of five options — call them 1 through 5, for my benefit — where each is better than the one before it, but where option 5 is dominated by option 1: option 1 is at least as good as option 5 in every single respect you mention, and strictly better in at least one. So the endpoint verdict is the boring unarguable one. Nobody is going to say 5 beats 1; that's not where I want the pressure. The pressure should fall entirely on the steps or on transitivity itself.

Constraints:

- Ordinary, realistic, choosable things. Apartments, job offers, guitars, cameras, holiday plans — stuff a normal adult actually picks between. No populations, no welfare levels, no gods or demons, no infinite series, no sci-fi.
- No numbers in the case itself. No percentages, no 'one more grain', no 'a bit brighter', nothing quantified. Comparisons are fine (darker, longer walk, noisier); measures are not. I want the pull of each step to come from the description, not from arithmetic.
- Every adjacent pair has to be a comparison a normal person shown only those two would make without hedging: 2 is better than 1, 3 better than 2, and so on. Not 'roughly as good', not 'arguably', not 'borderline'. If a step needs a footnote to feel like an improvement, it's the wrong step.
- Option 5 is still dominated by option 1 in the sense above. That's the whole trick, so don't fudge it, and don't tell me the case works if it only works once you've squinted.

Then walk me through it. Sections, in this order, with rough lengths:

1. 'The case' — the five options and the chain, one paragraph, 200 words or so.
2. 'Step check' — for each of the four adjacent pairs, a sentence or two on what a normal person would say and why nothing hinges on it. 150 words.
3. 'Why the ends are safe' — the dominance claim, spelled out respect by respect. 80 words.
4. 'What has to give' — the premises actually in play (each step is an improvement, better-than is transitive, the endpoints are as I say), which one you'd drop and why, 150 words. Say whether the vagueness reply — sure, each step looks like an improvement, but the relation is indeterminate somewhere — is even available to you given I've stipulated that no step needs a footnote.
5. 'The bit doing the work' — name the single feature of the case that makes it go, and tell me what happens if I take that feature out. 120 words.
6. 'Pushback' — the strongest thing a defender of transitivity says about this case, whether it lands, and whether what I've actually built is a counterexample to transitivity or only to something weaker, like 'better in some respect' being transitive, which nobody believes anyway. 150 words.

On confidence: be straight with me about whether this actually meets spec. If the constraints can't all hold at once, or if what I'm asking for is confused, say so at the top instead of quietly dropping a constraint to make it work. I'd rather be told the spec is broken then get a nice-looking case that cheats.
