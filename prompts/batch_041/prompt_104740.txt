A person could not have come from a different sperm and egg — so runs the necessity-of-origin thesis. Set against it a rival: what fixes which possible individual you are is your genome, so that any world whose history yields a person with your exact DNA contains you, regardless of which sperm and egg were involved. Compare the two theses. Do they ever diverge, and if so in which direction — in a world where a different sperm and egg would have produced the same genome, and in a world where the same sperm and egg would have produced a different genome? Do identical twins, chimerism, and epigenetic drift undermine the notion of a single determinate genome that the rival thesis needs, or are these distractions? Which thesis better explains the modal intuition that makes the necessity-of-origin thesis attractive in the first place, and is the rival ultimately a restatement of it rather than a genuine competitor?
