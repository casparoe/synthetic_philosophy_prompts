I keep bumping into money-pump arguments and i'd rather you compare the two main species of them than just tell me that pumping is bad. The first kind: someone prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A, so you start her off holding C, walk her to B, then to A, then back to C, skimming a small fee at each step — she's exactly where she began, minus the fees, and you can loop it until she's broke. The second kind goes after someone whose preferences are perfectly tidy at any given moment but drift over time, so you can buy a thing off her cheap while she's cold on it and sell it back once she's warmed to it. Are these the same argument in different clothes, or is the second one weaker — maybe it only shows she's exploitable rather than irrational? And does the usual fix for the second kind, settling on a policy in advance and refusing to budge from it, do anything at all for the first kind, or is resoluteness only on offer to the time-shifter? i'd also like to know what the diachronic pump needs in order to bite: does she have to be naive about her own future choices, or can it work on someone who sees the whole thing coming and still gets taken?
