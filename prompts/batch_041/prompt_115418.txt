I am a retired experimental physicist who has spent the past few years reading moral philosophy, and I have been struck by how long the dispute between impartialist moral theory and the integrity objection associated with Bernard Williams has run without either side shifting ground; my suspicion, as someone trained to look for two laboratories describing one phenomenon in incompatible vocabularies, is that part of it is verbal and the remainder is a small number of genuine cruxes. Please draft the joint statement that a committed act-utilitarian and a committed Williamsian could both sign after a serious adversarial collaboration. I want, first, the claims they agree on — about what the case of the man who saves his wife rather than a stranger does and does not establish, about what a moral theory is for, and about whether the complaint that a justifying thought of impartial duty would be one thought too many is a complaint about the content of the agent's deliberation or about the structure of the justification; second, the points on which they still differ, each phrased in wording that its own side would accept; third, the cruxes, together with the case or argument both agree would bear on each and the prediction each side would write down in advance; and finally, your own judgement as to which part of the dispute is merely a disagreement about words. Do not supply citations; I shall add those myself. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
