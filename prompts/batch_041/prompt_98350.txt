A colleague in the philosophy department, who I assume knows what he's talking about, sent me this one-paragraph summary of something called Fitch's paradox of knowability, and I'd like a second opinion before I go repeating it at dinner:

"Fitch's paradox is the proof that anti-realism refutes itself. Anti-realism says that truth is just what we could in principle come to know. Fitch showed that if that were right, then — since knowledge implies truth, and since anyone who knows a conjunction knows each half, and since knowledge is closed under logical consequence — every truth would already have to be known by somebody, down to the most boring facts that nobody has ever bothered to check. That's obviously absurd, so the anti-realist's knowability principle collapses. What the paradox really establishes is that some truths are in principle unknowable, which is exactly what the realist said all along. Fitch published it in the early sixties specifically to knock down Dummett's version of anti-realism."
