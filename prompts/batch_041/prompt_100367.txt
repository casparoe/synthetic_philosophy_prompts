Ok so I've been trying to sort out my own gut feelings about eating things that live in water, and I keep landing in wierd places. I'd like to know if there's one principle behind my reactions or if I'm just making it up as I go. Here are the verdicts.

Boiling a lobster alive in a pot: not ok, not close. If it's killed by chilling first, or by a proper stun, I feel basically fine about the killing part.

Raw oysters on the half shell: totally fine. Same for mussels and scallops. I have no pull at all toward thinking of those as somebody home.

Shrimp: the standard commercial kill (they get chilled or frozen while still alive) bugs me. Not as much as the lobster, but it bugs me. And the fact that it's billions of them a year bugs me more, not less — though I notice I'm not sure the number is doing real work here or just making me feel bad.

Octopus: worse than the lobster. Clearly worse. Whatever the octopus has going on in there, I don't want to be part of ending it.

A salmon killed by a good percussive stun on a farm: I basically think that's ok. The same salmon left to suffocate in the bottom of a boat: not ok, and honestly it bothers me more than the shrimp do, even though it's one fish against a whole trawler's worth of shrimp.

Crickets raised for protein and killed by freezing: I want to say fine, but I can't tell if that's because I really think there's nobody home or because I've already filed them under food and I'm rationalizing.

A crab in a pot: same as the lobster, no question.

So here's my problem. The principle that seems to fit most of this is something like "how much is there a someone in there" — a unified point of view that can be hurt. Oysters: no one home, so fine. Octopus: definately someone, so no. Lobster and crab: probably someone, so don't boil them. Fish: someone, so don't let one suffocate. That covers almost everything.

But it doesn't cover the shrimp-versus-salmon thing — why does one suffocating salmon bother me more than a trawler of shrimp, if shrimp are probably someone too and there are vastly more of them? And it doesn't tell me what to do with the cricket case, where my verdict might just be self-serving.

I also have a nagging feeling that "someone home" is doing two jobs at once: one is "can it suffer right now," and the other is "does it have a life it cares about continuing." Those might come apart. A shrimp might be able to suffer without having anything like a life it wants to keep; a fish might have the second thing even if I'm unsure about the first. If they do come apart, I don't know which one I've actually been tracking.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? If there is, state it precisely and tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed — say, a farmed eel killed by being packed in ice, or a wild-caught prawn. And if I have to give up one of my verdicts to get a clean principle, which one should go, and why that one rather than the others? I'd rather have a principle I can actually use standing at a fish counter than one that saves every last gut reaction.
