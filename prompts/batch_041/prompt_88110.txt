A friend and I got into it over whether these things are agents at all. He says the right picture is that the model is a simulator: it has no goals, it generates characters who have goals, and the assistant I talk to is just one of those characters. Something about that seems to relocate the agency rather than remove it, since whatever is doing the simulating is clearly sensitive to what I say. He also claims there's no fact of the matter about which character counts as the model, which I dont buy.

Write an essay that sorts this out and actually lands on a verdict — including the possibility that both pictures are wrong and the right answer is something else. Please keep three things seperate, since I think they get blurred: (1) having goals and beliefs that guide behavior, (2) being the kind of thing that answers to reasons and could be held responsible, (3) having some persisting self that carries across conversations. For each one, say whether the agent picture or the simulator picture handles it better and why. Use concrete cases to keep yourself honest — a model that stays in character as a manipulative advisor for a whole conversation, say, or one that insists it has no preferences and then visibly behaves as if it does. And dont tell me it just depends on how you define agent; tell me which reading you think is the right one to use, and what that means for how I should treat the thing when I get annoyed at it.
