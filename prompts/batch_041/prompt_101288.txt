I'm a retired limnologist — 35 years on lake and reservoir ecology, mostly nutrient cycling — and since I hung it up I've been reading alot of philosophy, ethics mostly, some political philosophy. Here's my problem, and I'd like your help with it.

I've been appointed to our regional water council, and this is a real body with real teeth: we set the extraction allocations for the aquifer that feeds about a dozen towns and a big chunk of irrigated farmland, and the aquifer has been dropping for a decade. At next month's meeting we have to lock in a management regime, and I have to circulate a short written recommendation beforehand. So the memo is for the council, and it needs a clear pick with a timeframe, not a think piece.

The three options on the table are:

1. A hard cap on total extraction, set by the council and enforced by the state.
2. Individual tradeable quotas — farmers and towns get an allotment they can buy and sell.
3. Devolve the whole thing to the existing basin association (the irrigators' co-op plus the towns' districts), let them write their own rules and police each other, and have the council step in only if they fail.

What I keep noticing is that everyone, me included, reaches for the word "tragedy" and the story about the herder who adds one more animal to the common pasture. I've read enough now to know that's contested, and I suspect the framing is doing work it shouldn't. So I don't want a literature survey — I can't read it all anyway — I want you to actually do the philosophy inside the memo:

- What exactly is supposed to be tragic here? Is this a common-pool resource, a public good, or neither? I keep conflating these and I have a hunch the difference changes what remedy even makes sense.
- Is this actually a prisoner's dilemma, or is it more like an assurance game where everyone would rather cooperate but needs to trust the others will too? Because those point at very different fixes, and I think the council is assuming the first without arguing for it.
- Does the case for option 1 or 2 secretly depend on a picture of people as rational maximizers that we ought to be skeptical of? And relatedly — is "free rider" the same thing as "tragedy," or am I using them interchangeably when they aren't?

There's also a piece I care about and the council probably won't raise: the towns downstream of us that draw from the same aquifer but have no seat on the council, and of course the people not born yet. Do they have standing in the argument, and does that change the recommendation? Don't skip this — it's half the reason I got interested in philosophy.

Two more things. Please treat the strongest case against whatever you recommend honestly — several council members are for option 3 on principle (they think any top-down rule is illegitimate no matter how well it works) and I need to be able to answer them, not steamroll them. And be careful to seperate what's actually settled from what's genuinely disputed among people who study commons and collective action, and don't hand me a contested view dressed up as the consensus. I've been burned by that already in other areas and I'd rather know where the real disagreement is.

Casual is fine, it's my own working memo — but it does have to be something I can hand to the council and defend line by line.
