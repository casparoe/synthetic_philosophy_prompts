Ok so I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for like fifteen years at this point, and the one conversation I never see actually finished is the fight *inside* the hedonistic-imperative crowd — everyone argues with the people who think suffering is fine or neccesary, and nobody argues about what the target even is.

So: write me a dialogue between two people who both think we should engineer the bad parts out of being a mind, but who disagree about what "the bad parts" are. Neither one is a strawman, and neither is secretly against the project.

Person A: suffering is one natural kind — aversive valence, the sting — and it's bad wherever it shows up, in a burn, in grief, in an anxious Tuesday. So the project is to take it out of every mind that has it, the way we took smallpox out of the population, and the "but then life would be meaningless" reply is just Stockholm syndrome talking. A thinks "keep the information, lose the sting" is obviously coherent: the signal that your hand is on a hot stove can stay, the awfulness can go.

Person B: thinks that's a picture of pain as a report with an extra gross feeling stapled to it, and that it's wrong — the awfulness isn't an ingredient sitting next to the signal, it just *is* the command ("stop, now"), so "keep the signal, drop the sting" is like "keep the meaning, drop the word." If B is right, the interesting version of the project isn't abolishing negative valence, it's making it consensual and useful: pain you can dial down or switch off, that tells you something true, that you'd endorse having had afterwards. B also suspects you can't have a mind that cares about anything at all with zero aversive states anywhere in it, which A thinks is just a failure of imagination.

Make them actually fight about this and not about whether the project is good. Have them run at least two concrete cases into the ground (say, someone crying after being dumped, and a rat in a lab), have each land at least one hit the other can't easily brush off, and have them notice at least once whether they've drifted from disagreeing about what to do into disagreeing about what to call it. Somewhere in there, one of them should have to narrow what they were claiming. Around 900 words, and please no citations or reading lists — I want the argument, not the literature.
