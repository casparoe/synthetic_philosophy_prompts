Last night a friend and I argued, well past the point of usefulness, about a thought experiment he calls the careenium, and I want a sober second pass at it. The setup is this: a vast flat arena is filled with a great many tiny magnetic balls that careen about, colliding, clumping, and dispersing. Step back, and the clumping turns out not to be random — recurring higher-order formations assemble, interact, and dissolve in patterns that track features of the world outside the arena, information about which reaches the balls through the arena's walls, whose bounciness varies from moment to moment. The scenario is built to raise the question of who shoves whom around inside the careenium: do the formations — which we are invited to read as symbols or concepts — push the balls around, or do only the balls ever push anything, with the formation-level story being a convenient gloss on the underlying physics? My friend is a hard-liner. For him, only the balls push; to speak of formations pushing formations is to adopt a stance we happen to find useful, and to take it literally is simply to be confused. I suspect he is wrong, but every attempt I made to say why either collapsed back into his view or appealed to a kind of downward causation I could not defend.

What I want from you is a tier list of the defensible answers to the careenium's question, ranked by how much genuine causal work they assign to the symbol level rather than to the substrate, with a sentence or two for each explaining where it lands and what it costs. Include at least the following, and add any real position I have left out:

- the eliminativist line, on which only the balls push and the formation level is a fiction or a gloss;
- the redescription line, on which formation-talk simply is ball-talk at a coarser grain, so the two descriptions are both true and do not compete;
- the autonomous-levels line, on which formations have causal powers of their own and genuinely push the balls, the loop closing on itself rather than bottoming out in the substrate;
- the stance-relative or instrumentalist line, on which there is no fact of the matter about who pushes whom independently of the explanatory project we have adopted;
- the interventionist line, on which a causal claim is settled by what happens under an intervention, so that symbols push exactly insofar as intervening on a symbol changes the outcome — with the awkward consequence that, since symbols are multiply realizable, an intervention on a symbol is also an intervention on its realizers;
- and the mechanistic line, on which the formation level is constitutively relevant to what the parts do without being a separate causal relatum, so that "pushing" is the wrong metaphor altogether.

For each, tell me plainly which of my friend's objections it survives and which it does not, and — this is the part I most want — which of these are genuinely distinct from his hard-line view and which are that view wearing a lab coat.
