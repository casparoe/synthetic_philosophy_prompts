I write a column on ideas for a UK paper and I'm putting together a piece on the ethics of voting. Two of the people I've been batting this around with are a philosopher who reckons casting a vote is basically a civic duty and an expressive act — you owe it to everyone who fought for the franchise, and the point is to register what you actually believe rather than game the outcome — and a hard-nosed consequentalist who thinks all that's a luxury and the only sane rule is to back whichever candidate does the least harm, conscience be damned. They've been sniping at each other for months and it's going nowhere. Can you draft the joint statement they could both actually put their names to? What can they agree on about what a vote is *for*, what a single vote actually does, and when abstaining or spoiling a ballot is defensible? Then the genuine disagreements, each one worded so that the person who holds it would sign off on the wording of their own side. And the cruxes — I suspect one is whether an individual vote ever makes a difference, and another is whether voting your conscience is self-defeating once enough people do it. For each crux, give a case or argument they'd both accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Number the agreed claims and put the disagreements in a table. Keep it tight — no lit reviews or citation-hunting, just the actual adverserial collaboration document.
