For my philosophy club's session on norms, I've been asked to write down my current position so the others can push back on it. Here is where I've landed:

1. A convention, in David Lewis's sense, is a regularity that solves a coordination problem: everyone conforms, everyone expects everyone else to conform, and everyone prefers to conform provided the others do, while some other regularity would have worked just as well.
2. Which regularity gets established depends on salience, and salience is often manufactured by whoever had power or simply got there first, so the origins of a convention are rarely neutral.
3. Promise-keeping is a convention in exactly this sense.
4. Because a convention's hold on us is just mutual expectation plus the wish to fit in, criticising a convention as unjust is like criticising the fact that we drive on one particular side of the road.
5. Norms evolve: conventions drift over time, and the drift is frequently nobody's intention.
6. Even so, conventions and norms are not the same thing. A norm can persist when almost everyone would rather break it if they could get away with it, because it is held in place by punishment and disapproval rather than by a shared interest in coordination.

Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how badly? Rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me, and identify the one change that would resolve the most. Please don't just tell me it all hangs together.
