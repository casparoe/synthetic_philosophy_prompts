I sit on a citizens' assembly that our council set up to recommend changes to how we run local elections, and I've been given the job of drafting the short written opinion we'll vote on at our last session on Thursday — so what I need is a memo I can put in front of twenty-two people, most of whom aren't political theorists but who will want to know exactly what they're agreeing to. The package on the table has three parts and the council wants a yes/no on each rather than a single bundled verdict: (1) compulsory voting at municipal elections, with a small fine, but with a 'none of the above' box and an explicit exemption for anyone who registers a conscientious objection to voting; (2) lowering the local voting age to sixteen; (3) letting non-citizens who have lived here for five years vote in local elections. My own instinct is that the three hang together — if voting is more like jury service than like speech, then making it mandatory and extending it downwards and outwards are all the same move — but the loudest objection in the room comes from a colleague who says that's exactly backwards: the moment you fine someone for not voting, a ballot stops being an expression and becomes a chore, and the honest response to that isn't to widen the franchise but to shrink the obligation. She'd rather we recommend nothing at all than a compulsory franchise she thinks is self-undermining, and she has enough support that I can't just wave her away. There's also a wrinkle I keep going back and forth on: our remit is technically only municipal, but everything we recommend will be read as a statement about national elections too, and I dont know whether it's more honest to own that in the memo or to stay strictly in our lane.

So: draft the opinion. It needs a clear recommendation on each of the three parts — including, if you think it's warranted, recommending that we sequence them, or that we only recommend some of them — plus the reasoning in a form my colleagues can actually argue with, and a serious engagement with the 'compulsion kills the point of voting' line rather than just asserting that rights and duties can coexist. I'd also like the memo to say plainly what would have to be true for the recommendation to be wrong, i.e. two or three concrete things the council could watch over the first term, because that's the bit that will actually move the sceptics. Keep it under two pages, in British English, and remember the audience: no jargon without a plain-language gloss, and nothing that presupposes they've read a word of democratic theory.
