Ok, background first: I spent 34 years as a materials guy, mostly fatigue and fracture in pressure vessels, and since I retired I've been reading a lot of philosophy and AI safety stuff, and there's one thing that keeps nagging at me. Everyone in the alignment world says we need worst-case guarantees rather than average-case or expected-value reasoning, and honestly my old colleagues nod along, because that's just how we did it: you don't design a boiler for the average load, you design for the worst credible load and then you slap a safety factor on top. Fine, sounds familiar.

But the more I think about it, the more I think the analogy is doing something sneaky, and I'd like you to make the argument I half see but can't quite finish. Here's where I keep getting stuck.

When we specified a worst credible load, two things were already given before any math happened: the load case was drawn from an enumerated set of failure scenarios (we had a century of cracked vessels telling us which ones), and the consequences were physically contained, so "worst case" meant worst case among a short list of known bad outcomes. Neither of those is handed to you in the AI case. If you don't fix the environment class and the failure-mode list first, "worst case" either goes vacuous (the adversary just deletes the universe, so everything scores the same) or it quietly turns into an average case over whatever distribution you smuggled in. And maximin can't even tell apart two systems with the same bound when one of them achieves it only by refusing to ever act.

So here's the position I'd like you to argue for, and argue hard: that "worst-case guarantee" talk in alignment is only meaningful once it's made conditional on an environment class plus an explicit enumeration of failure modes, and that the honest version of the engineering intuition is not a maximin objective at all but a constraint problem, hard bounds on a short enumerated list of catastrophes plus ordinary expected-cost minimization everywhere else. Then tell me what would have to be true for the straight maximin framing to be salvaged anyway, because I suspect it's a narrower set of conditions than people think, and I want to know if I'm wrong about that. No need to point me at papers, I don't have library access out here.
