Ok so I'm a novelist and I've got this idea I can't shake. In epistemic logic there's the problem of logical omniscience: model an agent's knowledge the normal way and you wind up with someone who knows every logical truth there is and knows every consequence of everything she knows, instantly, for free. Which is obviously not how any actual mind works, so the whole literature is basicly people trying to patch it — making the agent aware of only some propositions, or giving her a budget of inferences, or letting her reason in worlds where the logic itself is broken. Fine. But I want to write a story about a woman who really is like that.

Short story, 2500ish words. No lecture, and nobody in it ever says the phrase "logical omniscience" or explains the setup — I want it dramatized, not taught. What I'm chasing is the texture of it: she knows every consequence of what she knows and can't retrieve most of it, so she's the smartest and the most helpless person in every room, she wins arguments she can't follow and loses ones she was already right about. I want a scene where somebody demonstrates to her that she knew a thing all along, and she has to decide whether that counts as having known it. And please no ending where it destroys her mind or turns her into a calculator — the payoff should live in the gap between what's implied by what you know and what you can actually bring to mind, and in what it costs to be told you knew that already. Don't name any theories or thinkers in the text, I'll do that research myself, I just want the peice.
