I've been reading about Neurath and his idea that science should have a picture language -- a way of putting facts into pictures that works across languages and doesn't drag in the metaphysical baggage ordinary words do. I've sort of talked myself into it: I don't see why, with enough conventions, pictures couldn't handle negation, if-then, all/every, and numbers, and honestly it seems like it'd be less ambiguous than prose. So whats the strongest case against me? I want the specific stuff a picture language cant express no matter how many conventions you add, what would count as a genuine failure rather than just an undeveloped picture, and what a sympathetic Neurath fan would themselves concede is a problem. Be honest too about whether I've rigged this so nothing could count against it -- like every time someone points at a gap I just say the pictures arent good enough yet -- and if I have, whether thats fatal or just normal for a view like this. And tell me straight whether the whole thing quietly collapses into the boring claim that a picture language is just an ordinary language whose symbols happen to look like stuff.
