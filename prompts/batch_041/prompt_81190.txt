ok, a friend in my reading group handed me this and i've been circling it for a week.

(1) A theory is scientific only if it rules something out — it tells you which observations can't happen.
(2) Malthus's theory is that population grows geometrically and food supply arithmetically, so income per head gets pinned at bare subsistence in the long run, and any improvement gets swallowed by more people. That was more or less right about the world before him and stopped being right after.
(3) Since his time income per head has risen enormously, so the prediction failed.
(4) The usual reply: the theory is conditional. It says what happens holding technology, fertility and trade fixed; none of those were fixed; so it was never a prediction about us.
(5) But any failed prediction can be excused by saying some background condition moved.
(6) A theory whose predictions can always be excused that way never rules anything out.
(7) So Malthus's theory isnt scientific.
(8) Except the same excuse would sink the law of demand, the ideal gas law and natural selection, and i'm not giving those up.
(9) So something before (7) is wrong.

He says this shows falsificationism is a dead end. I think he's skipping a step but I can't say which. Which step do i give up, and what does giving it up cost? I'd like to keep (1) and (2) if i can — I think Malthus had a real mechanism, not just a slogan — and i'd like the theory to still be able to be wrong about something. So also tell me what, if anything, it actually rules out; if the answer is nothing, then (2) was never a theory to begin with. And dont let me off with it depends on the auxiliaries unless you name the auxiliaries and say why that isn't just (5) again.
