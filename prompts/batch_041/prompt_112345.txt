Everyone knows the naive conditional analysis of dispositions dies by counterexample. Two families of case do the killing: the fink, where the very trigger that's meant to set the disposition off instead destroys it, and the mask, where something stands in the way of the manifestation without touching the disposition itself. Fine. But I think we've been sloppy about what the two cases are actually meant to show. There's a difference between interference with the disposition and interference with the route from the disposition (or its causal basis) to the manifestation, and my hunch is that most of the going responses either quietly collapse that distinction or silently presuppose it. And I want to push on a case I haven't seen properly discussed: a disposition whose manifestation includes the removal of its own mask -- call it a self-unmasking disposition -- and ask whether anything in the standard accounts can handle it without either going circular or smuggling back in the very conditional they were trying to analyse. If it can't, that tells us something about what an account of dispositions has to look like; if it can, I want to see exactly what does the work.
