Ok so theres this thing I keep noticing and it bugs me. The hospital network near where I used to work pays departments partly on a "quality score" built out of three numbers: 30-day readmissions, how patients rate their stay on the survey, and how often staff tick every box on the discharge checklist. The administrators are totally upfront that none of these three is "the real goal" -- the real goal is that patients actually get better and stay better. Fine. But then when a department figures out that holding someone an extra day, or rewording the survey nudge, makes the score climb without anyone actually getting healthier, everyone treats it like a small ethical scandal, not like a measurement glitch. And the very same admins who say the score is just a proxy will also say your score is your job and you have to hit it.

What is this whole setup actually committed to, philosophically? It seems like it only hangs together if there's a real, determinate thing -- "actually getting better" -- that exists independently of the score, and the score is a bad instrument for tracking it. But is that presupposition really required? Could you run the identical institution while believing the score just IS the standard, that there's no fact of the matter about the patient's good over and above what we chose to measure? And if you did believe that, does "gaming the metric" even survive as a concept -- or is Goodhart's law quietly a realist thesis wearing a lab coat?

Theres a sharper version of the same puzzle I want you to fold in. Regulators sometimes greenlight a drug because it moves a surrogate marker -- a lab value or a scan measurement -- that correlates with the outcome people actually care about (living longer, not having the heart attack), on the promise of a confirmatory study later. Sometimes the surrogate pans out, sometimes the number improves and patients are no better or worse off. That practice seems to assume the marker-outcome link is a stable causal fact that survives us deliberately shoving the marker around -- which is exactly what a correlation you estimated from just watching people doesn't guarantee (the Lucas critique thing, roughly).

So: reconstruct the view of value and of causation this all presupposes, tell me which parts are strictly required versus just convenient, flag where its underdetermined, and say what would have to change if we dropped the realist bit. No reading list, just reason it out.
