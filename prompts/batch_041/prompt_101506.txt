I'm a third-year PhD student in political science, working in formal/positive political theory — spatial models of party competition, coalition and agency models of party organization, the endogenous-preference-formation and expressive-voting literatures — and I've spent the past year reading Simone Weil's wartime case against political parties with a theory reading group. What strikes me is how much of her indictment is, at bottom, a claim about mechanism: that a party is a device for generating, sustaining, and disciplining collective passion; that it fuses a scattered set of people into a body capable of acting, and in doing so degrades the members' capacity for independent judgment; and that this happens more or less regardless of the party's programme or its position on the left-right axis. I'd like you to take that indictment seriously and restate it, as faithfully as you can, entirely in the vocabulary I actually work in. Treat the party as an organization with a membership, a leadership, a platform, and a survival constraint, and use whatever machinery is apt — preferences and constraints, agency and selection, information aggregation, the externalities members impose on one another, the party as a team or coalition with a joint production function — while keeping the translation honest rather than charitable. I know the standard models well enough to be irritated by a hand-wavy rendering, so don't quietly rebuild her position out of assumptions she would have rejected and then call the result a translation.

Then give me the residue report, and treat it as the interesting half of the exercise. Which of her claims survive intact? Which have to be distorted to fit — for instance, does anything she says about the quality of attention, or about the corruption of judgment as opposed to the mere revision of belief, have a counterpart in a framework whose agents update beliefs from information and whose preferences are normally taken as given? Which claims have no expression at all: I have in mind especially the suggestion that a party is idolatrous, that it is totalitarian in its essence even when it is not totalitarian in form, and her apparent conviction that the remedy lies somewhere other than in a better party. For each item of residue, say whether you take the failure to be a defect of my vocabulary — something positive political theory simply cannot represent — or evidence that the claim was doing less work than it sounds like it is. I don't need citations or a reading list; I need the translation and the audit.
