Two of my freinds have been going at each other for a year over whether spacetime is a thing or just a way of talking about relations among things. One's a hardline substantivalist -- the metric field is a real physical object, so absolute rotation is a real feature of the world; the other's a relationist -- there's nothing to the geometry beyond the pattern of relations among material events. Write the document they should've produced: the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration they could both actually sign.

Ground rules: no reading lists, and don't lean on the famous historical episodes -- keep it self-contained. Treat it as a live dispute about one toy universe I'm stipulating. The universe is closed and contains exactly two counter-rotating concentric rings of dust and one bucket of water, nothing else, no outside frame. And suppose the field equations admit a whole family of solutions that differ only in which ring counts as non-rotating.

Start with a translation manual: each statement the substantivalist wants to make about that universe, paired with the relationist's rewording of it, and flag any sentence that has no counterpart on the other side -- those are your cruxes.

Then give me the numbered list of claims both can sign (about the formalism, about what actually needs explaining, about what the bucket experiment does and doesn't show); the residual disagreement written in wording each side would accept for its own position; and for each crux a concrete case both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If any part of their fight turns out to be about words, say which part and why. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and keep it short enough to read on a train.
