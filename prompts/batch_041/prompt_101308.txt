I build reward models and preference-aggregation pipelines for large language models, and my working vocabulary is fairly precise: individual preference profiles and pairwise comparisons; aggregation rules and the conditions we impose on them; the impossibility results showing that no rule satisfies all of them at once; cycles and intransitivity; the distinction between what a population actually prefers and what it would prefer under fuller information; the deliberate fiction of a single unitary preference that the whole pipeline is trying to estimate; and the standing worry that our proxy reward is not the thing we meant to represent — that the model games the proxy, or that the proxy drifts away from the target as the distribution shifts. I have no philosophical training beyond a general education, and I am fluent enough in the above to be dangerous.

Please take the debate about populism and the will of the people and restate it, as faithfully as you can, in that vocabulary.
