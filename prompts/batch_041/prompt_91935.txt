I spent most of last night arguing with a friend about the dogmatism paradox, and the argument went the way these arguments usually go. I held that if I know something, then any evidence I later meet that counts against it must be misleading, so I am entitled to dismiss it without further inquiry; my friend said this turns knowledge into a licence for closed-mindedness and that no notion of knowledge worth having could have that consequence. Neither of us could say whether the paradox is a genuine result about knowledge, an artefact of an implausible link between knowing and being entitled to ignore, or a confusion between the rationality of a belief and the rationality of a policy for gathering evidence. I want to turn it into a short research proposal — a term's work, a few thousand words, nothing empirical — and I would like you to write that proposal.

The line I want to develop, and which I have not seen put this way, is that the paradox trades on treating an agent's evidence as a flat body of propositions rather than as a history. On my hunch, what makes the later counter-evidence dismissible is not its content but the fact that the agent's knowledge also covers how that evidence was produced and how reliable she is as a producer of evidence; the paradox then becomes a clash between two ways of individuating an epistemic position, as information versus as track record. I suspect the first-person and third-person versions of the argument come apart along exactly that line, which would explain why the reasoning is compelling from the inside and absurd when we attribute it to someone else.

Write the proposal with the question stated sharply enough that there is a determinate answer to aim at, an account of why the existing treatments leave it open, a method laid out in explicit steps with a rough month-by-month schedule, and an honest section on what would count as the project failing — including the real possibility that once the premises are made explicit the paradox is just a theorem with nothing left to resolve. I will supply the citations myself, so no bibliography and no literature review by name. Continuous prose only, please: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — the way one would write a long letter to a colleague.
