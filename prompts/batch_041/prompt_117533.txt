'The common prior assumption is just the idea that rational people start from the same beliefs; disagreement only ever happens because you have different information. Aumann proved that two rational agents can never agree to disagree, so if two people who've genuinely shared their evidence still disagree, at least one of them is being irrational. That's why game theorists build their models on common priors, and it's why the no-trade theorems say you can't have speculative bubbles — if everyone's rational and starts from the same place, there's nothing to bet on. Harsanyi called this the doctrine that we're all basically the same person underneath.'
