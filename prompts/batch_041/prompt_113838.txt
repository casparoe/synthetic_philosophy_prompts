I'm putting together a short scoping paper for a small foundation that has roughly three postdoc-years to spend on conceptual work in AI alignment — philosophy, decision theory, formal epistemology, that sort of thing, no empirical ML and definately no benchmark-chasing. The bit I keep getting stuck on is the distinction everyone now half-uses between intent alignment and value alignment: the first being roughly 'the system does the thing the person actually meant when they asked for it', the second being 'the system's objectives line up with what's genuinely good'. I was arguing about this with a friend who does safety engineering and she thinks the whole intent/value split is a distinction without a difference once you get concrete, which is exactly the sort of thing I want stress-tested. Meanwhile I've been trying to convince myself there's a third thing worth separating out, which I've been calling mandate alignment — the system's behaviour stays within the remit it was actually authorised to act on by whatever process gave it that remit, regardless of whether the operator's intent was correctly read or the outcome turned out well. I'm not sure wether that's a real third category, a relabelling of something already sitting in the intent bucket, or just a way of smuggling the legitimacy question back in after the narrow framing was supposed to bracket it off. Take the role of someone who has done conceptual work in this area and also sat on the boring side of it — grant panels, safety team meetings — and who is allergic to agenda items that sound deep but don't change what anyone does.

I want a defended ordering of the open questions, not a survey. Start by brainstorming the candidates you see in the vicinity — what 'intent' even amounts to when the intent is partly constituted by the system's own suggestions, whether the narrow definition of the problem is tractable-but-trivial or tractable precisely because it relocates the hard part somewhere unexamined, whether the operator or the affected third parties are the right principal to align to, what 'mandate' would have to mean for my third category to earn its keep rather than being a synonym, how you'd even tell a genuine misalignment from a case of plain under-specification, and so on — then cut that list down and tell me which three or four most deserve the postdoc-years and in what order. For each, say what makes it upstream of other work (which other questions go quiet if it's settled), how tractable it is for a philosopher with no lab and no budget for experiments, whether it's neglected or merely hard, and what evidence over the next year or two would move it up or down the ranking. Be explicit about the criteria you're applying and where they pull against each other, and flag anything on my list that you reckon is a waste of time and why. I don't need reading lists or citations, just the reasoning — and please think it through carefully rather than giving me the standard tour of the debate.
