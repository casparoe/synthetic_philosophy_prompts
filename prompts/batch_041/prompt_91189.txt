I am assembling the research plan for a small theory group — three people, eighteen months, no new experiments (modeling, conceptual analysis, and reinterpretation of existing datasets only) — working on the timing of conscious experience. We want to anchor on the color phi phenomenon: a dot is flashed at one position in one color and then at another position in another color, and at suitable intervals observers report a single dot moving across the gap and changing color abruptly partway along the path, with the color change apparently seen before the second stimulus is even presented. The usual organizing axis is the Orwellian/Stalinesque one — is the content of experience rewritten after the fact, or staged in advance so that nothing is ever revised? We want to set that axis aside and replace it with a different one: read the reports as evidence about a commitment point, a moment after which the content of an experience can no longer be renegotiated, and ask where that point falls relative to the stimuli rather than whether any "revision" took place.

On that reframing, which open questions are worth the group's eighteen months, and in what order? Defend the ordering: which questions are upstream of which, which are tractable through modeling and argument rather than fresh data, which neighboring puzzles (backward masking, apparent motion, the reported timing of intentions) inherit their answers, and what would move a question up or down. I care more about the reasoning than the list.
