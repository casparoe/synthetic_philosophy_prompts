I have been arguing with a friend in my reading group about counterpart theory, and I would like a careful audit of the analogy on which her case rests. Her claim is that the counterpart relation stands to possible worlds exactly as the relation among a persisting person's temporal parts stands to times. On her picture, the part of me that exists on Wednesday and the part of me that exists on Thursday are not one and the same entity, but are held together by a unity relation that licenses us to speak of a single individual persisting over time; likewise, the individual who lives my life here and the individual who becomes a plumber in some other world are not one and the same entity, but are held together by a counterpart relation that licenses us to speak of what I might have been. She takes this parity to defuse the standard objection that counterpart theory changes the subject — that it tells us what some merely similar individual might have been rather than what I might have been — and to show that surrendering transworld identity costs us nothing we had not already surrendered in abandoning the view that a person is wholly present at each time at which they exist.

Please do the following. (1) Set the analogy out as a table of correspondences, saying precisely what plays the role of a world, a time, an individual, a temporal part, the unity relation, and a de re modal or temporal predication. (2) Identify where the mapping breaks, distinguishing the disanalogies that bear on her parity argument from those that are merely picturesque. (3) Say what the analogy quietly imports from the temporal case that the modal case may not contain — in particular, whether anything in the modal case answers to the whole person of which the temporal parts are parts, and what work that whole is doing in her argument. (4) Assess whether her conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and whether the changing-the-subject objection is defused, relocated, or merely restated. (5) If the analogy can be repaired, say what the repaired version is and what it would then support; if it cannot, say what the nearest defensible comparison would be.
