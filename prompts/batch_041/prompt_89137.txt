I've gotten pretty convinced by illusionism about phenomenal consciousness -- the view that there's no phenomenal consciousness in the sense the hard problem assumes, and that our sense of having qualia is itself an introspective illusion, the brain representing itself as having properties it doesn't really have. I want to know what would count against it. Not the "but I just know I feel pain" intuition pump -- I mean real defeaters: which empirical findings, which facts about introspection or first-person report, which representational/formal results, which explanatory failures would actualy tell against it. Rank them by how decisive they'd be, and separate the ones an illusionist would have to take seriously from the ones they can wave off. And be honest about whether the view has quietly made itself unfalsifiable -- if every report of qualia is just more illusion to be explained away, is there anything left that couldn't be accommodated? Which concessions would illusionists themselves grant as counting against them, and where does "it's an illusion" stop being an explanation and become just a label for the thing it's supposed to explain?
