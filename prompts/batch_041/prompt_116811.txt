So this is a weird thing to get obsessed with, but my sister has gotten really into some woo-woo stuff lately and it turned into a whole argument, and now I can't stop poking at it.

Her thing: she swears a healer in her town fixed her back, and she says I should believe her because she was there and felt it. I said no. She said what's the difference between believing that and believing my doctor when he tells me I've got some rare condition. And honestly I didn't have a clean answer, so I've been trying to figure out what I actually believe, case by case.

Here's where I've landed:

- My sister says she saw a wild boar in the park near her house. I believe her, no problem.
- Same sister, same park, says the boar talked to her. Obviously no.
- My doctor says I have a condition that affects about one person in a million. I believe him pretty much straight away.
- A hundred people who don't know each other all say they saw a guy walk on water at the pond. I still don't believe the walking-on-water part. Maybe that they saw something.
- A friend tells me he won the lottery twice. Don't believe him.
- A friend tells me he's been struck by lightning twice. Weirdly, I do believe him.
- A stranger on a forum describes being abducted by aliens. No.
- A guy confesses to a crime nobody suspected him of, which makes his life worse. I believe him.
- Somebody tells me a story that would, if true, get them money. I discount it a lot.
- A single grainy video of the water-walking guy shows up. This moves me almost not at all, even though a video is supposedly strong evidence.

So the pattern I keep noticing is it's not really about how rare the thing is, because I believe the one-in-a-million diagnosis and the double lightning strike but not the double lottery win. And it's not really about the number of witnesses either, because the doctor beats a hundred strangers. It's like... whether there's a way for the thing to happen that I already have a slot for? And whether the person telling me has a reason to make it up?

A friend who's a grad student said the real principle is something like: don't believe a report unless the report would be more surprising if it were false than the event would be if it were true — i.e. it's about the likelihood of the testimony, not the prior probability of the event. But that feels like it just pushes the problem around, and I don't see that it gets the doctor-vs-hundred-people case either. I'm pretty sure this whole thing is an old argument, something about miracles, but I don't remember who said it.

Is there a principle that actually gets all of these, or close? And what does it say about the case I actually care about, the healer and my sister's back? Also, if you have to throw out one of my verdicts to make it work, which one, and why.
