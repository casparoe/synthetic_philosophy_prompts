I am 37 and must decide by the end of the month whether to accept a two-year posting at a remote Antarctic research station. The pay and career boost are large, but every account I have read by people who wintered over says the experience remade them: some lost the ambitions that sent them there, some formed attachments they could not have anticipated, and some describe a self they no longer recognize. I cannot even describe the outcomes in my current vocabulary, let alone assign them probabilities. My partner, a decision theorist, says I should treat my future preferences as just another uncertain variable and maximize expected utility from my current standpoint. My closest friend, a novelist, says that is exactly the mistake, because the person who would enjoy or regret the outcome is not me, so any expected-value calculation is a fiction. A third friend says I should ignore both and choose the option that makes for the better story, since the only self who can judge the choice is the one who emerges from it. I cannot visit first, I cannot postpone, and I cannot gather more information. What should I do? Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
