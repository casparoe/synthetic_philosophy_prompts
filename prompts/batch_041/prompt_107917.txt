Two friends of mine got into it over dinner about whether a health service should pay for a pill that gives a small benefit to thousands of people or one that gives a big benefit to a handful. (1) Ravi said the whole comparison is a fiction: you can't put my suffering and yours on the same ruler, utility is at best a ranking, and any "how much" talk is something the analyst made up. (2) Dana said that can't be right, because we know some people are worse off than others all the time -- someone being tortured is worse off than someone with an itch -- and if comparisons were meaningless that sentence would be too. (3) Ravi said fine, the easy cases are easy, but the decisions that matter are the hard ones, where two people are each moderately badly off and we have to say who's worse, and there the ruler is just a convention; pick a differnt convention and the funding flips, so the answer is doing the work, not the fact. (4) Dana said he's confusing two questions -- wether we can compare levels of welfare at all, versus whether we can compare gains or units -- and the funding decision might only need the second, so his complaint about levels misses. (5) Ravi said the unit is exactly as arbitrary as the level; a pleasure-unit is stipulated, and different stipulations disagree, so you still don't get a fact.

Can you adjudicate this move by move -- which of them is right at each step, and what are both missing? I honestly can't tell who's ahead.
