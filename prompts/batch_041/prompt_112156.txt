I'm a third-year student taking a course on war and strategy, and the reading has begun to make me think my apartment is a case study. My roommate and I have eleven months left on a joint lease that neither of us can afford to break. He could move back in with his parents tomorrow, twenty minutes away; my family is abroad, so I have nowhere to go, and he knows it. Every large question is therefore settled, and settled his way — the rooms, who re-signs, whose name is on the utilities — and we both know it will not change. What has changed is everything small: the thermostat, the dishes, notes left on the fridge, things borrowed and not returned, guests who stay later than the other one wants. Our third housemate, who is my closest friend and would be dragged down with us if the household collapsed, told me not to worry, since the two of us will never actually do anything — which I think is precisely the problem. Two friends have given me opposite advice: one says to threaten to leave anyway, even though everyone knows I cannot afford to; the other says to stop pushing, because a stable standoff is the best I can hope for. I am also sitting on one piece of information about my roommate that would end the lease for both of us if it came out, and he almost certainly suspects I have it.

What should I do, and how do I tell which of my two friends has the better grasp of the situation? Is our locked-in stalemate the reason the small stuff keeps getting worse, and would it help to build a threat I could actually carry out? And is the information I am holding doing any work at all, or is it worthless to me precisely because I can never use it?
