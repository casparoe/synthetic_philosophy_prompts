Suppose you're advising a small foundation that can fund about five philosophers for two years on negative utilitarianism and suffering-focused ethics. They want a defended agenda, not a survey: which open questions or lines of work should they prioritise, and in what order? For each, say why it's upstream of other work, how tractable it is for philosophers rather than economists or neuroscientists, how neglected it is, and what evidence over the next year or two would move it up or down. Also fold in a wrinkle: a colleague has proposed that the field should focus on suffering without a subject -- cases where something is bad in a suffering-like way but there's no unified experiencer to undergo it, maybe in certain distributed cognitive systems or some sleep states. Is that a live question worth funding or a confusion? Use it to test your ranking: does it change what's upstream? And if the funder can only pick two lines of work, which two and why?
