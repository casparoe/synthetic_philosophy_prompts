Bit of a weird one. I was in a group chat argument about whether a forum should ban a guy who keeps posting stuff that's pretty clearly targeted harassment, and my mate pulls out the old line "your right to swing your fist ends where my nose begins" and says that settles it: the posts are a fist, the people on the recieving end are the nose, ban him. I don't think it settles anything but I also can't articulate why.

Can you lay out the different precise things that one line could actually be claiming, as a menu rather than an essay? For each reading I want to know what the "nose" is doing — my body, my property, my reputation, my ability to be heard at all, some threshold of psychological distress — what counts as the "fist," and who gets to decide. Does the slogan presuppose that we can already identify the harm before we know where the line falls, or is it just a fancy way of saying the law draws lines somewhere and this one is ours? Which readings make it a platitude, which make it a genuinely substantive and defensible principle, and which are false — e.g. any reading on which ordinary upset or disagreement counts as a bloody nose, which would hand everyone a veto over everyone else. And there's a reading where the location of the boundary is a fact of the matter that we discover versus one where we stipulate it and then argue about the stipulation — say where that shows up and whether it matters for the ban question.

Then tell me which reading my mate is most likely running, and whether any of them actually get him his ban.
