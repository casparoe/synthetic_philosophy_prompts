My friend Maya is a product designer who's gotten deep into the "do artifacts have politics" stuff, and she has annoyingly specific verdicts about which designed objects count as political and which don't. Her running list: park benches with armrests and dividers that make it impossible to lie down — political. Speed bumps — not political. Suburban street grids that dead-end into cul-de-sacs so nobody drives through — political, even though she agrees nobody who laid them out was trying to keep anyone out. The touchless soap dispensers and taps in airport bathrooms that keep failing to trigger on darker skin — political. Highway guardrails — not political. The little tactile bumps on the F and J keys — not political.

Now she's on the design panel for a new downtown park, and the fountain that's been proposed is a bottle-filler only: no bubbler you can put your mouth on, a shallow sloped basin so you can't sit on the edge or wash in it, and it shuts off after thirty seconds. She has to decide whether to flag it as a political design or just an ordinary design choice. What's she going to say, and why? I care way less about the verdict than about the rule she's actually running — tell me the two or three rules that fit everything on her list, what each of them predicts about the fountain, which one you think she's really using, and what kind of case I could put in front of her that would force her to choose between them. And tell me if her list is quietly inconsistent and she's just been winging it.
