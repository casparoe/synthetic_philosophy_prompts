What I want is a single realistic life-history, told in one paragraph, in which an agent does something plainly blameworthy and the character from which the act flows was formed without her ever having decided what sort of person to become. She becomes the way she is through a long series of episodes in which she attends to certain things rather than others, returns to certain practices rather than others, and takes certain things seriously rather than others — and in none of them is she deliberating about her own character. Constraints: realistic throughout (no brainwashing, no neurosurgery, no science-fictional devices, no interventions from outside an ordinary life); at every stage she must positively do something rather than merely fail to do something, so that no stage can be written off as pure passivity; at no stage is there a decision about what kind of person she is becoming; and give her enough particularity that a reader can see why each stage looked like nothing at the time. The case exists to test whether responsibility can be transmitted backwards along a chain of non-deliberative agency. It therefore fails if it smuggles in a hidden decision about character, or if any stage is described in terms that already presuppose the conclusion — for instance by calling her attention "hers" in a sense that settles the question at issue.

Then do the following work on it. State Galen Strawson's Basic Argument — you do what you do because of the way you are; responsibility for the act would therefore require responsibility for the character; responsibility for the character would require having intentionally brought it about that you are that way; and that in turn generates a regress that nothing can terminate, since nothing can be the cause of itself — and say what it concludes about this agent, in the demanding sense of responsibility at issue rather than the thinner sense in which an attitude is merely fitting or useful. Then state what a compatibilist who holds that responsibility for character can be transmitted through the agent's own earlier activity concludes about the same agent. Identify the one feature of the case on which the two verdicts turn, and name the conception of agency each side must be using to reach its verdict. Say which premise of the Basic Argument the case is designed to threaten, and whether it threatens that premise or merely relocates the regress. Then run the control: hold everything else fixed and replace a single episode of non-deliberative formation with a deliberate decision about her character; report whether any verdict changes, and if so why that one substitution accomplishes what the whole history could not. Say separately what the case collapses into if every episode is such a decision. Finally, give the strongest objection the Strawson side can raise against your case — most obviously that attention, habit, and repetition are things that happen to a person rather than things she does — and say honestly whether the objection succeeds, and whether the case can be repaired or is bound to fail.

Argue it on the merits: the scenario must carry the argument by itself, with no citations and no appeal to what any particular author has written.
