I am in a reading group on the modern revival of Stoicism, and we keep sliding between the ancient doctrine and the contemporary therapeutic version. Before I present my own position, I want a consistency audit. I am not asking for a survey of Stoicism, a history of the school, or a reading list; I want to know whether the package below hangs together, where the real joints are, and what would have to give.

Here is roughly where I have landed. (1) The dichotomy of control is the core insight: my judgments, desires, choices, and character are up to me; my body, reputation, other people's actions, and the outcomes of my efforts are not. (2) Virtue is the only genuine good, and everything else, including health, wealth, friendship, and the lives of those I love, is at best a preferred indifferent, to be selected when reason permits but not good in itself. (3) The Stoic account of the passions is basically right: grief, fear, anger, and the like are judgments that something genuinely bad is present or impending; the sage is free of them, and what remains are good emotions such as joy, reasonable wishing, and caution. (4) I take oikeiosis seriously: we are naturally attached first to ourselves and our families, then to fellow citizens, then to all human beings, so a Stoic should be a cosmopolitan and should work for the common good. (5) I want a Stoicism without Providence: I accept modern science, and I do not believe the cosmos is a rational animal or that everything happens for a purpose. (6) I think the dichotomy of control is a practical heuristic for anxiety, not a metaphysical thesis about free will; I am a compatibilist and do not think the Stoic needs libertarian freedom. (7) I think a modern Stoic should not aim at the elimination of grief. Grief is a natural and often appropriate response to loss, even if it can be moderated and should not become self-indulgent. (8) I think we should try to change unjust institutions and reduce suffering, not merely manage our attitudes toward them. (9) I have a soft spot for acting with a reservation: I pursue preferred indifferents, but always with the proviso that fate may not permit it, and I will not be disturbed if it does not.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look like they are. Order the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing every possible worry as equally bad. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cost would be, that is, what I would lose if I revised that commitment. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and whether the resulting package is still recognizably Stoic or has become something else. I have heard that the Stoics distinguished first movements or pre-emotions from full passions; if that distinction is the right lever for the apparent clash between (2), (3), and (7), show me where it actually gets purchase and where it merely relabels the problem. Be specific about which commitment is doing the damage, and do not tell me that all of this is fine as long as I hold it sincerely.
