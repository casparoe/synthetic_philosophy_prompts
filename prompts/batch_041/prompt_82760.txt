I hold a version of the extended mind thesis and want to know how exposed it is. On my formulation, an external resource is literally part of a person's cognitive system — not merely a tool they use — when they rely on it automatically, never check its deliverances against anything else, and could not reconstruct certain beliefs or intentions without it. This licenses a consequence I want to press: where two people keep their shared memory in a single archive that neither can now think without, that archive is part of each of their minds, and destroying it wrongs each of them as a thinker, not merely as an owner of property. Tell me what would count against the thesis so formulated, ranked from the defeaters that should genuinely move me to those I can shrug off; which of them would its own defenders concede; and what it predicts that its rivals do not. Finally, say honestly whether my formulation has quietly insulated the thesis from any possible evidence, and if it has, whether that is fatal for a thesis of this kind or simply the price of doing philosophy at this level of generality.
