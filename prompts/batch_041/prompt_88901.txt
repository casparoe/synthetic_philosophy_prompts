Some background: my flatmate did an MA in philosophy and I did ecology, and we've got into a running argument about when something counts as the same thing over time. I keep giving verdicts, she keeps telling me they can't all be true at once, and at this point I genuinely can't tell if she's right or if I just need a cleverer principle. So I'd like you to take my verdicts at face value, work out what principle or small family of principles actually captures them, and be honest about which ones have to go.

My verdicts, roughly in order of confidence. The lump of clay and the statue moulded from it: one object, not two — 'the clay' and 'the statue' are two descriptions of the same thing, and I don't believe in a statue over and above the clay. Same for the bronze and the bronze bell, the marble and the carved figure. This one I'd defend to the death. A person and their body, though: two things. When my grandfather died his body was still in the bed and he wasn't — the matter stayed and the person went, which is exactly the clay-outliving-the-statue shape I just said doesn't happen. She says pick one and I can't. The topiary hedge trimmed into a peacock for forty years and then let grow out: same hedge throughout, the shape isn't a thing that existed and then died — and I can't see why this doesn't cause the same trouble as the statue. The bronze bell melted down and recast as a different bell: the new bell is not the old bell, same bronze or not, so here losing the form loses the thing. Ship of Theseus: swap the planks one at a time and it's the same ship; collect all the old planks and rebuild a ship from them in a warehouse and the warehouse ship is not the original — so a continuous career beats continuous matter. Two lumps of clay kneaded into one ball: neither original lump survives, obviously. An amoeba dividing: parent gone, two new ones. A river: same river, all-new water. A haircut: same person. Total memory loss: still the same person, I think, though definately less sure.

I did try to state a principle myself and it fell apart, so here it is for you to test and repair: a thing is just some matter arranged a certain way, so the thing is the arrangement, and if the arrangement goes the thing goes — that's why the recast bell isn't the old bell. But then squashing the clay should destroy the statue-thing I said was identical to the clay, and it should also mean my grandfather's body was my grandfather, which I deny.

What I want: a definite recommendation, not a menu of every position in the literature. Give me the principle, or small set of principles, that covers as many of these as possible; for each clause, the case that forces it; its verdicts on two cases I haven't given — the planarian cut in half with both halves regenerating into complete worms (is the original one of them, both, neither?) and one more of your own choosing that you think is a good stress test; and a straight answer about which of my verdicts the principle can't save and which I should abandon. If my flatmate is right that no principle saves them all, say so and tell me which single verdict is doing the most damage. Please commit to something and argue for it rather than laying the options out and leaving the choice to me.
