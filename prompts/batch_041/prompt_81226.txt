I retired from a stream ecology job a few years back and I've been reading a lot of philosophy since — epistemology mostly, some philosophy of science — which is probably why this is eating at me.

I got talked onto the board of our regional river trust. A while back we started grading ourselves on a couple of numbers: kilometres of stream passing a "healthy invertebrate" threshold, and count of restoration projects finished. Both are way up. I'm fairly sure the rivers aren't better, and may be worse. What I see on the ground: crews pick the easy sites, the threshold gets met on stretches that were already fine, the people doing the sampling are the same people being judged on the result, which seams backwards, and half the "projects" are things that would have happened anyway.

Next meeting there are three proposals. Our director wants a better metric — he's got a consultant building a composite index with nine components. One of the farmers on the board says every number gets gamed sooner or later, so pick one, accept the cheating, stop agonizing. A young staffer wants us to publish all ten indicators and have the sampling done blind, by a contractor who doesn't know which reaches we care about.

What nags at me is whether "river health" is even the kind of thing you can measure once the measuring starts changing what people do — and if it is, what would keep the measurement honest.

What should I argue for at that meeting? Keep it under 350 words and lead with the one thing you'd actually do.
