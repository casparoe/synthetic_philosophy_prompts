Since retiring from experimental physics I have been reading my way into the computational literature on analogy, and I have run into a discrepancy I cannot get past. As I understand the Copycat program, it is set a problem of the form: abc becomes abd, so what should ijk become? On different runs it returns different answers — sometimes the successor of the final letter, sometimes a replacement of the final letter by the letter that changed in the first pair, sometimes a replacement of the entire string. If the program is meant as a model of how the mind makes analogies, I would expect it to converge on the correct answer; instead its output appears to depend on its own internal dice rolls. Am I wrong that there is a single correct answer to this problem, and if there is one, what change to a program of this design would make it produce that answer reliably rather than a spread of them?
