In my cognitive science seminar we have been working through Hofstadter's variation-on-a-theme picture of creativity—the knob-twiddling model, in our shorthand—according to which a mind does not create ex nihilo but takes an existing representation, identifies dimensions or parameters, and turns the knobs to generate new candidates, with the most creative acts being those that reorganize the space of variations rather than merely moving within it. I find the picture attractive, but when I tried to set it out as an argument for a short response paper, I produced a chain that seems to prove far too much. (1) An act is creative just in case it produces a novel and valuable variation on an existing theme. (2) Any two items can be represented as variations on a common theme, because for any pair one can specify a parameter space and assign each item coordinates within it. (3) So for any produced item there is some prior item or set of items of which it is a variation. (4) Novelty is difference along some dimension of such a space, and value is whatever an evaluative scheme assigns to the result. (5) Hence every produced item is a novel and valuable variation on an existing theme. (6) Therefore every act of production is creative. (7) But that is absurd: a random typographical error, a photocopy with a smudge, and the ten-thousandth identical brick are not creative acts merely because one can invent a dimension along which they differ from their predecessors.

I would like to keep (1) and (2) if possible, since the whole point of the model seems to be that creativity is continuous with ordinary cognition and that novelty arises from recombination rather than from ex nihilo invention. Yet I cannot see how to avoid (6) without giving something up. Which premise or step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? In particular, if the repair is to insist that only certain themes and certain knobs are relevant—those that are salient, projectible, or explanatory for a cognitive system—does that save the model, or does it simply move the creative act into the prior selection of a similarity space and leave the original account circular? I would like a diagnosis of the step that fails, an explanation of why it looked plausible, and a price list for the alternatives.
