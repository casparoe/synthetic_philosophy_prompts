I spent thirty-five years as an experimental scientist, most of it as a geophysicist at a national laboratory, and since retiring I have been working through the philosophical canon without much guidance. I have just finished the two long treatments of music in Schopenhauer's The World as Will and Representation — the one in the third book of the first volume and the supplementary discussion in the second — and I found them far stranger than I expected. I am no longer confident that I have hold of what he is actually claiming.

Wanting a second opinion, I read a popular book on the philosophy of music by a writer I will not name, which summarizes the position in a single paragraph:

"Schopenhauer is the philosopher who placed music at the summit of the arts. For him the other arts all perform the same office: painting, poetry, and architecture copy the Ideas, the eternal patterns that stand between the world we see and the blind striving that lies behind it. Music is different, and higher, because it dispenses with that middle term and copies the striving itself. This is why music moves us as no painting can, and why he calls it the language of feeling: it renders joy and grief, longing and triumph in their purest form, with the particular situations that arouse them stripped away. And because it needs nothing from the visible world, it cannot depict anything — a storm in a symphony is not a storm — so a composer who sets out to paint pictures in sound has misunderstood his art. What earns music the highest place is precisely its abstractness: of all the arts it is the least encumbered by content. His account also explains music's power over us. Like the other arts, it lifts us out of the turmoil of desire, but it does so more completely than they can, since it puts us in touch with the Will directly rather than through its images."

I would like an audit of that paragraph rather than a summary of Schopenhauer. Which sentences would a specialist accept as fair? Which are the sort of simplification that leaves a reader with the wrong picture? Which are simply false? I am especially uncertain about two things and would like them pressed hard. First, the claim that music is the language of feeling: my training makes me want to ask whether the Will, as Schopenhauer describes it, is the sort of thing that could be the content of an emotion at all, and what he actually says about music's relation to the affects. Second, the closing claim that music does the other arts' work more completely; I notice that he puts architecture at the bottom of his hierarchy, and architecture also depicts nothing, so I suspect that abstractness cannot be doing the work the writer says it does.

Two further questions. The writer draws a conclusion he attributes to Schopenhauer — that absolute music is the highest form and that program music is a mistake — and I want to know whether that follows from Schopenhauer's position or only from the writer's version of it. And I would like to know whether "copy" here is meant as a claim about what music is about, in the way a model is about the system it models, or as something looser. The same question applies to the scheme of correspondences he offers among bass, harmony, and melody and the grades of the Will's objectification: is that presented as doctrine, as analogy, or as something he himself flags as speculative?

Finally, tell me how confident you are in each of your main claims, and single out the one point on which you are least sure.
