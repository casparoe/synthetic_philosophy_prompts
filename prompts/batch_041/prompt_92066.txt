Okay, I need help from someone who actually thinks about self-referential rule systems for a living — legal philosopher, game designer, whatever, as long as you're comfortable with the weird stuff. I'm writing the rulebook for a self-amending club (basically a Nomic-style game where changing the rules is itself a legal move), and my gut keeps giving me verdicts that I can't get to line up under any one principle. I've spent alot of time on this. Before you answer, please brainstorm a few candidate principles and actually test each one against every case below, then tell me which survive and what each one costs me.

The setup is simple. Rule 1: the club adopts, amends, or repeals rules by a two-thirds vote of members present. Everything else gets passed under Rule 1. Here are the cases and what I think about them:

1. A rule is passed by two-thirds saying 'from now on rules need only a simple majority.' I think that's valid — the club changed its own threshold using the threshold it had.
2. A rule is passed by two-thirds saying 'the rule about the club colours can never be amended or repealed, by any majority, ever.' I think this one binds. My friend says that's nonsense, because any later two-thirds vote can just repeal the entrenching clause itself. I can't say why she's wrong without sounding mystical.
3. After a valid amendment, the threshold is 90%. A rule is then passed with 70%. I think it's invalid — it never became a rule at all.
4. A rule says 'a rule is valid only if it appears in the Schedule at the back of the book.' The rule itself is not in the Schedule. I still think it's valid and governs everything else. Validity rules shouldn't have to satisfy themselves, or nothing would ever get started.
5. The club passes a rule, by two-thirds, repealing Rule 1 — the rule that grants the power to pass rules in the first place. I think this is invalid, but I can't explain why without pointing at something outside the rulebook, which I refuse to do.
6. The club passes a rule today saying 'the rule we passed last year about the roof was void from the start.' I think the club can change what's true from now on, but can't reach back and unmake a rule that was validly made.
7. A rule says 'no rule may require more than a simple majority to pass.' Passed by two-thirds. I think it's valid and instantly lowers everything, including itself.

The case I keep flip-flopping on: the Charter. The Charter is the founding document, written before any of this, and it says Rule 1 is in force. I want to say the Charter isn't a rule and isn't subject to the rules — but then Rule 1's validity rests on something that isn't a rule, which is exactly the outside-the-rulebook move I said I wouldn't make in case 5.

My own tentative principle: a rule-change is legitimate just in case it was made in accordance with the rules in force immediately before the change. I like it because it's clean, but I suspect it either fails case 2 or fails case 5, and I can't tell which.

So: is there a principle (or small set) that gets all of this, or close? State it precisely, show which case motivates each clause, tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed, and be honest about which of my verdicts it forces me to drop — and whether the price is worth it. No reading lists or citations, please; I want the argument, not the bibliography.
