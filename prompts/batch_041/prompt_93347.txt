Hey, I'm in a seminar on immigration and political philosophy this term and for the final we have to write up our own position and then defend it in class, so I'm trying to get mine straight first. Writing it down I keep getting this itchy feeling that some of it can't all be true at once but I cant tell which bits. Here's the pile:

(1) I'm pro free movement. There should be a real presumption in favor of people crossing borders and settling where they want, and the burden of proof sits on whoever wants to keep them out.

(2) I'm not an anarchist about it though. States can be legitimate, and a legitimate political community has some genuine say over who joins it — not an absolute say, but not nothing.

(3) The all-affected principle: if a decision seriously effects your interests, you should have a say in it. That's my most basic commitment about political legitimacy.

(4) Where you're born shouldn't determine how your life goes. A border isn't a morally magic line.

(5) I think the thing that's really wrong with exclusion is that it derails a life that's already underway. It's not mainly that exclusion makes people worse off — it's that someone else gets to decide whether your plans are allowed to continue. So the strength of someone's claim goes up with how much of their life is already built around being somewhere.

(6) If there is a right to exclude, it belongs to the community as a whole — to the people — and not to the government, or to current citizens treated like they own the country.

(7) I'm unsympathetic to culture arguments for exclusion. I don't think a community's way of life can outweigh somebody's interest in moving somewhere and building a life.

(8) Property rights matter but I don't think they're doing the fundamental work here.

Two things I've already been told. One, that 1 and 2 obviously contradict each other, which I don't think is that simple but maybe it is. Two, a friend said the whole view is incoherent because you can't be pro free movement and believe in property rights at all — I'm pretty sure that's definately wrong but I couldn't say why.

What I want: which of these genuinely clash, which pairs only look like they clash, and how bad each real clash actually is. Be specific about what commits me to what, because I honestly don't know where the problem is.
