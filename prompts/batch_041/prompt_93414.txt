Ok so this is a bit of an odd one and I'm not sure it's really your remit, but I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for about fifteen years now and I'm the trustee who keeps dragging weird edge cases into our meetings, so I've been asked to write the paper for this one.

You're a legal philosopher — someone who's spent real time on statutory interpretation, textualism vs purposivism, that whole fight — and I want you to help me write the written opinion I'll circulate before our board meeting on the 14th.

Background: I'm a trustee of a small grantmaking foundation. We give away about £2m a year, mostly to things meant to improve the quality of public reasoning — argument-mapping tools, forecasting projects, that sort of thing. Our founding deed was written in 2014 by the guy who set the Fund up, and it contains this rule: "No grant shall be made to any person who is, at the time the grant is approved, a trustee of the Fund." That's the entire clause. Elsewhere the deed says: "Where the meaning of these Rules is unclear, the trustees shall have regard to the settled practice of the Fund."

Now the case. Priya was a trustee from 2019 until last March, when she resigned, citing workload — I believe her, though the timing was convenient, since she wanted to apply for money. In January she sent us an application for £180k over two years for a project our own strategy paper had named as a gap we wanted filled. On the words of the deed she's plainly eligible: she isn't a trustee at the time of approval. But the minutes of the founding meeting record the founder saying the point of the clause was to stop trustees "helping themselves" and to keep the Fund from looking like a vehicle for its own people. In 2021 we refused a grant to a former trustee who'd been off the board eight months, reading the clause in light of that purpose; it went 4-3, and one trustee filed a note saying we'd just legislated an unwritten cooling-off period. In 2023 we granted to a former trustee who'd been off for two years, with barely any discussion. So practice isn't settled either way. And two current trustees co-authored a paper with Priya, so even if we grant there's a conflicts process to run.

What I want: a clear recommendation — grant, refuse, or grant with conditions — the reasons for it, the best case for the losing side stated as strongly as I'd want it stated if we went that way, and a short section on what to do about the rule going forward (amend the deed, pass a cooling-off resolution, or minute a construction of the clause and live with it). The memo needs to be something we can actually vote on, not a survey of options.

Two bits of theory I'd like you to use, and please explain each in a sentence or two as you go, because half the board hasn't read any of this: (1) the interpretation/construction distinction — first settle what the words mean, and only once you're past that do you decide what to do about vagueness or gaps; and (2) the idea, which I think some theorists call "liquidation", that a vague rule's meaning gets fixed by a long course of consistent practice rather than by more word-chopping. If our precedents are inconsistent, say plainly what that does to idea (2).

Also be straight about the thing I'm worried about: we all want to fund this project, and I dont trust that we'd even be having this conversation if the applicant were someone we disliked.

Think it through carefully before you commit — start by laying out the plausible readings of the clause and what turns on each, then pick one. British English, and keep it under about 1,200 words, it's going in a board pack.
