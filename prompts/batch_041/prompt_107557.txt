I am not an academic; I have spent the last couple of years reading Kierkegaard on my own, mostly his writing about the press, the public, and the present age, and I run a small monthly discussion group that has been working through those texts. Next month our guest is a journalist and critic who has been developing a view I find both attractive and slippery, and I would like a set of interview questions that genuinely test it rather than let her restate it. I am deliberately not naming her, because what I want to interrogate is the position she has been building in her own essays rather than anything she has settled into.

Her thesis, as I understand it, has three parts. First, she accepts Kierkegaard's diagnosis of the public as a phantom: an abstraction that everyone appeals to and no one actually is, and that is precisely what lets people say and do things for which no one can be held to account. Second, and this is the part I have not seen anyone press her on, she argues that the remedy he proposes is itself a byproduct of the very thing he attacks. On her account the single individual, the one who is supposed to stand alone against the crowd, only becomes imaginable once there is a large anonymous reading public that a solitary reader can feel personally addressed by; the press therefore produces not only the phantom public but also the individual meant to cure it, so the two are twins and neither can be used to repair the other. Third, she proposes replacing both with what she calls role-responsibility: obligations that attach to positions people actually occupy, such as editor, reader, source, or commenter, rather than to the public or to the lone individual.

Please give me eight to ten questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, and make sure the set includes at least one question on each of the following: whether the public is really a nothing or a genuine distributed agent, and what turns on the answer; her claim that the single individual is a print artifact, together with the objection that his individual is a religious category addressed to the person before God and so both predates and outlives print; whether the leveling he describes is caused by the press at all or by something broader, such as money or the modern state, which would leave her media-centered story with much less to do; and whether role-responsibility escapes his complaint or simply reinstates it, since a person who explains that he was only doing his job is exactly the irresponsibility Kierkegaard was worried about. I would also like a question about anonymity, because he published some of his most important early work under pseudonyms and I have never understood how that squares with his attack on anonymous speech, so I want her to have to answer for it. Finally, one question on whether the algorithmic feed makes the public more or less phantom-like than the newspaper did, and a closing question that turns her own remedy back against his diagnosis, so that she must say what she thinks is actually left of his critique once her revision is in place. Press her hardest where the position is weakest, and let the follow-ups assume she will reach for her most familiar reply.
