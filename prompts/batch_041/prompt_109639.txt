For the final portfolio in my philosophy of mind seminar, I'd like a pantoum about the intentional stance and about what we are actually doing when we say that an AI system believes something. I want the form to do the arguing: in a pantoum the same lines keep returning in new company, so a recurring attribution can read one way when the lines around it concern next-token prediction and quite another when they concern promises, mistakes, and being wrong, with the words unchanged and only the stance shifting. Keep the repeated lines exactly repeated, and let the closing loop be the point rather than a flourish.

It has to be accurate for readers who have worked through the three stances. The intentional stance is a predictive strategy — treat the system as a rational agent, ask what it ought to believe and want given its situation, and predict what it will do — and it earns its keep by predicting well, not by uncovering an inner state. What I want the poem to hold is that its success on a language model is partly our own doing: the model was trained on a corpus of human attributions and tuned to be legible to us, so the loop that closes the poem should feel like the mechanism by which our stance-taking helps produce the very predictability that then seems to license the stance. Somewhere the poem should also mark the gap between the personal level, where beliefs, reasons, and aboutness live, and the subpersonal level, where the machinery that emits the text has no beliefs about anything — able to say it believes, and then to show what that leaves untouched. It should not settle into either "it is only autocomplete" or "it really believes"; someone holding either view should feel the poem taking the other one seriously.

Please write it in American English, and keep it to four or five stanzas.
