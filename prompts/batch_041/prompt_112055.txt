I run the public programming and the comment moderation for a mid-sized public library's civic series — we do a monthly in-person panel that gets livestreamed, plus a Discord and a comment thread that go with it, and I'm the one who has to decide, usually inside a day, whether we host something, whether we pull a clip, whether we boot someone, and whether we let them come back. We're partly city-funded so there's a faint public-records smell over everything, I have no lawyer on call, and anything I do can be appealed to our board, which means I have to be able to say why to the person I'm turning away without it sounding like I just don't like them. My current rule is basically "we host anyone who'll be civil, we remove personal attacks and slurs," and it is definately not doing the work anymore. What I keep running into is the tolerance thing — Popper's point, which someone told me is actually buried in a chapter note rather than the main text of the book about the open society, that a society that extends unlimited tolerance to the intolerant will eventually lose tolerance altogether, so the tolerant have to keep the right to defend themselves, and that the sharpest version of the worry is about people who won't meet you on the level of argument at all and start by denouncing argument. Someone else told me Rawls has a section where he goes the other way and says a just society has to put up with the intolerant except in real emergencies, and a third person sent me a Marcuse essay from the sixties where the anxiety is the exact opposite — that tolerance is what the powerful use to keep things as they are, he calls it repressive tolerance I think. I don't need a literature review, I need a way to make the call on a Tuesday afternoon, so please turn this into a procedure I can actually run.

Specifically I want the questions in the order I should ask them, what counts as a good-enough answer at each stage, the points where the procedure just says no and I stop, and — this is the part nobody ever gives me — the points where it isn't a factual question at all and I should hand it to the board with a note saying this is a values fork and here are the two prongs. The steps have to keep apart three things I keep conflating: speech that merely offends me or someone else, speech that advocates intolerance of a group, and speech that is itself an act of intolerance rather than a view — organizing a pile-on, coordinating exclusion, that kind of thing. If you can give me a test for whether someone is arguing in good faith or just probing where the fence is, and a reversibility test along the lines of would this same rule protect the people they're denouncing, I'll take both, as long as you also tell me where each one fails and what it will get wrong. I'd rather have something with visible seams than something that sounds confident everywhere.

Then run it out loud on three things from the last few months: a local group that wants our room for a talk arguing that a particular religion is inherently incompatible with democracy and shouldn't have legal protections; a regular in the Discord who keeps just asking questions about whether some ethnic group is on average less intelligent, dressed up as a debate about the science; and a guy I banned for a real death threat who now wants back in, has apologized, and points out that we let worse slide from someone on the other side last year. Fair warning: I already know which way I want the first one to go, and I suspect the procedure is going to tell me something I don't like about the third, so don't sand the edges off. Be straight with me about what the procedure can't do — where it's going to produce a confident-looking answer to a question that's genuinely contested, and where it's going to misfire. And please write the whole thing as straight prose, no headings, no bullet points, no numbered list hanging down the page, no bold, no tables; I want to read it like a letter from someone who's thought about it, so if the steps are sequential just walk me through them in order in the sentences.
