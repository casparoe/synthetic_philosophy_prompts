For most of my working life I studied the mechanical failure of metals, and I have come late to the metaphysics of dispositions. My old laboratory was saturated with dispositional language — a specimen was brittle, or notch-sensitive, or prone to fatigue — and we were never in doubt that whether a disposition showed itself depended on circumstances having nothing to do with the specimen. Reading the philosophical literature now, I find the counterexamples to the simple conditional analysis of dispositions far more instructive than the repairs, and I would like a disciplined comparison of them rather than a survey. By the simple conditional analysis I mean the proposal that an object is disposed to manifest M when stimulus S occurs just in case, were S to occur, M would occur.

Please sort the five devices below into tiers by how much trouble each makes for that unamended analysis, with a sentence or two justifying each placement. I describe them by structure rather than by the labels I have picked up, because I want the ranking to rest on the structure and not on my terminology.

(i) The object would lose the disposition if the stimulus occurred: the very obtaining of S destroys the basis of the disposition, so the counterfactual comes out false although the disposition is genuinely present while S is absent.

(ii) The disposition is present and its basis intact, but an external factor — one that operates only once S obtains — prevents M, so the counterfactual is false though the disposition is present.

(iii) The disposition is wholly absent, but S would reliably produce M by an independent route, so the counterfactual is true though the disposition is absent.

(iv) A case of my own devising, which I have not seen discussed. An object carries two intrinsic bases: one disposing it to M upon S, and another disposing it to N upon S, where N excludes M. The second basis is as much a part of the object as the first. When S obtains, N occurs and M does not. The simple analysis returns the verdict that the object is not disposed to M upon S — but this seems wrong, since the first basis is present and intact. The masking here is internal rather than external, and the masker is itself an intrinsic and retained property of the object.

(v) A further case of my own devising, which I suspect is no objection at all and include precisely in order to see where it lands. The disposition is present and its basis intact, and were S to occur the disposition would produce M; but S also triggers an independent mechanism that would produce M on its own. Both routes fire; M occurs. The counterfactual is true and the disposition is present — so far as I can tell nothing is amiss, except that the disposition was idle.

Now a second dimension. There is a well-known repair which holds that the object must retain some intrinsic basis throughout, and that the counterfactual should be evaluated conditional on that retention. Please rate the five devices a second time, by how much each threatens that repaired analysis rather than the simple one. I am particularly interested in whether (iv) survives the repair, given that the interfering basis is intrinsic and would be retained alongside the basis for M.

Finally, tell me for each of (iv) and (v) whether it is genuinely distinct from the three standard devices or a disguised version of one of them, and say for each whether the difficulty it raises is a defect in the conditional analysis itself or only a defect in reading the analysis causally or explanatorily. I would rather have a sharp placement that is wrong than a hedge that lists every consideration.
