I'm a novelist, about a year into a book set mostly in a public defenders office in a mid-sized southern city, so I've been reading and interviewing around capital cases for a while now, and there's a shape I keep running into that I dont have a word for. The practice keeps getting smaller — juveniles off the list, then people with intellectual disabilities, then whole classes of felony murder, then states that haven't actually executed anyone in twenty years but keep the statute sitting there — and every one of those contractions gets sold as a step forward, including by people who want the thing gone. What gets me is that the smaller it gets, the harder it is to argue against. Once it's reserved for the worst offenders, the conversation shifts to the particular man and the particular thing he did, and of course he's the worst, he's the one we picked. A DA I talked to said he only seeks it where the case "screams for it," and I sat with that for a week before I realized it's perfectly circular: screams for it just means where he seeks it.

I've started seeing the same shape elsewhere — solitary confinement (only for the worst, and the worst are the ones we put in it), the way drug laws get pruned down to the hardest stuff, arguments about what counts as a civilized way to fight a war — so I don't think it's about the death penalty specifically. I've read the line of argument that says court supervision and procedural tinkering are what kept capital punishment alive, and I think that's adjacent but not it; that one is about making the machinery look careful, whereas what I'm after is the target getting smaller and the shrinking itself doing the legitimizing. And it isn't just reforms-can-backfire or institutions-adapt-to-survive, either — I want the specific mechanism where each contraction raises the apparent justification of whatever's left, so that the people doing the contracting are at once the institution's gravediggers and its life support.

So: does this have a name, or is it a gap? If there's an established concept, tell me which one fits and where it doesn't, and how it differs from the nearest neighbors — entrenchment, regulatory capture, gradualism, whatever the honest candidates actually are. If there isn't one, coin it, define it, and give me the edges: is it the same thing when the narrowing is done by opponents of the practice versus by its supporters, does it require that the institution survive, does it still count if the thing dies anyway, just later and quieter? I'd take a usable term, but honestly what I need is the carving. I have a character who votes for one of these contractions and genuinely believes he's helping, and I need to know exactly what he's doing, because right now I can only describe it and not name it, and the scene reads like I'm hedging.
