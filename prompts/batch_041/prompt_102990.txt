I run a small philosophy of mind reading group and I want to do a session on the knowledge argument / Mary's room, but everybody already knows the standard moves, so I want to build a decision tree that actually sorts people by what they think about the case rather than by which label they'd give themselves. Can you make it?

Start from the one commitment everyone in the group shares -- when Mary first sees red she gains something she didn't have before -- and branch out from there. Each node should be a question someone could answer about the case without already knowing which position it leads to. The distinctions I care about are these: is what she gains propositional at all; if it is, is it knowledge of a new fact or a new way of grasping an old fact; if it's an old fact, does grasping it that way require a special kind of concept that can't be cashed out in physical terms; and does the whole thing really turn on what Mary knows, or on whether physicalism is a thesis about facts or a thesis about concepts.

The leaves should include at least: type-A physicalism (she doesn't learn anything new, or only abilities), a posteriori / type-B physicalism with the phenomenal-concept move, the 'new knowledge of an old fact' view, property dualism, Russellian monism, illusionism or eliminativism about phenomenal properties, and a primitivist view where phenomenal concepts are unanalyzable and sui generis. Give one sentence on each leaf's most awkward commitment.

Build nodes around at least these cases, plus whatever else you need: Mary as a colourblind scientist whose colour vision is restored by surgery; a Mary who had colour vision until an accident left her blind in that respect, so the 'new' experience is a re-acquaintance; Mary's twin who was raised normally but never saw one particular shade; and a version where Mary has the experience but no concept for it (can she know what it's like without being able to think about it?).

Please flag any leaves the tree can't actually separate -- I suspect property dualism and Russellian monism might collapse on these cases, and maybe the a posteriori physicalist and the 'old fact, new concept' view too -- and note any branch combinations nobody occupies and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Keep it to about 8-10 nodes and no more than 8 leaves. And dont ask 'do you think physicalism is true?' anywhere; the whole point is that the answers to the cases do the sorting.
