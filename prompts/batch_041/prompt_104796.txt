I am a novelist who teaches an upper-level seminar on philosophy and fiction, and I am now writing the final examination paper. Please draft one essay question on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, pitched so that it cannot be answered by reciting the standard statement of the regress and the usual replies. The question must make students apply the regress to a case they will not have met in the secondary literature — the unity of a fictional character — and it must be answerable by reasoning alone, with no citations, reading lists or bibliography required.

The case I want them to work with is this. In my current manuscript a character is introduced not through any action, nor through any description of her body, but through a bare list of properties: she is impatient; she is meticulous; she is estranged from her brother; she is a lapsed botanist. A reader asks what makes these properties properties of one and the same person rather than a mere heap. For a real person we can appeal to the substance that bears them; for a fictional person there is no such bearer, only the sentences of the text.

The question should oblige students to say, first, whether the regress reappears in this fictional case or is somehow blocked, and second, whether the standard escapes — a primitive unity of the proposition, Frege's unsaturated concept, or a relational or trope-theoretic treatment of predication — can be carried over to fiction, or whether fiction demands a different account of what binds a character's properties together. It should separate students who understand why the regress is a problem about predication from those who have merely memorised its steps.

Give me the examination question, a model answer of no more than 650 words, and a marking scheme that names the specific moves an A answer must contain and the specific mistakes that should cost marks. Set the marking scheme out as a table giving the marks for each move and totalling 20. Write in British English, and keep the whole response under 1,300 words.
