Not sure if this has a name or if I'm just going in circles. The pattern: once I really know something -- not just believe it a lot, but actually know it, like I know I locked the door -- I feel entitled to brush off any argument against it, because if it's true then whatever they're saying must be flawed somewhere. Ok, fine. But the same move seems to let me brush off any argument no matter how good, so long as I'm sure enough, and even to treat the fact that someone's arguing against me as a sign that they're the one whos confused. And it scales with certainty: the more sure I am, the more I get to ignore, so perfect certainty would let me ignore everything. That can't be how it works, but I can't spot the bad step. Does this thing have a name? And what is it actually claiming -- that knowing licenses ignoring counter-evidence, or just that being sure does? I don't really care about the lable, I want the carving: what the thing is, how it differs from plain closed-mindedness or from the boring fact that confident people dig in, and where its edges are. If nothing fits, coin something and give me the boundary cases.
