I am a software engineer by trade — I work mainly on compilers and, more recently, on machine translation — and I have been reading philosophy in my spare time for several years, without any formal training in it. I have arrived at a set of positions that I hold fairly firmly, but I suspect they cannot all be true together, and I cannot see where the fault lines run. I would be grateful for a consistency audit.

My positions are as follows.

First, the indeterminacy of translation, as Quine sets it out. For a language with which we have had no prior contact, rival manuals of translation can each be reconciled with the entire body of evidence about how the speakers behave, and there is nothing further that settles which of them is correct. Whether the native's utterance gavagai means rabbit, an undetached rabbit-part, or a momentary rabbit-stage is not a matter of fact. It is not that we happen to be ignorant of the answer; there is no answer of the kind we imagine.

Second, meaning is use. What an expression means is constituted entirely by how it is used within a community. There is nothing to meaning over and above that use.

Third, my own words and thoughts have determinate content. It is an objective fact what my expressions mean. When I think about rabbits I am thinking about rabbits, and not about undetached parts or momentary stages, and this is so independently of anyone's convenience.

Fourth, a programming language has a determinate meaning fixed by its definition. There is a fact of the matter about what any given program does, and a compiler that produces the wrong behaviour is objectively wrong, whatever anyone's habits of use may be.

Fifth, naturalism. Everything that exists is physical or is a matter of behaviour; there are no abstract meanings or propositions hovering above the physical world.

Please tell me which of these are genuinely in tension with which, and which only appear to be in tension but are in fact compatible. For each genuine tension, I would like to know how serious it is and what the least costly revision would be; please order the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally bad. I would also like to know whether there is a single revision that would resolve the greatest number of them at once, and what that revision would cost me elsewhere. I am more interested in tracing what commits me to what than in a general survey of the subject, so please keep the discussion tied to the specific positions above. Kindly write your answer in British English.
