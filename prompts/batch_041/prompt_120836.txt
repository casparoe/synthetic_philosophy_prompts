I am preparing a graduate seminar on Moore's paradox and want your credence in a precise interpretive claim, with the reasoning decomposed rather than surveyed. The claim is this: the absurdity of asserting a sentence of the form p but I do not believe that p is fully explained by a norm internal to believing as such, and not by a semantic defect in the sentence, a merely pragmatic clash with assertion, or a norm requiring knowledge for assertion. Give me your probability that the claim is true. Then decompose it into at least these sub-questions, with a rough probability and one line of justification for each: whether such sentences have a truth-value at all; whether the first-person present-tense form is essential, or whether the absurdity survives in third-person and past-tense analogues; whether the best explanation appeals to a constitutive aim or norm of belief; whether a knowledge requirement on assertion can do all the explanatory work by itself; whether credence analogues, such as asserting p while avowing a credence below 0.5 in p, are genuinely Moore-paradoxical or merely odd; and whether the phenomenon is better treated as a constraint on how rational agents may attribute beliefs to themselves than on belief as such. Show how the sub-probabilities combine into your final number, identify which sub-question the number is most sensitive to, and say what argument or evidence would move it most. Take a definite position and defend it; do not merely lay out the options and leave the verdict to me. Write in British English.
