Here's the practice I keep chewing on. The standard recipe for making a chat model right now goes: pretrain it on a giant pile of text, then fine-tune it on human ratings of which responses are better (RLHF), then at deployment prompt it to work through problems step by step on a scratchpad, and hand it tools -- a calculator, a browser, a code interpreter. That whole pipeline is itself a practice, and it bugs me because the field seems to run on two pictures of the mind at once. On one side everyone says the thing is just next-token prediction, a stochastic parrot, a pattern completer: you show it examples and it imitates, you reward it and its outputs shift. On the other side the same people carefully give it a scratchpad so it can reason, and bolt tools onto it so it can extend its own cognition.

So what does this practice presuppose about what kind of creature the model is? Reconstruct the implicit theory of mind. Is the training pipeline built on a Skinnerian picture -- reinforcement, shaping, stimulus-response -- while the deployment pipeline quietly assumes a Popperian one, a creature that can let its hypotheses die in its stead? And does handing the thing tools make it a Gregorian creature, or does that need something more -- actually folding the tool into its own thinking rather than just calling it and reading back the output?

Be careful to seperate what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with; alot of this is probably underdetermined and I want you to say where it is. Tell me which rival views it rules out too -- does it rule out a purely Darwinian, hardwired picture, and does it rule out there being a real inner reasoner at all?
