I'm giving a workshop paper defending a causal answer to the special composition question — the question of when some things together compose a further thing. My proposal: a plurality composes a whole exactly when the plurality, as such, constitutes a single unified locus of causal-explanatory difference-making, that is, when it can be cited as one cause, or as the bearer of one causal power, rather than merely as several things jointly involved in some process. The appeal, as I'll argue, is that this is non-arbitrary and world-involving: it avoids the over-generation of unrestricted composition and the implausibility of nihilism without reducing composition to a matter of our descriptive interests. Prepare my Q&A prep sheet for the audience I will actually face — a metaphysics workshop that is roughly a third neo-Aristotelian hylomorphists, who hold that genuine unity requires a substantial form; a third philosophers of physics, who are deflationary about composition and dubious that causation is fundamental; and one or two formal ontologists, who work on the logic of parthood and care most about transitivity, extensionality, and whether composition can be vague. Give me the eight or so objections I will really get, ordered by how damaging they are, each phrased in the vocabulary of whoever would raise it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft; tell me what to concede up front; and include the one question I am hoping nobody asks.
