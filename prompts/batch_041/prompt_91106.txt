I am convening an adversarial collaboration for a graduate seminar on the underdetermination of theory by evidence, and I want the joint statement the two parties could put their names to. The first holds that underdetermination is a local and transient feature of immature science, which further evidence and further ingenuity dissolve, so that it never threatens inference to the best explanation; the second holds that for any theory a rival can be constructed that agrees with it on every possible observation, so that theory choice is permanently underdetermined and agnosticism about unobservables is rationally required. They have now argued for a term without meeting.

Write the document. Give me a numbered list of claims both can sign — which formulations of the thesis they agree are well-formed and non-trivial, what they agree the evidence does and does not fix, and what neither of them is asserting. Then the residual disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept, marked as verbal or substantive with reasons. Then the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one party, and a table giving, for each crux, the case or argument both agree bears on it and each side's prediction about how it comes out.

Two items belong in the agreed section. First, both parties must address a constructed case: two theories that agree on every actual and every possible observation but differ over whether some unobservable quantity is continuous or discrete, with no observation able to distinguish them; they must say whether this is a genuine pair of rivals or one theory written down twice. Second, they must separate the reading on which a rival is actually constructed and compared from the reading on which the rival is merely unconceived, and say which, if either, the argument for permanent underdetermination establishes. My own hunch is that one crux is whether empirical equivalence relates theories or formulations of them, and that another is whether the demand that evidence alone determine theory choice is a substantive epistemic norm rather than a stipulation; have them say whether they accept these as cruxes.
