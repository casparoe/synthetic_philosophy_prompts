I am a graduate student working on the timing of conscious experience, and I am trying to find out whether a thing I keep running into has a name. I will describe it, because if there is a name I suspect it leads somewhere I have not thought to look.

Take a screen on which a single dot is briefly shown at one position and then, a moment later, a dot of a different colour is briefly shown at another position. With the timing and spacing set correctly, observers do not report two dots. They report one dot travelling from the first position to the second, and — this is the part I cannot get past — they report that it was the first colour for the early part of the journey and the second colour for the later part, the change happening somewhere in the middle of the path. The display is over before the second colour could have been seen; yet the report places the colour change at a moment earlier than the second stimulus, and places it at a location along the path where nothing was ever shown.

The illusion itself is charming, but what I want a name for is the shape of the only explanations I can construct. Story one: the visual system did not deliver an experience until both stimuli had arrived, then assembled a single continuous episode with the colour change already written into the middle, and delivered that finished product as something that had been unfolding all along. On this story the editing happened before the experience existed, and the experience was never anything other than the edited version. Story two: the experience unfolded as it came, with whatever colour and motion were really there at each moment, and the editing happened afterwards, to the memory; by the time I am asked, the stored record has been rewritten into a story of one dot changing colour halfway. In both stories the subject is the last to know and cannot tell the difference from the inside, and it looks to me as though no measurement of anything the subject does or says will separate them, because the report is itself part of whichever record got edited.

I keep finding the same fork elsewhere. A few taps near the wrist, followed a moment later by a few taps near the elbow, are felt as an evenly spaced series of taps hopping up the arm, including taps at places that were never touched — again the later stimuli seem to reach back and fill in the earlier part of the sequence, and again both stories can be told. And a stimulus shown very briefly and then immediately followed by a second pattern is often reported as not having been seen at all, although it evidently registered in the person somehow; I can tell the same two stories about that case as well, one in which it never made it into experience and one in which it did and was then struck from the record. If the fork covers that third case too, I begin to worry that it is not one thing at all but just the generic fact that any report is a reconstruction, and that worry is part of what I want you to address.
