For a graduate seminar handout on antinatalism, I want a decision tree that starts from the four claims that set up Benatar's asymmetry — that the presence of pain is bad, that the presence of pleasure is good, that the absence of pain is good even where no one enjoys that good, and that the absence of pleasure is not bad unless someone is deprived of it — and leads to a named position on whether procreation is permissible. Branch on the disputes that actually do the work: whether the third claim is a claim about impersonal value or only about what it would be fitting to regret, whether nonexistence can be better or worse for anyone at all (comparativism), whether the asymmetry belongs at the axiological or the deontic level, and whether the antinatalist conclusion needs the further premise that every life contains some harm. Leaves should include the standard asymmetry plus antinatalism, the asymmetry accepted with procreation still permitted, the symmetry view on which the absence of pleasure is bad, the neutralist view on which both absences are neither good nor bad so only the states of actual people count, and the reading on which the asymmetry is a fact about our attitudes rather than about value. Mark which combinations of answers leave a leaf nobody occupies, say whether each is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and flag any two positions the tree cannot separate.
