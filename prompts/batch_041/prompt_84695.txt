I am a philosopher of science, and I have agreed to sit on a public panel on pseudohistory and the limits of historical inference. One of the other speakers defends the historicity of Atlantis on the strength of a single maxim — that absence of evidence is not evidence of absence — which, when pressed, he glosses as follows: for events so remote, whatever evidence once existed would long since have perished, so its absence tells us nothing at all. I want a menu of the distinct theses that maxim could be expressing, ordered from the trivial to the radical, with an explicit verdict on each: which are tautologies, which are substantive and defensible, which are simply false, and which, if any, actually license his conclusion. I also want to know at what point along that menu the maxim stops being a piece of epistemic hygiene and becomes a shield against any conceivable discovery, and what that transition implies for the demarcation problem. Please lay the readings out as a set of options rather than settling on one.
