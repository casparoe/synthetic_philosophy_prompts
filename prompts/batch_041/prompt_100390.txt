I've been reading Dennett latley and I've basically bought his line on the color phi phenomenon. You flash a red dot, then a blue dot a bit later and somewhere else, and people report seeing one dot move across and change color about halfway. Dennett's point is that there are two stories you could tell about this. The Orwellian one: the experience really happened, with the color change at some particular moment, and then memory got rewritten after the fact (like Winston rewriting the past -- the original was real, the record was falsified). The Stalinesque one: the show trial is staged before the experience is ever broadcast, so the color change was never experienced at the "wrong" time in the first place -- the revision happens upstream of consciousness. Dennett says no possible experiment could distinguish these, and that this isn't just a limit on our instruments: it means the question of when the color change was experienced is ill-posed, because there's no finish line in the brain where the order of arrival becomes the order of experience. That's the position. I want to know what would count against it.

I'm not interested in the generic "but qualia" complaint. I want the specific things that should actually move me if I'm taking this seriously. For instance: does the color phi effect actually need any postdictive revision at all, or is it explicable by boring low-level mechanisms (lateral inhibition, motion extrapolation, whatever) in a way that leaves Dennett's conclusion untouched but also unsupported? Is the Orwellian/Stalinesque pair even exhaustive, or is there a third reading -- say, that the experience is genuinely indeterminate or gappy and the report is a retrospective confabulation -- that Dennett would have to either accept or rule out? And what would it take for "no fact of the matter" to be false: a single case where the two readings make different predictions about something independantly measurable (reaction times, confidence, neural timing, memory consolidation), or a principled argument that the distinction is coherent even if undetectable?

I'd personally be most moved by a case where Orwellian and Stalinesque make different predictions about something we can actually measure, or by a demonstration that the color phi case is just a low-level artifact that never needed the heavy philosophical machinery.
