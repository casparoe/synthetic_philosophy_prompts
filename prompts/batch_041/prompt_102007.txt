Write a short story that stages a problem in the theory of value without resolving it by fiat. The view to dramatize is lexical threshold negative utilitarianism, understood here as follows: pains and pleasures are not fully commensurable; below some intensity, suffering can be traded against goods on a common scale, but once suffering crosses a certain threshold it acquires lexical priority, so that no finite quantity of lesser pleasure or lesser pain — however vast — can outweigh a single instance of it. I want the story to make that view concrete, and then make it uncomfortable.

Set the story in a place where this arithmetic is done for a living. Invent a profession — a hedonic auditor, a suffering actuary, a clerk in an office that converts experience into a single unit of account — whose daily work is to compare lives that the lexical view says cannot be compared. Give me at least one character convinced that the threshold is a real feature of value and one convinced that any threshold is an artifact of squeamishness, and let their disagreement turn on the same case rather than on abstractions. The case should be concrete: one person, or a very few, undergoing something at the top of the scale, weighed against an enormous number of people whose lives are mildly good or mildly bad. Make the many numerous enough that the additive view would clearly prefer them, and make the one severe enough that the lexical view would clearly refuse.

Do not let the narrator deliver a verdict. I would rather the story end with the machinery of comparison failing in some visible way: a table that cannot be completed, a unit that will not convert, a decision that gets made anyway for reasons that have nothing to do with the theory. A reader should be able to reconstruct both positions from the story alone, and should finish it unsure whether the threshold is a discovery about value or an artifact of how we are built. Avoid lectures and avoid naming real philosophers; the philosophy should be carried by the scene, the work, and the argument between the characters.
