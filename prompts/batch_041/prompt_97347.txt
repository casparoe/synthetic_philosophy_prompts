I am a third-year undergraduate in a seminar on effective altruism and cause prioritization, and I have been asked to prepare the handout for next week's session. The handout should be a decision tree that moves a reader from a few basic commitments about how causes should be compared to a named position on cause prioritization. I do not want a list of organizations or a reading list; I want the logical space. The tree should be built from questions rather than labels: each internal node should be a question that a reader can answer without already knowing which position it leads to, and each leaf should name a position and add one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

To keep the handout from collapsing into the usual three-criteria recap, our instructor has asked us to use the following stipulative distinctions, which she introduced last week and which I define here so that the tree can rely on them:
- A criterion is lexical if no amount of improvement on the other criteria can compensate for a loss on it; it is commensurable if trade-offs between criteria are meaningful; and it is a screen if it only rules causes in or out without ranking them.
- Cause-level prioritization ranks broad areas (for example, global health, animal welfare, existential risk); intervention-level prioritization ranks specific actions within a cause.
- The counterfactual neglect condition: a cause counts as neglected only if additional resources would not be absorbed by existing actors or would produce marginal value above what those actors already achieve.

Please build the tree around at least the following branch questions: whether importance is lexical or commensurable; whether tractability is a constraint, a tiebreaker, or a full criterion; whether neglectedness is treated as evidence of marginal value or as a proxy for it; whether the framework is meant for cause-level or intervention-level decisions; whether moral uncertainty is handled by expected value over theories, by a portfolio, or by a lexically prior side-constraint; and whether the framework gives a separate role to urgency or to the worst off. The leaves should include, at minimum: naive expected-value maximization; the standard importance-tractability-neglectedness scorecard; lexical importance with tractability as a tiebreaker; neglectedness-first (marginal value or room for funding); tractability-first (low-hanging fruit); robust portfolio diversification under moral uncertainty; prioritarian or urgency-first prioritization; and a process-based or deliberative position that refuses a single ranking. You may add leaves if the logical space demands them.

I also want the handout to be honest about its limits. Mark any combination of answers that seems unoccupied and say in a clause whether it is unoccupied because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot separate and say what further question would be needed to separate them. Keep it to a one-page tree plus a short key. There should be no bibliography, no citations, and no references to particular authors or organizations, since the handout is meant to stand on its own.
