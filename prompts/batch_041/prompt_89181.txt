I am convening a reading group on Milton and the early modern controversy over grace and free will, and I should like a reading of Paradise Lost through the lens of Molinism. Molina's position turns on a third kind of divine knowledge, middle knowledge, distinct from God's natural knowledge of necessary truths and from his free knowledge of what he has decreed: it is his knowledge, prior to any decree, of what each possible free creature would freely choose in any set of circumstances it might be placed in. Because counterfactuals of creaturely freedom are true independently of God's will, Molinism promises that God can secure the outcome of history by actualising just those circumstances in which his creatures freely do what he intends, without overriding anyone's freedom; by the same token, some worlds are not feasible for God to actualise, and if every circumstance open to a creature is one in which it would freely sin — what Plantinga calls transworld depravity — then no arrangement of circumstances can be relied upon. I want to know whether Milton's poem needs this apparatus, supplies it, or quietly refuses it. Milton's God defends himself with the claim that foreknowledge does not compel, a weaker and more contested move than middle knowledge, and it is not obvious that it answers anything. Yet the drama keeps turning on circumstance: the decision to garden apart, the dream whispered into Eve's ear, the serpent finding her alone, and afterwards Adam's complaint that he never asked to be made and might have been made otherwise. Does the poem tacitly assume that God has surveyed every counterfactual and chosen among feasible worlds, with Satan doing the engineering of circumstances inside the world God actualised? Or does its insistence that the Fall was genuinely contingent sit badly with any scheme in which the outcome was already certain under those circumstances? Tell me where Molinism illuminates a scene or a stretch of reasoning, where the poem resists it and the honest critic must say so, and what the poem's handling of freedom adds that the Molinist argument on its own cannot. Please write in British English, and there is no need to rehearse the general debate about Milton's theodicy except where it bears on the question.
