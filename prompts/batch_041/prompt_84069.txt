Two friends of mine, Nadia and Ellis, got into it at dinner and I got drafted as referee and had no idea what to say. Setup: six years ago Ellis was in a bad place and asked Nadia to promise never to repeat a story about Ellis's family. Nadia promised. Ellis has basically forgotten the promise even exists, and has told the story to plenty of people himself since. Last week it came up again, and telling it would actually help a third friend of ours, Jo, who's dealing with something similar. Ellis said just tell her, I don't care, I've told it myself. Nadia said she still can't, at least not without Ellis actually releasing her from it, and that "I don't care" is not the same as releasing. There was alot of back and forth; it settled into this:

1. Ellis: the whole point of that promise was to protect me. I'm the only one with anything at stake and I've got nothing at stake anymore. A promise that protects nobody is just a superstition.
2. Nadia: what I did wasn't a pledge to make you happier, it was handing you the say over whether the story gets told. You still have that say, but you have to actually use it. If promises dissolved whenever the promisee stopped caring, every promise would ride on the promisee's mood, and the whole point of getting someone to promise is to get it off moods.
3. Ellis: fine, then here's me using it, right now: tell her. Done.
4. Nadia: but which is it — "I release you," or "I don't care"? If it's the second and you change your mind next month, where does that leave me? And I think something survives even if you do release me: I gave my word, I'd like to still be someone whose word counts for something, and you releasing me doesn't change that I made the promise.
5. Ellis: that last one is you making it about you. If you're keeping quiet for the sake of your own self-image as a keeper of promises, that isn't a reason about me at all, and it's a bit much to call it a duty you owe me.

Go through this move by move. For each one, give it a 0-4 (0 = flat wrong, 2 = a real point but overstated, 4 = right as far as it goes) and one line saying why, then say what each of them is missing. Then the actual question: once Ellis has said "I don't care," does Nadia have any reason left not to tell Jo the story — and if there's a reason left, is it a reason owed to Ellis or only a reason about herself? I care about the verdict on this case, not a survey of the general theory. Last line exactly: \answer{...} with just one of "still binds", "released", or "weakly binds".
