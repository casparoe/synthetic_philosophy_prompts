I want the specification before I read any more of the literature on psychiatric classification: what would an adequate theory of it have to deliver? Give me the requirements, each with the case or consideration behind it — what makes a diagnosis correct rather than merely useful; what the theory must say about the people who change once the category exists and then change the category in turn; how it accommodates the same distress being sorted differently in different cultures; how it keeps categories reliable enough for research and for clinics alike. Accommodate one further case for me: a man who seeks out a label, finds that it organizes his life, and whose symptoms afterward fit the checklist more tightly than they did before — is he evidence for the category, an artifact of it, or both? Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are commitments of a particular camp, and say whether any two of them cannot be jointly satisfied; I suspect that demanding that a category track a real disorder and demanding that it survive the reshaping of the people it classifies will collide, but show me if I am wrong. End with a brief verdict on how the descriptive, manual-based approach and the harmful-dysfunction approach fare against your list.
