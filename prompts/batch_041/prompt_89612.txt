I've got two colleagues in my department who've been going at each other for years about the trolley cases, and I want to force them to actually write something together instead of sniping at each other in seminars. One of them thinks the pattern across the cases -- flip the switch, don't shove the fat man, don't cut up the one to save the five -- is at bottom the doing/allowing distinction: it's about whether you bring the harm about or merely let it happen. The other thinks that's a dead end and that what's really doing the work is the intent/foresight distinction from double effect, i.e. whether the harm is aimed at as a means or only foreseen as a side effect. They keep talking past each other because they keep reaching for different cases.

Write the joint statement the two of them could both sign. I want the claims they can agree on (including about the standard cases, and about what each distinction even amounts to); the precise remaining disagreement, with each side's position worded so that side would accept the wording; the cruxes -- and I suspect one is a case where the harm is done but only foreseen, and another where the harm is merely allowed but intended; and for each crux a concrete case both of them would grant bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Number the agreed claims, then put the disagreements in a table. Also flag any stretch of their fight that's just verbal -- same words, different sense -- and say which bit.

And dont just hand me a menu of positions. Tell me which distinction you think actually carries the explanatory weight and why, and where you'd bet the crux cases come out.
