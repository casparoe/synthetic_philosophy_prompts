Consider a patient who, while competent, executes an advance directive refusing life-sustaining treatment should she develop a progressive dementia, and who appends a self-sealing clause: should she later ask to be treated, that request is to be read not as a change of mind but as confirmation that the directive's triggering condition has been met. Years later the diagnosis arrives; she is articulate and oriented, reports being content, has formed new attachments, and now asks for treatment. Write an essay on whether the directive should govern. Weigh the case for honoring her earlier, reflective judgment about the shape of her life against the case for deferring to the person now before you, and defend a verdict — including a verdict on whether a self-sealing clause can bind at all. Make explicit the conception of personal identity and the conception of autonomy your conclusion presupposes, and say what would have to be true for the opposing verdict to be the correct one.
