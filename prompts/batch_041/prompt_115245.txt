I'm a software engineer — data compression and pipelines, with some model deployment on the side — and I have no formal training in philosophy. I'd like you to translate a position into the vocabulary I actually think in, and then tell me what the translation loses.

The position is the one I associate with Dennett, though I may be mangling the attribution: that there are three stances one can take towards a system — physical, design, intentional — and that the patterns a stance picks out can be genuinely real rather than convenient fictions, with their reality cashed out in something like compressibility. As I understand it, the thought is that a pattern is there in the data if a description that exploits it comes out shorter than the plain record of the data, whether or not anyone has actually found that description.

First, state that position in your own words, as faithfully as you can; I won't be reading the originals, so please don't send me to them. Then render it in the terms I work in: description length and codecs, lossless versus lossy compression, model classes and hypothesis selection, null models and baselines, abstraction layers and the interfaces between them, the bias-variance trade-off, structural typing, and policies that are optimal for some reward function.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts survive the translation intact? Which survive only with distortion, and what exactly has to be bent to make them fit? Which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all?

I am suspicious of three things in particular, and I would rather you address them head-on than in passing.

1. A description is only shorter than another relative to a model class and a corpus, and my baselines are never the raw bits — every codec already presupposes a representation. Is the compressibility criterion doing real work here, or is it quietly hiding a choice of baseline?

2. The word stance suggests something an agent adopts, optionally, for a reason, whereas model selection in my world is just an operation, with nobody performing it and no purpose attached. Is that pragmatic, agent-relative element load-bearing, or is it a layer my vocabulary simply cannot express? Relatedly, the intentional stance's beliefs and desires have no obvious counterpart for me; the nearest thing I have is a policy that is optimal for some reward function, which seems to concede considerably more than the original claim does.

3. If a pattern is real when the description is shorter, and descriptions are shorter for us only because of the particular limits on our time and memory, does the view collapse into the claim that the best pattern is the best codec we can currently afford — instrumentalism under another name — or is there a fact about which patterns are real that does not depend on our budgets?

Finish by saying whether the residue, taken as a whole, points to a gap in my vocabulary or to the original position being less determinate than its reputation suggests. Give me a verdict rather than a survey.
