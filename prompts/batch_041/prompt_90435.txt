Right, so I'm trying to get my head round Dennett's ladder of creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — and I keep feeling like I've only got the fridge-magnet version. The standard gloss is: Darwinian creatures are hard-wired by natural selection; Skinnerian ones get to learn from their own reward history; Popperian ones can run a hypothesis inside their heads and let the hypothesis be the thing that dies instead of them; and Gregorian ones take on board the tools, notations and habits of the minds around them. Fine, I've got that.

What I can't get straight is what actually makes the Gregorian rung a rung, rather than just Popperian creatures with better kit. Two stories seem to be floating about and I suspect they're not the same story. One is the tower-of-generate-and-test one: each new level is built by the level below testing and selecting among the outputs of the level above, so you get a genuine cumulative hierarchy rather than just a list. The other is the scaffolding one: Gregorian creatures are simply the ones whose thinking is partly made of stuff that lives outside their skins — tools, notation, other people. On the second story it looks like you could be Gregorian without really being Popperian at all, just by inheriting the kit; on the first story that looks impossible.

So: explain what the Gregorian level is meant to add that the Popperian one doesn't already give you, and tell me which of those two stories the ladder actually needs to hold together. Then say what a creature would have to do to count as Gregorian-but-not-Popperian, and whether that's a coherent thing to be or just a sign that the taxonomy is quietly doing two different jobs at once. And finally, what would actually count as evidence that these levels are real joints rather than a handy sorting hat — not a reading list, just the kind of thing you'd look for.
