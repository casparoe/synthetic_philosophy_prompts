ok so i'm putting together a handout for a grad seminar on the ethics of pest control — insecticides, rodenticides, gene drives, culling programs, all of it — and i want a taxonomy of the positions laid out as a tree, where every branching point is the question that actually splits the view, not just a label people like to wear.

Each branch should be labeled by a commitment you can state as a yes/no question, phrased so someone can answer it without already knowing which position it leads to. Something like: does the welfare of the arthropod count at all, or only the human benefit? Is it the suffering that's bad, or the killing? Does it matter whether the pest is native or invasive, or is that irrelevant to its moral status? Does the sheer number of them — billions — multiply the stakes, or does it just make us notice? Do the pesticide's effects on non-target insects (bees, predators, the whole food web) go in the same ledger as the pest's death, or a separate one? And does uncertainty about whether insects are even sentient push you toward caution or toward shrugging it off?

I also want one branch on a distinction i keep running into: whether 'pest' is a relational category (a thing is a pest only relative to some human project, so the real moral question is about the project) or an intrinsic one (there's something about the organism itself that makes killing it the thing to justify). Work that in somewhere.

Then put the actual positions at the leaves — extensionist welfarism, ecological holism, plain anthropocentrism, a precautionary middle, the 'insects probably arent sentient so none of this is even a moral question' view, and whatever else the space throws up. For each leaf, give the name (or 'unnamed' if nobody really defends it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Three distinctions i care about most, so make sure they're load-bearing: (1) suffering vs death as the thing that matters, (2) whether scale is a multiplier on the stakes or just a salience effect, and (3) whether collateral damage to non-targets is folded into the same moral arithmetic as the pest's death.

Dont paper over cases where two leaves end up with the same practical upshot — flag them as ones the tree cant separate. And for the answer-combinations nobody occupies, say whether that's because the view is incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. Aim for around four branching levels and somewhere in the 8-12 leaf range, tight enough to fit on two sides of a handout.
