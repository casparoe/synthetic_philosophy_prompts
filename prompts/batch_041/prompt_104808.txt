I cover the forecasting beat, and for three years I have been reporting on a dispute that has hardened into ritual: the analysts I call the Actuaries, who treat the fates of great powers as draws from a reference class and reason from base rates and hazard curves, and the analysts I call the Chroniclers, who insist that each power's predicament is a singular historical configuration and that no outside view of empires can amount to more than numerology. In public they talk past each other; in private they concede more than either will admit. I want to convene an adversarial collaboration and publish the statement both camps could put their names to.

Two constraints on the exercise. First, the collaboration must work from a common artifact rather than from rival memories of the historical record. I have assembled what I call the Ledger: forty stipulated episodes of great-power decline, each with a power, a peak year, a decline onset, an end year or a censoring year if the power is still standing, and a categorical outcome — absorption, fragmentation, reconstitution, or persistence. Treat the Ledger as the only data. Where it is silent, say so, rather than supplying outside cases to fill the gap. Second, one side has proposed summarising the Ledger with a single number it calls the decline half-life; whether that statistic is even well defined once censored episodes are included should be treated as an open question, not a premise.

Write the statement both camps could sign. Give the numbered claims they jointly accept — about what the Ledger does and does not show, about what the reference-class problem does and does not undermine, about which of their differences survive once made precise. State the residual disagreements in wording each camp would accept as its own. Isolate the cruxes, the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side, and for each crux give the test, case, or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance. Say explicitly which disputes are verbal or artifacts of how I drew the reference class, and what, if anything, both sides agree would actually move the debate — including what each would count as evidence against its own position. Close with six live cases in which an incumbent power's share of world output has been flat or falling for a decade, and record each camp's forecast for each case before either has seen the other's.

After the statement, append a single JSON object as the machine-readable summary, with this schema: agreed_claims (array of strings); disagreements (array of objects with fields issue, actuarial_formulation, chronicler_formulation); cruxes (array of objects with fields id, question, kind — one of empirical, conceptual, or protocol_artifact — evidence, actuarial_prediction, chronicler_prediction); verbal_disputes (array of strings); joint_forecasts (array of objects with fields case, actuarial_forecast, chronicler_forecast).
