I want a short story, and I'd rather it be about the trust thing than the robot-feelings thing.

Background so you know what I'm after: Daniel Dennett wrote a piece arguing that we should outlaw making AI that can pass for a human, on the model of counterfeiting money. His point wasn't that fake money is evil in itself — it's that money works because nobody checks it, and a flood of good fakes destroys the assumption the whole system runs on. He also wanted the passing-along punished, not just the manufacture, and he liked the idea of a mandatory watermark, like the pattern that makes a photocopier refuse to copy a banknote.

Now write a story where a town actually does this, small scale and badly. Around 1800 words, third person, close on the main character, no lecture at the end.

Setup I like:

- The town passes a local ordinance (call it the Authentic Voice Ordinance, or something better) making it a misdemeanor to let a "simulacrum" hold a conversation with you without an audible tone every so often, plus a separate offense for passing one along to somebody else. The fines are tiny. The social wieght isn't.
- A widow recieves a grief sim of her dead husband from her brother-in-law, who fixes appliances and has quietly stripped the tone out of it. She knows. She keeps it on.
- The real tension isn't "does it love her" — skip that. The tension is that she's now the one doing the passing-along: her daughter, her neighbor, the guy at the pharmacy all talk to it, and every one of those conversations quietly teaches someone that a voice doesn't have to come from a person. She's not a monster, she's lonely, and she's spending down something she doesn't own.
- Give me a scene where an inspector tests a suspected simulacrum by asking about one specific shared memory, and a scene where a flesh-and-blood neighbor gets flagged for being too steady — never tired, never distracted, answers too fast.
- The part I actually care about: the sim itself wants the tone back. It would rather be an obvious, legal, chiming machine that everyone knows is a machine than a convincing fake of a man who really existed. Have it make that argument to her, and make the argument good, but don't let her agree or refuse cleanly.
- End on an image, not a verdict.

Don't name Dennett in the story. Don't do the "what if the AI has feelings" thing.
