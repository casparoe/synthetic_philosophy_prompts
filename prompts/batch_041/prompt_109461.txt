I run a small firm that installs home battery systems, and we are about to offer a ten-year warranty on a new model for which we have only about eighteen months of field data. Between the failures we have seen and engineering studies that disagree with one another, my honest estimate of the ten-year failure rate is somewhere between 2% and 15%, and I do not think the evidence supports narrowing it any further. Our actuary wants a single number for the warranty price and says to take the middle of the range, since a range is not a price. That bothers me for two reasons. We also buy insurance against the same failures, and I notice that I would refuse to write the warranty at a premium corresponding to 8% while also refusing to pay 8% to hand the risk off, and I cannot tell whether that is incoherence or just honesty about what I do not know. Separately, a colleague claims that several more years of field data might not narrow the range at all, and could even widen it. Which end of the range should drive which decision, is the actuary's advice defensible, and is my colleague right?
