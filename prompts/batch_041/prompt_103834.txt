I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about killing insects, going case by case on gut, and I want you to tell me what principle is doing the work here, or whether there's one at all.

Fine, no hesitation: spraying the wasp nest under the eaves by the back door, because the kids play right there and my youngest swells up badly enough that a sting is an ER trip. Putting out ant bait when they find the sugar. Dosing the dog with the flea stuff, the kind the vet says works because it's in her blood and anything that bites her dies — I know it doesn't stop at the flea, I know it comes out the other end, and I still do it. Fumigating the house for termites. Fogging the whole neighborhood during the dengue scare a couple summers back, and I'd have paid extra taxes for it; I didn't lose a minute of sleep over the bees.

Not fine: fogging the same streets this summer, no disease anywhere, just mosquitoes being mosquitoes. My neighbor's bug zapper, which I've privately decided is a small death camp for moths and beetles and the occasional bat — his yard, his money, I've never said a word, but I don't like it. Drowning slugs in a beer trap, even though I'll use the iron phosphate pellets without a second thought, and as far as I can tell those kill them just as dead, probably slower. And spraying the roses because the aphids made them look scruffy — did it once, felt bad about it for a week.

Here's the mud. I'll carry a spider out of the shower on a piece of card and put it on the windowsill, but I'll swat a moth in the bedroom without thinking, and the moth isn't biting me or eating anything, it's just flying around the lamp. I kill ticks on myself with real satisfaction and zero remainder. My friend who keeps bees kills the wasps raiding her hives and I think that's obviously fine, which sits oddly next to my neighbor's zapper. And my uncle plants a strip of ordinary corn alongside his engineered corn every year so the resistant bugs have something to mate with, which I gather is the entire trick, and I think that's clever rather than sinister.

My tentative principle: it's fine when a human interest is genuinely at stake — health, food, shelter — and not fine when the only thing at stake is that something looks worse or is mildly irritating. But that makes the ant bait shaky, forbids the rose spray I already regret, and says nothing about why drowning a slug is worse than poisoning it when the outcome is the same. Meanwhile my entomologist friend keeps saying there's no such thing as a pest, only a number — same species is a pollinator at low density and a plague at high density, and pest is just the word for when the number crosses a line in our favor. I can't tell if that's a deep point or a pun.

Also tell me whether your principle makes non-lethal stuff trivially fine, because if driving insects off to starve somewhere else counts as free, that seems like a bug.
