My friend Dana is finishing an econ PhD and has gotten really into the macro-history books everyone argues about — Sapiens, Guns Germs and Steel, The Dawn of Everything — and over lunch last week she gave me her grand unified theory of all of them. She reads a book a week and I've read none of these, so I mostly nodded along, but something about it felt off and I couldn't say what. I want to know how much of it I should actually believe before I repeat any of it to other people.

Her pitch, more or less: these books are all the same book with different villains. Diamond says Europe won because of geography — better crops, better animals, the east-west axis, done. Harari says it's because we learned to believe in fictions, so that strangers could cooperate in huge numbers. Graeber and Wengrow say it's agriculture: farming was the trap, you store grain, you get chiefs, then cities, then states, then inequality. The only real disagreement between the three, she says, is whether the trap was inevitable or an accident we could have avoided. She thinks the one genuinely new thing in Dawn of Everything is the argument that the noble savage was a European fantasy — that the whole romantic idea that indigenous people had it better was just colonizers projecting, so we should stop treating those critiques as serious. And she says the critics have basically killed the genre anyway: historians keep pointing out that these guys cherry-pick their evidence and that the whole thing is unfalsifiable storytelling, which she takes as a refutation. She calls all of it "big history," which I suspect is a real term she's using loosely.

Can you go through her pitch claim by claim and tell me which parts each of these authors would accept as a fair gloss on their own view, which parts they'd wince at, and which parts are just wrong? I'm inclined to think the critics' point is the strong one — I genuinely don't see how you'd test any of this — but I dont trust that instinct, and I'd like to know which specific criticisms land and which are just the standard complaint people make about popular synthesis books. If some of what she says is accurate but her conclusion still doesn't follow from it, say that too. And give me something I can actually say back to her, because she'll want an argument, not a vibe.
