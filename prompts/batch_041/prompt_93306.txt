I've been chewing on this one for weeks and my group chat is officially sick of it, so I'm outsourcing. I'm pretty sold on Nozick's entitlement theory — the Wilt Chamberlain argument is what did it for me, the whole point that any patterned distribution just gets undone by a million voluntary trades and the only way to hold the pattern in place is to keep interfering, which is basically a full-time tax bureaucracy. But I keep hitting the same wall: the theory is supposed to be historical, justice turns on the actual chain of acquisition and transfer, and every time you trace a real chain it turns out to be a mess. So I want you to do the Parthenon/Elgin Marbles. You know the outline — removed from Athens to London in the early 1800s under Lord Elgin, in the British Museum ever since, Greece wants them back, the BM says it holds them legally and that they belong in a universal museum in London. Please do NOT get bogged down in whether the Ottoman permit was a genuine sale or a coerced removal; thats exactly the thing I want you to leave unresolved, because the interesting question is what the theory does when that question is undecidable.

So run the whole thing through Nozick. By the theory's lights, who holds the marbles justly, and which facts does that actually turn on? Here's the crux I care about: is the Greek claim a *patterned* claim — cultural property belongs with the culture that made it — in which case the Chamberlain argument says it's the kind of thing that can't ground a stable just holding and would need continuous interference to enforce, so the theory shrugs it off? Or is it a *rectification* claim — the original taking was unjust, so justice positively demands return? Those two cut in completely different directions and I suspect half the argument online is people talking past each other because they haven't noticed which one they're actually making. Then the part I really want: does the case poke a hole in the theory? Nozick says next to nothing about how rectification is supposed to work, and Chamberlain is all about transfer, not acquisition — so tell me where the theory goes silent or starts hand-waving, and whether this case confirms the entitlement picture or shows it was never usable for the hard cases, the ones where the wrong is obviously real but the chain is hopelessly tangled. Be honest about where the theory actually speaks vs where your just filling in.
