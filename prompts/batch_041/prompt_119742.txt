You are a philosopher who works on toleration and free expression, and who is unusually good at the difficult craft of working out what implicit rule a person is following from a run of their individual decisions. I have been a member of a large, long-standing public discussion forum for about a decade, and I help moderate one of its quieter sections, so I have a fairly clear picture of how its head moderator rules on things. I have also been thinking a good deal about what people call the paradox of tolerance. Please think this through carefully, and before you commit to an answer, lay out for me the several rules that could each explain everything I am about to describe.

The moderator, Nadia, has never written her principles down, but her record over the years runs roughly as follows. A long thread arguing that the moon landings were faked stayed up, because she says she does not rule on the truth of what members say. A post that named a member's employer and home town and invited readers to pay him a visit came down, and the poster was banned. A member whose own posts were perfectly civil, but who organised a campaign of mass complaints to have three other members thrown off over a personal grudge, was banned as well. A careful, closely argued essay claiming that parliamentary democracy is overrated and that an unelected council of experts would govern the country better stayed up. A member of a small religious movement which, on his own account, shuns and expels anyone who dissents from its leader, posted an invitation to one of its meetings, and that stayed up too. A thread mocking another member's prose style, harsh but not threatening, stayed up, with a note that she does not police tone. And a member who argued politely that the forum's politics section had become poisonous and should be closed altogether, and who started a petition to that end, was left alone.

The new case is this. A group calling themselves the Clean Slate has posted a long, calm, closely argued case that the forum should adopt a rule permanently barring one particular family of views from being argued here at all, and they say that once the rule passes, those views will simply have no place in the forum's deliberations. They name no one, threaten no one, break no existing rule, say they will not use complaint campaigns against anybody, propose to do it by the ordinary route of petition and a vote of the whole membership, and say they will accept the outcome if they lose.

What will Nadia do with this, and what does her record actually commit her to? I am less interested in the verdict than in the reasoning: which rules fit everything she has done so far, which of those rules the Clean Slate case pulls apart, which of her earlier rulings is the closest analogue, and what your best guess at her ruling is and why. If her record genuinely underdetermines the answer, tell me what principle she would be taking on with each possible ruling. Finally, name the single further case you would put to her next, the one that would most cleanly separate whichever rules survive. Please write in British English, and do not hedge your way out of giving an actual prediction.
