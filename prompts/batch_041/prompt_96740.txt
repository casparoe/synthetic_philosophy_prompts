I'm writing the final paper for a third-year module on the metaphysics of social kinds — mostly race, gender and money — and the essay question is giving me grief. Every year they hand in the same script: Searle on how a bit of paper becomes money, Hacking's looping effects for race, Haslanger for gender, then a paragraph insisting that constructed things are still real. It's a recitation, not an essay, and that's my fault for setting a question they can answer off the lecture slides. So I want this year's question built around a case that isn't money or race or gender, where they actually have to work out which of the two things I've been calling "socially constructed" is doing the work. The case is the key worker lists from the pandemic — the ones that decided whose children could keep going to school and who got first go at the tests. What I want the question to force is: (i) a defended verdict on whether "key worker" is a socially constructed kind in the constitutive sense (money, where the kind exists because we collectively accept it), or only in the causal sense (where social processes bring the category into being and shape the people who fall under it), or both; (ii) an account of what abolishing the category would amount to on each of those readings — I want them to notice that on one reading you'd be withdrawing entitlements and on the other you'd leave the work and the labour market exactly where they were; and (iii) something on whether the case breaks Searle's model, given that the list was handed down by a government rather than accepted by the population, and whether the lobbying to get onto the list, and people starting to describe themselves as key workers, changes the verdict. If there's a tempting wrong move that looks like it's doing the work but isn't — something that lets them skip (i) altogether — I'd rather it got built into the marking scheme than into the question itself.

Could you write me the question (one essay, 20 marks, they'll have about 45 minutes and I'll tell them roughly 700 words), a model answer of about 700 words, and the marking scheme? For the scheme: five must-hit points, the marks each is worth out of 20, a one-line note per point on the mistake that usually costs students the marks, and a sentence at the end on what seperates a first from a 2:1. British English throughout please — it goes straight into the handbook, and I've already been told off once for spelling it behaviour. Ta.
