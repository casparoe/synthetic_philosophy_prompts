Write a deadpan buyer's guide, in the house style of a consumer-testing magazine, that reviews the physical stance, the design stance, and the intentional stance as though they were three rival products a shopper could buy, rate, and return. Invent the outlet and its conventions — headline, subhead, a how-we-tested box, a comparison table, star ratings, a verdict paragraph, and a brief reader-mail column — and aim for roughly 800 words.

The humor must come from the philosophy, not from the conceit. The reviewer is a competent, faintly pompous instrument-tester who has plainly absorbed the literature and has no interest in settling it; nothing in the piece should read as a lecture or a summary. Handle the following correctly, as a specialist would expect: the three stances are nested rather than rival products, since anything predictable at the design stance is in principle predictable at the physical stance at a steep computational cost; the intentional stance's selling point is compression and predictive yield rather than truth; and the question of whether the patterns it finds are real or merely useful is the thing the review keeps circling without ever quite posing.

To keep the piece from becoming a recap of familiar material, give the review a house gimmick. The outlet requires every tool it tests to be registered as a pattern with a measured compression ratio, and the lab has entered a fourth contender of its own invention, the underwriting stance — treating a system as a priced risk distribution — which outscores the intentional stance on prediction while surrendering all of its vocabulary. The intentional stance's mediocre showing should be reported with the flat affect of a mattress review. Include an aggrieved letter from a lab employee who was tested at the physical stance during the trials and now wants to know which stance the ethics board used on him.

The target is the habit, common to popular science writing and corporate consulting alike, of treating the stances as competing products and letting a predictive track record stand in for the question of what exists; land that objection without ever stating it. Do not resolve whether the stances are instruments or discoveries. A reader who has never heard of any of this should follow the review, and a philosopher of mind should find the ratings fair. No citations and no references to particular papers, books, or authors.
