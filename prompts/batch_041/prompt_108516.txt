I'm preparing a departmental podcast interview with a philosopher who defends a position she calls agential infinitism about the epistemic regress. Her view has three connected parts, and I'd like the questions to stay close to them. First, she holds that the regress is not primarily a relation among propositions but among acts of requesting and giving reasons: a chain counts as infinite because any answer to a why-question can be met with a further legitimate why-question, not because the subject stores infinitely many distinct justified beliefs. Second, she pairs this with a defeasible default entitlement for perceptual and memorial beliefs: such beliefs are justified without the subject's having any supporting reasons, yet the entitlement is reason-responsive and can be challenged, at which point the regress of reasons resumes. Third, she denies that justification requires the chain to be completed, claiming that finite subjects can participate in an infinite practice without ever finishing it.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions, each followed by a single follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. The set should press on the following, at a minimum:

- the self-application of her view: what justifies agential infinitism itself, and whether the answer at that meta-level has the same structure as the answer at the object level;
- the finite-mind objection, and specifically what makes a reason available to a subject rather than merely producible in principle;
- whether the chain may repeat propositional content, and if so, why that is not simply a circle;
- how her account differs from coherentism once both are described holistically;
- whether the default entitlement is really foundationalism in disguise, and whether defeater-defeaters generate a regress of their own;
- whether, since no chain is ever completed, her view leaves any belief fully justified or instead collapses into skepticism;
- whether redescribing the regress as a sequence of doxastic acts reintroduces the very finite-mind problem it was meant to solve;
- the transfer of justification along the chain: whether an infinite series of partial supports can yield anything other than a vanishing limit;
- the legitimacy of the demand for reasons, i.e., whether her view makes justification hostage to a sufficiently stubborn interlocutor.

At least two questions should force her to address the ordinary cases in which the regress never gets going — a belief formed in a flash of perception, say — and at least one should ask what could count as evidence against agential infinitism. I do not want questions that merely invite her to restate the position or to rehearse the other two horns of the trilemma; assume she knows those cold. Each follow-up should exploit the reply she is most likely to make rather than asking blandly for more detail.
