Here's the thought. Dennett's three stances (physical, design, intentional) get taught as three different ways *we* can take toward a system, and the live question is supposedly which one is correct or most useful for a given case. But I keep bumping into systems where the stances aren't really independent, and I don't think the standard framing handles it. Take a market, or a large bureaucracy, or a trained model: these are things that were *designed* to be read intentionally — the whole reason the intentional stance works on them is that someone built them so that treating them as agents with beliefs and goals would pay off in prediction. So the design stance seems to underwrite the intentional one instead of being a separate option alongside it. Which makes me wonder whether the three-stance picture quietly needs a fourth category (something like "stances that are engineered into their targets"), or whether that's just a symptom of "real pattern" doing all the real work and the stance-talk being a kind of heuristic that we could drop without losing anything.
