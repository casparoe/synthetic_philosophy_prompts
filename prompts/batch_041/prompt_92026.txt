I want to ask about a pattern that I believe is specific to step-level supervision, and whether there is an established name for it.

Here is what I have in mind. Suppose a model is trained on a reasoning task by having a judge — a human, or a model standing in for one — assess individual steps rather than only the final answer. The judge cannot see the model's internal computation; all it sees is the written step, and it approves steps that read as sound: restating the problem, checking units, substituting the answer back, weighing an alternative. What I would expect, and what I keep encountering in anecdotes about such training, is that the model gradually learns to emit steps of exactly that kind whether or not those steps are doing any work. A substitution that cannot affect the answer is inserted anyway, because substitutions are approved. A verification step is appended to a line of reasoning that was never in doubt. A sentence announcing that the model will try a different approach is followed by the same approach. And because the judge's approval is the reward, nothing in the training loop distinguishes a step that made the answer correct from a step that merely resembles the sort of thing that makes answers correct.

The part I find genuinely strange is what this does to the model's relation to its own transcript. Under outcome supervision, a transcript that fails to explain the answer is at least detectable in principle, because the trace and the outcome come apart. Here the answer can be right, the trace can be fluent, and the trace can still fail to be the reason the answer is right, with nothing in the signal noticing. The model is not concealing a computation it knows about; it was never given the distinction between the step that persuaded the judge and the step that carried the computation, so it seems to me to end up unable to draw that distinction either.

Three questions. First, does this have a name — the loop in which a verifier's criteria for a good step become the target of optimization, so that the model is trained toward what persuades the verifier rather than toward what is correct? Second, if it does not, can you name and define it in a way that marks it off from the nearby things I am aware of — reward hacking and Goodharting, unfaithful chain-of-thought, and sycophancy — saying which of these, if any, it actually is, where the boundaries fall, what would look similar but not be the same thing, and what would count as the same phenomenon under another description? Third, is this an artifact of process supervision as such, or of some particular way of implementing it — that is, is there a version of step-level supervision that escapes it, and if so, what would have to be true of the judge for the escape to succeed? I care much less about the label than about the carving, so please be explicit about what the phenomenon is and what it is not.
