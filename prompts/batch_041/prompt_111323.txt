For a long-form piece I am writing on the current quarrel over whether "the Middle Ages", "the Renaissance" and "the Scientific Revolution" name anything real, I need a taxonomy of the positions rather than a summary of the historiography, because most of the arguments I have sat in on turn out to be people answering different questions with the same words. Build the tree from the questions that actually do the separating: whether a period term is meant to pick out a genuine clustering of changes across otherwise independent domains or only a boundary drawn for convenience; whether the objection to a period is that it misdescribes the past or that it misdescribes the people who did the describing; whether the label is taken to have shaped the people it labels; whether "useful for inquiry" is offered as a justification of the category or as a substitute for one; and whether the scheme quietly assumes a European unit of analysis. Each leaf should be a position — named where a standard name exists, marked "unnamed" where it does not — with one sentence on that position's most awkward commitment, and the whole thing should come to no more than about ten leaves so that it can run as a sidebar. Tell me which combinations of answers nobody occupies, and whether that is because they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unvisited, and flag any leaves the tree cannot keep apart. Write for a general reader of the magazine: no citations, and no individual historian's name carrying structural weight.
