So I got roped into giving the remarks at the unveiling of the new automated fare gates at the Franklin Ave station, and I'm hoping you'll write them for me. Quick background: my sister's been a station agent their for 28 years, and these gates are the thing that finally lets the agency close her booth. The room's gonna be split straight down the middle. Half agency people and the vender's engineers, who sincerely believe a gate is just a gate and that whatever politics there is lives in the staffing decision, not in the hardware. The other half is my sister and about forty of her coworkers plus a bunch of regular riders, who think the gate itself is the decision. Two reporters in the back taking notes.

The one small thing everyone's actually fighting about: the old booth had a window, and the new gates have no window and nobody standing beside them, so there's nowhere left to ask a human for help. The agency keeps saying the new gates are more accessible, which my sister says is exactly backwards, and honestly they both have a point, which is the part I can't figure out how to say out loud.

What I want out of three minutes of speech: make the engineers feel respected, make the workers feel seen, and land one idea that neither side is quite saying — that a piece of equipment can be political without forcing anybody to do anything, just by quietly taking a question off the table. Once those gates are in, should there be a person here stops being a question anyone has to answer.

Don't turn it into a seminar, no name-dropping, no "as Langdon Winner argued..." — my sister would kill me. Warm, plain, a little funny if you can swing it. And short.
