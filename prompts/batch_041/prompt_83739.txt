My two closest friends are getting married next month and they've asked me to do the toast. Complicaton: they have been arguing about promises for basically their entire eleven years together, and they made me promise -- ha -- not to stand up and do the "marriage is a promise" bit as if it settled anything. She did a PhD in philosophy before she switched to law, and the vows she's written include a line saying that if he ever wants out, the vow doesn't hold him. He thinks that's incoherent: a promise you can walk away from whenever you feel like it isn't a promise, it's a description of your current mood. So the argument is live, at the wedding, and I have to be warm and funny in the middle of it without pretending it got resolved.

I want the toast to actually do something with the question of why promises bind, not just gesture at it. My own half-formed idea, wich may be nonsense: the reason a promise bites is the room. It's a made-up institution that only works because enough people agree to treat it as working, and a vow said out loud in front of witnesses is exactly where that gets switched on -- which would mean my toast isn't commentary on the promise, it's part of the machinery, and I should be honest about that from the podium.

So, four or five minutes, mixed room: half our grad school friends who will catch every reference, half family who won't. No bibliography, no seminar on the literature. And if the release-clause thing isn't really a coherent puzzle -- if I'm just confused about what a promise is -- say that straight out, to me or in the toast, rather than papering over it so the ending lands nicer.
